## Module 1 — Foundations of Self-Directed Learning
*This module comes first because mindset and prioritization are the pre-conditions for everything else — without these, even a perfect skill plan collapses under competing urgencies and fixed beliefs.*

### Lesson 1: Why Systems Beat Goals
**File:** `lessons/0001-systems-vs-goals.html`
**Key concepts:** systems thinking · process vs. outcome · continuous improvement
**Source:** https://jamesclear.com/goals-systems — Goals set direction but systems drive progress; winners and losers often share the same goal, the differentiator is the system behind it.
**Skill:** Write a one-sentence "system statement" for one skill you want to build — e.g. "I will read one chapter on communication every Tuesday and Thursday morning before work" instead of "I want to be a better communicator."
**Reference doc:** `reference/systems-thinking.html`

### Lesson 2: The Eisenhower Matrix — Protecting Your Learning Time
**File:** `lessons/0002-eisenhower-matrix.html`
**Key concepts:** urgency vs. importance · quadrant prioritization · eliminating busy-ness
**Source:** https://jamesclear.com/eisenhower-box — The Eisenhower Matrix divides tasks into four quadrants; what is urgent is rarely important and what is important is rarely urgent; personal development lives in Q2 (important, not urgent).
**Skill:** Take tomorrow's task list and classify every item into the four quadrants. Identify which Q3/Q4 items you can eliminate or delegate to protect Q2 learning time.
**Reference doc:** `reference/eisenhower-matrix.html`

### Lesson 3: The 1% Rule — Marginal Gains and Compound Growth
**File:** `lessons/0003-marginal-gains.html`
**Key concepts:** marginal gains · compound improvement · aggregation across dimensions
**Source:** https://jamesclear.com/marginal-gains — Breaking any skill into smallest components and improving each by 1% compounds to 37x improvement over a year; habits are the compound interest of self-improvement.
**Skill:** Pick one skill category from your roadmap and list five sub-components you could improve by 1% this week. Estimate what "1% better" would look like for each.
**Reference doc:** *(extend existing reference/systems-thinking.html)*

### Lesson 4: Growth Mindset — The Mental Pre-condition for Change
**File:** `lessons/0004-growth-mindset.html`
**Key concepts:** growth vs. fixed mindset · embracing challenge · sustained effort
**Source:** https://hbr.org/2016/01/what-having-a-growth-mindset-actually-means — Carol Dweck warns that merely claiming a growth mindset is not the same as practicing one; a genuine growth mindset means embracing challenges and learning from failure, not just labeling yourself positively.
**Skill:** Write a short reflection: describe one skill area where you have been thinking with a fixed mindset ("I'm just not good at this"). Then reframe it — what would approaching it with a growth mindset actually look like in practice?
**Reference doc:** `reference/growth-mindset.html`

## Module 2 — Learning Science and Habit Architecture
*This module comes second because knowing what to practice (Module 1) is only half the equation — this module provides the evidence-based methodology for how to practice and how to make practice sustainable.*

### Lesson 5: Deliberate Practice — How to Actually Get Better
**File:** `lessons/0005-deliberate-practice.html`
**Key concepts:** deliberate practice · purposeful repetition · chunking · deep focus
**Source:** https://jamesclear.com/deliberate-practice-theory — Deliberate practice is focused, goal-driven, and requires breaking skills into chunks and targeting weaknesses systematically; unlike routine repetition, it demands distraction-free concentration.
**Skill:** Choose one skill and design a 20-minute deliberate practice session: identify one specific weakness, one drill that targets only that weakness, and a way to measure whether you improved.
**Reference doc:** `reference/deliberate-practice.html`

### Lesson 6: Atomic Habits — Building a System That Runs Itself
**File:** `lessons/0006-atomic-habits.html`
**Key concepts:** habit loops · environment design · friction reduction · 1% daily gains
**Source:** https://jamesclear.com/atomic-habits — "You do not rise to the level of your goals, you fall to the level of your systems." Key levers: reduce environmental friction, use habit tracking, and plan for recovery — never miss twice.
**Skill:** Design the environment for one learning habit. Answer: Where will you practice? What will be visible/ready to reduce friction? What will you remove or hide to reduce temptation? Write the full setup.
**Reference doc:** `reference/atomic-habits.html`

### Lesson 7: Identity-Based Habits — Becoming the Person Who Does the Thing
**File:** `lessons/0007-identity-based-habits.html`
**Key concepts:** identity-based change · inside-out behavior change · self-image votes
**Source:** https://jamesclear.com/identity-based-habits — Lasting change works from identity outward: decide who you want to become, then prove it through small consistent wins; each completed rep is a vote for your new self-image.
**Skill:** Write an identity statement for each of the three skill categories you most want to develop. Not "I want to learn Python" but "I am someone who writes code every day." Then describe one small action you can take today that proves each identity.
**Reference doc:** *(extend existing reference/atomic-habits.html)*

## Module 3 — Technical Skills Roadmap
*This module comes third as a concrete application of the learning frameworks — showing how to sequence technical skill acquisition from zero to production-capable across Python, data science, web development, and software architecture.*

### Lesson 8: Starting with Python — Your Technical Foundation
**File:** `lessons/0008-python-roadmap.html`
**Key concepts:** programming fundamentals · domain tracks · portfolio building
**Source:** https://roadmap.sh/python — Community-driven roadmap from core Python (data types, OOP, functions) through three domain tracks: web/backend, data science, and automation/cloud; Git and portfolio projects throughout.
**Skill:** Decide which Python domain track matches your goals (web, data science, or automation). Write a one-paragraph "why this track" statement and list the first three topics from that track you will study.
**Reference doc:** `reference/technical-roadmaps.html`

### Lesson 9: AI and Data Science — A Five-Stage Path
**File:** `lessons/0009-ai-data-science-roadmap.html`
**Key concepts:** data literacy · progressive skill stages · portfolio vs. credentials
**Source:** https://roadmap.sh/ai-data-scientist — Five-stage path: foundations (math + Python) → small projects → structured courses → portfolio → professional experience; core competencies are data collection, cleaning, analysis, and visual communication.
**Skill:** Place yourself on the five-stage path: which stage are you currently at? Write one specific action for each of the next two stages — what will you do to advance from where you are now?
**Reference doc:** *(extend existing reference/technical-roadmaps.html)*

### Lesson 10: Full Stack Web Development — From HTML to Deployment
**File:** `lessons/0010-fullstack-roadmap.html`
**Key concepts:** front-end fundamentals · back-end development · database integration · deployment
**Source:** https://roadmap.sh/full-stack — Six-stage path: HTML/CSS/JS → UI framework → server-side + Node.js → SQL/NoSQL → integrated frameworks → Git, REST APIs, performance.
**Skill:** Map yourself onto the six stages. Identify the stage where you would place yourself today and write the single most important skill gap blocking your advancement to the next stage.
**Reference doc:** *(extend existing reference/technical-roadmaps.html)*

### Lesson 11: Beyond Coding — Software Design and Architecture
**File:** `lessons/0011-software-architecture.html`
**Key concepts:** architectural thinking · design patterns · systems over code
**Source:** https://roadmap.sh/software-design-architecture — Covers the layer of skills beyond writing code: architectural thinking, design patterns, and how components interact at scale; positions as the natural next step after mastering a language.
**Skill:** Think of an app or system you use daily. Sketch (on paper or in text) a simple architecture diagram: what are the major components, and how do they connect? This is systems thinking applied to something familiar.
**Reference doc:** *(extend existing reference/technical-roadmaps.html)*

## Module 4 — Soft Skills and Personal Foundations
*This final module covers the human and operational dimensions of self-improvement — communication, listening, time, and money — without which technical and cognitive skills plateau because there is no bandwidth or interpersonal context to apply them.*

### Lesson 12: Communication Skills — Listening Before Speaking
**File:** `lessons/0012-communication-skills.html`
**Key concepts:** listening vs. speaking · self-awareness · systematic skill development
**Source:** https://positivepsychology.com/communication-skills/ — Communication breaks into listening (attention, empathy, open-mindedness, nonverbal awareness) and speaking (clarity, tone, feedback); listening is identified as more important; five-step development: self-awareness → mindful pausing → active practice → nonverbal alignment → feedback.
**Skill:** After your next three conversations today, rate yourself 1–5 on: Did I let the other person finish before I started forming my response? Write a one-sentence observation for each.
**Reference doc:** `reference/communication-skills.html`

### Lesson 13: Active Listening — Seven Techniques to Practice Today
**File:** `lessons/0013-active-listening.html`
**Key concepts:** paraphrasing · emotional acknowledgment · open-ended questions · reflection
**Source:** https://positivepsychology.com/active-listening-techniques/ — Seven core techniques: paraphrasing, verbalizing emotions, asking open-ended questions, summarizing, clarifying, encouraging, and balancing; practice includes partner reflection, group listening, and solo mindfulness.
**Skill:** Pick one active listening technique and use it deliberately in two conversations today. Write a short note after each: what did you say, how did the other person respond, and what would you do differently?
**Reference doc:** *(extend existing reference/communication-skills.html)*

### Lesson 14: Time Management — Protecting Your Most Valuable Resource
**File:** `lessons/0014-time-management.html`
**Key concepts:** time as currency · four-quadrant planning · peak hours · interruption cost
**Source:** https://jamesclear.com/time-management — Randy Pausch's framework: time is irreplaceable; use four-quadrant planning, protect peak creative hours, limit interruptions (6–9 minutes lost per interruption + 4–5 minute recovery), delegate with full authority.
**Skill:** Track every 30-minute block of your next full working day. After the day, label each block with its quadrant (Q1–Q4) and calculate what percentage of your time went to Q2 (important, not urgent). Set one change for tomorrow.
**Reference doc:** `reference/time-management.html`

### Lesson 15: Financial Literacy — Your Money Foundation
**File:** `lessons/0015-financial-literacy.html`
**Key concepts:** budgeting · emergency fund · credit · investing basics · tax awareness
**Source:** https://www.financialeducatorscouncil.org/financial-literacy/ — NFEC curriculum spans five areas: budgeting (income vs. expense), saving (emergency fund + short/long-term), credit (scores, debt, interest), investing (compound growth, diversification, risk), and taxes (filing, deductions).
**Skill:** Answer these five questions about your own finances: (1) Do you have a written budget? (2) Do you have 3 months of expenses saved? (3) Do you know your credit score? (4) Do you have at least one investment account? (5) Have you filed your own taxes? For each "no," write one first step.
**Reference doc:** `reference/financial-literacy.html`

## Suggested Teaching Order

Follow the module sequence: Module 1 (mindset + prioritization) establishes the why, Module 2 (learning science) establishes the how, Module 3 (technical tracks) applies both to a concrete skill domain, and Module 4 (soft skills + foundations) completes the picture. Within each module, follow lesson order — each lesson builds on the prior one.
