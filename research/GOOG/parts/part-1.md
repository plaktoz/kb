## Part 1: Business Model

*Sources: Alphabet Inc. 10-K for fiscal year ended December 31, 2025 (filed February 5, 2026, SEC CIK 0001652044); Alphabet Q4 2025 Earnings Release; Statcounter Global Search Engine Market Share (July 2026); fourweekmba.com analysis; Yahoo Finance.*

---

### How the Company Generates Income

Alphabet operates three reportable segments: **Google Services**, **Google Cloud**, and **Other Bets**.

**Google Services** (85.2% of FY2025 revenue, $342.7B) contains two distinct income engines:

1. **Advertising (73.2% of total revenue, $294.7B in FY2025)** — Google runs a real-time auction system across its properties. Advertisers bid on keywords and audience signals; placement is determined by bid amount multiplied by a Quality Score. Revenue is recognised per click (Search, Display) or per thousand impressions (YouTube, Display). The model is fundamentally a two-sided market: Google provides free consumer-facing services (Search, Gmail, Maps, YouTube, Android, Chrome) to attract billions of users, then monetises their attention and behavioural data by selling targeted access to those users to advertisers. Importantly, Google controls the full chain: it owns the search engine, the browser (Chrome), the mobile OS (Android), the video platform (YouTube), and the advertising technology stack (AdMob, AdSense, Google Ad Manager). This vertical integration eliminates intermediaries and gives Google structural control over pricing and distribution.

2. **Subscriptions, Platforms, and Devices ($48.0B in FY2025)** — YouTube Premium/TV/Music, Google One (storage + Gemini model access), Google Workspace, Google Play app store commissions, and Pixel hardware sales.

**Google Cloud** (14.6% of FY2025 revenue, $58.7B) sells IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS to enterprises. Revenue is consumption-based and subscription-based. Google Cloud reached meaningful segment-level profitability, generating $13.9B in operating income in FY2025 (vs. $6.1B in FY2024), an operating margin of approximately 23.7%.

**Other Bets** ($1.5B revenue, ($7.5B) operating loss in FY2025) — a portfolio of early-stage ventures (Waymo autonomous vehicles, Verily life sciences, etc.) cross-subsidised by the core business. Not material to current economics.

### What the Company Provides and What Customer Needs It Addresses

| Product / Service | User Need Addressed |
|---|---|
| Google Search | Information retrieval; purchase research; navigation |
| YouTube | Entertainment; learning; creator monetisation |
| Gmail, Drive, Docs | Productivity; communication |
| Maps, Android, Chrome | Navigation; mobile OS; web browsing |
| Google Cloud (GCP) | Enterprise compute, storage, AI/ML infrastructure |
| Workspace | Business collaboration and productivity |
| Pixel / Nest | Consumer hardware |

The asymmetry is structural: consumer products are provided at zero monetary cost; the actual paying customers are **advertisers** (businesses paying for access to user intent signals) and **cloud customers** (enterprises paying for compute).

### Typical Clients and Client Concentration

**Advertising clients:** Millions of businesses globally, from individual SMBs using self-serve Google Ads to large agencies managing Fortune 500 brand campaigns. The base is extraordinarily fragmented. In Alphabet's 10-K history, no single advertiser has ever represented 10% or more of revenues. The lack of concentration is a fundamental feature: it prevents any one customer from exercising leverage and provides revenue stability.

**Cloud clients:** Enterprises, mid-market companies, public sector, and startups. Similarly, no disclosed concentration above 10%.

**Key caveat:** Apple is not a direct advertiser but is a critical revenue-sharing distribution partner. Google pays Apple approximately $15–18B per year to remain the default search engine on Safari and iOS. This inflates Search revenues materially. The arrangement is at legal risk following the August 2024 DOJ antitrust ruling that found Google holds an illegal monopoly in general search. The remedy phase (ongoing) could force termination of such exclusivity agreements — which would represent a direct, structural revenue impairment with no obvious replacement.

### Billing Model

| Revenue Type | Billing Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Search & Network advertising | Performance-based auction (CPC/CPM); invoiced monthly post-delivery |
| YouTube brand advertising | CPM reservation and auction; can include multi-quarter upfront deals |
| YouTube subscriptions | Recurring monthly/annual (consumer subscription) |
| Google Workspace | Per-seat monthly/annual SaaS subscription |
| Google One | Monthly/annual consumer subscription |
| Google Cloud (IaaS/PaaS) | Consumption-based with committed-use contracts available |
| Google Play | Revenue share on in-app purchases (typically 15–30% cut) |
| Pixel hardware | One-shot point-of-sale |

The dominant model is **performance-based variable revenue** (advertising), which is inherently not predictable quarter-to-quarter. The recurring revenue components (Cloud, Workspace, YouTube subscriptions) are growing but still represent a minority of total revenue.

### Cyclicality

The business is **moderately cyclical**, driven primarily through its advertising engine. Digital advertising correlates with corporate marketing budgets, which contract during economic downturns.

Evidence of cyclicality: In FY2022, overall revenue growth fell to ~9.8% (from 41% in FY2021) as advertisers pulled back during tightening macro conditions; YouTube advertising revenue declined outright (-2%) in FY2022. The Google Network segment (AdSense/AdMob) has been in multi-year decline since 2023 as the broader programmatic ad market contracts and advertisers shift spend toward owned-and-operated properties with higher conversion efficiency.

**Mitigant:** Search advertising is relatively recession-resilient compared to display/brand advertising, because it captures commercial intent at the bottom of the purchase funnel — advertisers maintain spend on high-ROI search terms even in downturns. However, it is not immune, and Google Cloud is structurally less cyclical as enterprise tech spending tends to lag the economic cycle.

### CAPEX: Magnitude, Trend, and Type

CAPEX has more than tripled in three years, becoming one of the most consequential financial characteristics of Alphabet.

| Year | CAPEX ($M) | Total Revenue ($M) | CAPEX / Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2020 | 24,640 | 182,527 | 13.5% |
| FY2021 | 31,485 | 257,637 | 12.2% |
| FY2022 | 32,251 | 282,836 | 11.4% |
| FY2023 | 52,535 | 307,394 | 17.1% |
| FY2024 | 91,447 | 350,018 | 26.1% |
| FY2025 | 91,447* | 402,836 | 22.7% |

*Per 10-K (FY2025): "Capital expenditures, which primarily reflected investments in technical infrastructure, were $91.4 billion for the year ended December 31, 2025."*

**Type of CAPEX:** Overwhelmingly **growth/expansion** in nature. The primary drivers are:
- Custom AI chips (TPUs — Tensor Processing Units) for training and inferencing Gemini and other AI models
- Data centre construction and expansion across multiple continents
- Networking infrastructure and undersea cables
- Server hardware refresh for Search, YouTube, and GCP

This is not maintenance capex. Google is building infrastructure capacity ahead of anticipated AI demand — a strategic bet that proprietary compute infrastructure will be a durable competitive advantage. The consequence is a substantial increase in fixed-cost base: depreciation on property and equipment grew from $11.9B (FY2023) to $21.1B (FY2025) and will continue rising as these assets come online. Operating cash flow of $164.7B in FY2025 comfortably covers this spend, but free cash flow (operating cash flow minus CAPEX) has been substantially compressed.

### R&D: Importance and Scale

R&D is central to Alphabet's competitive position and is non-negotiable. The company has invested over $200 billion in R&D over the past five years, per its own 10-K disclosures.

| Year | R&D ($M) | Revenue ($M) | R&D / Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | 45,427 | 307,394 | 14.8% |
| FY2024 | 49,326 | 350,018 | 14.1% |
| FY2025 | 61,087 | 402,836 | 15.2% |

R&D funding covers: the Gemini family of AI models; DeepMind (fundamental AI research, AlphaFold, Veo); Google Brain; custom TPU chip design; Android/Chrome development; quantum computing (Willow chip); and moonshots in Other Bets (Waymo, Verily). R&D intensity at ~15% of revenue is higher than most large-cap software companies and reflects an existential reality: Alphabet's core search advertising model faces structural replacement risk from AI. The company must simultaneously defend its existing position and build the AI layer that may cannibalise it — all while paying $61B/year for the privilege. Failure to sustain R&D leadership would accelerate competitive displacement.

### Pricing Power

Alphabet has **strong, structural pricing power** in its core advertising segment, with meaningful constraints.

**Sources of pricing power:**

1. **Self-reinforcing auction mechanism**: In Google Search, Alphabet does not set advertiser prices directly — advertisers bid against each other in a generalised second-price auction. As advertiser competition increases, yields rise automatically without Google having to raise list prices. In FY2025, cost-per-click (CPC) grew 7% year-over-year on 6% paid click volume growth, demonstrating ongoing yield expansion with no user or advertiser count decline.

2. **Structurally declining Traffic Acquisition Cost (TAC) rate**: TAC rate (TAC as % of advertising revenues) has declined as revenue mix shifts from Google Network properties (where Google shares ~70–80% of revenue with third-party publishers) toward Google's own properties (Search, YouTube) where it retains full economics. TAC: $54.9B in FY2024 and $59.9B in FY2025, representing approximately 20.3% of $294.7B in advertising revenue — down from historical levels above 23%.

3. **No viable substitute for intent-based advertising**: Google Search reaches users at the moment of expressed purchase intent. For performance advertisers in high-value verticals (e-commerce, financial services, legal, healthcare, travel), there is no channel with comparable conversion rates. This gives Google structural leverage to raise effective ad prices without losing advertiser volume.

4. **Cloud pricing**: More competitively constrained. Google Cloud is the #3 player behind AWS (~31% share) and Azure (~22% share), and uses pricing concessions and credits to win workloads. Pricing power in Cloud is limited.

**Constraints on pricing power:**
- The DOJ antitrust remedy could eliminate exclusive default-search distribution contracts, reducing query supply and weakening the competitive necessity of Google's ad inventory.
- AI-generated search answers (Google AI Overviews, competing LLM-based search tools) reduce the total pool of ad-eligible user interactions if queries resolve without a click.
- Cookie deprecation, GDPR enforcement, and US state privacy laws reduce targeting precision, which over time erodes advertiser willingness to pay for less-targeted impressions.

### Key Challenges Ahead for the Industry

**1. Generative AI disruption of the search-advertising link**
The 30-year model of search — user query → ranked blue links → advertiser clicks — is under structural pressure from AI-generated direct answers. Google's own AI Overviews in Search effectively compete with the commercial-intent clicks that fund the business. Every AI answer that resolves a query without a click eliminates an ad impression. The monetisation model for zero-click AI answer surfaces is unproven and unlikely to replicate current CPC economics at scale. Competing platforms (OpenAI SearchGPT, Microsoft Copilot, Perplexity) are growing, albeit from small bases. The industry faces a structural transition in information retrieval whose advertising economics at the destination are genuinely uncertain.

**2. Antitrust enforcement — existential tail risk**
The August 2024 US District Court ruling (United States v. Google LLC) found Google to hold an illegal monopoly in general search and general text advertising. The remedy proceedings are live. Potential outcomes include: prohibition on exclusive default-search distribution payments (the Apple deal is the most material); forced divestiture of Chrome or Android; mandatory data-sharing obligations with rivals. Any loss of the Apple default deal alone (~$15–18B/year in revenue-equivalent) would be a direct and permanent impairment. A second, separate DOJ suit targets the advertising technology stack; the remedy sought is full divestiture of Google's AdTech businesses (DV360, Google Ad Manager). EU Digital Markets Act enforcement adds parallel regulatory pressure in Europe.

**3. AI infrastructure capex spiral compressing free cash flow**
All hyperscalers are simultaneously investing $60–100B+/year in AI infrastructure. The industry is in a capital-intensity arms race that structurally compresses free cash flow across participants. For Alphabet specifically, CAPEX at 22–26% of revenue places operating leverage at risk unless AI monetisation delivers commensurate revenue acceleration. The bet is not yet validated.

**4. YouTube competition for creator and viewer attention**
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and Twitch compete directly for time-on-platform and creator economics. YouTube's advertising grew 11.7% in FY2025, but the short-form video ecosystem is fragmenting creator supply chains and viewer habits in ways that YouTube's long-form and mid-form formats cannot fully address.

**5. Privacy regulation eroding ad targeting precision**
Deprecation of third-party cookies, GDPR/DPA enforcement fines across Europe, and US state privacy legislation all reduce the behavioural targeting precision that underpins CPM premium pricing. Reduced targeting fidelity compresses advertiser willingness to pay for display and YouTube inventory, putting structural pressure on CPM rates over a multi-year horizon even as query volumes grow.
