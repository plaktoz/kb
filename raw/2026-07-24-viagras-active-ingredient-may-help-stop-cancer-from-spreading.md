# Viagra's Active Ingredient May Also Help Stop Cancer From Spreading

source_url: https://www.healthline.com/health-news/viagra-may-stop-cancer-from-spreading-study

---

A new study suggests sildenafil (Viagra's active ingredient) may slow cancer metastasis by blocking cancer cells' access to cholesterol — a key building block tumors need to spread to other organs. The research was led by the Weizmann Institute of Science in Israel, in collaboration with Clalit Health Services, and analyzed over 20 years of anonymized records from roughly 5 million patients, including an observational study of 40,000 males diagnosed with cancer.

Males who used Viagra before their cancer diagnosis showed better overall survival rates. Those combining Viagra with statins fared even better, as statins may further reduce cancer cells' ability to obtain cholesterol.

Researcher Samah Hayek cautioned: "Our study did not examine whether giving Viagra to patients after they are diagnosed with cancer improves outcomes."

The study was primarily male-focused since sildenafil is predominantly prescribed to men, leaving questions about female applicability unanswered.

Outside experts called the findings promising but preliminary. Oncologist Mike Lattanzi noted the results are "preclinical" and require human trial validation. Urologist Ramkishen Narayanan acknowledged the mechanism is "exciting" but noted translation to clinical practice "is still very limited."
