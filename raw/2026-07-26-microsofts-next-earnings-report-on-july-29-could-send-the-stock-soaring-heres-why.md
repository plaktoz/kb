# Microsoft's Next Earnings Report on July 29 Could Send the Stock Soaring. Here's Why.

source_url: https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/26/microsofts-next-earnings-report-on-july-29-could-send-the-stock-soaring-heres-why

---

By Bram Berkowitz, The Motley Fool — July 26, 2026

It's been a tough year for Microsoft shareholders: the stock, typically viewed as one of the market's safest plays, has lost nearly a fifth of its value on concerns about elevated AI-infrastructure capex and a potential "software armageddon." Microsoft reports fiscal Q4 2026 results after market close on Wednesday, July 29, with CEO Satya Nadella and management hosting the analyst call afterward.

## The chance to prove the AI strategy is working

Microsoft has guided for $190 billion in calendar-2026 capital expenditures, largely for AI infrastructure. Investors are also concerned Copilot isn't gaining enough traction — it had about 20 million paid enterprise seats on the last call, versus more than 450 million Microsoft 365 subscribers — and that M365 could eventually face AI-made alternatives.

Morgan Stanley analyst Adam Wood sees a good opportunity for Microsoft to prove its AI strategy is working, expecting accelerated Azure growth and picking-up Copilot adoption over the next few quarters. His bull case carries a $795 price target, implying more than a double from current levels.

## Long-term investors can buy the stock

The author argues long-term investors can buy in, while cautioning against trading around the near-term earnings event — other large AI companies with high capex guidance have seen significant sell-offs, a risk for Microsoft too if it guides for higher-than-expected capex, weak Azure revenue, disappointing Copilot adoption, or soft M365 subscriber growth.

Negative sentiment could also mean a strong print sends the stock sharply higher. Azure revenue has been growing at roughly 40% annually in recent quarters, and Microsoft trades around 23 times trailing earnings — well below its five-year average of about 32.5 — which the author sees as an undemanding multiple for a company well-positioned to maintain dominance in the business software world even as AI makes it easier to build competing tools.
