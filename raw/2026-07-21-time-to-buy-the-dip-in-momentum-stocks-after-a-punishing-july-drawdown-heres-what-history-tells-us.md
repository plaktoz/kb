# Time to buy the dip in momentum stocks after a punishing July drawdown? Here's what history tells us.

source_url: https://www.morningstar.com/news/marketwatch/2026072153/time-to-buy-the-dip-in-momentum-stocks-after-a-punishing-july-drawdown-heres-what-history-tells-us

---

By Joseph Adinolfi, MarketWatch (via Morningstar) — July 21, 2026

A powerful momentum trade that had swept up semiconductor names and other AI-infrastructure plays over the past month has hit the rocks. History suggests investors with the stomach for short-term volatility could be rewarded for buying back in. "If you want that long-term exposure, now is an excellent time. But in the very short term, it could be turbulent," said Travis Prentice, CIO at the Informed Momentum Company.

After rising more than 44% in the second quarter — its strongest quarterly performance on record — the S&P 500 Momentum Index slipped into correction territory, down 10.6% from its June 22 record close as of Monday's close, per Dow Jones Market Data.

## What history says

Dow Jones Market Data examined the past 10 times the Invesco S&P 500 Momentum ETF (SPMO) fell into correction territory (a 10%+ drop from a recent high). Over the three months following those corrections, SPMO rose 12.4% on average; a year later, average returns exceeded 24%, with win rates around 90%. The average maximum drawdown across those 10 corrections was about 15%, suggesting this selloff could still have room to run.

Some investment banks maintain their own momentum baskets incorporating long and short positions — Goldman Sachs' high-beta momentum basket has fallen more than 30% from its peak. Bespoke Investment Group noted the 47 Russell 1000 stocks that had gained 100%+ in H1 2026 were down an average of 21.6% since the start of July, while 17 stocks down 50%+ in H1 have bounced 10.6% — a rotation trade as energy, retail, and bank stocks rise while AI-related names struggle.

Melissa Brown, global head of investment-decision research at SimCorp, said the composition of momentum-tracking indexes could start to change as soon as early next month depending on how individual models are calibrated: "Momentum drawdowns tend to be big, but they don't tend to last a very long time, because eventually, the stocks with good momentum change." The Invesco SPMO ETF rebalances semi-annually, while the iShares Edge MSCI USA Momentum Factor ETF (MTUM) rebalances quarterly.
