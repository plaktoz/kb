# AI in practice: How new technology is affecting litigation practice

**Source:** https://masslawyersweekly.com/2026/07/27/ai-in-practice-how-new-technology-is-affecting-litigation-practice  
**Author:** Massachusetts Lawyers Weekly  
**Date:** 2026-07-27

---

AI is spreading through the daily work of litigators — from legal research and document review to drafting and discovery — and it's changing both what lawyers do and what firms look for in new hires.

**Where AI helps most:**  
Sean Carnathan (trial lawyer, O'Connor, Carnathan & Mack) uses AI for document review, brief enhancement, and deposition summaries via tools like Clio. He feeds opposing counsel briefs into AI along with his own draft and asks it to make his work more persuasive. Nicole Benjamin (trial and appellate litigator, Adler, Pollock & Sheehan) notes her firm uses one AI tool trained on legal material and is piloting five more — products like Lexis Protégé, CoCounsel, and Harvey that are designed to keep client data out of public models.

AI helps most with time-intensive work: document review, legal research, first-draft pleadings and motions, and managing discovery. But litigators treat these tools as a starting point, not a finished product.

**AI limitations in litigation:**  
- AI doesn't always grasp how a case decision applies — it will "tell you what it thinks you want to hear"
- AI-fabricated evidence is a real risk; Benjamin recently caught fabricated AI evidence because she knew the technology well enough to ask the right questions
- Remote depositions are not AI-suitable — a litigator must size up a witness in person to read demeanor

**Impact on billing:**  
Clients will not pay 20 hours for document review when software does it in seconds. Carnathan offered flat fees rather than arguing over bills. Howard Goldberg (MG+M, e-discovery committee chair) notes AI tool pricing ranges from modest for a general-purpose assistant to $1,200/seat for enterprise systems.

**Professional responsibility:**  
Attorneys using AI are required to understand the technology, educate themselves, and ensure output is accurate. "Your duties to your client and the court have not changed," Goldberg said.

**New skill priorities for junior hires:**  
AI changes what law firms look for. Benjamin now prioritizes:
- Interpersonal skills: managing client expectations, delivering bad news, knowing when to push settlement
- Analytical skills: making sense of discovery volume, spotting weaknesses in opposing arguments

"Now it's really critical that you have that coming in," Benjamin said. These skills used to be developed over time; now they must be present from day one.
