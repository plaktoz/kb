# Bending Spoons to Acquire Airtable for $1.29 Billion: What We Know

Bending Spoons has agreed to acquire Airtable for $1.29 billion in cash, according to an announcement reported by Axios on August 4, 2026. The transaction would put one of the best-known no-code database platforms inside the newly public software group behind products including Vimeo, Evernote, WeTransfer, Eventbrite, Meetup, and AOL.

The wording matters: this is an agreement to acquire Airtable, not a completed acquisition. No public announcement reviewed for this article gives a closing date, and Airtable has not announced changes to pricing, product availability, or its roadmap as a result of the deal.

## The deal at a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Announcement | August 4, 2026 |
| Buyer | Bending Spoons S.p.A. (Nasdaq: BSP) |
| Target | Airtable |
| Headline consideration | $1.29 billion in cash |
| Reported implied equity value | $2.25 billion, including Airtable cash and cash equivalents |
| Status | Agreement announced; completion has not been reported |

Axios reported a $1.29 billion cash purchase price and a $2.25 billion implied equity value after including Airtable's cash and cash equivalents. Those are related but different figures, so neither should be treated as a simple replacement for the other.

## Who is Bending Spoons?

Bending Spoons is a Milan-based software company built around acquiring and operating established digital products. Its own website says it intends to own businesses indefinitely and that the transformations it pursues are often deep. The company's portfolio now spans video, productivity, file transfer, events, communities, and internet services.

The Airtable agreement follows a major change in Bending Spoons' own profile. The company listed on Nasdaq under the ticker BSP in July 2026, raising $1.7 billion in its IPO. CEO Luca Ferrari told Axios at the time that predictability is the first characteristic the company looks for in acquisition targets.

That context helps explain why Airtable is notable: it is not merely a consumer app or media brand. For many teams, Airtable is part database, part workflow engine, and part internal application platform. It often holds operational data that is difficult to replace casually.

## Why the Airtable deal is getting attention

The price immediately invites comparison with Airtable's private-market peak. In December 2021, Airtable announced a $735 million Series F at an $11 billion pre-money valuation and said it had raised $1.36 billion in total. The new figures are not directly comparable: a financing-round pre-money valuation, a cash purchase price, and an implied equity value measure different things. Still, the gap captures how dramatically the market for high-growth SaaS companies has changed since 2021.

The deal also lands while AI is changing what teams expect from business software. A modern database platform is no longer judged only on forms, views, and automations. Buyers increasingly expect AI to understand operational context, act across live records, and help build the apps and workflows around that data. Airtable sits directly at that intersection.

## What is confirmed—and what is not

### Confirmed

- Bending Spoons has agreed to acquire Airtable.
- The reported consideration is $1.29 billion in cash.
- The reported implied equity value is $2.25 billion when Airtable's cash and cash equivalents are included.
- The agreement was announced after Bending Spoons' Nasdaq IPO.

### Still open

- The completion date and remaining closing process.
- Any changes to Airtable's plans, packaging, or pricing.
- How Airtable's product roadmap may evolve under new ownership.
- Whether customers will see changes to support or data policies.

In other words, it is reasonable for customers to review their dependency on Airtable. It is not reasonable to present unannounced pricing changes, layoffs, product cuts, or shutdowns as facts.
