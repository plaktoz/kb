# Artificial Sweetener Xylitol Linked to Higher Risk of Cardiovascular Events

**Author:** Elizabeth Pratt
**Date:** September 2, 2026
**Fact-Checked By:** Jill Seladi-Schulman, Ph.D.

## Overview

A new study presented at the European Society of Cardiology Congress 2026 in Munich found that higher blood levels of xylitol — a common sugar alcohol used in foods, beverages, and dental products — are associated with elevated cardiovascular risk.

## Key Findings

- Data from over 17,000 participants across Canadian and UK cohorts were analyzed
- Canadian cohort (6-year follow-up): highest xylitol blood levels correlated with a **57% higher risk** of major adverse cardiovascular events
- UK cohort (30-year follow-up): highest levels showed an **18% higher risk**
- The association held even after adjusting for hypertension, BMI, smoking, and lipid levels
- Findings suggest a dose-dependent relationship between xylitol levels and cardiovascular risk

## Expert Commentary

Cardiologist Cheng-Han Chen noted the study "adds to a growing body of research raising questions about whether some artificial sweeteners may have unintended effects."

Dietitian Dana Hunnes suggested "small amounts of real sugar" may be preferable to artificial sweeteners.

Cardiologist Eleanor Levin emphasized broader dietary strategies, noting avoiding sweeteners is "less important than other dietary modifications" like the DASH diet.

## Caveat

The research was presented at a conference but has **not yet been peer-reviewed or published** in a scientific journal.
