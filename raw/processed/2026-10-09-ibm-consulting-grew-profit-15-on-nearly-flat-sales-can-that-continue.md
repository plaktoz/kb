# IBM Consulting Grew Profit 15% on Nearly Flat Sales. Can That Continue?

International Business Machines Corporation (NYSE:IBM) produced a useful surprise inside its consulting business: second-quarter segment profit rose 15.1%, although revenue increased just 0.2%. Improving delivery economics can lift earnings, but repeated margin expansion becomes harder when sales barely move.

The July 22 results and quarterly filing showed $647 million of consulting segment profit on $5.327 billion of revenue. A year earlier, profit was $562 million. IBM attributed the improvement primarily to productivity actions, partly offset by investment in innovation.

## Each margin point has a measurable payoff

At the reported revenue base, one percentage point of consulting margin represents $53.3 million of quarterly segment profit. A one-point retreat would absorb more than three-fifths of the quarter's $85 million year-over-year profit gain. This is a sensitivity calculation, not a forecast.

Consulting gross margin also improved, to 28.9% from 27.5%. That places part of the gain in the economics of delivering client work. Better utilization and more efficient delivery can preserve profit without requiring a surge in billings.

There is supporting demand evidence. Consulting signings increased 5% as reported, and the trailing book-to-bill ratio was 1.05. Those indicators could help sales resume growing. Their conversion depends on project timing and customer commitments; signings are management estimates rather than a standardized revenue measure.

A book-to-bill ratio just above one indicates that trailing signings modestly exceeded revenue, not that revenue is about to accelerate by the same percentage. Contract duration and the pace of work can separate bookings from sales for several quarters. Demand conversion matters because efficiency gains eventually become a tougher year-over-year comparison.

## Better delivery economics need a durable customer base

A falling cost base can reward shareholders even before demand accelerates. The bear case is that further efficiency gains become harder to achieve, or customers retain some benefit through lower prices.

Repeating 15% profit growth with a flat margin would require roughly 15% revenue growth, far above this quarter's pace. With flat revenue, it would instead require the segment margin to rise from about 12.1% to 14.0%. These alternatives illustrate the burden of sustaining the profit growth rate; neither is management guidance.

At the October 8 close, IBM traded near 20.2 times trailing earnings. Consulting segment profit cannot be capitalized directly into a group price target because taxes, corporate costs and IBM's other businesses also affect shareholder earnings.

Insider Monkey's database recorded 74 hedge-fund holders in Q2 2026, up from 59 in Q1; AQR increased its shares roughly 43%. Consulting's improvement is real, but repeating 15% profit growth now requires another efficiency gain or much stronger sales conversion.
