# What Is The Current Evidence Into AI Tutoring And The Impact On Learners In School?

The UK Department for Education announced plans in 2025 to trial AI tutoring tools with up to 450,000 disadvantaged students by 2027, one of the most significant EdTech commitments any government has made, aiming to close the achievement gap for students who cannot access private tutors. The Education Endowment Foundation (EEF) identifies one-to-one and small-group tutoring as one of the most effective school interventions, worth an average of five additional months' progress — but scaling human tutoring affordably has always been the challenge.

A 2026 review by Vanacore, Baker et al. found that what makes human tutoring effective is the quality of the conversation: tutors scaffold reasoning rather than explain answers, sustain motivation through dialogue, and build trust that keeps students willing to struggle productively. Adaptive learning platforms that personalize content but aren't conversational don't replicate this; neither do on-demand chat tools, since large language models tend toward "over-helpfulness," reducing the metacognitive effort that produces real learning.

**Where generic AI falls short:** In a randomized controlled trial (Bastani et al., 2025), students who used ChatGPT to study performed about 50% better in practice sessions but 17% worse on the actual exam — the most common student prompt was "give me the answer." Even purpose-built tools aren't immune: a qualitative evaluation of Khan Academy's Khanmigo found it didn't tailor tasks to individual needs or support metacognitive skills.

**Where carefully designed AI tutoring shows promise:** Kestin et al. (2025) ran an RCT comparing a well-designed AI tutor against a flipped classroom in an introductory college physics course. The AI tutor produced median learning gains more than double the active-learning control group, with effect sizes of 0.73–1.3 standard deviations — among the largest ever recorded in higher-education research. A November 2025 Eedi and Google DeepMind RCT tested LearnLM, a Socratic AI tutor prompting students to identify their own mistakes; human tutors approved 82.3% of its suggestions with no or minimal edits, though full human oversight of every message isn't scalable.

**Independent evaluation of Third Space Learning's Skye tutor:** Professor Rose Luckin's Educate Ventures Research analyzed 9,320 student sessions and found students improved from 34% accuracy on diagnostic check-in questions to 92% on check-out assessments within a single session. Confidence outcomes rose for 63.8% of students, with only 11.1% showing a decrease. Struggling and anxious students benefited most, but so did high-achievers on challenging material; teachers described the tool as a "force-multiplier for practice."

The article notes the DfE's Generative AI safety standards (updated June 2025) require student-facing AI tools not provide direct answers by default, use progressive disclosure and hints instead, and keep human oversight central — the same principles the research shows produce genuine learning gains. Only 25.6% of disadvantaged UK students achieved a strong pass in English and maths at GCSE in 2024–25, versus 52.8% of non-disadvantaged peers, underscoring the urgency. The piece concludes AI tutoring can improve outcomes, but only when built around evidence-based tutoring principles rather than general-purpose AI capability.
