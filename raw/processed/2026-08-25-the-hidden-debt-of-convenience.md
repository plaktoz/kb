# The Hidden Debt of Convenience

**Author:** Sahil Bloom
**Date:** August 25, 2026

---

Bloom opens with Conan O'Brien's preference for commercial flying, enjoying TSA interactions despite his wealth. Abigail Disney is also quoted noting that private jets let you "get around a certain reality."

The piece argues that money legitimately reduces early-life stressors — grocery anxiety, unexpected bills, household burdens. As Dr. Arthur Brooks notes, early patterns create a psychological link where "the bell of money rings" and we expect happiness to follow.

But as wealth grows, the problems being solved become trivial — lawn appearance, hotel linens, TSA lines. The assumption that *more money → less inconvenience → more happiness* eventually **inverts**.

## The Core Argument

Inconvenience is a "tax on exposure to reality." Eliminating all friction, like over-prescribing antibiotics, destroys the very resilience it was meant to protect. The "adaptability muscle" atrophies without use.

Convenience is framed as **debt** with two repayment paths:

- **Involuntary:** Chaos derails you unexpectedly
- **Voluntary:** Intentionally embracing friction on your own terms

## Prescription

Take stairs, cook meals, stand in line phone-free, pursue physical challenges. Friction, he concludes, is what makes life feel **real and memorable**.
