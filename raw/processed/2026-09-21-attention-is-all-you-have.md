# Attention is all you have

The piece opens with the Tetris effect — the psychological phenomenon where prolonged focus on something reshapes perception. Whatever occupies your attention long enough begins to shape your thinking.

The core argument is that modern platforms have seized control of that attention. Algorithms on YouTube, Spotify, LinkedIn, and Reddit no longer let users choose their own information diet — instead serving engagement-maximizing content, AI-generated filler, and corporate-aligned messaging. Allowing others to control your screen is like giving them the key to your brain.

The author contrasts today's algorithmic web with an earlier, more deliberate internet — one built around personal bookmarks, niche blogs, wikis, and RSS feeds. That older web required intentionality: you chose where to go and why. Harmful content existed, but it wasn't served algorithmically alongside mundane searches.

The conclusion is optimistic but grounded: the intentional internet still exists beneath the corporate layer. Returning to it requires tolerating a slower, finite content experience — but like any habit, it builds with consistent attention.
