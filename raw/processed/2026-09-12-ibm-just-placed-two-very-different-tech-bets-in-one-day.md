# IBM Just Placed Two Very Different Tech Bets in One Day

On September 10, 2026, IBM announced two unrelated partnerships on the same day — one with a defense contractor, another with a space agency.

## Quantum Lab in Switzerland

IBM and Lockheed Martin announced a quantum innovation hub at ETH Zurich, tied to Switzerland's 2021 purchase of 36 F-35A jets from Lockheed. That deal created roughly $3 billion in offset obligations. Rather than fulfilling those obligations through factory work, Lockheed directed funds toward a quantum computing facility — Switzerland's first IBM Quantum System Two, expected by late 2026.

IBM supplies and operates the hardware. Notably, IBM had completed its acquisition of HRL Laboratories just weeks earlier, adding quantum sensing expertise. Quantum sensing for navigation is one of two named research areas for the hub.

## Lunar AI Model with NASA

The same day, IBM and NASA released the NASA IBM Lunar Foundation Model as open source, trained on decades of lunar observation data. It reportedly outperforms existing detection methods by up to 23% for identifying ice deposits and craters, though improvement on volcanic features is closer to 3%.

The model runs on TerraTorch, IBM's geospatial AI toolkit — useful for both civilian lunar mapping and satellite defense analysis.

## Stock Performance

IBM shares closed at $234.02 on September 10, down 2.47%, despite the positive announcements. Shares remain roughly 30% below their 2026 high of $332.46. IBM Z mainframe sales fell 42% last quarter, dragging infrastructure revenue down 7%.

Still, Wall Street holds a Buy consensus, with 25 analysts targeting a 12-month price of $245.35. Evercore ISI analyst Amit Daryanani argues investors are receiving IBM's quantum exposure "essentially for free at current prices."

IBM's quantum backlog reportedly exceeds $1 billion and is growing. The next earnings report is scheduled for October 21.
