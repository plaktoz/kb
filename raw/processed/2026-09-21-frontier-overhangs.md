# Frontier Overhangs

Thompson argues that Anthropic's "Pace the Frontier" safety rhetoric conveniently addresses several distinct **business problems** — what he calls "overhangs" — created by rapid AI improvement.

## Five Overhangs Identified

**1. Capability Overhang**
Models are now "good enough" that customers won't sacrifice data policies for cutting-edge performance. Christensen's integration→modularization theory applies: as performance sufficiency is reached, the basis of competition shifts. Microsoft's Copilot now supports model-swapping, separating harness from model.

**2. Product Overhang**
Meta's Muse personal agent — built on a non-state-of-the-art model — demonstrates that sufficient capability now exists to build genuinely sticky products. Frontier labs should focus resources on building moats rather than chasing raw capability gains.

**3. Pricing Overhang**
Current high prices reflect supply constraints, not true marginal cost. Slowing training would free compute for inference, allowing labs to fully capture the market at sustainable prices.

**4. Capital Overhang**
Anthropic claims adjusted profitability, but excludes training costs — which are massive. The correction notes Anthropic *is* profitable including training, but the broader capital risk across hyperscalers and neoclouds remains real.

**5. Safety Overhang**
Thompson distinguishes *alignment risk* from real cybersecurity threats. He argues the capability overhang is currently **offensive** — defenders need *more* advanced models, not fewer. Pacing the frontier widens the window of vulnerability.

## Core Argument

Thompson concludes the most significant overhang is **competition**: Anthropic's push for government-enforced pacing arrived precisely when OpenAI regained the frontier lead — suggesting strategic motivation dressed in safety language.
