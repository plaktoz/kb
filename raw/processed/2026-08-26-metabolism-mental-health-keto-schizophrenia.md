# How your metabolism could alter your mental health

In 2009, medical student Shebani Sethi watched a patient in a weight loss program make a remarkable recovery: a dietary intervention alleviated her schizophrenia symptoms after years of failed medication attempts. That experience sent Sethi down a medical path that would lead her to treating mental health in a radically different way.

Sethi, now at Stanford, discusses the emerging field of metabolic psychiatry and how ketogenic metabolic therapy may treat schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and depression. As trends like the keto diet have soared in general popularity, doctors are increasingly looking at a more measured, clinical use of the intervention specifically to treat mental health disorders — building on the same 2009 case that originally sent Sethi into the field. Sethi has since published further research on metabolic psychiatry in academic journals detailing how dietary interventions affect underlying metabolic processes implicated in psychiatric conditions.
