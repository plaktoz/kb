# The Business Of One: Three Pillars Of Sustainable Solopreneurship

According to the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://data.census.gov/table/NONEMP2024.NS00NONEMP), approximately 32 million businesses in the United States operated without paid employees in 2024, generating $1.86 trillion in total revenue. Between 2012 and 2023, the number of businesses without paid employees grew at an [average annual rate of 2.7%](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2025/07/nonemployer-business-growth.html), compared to 1.1% for businesses with employees. These figures reflect the growing prominence of solopreneurship.

What motivates people to become solopreneurs? What business models lend themselves to solopreneurship? What challenges do solopreneurs face? In this article, I explore these questions and discuss three key areas of building a sustainable solo business: financial, operational and personal sustainability.

## Understanding Solopreneurship

People become solopreneurs for different reasons. Some seek greater autonomy, while others turn to entrepreneurship after losing a job or struggling to find suitable employment. Some find traditional corporate environments limiting or seek a career that better aligns with their personal values and lifestyle. Others simply enjoy practicing their profession without managing employees.

Solopreneurship spans a wide range of industries and business models, from consulting and coaching to physical products, e-commerce, digital products and software as a service (SaaS). The business model you choose largely depends on your passions, professional experience and long-term goals.

Solopreneurship can be a rewarding career choice. However, many aspiring solopreneurs underestimate the challenges of running a business on their own.

## Financial Sustainability

While some pursue solopreneurship as a side hustle, our focus here is on those who want to make a full-time living. Set a financial goal based on your local cost of living, personal financial circumstances and long-term aspirations.

Building your reputation and gaining market traction takes time. It may take several months or longer to generate meaningful income. Some solopreneurs rely on personal savings during the early stages. Maintaining employment or taking on low-friction side gigs while building your business can help alleviate financial stress.

Develop a revenue model that supports long-term financial sustainability. For professional service providers, transitioning from hourly billing to value-based pricing can increase earning potential without being limited by billable hours. Diversifying your client base and limiting dependence on a single large client can help create a more stable revenue stream.

For SaaS businesses, tiered subscription plans based on usage or functionality can increase revenue from existing customers as their needs grow. For e-commerce businesses, improving profit margins, increasing average order value and encouraging repeat purchases can contribute to greater profitability.

## Operational Sustainability

Being a solopreneur does not mean you have to do everything alone. According to [Simply Business's 2025 Solopreneur Report](https://www.simplybusiness.com/resource/2025-solopreneur-report/), 61% of respondents underestimated the challenge of handling all business functions on their own. Many solopreneurs take pride in wearing multiple hats, from marketing and production to operations, customer service and finance. Limited financial resources may require this initially, but few of us are good at everything. Outsourcing tasks outside your core expertise to independent contractors or specialized service providers can save time, produce better results and potentially increase revenue without hiring employees.

AI can be your thought partner, administrative assistant, bookkeeping assistant and much more. It enables solopreneurs to handle tasks they previously needed to outsource. Developing AI skills requires an investment of time, but the potential benefits include cost and time savings, greater efficiency and an improved customer experience.

Watching Netflix at 2 p.m. on a weekday is a real thing for solopreneurs. Working for yourself offers tremendous flexibility, but without structure and self-discipline, you may find yourself busy all day without accomplishing much. Transitioning from a nine-to-five job to solopreneurship requires a fundamental shift in mindset. You are responsible for creating work opportunities, setting priorities and holding yourself accountable. Establish a routine that supports productivity and a healthy work-life balance.

Many solopreneurs excel in their profession but lack sales experience. As a solopreneur, you are often the only salesperson in your business. Developing strong sales skills requires courage, practice, a willingness to face rejection and, most importantly, persistence.

## Personal Sustainability

Entrepreneurship can be a lonely journey, especially for solopreneurs. Maintaining your personal resilience is essential to maintaining your business resilience. Building a community of mentors, fellow entrepreneurs and other professionals can reduce isolation and provide the intellectual stimulation found in traditional workplaces. Developing a strong personal network outside your business can also support your emotional well-being.

Burnout can be particularly challenging for solopreneurs facing financial stress, revenue uncertainty and the burden of making business decisions alone. Recognize and celebrate small accomplishments, establish healthy boundaries between work and personal life, and intentionally set aside time each day and week for activities that bring you personal fulfillment.

## The Human Side of Solopreneurship

Being a solopreneur is as much a personal choice as it is a professional one. One of the greatest benefits of solopreneurship is the opportunity to define success on your own terms. Take the time to understand what you want to achieve financially, professionally and personally, and build a business that supports those aspirations.
