# Drinking orange juice every day may change thousands of genes

A recent study shows regular orange juice consumption can influence the activity of thousands of genes inside immune cells, many of which help control blood pressure, calm inflammation, and manage how the body processes sugar. The study followed adults who drank 500ml of pure pasteurized orange juice daily for two months. After 60 days, genes associated with inflammation and higher blood pressure — including NAMPT, IL6, IL1B, and NLRP3 — had become less active, as had SGK1, a gene affecting the kidneys' ability to retain sodium.

Researchers suspect natural citrus compounds such as hesperidin, a flavonoid known for antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects, are partly responsible. The response varied by body size: people carrying more weight showed greater changes in fat-metabolism genes, while leaner volunteers showed stronger anti-inflammatory effects.

A systematic review of 15 controlled trials (639 participants) found regular OJ consumption lowered insulin resistance and blood cholesterol. Another analysis in overweight/obese adults found small reductions in systolic blood pressure and increases in HDL ("good") cholesterol after several weeks. One study found blood orange juice for a month increased gut bacteria that produce short-chain fatty acids, compounds that help maintain healthy blood pressure and reduce inflammation. A study of 68 obese participants found daily OJ improved endothelial function (how well blood vessels relax and widen), which is associated with lower heart attack risk.

Not all findings are uniform — LDL ("bad") cholesterol often falls, but triglycerides and HDL don't always change much across studies. Researchers note whole fruit remains the better choice due to fiber content, but a modest daily glass of pure orange juice appears to have cumulative effects on inflammation, blood flow, and long-term heart health markers.

Journal reference: Fraga et al., "A Global Transcriptomic Analysis Reveals Body Weight-Specific Molecular Responses to Chronic Orange Juice Consumption in Healthy Individuals," Molecular Nutrition & Food Research, 2025.
