# Wall Street suffers historic losses as Dow, S&P 500 plunge into correction territory

U.S. stocks plunged Friday in one of Wall Street's worst trading sessions in years, when a massive sell-off in artificial intelligence companies wiped out the Nasdaq's gains for the year and pushed the Dow and S&P 500 into correction territory.

The historic sell-off sent major U.S. indexes sharply lower, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average falling nearly 950 points, while the S&P 500 dropped 3.7% and the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite plunged 5.28%.

Both the Dow and S&P 500 entered correction territory, meaning they had fallen at least 10% from their recent record highs.

The steep decline erased the Nasdaq's gains for the year, marking its second-largest single-day point drop on record.

"It was a brutal day in the stock market with AI fears taking center stage," FOX Business anchor Gerri Willis reported Friday on "Special Report."

Investors directed much of their selling toward technology companies, putting additional pressure on the Nasdaq, Willis noted.

"Investors saved their real wrath for the tech-heavy Nasdaq," Willis said.

The massive sell-off came amid mounting concerns about the AI boom, with some of the industry's biggest names suffering steep losses, including chip-making giant Nvidia and newly public AI developer Anthropic.

Nvidia shares tumbled approximately 14%, while Anthropic fell about 9% during its second trading session as a public company.

Friday's declines came despite strong earnings from Nvidia and a highly anticipated stock market debut for Anthropic, which failed to ease investor concerns about soaring AI valuations and spending.

Recent technology disruptions affecting major companies and institutions over the past 10 days may have contributed to investor unease, Willis said.

"Much of the investor anxiety [has been] spiking this week on reports of tech meltdowns at companies as diverse as United Airlines and JPMorgan Chase," Willis said.

The historic losses capped a particularly difficult week for Wall Street, with the S&P 500 posting its worst weekly percentage decline since April 2025, while the Nasdaq recorded its largest weekly percentage loss since March 2020.

The Dow also suffered its steepest weekly decline since April 2025, according to Reuters.

Friday marked the S&P 500's seventh consecutive losing session, its longest losing streak since February 2020.

The market turmoil also sent Wall Street's closely watched volatility index, commonly known as the VIX or "fear gauge," to its highest closing level since April 2025.

Willis also noted that gold prices rose as investors sought safer assets amid the sell-off.

Reuters contributed to this report.
