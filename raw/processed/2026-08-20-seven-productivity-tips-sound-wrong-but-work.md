# 7 Productivity Tips That Sound Wrong (But Actually Work)

Counterintuitive habits that hold up in practice, centered on consistency rather than working harder:

1. **Do Less Than You Could** — an old Scrum principle: find a sustainable pace rather than cramming every available slot, so quieter periods aren't immediately filled with more commitments.
2. **Don't Work on Weekends** — protecting free time (even beyond "after hours" work) allows the brain to rest and actually creates room for creativity; ideas tend to surface once you're truly rested, not while grinding.
3. **Plan Your Week in Advance** — dump tasks into a tool (the author uses ChatGPT) to turn them into a daily plan weighted by urgency and importance, while still deliberately doing less than you could.
4. **Set Embarrassingly Achievable Goals** — vague, huge goals ("become a Staff Engineer") trigger avoidance; realistic, measurable, self-dependent goals ("get an internship") are achievable and keep momentum.
5. **Deliver Only What You're Asked For** — resist over-polishing; treat personal/work deliverables like an MVP, and agree separately (and get paid) for extra scope with clients.
6. **Know What "Done" Means Before You Start** — vague goals like "learn JavaScript" never finish; define a concrete completion criterion up front (e.g., "complete this tutorial and pass the test").
7. **Reply to Small Things Immediately** — if a message takes under five minutes, answer within the hour rather than letting it pile into a backlog, unless mid-deep-work.

The author frames these as antidotes to burnout: pushing hard is sometimes necessary for real deadlines, but when those moments become the default for months, it becomes a direct path to burnout.
