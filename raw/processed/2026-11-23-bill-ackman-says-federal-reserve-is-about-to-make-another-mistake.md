# Bill Ackman says Federal Reserve is 'about to make another mistake'

Billionaire hedge fund manager Bill Ackman is warning that the Federal Reserve would be making a mistake if it hikes interest rates as expected in early December, saying the central bank's push to return inflation to the 2% target could risk a recession.

The Federal Reserve is scheduled to hold its next monetary policy meeting on Dec. 8-9, with the market overwhelmingly expecting policymakers to raise interest rates by 25 basis points in an effort to slow the pace of inflation. The CME FedWatch tool shows a 96.1% probability of a rate hike of that size, which would leave the benchmark federal funds rate at a range of 4% to 4.25%.

Ackman, the founder and CEO of Pershing Square Capital Management, warned that conditions in financial markets are already tight and that hiking interest rates would squeeze companies' ability to finance their investments.

"I think the Fed is about to make another mistake on December 9th. Financial conditions have tightened massively," Ackman wrote in a post on X. "For example, @SpaceX, an investment grade company, just raised secured debt financing at an 8.259% interest rate versus below 6% for its unsecured bond offering at the time of its IPO."

Ackman added that the "cost of equity capital has also increased materially with the recent substantial decline in the stock market."

Members of the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC), the Fed panel tasked with managing monetary policy moves, have been increasingly focused on inflation, which has remained stubbornly above the 2% target for nearly six years.

The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, the personal consumption expenditures (PCE) index, was 3.6% in September with October's reading due out on Wednesday.

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh reaffirmed the central bank's commitment to the 2% inflation target as part of the Fed's dual mandate, which also includes a focus on promoting maximum employment, after its October meeting where policymakers left rates unchanged ahead of the election the following week.

"What I can do is tell you there's no soft inflation target and no implicit inflation target. There's a target of price stability and it's 2% as measured by PCE," Warsh said, adding that it's "our responsibility to get inflation down."

Ackman pushed back on that narrative, saying that the "Fed's 2% inflation ambition is misguided."

"Geopolitical risk and the required investment in global military spending, de-globalization and tariffs, and the massive AI infrastructure and related energy investment are features of the world that did not exist when the 2% target was set," he explained. "3% inflation is a more realistic ambition in the new world."

"The Fed will put the U.S. and world in recession if it continues to raise rates in a failed attempt to drive inflation to 2%," Ackman said.
