# The Focusing Illusion: A Science-Backed Life Trap

Bloom opens with a contrast: one man enjoying California sunshine, another scraping ice off a windshield in New York. Both are him, one year apart.

A 1998 Kahneman & Schkade study (~2,000 students) found no actual happiness difference between Midwest and California students — yet both groups predicted Californians were happier. People judged others by the most *visible* factor (weather), ignoring everything else.

This is the **focusing illusion**: overweighting one salient, visible factor when assessing happiness.

Kahneman's summary: *"Nothing in life is as important as you think it is, while you are thinking about it."*

A 2003 housing study reinforced this — students overestimated physical housing features and underestimated social ones. They judged on *what differed*, not *what mattered*.

The core trap: whether comparing yourself to others or making future decisions, you fixate on what's visible. But visible factors rarely drive actual happiness.

Bloom's antidote — the **Tuesday Test**: *"What would an average Tuesday feel like?"* — redirects attention from highlight-reel comparisons to lived daily reality.

His conclusion: moving to cold New York meant grandparents, community, and connection. The ice was irrelevant.

*"Stop comparing Januaries. Start comparing Tuesdays."*
