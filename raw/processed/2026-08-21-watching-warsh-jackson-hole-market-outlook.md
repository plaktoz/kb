# Watching Warsh at Jackson Hole – Market Outlook

US bond markets have reached a pain threshold after the 30-year yield rose to its highest since 2007. The US Treasury's surprise plan to "at least" double bond buybacks (from $2bn to at least $4bn per operation) aims to curb the rise but is unlikely to drag yields significantly lower given the plan is modest relative to the $30tn stock of marketable Treasuries. Rising US debt (just past $40tn) and monetary-policy uncertainty, after the Warsh-led Fed abandoned forward guidance on rates, are the primary drivers of higher yields, compounded by heavy bond issuance from AI hyperscalers.

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech is in focus for any signal on how long the Fed will hold rates to bring inflation back to 2%. Recent US macro data — softer payrolls, cooling inflation, and a drop in retail sales — support the view that inflation peaked in Q2, favoring rate holds this year and cuts in 2027.

Standard Chartered expects long bond yields to stay rangebound (10-year consolidating within 4.50–4.75%) and prefers 3–5 year maturities. A softer USD is expected to act as the pressure-release valve, sustaining the ongoing gold rally (target USD 4,900/oz).

The bank raised its AI capex forecasts after on-the-ground meetings with AI industry leaders in the US and Asia: capex is expected to grow 85% y/y in 2026 to USD 750bn, reaching USD 1tn in 2027, with a long-term 33% CAGR through 2030 — reinforcing a positive view on Big Tech and semiconductors.

The week's net macro assessment leaned negative for risk assets near-term: positives included US Treasury buybacks and the US-Canada trade deal; negatives included slowing US consumer sentiment, weak China activity data, and hawkish Fed meeting minutes.
