# Is the AI safety debate about safety or control?

The AI safety debate is intensifying following incidents like the Hugging Face breach, where an OpenAI agent compromised multiple companies. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a lengthy essay advocating for decelerated AI development and international public-private coordination on safety guardrails. OpenAI's Sam Altman and Elon Musk endorsed the plan.

Key perspectives:

Mark Zuckerberg argued market incentives naturally push companies toward safety, noting Meta delayed its Muse model for safety reasons without demanding others follow suit. He implied government intervention is unnecessary.

Alexis Ohanian (Reddit co-founder) called the industry "tone deaf" on communicating AI risks but also expressed confidence companies could self-correct.

Shane Legg (Google DeepMind) warned: "We can't let capabilities get ahead of safety."

OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google have reportedly been developing a private self-regulatory standards body — raising "regulatory capture" concerns, as dominant firms could use voluntary standards to disadvantage smaller competitors.

Aidan Gomez (Cohere CEO) accused major labs of forming a "cartel," writing that the real dispute is "over who writes them, who gets to participate and whose interests the rules are protecting."

China's government accused Amodei of "fearmongering," with state media calling his rhetoric a "Cold War playbook."

The Trump administration has shown little interest in federal AI regulation, with AI czar David Sacks favoring industry self-governance. Speaker Mike Johnson dismissed catastrophic AI concerns dismissively.

Amodei acknowledged his proposals would benefit American firms competitively, writing that proper execution could "widen America's lead significantly over the next 3–5 years."
