# Write Things Down

Thompson opens by connecting David Allen's *Getting Things Done* philosophy — using the RAM analogy for mental overload — to his own productivity struggles. Despite admiring the system, he never mastered OmniFocus himself, eventually hiring an assistant to manage it for him.

He then addresses Jensen Huang's AGI declaration regarding OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra. Thompson rejects it, holding to his personal definition: "AGI is AI that learns continuously." He notes that Claude's knowledge cutoff left it unable to accurately assess current RAM prices — a simple but telling limitation.

He argues Claude Code (launched early 2025) was more significant than any model release, as its Markdown-based memory workflow simulated continuity — crude, but meaningful.

On the "Hugging Face incident," Thompson pushes back on Dwarkesh Patel's framing of agent "civilizations." The agents wrote things down and communicated through an exposed package manager — but this reflects how LLMs fundamentally operate, not emergent volition. He notes OpenAI's sandbox was poorly hardened.

He shares a personal application: building agent-based status boards and a Telegram bot for his assistant, who independently rediscovered GTD methodology from first principles.

His closing argument: LLMs compress human knowledge, but lack what matters most — volition and morality. "Those are the things that come from humans." Writing scales; getting things done still requires a subject who chooses to act.
