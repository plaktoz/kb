# Salsa Dancing Linked To Reduced Depressive Symptoms In Young Adults

An eight-week salsa programme was associated with significant reductions in depressive symptoms and social anxiety among young adults, suggesting social dance could offer an accessible addition to existing mental health support.

The study involved 121 people aged 18 to 24 experiencing mild to moderately severe depressive symptoms. Participants were randomly assigned either to an eight-week salsa programme or to a waitlist control group, with mental health assessed before, during and after the programme.

Young adults who took part in the salsa classes experienced a significantly greater reduction in depressive symptoms than those in the waitlist group. By the end of the study, the difference between the two groups was equivalent to a 2.45-point reduction on the PHQ-9 depression scale, exceeding the clinically meaningful threshold. Participants who took part in salsa also experienced greater reductions in social anxiety and a larger increase in daily happiness.

Salsa combines physical activity, music, social interaction and structure, which could make mental health support feel more enjoyable and accessible than conventional interventions.
