# Obesity Treatment Should Focus on Better Health, Not Weight Loss Alone

Fact-checked by Jill Seladi-Schulman, Ph.D.

## Summary

The Endocrine Society has published a scientific statement arguing that obesity treatment success should be measured by improvements in heart, metabolic, liver, sleep, mental, and physical health — not solely by the number on the scale. The statement also addresses newer medications like semaglutide and tirzepatide, highlighting their promise while flagging unresolved questions about long-term safety and optimal use.

## Background

Obesity is a chronic condition that raises the risk of type 2 diabetes, hypertension, coronary heart disease, stroke, and certain cancers. Over 40% of U.S. adults are estimated to live with obesity, though expanded definitions incorporating waist measurements and fat distribution push that figure closer to 70%.

## Key Findings

The Endocrine Society's statement, published in *Endocrine Reviews*, contends that treatment goals should center on whether interventions reduce obesity-related complications and sustain health improvements over time.

GLP-1 pathway medications — semaglutide and tirzepatide — have significantly altered the treatment landscape. Researchers are examining their effects on cardiovascular disease, kidney function, liver health, sleep apnea, cancer, arthritis, and infertility.

Lead author Dr. Daniel Drucker of Mount Sinai Hospital explained that for some conditions like sleep apnea and osteoarthritis, weight loss correlates directly with improvement. For others — including heart attacks, stroke, and diabetic kidney disease — "there are contributions from both weight loss as well as weight loss-independent mechanisms."

He emphasized that success is patient-specific: "Every patient is different... a personalized treatment plan can be formulated, which will include, but not be limited, to a specific weight loss goal."

## Discontinuation Concerns

Roughly half of people taking GLP-1 medications stop within 12 months. Discontinuation can lead to rapid weight regain, along with worsening blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar. Researchers are investigating whether lower maintenance doses or transitional medications could help preserve health gains.

## Six Priority Research Areas

The statement identifies six areas for future study:

1. How the body regulates and stores fat
2. Why obesity develops differently across individuals
3. Redefining treatment targets beyond weight loss
4. How the body responds to anti-obesity medications over time
5. Which health outcomes improve with these medications
6. Long-term safety of newer obesity treatments

## Conclusion

The emerging focus is on personalized, outcome-based care. As Drucker noted, a patient with a BMI of 28 and recent heart attack may benefit from GLP-1 therapy without needing 15–20% weight loss, while someone with a BMI of 37 and sleep apnea "may indeed need to target at least 10 to 15% weight loss" to see meaningful health improvement.
