# Study Smarter, Not Harder: Productivity Tips That Actually Work

Research shows students retain information more effectively with evidence-based study techniques (retrieval practice, spaced learning) than with passive review or last-minute cramming.

**Active recall**: Rereading and highlighting feel productive but are less effective than closing the book and retrieving information from memory via flashcards, practice questions, or verbal summaries. Per Harvard's Derek Bok Center for Teaching and Learning, active learning strategies improve student performance and exam outcomes.

**Spaced repetition**: Psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus's "forgetting curve" shows people rapidly forget new information unless they revisit it over time. Reviewing a topic at increasing intervals — per Harvard Graduate School of Education research — strengthens long-term memory by reinforcing information just as it starts to fade.

**Time boxing (Pomodoro Technique)**: Focus fades after 30–40 minutes of continuous study. Structured sessions — 25 minutes of study, 5-minute break, repeat, with a longer break after four cycles — maintain concentration, reduce burnout, and make large assignments feel more manageable.

**Controlling the digital environment**: Per the American Psychological Association, constantly switching between studying and checking messages/social media fragments attention and increases stress. Silencing notifications, using website blockers, or removing the phone from the room helps sustain focus.

The article closes by noting that HBCUs have a long history of pairing these study techniques with mentorship, tutoring, and academic-success programs, and that UNCF helps remove barriers that distract students from their education.
