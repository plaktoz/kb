# Dollar falls as traders reduce Fed rate hike bets; sterling slips after GDP data

The U.S. dollar slipped on Thursday as Federal Reserve rate hike expectations took another hit from softer-than-expected producer inflation data. The U.S. dollar index was down 0.1% to 99.94, on track to snap a three-day win streak.

## Annual producer inflation moderates, echoing consumer prices data

A day after an in-line July consumer inflation report, investors focused on producer price readings for the same period. According to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the headline July producer price index (PPI) was flat month-over-month, while ticking up 4.7% year-over-year — softer than economists' expectations. July core PPI added 0.2% M/M and 4.2% Y/Y, against estimates of 0.3% and 4.2% respectively.

With both CPI and PPI now moderated on a year-over-year basis, currency market participants reacted by reducing expectations for Fed rate hikes. According to the CME FedWatch tool, the odds of a September rate hike slipped to about 34% after the PPI release, while the odds of the Fed holding rates steady rose to nearly 66%.

Rate-sensitive assets reacted accordingly, with U.S. Treasury yields falling as bonds were snapped up. The benchmark 10-year yield fell 6.1 basis points to 4.631%, while the 2-year yield slipped 5.4 basis points to 4.145%. Technology stocks got a lift, with the S&P 500 tech sector last up 1.1%.

"Softening price pressures have quelled rate-hike fears in the past two sessions, with Fed watchers yesterday delaying the projected start of the central bank's tightening from September to October and then extending it today to December," said José Torres, senior economist at Interactive Brokers.

Separately, initial claims for state unemployment benefits rose 9,000 to a seasonally adjusted 209,000 for the week ended August 8, above the 202,000 economists had expected.

## Yen and sterling

The Japanese yen was marginally weaker against the dollar, with USD/JPY last up 0.1% to 159.52, continuing to eat into gains made after a joint intervention by Washington and Tokyo at the end of the prior month. Sterling shed 0.1% to $1.3485 despite UK GDP data showing growth returning in June (+0.4% Q/Q), taking the annualized growth rate for the first half of the year to 2%.
