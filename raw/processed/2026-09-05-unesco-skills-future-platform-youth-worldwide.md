# UNESCO launches Skills for the Future Platform to empower youth worldwide

This year's World Youth Skills Day theme, "Youth empowerment through AI and digital skills," underscores the critical role of technology in shaping the future of work.

## A growing crisis: millions of youth left behind

The global skills crisis is deepening as technological change accelerates:

- **450 million young people worldwide** lack adequate skills to succeed in the labour market
- 1 in 5 individuals aged 15–34 remain disengaged from formal pathways to skill development
- In low-income countries, **90% of girls and young women remain offline**
- Only **22% of AI professionals globally are women**
- Nearly **40% of today's skillsets no longer match job market needs**
- **22% of jobs are expected to transform by 2030** due to technological disruption (World Economic Forum)
- Addressing the skills divide could unlock **US$6.5 trillion in economic value** over the next seven years (World Bank)

## The Skills for the Future Platform

The new platform allows private sector partners and civil society organizations to showcase their skills initiatives and join a global community building youth skills for improved employability, economic empowerment and resilience.

Key features:
- **Skills for the Future Initiatives dashboard**: leverages existing CSR and ESG reports to simplify data sharing, highlights partner contributions, and signals collaboration opportunities
- Helps ensure no young person is left behind through coordinated action
- Partners include KPMG and other Global Skills Academy members

The Global Skills Academy celebrates its 5th anniversary in 2026 — it has supported **1.2 million learners** with training in digital and green skills across 230 TVET institutions in 150 countries.

Free online courses cover: AI, coding, cybersecurity, entrepreneurship, and career development.

## New Global Skills Tracker

UNESCO launched the **Global Skills Tracker** (unevoc.unesco.org) providing insights into the skills landscape across countries, industries and occupations. Features:
- Identifies trends in skills demand
- Interactive dashboards for exploring skills data
- Supports strategic decision-making in skills development
- Currently covers: Argentina, Chile, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Idaho/USA, Jordan, Kenya, Malaysia, Nigeria and the Philippines

## Strategic direction

UNESCO is expanding partnerships, introducing new credentials aligned with labour market demands, and fostering an inclusive ecosystem that overcomes barriers of geography, gender, and socioeconomic status. The platform directly addresses the structural mismatch between what education systems produce and what AI-era labour markets demand.
