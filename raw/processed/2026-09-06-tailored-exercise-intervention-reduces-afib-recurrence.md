# 1-Year Tailored Exercise Program Reduces AFib Recurrence By Nearly 50%

A one-year individualized exercise intervention significantly reduced atrial fibrillation (AFib) burden — the proportion of time spent in abnormal heart rhythm — according to findings from the NEXAF trial, presented at the European Society of Cardiology Congress 2026 in Munich.

## Key Findings

- Exercise group spent 3.9% of time in AFib vs. 7.1% for usual care — a 45% relative reduction
- 37% fewer total hospitalizations in the exercise group
- 46% fewer AFib-related hospitalizations
- Improved cardiorespiratory fitness and resting heart rate
- Quality-of-life scores improved in both groups, but the difference was not statistically significant

## Trial Design

295 participants (average age 64, 30% women) from three Norwegian centers were randomized to either usual care or the exercise program. The intervention began with 8 supervised high-intensity sessions over 4–6 weeks, then shifted to home-based exercise monitored via smartwatch, app, and web platform. All participants received an insertable cardiac monitor tracking continuous heart rhythm data.

The research was led by Dr. Bjarne Nes of the Norwegian University of Science and Technology and has not yet been peer-reviewed.

## Expert Commentary

Cardiologist Rod Passman (Northwestern University) noted the study "adds more evidence to what we already believe to be true," while calling for replication across more diverse populations.

Rigved Tadwalkar, MD, called having "hard data proving a 45 percent relative drop in actual atrial fibrillation burden" a significant development, suggesting exercise may now qualify as "a primary medical treatment."

Paul Drury, MD, emphasized that individualized plans and medical oversight are essential, since exercise episodes could discourage patients from continuing without proper reassurance.

## Recommendations for AFib Patients

Experts suggest those with AFib who want to begin exercising should:
1. Consult a cardiologist first
2. Start with supervised sessions
3. Follow an individualized plan
4. Transition to independent exercise with heart monitoring

Notably, experts emphasized that these findings do not suggest exercise should replace medication.
