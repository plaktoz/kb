# The Fed Hiked Interest Rates for the First Time in 3 Years. Are AI Stocks in Trouble?

The Federal Reserve raised benchmark interest rates Wednesday — its first hike since 2023 — driven by higher-than-expected August inflation. The move increases borrowing costs broadly, but AI stocks face particular exposure given their capital-intensive infrastructure demands.

Morgan Stanley warned that the AI sector is entering "a more mature phase," shifting focus from excitement to profitability and monetization. Their key takeaway: investors should favor AI companies already generating profits and positive cash flows, as these firms rely less on capital markets when borrowing costs rise.

SpaceX's June IPO raised over $85 billion, with OpenAI and Anthropic reportedly pursuing their own offerings. SpaceX also completed a $25 billion bond offering that same month — underscoring how debt financing remains critical to AI infrastructure scaling.

A second rate hike in 2026 is considered likely by most FOMC members. Analysts disagree on duration: Mackenzie Investments expects "more rather than less" hikes into 2027, while Goldman Sachs suggests the Fed doesn't envision an "aggressive tightening cycle."

The bottom line: AI companies able to self-fund capital expenditures hold a competitive edge regardless of how high rates climb.
