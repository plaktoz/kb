# HIV: Once-Weekly Pill As Effective As Daily Treatment, Global Study Finds

A phase 3 international clinical trial (ISLEND-1) found that a once-weekly oral HIV pill combining islatravir and lenacapavir performed as well as the widely used daily three-drug regimen Biktarvy over 48 weeks.

## Key Findings

- Over 93% of participants in both groups maintained viral suppression throughout the study
- Zero participants on the weekly pill had a viral load ≥50 copies/mL at week 48
- No virological failures occurred in either group
- Medication adherence averaged ~99% (weekly) vs. ~96% (daily)
- Adverse events were comparable between groups, mostly mild-to-moderate

## Trial Details

- 607 adults across 106 sites in 12 countries
- All participants had maintained viral suppression for ≥6 months prior on Biktarvy
- Funded by Gilead Sciences and Merck Sharp & Dohme
- Published in *The New England Journal of Medicine* (July 29, 2026)

## Expert Perspectives

Johns Hopkins professor Charles Flexner described the trial as demonstrating "very convincingly" that the two-drug weekly pill matched the effectiveness of a standard daily regimen.

University of Alabama's Dr. Michael Saag noted that longer-term safety monitoring remains important, though both drugs individually carry established safety records from prior trials.

Senior study author Dr. Chloe Orkin emphasized that expanding options may help address stigma and improve adherence, noting that "offering more choices could help people to take their treatment better."

## Context and Limitations

A weekly regimen reduces annual doses from 365 to 52, potentially easing "pill fatigue" — the psychological burden of long-term daily medication. However, a key limitation is that participants were already highly adherent and virally suppressed, meaning results may not generalize to those who struggle with daily dosing — precisely the population most likely to benefit. Additionally, the weekly pill lacks hepatitis B activity, unlike Biktarvy; one unvaccinated participant on the weekly pill developed hepatitis B during the study.

96-week follow-up data are forthcoming.
