# Survivorship Bias: The Tale of Forgotten Failures

Survivorship bias is a logical error where we overweight success stories while ignoring the far more numerous failures, distorting our sense of probability and cause-and-effect.

## Core Problem

We see Bill Gates, the Beatles, and similar outliers and mistake exceptions for rules. As Kahneman notes, "A stupid decision that works out well becomes a brilliant decision in hindsight."

Business startup statistics routinely exclude collapsed companies, inflating apparent success rates. Learning only from winners obscures how much timing, luck, and background contribute to outcomes.

## The Wald Example

WWII statistician Abraham Wald recognized that studying returning damaged planes was misleading — the planes that didn't return held the most critical information. This illustrates the core fix: consider what you don't see.

## Key Takeaways

- Base rates matter; outliers are not templates.
- Failure narratives are as instructive as success stories.
- Survivorship bias isn't an excuse for inaction — it's a tool for realistic decision-making.
