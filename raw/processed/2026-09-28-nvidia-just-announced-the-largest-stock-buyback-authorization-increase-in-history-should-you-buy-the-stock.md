# Nvidia Just Announced the Largest Stock Buyback Authorization Increase in History. Should You Buy the Stock?

source_url: https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/09/28/nvidia-just-announced-the-largest-stock-buyback-authorization-increase-in-history-should-you-buy-the-stock/

By Bram Berkowitz | Sep 28, 2026

---

Nvidia recently completed close to $20 billion in stock repurchases within a single quarter, and the company signals even larger buybacks are ahead. On September 28, the board approved adding $150 billion to the buyback program, pushing the total remaining authorization up to $235 billion — described in the article as "the largest increase to a share repurchase authorization plan in history." Nvidia stated it plans to deploy this full amount by fiscal year 2028, and shares rose about 3% in after-hours trading following the news.

That $235 billion figure represents roughly 4.3% of Nvidia's market value, and with six quarters left before the FY2028 deadline, the company could theoretically buy back over $39 billion worth of stock each quarter.

CEO Jensen Huang connected the buyback to Nvidia's broader AI positioning, saying the company's financial strength lets it fund innovation while also rewarding shareholders, and calling the authorization a reflection of confidence in the years ahead.

Despite strong performance — the stock is up nearly 25% this year — the piece notes investor sentiment remains guarded. Reasons cited include the company's already massive $5.6 trillion valuation, worries that AI-related data center spending could eventually slow, and skepticism around Nvidia's financing arrangements with suppliers and customers, which some critics view as circular in nature. The article also points to potential gross margin pressure, referencing CFO Colette Kress's guidance that margins could dip from about 75% toward the 71%–72% range by fiscal Q4 2027, largely tied to rising memory costs.

The author concludes that Nvidia's roughly 70% projected revenue growth for fiscal 2028 and a forward earnings multiple near 24 make the stock reasonably valued given its role in AI infrastructure. Still, he emphasizes that unpredictable factors — including the pace of AI capital spending and uncertainty about how mature current AI technology really is — introduce real risk. He suggests that more conservative investors might prefer broad exposure through the S&P 500, where Nvidia already makes up close to 7.9% of the index.
