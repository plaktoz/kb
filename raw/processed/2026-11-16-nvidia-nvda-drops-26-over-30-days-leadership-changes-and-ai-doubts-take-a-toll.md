# NVIDIA (NVDA) Drops -26% Over 30 Days: Leadership Changes and AI Doubts Take a Toll

## Key Takeaways

NVIDIA shares declined roughly 26% over the 30 days ending mid-November, falling from a close near $221 to $162.85.

The drop left the stock about 30% below its October 6 record intraday high of $243.37 and at a 52-week low.

The selloff was driven by the resignation of co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang, renewed doubts about AI spending, and hotter-than-expected inflation.

Over the last quarter, the stock lost roughly 28% as the broader AI trade cooled from its early-October peak.

Wall Street responded with downgrades, including cuts from Bernstein and Baird, while Morgan Stanley removed the stock from its top picks list.

Investors now turn to NVIDIA's quarterly results, scheduled for November 17.

## NVIDIA (NVDA) Company Overview and Market Position

NVIDIA Corporation designs graphics processing units (GPUs) and accelerated-computing platforms that power artificial intelligence, data centers, gaming, and professional visualization. Its data center segment — driven by AI accelerators and the CUDA software ecosystem — has become the company's growth engine, accounting for roughly 92% of revenue in its most recent reported quarter. NVIDIA's leadership in AI training and inference, deep relationships with hyperscale cloud providers, and its software moat make the stock a bellwether for the entire semiconductor and AI infrastructure trade, which is why investors track it so closely.

## NVIDIA (NVDA) Stock Price Performance: Last 30 Days vs. Quarter

Over the 30 days ending mid-November, NVIDIA fell about 26%, from a close near $221 on October 14 to $162.85 on November 13. The decline accelerated sharply in November: the stock dropped more than 9% on November 5, shed roughly $470 billion in market value across two sessions in the second week of the month, and closed the period more than 30% below its October 6 record high of $243.37.

The last quarter tells a similar story. From around $225 in mid-August, the stock slipped roughly 28% through mid-November. After trading in a $210–$230 range through most of August and September and peaking in early October, NVIDIA entered a sustained downtrend as AI-related equities broadly repriced.

## What Drove NVDA Stock Price in the Last 30 Days

Several overlapping catalysts hit NVIDIA within a short window. On November 5, shares tumbled roughly 9% after Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress, raising concerns that a more skeptical political environment could weigh on AI data center construction, while weak outlooks from Qualcomm (QCOM) and Arm (ARM) renewed doubts about the AI boom.

The most direct shock was leadership-related. Reports emerged that co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang had a relationship with a Chinese businesswoman suspected of acting on behalf of Chinese intelligence, followed by reports of a second relationship with a company executive. The board launched an independent review, and Huang resigned on November 12, with CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO. The stock fell 4.3% that day after dropping as much as 14.3% intraday.

Macro pressures compounded the move. An October consumer inflation report came in above expectations, lifting the odds that the Federal Reserve would raise rates again in December and pressuring richly valued growth stocks. Analysts also weighed in: Bernstein cut NVIDIA to market perform from outperform, Baird downgraded it to neutral, and Morgan Stanley removed the stock from its top picks list. From what I see, this combination of events created a perfect storm for the shares.

## What Drove NVDA Stock Performance Over the Last Quarter

The quarterly decline reflects a broader cooling in the AI trade rather than a single event. After a powerful multi-year rally, investors spent the fall questioning whether surging AI capital expenditure would translate into sustained profitability, particularly as financing costs rose and debt-heavy AI infrastructure companies came under pressure. NVIDIA's own fundamentals remained strong — revenue grew more than 100% year over year in its latest reported quarter — but valuation multiples compressed sharply, and the leadership crisis amplified an already cautious mood across the sector.

## NVDA Stock Forecast Drivers: What Investors Should Watch Next

NVIDIA's next quarterly report, scheduled for November 17, is the most immediate catalyst. Investors will focus on data center revenue growth, gross margins, guidance, and any commentary from the new leadership on strategy and customer relationships. Beyond earnings, watch inflation data and Federal Reserve policy signals, hyperscaler capital expenditure plans, export-control and regulatory developments, and the search for a permanent CEO. Competitive pressure from AMD (AMD) and Broadcom (AVGO) also remains a factor. These inputs will shape sentiment around NVIDIA and the broader AI trade in the weeks ahead.
