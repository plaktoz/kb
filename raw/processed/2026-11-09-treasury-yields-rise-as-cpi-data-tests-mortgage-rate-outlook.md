# Treasury yields rise as CPI data tests mortgage rate outlook

A blowout October jobs report pushed bond markets higher; fresh inflation data could extend the pressure on home loan costs

US Treasury yields climbed Monday as bond markets absorbed a stronger-than-expected October jobs report and shifted attention to inflation data that could shape the mortgage rate trajectory heading into year-end.

The 10-year note — the primary pricing benchmark for home loans — rose more than 2 basis points to 4.884%, while the 30-year bond edged up over 1 basis point to 5.273%.

For mortgage brokers already navigating a demanding rate environment, Tuesday's consumer price index (CPI) release is the defining data point of the week.

## What the inflation data means for the mortgage market

Consensus forecasts compiled by Trading Economics point to October's annual CPI rising to 3.9%, up from 3.7% in September.

Core CPI, which strips out food and energy prices to give a cleaner reading of underlying inflation, is projected at 2.8%, compared with 2.6% the prior month.

A print at or above those levels would reinforce the Federal Reserve's posture of holding interest rates steady and add further pressure to yields that have already pushed home loan costs toward multi-year highs.

A producer price index (PPI) report, an early directional read on consumer inflation, is due later in the week, giving markets a second signal before the monthly close.

## Jobs strength complicates the rate picture

Friday's non-farm payrolls report from the Bureau of Labor Statistics set the stage for Monday's moves.

The US economy added 165,000 jobs in October, nearly three times the 59,000 economists had projected, while the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%.

That resilience pushed the 2-year Treasury note, which tracks short-term Federal Reserve policy expectations most closely, up more than 4 basis points to 4.57%, as markets priced out near-term rate cut expectations.

A hot jobs number reduces the probability of any Fed pivot before year-end, keeping the spread between the 10-year yield and the 30-year fixed mortgage rate elevated.

That spread, often running 2 percentage points above the 10-year benchmark, has been among the widest seen in recent years, compounding the affordability pressure that has cooled origination volumes across the US market.

Mike Fratantoni of the MBA, Seema Shah of Principal Asset Management, Eric Orenstein of Fitch Ratings, and Barry Habib of MBS Highway say robust employment data is reinforcing expectations for tighter monetary policy heading into 2027.

Oil offered partial relief. West Texas Intermediate futures for December delivery fell more than 3% to $87 per barrel after President Donald Trump said new Iran attacks were "highly unlikely."

But the upward pressure on longer-term Treasury yields throughout 2026 has shown that geopolitical tailwinds alone rarely shift the bond market's underlying direction.

Broker Shadi Nurani captured the structural concern when speaking with Mortgage Professional America about the inflation risk embedded in today's rate environment: "As far as its impact on the market, what people don't realize is war is inflationary. You have spikes in oil prices… the likelihood of the Fed needing to print money goes through the roof."
