# Microsoft (MSFT) Exits UK Data Center Deal Ahead of Nscale’s $3B IPO

Microsoft Corporation (MSFT) has reportedly pulled out of a planned UK data center deal with British AI infrastructure startup **Nscale**, according to the *Telegraph*. The withdrawal comes as Nscale prepares for a potential $3 billion initial public offering (IPO), raising fresh concerns about delays and the growing cost of AI infrastructure projects.

Microsoft was expected to be the main customer at Nscale’s first British data center in Loughton, Essex. However, the project has faced repeated delays, with local authorities reportedly warning that the site may not receive a power supply until the 2030s. The exact reason for Microsoft’s withdrawal was not disclosed.

The problems extend beyond the UK. Nscale’s flagship facility in Norway is running six months behind schedule, with just 6.4 megawatts of its planned 30-megawatt capacity currently operating.

On Friday, MSFT stock lost 1.93%, or $10.39, to close at $529.05.

## **Nscale’s IPO Faces Growing Pressure**

The timing is particularly difficult for Nscale, which is reportedly seeking to raise as much as $3 billion in an IPO as soon as October 2026.

According to its IPO filing, Microsoft and Anthropic account for around 85% of Nscale’s contracts. That customer concentration makes the loss of a planned Microsoft project especially significant, although the financial value of the Essex deal has not been disclosed.

Nscale told the *Telegraph* that its relationship with Microsoft remains strong and that the Essex power issues will not affect its other contracts. The company is also exploring alternative power sources and additional UK locations.

The news comes during a difficult period for AI infrastructure companies. CoreWeave (CRWV), another major AI cloud provider, saw its shares fall 25% last week, and nearly 12% on Friday, as investors grew more concerned about the debt needed to fund massive data center projects.

Australian data center developer **Firmus** also recently abandoned its IPO after failing to attract enough investor demand at its targeted valuation.

For Nscale, the immediate question is whether it can replace Microsoft as the Essex site’s main customer or secure a workable power solution. With its IPO potentially just weeks away, investors will be watching closely for updated project timelines and any changes to its expected contract revenue.

## **Is Microsoft Stock a Sell, Buy, or Hold?**

On the Street, Microsoft Corporation has a Strong Buy consensus, based on 37 analysts’ ratings issued in the past three months. The average MSFT stock price target stands at $602.72, implying about 14% upside from the current share price.
