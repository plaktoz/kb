# Stoke Space raises another billion to rival SpaceX at re-flying rockets

Stoke Space Technologies has closed the initial portion of a $1B Series E round, bringing its total funding to $2.3 billion. The round was led by Point72 Ventures and Spark Capital, with participation from General Innovation, Glade Brook Capital, Y Combinator, and others.

CEO Andy Lapsa described the round's purpose as being focused on scaling production, flight frequency, and funding a second-generation vehicle.

The company's Nova Pathfinder rocket is distinguished by its goal of full reusability — both the booster and second stage return to Earth — something never previously achieved. Its novel thermal protection system flows super-cooled liquid hydrogen through the heat shield rather than relying on passive materials, a solution Lapsa says has been extensively ground-tested.

First flight is targeted for early 2027. The Pathfinder can carry 3 metric tons to low-Earth orbit. A larger follow-on rocket, the Nova Block 2, capable of carrying 15 metric tons, is planned for 2029 — timed to coincide with the anticipated retirement of SpaceX's Falcon 9. Stoke has no plans to operate its own space assets, positioning itself as a pure launch provider for third-party customers.
