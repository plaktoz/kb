# A Lesson From a Mentor I'll Never Forget

Bloom recounts meeting Tim Cook in 2014 at a 4:45am gym session without knowing who he was. Cook had "zero pretension" and was simply part of the early-morning crowd.

Even as Cook's stature grew — presidents calling, pundits praising — he kept the same routine and continued mentoring Bloom despite Bloom having "little tangible value to offer in return."

The lesson Bloom draws centers on a Zen principle: *"What do you do before enlightenment? Chop wood, carry water. What do you after enlightenment? Chop wood, carry water."*

Success, Bloom argues, doesn't exempt anyone from fundamentals. Titles, outcomes, and influence may shift, but the underlying discipline — showing up consistently, doing unglamorous work well, staying grounded in core values — remains constant.

Cook embodied this not through words but through daily action. That consistency, Bloom writes, is the lesson he'll carry forward.
