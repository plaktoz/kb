# What Writers Who Use AI Want You to Know

Five professional writers share their distinct, evolving AI workflows — revealing no universal approach exists.

## Key Writers & Workflows

**Alexandra Samuel** (journalist, *Working Neurosmart*)
- Converted research into AI-generated podcasts; used ChatGPT interviews on walks to break writer's block
- Wrote 52,000 rough words in 10 days; AI served as "sounding board," never the author

**Kevin Roose** (NYT alum, *The AGI Chronicles*)
- Used agents to transcribe 150+ interviews, extract key details, suggest follow-up sources
- Built queryable "outboard brain" notebooks; estimated AI compressed 3–5 years of work into one
- Drew a firm line: AI did zero writing; human fact-checker handled final verification
- Ran chapters through a "council of Claudes" — personas modeled on AI skeptics, safety researchers, ML experts — but found ~95% of feedback was "slop"

**Maggie Appleton** (designer/anthropologist, GitHub Next)
- Built a custom spatial writing canvas (via tldraw SDK + MCP server) connecting to Claude Code and Codex
- Uses AI to surface overlooked research connections; it re-introduced her to anthropologist Lucy Suchman's relevant work
- Won't let AI write final prose: "How would an agent ever say the exact thing I'm trying to say?"

**Emilia David** (tech journalist)
- Fine-tuned a custom GPT to evaluate pitches from her target reader's perspective (senior AI engineer)
- Used it to suppress FOMO around AI announcements and surface angles she'd have dismissed
- Identified a vendor lock-in angle in an Anthropic product release she'd initially passed on

**Dan Shipper** (Every CEO)
- Actively uses AI to generate sentences, then reacts, revises, and rewrites iteratively
- Uses Codex to convert drafts into podcasts for re-immersion before writing sessions
- Views writing as "the act of taking something as complicated as reality and putting it down word by word" — AI helps him identify what he means

## Key Themes

- **Research:** Agents excel at transcription, gap-finding, and timeline organization
- **Ideation:** Custom audience personas help stress-test story ideas
- **Structure:** AI is "alarmingly bad" at subjective editorial calls (Roose); narrative arc still requires human judgment
- **Writing:** Most writers won't let AI generate final prose; Shipper is the notable exception
- **Editing/Fact-checking:** AI is a useful first pass — catching date/age inconsistencies, formatting fact-check emails — but requires human oversight

## Shared Takeaway

Writing with AI is itself a skill. The same tool that sharpens one writer's thinking can degrade another's prose. Each workflow is personal, idiosyncratic, and still evolving.
