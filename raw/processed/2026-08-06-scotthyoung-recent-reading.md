# Recent Reading

Scott Young shares thoughts on books read recently, including: *Envy: A Theory of Social Behaviour* by Helmut Schoeck, which argues envy is caustic and functional societies learn to suppress it, and that people are more envious of small differences among close associates than large gaps with distant others; *Infinite Powers* by Steven Strogatz, a history of calculus; *The Fragility of Goodness* by Martha Nussbaum, on ancient Greek tragedy and situations where no good choice exists; *Who We Are and How We Got Here* by David Reich, on ancient-DNA evidence for frequent human migration and mixture; *The Myth of Sisyphus* by Albert Camus, on finding freedom in an absurd, purposeless existence; *The Perfectionists* by Simon Winchester, a history of precision engineering; and *Catch-22* by Joseph Heller.

The most notable entry is *The Marginal Revolution* by Tyler Cowen, a book on generative AI built around the concept of marginalism — the idea that the right way to analyze a decision is to ask what one more unit costs, not the average cost. The classic diamond-water paradox illustrates this: water is necessary for life yet cheap, while diamonds are mostly useless yet expensive, because price tracks marginal value (how much one more unit is worth) rather than total value. Cowen argues marginalist thinking has been enormously productive for economics but its centrality is waning as econometrics and other mathematical techniques gain favor.
