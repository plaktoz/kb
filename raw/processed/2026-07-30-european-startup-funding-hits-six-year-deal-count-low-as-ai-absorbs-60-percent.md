# European Startup Funding Hits Six-Year Deal-Count Low as AI Absorbs 60 Percent

source_url: https://www.techtimes.com/articles/322282/20260730/european-startup-funding-hits-six-year-deal-count-low-ai-absorbs-60-percent.htm

---

By Jerry Owens

Seven of the largest VC rounds in European history closed in H1 as seed deals fell 44 percent.

European technology startups raised €44.1 billion (approximately $50.6 billion USD) in the first half of 2026 — a 27% rebound from the prior year — but the number of companies actually receiving investment fell to its lowest point in six years, according to the Tech.eu H1 2026 European Tech Ecosystem Report. Beneath the headline recovery, venture capital is concentrating into a shrinking number of deals at the top of the market while seed and early-stage funding collapses, and analysts warn the current deal-count drought is already creating a structural shortfall in fundable companies for 2027 and 2028.

The bifurcation is not incidental. Artificial intelligence absorbed 60.3% of all European venture deal value in H1 2026, according to the PitchBook Q2 2026 European Venture Report, up from 37.9% for the full year of 2025 — meaning AI crossed an absolute majority of European deal value for the first time in a six-month reporting period. LP capital is concentrating into established AI-focused funds, which grow in size, which forces minimum viable check sizes upward, which removes the capital that would otherwise reach sub-€1 million seed deals.

## Deal Count Hits Lowest Point Since 2020

In H1 2024, Europe hit a funding peak of €50.1 billion across roughly 2,000 deals. H1 2025 saw capital fall more than 30% year-over-year while deal count held broadly steady. H1 2026 marks a third, distinct phase: €44.1 billion recovered — not yet at the 2024 peak — while deal count declined further to just over 1,740, the lowest half-year total since 2020.

Mega-rounds — financings above €100 million — accounted for more than half of all H1 deal value, up from roughly 37% across the full year of 2025. Seven of the largest venture capital deals in European history were recorded in H1 2026. Six of the ten biggest transactions each exceeded €1 billion.

## AI Claims Majority of European Venture Capital for First Time

AI attracted €5.9 billion as the single largest sector by funding in the Tech.eu vertical breakdown, while PitchBook's broader analysis of AI-linked activity across all sectors puts the total at €26.5 billion for the half — meaning AI-related companies already surpassed the full-year 2025 AI total before the calendar year was even halfway through.

Fintech was the second-largest vertical by funding at €4.7 billion, followed by healthtech at €4.3 billion. Software remained the most active sector by deal count, with 338 transactions.

## What the Round Table Looks Like at the Top

London-based AI infrastructure company Nscale raised €2.9 billion in H1 2026, the single largest deal in the European venture market. Isomorphic Labs, the Alphabet-backed AI drug design company, closed a $2.1 billion Series B backed by Thrive Capital, GV, and Alphabet. German humanoid robotics developer Neura Robotics announced a Series C of up to $1.4 billion — the largest funding round ever recorded for a full-stack robotics company based in the EU, bringing together Bosch, Schaeffler, Amazon, NVIDIA, and Qualcomm as investors. Autonomous driving company Wayve raised $1.2 billion in a Series D, while UK-based Recursive Superintelligence emerged from stealth with over $650 million raised at a valuation of $4.65 billion.

The UK dominated the H1 2026 ranking, claiming six of the ten largest deals. UK companies raised €18.7 billion across 423 deals — more than three times Germany's total (€6.3 billion), while France followed with €6.0 billion from 132 deals.

## Why Is Seed Funding Drying Up in Europe?

Seed-stage deal counts fell 44% year-over-year in Q1 2026, while early-stage (Series A) volumes dropped 30%, according to GoHub Ventures. Late-stage deal volume held broadly steady. As LP capital concentrates into large, established venture funds — whose minimum viable investment size is measured in tens of millions rather than hundreds of thousands of euros — the natural suppliers of seed checks effectively disappear from the market. The median European VC fund size has risen to €60 million from €50 million in 2025, according to PitchBook.

"The 44% decline in European seed deal count in Q1 is the most quietly important data point on this side of the Atlantic," GoHub Ventures noted. "Fewer companies funded at seed today means a thinner pipeline reaching growth stage in 2027-2028."

Founders seeking seed capital in 2026 are expected to demonstrate product-market fit, paying customers, and a credible path to unit economics — criteria more commonly applied at Series A until recently. Series A rounds now raise a median of around $14 million, up from $8 million to $10 million a few years ago.

## The Investor Landscape: Fewer Funds, Larger Vehicles

More than 6,410 investors participated in European tech deals in H1 2026. European VC fundraising reached €8.2 billion in H1, pacing 32.8% ahead of the same period last year, yet fund count continues to fall. The largest single fund close was Jeito Capital's $1.2 billion second fund. One counterweight: emerging managers accounted for 52.8% of capital raised in H1, growing faster as a cohort than established firms.

## What the Exit Market Tells Founders

More than 150 venture-backed European companies were acquired in Q2 for a combined value of more than $11.5 billion, including Cognite's announced acquisition by Schneider Electric and VOI Technology's acquisition by Uber. IPO activity for European startups remains muted, creating a backlog of mature companies and an open question about whether late-stage private valuations will hold when tested by public markets.

The deeper structural question is whether Europe's innovation ecosystem can sustain the current bifurcation. With deal counts at their lowest since 2020 and seed stage down 44%, the pressure on the early-stage pipeline is not a warning sign to watch — it is already the condition on the ground.
