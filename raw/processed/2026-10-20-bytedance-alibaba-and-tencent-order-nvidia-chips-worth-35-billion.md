# ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent order Nvidia chips worth $35 billion

ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent ordered over $35 billion of Nvidia chips at list price

Nvidia has told customers it cannot guarantee full delivery of the RTX PRO 5500 cards as demand exceeds planned production

ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent have placed orders for more than 3 million RTX PRO 5500 graphics cards from Nvidia $NVDA, carrying a combined list price above $35 billion, according to Reuters. The chip is a less powerful design that complies with U.S. export controls on China.

The orders cover cards carrying a price of roughly $11,400 apiece, according to Reuters. Nvidia has warned customers and partners that it cannot promise full delivery, since Chinese tech companies want more chips than it had planned to make, Reuters said.

Nvidia warned partners that their allocations could be as little as 5% to 10% of what they asked for, according to Reuters. Nvidia also signaled that its output of RTX PRO 5500 cards in 2027 would top out at 3 million across all markets, while total orders from Chinese customers have exceeded 6 million, according to Reuters.

The RTX PRO 5500 is a workstation product aimed at professional PCs, not at large data-center clusters, according to Reuters. It is less powerful than Nvidia's H200 and B300 chips. U.S. export controls prohibit Chinese buyers from purchasing the Blackwell-based B300, and Hopper-based H200 shipments are approved only after individual review and under licensing terms, Reuters said.

The Chinese companies intend the cards chiefly for AI inference, the stage in which a finished model is put to work answering chatbot queries, according to Reuters.

Nvidia is expected to redirect a large share of its production slots at Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. to Rubin, its upcoming data-center platform, beginning in 2027, according to Reuters. Nvidia has advised customers that the first RTX PRO 5500 samples should arrive in December and that commercial shipments should start during the opening quarter of 2027, Reuters said.

The RTX PRO 5500 performs similarly to the RTX 6000D, according to Reuters. Nvidia launched the RTX 6000D in mid-2025 as a pared-back offering, but leading Chinese tech companies were largely uninterested, and a few passed on buying it altogether, Reuters said. That chip is no longer made, and it now sells for twice as much in China as it did before, according to Reuters.

Shenzhen-listed Tansun Technology said in an Oct. 13 filing that it had contracted to purchase 20,000 RTX PRO 5500 cards, though it did not name the seller, according to Reuters. The company warned that deliveries could be delayed or volumes cut because of supply shortages, industry demand and overseas technology controls.

An Nvidia spokesperson told Reuters that Chinese makers of chips and cards for workstations have enjoyed "unprecedented growth since 2022," the year the restrictions began. "U.S. firms continue to be restricted by a combination of outdated U.S. export controls, which cover gaming products released nearly a half a decade ago, and China's own limit on U.S. imports," the spokesperson said.

ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent did not respond to Reuters' requests for comment.
