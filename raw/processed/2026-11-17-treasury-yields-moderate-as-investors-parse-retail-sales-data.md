# Treasury yields moderate as investors parse retail sales data

U.S. Treasury yields gave up their gains Tuesday as investors digested key economic data from the federal government and comments from Federal Reserve officials.

The benchmark 10-year Treasury yield slipped 1 basis point to 5.109%, while the long-dated 30-year Treasury bond was trading relatively flat at 5.46%. Meanwhile, the 2-year Treasury note yield inched down 2 basis points to 4.806%.

The yields on all three Treasurys were up earlier in the session.

One basis point is equal to 0.01%, and yields and prices move in opposite directions.

In October, U.S. retail and food services sales rose 0.2% from the month prior to $748.2 billion, in line with expectations. That figure was up 6.88% on a year-over-year basis.

Stripping out the volatile gas and auto categories, retail sales rose 0.6% from the previous month. That's higher than Wall Street's 0.4% consensus estimate.

That stronger-than-expected reading could reinforce the case for higher Fed interest rates.

St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said Tuesday the Fed may need to hike as much as four more times, noting, "The economy will likely require several interest rate increases before the spring of 2027 to achieve 2% inflation in a timely manner."

Investors are also likely to give weight to comments from the Fed's Christopher Waller and John Williams later in the session.

The latest FOMC meeting minutes are due on Wednesday, with investors watching for insights on the Fed's October decision to leave interest rates unchanged. Traders are currently pricing in a 95% chance of a quarter-percentage-point hike in December, per the CME Group's FedWatch tool.
