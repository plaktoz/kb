# Burnout prevention starts with better benefits

There's a gap between what wellbeing support employees think they're getting and what they're actually accessing, and it's proving to be a significant challenge for employers.

Eight out of ten (82%) of UK employers believe they are supporting their employees' overall wellbeing, yet only 67% of employees feel that support, according to Epassi's Great Employee Benefits Study 2026. The issue is not a lack of investment — many organisations have introduced mental health initiatives, employee assistance programmes and wellbeing platforms. The challenge is ensuring that support translates into something employees genuinely experience and value.

## What employees say makes the biggest difference

When Epassi asked employees which initiatives were most effective in helping prevent burnout, their answers were practical. Flexible working arrangements came top, with 72% of employees identifying them as effective. In second place, cited by 48%, were benefits supporting a healthy work-life balance, including gym memberships, physical wellbeing programmes and opportunities to be active during the working day.

## Why physical wellbeing deserves a bigger role

Epassi's research found that 89% of employees with access to a fitness benefit say it positively influences their overall wellbeing. Yet only 41% of UK employees currently have access to one. Physical activity supports more than physical health — it can improve mood, reduce stress, increase energy and help employees recover during demanding periods.

## Benefits cannot solve burnout alone

Epassi's research also found that high workload and workplace stress remain the biggest barriers to employee wellbeing, cited nearly three times more frequently than any other challenge. Benefits cannot solve structural issues such as excessive workloads or poor job design, but they can provide practical support that helps employees maintain wellbeing and sustain performance over time.

Three characteristics stand out among organisations making the biggest impact:

1. Benefits that fit real lives — flexible wellbeing benefits that accommodate hybrid working, shift patterns and individual lifestyles.
2. Early intervention — preventative health support and wellbeing benefits that encourage healthy habits before challenges become serious.
3. A culture that encourages use — leaders and managers making wellbeing support feel normal, so employees access it before reaching a crisis point.

The organisations closing the wellbeing perception gap are not necessarily those offering the largest number of benefits — they are the ones ensuring their reward strategy reflects what employees genuinely value, communicates support effectively, and makes benefits easy to access.
