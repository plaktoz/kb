# These 2 Words From Fed Chair Kevin Warsh Sunk the Dow, S&P 500, and Nasdaq, and Put Wall Street on Notice

On September 16, 2026, the FOMC voted unanimously to raise the federal funds target rate by 25 basis points to 3.75%–4.00%. Despite the widely anticipated move, all three major indexes closed lower, with the Dow dropping 631 points.

The market reaction centered on Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's phrase "timelier return" — specifically his statement that the rate hike would "support a timelier return to the Committee's 2% goal." This language, paired with his earlier Jackson Hole remarks about inflation needing to fall at "sufficient speed," signaled the Fed isn't satisfied with the pace of disinflation.

The updated dot plot reinforced this hawkish posture: another 50 basis points of hikes are projected before year-end, with no cuts anticipated in 2027 and core inflation not expected back at 2% until 2029.

Warsh also noted he'd be "hard-pressed to describe broad financial conditions as restrictive," suggesting further tightening remains on the table. Analysts worry that rising borrowing costs could slow AI infrastructure investment, potentially threatening Wall Street's four-year AI-driven bull market.
