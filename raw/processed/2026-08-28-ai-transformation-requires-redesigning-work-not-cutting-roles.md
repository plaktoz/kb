# AI Transformation Requires Redesigning Work, Not Cutting Roles

Many AI-driven layoff announcements are better characterized as "AI-washing" — standard restructuring efforts reframed in AI language to signal technological awareness to investors.

Goldman Sachs research indicates AI has reduced monthly U.S. payroll growth by roughly 16,000 jobs over the past year, adding approximately 0.1 percentage points to unemployment — a figure encompassing both slower hiring and direct job cuts.

The authors argue that actual AI workforce disruption remains far more limited than dominant narratives imply, and that companies are acting faster than the available evidence supports. Early AI-driven layoffs have frequently missed their projected returns and revealed capability gaps within organizations.

The central argument: rather than eliminating roles outright, companies should focus on redesigning work to integrate AI effectively.
