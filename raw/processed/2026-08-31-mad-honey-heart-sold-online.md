# 'Mad Honey' That Can Stop Your Heart Is Now Being Sold Online Worldwide

Himalayan cliff bees (*Apis laboriosa*) produce a honey containing grayanotoxin, derived from rhododendron nectar. Known locally as "bhir mauri ko maha," this substance has gained global attention as "mad honey" and is now sold online for hundreds of dollars per kilogram.

## The Danger

Even small amounts can cause serious cardiac events. Consuming more than a few tablespoons can cause heart rate to drop to life-threatening levels. Symptoms — dizziness, nausea, low blood pressure — typically appear within 30–60 minutes. Treatment involves atropine and IV fluids; most patients recover within two days.

## Who Is Affected

A review of 68 documented cases (2009 onward) found:
- ~72% of patients were middle-aged men
- Most consumed the honey deliberately — as a home remedy for hypertension or to improve sexual function
- Poisonings cluster in spring (peak rhododendron bloom) and secondarily in autumn

## Going Global

Cases have been confirmed in South Korea, France, and Qatar. Online sellers often market it as a "psychoactive superfood" without adequate safety warnings. Doctors in these regions may not recognize the poisoning, delaying treatment.

## Conservation Concern

The cliff bee population is declining due to overharvesting, habitat loss, and climate change — threatening Himalayan pollination ecosystems before the species is fully understood.
