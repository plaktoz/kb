# ChronoScale plans 50 MW AI deployment under two-year Microsoft partnership

ChronoScale and Microsoft formed a two-year strategic partnership on August 6 to support a planned deployment of approximately 50 MW of AI compute capacity. ChronoScale disclosed the agreement in its annual report filed Wednesday, alongside fiscal 2026 revenue of $71.6 million, down 15% from the prior year.

The project would pair NVIDIA GB300 systems with advanced liquid cooling for high-density AI and accelerated-compute workloads. ChronoScale expects to complete the deployment in the first quarter of 2027, subject to financing and development, construction and operating milestones.

ChronoScale did not disclose the project's location, required investment, pricing or expected revenue. The filing also did not identify the agreement as take-or-pay or state that Microsoft had committed to purchase all 50 MW of planned capacity.

The agreement allows Microsoft to receive penalties and other credits if ChronoScale fails to meet applicable conditions or milestones, according to the filing. ChronoScale said it could not assure investors that it would secure financing on favorable terms or satisfy the project requirements.

ChronoScale reported $9.7 million of cash and a $42.6 million working-capital deficit as of May 31. The operator has recorded losses and relied on Applied Digital (NASDAQ: APLD), its parent firm, for operating funding.

A demand promissory note signed with Applied Digital in June provides up to $100 million of principal advances, subject to reductions for guaranteed liabilities and a lender-determined reserve. ChronoScale drew $7 million on July 1, leaving $93 million undrawn, but the filing did not specify whether that capital would fund the Microsoft-supported deployment.

Fiscal 2026 operating loss narrowed to $37.5 million from $55.3 million, while net loss from continuing operations improved to $49.3 million from $72.7 million. ChronoScale attributed the revenue decline mainly to a customer loss in December 2024.

ChronoScale's cloud business operates from third-party colocation centers in Colorado, Minnesota and Utah. The company plans to divest its Ekso Bionics business during fiscal 2027 and focus on cloud services for AI and machine-learning customers.
