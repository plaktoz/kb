# A Born-Digital Librarian's Reflection on Digital Educational Tools

Expecting all students to be fluent with laptops and digital tools is itself a barrier for those with less technological experience. A mature student returning to academia describes the shift as a huge learning curve navigating technology — "it gets in the way of learning the actual material."

Many popular classroom "engagement" tools are digitizations of existing analog activities rather than genuinely new capabilities: Miro is essentially a virtual blackboard, Quizlet turns flashcards digital and renders the act of handwriting them obsolete, and Kahoot replaces old-fashioned pen-and-paper trivia. The author argues these tools reduce tactility and contribute to a disembodiment of learning, while also being privately owned by for-profit companies that require students to hand over personal information such as email addresses or browsing data.

The friction of physically distributing papers or having students get up to add ideas to a real blackboard reintroduces a dynamism to learning that can pull people out of the "digital funk" created by many learning technologies.
