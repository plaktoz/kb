# Markets News, Aug. 20, 2026: Major Indexes Slide After Snapping 3-Day Skids; Oil Prices, Treasury Yields, Bitcoin Rise; Walmart Stock Drops

Major U.S. stock indexes slumped on Thursday, a day after they snapped three-session losing streaks, as Walmart shares tumbled. Meanwhile, bitcoin, oil prices, and Treasury yields rose. The Dow Jones Industrial Average, Nasdaq Composite, and S&P 500 declined 1.3%, 1%, and 0.9%, respectively.

Shares of Walmart (WMT) sank 9% after it issued soft third-quarter guidance, making it the worst-performing stock in the Dow and Nasdaq 100. Walmart's CFO said the company is eligible for about $2.9 billion in tariff refunds. The company reported adjusted EPS of 81 cents on a 6% jump in revenue to $187.94 billion, above consensus, and lifted its full-year sales-growth forecast to 4%-5%, but comparable U.S. sales growth of 3.1% missed the 3.7% analysts projected.

The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.71% from 4.65% Wednesday, even after the Treasury said it would "at least double" its buyback of longer-term debt. This week's volatility coincides with U.S. national debt exceeding $40 trillion for the first time.

Bitcoin traded around $72,700, its highest level since early June, with altcoins including ether, xrp, and hype rising more than 20% in 24 hours. Crude oil rose after Trump threatened "economic D-Day" on Iran; WTI futures rose nearly 3% to about $88.15 a barrel.

In post-earnings moves, Deere & Co. (DE) rose 7% after beating estimates on strength in its construction and forestry unit, while Advance Auto Parts (AAP) tumbled 26% on a sales miss tied to "tighter household budgets." Shares of Moderna (MRNA) pulled back 24% after skyrocketing more than 175% a day earlier on positive cancer-vaccine trial results.

Fed minutes released Wednesday showed a growing number of officials losing patience with high inflation and increasingly inclined to raise rates; the minutes also confirmed Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is considering cutting meeting frequency to six a year from eight.

Separately, Morgan Stanley noted a growing gap between institutional ownership and benchmark weights for mega-cap tech stocks including Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet — a pattern the bank's historical analysis links to future gains.
