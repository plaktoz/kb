# IBM and NASA Release Open-Source AI Model to Support Lunar Exploration

IBM and NASA have released the NASA-IBM Lunar Foundation Model, an open-source AI system designed to help scientists analyze decades of lunar observation data. The model was trained on a curated multi-instrument dataset and aims to support sustained human presence on the Moon.

The model addresses a longstanding challenge: vast amounts of lunar data existed, but scientists had to manually sift through imagery or rely on low-resolution, task-specific tools. The new model aggregates over 30 spatially aligned data layers from nine instruments across four missions.

Key capabilities include:

- Lunar ice detection — reduces error by up to 22% vs. comparable baseline models in identifying potential ice deposits in permanently shadowed regions
- Volcanic feature mapping — improves capture of Irregular Mare Patches by ~3% over baseline
- Crater detection — outperforms baseline models by nearly 19% at context-scale resolution using half the training data

NASA's chief science data officer noted the goal of "turning large-scale data into new discoveries," while IBM Research's Juan Bernabe-Moreno emphasized the model's ability to "connect observations across instruments, revealing patterns that are difficult to see in isolation."

The model joins IBM's Prithvi family of open foundation models, which also covers geospatial, weather, and heliophysics domains.
