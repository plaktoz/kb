# Netflix Has $27 Billion Left to Buy Back Its Own Stock -- About 8% of the Whole Company

Netflix repurchased a record $4.7 billion of its own shares in Q2 2026, closing the quarter with $27.1 billion remaining in buyback authorizations — enough to retire roughly 8% of the company at current prices (~$76/share).

A collapsed Warner Bros. acquisition deal was a key funding source. Netflix walked away from the deal and received a "$2.8 billion termination fee," boosting management's 2026 free cash flow forecast to ~$12.5 billion. The board subsequently added $25 billion to its repurchase program.

The Q2 buyback pace was unsustainable — Netflix generated only $1.5 billion in free cash flow that quarter, meaning repurchases ran about three times operating cash production.

The fully diluted share count fell roughly 2% year-over-year (~4.26B vs. 4.35B shares), which helped EPS grow 11% even as net income rose only 9%.

Sparks cautions that buybacks amplify per-share results but don't alter business trajectory. Revenue growth decelerated from 16% to 13%, and he's not buying the stock solely on buyback momentum.
