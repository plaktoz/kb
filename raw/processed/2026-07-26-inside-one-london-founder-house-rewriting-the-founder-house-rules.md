# Inside one London founder house rewriting the founder-house rules

source_url: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/26/inside-one-london-founder-house-rewriting-the-founder-house-rules

---

By Dominic-Madori Davis, Senior Reporter, Venture — July 26, 2026

Six twentysomethings in East London have built what they say is the anti-San Francisco hacker house. The goal is a "holistic improvement in life," rather than "12 weeks, Demo Day is coming," Rowan Aldean, 26, explained.

The house is called the London Island Founder House — or "Lift House" — and Aldean and his wife, Zahraa, 22, have lived there since May, a few months after it officially launched in March. Aldean sold his previous company last year for millions and now runs an "applied AI" startup helping companies deploy agents.

Lift House is part startup workspace, part co-living space, and one of the very few co-living hacker houses in London (compared to San Francisco, where dozens are scattered around the city). It's a bet that U.K. founders can build successful companies without mimicking Silicon Valley's over-the-top hustle culture — no illegal warehouse parties, no 72-hour "996" sprints.

Instead, Lift House is part of a trend called "Londonmaxxing," in which founders attempt to optimize everything the London tech scene offers. London AI startups have raised $12 billion so far in 2026, out of $14.7 billion raised by all London startups, per Dealroom. Six companies have raised more than $500 million: Wayve, Superintelligence, ElevenLabs, Recursive, Ineffable Intelligence, and Isomorphic Labs — the latter three founded by DeepMind alumni.

## Journaling vs. demo day

Residents buy their own groceries but often cook together; cleaning is split among the group. On Sundays the group journals together — tracking time spent in nature, how well they ate, how much they moved. "I'm eating healthier, working out more, and sleeping more," said Luke, 27, who runs an AI-marketing company. Tuesdays are for volleyball in a local league; other evenings might include piano, Catan, or art exhibitions.

Each founder follows their own schedule. David Amor, 28, who runs a brain-coaching company, is up by 8 a.m., gives himself 30 seconds after waking before starting work, then takes a cold shower "because it increases your dopamine by 250%." Aldean rarely wakes before 10 a.m. unless something big is happening.

British founders, Aldean said, face a different pressure than U.S. founders: a cultural aversion to risk, an inclination toward humility, and "tall poppy syndrome," where the media builds someone up only to tear them down for success — making some founders wary of displaying wins.

Still, residents say London is a strong choice for early-stage founders: a good network, ample early capital, an option for life outside tech, and access to SEIS/EIS tax incentives that make angel investment more attractive than in the U.S. Aldean also feels the ecosystem is less cutthroat and less prone to gossip than the Bay Area hacker houses he lived in.

## Coming to America

Eventually, though, the road for many U.K. startups leads to the U.S., where founders get access to the world's largest economy and investors willing to write large checks from pre-seed to growth stage. "It's almost like a factory line in a way," said Varun, Luke's co-founder. "You start here, and then you expand there or vice versa." American investors are also pulling talent directly — one founder reportedly had to relocate to the U.S. just to secure backing from a top investor.

The Lift House lease has about a year left, with sentiment in the house to keep it going. For prospective residents, the bar is a hobby outside the company and an interest in fitness — the pitch of Londonmaxxing: that here, one can have it all. "The culture is to build something that lasts," Aldean said, "not necessarily burn out chasing a flash."
