# US producer prices are up 6.5% on the year, the most since November 2022

Goods prices drove most of October's increase, with steel mill products and industrial chemicals among the biggest risers, while core producer prices ran 4.9% above last year. CME FedWatch put the odds of a quarter-point December hike at 89.7% after the data.

A wholesale inflation number that came in softer than forecast still left traders betting almost for certain on a Fed rate hike four weeks from now. If you own growth or AI stocks, the level of prices matters more than the miss.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday, November 13, that its Producer Price Index for final demand rose 0.3% in October on a seasonally adjusted basis. Over the 12 months through October, prices climbed 6.5%, the biggest annual jump since the 7.4% recorded in November 2022. Economists had expected 0.4% on the month, according to Cointelegraph's coverage of the release, so the print was a notch cooler than forecast. It wasn't cool in any way that helps anyone.

Goods did most of the work. The BLS says final demand goods rose 0.5% while services rose 0.2%, and more than 60% of the October advance came from goods other than food and energy. Take out food, energy and trade services and the index still rose 0.5% on the month and 4.9% on the year. Summaries of the release put steel mill products up 2.7% and industrial chemicals up 3.1% in the month, while crude petroleum fell, which is why the headline number looks tamer than the factory-gate picture underneath it.

PPI tracks what businesses get paid for what they sell, before a shopper sees a price tag. When steel and chemicals cost more, a manufacturer either eats the difference in margin or passes it down the line, and traders read the index as an early look at both consumer prices and corporate profits. That's why it gets watched on a morning like this one, a few days after a consumer price report that Yahoo Finance said had already pushed up rate-hike odds.

It's a noisy gauge, and one month proves little. Still, a 4.9% core reading sits a long way above anything the Fed wants to see.

## The December hike is close to settled

Following the release, CME Group's FedWatch tool showed the odds of a 25-basis-point hike at the December meeting rising from 85.4% on Thursday to 89.7%, per Cointelegraph. The chance of a 50-basis-point move fell from 14.5% to 10.3%. So the data did one useful thing for markets: it took some of the supersized-hike fear off the table and left the ordinary hike standing.

The path to that number is worth noticing. A week earlier, 24/7 Wall St. reported December hike odds at 77% after a strong jobs report. Jobs data, consumer prices and now wholesale prices have each nudged the same probability higher, and none of them has nudged it back down.

For long-duration stocks, meaning companies whose big profits are expected years from now, that's the uncomfortable part. A higher policy rate shrinks the present value of those distant earnings, and a lot of the AI trade is priced on exactly that kind of future.

## Was PPI really what hit tech?

The PPI print was not the only thing weighing on tech. Cointelegraph reported the S&P 500 down 1.2% and the Nasdaq Composite down 1.5% at the time of writing, with bitcoin about 4% lower near $68,700, and it attributed the stock weakness in part to turmoil around Nvidia's leadership transition. An 8-K filed by Nvidia shows Jensen Huang resigned as president, CEO and board member effective immediately, with CFO Colette Kress named interim chief executive.

Frankly, a producer price print a tenth of a point under forecast doesn't knock a market down by itself. What it can do is take away the cushion. Nvidia is the biggest weight in the AI trade, and when its leadership is in question a day before inflation data confirms rates are going up, investors have no soft landing to point to. The index figures above are intraday readings, not closing levels.

The next test is already scheduled. The BLS will publish November's PPI on December 15 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern.
