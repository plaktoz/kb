# The Gift Of Delegation: How To Lead Without Doing It All

I rarely work with an existing leader or rising leader who isn't out of time and overcommitted. They are exhausted all the time. They seem to be shortchanging everyone in their life—their family, friends, boss and employees. They feel like they still have to do their old job while the demands of their current job bear down upon them. They just want to find some space to do it all.

When I ask them, "What do you want more of?" they all say, "More time!"

They can't find time to think, time to reflect, time for their family, time for their hobbies, time to be present in the moment or, most importantly, time to think strategically. Even with strong boundaries, it's a daily struggle and a never-ending challenge.

## Delegation: Not All Or Nothing

The challenge is that many leaders view delegation as all or nothing. They believe delegation means they must give up total control. Many leaders are perfectionists to some degree, and they see delegation as uncomfortable or something to avoid.

The best-kept secret is that thoughtful delegation leads to two big gifts: time back on your calendar and the accelerated development of your people. If you're always pressed for time and your people aren't rising, delegation may make the biggest impact.

Most leaders explore only two options when considering delegation: They delegate 100% of the task, throw it over the fence and cross their fingers, or they delegate very little, if any, of the task and practice a version of micromanaging.

## How To Decide When To Delegate

When the next task hits your inbox, consider the following steps.

1. Ask yourself: Am I the best and cheapest resource to complete this task? If not, delegate.

2. Collaborate with a team member to determine what they need to complete the task.

3. Identify where they need your help or sponsorship.

4. Assess the potential risks for succeeding or failing to complete the task.

5. Make accommodations to mitigate the risk with clear boundaries.

6. Identify when and where you want to be involved in decisions.

7. Set the expectation of communicating the status and completion of the task to you.

The best delegators avoid the extremes. They consider the resources needed, the collaboration required and the potential risks. They determine what they can let go of and what they must keep in their sights. They customize their level of delegation to suit the person and situation. If you aren't delegating because you can't let go, find a comfortable medium between the extremes.

## The Gift Of Delegation

The beauty of delegation is the gift it provides. Delegation gives your employees an opportunity to learn, grow, show you what they can do and build their brand. In turn, they can grow more confident, trust you more and be more loyal to you.

Delegation gives you more time for your strategic priorities and to accelerate your results. You no longer have to do your old job and your current job, and you can improve your performance. By focusing on your strategic priorities, you can exceed expectations and yield results. The best gift of all is that you can also have more time to spend with your family and friends.

Delegate. It is a gift, and excellent results closely follow thoughtful delegation.
