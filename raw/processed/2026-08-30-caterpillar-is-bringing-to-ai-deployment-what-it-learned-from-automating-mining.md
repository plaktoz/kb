# Caterpillar is bringing to AI deployment what it learned from automating mining

Caterpillar is leveraging decades of autonomous machine deployment in mining to inform its broader AI strategy. The company's automation journey began in mining, where labor shortages and dangerous conditions made robotics especially valuable. Its portfolio now includes automated haul trucks, drilling equipment, underground loaders, dozers, and remote-controlled construction gear, plus fleet management software.

CTO Jaime Mineart described the current moment as an opportunity to take mining-derived knowledge into "much more dynamic environments, jobsites, quarries, and construction sites."

## Key AI Initiatives

- **Cat AI Assistant** — voice-activated tool for field technicians to retrieve repair procedures and identify parts
- **Digital twins** — AI-powered site scanning and manufacturing analysis
- **Enterprise AI** — including agents that modernize legacy code and flag software defects

Mineart emphasized that the real challenge isn't building the tech — it's integrating it into customer workflows and redefining how workers operate alongside machines. Some operators may transition from running single machines to remotely overseeing multiple units.

## Workforce Investment

To support this shift, Caterpillar plans to invest **$100 million over five years** in workforce training for AI, autonomy, and robotics across its 118,000 employees.

## Financial Context

The company posted a record **$20.5 billion** in quarterly revenue (Q2), with its power-generation division — supplying data centers — surging **72%** to $3.1 billion.
