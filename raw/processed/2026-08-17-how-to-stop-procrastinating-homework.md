# How to Stop Procrastinating on Homework: 7 Strategies That Work

Procrastination isn't laziness — research shows it's usually about avoiding an uncomfortable feeling (boredom, anxiety, fear of doing the task badly). The brain reaches for quick relief (phone, snack) to escape that discomfort, repeating the cycle. Seeing procrastination as an emotion-management problem rather than a character flaw reframes the fixes below, which all work by lowering the emotional barrier to starting.

1. **Break the Task Into Tiny Steps** — "Write essay" is overwhelming; "open a doc and write one bad sentence" is not. Shrink the first step until it's almost too easy to refuse; momentum does the rest.
2. **Use the Two-Minute Rule** — commit to just two minutes of work, which slips past the brain's resistance before dread kicks in; anticipation of a task is usually worse than the task itself.
3. **Try the Pomodoro Technique** — 25 minutes focused work, 5-minute break, repeat; makes work feel finite and sidesteps the overwhelm of an open-ended session.
4. **Remove Distractions Before You Start** — willpower is unreliable with a phone nearby; put it in another room, close tabs, use website blockers, so the focused choice is the default one.
5. **Give Yourself a Real Deadline** — open-ended tasks drift forever; set a specific finish time and attach a small stake (a reward, or telling someone) for accountability.
6. **Forgive Yourself and Restart** — studies find students who forgive themselves for procrastinating procrastinate less next time; guilt spirals feed the cycle rather than breaking it.
7. **Build It Into a Routine** — a consistent study time and place turns focus into habit instead of a daily willpower battle.

The piece closes by encouraging picking one strategy and trying it tonight rather than waiting for the "right" moment.
