# What just happened to tech stocks?

'Tis the season… to be dreary about tech stocks.

On Thursday, Anthropic (ANT) stormed onto the public markets. Investors piled into the IPO. Interested bystanders digested smiling photos at Nasdaq of the brother-sister leadership duo, Dario and Daniela Amodei. The AI dream was alive and well.

But everyone missed the other story: The rest of tech was getting absolutely demolished.

The Nasdaq fell more than 2% by the close of trading on Thursday. Nvidia (NVDA) and AMD (AMD) were getting crushed. Chip stocks were bleeding. And the S&P 500 (^GSPC) logged another ugly day.

The selling pressure extended into Friday. So much so that Nvidia fell to fourth place on the world's most valuable company list. The stock was down 23% over five days, suffering its worst week since October 2008.

What is going on? I would say it's a perfect storm.

The Democratic blue wave still has Wall Street questioning whether Washington is about to put the brakes on AI and data center construction. Lots of measured takes on the AI boom have hit my inbox post-election, trust me.

Nvidia has lost founder Jensen Huang, the face of this entire AI revolution, and the leadership fallout is shaking confidence. There is no timeline on when a permanent CEO will be announced, but interim CEO Colette Kress is not seen as the answer.

China is back at the center of the semiconductor trade, with fresh regulatory fears and a damaging short seller report targeting AMD.

Then there's the technical selling. Market-clearing events are forcing investors to raise cash.

Now throw in sticky inflation, hawkish Fed rhetoric, and the prospect of tighter financial conditions.

"What's happening in tech is more of an absolutely rational repricing, given that we have new facts," former Bridgewater Associates chief investment strategist Rebecca Patterson said on Sozzi Unleashed. "I mean, we lost the CEO of the central bank of AI, Jensen Huang. That deserves to be repriced."

"We have export controls. We have a Democrat Congress coming in that's probably going to take a harder look at things like data center construction," Patterson added. "And we know, especially with the outages and all those weird technical things in the last couple of weeks, we're probably going to have more of those happening as AI leads people to say, 'Oh my gosh, I need to patch. I need to find my vulnerabilities and fix them.' Things will break along the way. And so you have had a number of important catalysts leading to this repricing. I think the lower valuations make sense."

Therein lies the disconnect: Anthropic can become a nearly $2 trillion AI sensation while the rest of the AI trade is getting repriced in real time.

The Anthropic IPO suggests investors still want the AI future. The sell-off says they're suddenly a lot less willing to pay any price to get there. And that is the perfect storm market we are dealing with.

Wondering when to step into the rubble? Keep waiting, pros like Patterson tell me.

"So would I buy the dip today? Probably not because I already have so much AI exposure simply by owning the index," Patterson said. "I do think people who are excited about AI, who want to have outsized exposure, you're probably getting to levels soon where you might want to be averaging in. But I think right now I prefer things like infrastructure. Frankly, sectors where I know I'm going to get some inflation protection. I know governments around the world are spending on resiliency, energy, national security. So I feel like that's one that gives me some protection and diversification in my portfolio."
