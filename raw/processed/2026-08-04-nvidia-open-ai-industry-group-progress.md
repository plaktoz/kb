# Nvidia doesn't mess around: A week after open AI industry group formed, it's already showing progress

The week-old Open Secure AI Alliance (OSAA), an industry group spearheaded by Nvidia that has already grown to over 120 companies, has developed a working group called the Shared AI Findings Exchange (SAFE). The group is already presenting proposals for open comment, managed by member The Linux Foundation.

The group developed the proposals while members gathered at the Black Hat cybersecurity conference in Las Vegas. The guidelines cover areas like how to confidentially report AI cybersecurity incidents, alert those affected, and then do blame-free analysis so everyone can learn from them.

Members are also contributing open source technology: Nvidia offers an open source LLM vulnerability scanner called Garak, Okta is working on agent identity tech, Red Hat is working on agent governance, and Amazon has contributed an open agent-building tool (Strands Agents) and an authorization language (Cedar).

The group includes Adobe, BlackRock, Cisco, Intel, Microsoft, and Visa, but notably not Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google — even though OpenAI and Google signed the original open letter (published last week and championed by Nvidia, signed by 200+ companies) urging the White House to support open source AI rather than restrict it. Both OpenAI and Google have released open-weight models of their own.

The group's formation follows reports that the Trump Administration was considering banning Chinese open-weight models, which caused industry alarm. The group's letter states: "Openness may be one of the most important paths to AI safety and security."
