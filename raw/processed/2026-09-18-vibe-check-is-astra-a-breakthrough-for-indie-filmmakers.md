# Vibe Check: Is Astra a Breakthrough for Indie Filmmakers?

Afterimage cofounders Cyrus Duff and Josh Lee tested GPT-6 Astra on real commercial footage to evaluate its VFX capabilities for independent filmmakers.

## Key Findings

### Strengths

- **Masking:** Astra outperformed Sol, using Robust Video Matting to cleanly isolate a boxer shot on 16mm film—handling grain, flash shadows, and fine hair better than Sol's GrabCut approach.
- **Tracking:** Both models followed broad motion, but Astra produced a "usable mask" with follow-up prompting; Sol did not.
- **3D Modeling:** Standout results included a rendered building composited into a Brooklyn street, a transparent wineglass with realistic reflections, and a Snoopy parade float inserted into casual iPhone footage.
- **Workflow integration:** Astra controlled professional software (Nuke) via Python API without the filmmakers touching it directly.

### Weaknesses

- **Scene interaction:** Eyes added to fingertips looked "tacked onto" surfaces—skin merging and realistic blinking proved elusive even after extensive iteration.
- **Organic forms:** Composited cows lacked grass interaction, natural body deformation, and convincing movement. Repeated passes didn't resolve the issues.

## Aesthetic Takeaway

The authors note the most exciting outcome is creative rather than operational. They describe a new aesthetic possibility—casual footage combined with visual maximalism—as "genuinely new and quite bizarre," unlocking images that previously couldn't exist at indie budgets.
