# 1 S&P 500 Stock Worth Investigating and 2 We Avoid

The S&P 500 is home to the market's biggest, most well-known companies, but not all large-cap constituents are equal — some face slowing growth, declining margins, or rising competition.

**Stocks to sell**: Delta Air Lines (DAL, $53.02B market cap) — sluggish revenue-passenger-mile trends, free cash flow margin not expected to grow next year, and eroding returns on capital suggesting recent investments are destroying value; trades at $81.26/share, 10x forward P/E. HP Inc. (HPQ, $26.95B market cap) — sales fell 1.2% annually over five years, projected sales flat for the next 12 months, and EPS flat over two years despite revenue growth, implying less-profitable incremental sales; trades at $29.52/share, 10.9x forward P/E.

**Stock to watch**: Lennox International (LII, $14.03B market cap), a Texas-based HVACR company — excellent 17.7% operating margin with profits that grew over five years as it scaled, free cash flow margin up 8.6 points over five years, and a 37.3% ROIC. Trades at $413.10/share, 16.8x forward P/E.

The piece frames momentum as a key differentiator right now: "The best time to own a great stock is when the market is finally noticing it," pointing to prior momentum-screen picks like Nvidia (+1,460% June 2020–June 2025) and smaller names like Kadant (+214% over the same period).
