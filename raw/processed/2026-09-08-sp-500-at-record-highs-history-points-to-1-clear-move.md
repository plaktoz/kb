# With the S&P 500 Setting Record Highs, History Points to 1 Clear Move

Despite headwinds including a war in Iran and elevated Treasury yields, the S&P 500 has climbed ~13.6% year-to-date through early September 2026, as tracked by the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO).

Records aren't sell signals. Historically, new highs tend to precede more gains. The S&P 500 logged 461 record highs between 2013 and June 2026, delivering roughly 400% cumulative returns over that period. Fidelity research going back to 1950 found the index averaged a 12.7% gain in the 12 months following a record close.

Staying invested matters. Fidelity notes that $10,000 invested on Jan. 1, 1988 would have grown to nearly $523,000 by end of 2024 — but only if the investor never exited the market. Missing even a handful of the best days significantly erodes long-term returns.

Calendar tailwinds ahead: October closes out the historically weaker six-month period for equities. The upcoming third year of the presidential cycle (2027) has historically been the strongest of the four. Additionally, analysts project S&P 500 Q3 earnings growth of 28.5%, which could fuel further highs.
