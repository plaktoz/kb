# Morgan Stanley's Latest AI Call Could Spark a Comeback in Momentum ETFs

The momentum trade suffered a painful selloff in recent weeks, but Morgan Stanley believes the worst may be over. Michael Wilson, the bank's chief U.S. equity strategist, said one of the "worst momentum selloffs in history" is giving way to a healthier environment where leadership rotates away from semiconductor stocks toward companies with stable earnings, stronger margins, and better operational efficiency — a reset rather than the end of the momentum factor. The comments follow a Goldman Sachs basket of momentum stocks plunging 35% from its June peak as investors unwound crowded AI and semiconductor positions; the basket remains up 9.4% for the year, roughly in line with the S&P 500.

Momentum ETFs track stocks with the strongest recent price performance and rebalance periodically to capture market leadership. During the AI rally, many accumulated large positions in semiconductor and mega-cap tech names, leaving them vulnerable when investors began taking profits amid concerns over AI infrastructure spending. iShares MSCI USA Momentum Factor ETF (MTUM), the largest fund in the space with $25 billion in assets, lost almost 9% in the past month; Invesco Dorsey Wright Momentum ETF (PDP) is down more than 7%. Invesco's S&P MidCap and SmallCap Momentum ETFs (XMMO, XSMO) apply the same screens to smaller-cap stocks and could benefit if market leadership broadens beyond mega-cap tech.

Wilson's outlook suggests investors should distinguish between a collapse in the momentum factor and a rotation within it, with companies posting stable earnings gradually replacing higher-beta AI beneficiaries in momentum rankings as the cycle matures. Goldman Sachs strategist Ben Snider echoed the view, noting the AI consolidation resembles previous momentum pullbacks and that investor deleveraging could improve the setup for another advance if earnings remain supportive. S&P 500 companies are enjoying one of the strongest earnings seasons in years, with 86% beating estimates, according to Bloomberg Intelligence — a dynamic that could give momentum ETFs fresh drivers for gains if strength broadens beyond AI and semiconductors.
