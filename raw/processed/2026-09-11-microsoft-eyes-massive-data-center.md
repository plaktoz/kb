# Microsoft Eyes Massive Data Center Push

Microsoft plans to expand its global data center capacity from roughly 12 gigawatts to over 38 gigawatts by 2032 — a more than threefold increase aimed at resolving severe server shortages.

## Key Points

- **Capacity goal:** Surpass 38 GW total power by 2032 (owned + leased facilities)
- **AI focus:** AI-dedicated capacity expected to grow from ~2 GW now to roughly one-third of the 38 GW total
- **Capex:** Microsoft spent $145 billion in capital expenditures last fiscal year
- **Competitors:** Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta have collectively earmarked nearly $2.4 trillion toward computing infrastructure

## Background

Capacity problems worsened after Microsoft temporarily paused some data center projects around early 2025 over overbuilding concerns. Consequences included:
- Temu signing a cloud deal with Oracle after Microsoft couldn't supply capacity in preferred regions
- GitHub suffering an eight-hour outage in August, rerouting traffic through overseas facilities and rival AWS infrastructure
- Xbox capping cloud game streaming time for subscribers

Microsoft is bringing a new "East US 3" cluster near Atlanta online, relying heavily on **Intel CPUs** rather than GPUs.

## Headwinds

Public opposition to data centers — over energy and land use — has led governors in Texas and New York to halt new construction projects. Internal projections may shift due to regulatory challenges and evolving technology.

**MSFT stock** was up approximately 1.6% year-to-date at time of publication.
