# AI Terms Glossary — TechCrunch

TechCrunch's living AI glossary covers essential terminology as the field rapidly evolves. Key terms include:

- **AGI** – AI outperforming humans at most tasks; definitions vary across OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and researchers
- **AI Agent** – Autonomous system performing multistep tasks like booking, coding, or expense filing
- **Chain of Thought** – Breaking problems into intermediate steps to improve LLM reasoning accuracy
- **Opaque Recurrence** – An AI loops queries through internal layers repeatedly rather than reasoning in plain language, reducing readable traces and complicating safety oversight
- **Recurrent Depth** – The engineering term for the same underlying method as opaque recurrence
- **Neuralese** – Hypothetical scenario where a model reasons entirely in internal numeric representations, becoming a total black box
- **Hallucination** – AI generating factually incorrect information due to training data gaps
- **RAMageddon** – Growing RAM chip shortage driven by AI infrastructure demand, raising costs across consumer electronics and enterprise computing
- **MCP (Model Context Protocol)** – Anthropic-originated open standard connecting AI models to external tools and data, described as "a USB-C port for AI"
- **Mixture of Experts (MoE)** – Architecture activating only specialized sub-networks per task, enabling large but efficient models
- **RLHF** – Reinforcement learning from human feedback, central to fine-tuning models for safety and helpfulness
- **Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI)** – AI improving itself without human input; framed both as a research frontier and a potential safety threshold
- **Validation Loss** – Training metric indicating how well a model generalizes; helps detect overfitting
- **Weights** – Numerical parameters shaping model output by determining feature importance during training

The glossary notes that "opaque recurrence" has surged in usage following coverage of OpenAI's Astra model, which safety researchers flagged as a step toward less transparent reasoning.
