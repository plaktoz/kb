# Why You Need to Subtract: The Vasa Effect

**Author:** Sahil Bloom
**Date:** August 20, 2026

---

In 1625, Sweden's King Gustavus Adolphus commissioned a grand warship to symbolize military dominance over Poland. He continuously expanded its scope — extending the hull to 69 meters, adding a second gun deck, dozens of heavy cannons, and elaborate gilded carvings. The result was the most expensive object Sweden had ever built.

Before its maiden voyage, a stability test nearly capsized the ship at the dock. Despite this warning, the voyage proceeded. On August 10, 1628, the *Vasa* sank less than a mile into its journey, killing 30 crew members. The tribunal blamed the builders and crew — but the real cause was unchecked addition.

Bloom calls this the **Vasa Effect**: "The addition of individual positives can eventually create a catastrophic negative."

Dr. Leidy Klotz (University of Virginia) studied this bias experimentally. In a Lego stabilization task, only 41% chose the cheaper subtractive solution. When explicitly reminded subtraction was free, that rose to 61%. His research found that "people systematically default to searching for additive transformations."

Bloom argues we do the same in life — endlessly asking what to *add*, rarely what to *remove*. Better questions: What am I leaving behind? What can I subtract?

Lao Tzu: *"To attain knowledge add things every day. To attain wisdom subtract things every day."*
