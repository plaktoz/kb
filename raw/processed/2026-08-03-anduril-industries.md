# Anduril Industries

Anduril Industries, Inc. is an American military technology company specializing in advanced autonomous systems. Founded April 20, 2017, by Palmer Luckey, Trae Stephens, Matt Grimm, Joe Chen, and Brian Schimpf. As of May 2026, valuation stands at $61 billion with ~7,000 employees and $2 billion in 2025 revenue.

## History

### Origins

In June 2014, Oculus Rift creator Palmer Luckey attended a Founders Fund retreat on Sonora Island, BC, where he met Trae Stephens, a former Palantir employee recruited by Peter Thiel. The two shared interest in defense contracting for tech-startup-style companies.

After the 2016 election, Stephens joined the Defense transition team and later the Defense Innovation Board. He and Luckey recruited from Palantir and Oculus, planning to combine low-cost hardware with sophisticated software. A fifth co-founder, Brian Schimpf (now CEO), came from Palantir's directorship.

### Key Milestones

- **2017:** Incorporated; seeded by Founders Fund; first DHS border security pitch
- **2018:** Informal tower tests on Texas ranch land
- **2019:** UK Royal Navy purchased Lattice; $120M funding round; valuation exceeded $1B
- **2020:** $200M raised; CBP five-year $25M contract; $950M Air Force ABMS contract ceiling
- **2021:** Acquired Area-I; $450M Series D at $4.6B valuation
- **2022:** Acquired Dive Technologies; UK Home Office contract (~£16M)
- **2023:** Acquired Adranos and Blue Force Technologies
- **2024:** $1.5B Series F at $14B valuation; sanctioned by China over Taiwan arms sales
- **2025:** Arsenal-1 announced; $2.5B raised at $30.5B valuation
- **2026:** $5B raised; valuation reaches $61B

## Products

### Altius
Fixed-wing, tube-launched UAV series. The Altius 600 accepts modular nose payloads and can launch from C-130s, UH-60 Blackhawks, ground vehicles, and larger UAVs including the MQ-1C Grey Eagle.

### Anvil
A quadcopter UCAV designed to destroy other drones via computer vision-guided ramming, reaching up to 200 mph. The **Anvil-M** variant (unveiled Oct 2023) adds an explosive munitions payload for Group 1/2 UAS.

### Barracuda
Air-breathing cruise missile family in three variants:
- **Barracuda-100:** <160 km range, 16 kg warhead
- **Barracuda-250:** 280–370 km range, 16 kg warhead
- **Barracuda-500:** 800 km range, 45 kg warhead

### Bolt / Bolt-M
A 12 lb backpack-deployable UAV with 45-minute flight time and 20 km range. The **Bolt-M** carries up to a 3 lb ordnance payload (anti-personnel or anti-materiel).

### Copperhead UUV
Autonomous underwater vehicle family with utility and loitering munitions modes:
- **Copperhead-100:** Comparable to Mark 54 lightweight torpedo
- **Copperhead-500:** Comparable to Mark 48 heavy torpedo

### Dive-LD
AUV developed by acquired firm Dive Technologies; suited for littoral and deep-water ISR. Can deploy Copperhead UUVs.

### Dust
A 4 lb ground sensor for monitoring areas with limited line-of-sight. Battery life of two months or solar-powered.

### Fury (YFQ-44)
A stealthy, subsonic drone with 17-foot wingspan. Originally designed by Blue Force Technologies, significantly modified by Anduril. Competed against General Atomics' YFQ-42A for the Air Force Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA) program. Maiden flight: October 31, 2025.

### Ghost
Single-rotor helicopter-style UAV with low acoustic signature. Ghost 4 specifications:
- Length: 2.725 m; collapses to 1.07 m for backpack transport
- Flight time: 100 minutes; cruise speed: 52 knots
- Payload: 35 lbs across five modular bays
- Uses Nvidia chips originally designed for self-driving vehicles

The **Ghost X** was selected by the U.S. Army in September 2024 for Company-Level Small UAS (75-minute flight time, 25 km range).

### Ghost Shark (XL-AUV)
Autonomous submarine developed for the Royal Australian Navy. Three prototypes contracted in May 2022. First unit delivered November 2025, ahead of schedule. Can carry "dozens" of Copperhead-100 class UUVs and "multiple" Copperhead-500 class UUVs.

### Lattice
Core AI software platform fusing data from diverse sensors. As of 2023, approximately 290 CBP surveillance towers operate along the US-Mexico border using Lattice. Used in the 2020 ABMS exercise simulating Russian cruise missile intercepts at White Sands.

### Roadrunner
A 6-foot twin-turbojet delta-winged craft capable of vertical takeoff/landing. Described as between "an autonomous drone and a reusable missile." The **Roadrunner-M** variant carries an explosive warhead. Unveiled December 2023; named as a reference to RTX's Coyote Block 2 competitor.

### Sentry Tower
A 33-foot solar-powered portable surveillance tower containing camera, radar, communications antennae, and thermal imaging. Deployable in under two hours; fits in a pickup truck when disassembled. Feeds data into Lattice. Referred to as a "virtual border wall."

An illumination technique uses an infrared laser repurposed from a hair-removal device, functioning like a photographic flash to image distant targets cheaply.

### Thunder
Autonomous unmanned attack rotorcraft co-developed with Archer, designed as a "loyal wingman" for helicopters such as the Apache. Uses optimum-speed tiltrotors.

## Military Programs

- **ABMS/JADC2:** Digital battlefield architecture connecting jets, drones, ships, and soldiers across incompatible data systems
- **IVAS:** Anduril took over development from Microsoft on February 11, 2025; up to 121,000 units potentially ordered by the Army
- **Project Maven:** AI-for-military initiative started 2018

## Headquarters and Facilities

- **HQ:** Costa Mesa, California ("The Press" — a former LA Times printing facility, ~450,000 sq ft)
- **Satellite offices:** Boston, Atlanta, Seattle, Washington D.C., London, Sydney
- **Testing range:** Near Camp Pendleton

### Arsenal-1
A ~$1 billion manufacturing facility under construction at Rickenbacker International Airport, Pickaway County, Ohio, in partnership with Ohio State University. Designed for hyperscale production of autonomous weapons systems.

## Criticism and Controversies

- ACLU and immigration advocates criticize CBP's use of Sentry Towers for pushing migrants toward dangerous routes
- UK Migrants Rights Network criticizes Home Office use of maritime sentry towers
- The company name references Andúril, the fictional sword of Aragorn in *The Lord of the Rings* (meaning "Flame of the West" in Quenya). Critics argue this is inappropriate given Tolkien's anti-war stance, though supporters draw parallels between the novels' themes of defending one's home and Anduril's stated mission.
- In 2019, Bloomberg called Anduril "Tech's Most Controversial Startup"
- November 2025: Partnered with UAE weapons company Edge Group, criticized by *The Intercept* over Edge Group's alleged links to Sudan arms
