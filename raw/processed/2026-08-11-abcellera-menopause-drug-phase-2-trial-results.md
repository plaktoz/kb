# AbCellera stock surges after menopause drug shows strong trial results

A single dose of ABCL635 cut hot flash frequency by 83% after four weeks, compared with 33% for placebo

AbCellera stock rose 34% on Monday after the Vancouver-based biotechnology company announced positive top-line results from its Phase 2 trial of ABCL635, an experimental non-hormonal antibody treatment for moderate-to-severe hot flashes associated with menopause.

The trial met its primary efficacy endpoints. Patients who received a single 600-mg subcutaneous dose of ABCL635 saw an 83% reduction in hot flash frequency after four weeks, versus 33% for placebo, the company said. On severity, ABCL635 produced a 58% reduction compared with 12% for placebo. Beyond hot flash reduction, the drug also delivered statistically significant gains in sleep quality and in how patients assessed their own overall condition.

The randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study enrolled 92 postmenopausal women averaging roughly 10 moderate or severe hot flashes per day. No patient dropped out of the study due to adverse events, with every one of the 92 enrollees finishing the full course of the trial. Among patients who received ABCL635, headache, fatigue, and injection site reaction were the adverse events reported most frequently, the company said.

ABCL635 works by blocking the neurokinin 3 receptor, a signaling pathway involved in the brain's control of body temperature. The drug is being developed as a once-monthly subcutaneous injection, positioning it as a long-acting alternative to daily oral treatments currently on the market.

AbCellera Chief Medical Officer Sarah Noonberg, M.D., Ph.D., said in a statement that the results demonstrate "potential best-in-class vasomotor symptom relief over four weeks with a single subcutaneous dose."

Additional 12-week data from the trial are expected later this year, the company said.

The antibody began its first human study in July 2025, making it the inaugural candidate from AbCellera's G protein-coupled receptor and ion channel platform to advance into clinical testing, the company said. Hot flashes and night sweats affect up to 80% of women going through menopause, with approximately 12 million women in the U.S. experiencing moderate-to-severe symptoms, of whom more than six million seek treatment.

Heading into the second half of the year, AbCellera reported a cash and marketable securities balance exceeding $565 million, with total available liquidity topping $675 million.
