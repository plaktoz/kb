# Credit Card Delinquencies Run 6.4% at Small Banks and 2.9% Across All of Them

The Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis tracks quarterly credit card delinquencies, offering insight into consumer health and bank stock prospects.

**Overall trends:** The Q2 overall delinquency rate (30+ days past due) was 2.85%, down from 2.91% in Q1 and 3.04% in Q2 2025. While improving, it remains elevated compared to the 2012–2023 period, when rates stayed below 2.85%.

**Large banks:** The top 100 banks saw delinquencies fall to 2.58% in Q2, down from a recent peak of 3.10% in Q3 2024. Diversified revenue streams — investment banking, institutional trading — allow large banks to tighten lending standards.

**Small banks:** Community banks saw delinquencies rise to 6.49% in Q2, up from 6.44% in Q1. Without diversified income, smaller banks take on higher credit risk, serving customers with lower credit scores. Rising delinquencies suggest their customers are facing more hardships in this economy.

**Impact on stocks:** Higher delinquencies reduce interest income, trigger loan charge-offs, and force higher provisions for credit losses — hurting earnings. Despite this, the KBW Nasdaq Bank Index is up approximately 14% year-to-date and the Regional Banking Index approximately 16%.
