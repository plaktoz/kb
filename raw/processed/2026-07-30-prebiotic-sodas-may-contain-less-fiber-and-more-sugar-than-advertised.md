# Prebiotic Sodas May Contain Less Fiber and More Sugar Than Advertised

source_url: https://www.healthline.com/health-news/prebiotic-sodas-may-contain-higher-sugar-less-fiber-than-advertised

---

By Corrie Pelc | Fact-checked by Jill Seladi-Schulman, Ph.D. | Published July 30, 2026

A study presented at NUTRITION 2026 (July 25–28, Washington, D.C.) analyzed 108 prebiotic sodas launched in the U.S. between 2021–2025, comparing their nutritional content against regular sodas, diet sodas, fruit drinks, and flavored sparkling waters.

Key findings per 12-oz can (average):
- 35 calories
- 5g sugar
- 5g fiber (range: ~2–9g)

Prebiotic sodas generally contained more calories and sugar than diet sodas and flavored sparkling water, though less than regular sodas.

Lead author Carlos Soto Díaz noted that consumers should check nutrition labels rather than relying on "prebiotic" marketing language.

Gastroenterologist Rudolph Bedford cautioned that while prebiotic sodas aren't a scam, they're "not equivalent to eating a high fiber diet."

Dietitian Monique Richard recommended obtaining prebiotics from whole foods — asparagus, bananas, oats, garlic, legumes, and onions — noting a diverse dietary pattern consistently outperforms isolated fiber added to beverages.

The study has not yet been peer-reviewed.
