# Healthy Diet and Fitness Program May Improve Prostate Cancer Outcomes

A UK study published in *The Lancet Oncology* found that the STAMINA lifestyle program — combining supervised exercise, dietary guidance, and behavioral support — reduced quality-of-life deterioration by up to one-third in men receiving androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) for prostate cancer.

The trial monitored 700 men (median age 71) across 15 NHS facilities in England. Over 80% of STAMINA participants completed their scheduled sessions. Those enrolled reported better cancer-specific quality of life and less fatigue compared to standard-care recipients.

ADT, a hormone-blocking treatment that lowers testosterone, carries side effects including fatigue, erectile dysfunction, mood changes, and bone density loss. The STAMINA program was designed to counteract these effects.

Experts noted the study's limitations: it measured quality of life over 12 months rather than cancer survival outcomes. Ying Wang of the American Cancer Society observed that "information alone may not be sufficient" — structured delivery and coaching matter as much as the recommendations themselves.

Urologist S. Adam Ramin emphasized that diets emphasizing leafy greens, legumes, and healthy fats support prostate health, and that regular aerobic exercise "releases endorphins...which positively affect overall health."

The American Cancer Society recommends at least 150 minutes weekly of moderate aerobic activity, resistance training, and a fruit-and-vegetable-rich diet for prostate cancer survivors. Nuffield Health plans to expand a version of STAMINA to breast and bowel cancer patients at 50,000 facilities by 2030.
