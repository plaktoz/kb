# What We Learned From 15 Hours of Anthropic Certification Training

**Author:** Natalia Quintero | **Published:** August 31, 2026

---

## Overview

Natalia Quintero, head of consulting at Every, completed four Anthropic certification courses to deepen the company's partnership with Anthropic. Her key takeaway: AI education today is primarily about establishing shared vocabulary, not delivering workflow playbooks.

---

## The Certification Program

To earn Anthropic certification, companies complete four courses (~10–15 hours total):

- Introduction to Agent Skills
- Building with Claude API
- Introduction to Model Context Protocol (MCP)
- Claude Code in Action

The format mirrors traditional online learning — text, short videos, and exercises.

---

## Main Findings

### Shared Definitions Over Playbooks

The courses don't map workflows or identify automation opportunities. Instead, they establish common terminology. As Quintero notes, diverging definitions of tools like MCPs between Anthropic and its partners could hinder effective implementation.

### A Stabilizing Industry

Anthropic's Opus 4.5 and OpenAI's GPT-5.5 marked a turning point — models now perform tasks reliably enough that new releases no longer upend existing workflows. This stability made certification training possible in the first place.

### Mixed Team Reactions

Responses across Every's ten participants varied widely:

- **Mike Taylor** (head of evals) found the API course overly technical and questioned its relevance for non-developers
- **Becky Isjwara** (social media) found it largely unnecessary, having already shipped apps through vibe coding alone
- **Yash Poojary** (growth engineer) immediately proposed integrating the API course into company onboarding
- **Lee Knowlton** (engineer) valued the prompt evaluation section as unusually compact and clear

### Documentation Beats Video

The team consensus: Anthropic's publicly available [documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/home) surpasses the certification courses for depth and clarity.

---

## Limitations

- Courses reference deprecated models (e.g., a Sonnet version no longer in the API)
- No role-based or experience-level customization
- No real-world workflow examples grounded in specific industries

---

## Bottom Line

The certification succeeds at building a shared foundational vocabulary and reducing intimidation around AI tools. It doesn't transform workflows or serve time-constrained executives — but given where the technology stands, that may be an inherent constraint rather than a course design failure.
