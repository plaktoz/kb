# IBM Taps Swift's 17-Bank Blockchain, Lets Banks Keep Assets In-House

IBM connected its Digital Asset Haven platform to Swift's blockchain-based shared ledger, which 17 global banks — including BNY, Citi, HSBC, UBS, Wells Fargo, ANZ, BNP Paribas, DBS, MUFG, Standard Chartered, and Lloyds — are using to pilot tokenized deposit transactions.

IBM announced two beta updates on Sept. 24 aimed at regulated financial institutions. Tom McPherson, IBM's general manager for Z and LinuxONE, noted that "tokenized and traditional assets will need to move side by side."

**Update 1 — ISO 20022 Messaging Adapter:** Allows banks to instruct tokenized transactions using familiar payment message formats rather than blockchain-specific workflows. Funds can move around the clock, including weekends, while final settlement still runs through existing systems.

**Update 2 — On-Premises Version:** Runs entirely on a client's own IBM Z or LinuxONE hardware. Software and cryptographic keys remain in-house, secured by Crypto Express hardware security modules, with formal key ceremonies producing regulatory documentation.

Swift unveiled the ledger at Sibos 2025, built on a Consensys prototype, declaring it ready for use in July after nine months of development. Digital Asset Haven originally launched in October 2025 with wallet infrastructure provider Dfns, previously available only as cloud or hybrid.

IBM shares rose roughly 2.5% to $237.19 on Sept. 25.
