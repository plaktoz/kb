# Oura's $2.2B IPO is mostly a payday for existing shareholders

Oura is launching an IPO targeting up to $2.2B, offering 50 million shares priced between $40–$44. However, existing shareholders are offloading ~36.5 million of those shares — nearly two-thirds of the total.

At the $42 midpoint, shareholders would collect roughly $1.53B while Oura itself receives only ~$567M. The biggest beneficiary is Forerunner Ventures, which is liquidating its entire 9.3% stake (~28.7M shares) for approximately $1.2B — representing nearly 80% of the shareholder-sold portion. Forerunner originally invested during Oura's 2020 Series B.

Oura's intended use of its ~$532.6M in proceeds is notable: approximately $526.4M goes toward employee share-grant tax obligations, leaving just ~$6.2M for general purposes. The company is avoiding debt and leaving its ~$372M cash reserves untouched.

On the business side, Oura's membership segment is expanding rapidly, with an 89% gross margin and revenue that more than doubled to $240.5M — about 20% of total sales. Hardware remained dominant at $974M. Oura projects ~5.7 million paying members by fiscal year-end, nearly double year-over-year.

At the top of its price range, Oura's market cap could reach $14.1B, up from an $11B private valuation in October 2025.
