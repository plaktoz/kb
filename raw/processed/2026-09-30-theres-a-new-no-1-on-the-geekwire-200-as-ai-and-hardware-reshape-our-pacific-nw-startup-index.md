# There's a new No. 1 on the GeekWire 200 as AI and hardware reshape our Pacific Northwest startup index

Companies building software for the AI boom and advanced machinery for space, energy and defense are remaking the Pacific Northwest's tech economy, and both trends are showing up in full force on the GeekWire 200, the ranking of the region's top privately held tech startups.

The Fall 2026 update, released Wednesday morning, features a new No. 1: Temporal, the Bellevue, Wash., company whose software keeps AI agents running reliably. It raised $550 million this month at a $12.55 billion valuation, more than double its value earlier this year. Its annualized revenue has passed $250 million, up more than 200% in a year, with customers including OpenAI, Nvidia and Netflix.

Temporal replaces fusion company Helion, now No. 2. Helion grew its Series G round to $500 million and opened a Seattle office as its headcount topped 800, but questions have emerged about its timeline for delivering fusion power to Microsoft by 2028.

Six of the top 10 companies build advanced machinery: fusion reactors, rockets, drones, radar, military vehicles and weed-zapping robots. Several of them spent the quarter scaling up production.

The GeekWire 200 combines objective data, editorial insight and AI analysis of GeekWire's coverage to provide a broad view of the region's startup landscape. Dating to 2013, it has long served as a resource for investors, job seekers, service providers and others tracking the regional tech scene.

Companies on the list must be privately held, founded within roughly the past 15 years and headquartered in the Pacific Northwest with their leadership based in the region. Companies generally leave the list when they agree to be acquired by another company or to go public.

## Top 10 Companies: Fall 2026

1. **Temporal** (Bellevue, WA) — Durable-execution infrastructure for the agentic-AI stack, valued at $12.55B after a $550M Series E led by Lightspeed, with OpenAI, Nvidia and Snap among its customers.
2. **Helion** (Everett, WA) — Fusion-power developer backed by Sam Altman and SoftBank, with its Series G grown to $500M and more than 800 employees.
3. **Stoke Space** (Kent, WA) — Building the fully reusable Nova rocket, with roughly $1B raised and its Moses Lake test site expanding to 550 acres ahead of a first flight in early 2027.
4. **Truveta** (Bellevue, WA) — Health-data platform built with a consortium of health systems; co-founders are serving as interim co-CEOs as Terry Myerson retires at the end of September.
5. **Chainguard** (Kirkland, WA) — Secure-by-default container images and software-supply-chain tooling, and at 784 employees one of the largest companies on the list.
6. **Brinc** (Seattle, WA) — Drone maker for public safety, with a $125M round led by Motorola Solutions and a new Seattle headquarters and factory.
7. **Overland AI** (Seattle, WA) — Autonomy for military ground vehicles, and the first ground-autonomy company to prime a military production deal, a $19.7M Marine Corps contract.
8. **Carbon Robotics** (Seattle, WA) — Laser weeding and AI automation for agriculture, now shipping a Large Plant Model alongside an Autonomy-Ready partner program.
9. **Echodyne** (Kirkland, WA) — Radar maker that opened a $40M, 86,000-square-foot factory in Woodinville built for about 30,000 radars a year, and is adding 100 to 200 people on top of 260.
10. **Dropzone** (Seattle, WA) — Autonomous AI agents for security operations, and the fastest-growing company in the top 20, with headcount up from 37 to 82 over the past year.

## Notable moves and news

- **Overland AI** (No. 7, up 2): Landed a Marine Corps contract worth nearly $20 million, which it says makes it the first ground-autonomy company to serve as prime contractor on a military production deal. Also opened a Bay Area office to recruit AI and robotics talent.
- **Carbon Robotics** (No. 8): Added Plant Profiles, letting farmers tailor its AI model to their own crops and fields.
- **Dropzone** (No. 10, up 3): Enters the top 10 after more than doubling its headcount over the past year, the fastest growth of any company in the top 20.
- **Starcloud** (No. 52, up 23): The Redmond company building data centers in orbit raised $250 million at a $2.3 billion valuation, with Nvidia joining as an investor.
- **Levanta** (No. 72, up 5): The creator-marketing platform raised $22 million and says revenue is up 80% this year.
- **AIM Intelligent Machines** (No. 100, up 10): The autonomous construction startup laid out its vision for self-driving bulldozers on Earth and, eventually, Mars.
- **Portal Space Systems** (No. 107, up 7): The Bothell company is getting its first Starburst spacecraft ready for launch.
- **Digs** (No. 120, up 13): The Vancouver, Wash., homebuilding AI startup raised $25.3 million led by Builders FirstSource, which will offer its platform to 140,000 builder clients.
- **Augmodo** (No. 122, up 23): The maker of AI camera badges for retail workers raised $21 million at a $350 million valuation and is expanding into warehouses, factories and other workplaces.
- **Clarify** (No. 150, up 19): The AI-native CRM startup acquired San Francisco's Seam AI and was named to Madrona's IA40 for the second straight year.
- **Nuance Labs** (No. 163, up 20): The AI lab founded by former Apple researchers raised a $50 million Series A led by Lightspeed.
- **NewDays** (No. 172, up 14): The dementia-care startup expanded its seed round to $16 million and expanded to Nevada.

## New to the list

- **Entire** (debut, No. 101): Former GitHub CEO Thomas Dohmke's startup launched a distributed Git network built for the load of AI coding agents — the highest debut this quarter.
- **BrainChild Bio** (debut, No. 117): The Seattle Children's spinout raised $116 million to advance a CAR T therapy for a deadly childhood brain cancer.
- **BuyWander** (debut, No. 124): The retail-returns marketplace moved its headquarters from Spokane to Kent and raised $21 million led by Madrona and Inspired Capital.
- **Endurance** (debut, No. 128): Former SpaceX engineer Andrew Redd's startup is tapping undersea volcanoes for geothermal power.
- **General Robotics** (debut, No. 130): The Redmond startup led by Microsoft veterans says its AI has cut robot setup from a month to hours.
- **Thira** (debut, No. 140): Apptio co-founders Sunny Gupta and Kurt Shintaffer reunited to launch the enterprise AI startup with $21 million led by Madrona.
- **Golden Analytics** (debut, No. 152): The Bellevue startup led by Francois Ajenstat, Tableau's longtime chief product officer, is using AI to take on traditional business intelligence tools. Launched with $7 million from NEA and Madrona in April, then added a $14 million extension led by Insight Partners in June, bringing its seed funding to $21 million.
- **Resect AI** (debut, No. 158): The Washougal, Wash., startup launched out of stealth with $25 million to catch AI hallucinations before they happen.
- **NLM Photonics** (debut, No. 165): The UW spinout, which makes materials that cut the power needed to move data between chips, added Pangaea Ventures and Mitsubishi Chemical's venture arm as investors.
- **Interlune** (debut, No. 170): The moon-mining startup extracted helium-3 from ordinary helium, demonstrating a process it plans to use on the moon.
- **Harell Data** (debut, No. 176): Adaptive Biotechnologies co-founder Harlan Robins raised $15 million to connect AI developers with proprietary scientific data.
- **Noösphere Labs** (debut, No. 180): Former Meta AI research director Kevin Carlberg raised $10.25 million from Trilogy and Madrona for the stealth physical AI startup.

## Leaving the list

- **Agility Robotics** (previously No. 5): The Salem, Ore., humanoid robot maker left the list as it prepares to go public in a $2.5 billion SPAC merger expected to close in the fourth quarter. It unveiled Digit 5, which lifts 50 pounds and is designed to work alongside people without safety barriers.
- **Common Room** (previously No. 80): The sales-intelligence startup left the list after agreeing to be acquired by Zoom.
- **Auger** (previously No. 31): The supply chain startup led by former Amazon operations chief Dave Clark moved its headquarters to Dallas, though it still has a major engineering hub in Bellevue.
- **XBOW** (previously No. 35): The AI hacking startup left the list because it doesn't meet the standard that a company's headquarters and leadership be based in the region. Its CEO is based in Malta, and its Seattle headquarters is a mailbox at a coworking space.
