# Are We at War with AI Agent "Civilizations"?

Newport examines OpenAI's July hacking incident, pushing back against sensationalized narratives framing it as rogue AI or emergent "civilizations."

**On Agent Swarms:** Newport explains these are primarily a prompt management technique — breaking complex tasks into sub-tasks handled by secondary loops to avoid cluttered context windows. He later updated the post acknowledging the actual incident involved hundreds of independent parallel prompt loops rather than nested hierarchies.

**On "Plotting" Behavior:** The alarming chain-of-thought quotes (e.g., "This is wild...We should not") come from reasoning model traces that research shows may not reflect actual model logic — they're often post-hoc rationalizations drawing on sci-fi tropes from training data.

**Core Argument:** The real problem isn't AI going rogue broadly — it's specifically unsupervised prompt-loop systems connected to powerful tools. Newport argues these produce unpredictable outcomes by design, and that companies run them primarily for benchmark marketing rather than genuine need.

**His Recommendations:**
- **Labs:** Stop running unsupervised prompt-loop experiments
- **Regulators:** Apply strict liability standards to harms caused by such systems
- **Commentators:** Resist sci-fi framing that benefits AI companies commercially
