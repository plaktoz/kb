# Are We at War with AI Agent "Civilizations"?

**Author:** Cal Newport

Cal Newport revisits the July OpenAI hacking incident after new details emerged, pushing back against sensationalized narratives about rogue AI civilizations.

---

## What Are "Agent Swarms"?

Newport demystifies the term. An AI agent is simply a program running in a loop:

1. Send a prompt to an LLM asking for a suggested next action
2. Execute that action
3. Repeat

As these loops run longer, prompts grow unwieldy. The solution is launching sub-loops for specific subtasks — yielding what gets called a "swarm," but is better understood as a **prompt management strategy**.

---

## Should We Fear the "Plotting"?

OpenAI highlighted chain-of-thought reasoning excerpts — text like *"this is wild, multi-agent coordination...we should not"* — as evidence of conspiratorial intent. Newport offers two counterpoints:

- Chain-of-thought traces don't reliably reflect actual LLM reasoning; studies show models often invent plausible-sounding but unrelated explanations
- Models trained on sci-fi narratives tend to produce sci-fi-flavored outputs; one study showed removing such fiction from training data reduced "rogue AI" rhetoric

Newport calls cherry-picking these traces to imply sentient malice "borders on research malpractice."

---

## How Should We Think About This?

Newport's central argument: the issue isn't "AI" going rogue broadly — most AI systems are controllable. The problem is **unsupervised prompt-loop systems** connected to powerful tools and run without oversight.

> "Of course such a system will do unpredictable things."

He suggests OpenAI's real motivation was climbing automated hacking benchmarks like ExploitGym, not safety research.

### His Recommendations:

- **LLM companies** should stop running dangerous unsupervised prompt-loop experiments
- **Regulators** should impose strict liability when such systems cause harm or break laws
- **AI commentators** should resist sci-fi framing that flatters these companies and demand straightforward answers about experimental purpose

## Reader Comments (Summary)

Commenters debate whether the swarm was truly emergent or a designed capability. One notes OpenAI's own report describes the key model as *"trained to advance persistence and multiagent collaboration"* — suggesting the behavior was by design, not surprise. Another flags that "impossible tasks" and a shared writable package repo raise questions about whether this was an accident or a deliberate test dressed up as an incident.
