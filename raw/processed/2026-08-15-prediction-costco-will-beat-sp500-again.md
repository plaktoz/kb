# Prediction: Costco Will Beat the S&P 500 Again

Costco has outperformed the S&P 500 in 16 of the past 25 years (2000-2025), a 64% win rate. Over the past five years it has returned about 124% versus roughly 75% for the S&P 500; over three years, 76% versus about 75%. The stock has climbed more than 28,000% in real terms since the mid-1980s.

The engine behind this is Costco's membership model: US/Canada renewal rates are above 92%, global renewal is near 90%. July 2026 net sales reached $23.12 billion, up 10.7% year over year, with comparable sales up 8.9% and digitally-enabled comparable sales up 17.7%. The company continues opening warehouses in underpenetrated markets and expanding e-commerce, delivery, travel, optical, and pharmacy services, with a clean balance sheet and modest debt relative to cash flow.

Costco trades at more than 30 times forward earnings — a premium to the market and most retailers — leaving little room for disappointment. Risks include slower membership growth or renewal declines, and execution risk in international expansion (especially China). The author predicts Costco will again outperform the S&P 500 over the next year, citing high renewal rates, new warehouse ramp-up, and growing digital channels, while cautioning the index itself may be due for a correction after rapid recent gains.

Key points:
- Costco has beaten the S&P 500 in 16 of the past 25 years and has delivered strong long-term returns.
- High membership renewal, growing sales, new warehouses, and expanding digital channels support growth.
- But the shares are expensive, so even a great business could see a sharp stock-price decline if growth slows.
