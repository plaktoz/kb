# FDA Approves Second Blood Test to Help Close Colorectal Cancer Screening Gap

Colorectal cancer is the second leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide and the top cancer killer among adults under 50. Despite recommendations that average-risk adults 45+ undergo regular screening, roughly 60 million eligible Americans remain unscreened.

The FDA approved Freenome's **SimpleScreen CRC** blood test in July 2026 — the second such blood-based test approved, following the Shield test in 2024. In the large PREEMPT CRC clinical study (48,000+ participants), SimpleScreen correctly identified about **8 in 10 colorectal cancers** and returned negative results for approximately **9 in 10** people without cancer or advanced precancerous lesions.

The test detects CpG methylation patterns — DNA changes that emerge early in cancer development. Results are available in roughly two weeks, and a positive result requires follow-up colonoscopy.

## Key Context

Common barriers to screening include embarrassment, preparation burdens, fear, cost, and inconvenience. Blood tests lower these barriers, and recent data suggests adherence to blood-based screening could reach 95% in real-world settings.

However, both the American Gastroenterological Association and the American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy note that blood tests are "better than no screening" but less effective than colonoscopy or stool-based tests. They are recommended primarily for individuals unwilling to complete preferred screening methods.

Paul Limburg, MD, MPH (Abbott Cancer Diagnostics) emphasized that "screening decisions should be made between patients and their healthcare providers" and that "CRC screening is not one-size-fits-all."

The American Cancer Society's updated guidelines now include blood-based tests as an option for those who decline preferred screening approaches.
