# Mini-strokes linked to 34% higher long-term dementia risk in new study

A new observational study published in *Neurology* examined long-term dementia risk following transient ischemic attacks (TIAs), commonly called mini-strokes — temporary brain blood flow blockages that cause stroke-like symptoms.

**Key findings from the study of 110,000+ participants:**
- ~19% of TIA patients developed dementia during ~7 years of follow-up, vs. 15.2% of matched controls
- Overall dementia risk was **34% higher** in TIA patients vs. those without stroke history
- Risk was **75% higher in the first year**, but remained elevated across 20 years
- After ~5 years, dementia rates converged with those of full-stroke patients
- Dementia occurred roughly **three times more often** than a subsequent stroke in TIA patients

Study author Raed Joundi noted the elevated risk "did not disappear after that early period but remained elevated over the long term."

Vascular surgeon Christopher Yi, unaffiliated with the study, emphasized the need to consider "long-term risk of cognitive decline," while cautioning that TIAs may reflect underlying vascular disease rather than directly causing dementia.

Both experts stressed that a TIA should never be dismissed simply because symptoms resolve.
