# How I Escaped AI Autopilot

**Author:** Katie Parrott
**Publication:** Working Overtime (Every)
**Date:** April 20, 2026

---

Parrott opens with a jarring personal story: she unknowingly redid a completed client assignment because AI-assisted work had become so automatic that her brain never formed a lasting memory of the first draft.

She argues this isn't simple forgetfulness — it's cognitive outsourcing. When AI handles judgment calls, the brain stops encoding the work meaningfully.

## The Research Behind It

Studies on automated cockpits found pilots sometimes trusted incorrect automated recommendations even when instruments said otherwise. A broader 2010 review concluded: the more reliable a system becomes, "the more likely humans are to let it pass unchecked."

Fluency compounds the problem. A 1999 psychology study showed easy-to-read text was rated *more true* than identical hard-to-read text — same words, different perceived accuracy. AI output is maximally fluent, which triggers false confidence.

## Why Review Often Fails

"The feeling of having reviewed is easy to produce. The act of reviewing is harder." Parrott notes that 2021 research found "cognitive forcing functions" — requiring users to form judgments *before* seeing AI output — reduced overreliance, but users rated those interventions poorly. Friction works; people dislike it anyway.

## Three Countermeasures

1. **Think before you look** — Write a rough position (even five bullets) before prompting the AI, creating something to argue against rather than accept.
2. **Build in a gap** — Separate generation from review across time or device to let attention reset.
3. **Explain each acceptance** — Before keeping AI-generated language, write one sentence justifying *why* it fits. A 2026 study found this cut mistaken acceptances roughly in half.

## Closing Argument

"The central risk of AI-assisted work is not the machine thinking for you. It is the machine making it feel as if you already thought." Parrott calls this also a design problem — tools should build in friction, make AI provenance visible, and treat human judgment as a genuine workflow stage, not a rubber stamp.
