# Should You Buy Microsoft Stock Now That It's Back Within 6% of Its Record?

Microsoft (MSFT) fell roughly 35% from its October 2025 record close of $542.07 to a June 25 low of $352.83, then rebounded roughly 46%, trading near $516 — approximately 5% below the record.

Most of the recovery came in one week following the fiscal Q4 earnings report on July 29, when Azure growth accelerated to 43% year-over-year, beating management's 39–40% guidance. CFO Amy Hood attributed this to efficiency gains and faster capacity delivery that "was quickly monetized," and guided for roughly 45% Azure growth next quarter.

Capital spending remains elevated — rising 69% year-over-year to $41 billion in Q4 — though the stated full-year 2026 figure was revised down to roughly $175 billion due to an accounting change, not an actual reduction. Spending is expected to rise again in fiscal 2027.

Valuation has improved: shares trade at roughly 29x earnings versus roughly 40x at the October peak, aided by 32% EPS growth. However, free cash flow fell roughly 23% in Q4 and declined for the full fiscal year, with Hood's goal for 2027 simply to "remain free cash flow positive."

The author views MSFT as approximately fairly valued now, noting the stock already prices in much of the good news. He would consider buying if Azure hits roughly 45% growth and free cash flow resumes rising.
