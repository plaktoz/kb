# How Long Should a Focus Block Be? The Data Behind Optimal Length

## Match Block Length to Task Type

- Admin, email, expenses: 20-30 minute blocks. Long enough to make progress, short enough to prevent boredom-driven distraction.
- Writing, coding, design: 60-90 minute blocks. Enough runway to enter deep focus and produce meaningful output.
- Strategy, research, complex problem-solving: 90-120 minute blocks. Requires sustained cognitive load.
- Reading, learning: 45-60 minute blocks. Beyond this, retention drops.
- Reactive work (support, sales calls): 15-25 minute blocks between meetings.

## The Ramp-Up Reality

Blocks under 30 minutes rarely reach deep flow. Fine for admin, insufficient for creative or strategic work.

Every interruption resets the ramp-up. Which is why context-switching is so devastating.

## The Personal Testing Framework

- Monday: 25-minute focus blocks with 5-minute breaks.
- Tuesday: 50-minute blocks with 10-minute breaks.
- Wednesday: 90-minute blocks with 20-minute breaks.
- Thursday: 2-hour blocks with 30-minute breaks.
- Friday: Mixed — short blocks for admin, long blocks for deep work.

## The Break Length Trap

- Walking (ideally outside)
- Drinking water
- Standing and stretching
- A conversation not about work

Rest, not another cognitive load

## The Simple Default

- One 90-minute block first thing in the morning for your highest-priority work.
- One 60-minute block in the afternoon for a second priority.
- Two 25-minute admin blocks for email/Slack (mid-morning and end of day).

## The Environment That Makes Long Blocks Possible

Phone in another room. Not silenced, not face-down — physically not on your desk. Research from the University of Texas found that even a silenced phone within eyeline measurably reduces cognitive capacity.

Full-screen the work tool. Close every browser tab and app you don't need. Visual clutter costs attention even when you're not looking at it.

One monitor if possible. Multi-monitor setups quietly encourage micro-switches between the "primary" and "reference" screens. During deep blocks, unplug the second one.

Physical cue. Headphones on, door closed, a specific playlist, or a Post-it that says "Focus." Signals to your brain (and to teammates) that this window is different. We have always done this in our office. No earbuds; you can bother me. Earbuds, approach with caution. Headphones–don't even think about talking to me. We've done this for years.

A pre-committed opening move. Decide the first action before the block starts. Nothing kills a focus block faster than opening it with "what should I work on?" Calendar your schedule and stick with your plan.
