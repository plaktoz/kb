# My Friends All Hate AI; I Just Joined an AI Startup

Rachel Thomas, co-founder of fast.ai, announces her return to AI work after burning out and pursuing a microbiology degree. She acknowledges the legitimate frustrations people have with AI while explaining why she rejoined the field anyway.

## The Problems She Acknowledges

- AI is flooding the internet with low-quality content ("slop")
- Students submit AI-generated essays; professors' curricula are disrupted
- Most AI education products chase gameable metrics rather than genuine learning
- AI lab executives make "overhyped" capability claims
- Skills like reading and critical thinking atrophy when outsourced to AI

## Her Background

- Co-founded fast.ai in 2016 with Jeremy Howard
- Founded the Center for Applied Data Ethics at USF
- Left the field in 2023, earned an MS in Microbiology-Immunology
- Rejoined Answer.AI (fast.ai's evolution) to work on SolveIt

## Why She Returned

She argues AI "is a genuinely useful technology" beneath the hype, and that the major labs' vision isn't the only possible one. SolveIt, named after George Pólya's 1945 problem-solving framework, is designed so users directly edit AI responses and remain in control — the opposite of an overly automated chatbot.

## Core Argument

> "OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and xAI don't own AI."

Thousands work on AI outside the dominant narrative. Her goal: use AI in ways that protect human creativity and autonomy.
