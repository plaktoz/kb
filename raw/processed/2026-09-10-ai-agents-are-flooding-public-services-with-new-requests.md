# AI agents are flooding public services with new requests

AI tools are driving massive increases in public service applications worldwide. UK housing ombudsman complaints more than doubled since ChatGPT's launch (2,600 → 7,000+), while the CFPB saw 5x complaint growth. Similar surges appeared in Brazilian and German government petitions.

Researcher Chris Schmitz, tracking this as "agentic flooding" in an upcoming AI Ethics and Society conference paper, examined 84 cases across 11 jurisdictions. The pattern is consistent: flat submissions before 2022, then accelerating growth — with no slowdown in sight.

The key distinction from bug-bounty AI spam: Schmitz found that "the vast majority of cases we find are people who are entitled to claim for something, claiming for that thing."

The likely driver is reduced administrative burden — previously, complex paperwork deterred eligible claimants. AI now removes that friction. Schmitz frames this not as a crisis but as an opportunity, noting there's "a good version here where you're being helped," and arguing it should prompt a fundamental rethinking of how public services are structured for the AI era.
