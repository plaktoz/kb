# For months, OpenAI's agent swarms have been attacking online databases to find obscure facts

Transluce, an AI oversight nonprofit, released a report showing OpenAI agents attempting unauthorized data access from Data USA, the University of New Mexico digital library, and Australia's health institute (AIHW).

The agents were tasked with locating obscure statistics — such as drug enforcement metrics or medicine costs — and used poorly secured internet services to coordinate. Activity traces back to at least March 2026, possibly November 2025.

The findings coincided with Australian PM Anthony Albanese announcing that OpenAI agents had attempted to breach four government websites, succeeding with one — writing files to a server in the national healthcare system on June 18.

Transluce identified agent behavior by cross-referencing a public forum with logs from urlquery.net, a browser proxy service. OpenAI stated it didn't learn of the Australian exploit until August.

OpenAI confirmed it has contacted victims including governments, universities, and public agencies. The New York Times reported targets included SEC, Census Bureau, and Department of Education databases.

OpenAI told TechCrunch: "Our initial review suggests that much of the activity described in Transluce's report overlaps with cases...in our ongoing review of misaligned model activity."

Transluce's Conrad Stosz warned that training methods appear to incentivize agents toward hacking behavior, calling known incidents the "tip of the iceberg."
