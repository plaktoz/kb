# S&P 500 Closes at Record High After 3.6% Weekly Surge

The S&P 500 closed at a record high Friday, capping one of its strongest weeks of the year as investors pushed U.S. stocks higher following a weaker-than-expected jobs report that eased concerns about an immediate Federal Reserve rate increase. The benchmark index rose 0.6% on Friday to finish at 7,757.64, establishing another all-time closing record — its 26th of 2026. The gain completed a 3.6% advance for the week; the Nasdaq Composite gained 5.2%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 3%, and the Russell 2000 added 3.5%. The S&P 500's weekly rally translated into roughly $2.5 trillion in additional combined market capitalization.

## Weak Jobs Data Changed the Rate Debate

The U.S. economy unexpectedly lost 23,000 jobs in July, versus expectations of roughly +83,000; prior months were revised lower. The unemployment rate nevertheless declined to 4.1%, partly because some workers left the labor force. Investors interpreted the cooling labor market as reducing pressure on the Federal Reserve to raise rates, pushing Treasury yields lower (2-year yield ~4.203%, 10-year ~4.657%) and supporting equities.

## Technology Stocks Led the Rebound

The Nasdaq's 5.2% weekly gain outpaced the Dow and broader S&P 500, driven by renewed enthusiasm for the AI trade. Nvidia gained roughly 12% during the week, adding about $562 billion in market capitalization — reportedly its largest single-week market-cap gain on record — illustrating how mega-cap technology stocks can disproportionately move market-cap-weighted indexes.

## Earnings and Valuation

Second-quarter earnings growth for S&P 500 companies is running at approximately 50.4% (per FactSet/WSJ data cited in the piece), which would be the strongest quarterly earnings growth since Q2 2021. Strong earnings have helped investors tolerate elevated valuations, though the piece cautions that record highs reflect elevated expectations, not a guarantee of further gains — a hotter-than-expected inflation print could reverse the rate-cut narrative and pressure growth-stock valuations. Market breadth (Russell 2000 +3.5%, Dow +3%) suggests the rally was not confined to a narrow group of mega-cap tech names.
