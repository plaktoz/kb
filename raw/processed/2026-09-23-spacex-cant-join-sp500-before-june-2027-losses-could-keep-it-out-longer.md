# SpaceX Can't Join the S&P 500 Before June 2027 -- and Its Losses Could Keep It Out Longer

SpaceX (SPCX) joined the Nasdaq-100 quickly after its IPO, but the S&P 500 presents a harder path. On June 4, S&P Dow Jones Indices rejected proposals to fast-track megacap IPOs, preserving three existing requirements:

1. **Seasoning:** At least 12 months of trading on an eligible exchange — meaning SpaceX can't qualify before June 2027.
2. **Profitability:** Positive GAAP net income in the most recent quarter *and* across the four most recent quarters combined.
3. **Float:** At least 10% of shares available to the public.

The float issue is resolving itself on a published schedule, with ~328 million locked-up shares becoming eligible around September 24, and more following by December.

The harder obstacle is profitability. SpaceX posted a $541 million net loss in Q2 2026, with the trailing four-quarter combined loss exceeding $8 billion — largely due to a $4.3 billion Q1 loss that included ~$1.5 billion in one-time debt-retirement charges. That Q1 figure will eventually roll out of the four-quarter window.

There are improving trends: operating losses shrank to $143 million in Q2 (vs. $970 million a year prior), and revenue growth accelerated to 92% year-over-year. SpaceX was profitable in 2024, earning $791 million.

However, $28.5 billion in capital expenditures in H1 2026 — mostly on AI data centers — will convert into rising depreciation charges. D&A hit $2.8 billion in Q2, up from $1.5 billion a year earlier. A $25 billion bond issuance in June adds $629 million in quarterly interest expense.

The article concludes that quarterly GAAP net income — not the June 2027 calendar date — is the metric investors should monitor, and the author would not buy the stock purely in anticipation of forced index-buying.
