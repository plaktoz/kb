# What Happens When You Invest Just $100 a Month in the S&P 500 for 20 Years?

Many assume significant capital is required to build wealth through investing, but consistent small contributions can be surprisingly powerful.

The S&P 500 has delivered roughly 10% average annual returns over the past century. Using that benchmark, someone contributing $100 monthly — for example via the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO) — starting from zero could accumulate approximately $76,000 over 20 years. Of that, only $24,000 represents actual contributions; the remaining ~$52,000 comes from compounded gains.

The article emphasizes that consistency matters more than return rate. Continuing to invest during market downturns is particularly valuable, as "you're buying shares at a discount," potentially improving long-term outcomes compared to pausing contributions when markets decline.

The core takeaway: compounding, not the monthly deposit itself, drives the bulk of long-term portfolio growth.
