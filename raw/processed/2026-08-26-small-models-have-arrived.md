# Small Models Have Arrived

The author has been experimenting with gpt-5.6-luna, finding it fast (~100 tps) and surprisingly affordable. Even complex research tasks involving thousands of emails cost "tens of cents."

## The Consumer AI Problem

A question circulating among investors: why aren't more consumer AI companies emerging? The answer comes down to token costs. Traditional consumer apps follow a familiar playbook — cheap infrastructure, viral growth, ad monetization. Adding AI breaks that model by introducing real per-request inference costs.

The author uses a personal benchmark: building a personalized daily news site by researching the user's interests and pulling from HN, Reddit, Twitter, etc. With previous "Sonnet-class" models, this cost ~$1 per run — unworkable at consumer subscription prices. With luna, results are "pretty decent" at ~$0.10.

## Two Types of Work

Co-founder Peter Reinhardt (Segment, Charm Industrial, Revoy) distinguishes between:

1. **"IQ 180" work** — rare, breakthrough-level problem solving
2. **"Token spewer" work** — responsive, high-volume blocking and tackling across many fronts

Peter estimates ~95% of his own work falls into category 2. The author argues this mirrors broader hiring patterns — most organizational effort skews toward the "fast/cheap/good-enough" archetype.

## Outlook

Frontier models will remain essential for novel discovery and deep engineering. But demand for capable, cheap, fast models is poised to accelerate — provided the ecosystem catches up with necessary tooling around safety, permissions, and prompt injection.
