# Taking the stairs could protect your heart and help you live longer

New research from the University of East Anglia and the Norfolk and Norwich University Hospital suggests something as simple as choosing the stairs could contribute to a longer life. Frequent stair climbers were 39% less likely to die from cardiovascular disease and 24% less likely to die from any cause than people who climbed stairs less often, and also had lower risks of heart attack, stroke and heart failure.

Researchers reviewed nearly 1,900 studies before analyzing data from more than 480,000 people across nine high-quality studies, following participants for a median of 14 years. The group included both healthy individuals and people with a prior history of heart problems, ranging in age from 35 to 84, with 53% women.

"Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death worldwide — with cases nearly doubling between 1990 and 2019," said Prof Vassilios Vassiliou of UEA's Norwich Medical School. "But it is largely preventable through a healthy lifestyle... Taking the stairs is a practical and often overlooked way to build physical activity into daily life."

Dr. Sophie Paddock, a cardiology specialist registrar at NNUH, said: "After pooling results from over 450,000 participants, we found a consistent association between stair climbing and lower risk of both cardiovascular death and overall mortality. Unlike going to the gym or doing a workout, climbing the stairs is something you can easily fit into your day... Even brief bursts of physical activity have beneficial health impacts."

One large cohort study included in the analysis indicated climbing about six flights of stairs each day might provide the greatest benefit, though researchers say more work is needed to determine the ideal "dose." The researchers note stair climbing may not be appropriate for everyone, especially people with mobility limitations or joint conditions, and say future studies should use wearable technology to track activity more precisely. The study, "Evaluating the Impact of Stair Climbing on Cardiovascular Risk Reduction: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis," is published in the American Journal of Cardiovascular Drugs.
