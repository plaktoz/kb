# The sell off deepens: Nvidia slumps, as debt costs rise

The November plunge continues on Wednesday and US stocks are falling rapidly. The Nasdaq is down by more than 4%, and the S&P 500 is falling 2.5%, as Nvidia slumped more than 8% at one stage. The stock is now down more than 13% on the year and is lower on the week, as Monday’s post Jensen Huang rally unravels.

## Low liquidity exacerbates volatility

So, it turns out that Huang is not the trillion-Dollar man that we thought, and the market has unwound Monday’s stunning and unexpected rally. But, what is driving the sell off, and why is all risk falling together? Treasuries and other sovereign bonds are surging, but aside from that, there are few harbours in this storm. A holiday-shortened week for Thanksgiving is not helping matters as these moves are likely exacerbated by low levels of liquidity.

## Tariffs and AI

The key change that markets are digesting on Wednesday is confirmation that the US will impose new tariffs, including a 20% levy on China, which could rise to 40% without a trade deal. Tariffs on Vietnam are also expected, along with the EU, Japan and South Korea. Mexico and Norway are excluded. These tariffs are not expected to come into force until January, but they are adding to the negative sentiment in markets.

## Jensen Huang: A poison chalice for Nvidia stock

However, we think that AI fears are the main reason why markets are selling off today. There are fears about what the return of Jensen Huang at Nvidia, seemingly at the behest of the President, says about corporate governance at one of the US’s biggest firms.

Circular financing concerns are amplified now that Huang is back, and the prospect of more scrutiny and tighter regulation are adding to fears. If substantial revenues at Nvidia were generated via Chinese conduits, the latest round of export controls and tariffs could knock Nvidia profits, and today’s sharp sell off suggests that the market is slashing its expectations for future earnings growth at the chip giant.

## The Fed looms large

The Fed also looms large over this sell off. Even though the Fed’s John Williams suggested that the Fed may need to pivot and cut rates next year, for now, inflation pressures are high. The core PCE rose to 3.3% from 3.2%, and Q3 GDP was revised higher to 4.3% from 3.4% in the first reading. This is a massive rate of growth, and is nearly as strong as China’s, which rose at 4.5% last quarter.

Today’s US economic data tips the balance in favour of prolonged high interest rates in the US, which makes the AI infrastructure build out more expensive to fund, and adds pressure to AI stocks. Nvidia’s CDS spreads are close to as record. However, it is worth noting that Nvidia is very unlikely to default. The company reported more than $40bn of free cash flow for last quarter. Firms with this level of cash do not just collapse.

Anthropic is also down 5% today and is well below its IPO price, suggesting that this was a bad time to list an AI Lab on the stock market. We have not seen any dip buying this week, and instead the Nasdaq 100 is back at the lows from last week, suggesting that Monday’s rally was a dead cat bounce.

After a relentless sell off, Thanksgiving cannot come quickly enough.
