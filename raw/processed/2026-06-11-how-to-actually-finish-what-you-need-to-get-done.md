# How to Actually Finish What You Need to Get Done

**Publication:** HBR On Leadership (Episode 165)
**Guest:** Marc Zao-Sanders, CEO/co-founder of Filtered.com, author of *Timeboxing: The Power of Doing One Thing at a Time*

## Core Concept

Timeboxing merges a to-do list with a calendar by scheduling dedicated time blocks for specific tasks. Zao-Sanders describes it as "a calendar multiplied by the to-do list," yielding productivity gains beyond what either tool offers alone.

## How It Works

- Start each morning with a 15-minute planning session to map out the day
- Use three timebox sizes: 15, 30, and 60 minutes
- Reserve roughly 70% of your day, including personal activities like exercise and family time
- Allow slack for unexpected interruptions (~10% of plans actually change)

## Key Benefits

- **Focus:** Reduces the cognitive burden of deciding what to work on moment-to-moment
- **Record-keeping:** Your calendar becomes a log of what you accomplished
- **Collaboration:** Telling colleagues "it's timeboxed for Thursday at 3pm" replaces vague "will do" responses
- **Agency:** Zao-Sanders summarizes the entire concept in one word — *agency*

## On Distraction

When distracted, he recommends saying aloud "one thing at a time" as a mantra, then returning to the calendar's current task.
