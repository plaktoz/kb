# Workplace AI can damage productivity and trust, study finds

A new, ongoing study by Stanford University's Social Media Lab and behavioural research firm BetterUp Labs suggests workplace AI may be undermining productivity while damaging workplace relationships. 40% of US employees reported encountering "workslop" — AI-generated content that appears polished and professional but lacks the depth or accuracy needed to genuinely move a task forward — in their work lives within the last month.

Workslop is content someone else must clean up, rewrite, or verify, costing time, trust, and money. The Stanford/BetterUp study estimates that sorting through AI-generated slop costs employers approximately $186 per employee per month in lost productivity.

Beyond productivity losses, workslop is damaging professional relationships. Among those who received AI-generated content, over 50% reported feeling annoyed, more than a third said they were confused, and nearly 25% felt offended. Forty-two percent of respondents said they trusted the sender less after receiving AI-written material, and more than a third judged the sender to be less intelligent or creative than previously believed.

The flow of workslop isn't one-directional: workers send 18% of it up to managers, while 16% of AI-generated content comes from managers themselves.

Despite mixed results, employees face pressure to use AI. Accenture's 2025 fiscal report revealed employees in non-augmentable roles who fail to adapt to AI upskilling may be let go. Yet a three-month trial of Microsoft's M365 Copilot in a UK government department found no definitive evidence of improved productivity. A separate Asana study found 64% of British employees view AI agents as unreliable, and an Ipsos survey for The Guardian found British workers are concealing their AI use at work, fearing it could damage their reputation.
