# Sam Altman's OpenAI Is in Talks for a New Funding Round Valuing It at $1.2 Trillion Instead of an IPO

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has postponed the company's IPO, telling Fortune magazine that going public now would be "ill-advised." His stated concern centers on AI safety — specifically the risk that AI systems could autonomously cause serious harm to humanity. When asked whether a 10% probability of catastrophic outcomes was reasonable, Altman neither confirmed nor denied the figure, but stressed the need to treat any such risk seriously.

Meanwhile, OpenAI is reportedly in private funding discussions that would value the company above $1.2 trillion. Existing major stakeholders include Microsoft, Amazon, Nvidia, Peter Thiel, and Andreessen Horowitz — all potentially being approached again. Private institutional investors are seen as less sensitive to near-term regulatory pressures than public markets.

The delay carries a double-edged risk: retail investors may face a higher entry valuation at IPO, but if AI infrastructure investments fail to demonstrate strong returns in the interim, OpenAI's eventual public offering could suffer. The core tension is whether the postponement reflects genuine safety concerns, investor demand worries, or both.
