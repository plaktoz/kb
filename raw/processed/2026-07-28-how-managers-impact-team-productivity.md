# How Managers Impact Team Productivity

source_url: https://www.gallup.com/workplace/713033/managers-and-team-productivity.aspx

---

By Gallup, Workplace

Of all the variables that determine whether a team performs well or poorly, none is more influential than the manager. Gallup's research consistently finds that the single greatest driver of team engagement — and the team performance outcomes that follow from it — is the quality and engagement of the person leading the team.

## Why Do Managers Have Such an Outsized Effect on Team Productivity?

**KEY INSIGHT:** Gallup's research finds that 70% of the variance in team engagement is determined solely by the manager, which means manager quality is the primary driver of whether a team performs well or poorly.

Managers shape the day-to-day conditions that determine workplace engagement levels: whether employees know what is expected of them, whether they have opportunities to do what they do best, whether they receive recognition, and whether someone at work cares about their employee development.

Gallup finds that performance variability across teams within the same organization is explained less by compensation, strategy or company culture than by who manages each team.

## The Manager Engagement Crisis

**KEY INSIGHT:** Manager engagement has fallen nine percentage points since 2022 — the steepest decline of any worker category — and now sits at 22% globally.

According to Gallup's 2026 State of the Global Workplace report, the largest single-year drop occurred between 2024 and 2025, when manager engagement fell five points. The "engagement premium" managers once held over the employees they lead has essentially vanished. The decline has not been uniform — the steepest drops are among managers under 35 and female managers. In best-practice organizations, 79% of managers are engaged — nearly four times the global average.

## What High-Impact Managers Do Differently

A Gallup study across 2.5 million manager-led teams in 195 countries identified five behaviors that define highly effective managers:
- They motivate every employee with a compelling mission and vision.
- They drive outcomes with assertiveness and overcome adversity and resistance.
- They create a culture of clear accountability.
- They build relationships based on trust, open dialogue and full transparency.
- They make decisions based on productivity, not politics.

Only 31% of U.S. employees strongly agree that they feel connected to their organization's mission, down from 38% in 2021. Improving that connection can lead to a 32% reduction in turnover and a 15% improvement in productivity.

Gallup finds a threefold increase in engagement among employees who trust their leaders; those employees are 61% more likely to stay with their company. Managers who consistently focus on strengths can achieve a 60-to-1 ratio of engaged to actively disengaged employees. Only 37% of employees strongly agree their supervisor focuses on their strengths — but among that group, 61% are engaged, twice the U.S. average.

## Manager Talent Is Rare, and Most Organizations Are Getting Selection Wrong

**KEY INSIGHT:** Companies miss out on high managerial talent in 82% of their hiring decisions, most often because they promote based on tenure or past role performance, neither of which predicts manager effectiveness.

About one in 10 people possess the innate high talent required to be a great manager. Organizations that select managers based on talent see: 48% higher profitability, 22% higher productivity, 30% higher employee engagement scores, 17% higher customer engagement scores, and 19% lower turnover. Companies that systematically select the right managers can achieve 27% higher revenue per employee than average.

## The Manager's Role in AI Adoption

Gallup identifies manager-led adoption as one of the two strongest drivers of frequent AI use. As of February 2026, half of U.S. employees use AI in their role at least a few times a year, but only 13% use it daily.

- Employees who strongly agree that their manager actively supports AI use: 79% use AI frequently.
- Employees who do not strongly agree: 46% use AI frequently.

Employees whose managers actively support AI use are also 7.4 times as likely to say AI gives them more opportunities to do their best work, and 8.7 times as likely to say AI has transformed how work gets done. Yet only 21% of employees strongly agree their manager actively supports their team's AI use.

## What HR and C-Suite Leaders Can Do Right Now

- Select managers based on talent, not tenure or past performance in another role.
- Invest in coaching-focused manager development.
- Hold managers accountable for team engagement scores.
- Ensure managers are themselves engaged.

Managers who receive coaching-focused training improve their own engagement by up to 22%; their teams improve engagement by up to 18% and see 21–28% lower turnover.

## Key Stats (FAQ Summary)

- 22% of managers globally are engaged at work, down from 31% in 2022 (79% in best-practice organizations).
- Highly engaged teams achieve 14% higher productivity in production environments and 18% higher in sales organizations.
- Employees supervised by highly engaged managers are 59% more likely to be engaged themselves (the "cascade effect").
