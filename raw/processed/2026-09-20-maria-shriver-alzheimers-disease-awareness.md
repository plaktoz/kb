# Think You're Too Young to Worry About Alzheimer's? Maria Shriver Says Think Again

Maria Shriver, journalist and founder of the Women's Alzheimer's Movement at Cleveland Clinic, has partnered with Eli Lilly's Brain Health Matters campaign to highlight that women face nearly twice the Alzheimer's risk as men.

After her father's 2003 diagnosis, Shriver became a leading advocate. She emphasizes that Alzheimer's pathology begins in the brain more than 20 years before symptoms appear, making midlife brain health critical — even for people in their 30s and 40s.

Key facts:

- Up to 45% of dementia risk may be preventable through modifiable lifestyle factors (per new WHO guidelines)
- Over 100 genetic variants are linked to late-onset Alzheimer's
- The ApoE4 gene is the most studied risk factor; inheriting two copies raises risk further
- Four new FDA-cleared blood tests now aid diagnosis; three new treatments have been approved

Lifestyle strategies highlighted:

- Regular exercise
- Quality sleep
- Nutritious diet
- Cognitive training
- Social engagement

Shriver personally practices daily meditation, exercise, and Mahjong for social and cognitive stimulation.
