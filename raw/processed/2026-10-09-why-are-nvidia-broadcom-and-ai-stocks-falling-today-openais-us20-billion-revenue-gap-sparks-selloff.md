# Why Are Nvidia, Broadcom and AI Stocks Falling Today? OpenAI's US$20 Billion Revenue Gap Sparks Selloff

**Key points**

- Nvidia, Broadcom, Oracle and other AI-linked stocks fell sharply on October 8 after a new report showed OpenAI's annualized revenue was lower than some investors had assumed.
- OpenAI told investors its annualized revenue was approaching US$50 billion at the end of September, compared with roughly US$70 billion in earlier media estimates.
- The US$20 billion difference does not mean OpenAI suddenly lost US$20 billion in revenue. It largely reflects how revenue through cloud partners is reported.
- The selloff shows how sensitive highly valued AI stocks have become to any sign that revenue growth may not fully match the huge spending on chips and data centers.

Nvidia, Broadcom and other artificial intelligence stocks fell sharply on Thursday after a new report raised fresh questions about how quickly OpenAI is turning the AI boom into revenue. Nvidia (NASDAQ:NVDA) dropped more than 2%, Broadcom (NASDAQ:AVGO) fell nearly 4%, and Oracle (NYSE:ORCL) lost more than 4% during the October 8 session. Microsoft (NASDAQ: MSFT) also moved lower, while the broader Nasdaq Composite fell about 1.4%.

The catalyst was a Financial Times report that OpenAI's annualized revenue was approaching US$50 billion at the end of September. That was roughly US$20 billion below the US$70 billion figure that had appeared in earlier media reports. But the difference needs an important explanation.

## What is OpenAI's US$20 billion revenue gap?

OpenAI did not suddenly lose US$20 billion of revenue. The gap largely reflects how OpenAI and Anthropic report revenue generated through cloud partners.

Anthropic recognizes the gross value of certain marketplace sales, while OpenAI reports comparable partner revenue on a net basis. That means the US$50 billion figure is better understood as a clearer measure of OpenAI's reported annualized revenue, not a collapse in the company's business.

OpenAI also told investors that its revenue had grown more than 70% over the period covered by the new figures.

## Why are Nvidia and Broadcom falling?

The bigger issue is expectations. Nvidia, Broadcom, Oracle and other AI infrastructure companies have benefited from enormous spending on chips, networking equipment, cloud capacity and data centers. Investors are paying high valuations for many of these stocks because they expect AI demand to keep growing rapidly.

If OpenAI's reported annualized revenue is lower than investors had assumed, Wall Street may question how quickly that huge infrastructure spending can generate returns. That kind of expectations reset can trigger sharp selling in highly valued AI stocks.

Broadcom is especially exposed to custom AI chips and networking. Nvidia remains the dominant supplier of AI accelerators, while Oracle has committed heavily to expanding cloud and data-center capacity.

## But OpenAI is still growing very fast

The selloff does not mean AI demand has disappeared. An annualized revenue run rate approaching US$50 billion would still represent extraordinary scale for a company at the center of the generative AI boom.

The issue is that expectations have become extremely high. When investors are pricing in huge future growth, even a reporting difference can matter if it makes the AI buildout look less profitable than previously assumed.

## What should investors watch next?

The key question is whether AI revenue can keep growing fast enough to justify the enormous spending on chips, cloud infrastructure and data centers. Investors should watch future OpenAI revenue updates, cloud demand, AI infrastructure orders and spending plans from Nvidia, Broadcom, Oracle and Microsoft.

For now, Thursday's selloff is not a sign that the AI boom is over. It is a warning that Wall Street is becoming much more demanding about the returns behind the hundreds of billions of dollars being invested in AI infrastructure.
