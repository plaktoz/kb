# Reciprocity: Getting What You Give

The article explores **reciprocity** as a mental model drawn from physics, biology, and human behavior.

## Physics Foundation

Newton's Third Law — every action produces an equal and opposite reaction. Jet propulsion, football tackles, and gravitational forces all illustrate that "when you act on things, they act on you."

## Human Application — Norman Bethune

A Canadian surgeon who volunteered medical innovations (mobile blood transfusion units, battlefield surgery) in Spain and China at personal cost, dying at 49 from septicemia. His story illustrates that giving often returns satisfaction and meaning, even when lacking material reward.

## Volunteering Research

Studies show volunteering improves mental/physical health, self-esteem, and life satisfaction — suggesting giving produces measurable personal benefit.

## Win-Win Relationships

Evolutionary biology supports reciprocal behavior as survival-enhancing. Humans engage in *direct* reciprocity ("I help you, you help me") and *indirect* reciprocity (reputation-building or pay-it-forward).

## Historical Example

Around 1250 BCE, Egypt's Ramesses and the Hittites' Hattusili forged history's first known peace treaty — the "Eternal Treaty" — driven by mutual self-interest, demonstrating that self-interested reciprocity still produces cooperative outcomes.

## Core Takeaway

Positive reciprocal behavior compounds over time. Win-win relationships are strategically and personally superior to zero-sum interactions.
