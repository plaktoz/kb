# Nvidia Just Locked In a $279 Billion Bet on Memory Chips

Nvidia CFO Colette Kress disclosed during Q2 earnings that the company has committed $279 billion in supply obligations over the next several years — more than double the $119 billion figure from three months prior. The increase is driven primarily by memory procurement needs for Blackwell GPUs and Vera CPUs.

The spending breaks down as roughly $92 billion in the remainder of fiscal 2027, followed by ~$87B and ~$88B across fiscal 2028 and 2029. Nvidia's data center revenue hit $89 billion in Q2 FY2027, up 117% year-over-year, with a $108 billion guide for Q3.

Management flagged that gross margins — currently 75% — may dip into the "low 70%" range by fiscal year-end, largely due to rising HBM and DRAM costs.

SK Hynix and Micron Technology are the primary beneficiaries, as they are the leading qualified HBM suppliers for Nvidia's platforms. Despite this, both trade at forward P/E multiples near 6x. The author argues this undervaluation persists due to fears of an AI capex bubble and memory's historical boom-bust cycles — concerns he believes Nvidia's multi-year commitment substantially reduces.
