# LLMs Reward Expertise

**Tags:** AI

## Overview

Domain expertise is the most critical skill when working with LLMs — not generic "prompting tricks."

## Evidence from Terence Tao

Using Terence Tao's ChatGPT conversation about a counterexample to the Jacobian Conjecture as evidence, the author shows that expert users get qualitatively different results. Tao's messages are brief and targeted, he redirects the model when responses seem off, and he drives the conversation rather than following the model's lead. Crucially, none of this is replicable without deep mathematical knowledge.

## Programming Experience

The author connects this to their own programming experience: having a strong mental model of a codebase lets you push an LLM harder — questioning its suggestions, proposing simpler alternatives, and grounding prompts in specifics.

## Key Takeaways

- **Non-experts** can use LLMs as a floor — getting *something* useful
- **Experts** use LLMs as a force multiplier, steering toward better solutions
- "The human is the bottleneck, not the model" — the hard part is articulating exactly what kind of solution you want
- Domain knowledge will remain valuable even as models improve, because extracting the right output requires knowing what to ask for
