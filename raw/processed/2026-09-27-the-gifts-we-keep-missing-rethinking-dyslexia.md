# The Gifts We Keep Missing: Rethinking Dyslexia

The piece marks Dyslexia Awareness Month, exploring the theme "The Gifts We Keep Missing." One author describes an upcoming talk to pediatricians about identifying and supporting dyslexic children, noting that dyslexia affects "approximately 17% of the population" and is "neurobiologically based, and highly heritable," yet often overlooked when only deficits are considered.

The authors compare dyslexia's developmental trajectory to a slow-blooming flower, suggesting that early reading struggles can give way to flourishing strengths with proper support. They cite research and community survey data (from Dyslexic Advantage) pointing to three notable strength areas: superior three-dimensional spatial reasoning (illustrated by ocean explorer Robert Ballard's mental visualization abilities), heightened divergent creativity on tasks like alternative-uses tests, and an aptitude for "forming unusual combinations of ideas" alongside strong recognition memory.

The article criticizes what it calls a "deficit-only view of dyslexia" for causing emotional harm, particularly among twice-exceptional students who show "spiky profiles" — high verbal reasoning paired with weak reading fluency — leading to shame and withdrawal. It also notes that ADHD co-occurs with reading disabilities in "25% to 40% of cases," cautioning that ADHD treatment alone won't resolve dyslexia's core challenges. The piece closes by referencing MIT professor Catherine Drennan's comments on dyslexic visualization skills aiding scientific problem-solving, advocating for a strengths-based approach to replace stigma with resilience and self-esteem.
