# a16z Is Challenging Silicon Valley's Love for Drop-Outs by Launching a School

Andreessen Horowitz is launching **The Horowitz Andreessen Academy**, targeting high school graduates who haven't yet attended college and want to break into tech.

The San Francisco-based program offers hands-on coursework spanning technical subjects like AI research to philosophical topics such as "The Geometry of Luck." Students also work on personal projects and gain access to prominent tech industry mentors.

**Key details for the first cohort:**
- ~50 students selected based on demonstrated work (things built, shipped, or earned)
- One-year program with **no tuition**
- Students cover their own housing (school assists with placement)
- **Not an accredited institution** — no degree or transferable credits

The school has raised $42 million and is structured as a for-profit venture. By 2028, it plans to offer a two-year program with tuition described as "similar in cost to an elite private university." Students will have access to 200 mentors and speakers, including representatives from 10 founding corporate partners: Anduril, Anthropic, Coinbase, Google, Meta, Nvidia, OpenAI, Palantir, Replit, and Stripe — all of which hope to hire graduates.

The academy positions itself as a middle path between earning a traditional degree and dropping out to found a startup — conceptually blending elements of Y Combinator and the Thiel Fellowship, but with tuition, trade-focused skills, and direct hiring pipelines rather than short-term funding.
