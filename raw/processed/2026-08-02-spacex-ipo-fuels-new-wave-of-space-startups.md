# SpaceX's record IPO is helping spur a new wave of startups aiming for orbit and opening a vast new market beyond Earth

SpaceX's blockbuster IPO did more than turn Elon Musk's rocket company into one of the world's most valuable businesses — investors and founders say it is transforming space from a government-dominated industry into a supercharged startup market. The company raised about $85.7 billion in its June IPO, the largest on record, pricing shares at $135 (shares have since fallen to $110).

Global funding for space companies reached an all-time high of $7.95 billion in the first quarter, nearly double the prior quarter's $3.93 billion, according to Seraphim Space data. Investors completed 159 transactions in the quarter, bringing the trailing 12-month total to a record 654 deals.

These startups go beyond rockets and satellites. Beyond Reach Labs is building expandable solar panels for space, and Galactic Resource Utilization Space (GRU) aims to build the first hotel on the moon. Keval Desai, founder and partner at early-stage investment firm Shakti VC, said SpaceX's IPO could have as big an impact on the space industry as Amazon's 1997 listing did on e-commerce — before which there had been only a handful of e-commerce IPOs.

SpaceX's reusable rockets have cut the cost of sending mass into space by 95% since 2008. Shakti has placed roughly 10% of its portfolio in space technology over the past five years and expects that to climb to 25–30%. One portfolio company, Cosmoserve Space — founded by a former Indian Space Research Organization scientist — is building a robotic spacecraft with Venus-flytrap-inspired "petals" to capture and remove orbital debris; it conducted its first space test in July aboard India's first privately developed orbital launch vehicle.

Challenges remain: a limited talent pool for advanced space engineering, cross-border regulatory complexity, and scarce funding for space-specific startups. Still, Desai argued SpaceX's IPO will have a lasting impact, calling space "a massive total addressable market" beyond Earth.
