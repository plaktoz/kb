# Anthropic's first embedded evaluator is … Accenture?

Anthropic has named Accenture as its first embedded safety evaluator — a surprising choice given the consulting firm's distance from cutting-edge AI research. Staff from Faculty (an AI company Accenture acquired in January) will work inside Anthropic, conducting model evaluations, red-teaming, alignment assessments, and safeguard testing. Both firms plan to invest a combined minimum of $1 billion over five years.

The announcement caught markets off guard, with Accenture shares rising 8% after hours. Observers had anticipated AI safety nonprofits like METR, Redwood Research, or Apollo Research would fill this role first. Anthropic noted those organizations may still participate via pilot programs using their own funding.

Anthropic cited Accenture's practical enterprise and government deployment experience, plus its independence as a pre-existing public company, as key advantages. The lab acknowledged that "no standards yet exist for evaluators' access or communications."

Critics view the self-policing scheme as accountability evasion. Anthropic countered that evaluators "do not reduce our accountability, but help to make it more verifiable."
