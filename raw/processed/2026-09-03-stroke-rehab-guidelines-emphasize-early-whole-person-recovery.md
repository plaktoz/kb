# Stroke Rehab Guidelines Emphasize Early, Whole-Person Recovery

New 2026 guidelines from the American Stroke Association call for stroke rehabilitation to begin within 48 hours of a patient becoming medically stable, while avoiding moderate-to-high-intensity exercise in the first 24 hours.

The *2026 Guideline for Adult Stroke Rehabilitation and Recovery*, published in the journal *Stroke*, replaces 2016 recommendations and reflects a broader, whole-person approach to recovery — covering physical, cognitive, communication, and mental health needs through coordinated multidisciplinary care.

Stroke affects nearly 800,000 Americans annually and is the country's fourth-leading cause of death and a primary driver of long-term disability.

## Key Recommendations

**Early mobilization** is emphasized because neuroplasticity genes activated after stroke remain elevated for one to three months, creating a window where the brain is more receptive to learning and recovery. Guideline chair Lorie Richards, PhD, FAHA, noted: *"Early therapy takes advantage of this enhanced neural milieu."*

Rehabilitation settings vary based on stroke severity — options include inpatient rehab, skilled nursing facilities, outpatient therapy, or telehealth.

## Mental Health and Quality of Life

Approximately one in three stroke survivors experience depression, and up to a quarter develop anxiety — both of which can hinder rehabilitation progress. The guidelines recommend regular mental health screening alongside physical rehab.

The guidelines also address sleep, pain, sexuality, and relationship changes as components of full recovery. Richards noted that *"role changes that partners/spouses experience can also impact sexuality"* and that these issues, though often avoided, significantly affect quality of life.

## Long-Term and Caregiver Support

Recovery has no fixed endpoint. As stroke survival rates improve and stroke incidence rises among younger adults, rehabilitation planning increasingly must account for employment return and parenting responsibilities.

An estimated 2.7 million Americans care for stroke survivors. A 2025 systematic review found nearly 44% of caregivers experienced depression symptoms. The guidelines recommend involving caregivers early in rehabilitation planning and repeating educational information over time, since families in crisis are less able to absorb new information initially.

Richards emphasized the value of active education: having families practice hands-on care and use teach-back techniques to reinforce understanding.
