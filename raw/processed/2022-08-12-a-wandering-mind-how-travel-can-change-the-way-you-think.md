# A Wandering Mind: How Travel Can Change the Way You Think

Most vacations follow a familiar arc: excitement beforehand, enjoyment during, then a quick return to routine afterward — the experience fading like a dream. The author argues this happens because most people travel as passive observers rather than active participants.

The core idea: "Travel without participation and reflection is entertainment." By applying mental models, travel can become genuinely transformative.

## Inspiration from Mary Wollstonecraft

During a painful period in her life, Wollstonecraft traveled Scandinavia and documented the journey in her *Letters*. Rather than simply sightseeing, she questioned, compared, and reflected — observing local customs, exploring history, and drawing conclusions about her own society. Her approach yields four travel goals:

1. Actively engage with the place — observe customs, talk to locals
2. Investigate the *why* behind what you see; set aside prior assumptions
3. Notice how the journey affects you internally
4. Resist over-planning; leave room for the unexpected

## Mental Models That Amplify Travel

- **Small sample bias** — Our home environment becomes our default frame; travel widens the sample
- **Confirmation bias** — Encountering genuinely different perspectives disrupts echo chambers
- **Algebraic equivalence** — Many paths can lead to a good life; equality doesn't require sameness
- **Galilean relativity** — Distance from routine allows clearer self-observation

## Practical Tips

1. Keep a travel journal — idle moments are perfect for reflection
2. Allow for serendipity; over-planning reinforces existing biases
3. Set a deliberate intention before departing
4. Stay open to identity growth — new experiences expand who you are

Travel, approached this way, can reframe how you see both the world and yourself.
