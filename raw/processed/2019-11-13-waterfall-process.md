# Waterfall Process

## Summary

"Waterfall" describes a software development style contrasting with iterative/agile approaches. Its core characteristic is **decomposing effort into phases based on activity** (e.g., analysis → design → coding → testing) rather than by feature.

## Origins

Most trace the term to a Winston Royce paper (1970), though ironically "waterfall" never appears in it. The paper's cascade-shaped diagram became the concept's visual shorthand.

## Waterfall vs. Iterative

- **Waterfall:** "do one activity at a time for all the features"
- **Iterative:** "do all activities for one feature at a time"

## Key Criticisms

- Testing/integration land at the end — the hardest phases to predict
- Easy to falsely declare early phases "done" while work remains hidden
- No opportunity for early releases
- Forces **predictive planning**, assuming requirements stay stable — they rarely do

## Waterfall vs. Agile

These aren't simply opposites. Iterative does not equal agile. Agile requires **adaptive planning** — updating plans as learning occurs, measuring success by business value rather than adherence to an original schedule.

> "Anyone who says 'we were successful because we were on-time and on-budget' is thinking in terms of predictive planning"
