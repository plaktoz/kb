# 9 CEOs Told Us How They Manage Their Time

Ever wonder how top executives actually fill their workdays? Nine CEOs open up about their calendars, revealing surprising insights on how they prioritize direct reports, customers, and deep thinking.

Last week, *Modern CEO* explored the role of the chief executive officer, and we invited CEOs to let us know how they spend their time. Across industries spanning retail, technology, healthcare, and professional services—and among leaders at private firms and large-cap public companies—our CEO respondents' schedules reflect a high prioritization of their direct reports, senior leaders, and their customers.

Nine CEOs offered us a self-reported glimpse into their calendars. (A more rigorous approach, employed by Nitin Nohria and Michael Porter of Harvard Business School for their [groundbreaking study](https://hbr.org/2018/07/how-ceos-manage-time) on CEO time published in 2018, had executive assistants code their bosses' time in 15-minute increments, 24 hours a day, seven days a week.) We gathered responses from Constantine Alexandrakis of Russell Reynolds Associates (RRA); Kelly Cook of David's Bridal; Mary Ellen Coyne of J.Jill; Vimal Kapur of Honeywell Technologies; Raj Mehra of Sage; Brieane Olson of Pacsun; Tyler Perry of Mission North; René Saúl of Kapital; and Job van der Voort of Remote. Here's what we learned:

## Leaders get lots of love

Across almost all the respondents, direct reports and senior leadership ranked No. 1 or No. 2 for CEO attention. Brieane Olson of Pacsun, the youth-oriented apparel retailer, says that about 30 percent of her time—her biggest bucket—is spent with leadership in one-on-one sessions, weekly meetings, or strategic discussions. Tyler Perry of the consultancy Mission North says he spends about 25 percent of his time coaching direct reports and in leadership meetings. (He says he devotes another 25 percent with "new business, networking, and thought leadership.") Kelly Cook of David's Bridal, the special-occasion dress retailer, says she spends about 30 percent of her time with her direct reports and executive team, "mostly [in] 15- and 30-minute meetings, stacked back-to-back like a bridal party in a group photo."

The outlier here is Kapital CEO and cofounder René Saúl, who says 50 percent of his time goes to "engaging direct reports and their teams across every level and division of the business," describing his approach as "very hands-on."

## Customers are another top priority

Many of our CEO respondents said they prioritize time with customers—and sometimes they combine face time with employees and clients. Vimal Kapur, CEO of automation company Honeywell Technologies, says he spends about 40 percent of his time with customers and partners. "This is the biggest portion of my time, and that's by design," he says. "It includes time in the field with our sales teams, because I usually see customers with them. It keeps me honest about the business. It's also where I test our ideas before we make big decisions on products or acquisitions."

Raj Mehra, CEO of Sage, a tech platform for senior-living communities and caregivers, says he spends about 40 percent of his time with customers (the same amount of time he spends with his team). "A company built to support caregivers should be a place where its own people feel seen and valued, so I try to show it," he says. "I bring a notebook to meetings instead of my laptop, so people have my full attention."
