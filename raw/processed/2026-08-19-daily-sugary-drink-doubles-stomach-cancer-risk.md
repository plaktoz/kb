# Your Daily Sugary Drink Habit Could Double Your Risk of Stomach Cancer

A new study found that drinking at least one sugar-sweetened beverage a day — carbonated drinks, punch, lemonade, sports drinks — was associated with a 2.45x higher risk of stomach (gastric) cancer compared to drinking less than one serving a month. Artificially sweetened beverages were not associated with higher stomach cancer risk. The results were published in Gastro Hep Advances on August 17, 2026.

Researchers, led by senior author Dr. Andrew T. Chan (gastroenterologist and epidemiologist, Mass General Brigham Cancer Institute), analyzed health data from more than 112,000 people enrolled in large studies starting in 1976 and 1986, followed for many years with diet and lifestyle questionnaires. Sugar-sweetened beverages had already been linked to higher risk of colorectal, breast, and liver cancers; given that almost 65% of U.S. adults drink at least one such beverage daily, the researchers examined whether the link extended to stomach cancer, currently the fifth most common cancer globally and often diagnosed late with limited treatment options — and no standard U.S. screening test.

The exact mechanism is unclear. One surgical oncologist not involved in the study (Dr. Anton Bilchik) noted that sweetened beverage intake is linked to overweight/obesity and possibly insulin resistance, and that inflammation — a suspected driver of many cancers — could play a role, though this remains unstudied for this specific link.

Practical advice from a registered dietitian (Meridan Zerner): swap one sugary drink per day for water, sparkling water, or unsweetened iced tea; taper gradually rather than quitting abruptly (e.g., mix sparkling water into soda, shift the ratio of sweetened/unsweetened tea); and read labels for added-sugar terms like high-fructose corn syrup, cane syrup, and concentrated fruit juice, since lemonade, smoothies, and some sports drinks also count as sugar-sweetened beverages.
