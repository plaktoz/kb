# How Elon Musk Uses First Principles Thinking to Challenge Industries

First-principles thinking means breaking a problem down to fundamental, verifiably true components — rather than reasoning by analogy to existing industry practice — and rebuilding a solution from those basics. The article frames Elon Musk's approach as a three-step method: (1) question every assumption an industry holds (e.g., "transferring money takes days" led to PayPal; "electric cars must be slow" led to Tesla; "rockets can only be used once" led to SpaceX's reusable rockets); (2) break the problem down to physical/economic fundamentals rather than market conventions (e.g., asking what a rocket's raw materials actually cost, rather than what rockets have traditionally cost); (3) rebuild a solution from those fundamentals rather than incrementally improving an existing design.

Example: rather than accepting that rockets were inherently expensive, Musk asked what a rocket is made of (aluminum, titanium, copper, carbon fiber) and found the raw materials were roughly 2% of a typical rocket's price — implying the other 98% reflected inefficiency and industry convention rather than a hard cost floor. That question underpinned SpaceX's approach to reusable rockets.

The article argues first-principles thinking is mentally demanding because it requires discarding assumptions that function as cognitive shortcuts, tolerating being wrong or looking mistaken while iterating, and sustained focus over long timeframes — traits it contrasts with traditional business-school training, which emphasizes benchmarking against competitors and incremental improvement rather than questioning industry fundamentals directly.
