# Time Saving Tips: 15 Ways to Save Time Every Day

- Track where your time goes first. You can't fix what you can't see.
- Protect focus time. Interruptions and task switching quietly eat hours.
- Batch similar tasks like email, errands and cooking so you switch on only once.
- Let templates, automation and AI handle repeat work.

Your day is not too short. It is just leaking. A ping here, a quick meeting there, and suddenly it's 5 p.m. These time saving tips help you plug those leaks at work and at home.

The leaks are real. Microsoft's 2025 infinite workday report found that heavily pinged Microsoft 365 users get interrupted every 2 minutes during core hours, about 275 times a day. Almost half of employees (48%) say their work feels chaotic and fragmented.

The good news? You don't need a 25-hour day. You need a few smart habits. Below are 15 practical ways to save time every day, each with a simple "try this" step you can use today.

## Why Saving Time Feels So Hard Today

Most of us don't lose time in big chunks. We lose it in small pieces.

Messages: The average worker gets 117 emails and 153 Teams messages every weekday, according to Microsoft.

Switching: The American Psychological Association notes that switching between tasks can cost as much as 40% of your productive time.

Searching: Atlassian's State of Teams 2025 found that leaders and teams waste 25% of their time just searching for answers.

Home chores: On days they did housework in 2024, women spent 2.7 hours and men 2.3 hours on it, per the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics.

That's the problem. Here are the fixes.

## 15 Time Saving Tips for Work and Home

## 1. Track Where Your Time Actually Goes

You can't save time you can't see. Most people guess wrong about where their hours go. A simple time audit fixes that.

- Log every task for five workdays in 15- or 30-minute blocks.
- Tag entries as deep work, meetings, admin or interruptions.
- Pick the biggest time drain and fix it first.

If your team works in Microsoft 365, Timesheet 365 lets people log hours directly inside Microsoft Teams and SharePoint, so tracking isn't one more app to open.

## 2. Plan Tomorrow Tonight

Start your day without a plan and the first email decides your priorities. Spend five minutes each evening choosing tomorrow's top three tasks.

A clear plan cuts morning decision fatigue. You sit down, open your list and start, instead of spending the first hour reacting to whatever lands in your inbox.

- Write your "Big 3" before you log off.
- Put the hardest task first.
- At home, lay out clothes and pack bags the night before.

## 3. Time Block Your Calendar

A to-do list tells you what to do. A time-blocked calendar tells you when. In a Microsoft Research study, workers given protected calendar time mainly used it for focused work.

Try this:

- Block 60–90 minutes of focus time each morning.
- Turn on "Do not disturb" in Teams during that block.
- Treat the block like a meeting you can't skip.

## 4. Do Hard Tasks During High-Energy Hours

"Eat the frog" means tackling your hardest task first. Even better, do it when your energy peaks. Microsoft found that half of all meetings land between 9–11 a.m. and 1–3 p.m., which is when many people feel sharpest. Guard those hours.

- Notice when you feel most alert for one week.
- Schedule tough work in that window.
- Move filing and simple replies to low-energy hours.

## 5. Stop Multitasking and Single-Task Instead

Multitasking feels productive, but your brain is really switching back and forth. UC Irvine researcher Gloria Mark found people spend about 47 seconds on a screen before switching. It can take up to 25 minutes to return to a project after an interruption.

- Close tabs and apps you don't need right now.
- Keep a "parking lot" note for stray ideas.
- Finish one clear chunk before starting the next.

## 6. Batch Your Email and Messages

Checking email all day hands your attention to everyone else. Batching means checking at set times instead.

With 117 emails arriving on an average day, constant checking means constant switching. Two or three focused passes clear more mail in less time, and your deep work stays protected between them.

- Check email and Teams three or four times a day.
- Agree on team norms, like replying to non-urgent messages within 24 hours.
- Turn off desktop email notifications.

## 7. Use the 2-Minute Rule

Productivity expert David Allen's 2-minute rule is simple: if a task takes less than two minutes, do it now. Tracking a tiny task later takes longer than finishing it.

- Answer quick yes/no emails right away.
- Approve that timesheet or sign that form now.
- At home, hang up the coat and rinse the plate as you go.

## 8. Cut Unnecessary Meetings

Asana's research found individual contributors spent 3.7 hours a week in unproductive meetings in 2024, up 118% since 2019. Managers lost 5.8 hours a week.

- Ask: could this be a message or a shared doc?
- Default to 25- or 50-minute meetings.
- No agenda, no meeting.

## 9. Automate Repetitive Tasks

If you do the same task every week, a tool can probably do it for you. Think reminders, approvals, reports and data entry.

- List every task you repeat weekly.
- Automate one per week with Power Automate, Outlook rules or scheduled reports.
- Automate timesheet reminders so no one chases people on Friday.

Timesheet 365 runs timesheet reminders and approval workflows inside Microsoft 365, so managers approve from Teams or SharePoint instead of chasing spreadsheets.

## 10. Let AI Handle the Busywork

AI tools are now real time savers. The Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis found generative AI users saved 5.4% of their work hours, about 2.2 hours in a 40-hour week.

- Use AI to draft first versions of emails and reports.
- Ask AI to summarize long threads and meeting notes.
- Always review AI output before sending.

Timesheet 365 includes an AI agent for faster reporting and quicker timesheet entry, so you ask for the report instead of building it by hand.

## 11. Create Templates for Everything You Repeat

Writing the same email or report from scratch wastes time. Templates turn 20-minute tasks into 2-minute ones.

This is one of the easiest time saving tips to start today. Every time you write something for the second time, save it as a template. Within a month, you'll have a library that does half your writing for you.

- Save templates for common emails, updates and agendas.
- Keep a shared template library in SharePoint or OneDrive.
- At home, keep a master grocery list sorted by store aisle.

## 12. Keep Information in One Place

Atlassian also found that 56% of workers say the only way to get information is to ask someone or book a meeting. One shared home for files ends the hunt.

- Store files, notes and decisions in one SharePoint site or Teams channel.
- Use clear file names, like "Client_Project_2026-11."
- At home, set up a launch pad by the door for keys and bags.

## 13. Delegate and Share the Load

You don't have to do everything yourself. Delegating isn't lazy; it's smart.

Good delegation also grows your team. People learn new skills, and you free up hours for planning, coaching and the work only you can do.

- Ask: is this the best use of my time?
- Give clear steps, a deadline and the right tools.
- At home, assign chores and share a family calendar.

## 14. Batch Errands, Cooking and Chores

Batching works at home too. Group similar tasks so you set up once and finish fast. Classic home habits, like batch cooking and running all your errands in one trip, belong here because they really work.

One planned shopping trip with a list sorted by aisle can replace three rushed trips during the week. One cooking session can cover several dinners.

- Errands: Run them on one day each week and plan an efficient route.
- Cooking: Cook double portions and freeze half.
- Chores: Clean as you go and declutter one shelf a week.
- Laundry: Sort with a divided hamper and fold clothes right away.

## 15. Use Timers and Take Real Breaks

Breaks feel like lost time, but they protect your focus and help you avoid burnout. In a 2025 DeskTime study, top performers worked about 75 minutes, then rested for 33.

- Set a 15-minute timer to start a task you're avoiding.
- Work in focused sprints, then step away from your screen.
- Use a timer to cap social media scrolling.

## A Simple Daily Time-Saving Routine

Want to put these time saving tips into action without overthinking it? Follow this simple routine for two weeks, then adjust it to fit your job and your home life.

- Evening (5 minutes): Plan tomorrow's Big 3 and set up your launch pad by the door.
- Morning (60–90 minutes): Do your hardest task in a protected focus block with notifications off.
- Midday and afternoon: Check email and Teams at set times and use the 2-minute rule for small tasks.
- End of day (2 minutes): Log your time so next week's audit takes seconds, not hours.
- Once a week: Batch errands, prep meals and review where your hours went.

Small changes add up. Save just 20 minutes a day and you win back more than 80 hours a year.
