# What’s behind Microsoft’s surging share price

Microsoft’s share price closed at a record high Tuesday, capping off a four-month rally that followed a rocky year for the company on Wall Street.

The Redmond-based tech giant’s stock price closed at $543.45. A day earlier, it reclaimed a $4 trillion market valuation. The company’s previous record share price was set in October 2025, at $542.07, before it began an eight-month slide that wiped out about $1 trillion in market value.

During much of its 2026 fiscal year, which ran from July 1, 2025 through June 30, Microsoft fended off concerns surrounding its growing capital expenditures. Like other tech giants, Microsoft has been spending hundreds of billions of dollars on AI-related infrastructure like data centers and high-powered computer chips. The software maker has said it is on pace for $190 billion in capital expenditures this calendar year.

Coupled with slightly slower cloud computing revenue growth during the year, investors eyed the exorbitant spending skeptically, uncertain if there would be a decent return on investment for the massive AI buildout. That concern culminated in a brutal drop in June, during which Microsoft’s share price fell by 17%, its worst month on Wall Street since 2000.

But in July, Microsoft reported its 2026 fiscal year earnings, which revealed the fastest cloud computing revenue growth in four years. After almost a year of souring on Microsoft, the market turned and the company’s stock rebounded. Since July 29, when the company reported earnings, Microsoft’s stock has increased by almost 39%.

Microsoft’s surge this week, which brought its market capitalization back above $4 trillion for the first time since last October, came after the company unveiled the third generation of its custom AI chip. Microsoft said Monday that it will begin widely deploying its Maia 300 chips to data centers next year, a move that will reduce the company’s reliance on outside chipmakers like Nvidia.

In a social media post on X, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella said the company was announcing its “biggest bet yet on our own silicon.”

In a corporate blog post, Microsoft’s cloud and AI boss Scott Guthrie emphasized the added efficiency from Microsoft’s custom AI chips. Guthrie wrote that Microsoft expects Maia 300 chips to deliver 40% more tokens per megawatt compared to leading graphics processing units, effectively upping the output per computer chip while using less electricity to do so.

Thanks to Microsoft’s rally in the latter half of the year, the company’s stock price is faring better among other large tech companies and is catching up to the gains from the Nasdaq-100 index, which is loaded with tech companies. The Nasdaq index has risen 21% on the year to Microsoft’s 15%. The company’s year-to-date gains are now ahead of cloud and AI rivals Amazon and Google parent Alphabet, but are slightly behind Meta, which has grown 17% this year.

As Microsoft builds out the infrastructure layer of what it calls the AI stack — data centers and custom chips — the company has also spent the past year trying to figure out the AI angle for its software business. A main focus has been Copilot, an AI-powered assistant.

Earlier this year, as customers complained of buggy Copilot features in Microsoft’s flagship Windows operating system, the company pulled back the inundation of consumer-facing AI. Instead, Microsoft has pivoted to a Copilot “super app,” which it unveiled in September.

Microsoft reported more than 30 million paid Copilot users in July, during its earnings call, a significant jump from the previous quarter when it reported 20 million.

The company is not giving up on consumer-facing AI products. Last week, it launched new Surface personal computers, a line of products that run AI features like autonomous agents locally, instead of in the cloud — a key security feature.
