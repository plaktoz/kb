# Stair Climbing May Significantly Lower Cardiovascular Risks

The article reports on a meta-analysis published in the *American Journal of Cardiovascular Drugs* on Aug. 13, 2026, examining stair climbing's link to heart health. The review pooled data from nine studies covering 480,479 people aged 35 to 84 (53% women), including both healthy individuals and those with existing cardiovascular disease.

Key finding: stair climbing was associated with a 39% lower risk of dying from cardiovascular problems and a 24% reduced risk of dying from any cause. Regular stair climbers also showed a lower likelihood of heart attack, stroke, and heart failure compared to non-climbers.

The piece notes this is observational research, meaning it can't prove that stair climbing lowers cardiovascular and other death risks — just that an association exists. No specific daily stair-climbing target was established, but the findings support the broader idea that brief, heart-rate-raising activities can benefit cardiovascular health. The practical takeaway offered is to choose stairs over elevators when possible, since small bouts of activity can accumulate into meaningful exercise.
