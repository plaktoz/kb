# Dell's AI Server Backlog Has Ballooned to $95 Billion. Here's What a $1,000 Investment Today Could Be Worth by 2030

The article examines Dell Technologies' expanding role in AI infrastructure, driven by surging demand for servers, networking, and storage.

Dell's Infrastructure Solutions Group (ISG) posted an 89% year-over-year revenue jump in fiscal Q2 2027, reaching a record $31.8 billion, largely due to AI server demand.

Dell's AI server orders hit a record $60.9 billion in fiscal Q2 (ended July 31), while the company recognized $16.4 billion in AI server revenue that quarter — pushing its backlog to $95 billion. Over the trailing 12 months, Dell has received $131.7 billion in AI server orders. The company projects $74 billion in AI server revenue for fiscal 2027, three times its fiscal 2026 total, and notes its potential order pipeline is a multiple of the current backlog.

The piece cites Goldman Sachs' updated forecast that the AI server market could generate $1.24 trillion in revenue by 2030, up from an earlier $961 billion estimate, driven by both higher shipment volumes and a 15% increase in average selling price assumptions.

Dell's market share in AI servers has grown sharply, rising to 17% in Q1 2026 from just 5% a year earlier, fueled by enterprise and neocloud adoption. The author estimates that if Dell holds 20% share by 2030, annual AI server revenue could reach $248 billion — more than triple this year's anticipated figure.

On valuation, the article notes Dell trades at 32 times trailing earnings and a forward multiple of 21, below the Nasdaq-100's forward multiple of 24. Dell expects non-GAAP EPS to jump 148% in fiscal 2027 to $25.50, though analysts anticipate slower growth afterward — a slowdown the author considers unlikely given strong demand and pricing trends.

Using a hypothetical scenario where EPS reaches $34.62 in fiscal 2029 and grows 38% annually in fiscal 2030 and 2031, the author projects EPS could hit $65.93 by fiscal 2031. Applying a 24 times earnings multiple (matching the Nasdaq-100's forward multiple) would imply a stock price of $1,582 — nearly triple Dell's current price.

The article concludes that Dell represents a compelling opportunity for investors with $1,000 in discretionary funds, citing its potential to roughly triple in value by 2030 based on AI server growth, market share gains, and improving pricing.
