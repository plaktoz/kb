# How Universities Should Prepare Founders

Graham argues that universities already possess the tools needed to produce successful founders — they simply need to lean into them.

## Core thesis

YC partners seek founders who are skilled builders with a habit of building. The hard part of startups is product, which comes from studying CS, engineering, or biology — not management.

## Two key changes universities need

1. **Make founding feel viable** — Harvard alumni apply to YC at roughly twice the rate of Yale/Princeton peers, suggesting culture drives behavior more than innate difference.

2. **Encourage independent projects** — Projects are "the best source of knowledge, the best source of founding teams, and the best source of startup ideas." Both Microsoft and Meta originated during Harvard's reading period.

## What universities should avoid

- Entrepreneurship classes (startups must be learned by doing)
- Business plan competitions, which train founders to "think that fundraising is the essential step" rather than building products users love
- Hiring "deans of entrepreneurship" or building innovation centers

## Key insight

The optimal university startup strategy "looks too quiet" — deep technical education plus unstructured time. No new infrastructure needed.
