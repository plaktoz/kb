# Dow Rallies 500 Points Friday, but Weekly Losing Streak Continues

The S&P 500 rose Friday as investors stabilized after a sharp sell-off fueled by rising Treasury yields. The S&P 500 gained 0.43% to 7,674.37, the Nasdaq Composite advanced 0.43% to 26,180.45, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average outperformed, climbing 517.80 points (0.98%) to 53,277.01, supported by healthcare stocks including Merck and Johnson & Johnson.

Financial stocks also helped lift the market, with crypto-related companies posting notable gains as bitcoin recorded a 22% weekly advance — Robinhood surged nearly 14% and Coinbase gained 8%. The materials sector rose 2% during the session.

Friday's rebound followed a weak session where stocks came under pressure as Treasury yields resumed their climb, even as the government moved to ease stress in the Treasury market; longer-dated bonds stayed under pressure amid concern that elevated oil prices could fuel inflation.

For the week overall, the picture remained negative: the S&P 500 fell 1.4%, the Nasdaq declined 2%, ending three consecutive weeks of gains for both indexes, and the Dow lost 0.9% for its second straight weekly decline. Weakness extended globally, with the MSCI All Country World Index falling nearly 1% for the week. Markets now turn to Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech for clues on interest rates, the bond market, and central bank independence.
