# Research Highlights of the Month – August 2026

**Road traffic noise linked to higher Parkinson's disease risk.** A Danish nationwide prospective cohort study investigated the association between long-term residential exposure to road traffic noise and incident Parkinson's disease (PD) in 3,103,577 participants (mean age 50.9 years, 51.2% female). Over follow-up from 2000 to 2017, 20,587 participants developed incident PD. In models adjusted for individual- and area-level sociodemographics, air pollution, and green space, road traffic noise exposure at the most-exposed building facade was associated with higher PD risk, with a hazard ratio (HR) of 1.03 (95% CI, 1.00–1.05) per interquartile increment of 11.5 dB. Noise exposure at the least-exposed facade showed a similar association (HR 1.04; 95% CI, 1.02–1.07 per interquartile increment of 8.9 dB).

**Global genetic study maps Parkinson's risk variants across ancestries.** A study analyzed GP2 release 11 data from 99,783 individuals, including 58,559 people with Parkinson's disease and 41,224 controls, spanning 11 genetically inferred ancestry groups (African, African admixed, Ashkenazi Jewish, Latino and Indigenous peoples of the Americas, central Asian, complex admixture, east Asian, European, Finnish, Middle Eastern, and south Asian). Researchers investigated causal and risk variants, including copy number variants, in established Parkinson's disease and parkinsonism-associated genes such as GBA1, LRRK2, SNCA, VPS35, RAB32, PINK1, PRKN, and PARK7.

**Amateur soccer heading linked to transient brain-injury biomarker spikes.** High-impact headers were associated with significantly greater immediate increases in blood-based p-tau217 (n=148, P=.03, Cohen's d=0.40) and S100B concentration (n=149, P=.02, Cohen's d=0.43) relative to no heading exposure, in a dose-response and impact-intensity-dependent relationship. All observed biomarker elevations normalized within 24 to 48 hours post-match, and no other significant biomarker associations were identified, indicating that even at the amateur level, heading is acutely associated with transient reductions in neural integrity.
