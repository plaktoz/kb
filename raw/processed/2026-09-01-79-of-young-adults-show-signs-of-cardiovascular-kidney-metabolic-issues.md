# 79% of Young Adults Show Signs of Cardiovascular, Kidney, Metabolic Issues

New research in *Circulation: Population Health and Outcomes* (published August 20) reveals that roughly 79% of adults in their 20s already display signs of cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic (CKM) syndrome — a framework linking obesity, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and heart disease.

## Study Details

- **Participants:** 1,283 individuals, average age ~23, drawn from the Future of Families and Child Wellbeing Study
- **Methods:** CKM staging, Life's Essential 8 (LE8) cardiovascular health scoring, and carotid artery ultrasounds

**CKM Stage Distribution:**

| Stage | Description | % of Participants |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|
| 0 | No risk factors | 20.7% |
| 1 | Excess/dysfunctional body fat | 40.2% |
| 2 | Metabolic or kidney risk factors | 36.2% |
| 3 | Subclinical cardiovascular disease | 2.8% |
| 4 | Clinical cardiovascular disease | 0% |

**Average LE8 Scores by Stage** (scale 0–100; higher = better):
- Stage 0: 77.6 | Stage 1: 70.0 | Stage 2: 63.8 | Stage 3: 64.4

Ultrasounds confirmed that higher CKM stages correlated with greater arterial wall thickness, a marker of elevated cardiovascular risk.

## Expert Perspectives

Cardiologist Kevin Shah noted the findings suggest cardiometabolic-kidney problems may begin "much earlier in life than we traditionally recognize," emphasizing early identification before conditions like diabetes or kidney disease fully develop.

Internist Jonathan Jennings described the carotid artery findings as "extremely alarming," since arterial disease is typically associated with advanced age.

## Prevention: Life's Essential 8

The American Heart Association's LE8 framework — associated with better CKM outcomes in the study — focuses on:

- Healthier eating and physical activity
- Quitting tobacco; adequate sleep
- Managing weight, cholesterol, blood sugar, and blood pressure

Shah emphasized optimism about the CKM framework: rather than treating conditions in isolation, it recognizes their interconnection, enabling earlier, more holistic intervention.
