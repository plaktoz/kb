# Microsoft Stock Forecast: MSFT May Break $570 as Azure Growth Holds at 40%

As of market close on August 21, Microsoft (MSFT) stock stood at $481.15, having rebounded about 30% from a July low of ~$380 since its July 29 earnings release. 56 analysts set a consensus price target of $569.56, implying about 17% upside — but reaching it depends on two conditions.

**Why the stock has swung wildly.** After Microsoft's fiscal Q2 (December 2025) report — revenue of $81.27B and EPS of $4.14, both beating expectations — Azure's 39% growth rate was read as a "slowdown," and combined with a 65% YoY capex surge, triggered an 11.7% single-day plunge; the stock fell nearly 20% cumulative in H1 amid broader AI-capex anxiety. The July 29 Q4 report reversed this: Azure growth accelerated to 43% from 40% (beating the 40% consensus), revenue hit $90.01B (~$2.4B above expectations), and adjusted EPS was $4.74 (including $3.2B in Anthropic investment gains; still beat by ~$0.36 excluding that). CEO Nadella noted Azure's full-year revenue topped $100B for the first time; the stock jumped as much as 9% after-hours. The lesson: for a growth giant like Microsoft, the market reacts to the direction of growth trends more than absolute quarterly profit.

**Condition 1 — Azure growth must hold above 40%.** JPMorgan raised its price target to $625, forecasting ~45% Azure growth next quarter, matching management's own guidance (demand continues to outpace available capacity). Citi raised its target from $570 to $600 after Q4's 43% constant-currency Azure growth beat by 4 points, with the outlook raised to 45% (also beating by 4 points). M365 Copilot paid seats have surpassed 30 million (net additions more than doubled quarter-over-quarter), or roughly 7% penetration of an estimated 430 million commercial M365 users — the metric analysts watch to verify real Azure demand.

**Condition 2 — a valid technical breakout above $500.** MSFT closed at $481.15 on August 21 (-0.47%), having pulled back from a August 10 high of $513.73. $500 is the key resistance, tested multiple times in early August without a confirmed break; the 7-day/12-day moving averages converge near $493 as near-term resistance; $480 and then the 20-day MA (~$472) are support. RSI (~63.1) is neutral. Short-term range is expected to be $472–$494; a heavy-volume close above $500 (ideally on 250M+ shares vs. the ~200M 20-day average) would open the path toward $570. Signals that would undermine the $570 target: Azure growth dropping below 35% for two straight quarters, repeated failure to hold $500, a break below $472, or capex-to-revenue continuing to climb without management signaling spending is "adjustable."

**Bottom line:** Azure growth staying above 40% is seen as the higher-certainty condition; the $500 technical breakout is the bigger variable. A sustained close above $500 on heavy volume within two weeks would raise the odds of reaching $570 within 6–12 months.
