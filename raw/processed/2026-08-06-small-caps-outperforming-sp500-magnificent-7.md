# Small caps are quietly outperforming the S&P 500 and 'Magnificent 7' this year: AlphaCheck

While the S&P 500 and Dow Jones Industrial Average reached record highs this week, another group of stocks in an overlooked part of the market also quietly showed its strength.

The Russell 2000 index touched a record high on Wednesday, surpassing the 3,000 level for the first time.

The small-cap index's 20.7% return has outperformed the S&P 500's 12.8% gain this year, and it has also outperformed the "Magnificent Seven" stocks year to date, as small caps benefited from profit-taking in the larger, growthier parts of the market.

As concerns swirl about a top-heavy market dominated by AI-related names, small caps may be seen as an attractive entry point for exposure to the AI boom, as the iShares Russell 2000 ETF (IWM) has a price-to-earnings multiple of 18x, compared to the SPDR S&P 500 ETF's (SPY) 27x, according to AlphaSpace data.

In a note last week, RBC Capital Markets' Lori Calvasina laid out the case for small-cap leadership to grind higher: below-average valuations, a stronger bottom-up earnings growth outlook, and several economic tailwinds, such as a reacceleration in job growth and manufacturing activity.

Still, there are plenty of risks, and Calvasina noted it wouldn't be surprising to see small caps take a pause.

The trade has been choppy, with the S&P 500 again taking the lead over the Russell in the past month amid market rotations. Tech valuations have also come down, giving investors a reason to rethink large caps.

Small caps' greater exposure to interest rates and sensitivity to may also restrain the trade, particularly if the Iran war begins to weigh on growth, Calvasina said.

"In recent years, pricing in hikes or taking out cuts tends to trip up the Small Cap outperformance trade in the short term," Calvasina wrote. "Note, we are starting to pay more attention to the M&A backdrop where a pick-up in activity could generate some excitement in the Small Cap arena."
