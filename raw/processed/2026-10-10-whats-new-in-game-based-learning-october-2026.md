# What's New in Game-based Learning - October 2026

Flavor Finder is a career awareness and cognitive skills learning game developed with Twin Cities PBS that focuses on working memory. Players take on the role of a flavor scientist, combining ingredients and recalling specific ratios.

Uncovering Loyalties with Colonial Williamsburg is a history learning experience set in 1774 in pre-revolutionary Virginia. Commissioned by Lord Dunmore, players question townspeople to determine where loyalties lie as tensions around independence rise.

## Major Edtech Companies Invest in AI and Assessment

September was a busy month for edtech dealmaking. McGraw-Hill acquired Teachally on September 2, adding an AI platform that helps educators create, localize, and translate standards-aligned instructional materials. Two weeks later, the company acquired TeachFX, an AI coaching tool that analyzes classroom audio and gives teachers private feedback on patterns like student talk time and questioning techniques. Pearson made two moves of its own, acquiring ITS, a Baltimore-based assessment technology company specializing in test delivery and remote proctoring, and agreeing to acquire Workera, a platform that measures workforce proficiency through role-specific scenarios and simulations. Workera's model of verifying skills through simulation is especially relevant to your next learning game, since a well-designed game can assess what a learner is able to do while they play.

## Gamified Design Boosts Engagement and Persistence in a New Study

A new study published in Humanities and Social Sciences Communications followed 100 secondary students through an eight-week mathematics unit, with half using a gamified version of their school's learning management system and half using the standard version. The gamified platform added progression tracking, adaptive challenges, real-time feedback, and digital rewards. Students in the gamified group completed more of their tasks (a median of 84.2% compared to 64.3%), followed through on more assignments, and dropped out at half the rate of their peers. The researcher also observed narrower performance gaps across socio-economic and demographic groups, and the study's single-school sample means those equity findings call for larger follow-up research. The features most closely tied to these gains, adaptive challenge and immediate feedback, are core game mechanics, which makes a strong case for building them into the foundation of your next learning experience.

## Video Game Data Could Teach AI to Navigate the Physical World

Wired reported on Worldmodeldata, a British startup that has licensed close to one million hours of gameplay from game studios. The company packages what players see on screen alongside the controller inputs they used, then offers those datasets to labs building world models, which are AI systems designed to predict how an environment changes in response to an action. Supporters believe this data could help robots and autonomous vehicles learn to move through the world, while Nvidia's world model lead warns that game physics are too approximate for delicate tasks like grasping objects. Games record cause and consequence with every button press, and that same quality is what makes them such effective teachers for human learners, too.

## High Schoolers Learn to Code by Building Their Own Video Games

At Waverly High School in Lansing, Michigan, students in a video game programming elective are learning to code by building their own games, starting with lessons on creating a character and making it move. Teacher Jared Tasch told WILX that the course gives students a chance to explore career interests like software engineering, and students described the class as a look at how games are made behind the scenes. The program used in class is free and available at home, so students can keep building in their free time. Game creation turns abstract programming concepts into visible, playable results, which makes it an excellent entry point for your own computer science learners.

That's a wrap on September! Whether the player is a high schooler programming a character's first steps or a professional completing a Workera module, games give learners a safe space to make decisions and watch the consequences play out. That same feedback loop is what allows a well-designed learning game to show you what your learners have mastered, which is why the gamified math study and Pearson's investment in simulation-based assessment deserve a close look from anyone planning a training or curriculum project.
