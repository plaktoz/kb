# Alphabet Stock Just Hit a 1-Year Low. Why Is Google's Parent Borrowing So Much Money?

Friday was an ugly day for tech stocks. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.3% and finished the week down 10%. Nvidia (NASDAQ:NVDA) dropped 14% after Reuters reported that the SEC is looking into its accounting. And Alphabet (NASDAQ:GOOGL)(NASDAQ:GOOG) didn't escape. The stock was up nearly 4% early in the day and then reversed, closing at $265.29, down 2.7%. That's its lowest close since October 2025. Shares are now 34% below their record close of $402.62 on May 13, which was also their 52-week high, and 15% lower than where they started the year.

And perhaps most importantly for the stock and its sentiment right now: Alphabet has been focused on borrowing more money.

## A busy month in the bond market

Alphabet raised about $10.9 billion in two bond sales this month. On Nov. 5, it closed a €6 billion sale of euro bonds (about $6.7 billion), split across seven series that mature between 2028 and 2045. Two weeks later, it priced C$6 billion of notes in Canada (about $4.2 billion). The longest of those matures in 2066 and pays 6% per year, meaning Alphabet will pay interest on it for 40 years.

The filings say the money is for "general corporate purposes, which may include the repayment of outstanding debt."

These deals cap a big year of borrowing. Alphabet's long-term debt was $125.1 billion at the end of September, up from $46.5 billion at the end of 2025, according to its latest quarterly report. And that doesn't count the November bonds.

It has sold stock, too. In June, Alphabet announced an $80 billion equity raise, including a $10 billion private sale to Berkshire Hathaway (NYSE:BRKB). Berkshire paid $351.81 for each Class A share. At Friday's close, that stake is down about 25%.

## Where the money is going

The short answer is data centers.

Alphabet spent $132 billion on property and equipment in the first nine months of the year, more than double the $63.6 billion it spent a year earlier. In June, the company said it expected to spend $180 billion to $190 billion for all of 2026, and that spending in 2027 would "significantly increase."

That spending has outrun the cash the business brings in. Alphabet's free cash flow, the cash left over after capital spending, was negative $9.1 billion in the third quarter and negative $5.9 billion in the second, according to its earnings release. As recently as the fourth quarter of 2025, it was positive $24.6 billion.

The business itself is still growing quickly. Third-quarter revenue rose 25% to $127.6 billion, and Google Cloud revenue more than doubled to $30.7 billion. That cloud growth is a big part of why Alphabet is building so fast.

## What investors should watch

Alphabet isn't in financial trouble. It ended September with $265.1 billion in cash and marketable securities, and its bonds are rated AA+ by S&P, just one step below the top rating. Borrowing at roughly 3.7% to 6% to build data centers can make sense if those data centers keep filling up.

The risk is what happens if they don't. Alphabet is spending as if demand for AI computing will keep climbing for years, and it's using borrowed money and new shares to do it. Investors are starting to question that kind of bet across the industry, which is a big reason the stocks are falling.

For long-term investors, the stock is far cheaper than it was in May. Two numbers to watch are free cash flow and Google Cloud growth. If cloud revenue keeps doubling, the borrowing will look smart. If it slows while the spending keeps rising, today's lower price may not be the bottom.
