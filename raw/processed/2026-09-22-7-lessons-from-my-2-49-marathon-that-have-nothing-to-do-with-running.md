# 7 Lessons From My 2:49 Marathon (That Have Nothing To Do With Running)

After a previous marathon failure, Bloom returned six days later and ran the course solo at 5am, finishing 26.25 miles in 2:48:47 at a 6:26/mile average pace.

## The 7 Lessons

**1. Progressive Overload**
The ancient legend of Milo of Croton — who grew strong by carrying a calf daily as it grew into a bull — illustrates how slowly increasing demands on any system produces growth. Progress compounds over time; the exponent is time itself.

**2. Visualize the Work, Not the Outcome**
Science suggests that imagining success prematurely can trick the brain into feeling accomplished before earning it. Instead, visualize the actual process: the early mornings, the hard miles, the unseen effort.

**3. Lay One Brick Today**
Drawing from a Will Smith story about rebuilding a wall as a child, Bloom emphasizes: *"just lay one brick today."* Daily consistency — unremarkable in the moment — compounds into extraordinary results.

**4. You Can't Win Early, But You Can Lose Early**
Every meaningful life pursuit is a long game. Going out too fast burns you out before the real race begins. Bloom's marathon showed a disciplined negative split: 1:25 first half, 1:24 second half.

**5. Track Everything You Want to Improve**
Whether finances, nutrition, strength, or happiness — measurement creates accountability and reveals what actually works.

**6. Pair a "Want" With a "Should" (Temptation Bundling)**
Researcher Katy Milkman's 2014 concept involves linking an enjoyable activity to a necessary-but-dreaded task. Bloom paired "junk food" audiobooks exclusively with long runs, turning dread into anticipation.

**7. Never Bet Against the Person Who Keeps Showing Up**
Consistency is rare. Most people quit when rewards are uncertain or progress is invisible. Simply continuing to show up — especially through difficulty — is a decisive competitive advantage.

---

**P.S. (Running-Specific):** Weather dramatically affected performance. Same course, one week apart — 70°F/90% humidity vs. 50°F/low humidity. At mile 21: warm conditions produced a 6:42 pace at HR 182; cool conditions yielded a 6:14 pace at HR 170.
