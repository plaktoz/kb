# 7 Lessons From My 2:49 Marathon (That Have Nothing To Do With Running)

After failing a previous marathon attempt, Bloom returned six days later and ran the same course solo at 5am, completing 26.25 miles in 2:48:47 at a 6:26/mile average pace.

## The 7 Lessons

**1. Progressive Overload**
Slow, steady increases in load force adaptation and growth — in fitness, focus, or resilience. The ancient story of Milo of Croton illustrates this: daily repetition compounds into extraordinary capability.

**2. Visualize the Work, Not the Outcome**
Research suggests outcome visualization can create premature satisfaction and reduce motivation. Instead, mentally rehearse the actual effort: early mornings, hard sessions, unglamorous consistency.

**3. Just Lay One Brick Today**
Drawing on Will Smith's story of rebuilding a wall as a child, Bloom emphasizes doing small things well daily. Compound interest applies to effort: starting unable to run a 7-minute mile, he eventually averaged 6:26 across a full marathon.

**4. You Can't Win in the Early Miles (But You Can Lose)**
Life's meaningful pursuits — career, health, relationships — are marathons. Starting conservatively and building strength over time beats burning out early. His race splits: 1:25 first half, 1:24 second half.

**5. You Can't Improve What You Don't Track**
Deliberate tracking of inputs and progress separates achieved goals from missed ones — whether finances, nutrition, strength, or happiness.

**6. Pair a "Want" With a "Should" (Temptation Bundling)**
Researcher Katy Milkman's concept: attach an enjoyable activity to a dreaded necessary one. Bloom paired "junk food" audiobooks exclusively with long runs, transforming something he dreaded into something he anticipated.

**7. Never Bet Against the Person Who Keeps Showing Up**
Consistency through difficulty — when no one's watching, when rewards are uncertain — is rare and decisive. Bloom's closing conviction: "When I say I'm going to do something, I do it."

**P.S. — Weather Impact:** Running the same course one week apart, the difference between 70°F/90% humidity and 50°F/low humidity was dramatic. At mile 21: warm conditions produced a 6:42 pace at HR 182; cool conditions yielded a 6:14 pace at HR 170.
