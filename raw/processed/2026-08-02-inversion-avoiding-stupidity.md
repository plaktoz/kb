# Inversion and The Power of Avoiding Stupidity

Charlie Munger famously quipped that he only wants to know where he'll die — so he can avoid going there. This idea traces back to mathematician Carl Jacobi, who tackled hard problems with a simple motto: *"invert, always invert."*

Munger notes that "many hard problems are best solved when they are addressed backward." Inversion isn't just for mathematics — it's one of the most practical thinking tools available.

## The Core Idea

Thinking about problems only in one direction is insufficient. Inversion forces you to surface hidden assumptions and consider perspectives you'd otherwise miss. Some problems genuinely cannot be solved by thinking forward alone.

**Example:** Instead of asking *what fosters innovation*, ask *what kills it* — then stop doing those things.

## Additive vs. Subtractive Thinking

Forward thinking tends to be **additive** — adding actions, interventions, and solutions. Backward thinking is **subtractive** — removing obstacles and avoiding mistakes. The subtractive approach carries less risk of unintended harm.

## Key Takeaway

> Avoiding stupidity is easier than seeking brilliance.

Inversion won't always solve a problem outright, but it reliably helps you sidestep avoidable errors. The practical prescription: spend less energy chasing brilliant outcomes and more energy eliminating obvious mistakes.
