# Stock futures edge up as oil falls, with AI and Fed in focus

Cheaper oil lifts stock futures, but the AI trade and the Fed still loom large

Dow futures added 209 points as Brent crude fell 1.7%, with the Fed due to announce its rate decision on Wednesday

Stock futures edged up early Monday as oil prices fell, while concerns about AI-linked stocks and this week's Federal Reserve decision weigh on traders.

Dow Jones Industrial Average futures gained 209 points, a 0.4% advance. S&P 500 futures gained 0.1%, while Nasdaq $NDAQ-100 contracts hovered close to the unchanged mark. Brent futures were down 1.7% at $114.77 a barrel, with U.S. West Texas Intermediate off 2% at $100.97.

Brent climbed on Friday to its priciest mark since April, according to CNBC, as press accounts suggested the U.S. and Israel might be preparing to attack Iran once the midterms are over. Higher oil prices, along with a sweeping market sell-off, pushed all three benchmark indexes into the red on Friday. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite each posted a second straight weekly loss.

CoreWeave stock slid in premarket trading, and other neocloud stocks also dropped. Elon Musk warned that upward of 200 firms in the sector are staring down "guppy-level death," according to CNBC. Investors have feared that these companies could be overextending themselves with debt as they scale up their AI capabilities, according to CNBC.

Hyperscalers Alphabet $GOOGL, Amazon $AMZN and Microsoft $MSFT are due to report earnings this week and update investors on their AI spending, according to The Wall Street Journal.

The Fed is scheduled to announce its decision on Wednesday. Fed funds futures trading points to 90% odds that policymakers will leave rates unchanged within the current 3.75% to 4% band.

Bank of America $BAC expects the Fed to hike by 0.25 percentage point, a move the bank described as a "heavy lift," according to CNBC.

"The Oct decision will be a test of Chair Warsh's resolve to bring 'sea change' to the Fed's forward guidance strategy," economist Claudio Irigoyen said in a research note from the bank, according to CNBC. "If the Fed hikes, investors might worry about overtightening. If the Fed holds, markets could question the Fed's inflation-fighting credibility."

Brazilian assets are also in focus after President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won re-election on Sunday, beating Flávio Bolsonaro. The iShares MSCI Brazil ETF is poised to lose a quarter of its value, which would mark its steepest drop ever recorded, according to The Wall Street Journal.
