# Inherent, founded by DeepMind alumni, says its AI 'teammate' just outperformed Anthropic and OpenAI at replicating research

Inherent, a London AI lab founded by Google DeepMind alumni, says its AI agent just outperformed much larger models from Anthropic and OpenAI using a fraction of the size.

Just weeks after emerging from stealth with a $50 million seed round, the British startup says its newly released AI agent, Faraday, has outperformed larger, better-known models at a specific task: independently reproducing the findings of published scientific papers without being told the answer in advance.

Paper replication is a standard training exercise for human scientists too, co-founder and chief scientist Edward Hughes said: "Many PhD students actually start by doing this." Beating other AI systems at the task wasn't the point, Hughes said; how they got there was.

Measured against Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 and OpenAI's GPT-5.5 — both much larger, frontier-scale systems — Faraday runs on a comparatively tiny model called Qwen 3.6 with just 27 billion parameters. Inherent's bar for success was also higher than accuracy alone: it wanted Faraday to demonstrate "research taste" — an instinct for what experiments are worth running and how to design them well.

Rather than training its agents primarily on how science is conducted, Inherent leans on reinforcement learning — a training method that rewards good outcomes rather than spelling out rules — betting it will generalize better to the company's longer-term goal of agents capable of contributing across many scientific fields.

"We're always guided by that north star of building an AI scientist agent and imbuing our agents with taste," Hughes said. That focus has also shaped what Inherent chooses not to build: rather than developing its own coding tool, it had Faraday use OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Codex instead, the way human scientists lean on existing software.

Inherent's dozen employees all work in person out of an office in King's Cross, London — the neighborhood Google DeepMind's presence helped turn into one of the world's top AI hubs. Hughes has also added his voice to calls to end "garden leave," the UK practice of barring departing employees from joining or starting a rival company for months after they resign — a restriction American researchers generally don't face.

The startup plans to grow its headcount to "about 20 to 25" by the end of the year.
