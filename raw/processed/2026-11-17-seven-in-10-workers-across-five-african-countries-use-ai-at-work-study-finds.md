# Seven in 10 Workers Across Five African Countries Use AI at Work, Study Finds

More than seven in 10 employees surveyed across five African countries have used AI at work. But only 55% trust senior leadership, highlighting the importance of skills development, employee confidence and responsible leadership in workplace transformation.

Artificial intelligence is becoming increasingly embedded in working life across parts of Africa, with 71% of employees surveyed reporting that they have used AI at work, according to new research from PwC. The Africa Workforce Hopes and Fears Survey 2026, drawing on responses from 1,733 workers across Algeria, Kenya, Morocco, Nigeria and South Africa, reveals a workforce embracing technological change while navigating questions about leadership, skills development and future career opportunities.

More than eight in 10 respondents express pride in their work and a willingness to go beyond their formal responsibilities. However, confidence in organisational leadership is less widespread, with 66% trusting their direct manager and just 55% trusting senior leaders.

The findings suggest that Africa's growing adoption of workplace AI presents significant opportunities. But businesses must ensure technological transformation is accompanied by investment in people, trust and workforce development, noted the report.

## SEVEN IN 10 EMPLOYEES HAVE USED AI AT WORK

One of the report's most significant findings is the extent to which employees are already using artificial intelligence. PwC found that 71% of workers surveyed have used AI in their jobs. This shows that employees now use the technology beyond specialist roles and experimental projects.

However, the research also reveals an important distinction between using AI and experiencing tangible benefits from it. Just 37% of respondents say AI helps them perform complex tasks better. That gap suggests employers need to look beyond technology adoption and examine whether employees have the skills, support and working conditions required to use AI effectively.

Access to technology may represent an important first step. But meaningful organisational benefits also depend on how AI is integrated into everyday work. The findings reflect wider global concerns about the relationship between AI adoption and employee productivity.

Recent research found that seven in 10 workers believe AI improves productivity, although workloads are not necessarily falling. Another study revealed that nearly half of employees using AI face rising workload expectations. This study raised questions about whether employers are using the technology to improve the quality of work or simply increase output. Although those studies cover different populations, together they highlight why AI adoption should not be treated as an automatic measure of workplace progress.

## AFRICA'S WORKFORCE IS EMBRACING TECH CHANGE

The findings build on PwC's previous research into the changing expectations of workers across Africa. The company's 2025 African workforce survey reported that 64% of respondents had used AI at work during the preceding year, compared with 54% in its wider global survey. That research also revealed that just 17% of African workers used generative AI daily, highlighting a distinction between general exposure to AI and its regular integration into work.

The new 2026 survey reports 71% AI use, although differences in survey samples and question wording mean the two figures should not automatically be interpreted as a directly comparable year-on-year increase. PwC's earlier research also highlighted widespread optimism about technological change. Some 76% believed generative AI improved the quality of their work, while 72% expected productivity improvements over the following three years.

Nevertheless, optimism was accompanied by uncertainty about the future relevance of existing skills. In that previous survey, only 35% believed their current skills would remain relevant in three years. Taken together, the findings reinforce why organisations need to invest in workforce development as the capabilities required for employment continue to evolve.

## DIRECT MANAGERS TRUSTED MORE THAN SENIOR LEADERS

Leadership trust emerges as another important theme in the 2026 findings. According to PwC:

- 66% of respondents trust their direct manager.
- 55% trust senior leadership.

The 11-percentage-point difference suggests employees may feel more confident in the people they interact with regularly than in those making strategic decisions about organisational change. For businesses introducing AI, that distinction matters. Senior executives may determine investment priorities, organisational restructuring and future workforce strategies, but direct managers are often responsible for explaining changes and helping employees adapt.

If employees trust their managers more than senior leadership, organisations need to ensure that communication about technological transformation reaches employees through credible, informed and supportive channels. The findings connect with recent research revealing that four in 10 employees want greater CEO transparency about how AI will affect their careers. That global BCG research found that nearly nine in 10 CEOs believed employees trusted their leadership, while only 46% of workers agreed.

Another global study found that just 24% of people consider business leaders trustworthy. The studies use different definitions and populations, so the percentages are not directly comparable. Nevertheless, they reinforce the importance of leadership credibility as organisations navigate technological and economic change.

## STRONG MOTIVATION DESPITE UNCERTAINTY

Despite uncertainty around the future of work, PwC's latest African survey highlights substantial workforce motivation. More than eight in 10 respondents express pride in their work and a willingness to go beyond what their roles formally require. This represents a potentially important advantage for employers looking to introduce new technology, improve productivity and develop new capabilities.

Employees who feel motivated and committed may be more willing to participate in training, adapt to changing responsibilities and contribute ideas about improving how work is organised. However, workforce motivation should not be mistaken for unlimited capacity.

Employers still need to consider employee wellbeing, reasonable workloads, career progression and whether staff have the resources to meet changing expectations. The wider challenge is ensuring that enthusiasm for innovation is supported by meaningful investment in employee development.

## HOW AI COULD RESHAPE WORKFORCE OPPORTUNITIES

PwC's 2026 findings also suggest that more than half of surveyed workers occupy roles positioned to benefit from AI. That presents opportunities for organisations to rethink how employees develop skills, contribute to business performance and progress in their careers.

However, benefiting from AI is not simply about introducing new software or expecting employees to acquire new technical skills independently. Organisations need to consider how technology changes the tasks employees perform, which capabilities become more valuable and whether opportunities for development are distributed fairly.

For example, employees in roles with greater access to training, technology and supportive managers may be better positioned to benefit than colleagues working in environments with fewer resources. This raises important questions about inclusion and equitable access to the opportunities created by digital transformation.

The importance of investing in human capabilities was highlighted in IBM's research showing that six in 10 workers worry AI is weakening their skills. That study also identified critical thinking and human judgement as increasingly important capabilities in the AI era.

## LEADERSHIP AND FUTURE-READY SKILLS

PwC has previously emphasised the need for African employers to convert workforce optimism into sustained improvements in organisational performance. Commenting on the 2025 findings, Dr Dayalan Govender, PwC Africa's People and Organisation Leader at the time, said: "Africa's workforce is optimistic and ready for change, but leaders must accelerate digital adoption and invest in future-ready skills"

The latest research brings renewed attention to that challenge. AI adoption is already widespread among the workers surveyed, yet its potential benefits depend on how effectively employers support employees in using the technology. That requires managers who can explain changes, accessible development opportunities and organisational cultures in which employees feel able to ask questions, challenge assumptions and contribute to decisions.

Earlier research found that 70% of workers felt ready to work with AI agents, while only 39% of business leaders believed employees were comfortable doing so. The findings illustrate why employers should listen directly to employees rather than making assumptions about workforce readiness.

## WHAT EMPLOYERS SHOULD DO NEXT

PwC's findings suggest several recommendations for organisations seeking to translate AI adoption into lasting workforce and business benefits. They include:

- Invest in accessible AI training. Employers should offer development opportunities to employees across all roles, seniority levels and working environments, rather than focusing solely on technology specialists.
- Strengthen trust in leadership. Senior executives should communicate how AI investments relate to business strategy, employee opportunities and organisational decisions.
- Equip managers to support change. Managers need sufficient knowledge and resources to explain how technology will affect employees' responsibilities and development needs.
- Measure meaningful benefits. Organisations should assess whether AI improves work quality, efficiency, employee experience and career opportunities rather than focusing solely on adoption rates.
- Protect employee wellbeing. Employers should ensure technological productivity gains do not automatically translate into unsustainable workloads or expectations.
- Promote equitable access. Businesses should review whether different employee groups have fair opportunities to acquire new skills and benefit from technological change.
- Maintain human oversight. Decisions affecting recruitment, progression, performance and job security should remain subject to appropriate accountability and safeguards.

Importantly, multinational employers should recognise that workforce experiences and access to technology differ between countries, sectors and organisations. Strategies must respond to local circumstances rather than assuming a single approach will work everywhere.

## AFRICA'S AI OPPORTUNITY DEPENDS ON PEOPLE

The 2026 Africa Workforce Hopes and Fears Survey highlights a workforce that is engaging with technological change and demonstrating considerable motivation. With 71% reporting AI use at work and more than eight in 10 expressing pride and commitment, there is a strong foundation for continued workforce transformation.

However, the findings also reveal why technological adoption cannot be treated as the final measure of success. Just 37% say AI helps them perform complex tasks better, while confidence in senior leadership is considerably lower than trust in direct managers.

For employers, the challenge is to convert technological engagement into better opportunities, stronger skills, meaningful productivity improvements and greater confidence in the future. Ultimately, responsible AI transformation will depend as much on how organisations develop, support and lead their people as on the technology they introduce.
