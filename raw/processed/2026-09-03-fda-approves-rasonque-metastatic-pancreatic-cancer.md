# FDA Approves Rasonque for Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer

The FDA approved Rasonque (daraxonrasib) on August 26 to treat pancreatic adenocarcinoma, the most prevalent form of pancreatic cancer. The approval follows a Phase 3 clinical trial showing patients on Rasonque survived an average of 13.2 months, versus 6.7 months for those receiving chemotherapy alone.

Pancreatic cancer accounted for over 490,000 deaths globally in 2024, making it the sixth leading cause of cancer-related mortality.

Surgical oncologist Anton Bilchik called it "probably the most exciting study...in pancreas cancer in the last 30, 40 years."

Rasonque targets RAS gene mutations, which drive tumor growth in over 90% of pancreatic adenocarcinoma cases. The drug acts as a "molecular glue," binding to a cellular protein called cyclophilin A, forming a complex that blocks RAS from signaling cancer cell growth.

Unlike intravenous chemotherapy — which harms healthy and cancerous cells alike — Rasonque is a once-daily oral pill. Common side effects include rash, mouth sores, nausea, diarrhea, and fatigue.

Outstanding questions remain: whether Rasonque could be effective as a first-line treatment, how it handles drug resistance over time, and its real-world impact given pricing of nearly $500,000 per year of therapy.
