# How to improve employee engagement in 2026: 10 practical strategies

In 2026, employee engagement challenges are increasingly shaped by communication overload, disconnected frontline employees, burnout, poor leadership visibility, and fragmented workplace technology. Organizations that improve engagement successfully typically focus on clearer communication, employee recognition, wellbeing, career development, manager visibility, feedback and listening, flexible working, stronger onboarding, connected digital workplace experiences, and easier access to information and tools.

Research cited in the piece found 92% of business executives believe engaged employees perform better. Engagement is closely tied to retention: engaged teams are more likely to go the extra mile, build longer careers at the company, and recommend the workplace to others, while disengaged teams are more likely to disconnect from managers and become open to new offers.

**Why engagement is harder in 2026:** hybrid/remote friction, burnout and workload pressure, communication overload (Slack/Teams fatigue and fragmented channels), economic uncertainty and job insecurity, and generational shifts as Gen Z employees prioritize purpose, transparency and personal growth more than prior generations.

**10 practical steps to improve engagement:** improve workplace efficiency; improve communication; give recognition (important to 37% of employees per Great Place to Work); encourage interaction and community building; invest in employee wants/needs (e.g. via pulse surveys); focus on employee wellbeing (one report found 19% of people who suffered work-related health issues attributed them to burnout); encourage regular feedback (33%+ of workers want more frequent feedback per a Joblist survey); emphasize company culture; provide strong onboarding (a positive onboarding experience can improve retention by up to 82% per one study, and Burger King UK cut new-starter dropout 40% and hourly crew turnover 32% after overhauling onboarding); and offer working flexibility (87% of employees expect employers to help them balance work and personal commitments).

**Measuring engagement:** the piece recommends short, frequent pulse surveys (5–10 questions, quarterly or monthly) plus tracking metrics like employee Net Promoter Score (eNPS), turnover rate, absenteeism rate, participation rates, and self-reported wellbeing scores — combined with qualitative feedback from listening sessions and town halls, then turned into a concrete action plan that gets re-measured after three to six months.
