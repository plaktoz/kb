# Redefining What Efficiency Means in the Age of AI

HBR On Leadership, Episode 161. Host: Alison Beard. Guest: Mithu Storoni, neuroscientist and author of *Hyperefficient: Optimize Your Brain to Transform the Way You Work*.

Storoni argues that the AI era demands a new definition of efficiency — one centered on **quality of output** rather than quantity. Since AI handles repetitive, volume-based tasks, humans must focus on higher-order thinking, creativity, and judgment.

## Three Brain "Gears" (driven by norepinephrine levels)

- **Gear 1** – Slow, hazy, mind-wandering state (early morning/pre-sleep); optimal for creativity
- **Gear 2** – The "Goldilocks" focused state; best for deep, complex cognitive work
- **Gear 3** – Reactive, fast, error-prone; triggered by deadline pressure and constant notifications

Storoni notes that gear three prioritizes speed at the cost of accuracy — workers "miss nuances" and "second- and third-order consequences."

## Timing matters

Creative peaks occur in early morning transition windows and late evening. Focused work peaks mid-morning and mid-afternoon. Routine meetings fit best just after lunch.

## Physical movement aids thinking

Walking prevents rumination while keeping the mind alert and exploratory — body state directly influences brain state.

## Learning thrives on mild tension

Slight apprehension primes the brain for neuroplasticity. The skill is staying at the "top edge of gear two" without tipping into panic.

## For Managers

- Offer flexible, role-tailored scheduling
- Protect creative and focus windows from poorly-timed meetings
- Allow employees dedicated time to pursue curiosity-driven learning — a practice that builds intrinsic motivation and reduces burnout
