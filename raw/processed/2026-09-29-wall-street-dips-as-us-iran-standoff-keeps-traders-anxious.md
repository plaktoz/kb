# Update: Wall Street Dips as US-Iran Standoff Keeps Traders Anxious

source_url: https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/wall-street-dips-us-iran-211514161.html

By MT Newswires | Tuesday, September 29, 2026

---

Stocks fell Monday as tensions between the US and Iran remained unresolved, keeping oil prices and bond yields elevated. The Nasdaq Composite lost 0.9% to 26,820.38, the S&P 500 dropped 0.8% to 7,683.69, and the Dow fell 0.7% to 51,481.51. Communication services led sector declines while consumer staples outperformed.

President Trump reportedly turned down a conditional peace offer from Iran, with plans reportedly in place to resume military action after the November midterms. Iran had proposed reopening the Strait of Hormuz within seven days in exchange for renewed negotiations. Trump told Axios he expects further engagement with Iran this week, and Qatari mediators were reportedly arranging separate talks with both sides. Saxo Bank commented that Trump believes Iran "had overplayed its hand."

Oil prices rose, with WTI crude up 0.9% to $93.28/barrel and Brent up 1.7% to $106.09. Treasury yields climbed, with the 10-year yield rising to 5.24% and the two-year to 4.93%.

Investors are awaiting this week's PCE inflation data and September nonfarm payrolls report. Jefferies projected nonfarm payroll growth exceeding 175,000 jobs, noting labor market conditions have improved substantially since mid-2025. Fed rate hike odds for next month rose to 70%, per CME FedWatch data.

In corporate news, Boeing shares fell 6.9% amid a reported software issue in 737 MAX jets tied to navigation and landing safety concerns. Nvidia rose 1.7% after unveiling a $150 billion buyback expansion and a new AI governance software platform. Micron, Accenture, and Nike are among companies reporting earnings this week. Spot gold rose 0.4% to $4,131.44/oz, while silver fell 5.9% to $61.01/oz.
