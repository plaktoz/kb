# I Don't Want the Details

After an incident, an SVP cut off a detailed explanation with: "I don't want the details. I want to know what we're changing."

What initially seemed dismissive was actually a statement of trust — the executive already assumed competence and wanted to skip straight to systemic improvement.

## Core Argument

Most organizations ask *why* something went wrong. The problem: a compelling explanation can actually reduce urgency to fix anything. Once everyone agrees behavior was reasonable, nothing changes — and the same failure recurs.

Better question: **"Given that reasonable people produced this outcome, what needs to change?"**

## Key Principles

- **Explanation ≠ Fix.** Understanding an incident doesn't prevent recurrence.
- **Fix systems, not people.** Ownership gaps, changing requirements, and alert fatigue are structural problems.
- **Test your corrective actions:** "If everyone involved left tomorrow, would the fix still work?"
- **Not every failure needs a new process.** Sometimes accepting a risk consciously is valid — but it must be a deliberate choice, not rationalized inaction.

## The Real Question

"If the same situation happened tomorrow, what would cause a different outcome?"

Vague commitments to "communicate better" or "be more careful" are hopes, not fixes.
