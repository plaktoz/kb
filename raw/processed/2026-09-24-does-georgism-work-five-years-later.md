# Does Georgism Work? Five Years Later

Lars Doucet, who won the first ACX book review contest with his piece on Henry George's *Progress and Poverty*, returns to assess what's changed in five years. He now works full-time at the Center for Land Economics.

## Key developments

- Virginia and Kentucky passed land value tax (LVT) enablement laws in 2026
- UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham and South Korean President Lee Jae Myung are both LVT-friendly
- Germany's Baden-Württemberg implemented a new LVT in January 2025

## What he got wrong

Doucet believed winning online arguments was crucial. He now thinks local lawmakers matter far more than internet debates, and that doing policymakers' homework — building models, one-pagers, visualizations — drives real change.

## Three original claims revisited

1. *Land is a big deal* — Holds up strongly; South Korea's land value exceeds 500% of GDP
2. *LVT can't be passed to tenants* — Still largely true, with one exception: pervasive rent control paired with landlord tax-increase exemptions (as seen in Denmark)
3. *Land can't be accurately assessed* — More nuanced now; South Korea values all land annually per parcel in five months. Key insight: data quality and regular updates matter more than sophisticated algorithms

## Personal note

Doucet's son Nikolas suffered a catastrophic brain injury in 2023 and died in 2024. He nearly abandoned the movement before Greg Miller recruited him to lead the renamed Center for Land Economics.

## Why historical Georgism faded

The automobile enabled suburban sprawl, which relieved land-rent pressure for roughly a century — effectively a "second frontier." That frontier is now closed, giving rise to what historians are calling the Second Gilded Age.
