# Start Menopausal Hormone Therapy Early for a Lower Heart Disease Risk

A new study in *JAMA Internal Medicine* found that women who begin hormone replacement therapy (HRT) during perimenopause or early postmenopause may reduce their cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk by 22% compared to non-users. The protective effect was strongest when therapy started within 10 years of menopause onset, yielding a 27% lower heart disease risk. Black women saw the largest benefit — a 49% reduction in CVD risk.

## Study Details

Researchers analyzed 20 years of health data from over 2,700 participants in the SWAN (Study of Women's Health Across the Nation) project. All participants self-reported vasomotor symptoms (hot flashes, night sweats) and had no prior heart disease diagnosis.

Co-senior author Samar R. El Khoudary noted that most prior research focused on already-postmenopausal women, leaving a gap around perimenopause — when hormonal shifts are most pronounced. The team used the long-term SWAN dataset to examine whether timing of hormone therapy initiation matters.

El Khoudary cautioned: "Because this was an observational study, residual confounding...could also contribute to the findings" and results require confirmation via clinical trials.

## Expert Commentary

Jennifer Wong, MD (cardiologist, MemorialCare): Vasomotor symptoms may coincide with higher blood pressure, abnormal cholesterol, and elevated blood sugar — all cardiovascular risk factors. She emphasized that hormone therapy "is not recommended for everyone, so the decision should be individualized."

Prudence Hall, MD (OB/GYN): Estradiol (a form of estrogen) helps reduce blood sugar, inflammation, and LDL cholesterol, including harmful subparticles linked to arterial plaque. She noted these effects may reduce risk of heart disease, stroke, Alzheimer's, and diabetes — though her comments reflect her private practice perspective, not the study itself.

## Key Takeaways

- Starting HRT in perimenopause/early postmenopause: ~22% CVD risk reduction
- Starting within 10 years of menopause onset: ~27% CVD risk reduction
- Strongest effect observed in Black women (~49% reduction)
- Study is observational; causation is not established
- Women with significant vasomotor symptoms should discuss individualized options with their healthcare provider
