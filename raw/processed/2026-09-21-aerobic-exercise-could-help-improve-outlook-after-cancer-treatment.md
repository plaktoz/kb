# Aerobic Exercise Could Help Improve Outlook After Cancer Treatment

Fact-checked by: Jill Seladi-Schulman, Ph.D.

## Summary

A study published in the *British Journal of Sports Medicine* found that structured, intentional exercise after cancer treatment may reduce recurrence risk and improve survival outcomes.

## Key Findings

Researchers analyzed 21 randomized controlled trials involving 8,449 participants. Those who followed structured exercise programs showed:

- 17% greater likelihood of living without cancer recurrence
- 26% lower risk of cancer-specific death
- Improved overall and disease-free survival
- Lower all-cause mortality

Aerobic exercise alone showed a 29% lower relative risk of death; combined aerobic and resistance training showed roughly 25%. Resistance training alone was not statistically significant.

Cancers most studied: breast, colorectal, and pancreatic.

## Expert Perspectives

Physical therapist Diana Garrett noted that "exercise leads to increased cardiac output...which can lead to a more efficient heart," and that regular exercise may boost immune activity — relevant for cancer survivors.

Oncologist Mike Lattanzi noted exercise "might impair cancer cell metabolism and enhance the efficacy of antitumor immune cells."

Both experts emphasized tailoring any program to individual fitness levels, treatment history, and health status — ideally in coordination with an oncology care team.
