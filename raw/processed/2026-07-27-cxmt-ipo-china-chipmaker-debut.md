# China chipmaker CXMT jumps 472% in debut after US$9.8 billion IPO

**Source:** https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/companies-markets/capital-markets-currencies/china-chipmaker-cxmt-jumps-472-debut-after-us9-8-billion-ipo  
**Author:** Business Times Singapore  
**Date:** 2026-07-27

---

CXMT (formerly ChangXin Memory Technologies), China's largest DRAM chipmaker, debuted on Shanghai's STAR Market technology board and surged 472% on its first trading day after raising $9.8 billion in Asia's largest IPO of 2026. The company briefly became China's most valuable listed company with an estimated market capitalization of 3.3 trillion yuan (~$490 billion).

**IPO details:**
- Raised: $9.8 billion (US) — Asia's biggest IPO of 2026
- Exchange: Shanghai STAR Market (technology board)
- Opening surge: +472% on debut day
- Peak market cap: ~3.3 trillion yuan ($490 billion)

**CXMT's strategic significance:**  
CXMT is the principal player in China's memory chip self-sufficiency drive. It manufactures DRAM — the memory chip that powers AI servers, data centers, and high-performance computing. The company's ability to scale domestic DRAM production is critical to China's goal of reducing dependence on South Korean (Samsung, SK Hynix) and US-adjacent supply chains.

**Why the IPO surged:**  
- AI infrastructure buildout globally has created massive demand for memory chips (HBM, DRAM)
- CXMT represents the only domestic Chinese alternative to SK Hynix and Samsung for AI memory
- Investors priced in China's strategic imperative: memory chips are critical national infrastructure
- US export controls on advanced chips have made domestic alternatives more valuable than their technical specs alone would suggest

**Geopolitical context:**  
CXMT's IPO coincides with the broader US-China tech decoupling. US sanctions restrict access to ASML extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography equipment, limiting CXMT's ability to manufacture the most advanced chips — but its DRAM technology serves current-generation AI server demands. The IPO surge signals market confidence that demand is sufficient at the available technology tier.

**Investment implications:**  
For portfolios with China/EM exposure (VWRA), CXMT's debut underscores how the AI trade is reshaping the global semiconductor competitive map. China's memory chip ambitions — if successful — would materially reduce Samsung and SK Hynix's long-term market share in AI infrastructure.
