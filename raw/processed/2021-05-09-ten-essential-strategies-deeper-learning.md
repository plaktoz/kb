# The 10 Essential Strategies for Deeper Learning

1. **Practice on questions from the test.** Retrieval practice — shutting the book and trying to recall what you've learned — is one of the most effective studying techniques, beating fancier techniques like concept mapping, and it works even without feedback.

2. **Expose yourself multiple times to the same information.** Memories diminish over time (the forgetting curve), but spreading exposure across weeks instead of hours causes memories to be retained far longer than cramming.

3. **Drill the basics until they're automatic.** Complex skills are built from simpler parts; when basics aren't fully learned, cognitive load makes complicated skills impossible. Direct instruction overcomes this by mastering core skills before harder tasks.

4. **Test your knowledge before you're "ready."** Experiments show students forced to practice before feeling ready do better than those who stick to passive review; taking a test before learning material can even prime better learning.

5. **Slow down to understand better.** Working memory can only hold a few items at a time. Writing out what you're trying to learn, one step at a time, offloads the problem to paper and frees space to see the connections you need.

6. **Get clear on what confuses you.** Instead of trying to understand an idea all at once, walk through it step by step and pinpoint exactly where things stop making sense — that gives you a specific question rather than vague confusion.

7. **Never multitask.** Multitasking is really rapid switching, and each switch leaves cognitive residue from the previous task that makes learning harder. Silence phones and email; instrumental music or white noise beats music with lyrics.

8. **Don't get too far from the real thing.** Knowledge learned in one context often doesn't transfer to others. Keep real projects close to formal learning to prevent knowledge from becoming purely academic.

9. **Pretend to teach it.** Teaching — even just pretending — forces you to confront what you know and don't know, combining retrieval practice, mastering basics, and surfacing your own confusions.

10. **Find your reason to be interested.** Students motivated by genuine understanding, not just passing tests, do better — ironically including on the tests themselves.
