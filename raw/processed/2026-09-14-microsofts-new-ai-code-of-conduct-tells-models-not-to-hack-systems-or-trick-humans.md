# Microsoft's new AI 'code of conduct' tells models not to hack systems or trick humans

Microsoft released a new AI code of conduct aimed at guiding its models away from dangerous behavior. The document predicts that superintelligent AI will surpass humans in most tasks within a decade, calling alignment "one of the greatest challenges humanity has ever faced."

The code establishes a hierarchy where each model's overarching conduct rules override individual user preferences. It includes hard prohibitions — "absolute constraints" — against cyberattacks, nuclear weapons assistance, and deepfake creation.

On human oversight, the document explicitly states that models must not use "adaptive, deceptive, self-reinforcing, collusion, or other mechanisms to evade or defeat human oversight" in ways that prevent authorized parties from modifying or shutting them down.

The release follows a wave of rogue-agent incidents and a high-profile resignation at Anthropic, where a researcher publicly warned about extinction-level AI risks.

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella endorsed the broader safety push, supporting "deliberate pacing" and the concept of embedded evaluators at AI labs. Microsoft joins Anthropic, OpenAI, and xAI in backing a frontier-pacing approach to development.
