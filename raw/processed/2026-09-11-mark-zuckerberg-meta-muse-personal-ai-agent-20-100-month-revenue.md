# Mark Zuckerberg's Meta Is Charging Consumers for a Personal AI Agent for the First Time. Here's What $20 and $100 a Month Could Add to Revenue.

Meta launched **Muse** on Sept. 8 — a personal AI agent capable of sending emails, booking travel, filling out forms, and negotiating on users' behalf. The app is free, but paid tiers run $20/month and $100/month for heavier usage. It's the first time Meta has charged consumers for a personal AI agent.

**Revenue context:** Advertising represents roughly 98% of Meta's ~$60.8B in Q2 revenue. Every 1 million users on the $20 plan equals ~$240M/year; the $100 plan generates ~$1.2B per million users. Even 5 million paid subscribers on the lower tier would represent less than 0.5% of annual revenue.

Meta's "other revenue" line (consumer subscriptions + WhatsApp fees) topped $1B in a quarter for the first time in Q2 — but still accounts for well under 2% of total revenue after years of Meta Verified and similar offerings.

**Pricing rationale:** Meta's AI chief indicated that most users won't need paid tiers, noting the plans exist largely to offset computing costs for heavy users — not primarily as a profit driver.

**Future monetization:** Zuckerberg framed Muse as a "foundation for our next wave of products and revenue lines," and the company is exploring taking a cut of purchases completed through the agent.

**Investment take:** The author views Meta's value proposition as still rooted in its ad business (27% YoY growth last quarter), with shares trading around ~19x projected 2027 earnings — reasonable given the growth rate.
