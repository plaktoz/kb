# Meta Hasn't Split Its Stock Since Its IPO. Would a Stock Split Get It Into the Dow?

source_url: https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/09/28/meta-hasn-t-split-its-stock-since-its-ipo-would-a-stock-split-get-it-into-the-dow/

By Daniel Sparks | September 28, 2026

---

Meta Platforms shares trade around $721, the highest among the Magnificent Seven, and the company has never split its stock since its 2012 IPO — even after hitting a record close near $790 in August 2025. The author notes that other tech giants, including Amazon and Nvidia, split their shares before joining the Dow Jones Industrial Average, which raises the question of whether Meta might do the same.

The piece explains that a stock split is purely cosmetic — it divides existing shares into more units without changing the company's overall value or valuation multiples. The author walks through a hypothetical 10-for-1 split scenario, noting per-share earnings would shrink proportionally while the price-to-earnings ratio would stay the same. Meta's closest brush with a split-like action was a 2016 shareholder-approved plan to issue new nonvoting shares, which the board later abandoned in 2017.

The author argues that Meta's price alone isn't the primary obstacle preventing Dow inclusion, since other high-priced stocks like Goldman Sachs and Caterpillar are already part of the price-weighted index. The piece suggests that Meta actually might carry outsized influence in the Dow at its current price if added, while a split could shrink its weighting to under 1%.

The bigger obstacle, according to the author, is that Alphabet already filled the communication-services slot in the Dow after replacing Verizon in June, leaving Meta and Tesla as the only Magnificent Seven companies not yet included. The article closes by discussing Meta's underlying business strength, citing improving ad pricing and revenue growth, and concludes with the author's personal view: "I doubt it, and I don't think the Dow is a strong reason for one," referring to the likelihood of a near-term stock split.
