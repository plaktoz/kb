# NAIS Research: 2026 AI in Independent Schools Report

AI has moved from the periphery of K–12 education toward its center at remarkable speed. Whatever a school’s position on AI, its students and teachers are increasingly encountering technology as a routine feature of academic life. The question facing school leaders is no longer whether AI will enter their communities. It already has.

Now schools must ask themselves how they are evolving in a student, and school, environment that is becoming more and more AI-driven. Yet the pace of adoption has outrun the field’s understanding of AI’s effects on students, and the early evidence urges caution alongside optimism.

This report is an effort to help independent school leaders navigate that tension with evidence rather than anecdote. It examines the adoption and impact of AI in independent schools through multi-year snapshot survey data collected by NAIS from teachers, department heads, and division heads, alongside in-depth interviews with teachers and school leaders across the country.

The survey data establish where independent schools stand: how policies have formalized, how adoption differs across roles, how training is developing, and how perceptions are evolving. The interviews reveal how and why schools are making choices—from developmental calibration to assessment redesign.

These findings offer a grounded picture of the role AI can play in the independent school learning ecosystem, one that neither dismisses the technology’s risks nor loses sight of what it makes possible.

Sections include:

- Snapshot Survey Analysis
- Interview Themes
- AI Policy Exemplars
- Methodology
