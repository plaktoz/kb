# Exclusive: This $100 Million Startup Wants to Give Companies AI Employees

At the San Francisco-based startup Anything AI, there's a robust team that seems like any fast-growing startup's organizational chart.

There's Emma, who investigates reported bugs across logs, databases, and code, then drafts the reply; Otis, who runs engineering operations and forecasts launch dates; Clementine, who manages the company's brand imagery; Tilda, who deals with the CEO's inbox and calendar and briefs him each morning; and JB, the office manager, who places orders for coconut waters and snacks.

The twist? None of these employees are human. They're AI agents, and Anything thinks they represent the future of how companies staff up and operate.

## 'They feel a little bit like coworkers'

Dhruv Amin and Marcus Lowe, two former Google product leads, created Anything last year to give nontechnical people a platform to build apps using artificial intelligence. The vibe-coding startup raised a $11 million financing round with a $100 million valuation backed by Footwork, Uncork Capital, Bessemer Venture Partners, and M13. According to the company, more than a million people have used Anything to build millions of apps, such as project trackers and customer relationship management platforms.

Now, the team is betting on an idea that works in tandem with their app-building service: hiring an employee could become as simple as typing out a description of the job you need done.

Their new product, Skydive, lets users build a custom AI "teammate" by describing the role or sifting through a catalog of four agent templates. Then, agents are added to Slack, email, and the web; plus, AI agents have their own phone numbers and can be reached on iMessage.

The agents are always on, working when human employees are sleeping.
