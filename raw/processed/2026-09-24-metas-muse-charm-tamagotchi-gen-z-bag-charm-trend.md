# Meta's Muse Charm: Tapping Into Gen Z's Bag Charm Trend

Meta's newly announced Muse Charm — a wearable AI gadget — visually recalls the Tamagotchi of the late 90s, but its design is more directly aligned with contemporary Gen Z fashion trends around decorative bag charms and retro-inspired tech accessories.

## The Bag Charm Trend

Gen Z has built a strong market for dangling accessories — beauty products, characters, and whimsical objects worn on bags. Hailey Bieber's Rhode lip gloss phone case helped normalize beauty-as-accessory, while the Labubu craze drove Pop Mart's revenue to $1.8 billion in 2024. Analysts project the global bag charm market will exceed $1 billion by 2030.

## The Apple Watch Keychain Connection

The more current reference isn't the Tamagotchi but rather the viral trend of repurposing Apple Watches as keychains or bag charms, with thousands of related products now available across Amazon, Etsy, and Walmart.

## Retro-Tech Overlap

This intersects with a broader revival of older technology — cameras, flip phones, CDs, cassette tapes — driven by Gen Z's desire for tangible, controllable tech in response to what they describe as the "soulless" nature of algorithm-driven platforms.

## The "Cyberdeck" Angle

Young women have also been customizing portable mini-computers fitted into clamshell purses, bedazzled with jewels and stickers, reflecting tech-as-personal-expression.

## The Muse Charm's Fit

The device features a customizable avatar, making it a reflection of the user's personality — consistent with the broader charm-as-identity trend.

## Skepticism

Despite being on-trend in form factor, the article questions whether Meta's history of privacy violations — FTC settlements, Congressional hearings, child safety lawsuits, and whistleblower disclosures — will undermine consumer trust. The piece notes Meta plans to monetize Muse by taking "a small fee from transactions," framing it as a potential tradeoff consumers may or may not accept.
