# IBM stock rebounds: Can it clear $237 despite AI spending fears?

IBM has regained $227.5 as fresh AI and fraud-detection wins improve the company-specific backdrop. The next hurdle is the 50-day SMA near $237, while a break back below $227.5 would reopen downside risk.

According to IBM, Sterling Order Management solution was recognized as a market leader in the IDC MarketScape study. The solution leverages AI capabilities to assist companies in managing orders, inventories, and supply chain operations. On the same day, IBM announced a Fiserv use case where Safer Payments solution is used for detecting fraudulent transactions. As per Fiserv, the system processes over 20 billion transactions every year.

The broader market backdrop, however, remains unstable. Even Jensen Huang's return has failed to eliminate concerns over the scale and potential returns of AI investment. Investors are again turning cautious ahead of U.S. inflation data that could influence the Federal Reserve's December policy decision.

## IBM stock recovery faces a key test at the 50-day SMA

IBM shares have moved back above the key $227.5 level, invalidating the bearish scenario outlined previously.

The technical structure has improved modestly, bringing the 50-day SMA near $237 back into focus. A break above this level could open the way toward resistance at $242–244.

Macroeconomic and geopolitical risks, however, remain unresolved, while Huang's return has so far been largely a sentiment-driven catalyst that has not changed the fundamental questions surrounding the AI sector.

Renewed selling across technology stocks therefore cannot be ruled out. The first sign of pressure on IBM would be a sustained move back below $227.5. In that case, the next downside targets would be $218 and $212.3.

## Market anxiety keeps IBM exposed to renewed selling

Despite the recent rebound, fear and uncertainty remain elevated across equity markets. Any deterioration in sentiment could trigger another sharp wave of selling, pushing investors back toward dollar liquidity and government bonds.

For IBM, this means that an improving technical setup does not yet guarantee a sustainable recovery. As long as questions over the returns on massive AI investment and broader macroeconomic risks remain unresolved, the stock is likely to stay sensitive even to relatively minor negative developments.
