# FDA Approves Lilly's New Oral Combo Pill for Common Breast Cancer Mutation

The FDA approved a combination oral therapy on September 18 — Inluriyo (imlunestrant) paired with Verzenio (abemaciclib), both made by Eli Lilly — for adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative advanced or metastatic breast cancer carrying an ESR1 mutation that progressed after hormone therapy.

Inluriyo blocks and degrades estrogen receptors, while Verzenio inhibits CDK4/6 proteins that drive cancer cell proliferation. Verzenio had been approved since 2017; Inluriyo received standalone approval in September 2025.

Clinical trial data (EMBER-3, 159 participants with ESR1 mutations) showed those on the combination went a median of 11.1 months without disease progression or death, versus 5.5 months on Inluriyo alone. Tumor shrinkage occurred in 35% vs. 15%, respectively. Survival data remain unavailable.

ESR1 mutations are present in roughly 1% of tumors at diagnosis but rise to 10–50% in hormone therapy-resistant metastatic cases. The Guardant360 CDx blood test is FDA-approved to detect the mutation.

Key side effects include diarrhea (86%), low white blood cell counts, liver issues, and blood clots. About 6% discontinued due to adverse effects.

Separately, AstraZeneca's Etcamah (camizestrant) received accelerated FDA approval on September 4 for ESR1 mutations detected during first-line treatment.
