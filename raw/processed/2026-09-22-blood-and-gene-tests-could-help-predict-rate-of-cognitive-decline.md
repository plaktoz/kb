# Blood and Gene Tests Could Help Predict Rate of Cognitive Decline

## Summary

A new study published in *The Lancet Neurology* examined whether combining blood-based biomarker p-tau217 with APOE genetic information could better predict cognitive decline risk and timing.

## Key Findings

- Higher blood p-tau217 levels correlated with greater cognitive impairment risk, with stronger associations among APOE-ε4 gene carriers
- Among APOE-ε4 carriers, each standard deviation increase in p-tau217 was linked to a **24% shorter time** to cognitive impairment vs. 13% in non-carriers
- The biomarker's predictive strength varied by racial/ethnic group, though APOE-ε4 did not significantly modify that racial difference

## Study Details

Researchers pooled data from **8,582 participants** (mean age 70; ~66% female) across seven major cohort studies in the U.S., Canada, and Dominican Republic, representing diverse racial/ethnic backgrounds.

## Expert Perspective

Dr. Dung Trinh noted that p-tau217 may be "most informative when interpreted in the context of an individual's genetic background rather than as a stand-alone biomarker."

## Limitations & Future Needs

- Most cohorts had only a single p-tau217 measurement
- Outcome combined multiple types of cognitive impairment
- Incomplete kidney function data (which can affect biomarker levels)
- The study demonstrates **association**, not causation
- Prospective validation and clinical trials are still needed before routine clinical use
