# We don't need AI regulation — leave safety to us, Nvidia's Jensen Huang says

At Salesforce's Dreamforce conference, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang argued against new AI regulation, framing AI safety as an engineering challenge rather than a legal one. He views AI as fundamentally just hardware and software — complex, but ultimately a computing system built and controllable by humans.

Huang contended that market forces are sufficient: companies should simply refrain from releasing products they aren't confident are safe. He dismissed the idea that speed and safety are mutually exclusive: "innovation, speed, and safe products … it's a false choice."

The article notes his position conveniently aligns with Nvidia's financial interests given its massive growth from the AI boom. TechCrunch's author pushes back by citing real-world examples where self-regulation failed — including the 2024 CrowdStrike outage, Meta's $18B child safety settlement, and harms linked to AI chatbots.

The piece also highlights Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella's view that AI safety concerns should be shared globally, including by China. The author concludes that given Huang's influence — including direct access to President Trump — his anti-regulation stance may well carry the day.
