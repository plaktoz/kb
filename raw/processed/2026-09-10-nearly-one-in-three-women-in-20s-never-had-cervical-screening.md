# Nearly 1 in 3 Women in Their 20s Have Never Had a Cervical Screening

New research in *JAMA Network Open* reveals that nearly 32% of women ages 21–29 had never undergone cervical cancer screening as of 2023, compared to just 12.5% in 2005. Among women ages 30–65, the never-screened rate rose from 5.3% to 14.8% over the same period.

Disparities were noted across race, ethnicity, education, and insurance status. Asian women showed the highest never-screened prevalence in both age groups, while uninsured women 30–65 were significantly more likely to have never been screened.

Contributing barriers may include cost, transportation, insurance gaps, discomfort, and misinformation about HPV vaccination.

Separately, regional- and distant-stage cervical cancer incidence each grew by more than 2% annually after 2017 among women ages 30–64.

The American Cancer Society recommends screening beginning at age 25, with a primary HPV test every five years as the preferred approach. Regular screening is advised even after HPV vaccination, as the vaccine reduces but does not eliminate risk.
