# BMI vs. Waist Size: Belly Fat Matters More Than Weight for Death Risk

A study published in the *Journal of the American Geriatrics Society* examined 6,905 adults aged 65+ from 2011–2024, finding that waist circumference is a stronger mortality predictor than BMI.

## Key Findings

- Overweight BMI was linked to a **46% lower** all-cause mortality risk in older males
- Class I/II obesity associated with **51% lower** risk in that group
- High waist circumference linked to a **24% higher** all-cause mortality risk in both sexes
- Underweight BMI correlated with the **highest** mortality risk overall

**Waist thresholds flagged as high-risk:**
- Men: 40+ inches
- Women: 35+ inches

## Expert Context

Dr. Mir Ali noted that high waist circumference "is a better predictor of mortality risk" than BMI, since abdominal fat affects internal organs.

Cardiologist Dr. Jayne Morgan suggested elevated weight in older adults may signal "less frailty" rather than true health. She also cautioned that BMI can obscure risk in women — particularly those with normal weight but redistributed abdominal fat — a pattern carrying higher cardiovascular and metabolic burden despite a normal BMI classification.
