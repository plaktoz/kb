# IBM's Summer of Contradictions: A Historic Rout, a Quantum Milestone, and a Patent Windfall

source_url: https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/boerse/news/unternehmensnachrichten/ibm-s-summer-of-contradictions-a-historic-rout-a-quantum-milestone/69903229

date: 2026-07-31

---

IBM's stock fell 25% on weak earnings, yet quantum advances, a patent deal with Circle, and a century-old dividend streak signal long-term resilience. IBM's share price has been cut by more than a quarter since January, yet the company keeps handing investors reasons to look past the carnage — in the span of three weeks this summer, Big Blue unveiled a breakthrough in quantum computing, struck a patent deal with a major stablecoin player, and confirmed a dividend streak stretching back more than a century.

## The day everything changed

July 14 marked an inflection point: preliminary quarterly results came in below analyst expectations, and the market responded with a 25% single-day collapse — described by some observers as the worst trading session in the company's history. The damage was concentrated in the infrastructure division, where the flagship IBM Z mainframe business is losing ground as customers redirect capital toward AI chips and servers. CEO Arvind Krishna acknowledged the shift openly, and the company trimmed its full-year revenue guidance. The software segment, buoyed by Red Hat, continues to grow, but that growth is being overshadowed by the structural decline in legacy hardware. IBM now expects currency-adjusted revenue growth of 4–5% for the year, with free cash flow projected to rise by roughly $1 billion.

## A quantum counterweight

Nine days after the rout, on July 23, IBM announced the acquisition of HRL Laboratories, a pioneer in quantum-dot technology previously owned by Boeing and General Motors, expected to close by the end of Q3 2026. A week later, on July 30, IBM and Algorithmiq reported progress toward "quantum advantage": using the Heron quantum processor, the teams simulated heterogeneous quantum materials — a task that has eluded classical supercomputers. Management has been candid that quantum computing won't contribute meaningfully to profits until the end of the decade.

## The patent play

IBM sold more than 680 patent families — nearly 1,000 granted patents worldwide — to Circle, the company behind the USDC stablecoin. The portfolio covers blockchain technology, banking, insurance, and cloud security; financial terms weren't disclosed. Circle plans to deploy the IP across its Arc blockchain, AI-driven financial tools, and the Circle Payments Network. IBM had already ranked among the largest holders of blockchain patents in the US (790 patents per a December 2025 PatSnap analysis, alongside Advanced New Technologies and Bank of America), built up since its blockchain push in the mid-2010s.

## The numbers behind the noise

The stock closed at 192.24 euros, down 2.89% on the day, leaving shares 34.36% below their 52-week high of 292.85 euros reached in early June. Since the July low of 175.14 euros, the stock has recovered roughly 10%, though the rebound looks fragile — the RSI sits at 39.4 (approaching but not yet oversold), and annualized 30-day volatility stands at nearly 86%. Analyst price targets average 212.87 euros, implying about 10% upside from current levels.

## A dividend as a lifeline

Amid the turbulence, the board confirmed the regular quarterly dividend of $1.69 per share (payment date September 10, 2026; record date August 10). IBM has paid a dividend every year since 1916 — one of the longest streaks in the US equity market — currently serving as a buffer against the falling share price even as debt load during this transition remains a topic of debate.

## The core question

The central tension of IBM's summer is whether the quantum promise can gain substance quickly enough to offset double-digit declines in the traditional hardware business. Until the mainframe business stabilizes or quantum computing produces its first revenues, the stock appears caught between two narratives — a legacy business in retreat, and a future that has not yet arrived.
