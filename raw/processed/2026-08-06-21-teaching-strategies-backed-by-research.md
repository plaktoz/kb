# 21 Teaching Strategies Backed by Research (2026 Guide)

A research-grounded roundup of 21 evidence-based teaching strategies drawn from Barak Rosenshine's Principles of Instruction, John Hattie's Visible Learning meta-analyses, and the UK's Education Endowment Foundation (EEF) guidance reports. The guide organizes practical classroom techniques around four pillars: direct instruction (clear modeling, worked examples, and explicit step-by-step teaching before independent practice), effective questioning (cold-calling, wait time, and questions that check for understanding rather than just recall), feedback (specific, timely, and focused on the task and process rather than the person), and retrieval practice (low-stakes quizzing and spaced review that strengthens long-term memory over passive re-reading).

The throughline across all three research bases is that strategies producing the largest effect sizes on student outcomes are typically the least flashy: consistent formative feedback, deliberate retrieval of prior learning, and breaking complex skills into small, scaffolded steps outperform novelty-driven or purely discovery-based approaches for most learners, particularly novices still building foundational knowledge.
