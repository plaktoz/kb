# How investors are interpreting corporate earnings amid uncertainty over interest rates and Fed signals

Wall Street investors are heading into a pivotal week preparing for fresh US labor market data and another wave of corporate earnings, while balancing geopolitical risk, shifting monetary policy expectations, and swings in major tech stocks. The S&P 500 edged higher last week but remains about 1.6% below its June record high. Microsoft shares surged on an upbeat cloud outlook, while Meta Platforms fell on deteriorating free cash flow.

Investors are still assessing the Fed's decision to hold rates steady, with several policymakers signaling another rate increase later this year remains possible. New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh reiterated his commitment to the 2% inflation target but offered limited clarity on how the Fed will get there. Economists expect the July jobs report to show about 83,000 new jobs and unemployment steady at 4.3%; a stronger-than-expected report could reinforce tightening expectations.

Roughly a quarter of S&P 500 companies report this week, including Caterpillar, Palantir, and Merck. LSEG IBES estimates put aggregate Q2 earnings growth at 29.3% year over year. Investors are also awaiting SpaceX's first earnings report since its IPO. According to FactSet, EPS growth for the quarter is expected near 22.5%; Bloomberg forecasts closer to 25%, which would be the strongest since 2021. Energy sector profits are projected to surge 121% on the Iran-driven oil price spike, while information technology (Nvidia, Apple, Micron) is expected to grow about 62%; healthcare earnings are projected to decline roughly 9%.

Capital Economics warned that AI-related equities may be approaching a point where it becomes difficult to justify lofty earnings forecasts and aggressive capex assumptions, cautioning that a downward revision could trigger a broader correction.
