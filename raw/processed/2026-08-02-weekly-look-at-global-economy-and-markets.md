# The Weekly Look at the Global Economy and Markets

Markets faced a week of dramatic developments: severe tech-sector deleveraging followed by stabilization (Korea's Kospi fell 17% over three days then surged 18% the next day; a highly levered tech fund, Situational Awareness, reported a 46% loss for July alone), unusual coordinated currency intervention (Japan spent over $50 billion, with additional US and Korean support, to move the yen from a multi-decade low of 164 to 157 per dollar), a repricing of the yield curve, a new Federal Reserve communication style under Chair Kevin Warsh, and fragile calm in the Middle East.

On rates: rising longer-term yields reflect real-rate pressure from heavy capital demand for AI infrastructure financing, not merely Fed communication — a dynamic El-Erian previously flagged in the Financial Times. The FOMC held rates steady with three dissenters favoring an immediate hike; Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack argued inflation is increasingly demand-driven, not just supply-chain related.

Data: US inflation (PCE, CPI, PPI) came in softer than expected; Eurozone inflation surprised to the upside (2.9% vs. 2.8% prior), raising the odds of a September ECB hike. The Bank of Japan held rates with a hawkish tone, but markets tested its resolve, forcing the historic multi-country yen intervention. China's official PMI fell to 49.2 from 50.3, the lowest since February. US Q2 GDP came in at 1.5% (down from 2.1%), though Chicago PMI beat expectations at 57.6 and University of Michigan consumer sentiment rose to 55.2.

Week's price action: significant equity dispersion (Microsoft surged while Apple fell; SpaceX shares fell below $110, well under its $135 IPO price); 10-year Treasury yield closed at 4.73%, 30-year at 5.27%; the 2s-10s curve steepened to 44 basis points; Brent oil rose to $88/barrel.

Week ahead: the US jobs report (consensus: 85,000 jobs added in July, 4.2% unemployment) headlines a big data week, alongside JOLTS, Challenger layoffs, and ISM data. Overhanging everything: whether the late-Saturday de-escalation on US-Iran tensions (Trump calling off strikes) holds, since renewed escalation could spike energy prices again. Earnings to watch: Caterpillar, Disney, Palantir, and Uber.
