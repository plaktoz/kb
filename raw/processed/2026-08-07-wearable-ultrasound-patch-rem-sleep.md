# Wearable ultrasound patch boosts REM sleep without drugs or surgery

Researchers led by the University of Texas at Austin created a soft wearable patch, called NEUSLeeP, that improved REM sleep in real-world testing without medication or surgery. The device combines gentle ultrasound stimulation with electrodes that track brain activity in real time, allowing it to influence deep brain regions involved in sleep while monitoring the brain's response.

"This is the first time we've been able to noninvasively target deep brain regions involved in REM sleep, while simultaneously monitoring brain activity," said Kai Wing "Kevin" Tang, who led the research. In a study of 28 participants published in Nature Communications, users entered REM sleep 43 minutes sooner on average and remained in that stage about 16 minutes longer. Benefits appeared in both healthy sleepers and those with some sleep difficulties, and the patch was described as comfortable and safe with minimal adverse effects.

Among healthy participants, stimulation also increased heart rate variability (an indicator of stress-response capacity), and brain imaging showed changes in circuits tied to emotion — suggesting potential support for mood regulation. "REM sleep is not just about dreaming — it's about emotional reset and stress adaptation," said co-PI Gregory Fonzo. "By enhancing REM, we may help people better cope with stress and improve their overall well-being."

REM disruptions have been linked to depression, anxiety, and PTSD. The team plans larger trials to evaluate whether NEUSLeeP could help people with PTSD, depression, and chronic insomnia, and is working with UT's commercialization unit, Discovery to Impact, to move the technology toward market; a patent application has been filed.

Journal reference: Tang et al., "Skin-attached bioadhesive patch enabling ultrasound deep brain stimulation and real-time electrophysiological monitoring for REM sleep enhancement," Nature Communications, 2026.
