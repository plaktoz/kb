# OpenAI Revenue at $50bn: Why One Number Triggered an AI Selloff

OpenAI revenue was running at about $50bn a year at the end of September 2026, according to a Financial Times report confirmed by [Reuters](https://www.zawya.com/en/special-coverage/openais-september-annualized-revenue-nears-50bln-less-than-previously-indicated-source-says-1586243). That is roughly $18bn to $20bn below the $68bn to $70bn figure that had circulated among investors. Reports attribute the gap mainly to accounting. The higher number included gross sales made through cloud partners, which OpenAI does not book in full as its own revenue.

The news hit the AI trade on Thursday 8 October. The Nasdaq Composite fell 1.25% to 27,193.34, its worst session since mid-August, as reported by [Wealth Professional](https://www.wealthprofessional.ca/investments/equity-markets/ai-selloff-drags-nasdaq-down-125-as-tsx-holds-its-ground/393743). CoreWeave lost nearly 8% and Oracle about 5.5%. Nvidia slipped 2.9%. On 9 October, Bloomberg reported that OpenAI had told investors it still expects a run rate of $70bn or more by year-end, as summarised by [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-revenue-70bn-year-end-50bn-september).

*The information in this article is provided for educational purposes only and does not constitute financial advice. Consult a financial advisor before making investment decisions.*

This article explains how the OpenAI revenue run rate is measured and why the market reacted so sharply. It also looks at circular financing between chip suppliers and AI labs. Finally, it sets out the late-October Big Tech earnings that markets are watching next.

## Why Is OpenAI Revenue Lower Than Expected?

Based on the reports so far, the two figures mainly measure different things.

### Gross and Net Revenue Accounting

OpenAI and Anthropic book partner sales in different ways, as reported by [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-revenue-70bn-year-end-50bn-september) citing Bloomberg. Anthropic counts the gross amount of some sales made through cloud partners. OpenAI counts only its own share of partner revenue.

| Point | OpenAI | Anthropic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sales through cloud partners | Books its net share | Books the gross amount of certain sales |
| Partners named | Microsoft | Amazon Web Services, Google, Microsoft |
| Effect on headline run rate | Lower | Higher |
| Revenue shared with Microsoft | About 20% historically | Not applicable |

*Source: [The Next Web, citing Bloomberg and The Information](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-revenue-70bn-year-end-50bn-september).*

Investors tried to build a like-for-like comparison with Anthropic. That calculation produced a number close to $70bn. A source familiar with the documents told CNN that the higher figure did not come from OpenAI, as noted by [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-revenue-70bn-year-end-50bn-september). Reuters, by contrast, reported that OpenAI had indicated a figure near $70bn at a separate investor event.

### How Is Run Rate Calculated?

A run rate usually takes one month of revenue and multiplies it by 12. Analysts consider it a sometimes misleading metric, as noted in the [Reuters report](https://www.zawya.com/en/special-coverage/openais-september-annualized-revenue-nears-50bln-less-than-previously-indicated-source-says-1586243). The figure is unaudited. It also changes with the accounting choices described above.

* A run rate is a snapshot of one month projected over a year.
* It can move fast when a company grows quickly or loses a large contract.
* Private companies are under looser disclosure rules than listed ones.
* Comparisons between AI labs may be misleading if their booking methods differ.

## Is OpenAI Revenue Growth Still Strong?

The reported growth rates remain high, although they rest on unaudited figures. OpenAI's total run rate grew 77% in the third quarter, according to a person familiar with the investor deck cited by [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-revenue-70bn-year-end-50bn-september). Its enterprise run rate grew 107% over the same period.

Chief financial officer Sarah Friar put growth from end-June to end-September at 70%. This was reported by The Information and summarised by [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-revenue-70bn-year-end-50bn-september). To reach $70bn by year-end from about $50bn, the run rate would need to rise by about 40% in a single quarter.

### Why Circularity Matters for Markets

When suppliers fund their customers, revenue across the chain depends on the same few labs. A doubt about one lab's income can therefore spread across suppliers and lenders at once. This may help explain why a single accounting report coincided with falls in shares worth trillions of dollars.

> The 8 October drop appeared to reflect doubts about the numbers behind the AI supply chain. When the reported run rate came in about $20bn below the circulated figure, many companies that rely on OpenAI's future spending traded lower within hours.

There is a fair counterpoint. Equity stakes and supply deals are common in fast-growing industries. They can also help secure capacity. The risk lies in concentration, because several of these contracts rest on one loss-making customer.

## How Traders Often Approach an AI Selloff

During a stock market selloff, traders often focus on a few practical questions. These are observations of common market behaviour. They are not recommendations.

* Traders often separate cash-rich companies from those that rely on debt.
* Many watch the economic calendar closely in the weeks of major earnings.
* Some reduce position sizes when volatility rises.
* Others wait for the next set of hard data before forming a view.
* Some traders give more weight to Big Tech spending plans than to reports about private companies.

### Risk Management Checklist

Leverage magnifies both gains and losses. Volatile sectors such as AI can move several per cent in a day.

| Step | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Setting a stop-loss level before opening a position | Helps limit losses if the market moves against a position |
| Sizing positions relative to account equity | Helps manage risk across several trades |
| Checking the calendar for earnings and data | Helps identify scheduled events that may cause price gaps |
| Reviewing margin requirements | Helps in monitoring the risk of margin close-outs |

*General educational checklist. A stop-loss may not protect against gaps or slippage.*

## Conclusion

Based on the reports so far, the OpenAI headline appears to be mainly an accounting story. A figure built on gross partner sales met a figure built on net sales, and the gap was about $20bn. Markets appeared to react because so much of the AI chain depends on OpenAI's ability to keep growing and keep paying.

The next tests are the Fed meeting and Big Tech earnings in late October. Spending plans from Microsoft and Alphabet may tell investors more than any private run rate. Meta and Amazon are expected to report soon after, with dates yet to be confirmed.
