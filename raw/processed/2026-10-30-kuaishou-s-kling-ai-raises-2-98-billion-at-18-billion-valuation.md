# Kuaishou's Kling AI raises $2.98 billion at $18 billion valuation

Kuaishou Technology announced that its AI video unit, Beijing Kling, completed a $2.98 billion funding round on October 30, 2026, valuing the business at approximately $18 billion post-money while Kuaishou retains majority control.

## What happened

Kuaishou Technology announced on October 30, 2026, that its AI video generation unit, Beijing Kling, has completed a fundraising round of approximately 19.8 billion yuan, or US$2.98 billion. The transaction values the unit at roughly US$15 billion pre-money and US$18 billion post-money. Kuaishou will retain two-thirds of the equity and 52.45% of the voting rights through its holding vehicle, Lucky Labs, ensuring the parent company maintains control over the subsidiary's strategic direction.

Kuaishou Technology officially announced on October 30, 2026, that its AI video unit, Beijing Kling, has closed a financing round worth approximately 19.8 billion yuan, equivalent to US$2.98 billion. This figure was reported by Reuters and corroborated by other outlets such as MarketScreener and TechNode. The round values the business at approximately US$15 billion before the investment and US$18 billion after, a standard pre-money and post-money calculation that aligns with the size of the raise.

The corporate structure of the deal ensures that Kuaishou maintains significant control. Through its holding vehicle, Lucky Labs, Kuaishou will hold two-thirds of the equity in Beijing Kling and 52.45% of the voting rights. This governance arrangement allows the parent company to remain in charge of the unit's strategic decisions while utilizing outside capital to fund the next phase of its generative video initiatives. The gap between equity ownership and voting rights suggests that outside investors have negotiated specific governance protections, though the exact mechanisms have not been publicly disclosed.

The announcement does not provide a detailed breakdown of the use of proceeds, nor does it confirm the identities of the participating investors. While the round is substantial, the source material does not establish whether this constitutes a record for AI video financing, as no comparative was provided. The lack of specific product news, such as new model releases or pricing changes, indicates that this is primarily a financial and structural development rather than a product launch.

## Why it matters

This significant capital injection signals that generative video has matured into a capital-intensive industry requiring substantial resources for and data. By isolating Kling as a separate entity with dedicated funding, Kuaishou can accelerate development without directly impacting its parent company's cash flow, while the high valuation provides a market for AI video assets. The structure allows outside investors to gain exposure to the fast-growing video generation sector while Kuaishou preserves its integration with the short-video platform.

The $2.98 billion raise highlights the intense capital requirements of the AI video generation sector. Video models are significantly more -hungry than text-based models, necessitating large investments in GPUs, data licensing, and safety infrastructure. This funding provides Beijing Kling with a substantial war chest to sustain operations and expand capabilities without relying solely on Kuaishou's parent company cash flow.

By carving out Kling as a distinct entity with a high valuation, Kuaishou creates a clear market reference point for the value of AI video assets. This structure isolates the heavy spending associated with AI development from the parent company's reported financial results, potentially protecting Kuaishou's broader financial metrics while allowing the AI unit to operate with the agility of a startup. The 52.45% voting stake ensures that Kuaishou can continue to integrate Kling's technology with its core short-video platform and advertising business.

For the broader AI industry, this move underscores the trend of established tech platforms spinning out their AI divisions to attract specialized venture capital. It also intensifies competition for independent AI video startups, which must now compete against a rival with dedicated, substantial funding and the backing of a major listed company. The deal reflects a maturing market where capital allocation is becoming a key differentiator in the race to dominate generative media.
