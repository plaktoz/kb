# As some medical schools embrace RFK Jr.'s nutrition push, they deftly sidestep MAHA priorities

McGovern Medical School at UT Health Houston is adding hands-on nutrition experiences this year — cooking demonstrations in a teaching kitchen, tours of a campus "holistic garden," and a culinary-medicine elective — in response to HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s call for medical schools to teach more nutrition as part of the Trump administration's push to address rising chronic-disease rates. Kennedy has blamed part of the problem on doctors not routinely discussing diet and nutrition with patients.

The push includes 71 nutrition-related topics HHS wants future physicians versed in under its Advancing Nutrition Education initiative, including enhancing nutrient bioavailability through soaking, sprouting, and fermenting foods, and prioritizing food-based medicine as the primary approach to metabolic-driven chronic disease. So far, 73 of 163 M.D.-granting and 48 D.O.-granting medical schools (54 M.D. and 19 D.O. programs, across 36 states) have signed on — the vast majority of schools, including several leading institutions, have not yet joined.

Even participating schools told STAT they plan to avoid topics not backed by evidence, including some aligned with the broader Make America Healthy Again (MAHA) movement Kennedy champions. Nutrition experts cautioned that some HHS-requested topics could lead to "wellness creep" in medical education — i.e., the introduction of scientifically unsupported wellness content into formal medical curricula.
