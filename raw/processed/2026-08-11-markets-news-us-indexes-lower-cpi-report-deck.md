# Markets News, Aug. 11, 2026: U.S. Indexes End Lower for Second Straight Day; Oil Prices Extend Gains; CPI Report On Deck

Major U.S. stock indexes closed lower for a second straight day as investors awaited a key inflation reading. The Nasdaq Composite, Dow Jones Industrial Average, and S&P 500 finished Tuesday down 0.6%, 0.3%, and 0.3% respectively, following a modestly red Monday after the S&P 500 closed at a record high the prior Friday.

Investor attention shifts to Wednesday's July CPI inflation report, which will help set Fed rate expectations. Friday's softer-than-expected July jobs report had eased concern about an imminent rate hike, but a hot inflation reading could revive those worries.

Crude oil futures rose modestly as investors watched for developments in the Strait of Hormuz: WTI rose 1.6% to about $83.50/barrel, Brent rose 1.6% to $89.15. The 10-year Treasury yield was above 4.69%, down from Monday's close, after earlier rising near 4.74% on fears rising energy prices could trigger inflation.

Big Tech retreated. Alphabet (GOOGL) led the Magnificent Seven lower with a nearly 4% decline; Nvidia (NVDA) closed fractionally lower despite signing MOUs with six asset managers for up to $500 billion in financing for hyperscaler AI data center build-outs. The Roundhill Magnificent 7 ETF (MAGS) fell 0.9%. SpaceX (SPCX) sank about 4%. Intel (INTC), which fell 4% the prior day after announcing a $15 billion stock offering, ended slightly higher after boosting the offering to $20 billion.

Riot Platforms (RIOT) advanced more than 4% after Bloomberg reported a roughly $9 billion, 20-year cloud deal to supply Anthropic with 191 megawatts of computing capacity from its Rockdale, Texas facility. In post-earnings moves: On Holding (ONON) sank 20%, Hims & Hers (HIMS) slipped nearly 4%, Sea Limited (SE) jumped 14% on Shopee revenue strength ($7.79B revenue, up 48% YoY), Plug Power (PLUG) surged 5%, and Cardinal Health (CAH) rose over 1%.

Only one Dow component — Home Depot (HD), up 1.1% — gained at least 1%; Honeywell (HON) led decliners, down 5.3%. Alphabet's decline dragged the Communication Services sector down 1.9%, the S&P 500's worst-performing sector.

Analysts note a market regime shift: Morgan Stanley's Michael Wilson wrote the market is "transitioning from an early- to mid-cycle regime," with leadership shifting toward higher-quality, durable-earnings stocks. The S&P 500 record run in April-May (fueled by AI infrastructure spending) stalled in June as semiconductor stocks entered a bear market, while the equal-weight S&P 500 kept rising — outperforming the market-weight index by the widest margin since 2009, per Goldman Sachs, reflecting broadening profit growth.

Economists expect Wednesday's CPI to show headline inflation of 3.4% year-over-year (down from 3.5% in June) and core inflation of 2.5% (down from 2.6%), which would mark a second straight month of deceleration.

Separately, the U.S. unemployment rate ticked down to 4.1% in July, its lowest in over a year, marking the 57th month at or below 4.5% — a record. Apollo Global Management (APO) shares jumped roughly 5% after its Sports Capital arm agreed to provide the New York Yankees' holding company with $2.6 billion in financing.
