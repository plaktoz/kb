# Nvidia's AI Advantage Is Moving Beyond the GPU

Nvidia's competitive edge is expanding beyond its dominant GPU business into the broader systems that support AI infrastructure at scale.

For years, Nvidia's story was simple: it was the primary supplier of high-end GPUs during the AI boom. Hyperscalers like Amazon and Google have since developed their own chips, raising questions about Nvidia's durability. However, following its recent earnings, a new narrative has emerged.

As AI compute scales to gigawatt levels, orchestrating that compute has become enormously complex. Nvidia has built much of the hardware needed for this orchestration layer. Its current **Vera Rubin architecture** bundles the Rubin GPU with supporting components — the Vera CPU, the Groq 3 LPX inference accelerator, and specialized networking and storage racks.

The Vera CPU focuses on data orchestration. As Jason Hardy, Nvidia's VP of storage technology, explained: *"We saw upwards of 3x improvement in these operations, where the Vera CPU is allowing for acceleration."*

OpenAI's custom **Jalapeño chip** reflects similar thinking from a different angle — it was designed to *"minimize data movement and communication delays"* by keeping workloads within one integrated system.

The broader insight: efficiency gains now come from smarter data traffic management, not just raw processor power. Nvidia faces competition at this new layer too, but currently holds a commanding early lead.
