# Google faces a drop toward $250 as AI investment risks deepen the sell-off

GOOGL remains under selling pressure on November 20, trading around $280–281 despite an attempt to recover from yesterday's sell-off, during which Alphabet shares lost 3.46% and closed at $272.66. On the 4H chart, the stock remains in a pronounced downtrend following its decline from the May high near $409.

Further pressure comes from deteriorating conditions in the U.S. stock market: on November 19, the Nasdaq Composite lost approximately 2.3%, while the S&P 500 declined 2%. Technology stocks continue to face selling pressure today amid growing concerns about AI company valuations and the returns on investments in computing infrastructure. As a result, the current rebound from support at $272–275 remains corrective and does not change the negative outlook for GOOGL.

## Anthropic's record IPO increases Alphabet's exposure to AI valuations

One of the most significant developments over the past 24 hours was the completion of Anthropic's initial public offering on November 20, which provided greater clarity on the actual scale of Alphabet's investment in the developer of Claude. According to Anthropic's official announcement, the total IPO proceeds reached approximately $93.5 billion after the underwriters fully exercised their option, of which the company itself received around $91.6 billion.

At the same time, a $26 billion private placement of Class N shares among strategic investors, including Google, Microsoft, Nvidia, AMD, and Broadcom, was completed. Of particular importance to Alphabet are the details in the final prospectus, according to which Google owns approximately 1.775 billion Anthropic shares, representing 13.4% of its capital after the offering. At the IPO price of $83, this stake is worth approximately $147 billion, while the purchase of an additional 120.48 million shares for $10 billion increases Alphabet's financial exposure to the future valuation of the developer of Claude.

However, yesterday's price action demonstrated that an increase in Anthropic's value does not necessarily translate into gains for GOOGL. ANT shares opened at $111.50 but closed their first trading session around $97.17, significantly below the opening price. Today, they came under renewed pressure, losing approximately 9% in early trading. For Alphabet, such volatility means additional fluctuations in the market value of its investment portfolio, while continued selling in ANT could intensify pressure on Google shares. At the same time, the public offering provides greater transparency in valuing Alphabet's stake in Anthropic but also increases investor concerns about excessive valuations among artificial intelligence companies.

## Cloud commitments raise questions about long-term profitability

Equally important details are contained in Anthropic's published prospectus regarding its long-term commitments to using Google's computing infrastructure. According to the documents, the developer of Claude has committed to spending at least $121.3 billion on Alphabet's cloud services between April 2026 and July 2033. The agreement provides for minimum payments regardless of actual resource usage, subject to certain specified exceptions.

On average, this represents approximately $16.7 billion in annual commitments to Google, creating a significant potential revenue stream for Google Cloud. For Alphabet, such a contract is particularly important following the cloud division's 103% growth in the third quarter, as it provides greater visibility into future utilization of data centers and specialized TPU processors.

However, the downside is that long-term demand becomes dependent on the financial stability of customers that are themselves incurring enormous expenses to develop artificial intelligence. According to the final prospectus, Anthropic's total commitments for computing resources, equipment, and infrastructure leases amount to approximately $900.9 billion, with around $451.6 billion expected to be spent by the end of 2030.

Such substantial commitments increase risks across the entire industry, as the ability to fulfill these contracts depends on AI companies maintaining high revenue growth rates and securing financing. As a result, Anthropic's large commitments simultaneously improve the predictability of Google Cloud's future revenue and heighten investor concerns about the sustainability of the current AI infrastructure financing model. This helps explain why positive corporate developments have so far failed to produce a sustained recovery in Alphabet shares.

## Project Suncatcher explores orbital data centers

Another development attracting attention on November 20 is the new agreement announced yesterday between Google and Blue Origin to use the New Glenn rocket to launch satellites equipped with computing hardware as part of Project Suncatcher. The first dedicated heavy-lift launch is scheduled for the first quarter of 2029. According to details published by Google, the satellites will be equipped with specialized TPU processors, with each satellite's computing capacity expected to be roughly equivalent to one rack in a conventional data center.

The central idea of the project is to use nearly continuous solar power in orbit to perform artificial intelligence tasks, potentially reducing the dependence of future computing infrastructure expansion on terrestrial power grids, land availability, and cooling systems. For Alphabet, this initiative is strategically important, as energy constraints are gradually becoming one of the main obstacles to building new data centers.

Nevertheless, GOOGL remains under pressure, with a continued risk of a break below support around $280 and a decline toward $260–250, which is most likely to occur if selling pressure persists across stock markets. A return of the bulls to the markets, as stated in Google faces a drop toward $250 as AI investment risks deepen the sell-off, could support a rebound toward $320–340.
