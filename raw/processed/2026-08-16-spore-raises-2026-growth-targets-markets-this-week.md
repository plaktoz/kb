# S'pore raises 2026 growth targets; graphics card shortage to drive up PC prices: Markets this week

- SGX plans to expand its ETF offerings and partnerships to strengthen its role as Southeast Asia's key multi-asset exchange, with $21 billion assets under management and possible new product types.
- JPMorgan raised the Singapore stock target due to strong economic growth, a robust Singapore dollar, and narrowing valuation gaps, with the Straits Times Index (STI) expected to rise up to 7,000 in 12 months.
- Property demand on the mainland boosted CDL's profits significantly, while Sentosa Cove properties saw many resale losses; meanwhile, companies like Singtel, Sembcorp, Wilmar, and Shopee showed mixed financial results and future growth plans.

SINGAPORE — In a surprise move on Aug 11, the Ministry of Trade and Industry upgraded its 2026 economic growth forecast from between 2 and 4 per cent to 4.5 and 5.5 per cent, reflecting better-than-expected performance in the first half of the year. The ministry cited the acceleration in global AI-related capital expenditure for its improved outlook for the rest of 2026.

On Aug 12, JPMorgan Chase raised its targets for Singapore stocks, citing stronger economic growth, an appreciating Singapore dollar and expectations that local shares would close some of their valuation discount to other developed markets, including Hong Kong. JPMorgan wrote that the STI may hit the 7,000 mark over the next 12 months in a bull case scenario — a 22 per cent increase from the STI's close on Aug 11. The STI has gained more than 23 per cent in 2026, outperforming Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index.

Genting Singapore was the lead gainer on the index this week, rising 6.4 per cent to 66 cents on Aug 14, despite the resort and casino operator recording a 33.5 per cent fall in first-half profit to $156.1 million due to higher depreciation, lower interest income and asset refresh works.

## Graphics card shortage set to drive PC prices higher

A worsening shortage of graphics cards and other key components could make PCs more expensive in the second half of 2026. PC Partner Group, a Hong Kong-based, Singapore-listed manufacturer of graphics cards, warned on Aug 14 that availability is likely to deteriorate further, with entry-level cards facing particularly severe shortages. Media reports have attributed the shortage in part to intensifying demand for advanced chips and memory from AI companies to power their infrastructure.

## CDL triples profits, HPL falls into the red

Strong demand for residential property in Singapore helped City Developments Limited (CDL) to almost triple its first-half net profit to $300 million (up over 230 per cent), on a 61.1 per cent year-on-year jump in revenue to $2.72 billion, driven by fully-sold executive condominium Lumina Grand and luxury development Newport Residences.

Property on Sentosa Island proved less lucrative — the average gain from profitable resale transactions shrank by about 60 per cent, with almost 65 per cent of resale transactions between May 2023 and June 2026 unprofitable, according to property portal Mogul.sg.

Hotel Properties Limited (HPL) posted a net loss of $39.1 million for the first half, compared with a net profit of $11.4 million a year earlier, as the Middle East conflict disrupted air travel and drove up airfares and operating costs.

## Sea Ltd reports better than expected results

Sea Ltd jumped early in the week after saying it expects US$1 billion in adjusted EBITDA for its online retail arm Shopee in 2026, up from its earlier forecast of at least US$880.6 million, driven by a 70 per cent rise in ad revenue. Second-quarter revenue for the company rose 48 per cent to US$7.8 billion.

## Other market movers

Singtel reported a 71.6 per cent drop in first-quarter net profit to $818 million, largely due to exceptional gains a year earlier from asset sales; underlying net profit was actually up 21 per cent to $831 million. Sembcorp raised its interim dividend to 11 cents per share despite a 25 per cent fall in underlying net profit, citing confidence in its outlook including the newly acquired Alinta Energy. Wilmar International reported a 9.9 per cent rise in half-year core net profit to $820 million, driven by its feed and industrial products business.

Minutes of the Federal Open Market Committee's July 28-29 meeting, when it voted to leave US interest rates unchanged, will be released on Aug 20.
