# 2026 State of the Workplace: AI Adoption and Workforce Performance Benchmarks

ActivTrak's fifth annual State of the Workplace report analyzed 443 million hours of work across 1,111 companies and 163,638 employees over three years. The prevailing assumption is that AI and productivity tools make the workday lighter — the data tells a more complicated story.

## AI adoption is accelerating work, not reducing it

Eighty percent of employees now use AI tools, up from 53% two years ago, with time spent in AI tools increasing eightfold and 92% average monthly retention. Among 10,584 users compared 180 days before and after AI adoption, time spent across every measured work category increased — email up 104%, chat and messaging up 145%, business management up 94%. No activity category decreased. AI is functioning as an additional productivity layer, not a substitute for existing work.

## Tool sprawl and the productivity sweet spot

The average organization used 2 AI tools in 2023 and 7 in 2025, with 83% now using 6 or more. Employees who spend 7-10% of work hours in AI tools have the highest productivity rate (95%) of any usage tier, yet only 3% of employees fall in that range — 57% spend less than 1% of hours in AI.

## Shorter days, less focus

The average workday shrank from 8:53 to 8:44, while productive hours rose 5% to 6:36 daily. But focus efficiency — time spent in focused, uninterrupted work — fell to 60%, a three-year low, with average focus sessions down to 13 minutes 7 seconds. Collaboration surged 34% and multitasking rose 12%.

## Burnout down, disengagement up

Burnout risk fell 22% to 5% of employees, the healthiest level in three years. But disengagement risk rose 21% to nearly 1 in 4 employees — workers whose freed-up capacity isn't being leveraged.

## Weekend work has become structural

Saturday productive hours jumped from 3:10 to 4:37, and Sunday from 2:30 to 3:58, with weekend start times shifting more than an hour earlier over the study period.

## No work model wins outright

Remote-only workers log the highest productive time (7:01 daily); office-only workers show the highest focus efficiency (64%); hybrid workers span the longest workdays but log the lowest productive and focused time of any location type.
