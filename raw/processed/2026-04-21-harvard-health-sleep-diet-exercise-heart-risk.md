# Small upgrades to sleep, diet, and exercise may cut heart risk

New research suggests that even small, combined lifestyle improvements can meaningfully reduce cardiovascular risk. A study published online March 23, 2026, in the *European Journal of Preventive Cardiology*, following more than 53,000 adults over eight years, found that sleeping just 11 additional minutes, doing five extra minutes of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, and eating a quarter-cup more of vegetables each day was linked to a 10% reduction in heart attacks, strokes, and other serious heart problems compared with people with less healthy habits.

The optimal combination of habits — 8 to 9 hours of sleep per night, 42+ minutes of moderate-to-vigorous exercise daily, and a modest-to-good diet quality score (more vegetables, fruits, fish, dairy, whole grains, and vegetable oils; fewer refined grains, processed meats, red meats, and sugary beverages) — was associated with a 57% lower risk of heart-related problems compared with people with the least optimal health profiles.

The study's authors concluded that incorporating several small changes into a daily routine may be both beneficial and more achievable than attempting one single major lifestyle overhaul.
