# Aerobic Exercise May Improve Brain Health in Postmenopausal Women

A study published in the *Journal of Alzheimer's Disease* (August 1) found that regular aerobic exercise may strengthen executive function in postmenopausal women, with cognitive benefits potentially emerging within 3–6 months.

## Key Findings

The study followed 93 cognitively healthy females (ages 20–67), with 43% postmenopausal. All had below-average cardiorespiratory fitness and were sedentary. Participants were assigned to either a 6-month aerobic program or a stretching/toning program.

The strongest cognitive gains came from those doing **45 minutes of moderate aerobic activity four times weekly**, with improvements in executive function — encompassing working memory, decision-making, and staying organized — first appearing at 3 months.

## Expert Commentary

Dr. Dung Trinh cautioned that this was "a secondary analysis of a relatively small study," calling it "important evidence that deserves confirmation rather than definitive proof."

Dr. Kecia Gaither noted that aerobic activity produces "a more sustained cardiorespiratory and metabolic stimulus" compared to stretching alone.

## How Exercise Benefits the Brain

Aerobic exercise may help through multiple mechanisms:
- **Cerebrovascular function** — improved oxygen delivery to active brain areas
- **Metabolic health** — better blood pressure, glucose, and lipid regulation
- **Reduced inflammation and oxidative stress**
- **Brain plasticity** — supporting synaptic remodeling and neuronal survival

## Menopause and Cognitive Decline

Declining estrogen levels after menopause may reduce neuroprotective effects, contributing to higher rates of cognitive decline in women. Roughly two-thirds of Alzheimer's cases occur in women.

Trinh noted the study highlights that "menopause status is often overlooked in exercise and brain-health trials," despite women carrying a higher lifetime dementia burden.

## What Counts as Moderate Aerobic Activity

Activities where you can talk but not sing, such as brisk walking, cycling, water aerobics, dancing, active gardening, or doubles tennis/pickleball.
