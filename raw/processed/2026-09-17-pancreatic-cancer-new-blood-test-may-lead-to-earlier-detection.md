# Pancreatic Cancer: New Blood Test May Lead to Earlier Detection

A new investigational liquid biopsy called PANXEON combines three biological markers with AI analysis to detect pancreatic cancer at earlier stages. A large international study of nearly 1,800 participants, published in *Nature Medicine*, found the test correctly identified stage 1 and 2 pancreatic cancers 87% of the time. False-positive rates were approximately 3% in low-risk groups and 16% in high-risk groups.

## Background

Pancreatic cancer accounts for roughly 3% of U.S. cancers but causes around 8% of cancer deaths. An estimated 67,530 Americans will be diagnosed in 2026, with about 52,740 deaths expected. The 5-year survival rate is approximately 44% when localized, dropping to just 3% when the disease has spread distantly — underscoring why earlier detection matters enormously.

## How PANXEON Works

Rather than relying on a single biomarker, PANXEON integrates three biological signals:

- Circulating microRNAs
- Exosomal microRNAs
- Carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9)

An AI system combines these measurements into a single probability score. Previous single-biomarker approaches have struggled with sensitivity or specificity; the multi-biomarker strategy aims to provide a more comprehensive picture of pancreatic changes.

The study tested PANXEON across people with varying risk levels — not merely comparing cancer patients against healthy individuals — to better reflect real clinical conditions.

## Key Findings

- 87% sensitivity for early-stage (stage 1 and 2) pancreatic cancer detection
- ~3% false-positive rate in low-risk individuals; ~16% in high-risk individuals
- Over 64% detection rate for high-grade dysplasia (a precancerous condition) among people with high-risk pancreatic cysts

Study senior author Ajay Goel, PhD, noted that the test was evaluated across "a large international, multicenter population" in people with clinically relevant conditions — not simply healthy controls versus cancer patients.

## Precancerous Detection: A Notable Finding

The ability to detect high-grade dysplasia was highlighted as especially significant. This precancerous state, found in pancreatic cysts, can precede invasive cancer. Goel described this as potentially enabling "cancer interception rather than simply earlier cancer diagnosis."

However, researchers caution that current data are insufficient to confirm that intervening at this stage definitively prevents invasive cancer. Larger prospective studies are needed.

## Who Could Benefit?

PANXEON is seen as most immediately useful for people already at elevated pancreatic cancer risk, including those with:

- Inherited genetic predispositions
- Family history of pancreatic cancer
- Pancreatic cysts
- Chronic pancreatitis

These individuals often already undergo surveillance imaging. A blood test could offer a less invasive complementary tool to flag who needs closer or more urgent evaluation.

## Limitations and Next Steps

- PANXEON remains investigational and is not yet ready for routine clinical use.
- It is not intended to replace imaging (e.g., MRI or endoscopic ultrasound) but could supplement existing surveillance.
- Performance in routine clinical practice and impact on survival outcomes remain to be established.
- Further studies are needed to confirm reproducibility across additional populations.

Goel envisions the test as "an additional layer of information within existing surveillance," potentially helping personalize monitoring by combining genetic risk, imaging data, and molecular blood signals over time.

## Bottom Line

PANXEON represents a promising early-detection approach for one of oncology's most challenging diseases. Its multi-biomarker, AI-assisted design addresses limitations of prior single-marker tests. If validated in prospective trials, it could shift pancreatic cancer diagnosis toward earlier, more treatable stages — and possibly even identify dangerous precancerous changes before invasive disease develops.
