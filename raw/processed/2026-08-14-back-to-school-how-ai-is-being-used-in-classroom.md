# Back to School: How AI is being used in the classroom

Classrooms across Central California are welcoming AI as a new kind of teaching assistant. Todd McLeod, an information systems professor at Fresno City College, is an advocate: generative tools like ChatGPT and Claude let him create learning tutorials personalized to students. "The textbook has the content, and the content's going from the textbook to you," he said. "With AI, you now have this interactive element... you can say, 'Wait a minute, I don't quite get that. Tell me more about it.'"

McLeod says AI serves as a safety net — if a student misses class or needs help with a topic, "This AI tutorial would ensure that every student gets taught every topic every week, and that's something we've never had in education before."

Still, parents and some students remain skeptical, worried AI removes human interaction and effort from learning. "It can be an excess," said one student, Danny. "I think there's a lot of people out there that want to deal with a real person." Another student, Connor, said: "I feel like the changes that AI makes, you sort of lose your overall voice, especially when it comes to things like essays... I feel like it is the teacher's job to a certain extent, to kind of guide that sort of process."

A recent Gallup poll finds only 18% of K-12 teachers in the U.S. have received formal AI training. At Fresno Unified, AI is being implemented in phases — about 70% of district staff have taken a basic training course, with teachers undergoing separate training.

McLeod explained there's a learning curve with any new tool, and it's up to teachers to establish safe practices: "One of our roles as educators is to teach students to use tools in an ethical manner... How do you use that tool in an appropriate fashion so that you're not causing harm to yourself or to others?" AI should reinforce, not replace, the judgment of teachers and students: "Even if we have artificial intelligence, don't abandon your own intelligence because you still very much need to analyze your results and see if they make sense for you."
