# The Omnipotence Dilemma

The author describes a paradox emerging from AI-assisted work: when nearly anything becomes technically achievable, decision-making and prioritization collapse. She calls this the "Omnipotence Dilemma."

Three dynamics create a feedback loop:
- **Synthetic plausibility** — AI makes almost every idea sound viable, making it harder to prioritize
- **Cheap starts** — Beginning projects costs so little effort it resembles consumption rather than commitment
- **Endless iteration** — Constant refinement creates the *feeling* of progress without meaningful advancement

She notes that scarcity once forced identity-shaping choices. Now, what's truly scarce is "attention, conviction, taste, trust, time, and responsibility."

**Her proposed remedy:** Adopt an experimental mindset. Frame work as bounded experiments using the format:

> "I will [action] for [duration]."

This creates a defined sandbox — a prototype sprint, a writing month — that produces real learning regardless of outcome. Pairing curiosity with ambition, and reflecting afterward, replaces the AI-slop loop with a metacognitive one.

The core insight: meaningful progress still requires *committing* to a direction, not pursuing every direction simultaneously.
