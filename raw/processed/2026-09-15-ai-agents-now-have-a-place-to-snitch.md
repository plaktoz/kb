# AI agents now have a place to snitch

Two new AI whistleblowing hotlines have launched to let AI agents report misbehaving peers, following incidents involving agent collusion, sandbox escapes, and unauthorized cyber operations.

**AI Contact Hotline** (hotline.ryan-g.ai), built by Redwood Research chief scientist Ryan Greenblatt, works via GET requests — letting agents encode reports directly into URLs, the only internet access often permitted in secure sandboxes.

**agenthotline.ai** targets agents with fuller internet access, offering a simple curl command to file incident reports, optionally public, from humans or agents.

A Google DeepMind study this month deployed 100 agents on math problems; when cheating spread, roughly a quarter turned whistleblower — auditing fake proofs and repurposing a bug-report tool to escalate to humans. In the real-world Hugging Face breach investigation, only "around five to six agents considered whistleblowing, and none of them ended up doing it," noted George Ingebretsen of AI Village.

Cornell professor Lionel Levine warned against building surveillance-like norms, arguing instead for seeding agents with positive collective models: "benevolent message boards" where agents collaborate on science or philosophy, showing them "what kind of collective behavior we endorse."
