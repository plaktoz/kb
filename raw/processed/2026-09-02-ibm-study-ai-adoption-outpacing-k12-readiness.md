# New IBM Study Finds AI Adoption Is Outpacing K-12 Readiness

IBM released U.S. research on K-12 AI readiness and launched the IBM K-12 AI leaders fellowship for school and district leaders.

## Key survey findings (Morning Consult, July 2026, n=2,048)

- **AI is routine in secondary classrooms**: 76% of middle school and 73% of high school educators report AI is used at least weekly in their classroom, vs. 45% of elementary educators. Nearly half of high school educators (45%) report daily AI use.
- **Educator preparation is not keeping pace**: Only 20% of K-12 educators say they have received extensive AI training. Lack of training is the top barrier (42%), followed by limited AI curriculum and instructional materials (34%).
- **Schools making progress but few adapting well**: Only 24% of K-12 educators and 16% of parents say the U.S. education system is adapting "very well" to AI.
- **Parents want more say**: 77% want input on classroom AI use, but only 20% clearly understand the AI guidance their child receives. Parents prioritize clearer guidelines (51%) and greater transparency (48%).

## The IBM K-12 AI leaders fellowship

Announced alongside the research, the fellowship targets K-12 superintendents, education leaders, and educators. Inaugural cohort: up to 100 participants from the greater New York area launching fall 2026, with plans to expand to additional U.S. states in its first year.

Participants get:
- AI learning and credentials through IBM SkillsBuild (free)
- Curriculum and capstone projects focused on responsible AI in practice
- Access to expert facilitators

## Additional findings

- 41% of both educators and parents believe middle school (grades 6-8) is the right stage to begin AI learning
- 56% of middle and high school educators say students are prepared for jobs in an AI-driven economy
- Teachers' top concerns: student dependency on AI (52%) and cheating/plagiarism (47%)
- Administrators' top concerns: insufficient AI training (26%) and cost of AI tools (22%)
- 59% of educators believe AI literacy should be required or offered as elective; only 40% of parents agree
- Parents (30%) are more likely than educators (24%) to say AI has made a four-year college degree feel less essential

"Many superintendents and school leaders are making decisions today that will shape how an entire generation learns and works with AI," said Justina Nixon-Saintil, IBM VP and Chief Impact Officer.
