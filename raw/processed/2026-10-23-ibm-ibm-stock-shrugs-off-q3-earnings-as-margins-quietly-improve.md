# IBM (IBM) Stock Shrugs Off Q3 Earnings As Margins Quietly Improve

IBM just reported Q3 2026 and the headline is simple. Revenue landed at US$17.1b and basic earnings per share came in at US$2.33, while operating pretax margin widened by 70 basis points. The twist is in the mood, not the math. After a 17.7% gain over the past three months, the stock barely moved today, up only 0.3%. That muted tick reflects investors catching their breath rather than reacting to fresh information, even as profit margins continue to quietly reshape the story.

## Q3 2026 Earnings Summary

Revenue (Q3 2026 vs. Q3 2025): US$17.10b vs. US$16.33b (up about 4.7%)

Net Income (Excl. Extra Items, Q3 2026 vs. Q3 2025): US$2,200m vs. US$1,744m (up about 26.2%)

Basic EPS (Q3 2026 vs. Q3 2025): US$2.33 vs. US$1.87 (up about 24.7%)

Operating Pretax Margin (Q3 2026 vs. Q3 2025): Margin widened by 70 basis points (indicating improved profitability year on year)

Earnings tell you what International Business Machines just produced on the income statement, but investors who care about what they are paying for the stock usually focus on cash flowing through the business over time. When you look at International Business Machines' cash flows in isolation, the stock could be a slight 11% undervalued.

## IBM's Bull Story Meets Hard AI And Software Milestones

The upbeat thesis hangs on IBM turning its newer platforms into real, recurring growth rather than just a slide deck story.

"The main thing that has to go right is that IBM converts its strong positions in Red Hat OpenShift, automated security remediation and quantum computing investment into durable software and services growth that more than offsets any pressure on mainframe and other CapEx sensitive revenues."

Q3 points squarely in that direction. Software revenue rose 9%, with OpenShift ARR at US$2.4b and growing above 30%. Data software advanced 20% helped by Confluent. Lightwell already carries a roughly US$700m pipeline. Even mainframe linked Transaction Processing increased 10%, so the quarter shows pressure easing rather than weighing.

## IBM Bear Case: Mainframe Volatility Meets Mixed Evidence

The skeptical view hangs on IBM failing to smooth out hardware and software cycles, leaving earnings exposed when mainframe demand fades.

"the 42% decline in IBM Z Infrastructure revenue in Q2 2026 off a strong launch comparison points to lumpier demand and a risk that future mainframe cycles increasingly front load orders and then sag more sharply."

Q3 brings a partial rebuttal. Infrastructure revenue grew 5% and Distributed Infrastructure climbed 32%, while Transaction Processing advanced 10%. That recovery suggests slipped Z deals and related software did close. The pattern still supports the lumpiness concern, since a sharp rebound after a prior drop does not yet prove smoother cycles, just effective catch up.
