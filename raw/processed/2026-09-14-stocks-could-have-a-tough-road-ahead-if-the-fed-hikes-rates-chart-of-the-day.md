# Stocks could have a tough road ahead if the Fed hikes rates: Chart of the Day

Oil prices have returned above $100/barrel, August payrolls came in triple expectations, and core inflation ticked higher than forecast — all building the case for the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates Wednesday for the first time in three years. Markets are pricing roughly 90% odds of a quarter-point hike.

Goldman Sachs analysts led by Ben Snider warn that history suggests stocks could face near-term pressure. The S&P 500 has averaged a negative 2% three-month return at the start of Fed tightening cycles. Three reasons drive this:

1. Tightening could weigh on economic growth, which matters more than rate levels for equity performance.
2. Hiking cycles have historically marked peaks of "high-valuation, high-concentration bull markets."
3. The AI boom has made this cycle "particularly capital intensive," increasing sensitivity to higher borrowing costs.

Despite short-term headwinds, the 12-month average return following a tightening cycle start is +9%. Markets have already priced in roughly 75 basis points of tightening through mid-2027, potentially softening the blow.

Goldman's ultimate view: earnings growth remains the key driver, and "the bull market" is expected to continue.
