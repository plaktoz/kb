# Evolution is Blind but We're Not

Organizations respond to failure by identifying causes and trying to prevent recurrence. Gary Klein's *Seeing What Others Don't* is cited, noting that managers spend most energy reducing errors rather than boosting insights.

## The Central Idea

We can't truly repeat the same mistake twice because circumstances constantly shift. Drawing on Heraclitus — *"No man ever steps in the same river twice"* — both the person and the environment are always changing.

## Evolution's Blind Advantage

Unlike humans, evolution has no memory of past failures. It blindly repeats mutations without regard for prior outcomes. This repetition is actually adaptive — a trait that failed before might succeed in a changed environment. Natural selection works *because* of this blindness.

## Two Types of Failure

- **Ignorance-based** — knowledge simply doesn't exist yet (forgivable)
- **Ineptitude-based** — knowledge exists but isn't applied (harder to excuse)

## Challenging Organizational Habits

The author challenges the organizational habit of punishing repeated mistakes, arguing that conditions are never truly identical. True learning requires understanding key variables, decision-making processes, and the knowledge available *at the time*, not just cataloguing what went wrong.
