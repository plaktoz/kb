# Will the Federal Reserve Trigger a Bear Market? History Has Good and Bad News for Investors

After months of delay, the Fed raised its benchmark rate, with Chair Kevin Warsh citing a need to better assess persistent inflation's underlying causes.

The hike appears to be the first in a series — fed funds futures markets are pricing in "two to three more quarter-point hikes by the end of 2027."

Key concerns for investors:

- Higher rates squeeze corporate profit margins and consumer borrowing
- Most past tightening cycles have led to recessions
- Bear markets become more likely during recessions

Some nuance softens the outlook:

- Recessions from rate hikes typically lag the first increase by over a year
- Stocks often recover after an initial post-hike dip
- Inflation is partly driven by a Persian Gulf conflict and oil prices — factors the Fed cannot directly address
- Bond yields were already rising before the hike

The counterbalancing positives: unemployment is low, GDP growth is running at roughly 5.1% annually per the Atlanta Fed, and corporate earnings remain robust — factors that could make the economy more resilient through the tightening cycle.
