# Scientists reveal how much exercise you really need to protect your heart

A study of 17,088 UK Biobank participants (average age 57, wrist-worn activity trackers, cycling test to estimate VO2 max, published in the British Journal of Sports Medicine) found that meeting the standard guideline of 150 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous exercise per week is associated with only a modest 8–9% reduction in cardiovascular risk, consistent across all fitness levels.

Achieving "substantial protection" — defined as a risk reduction of more than 30% — was associated with 560 to 610 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity per week (roughly 3–4x the standard guideline). Only 12% of participants reached that level. Over an average 7.8-year follow-up, researchers recorded 1,233 cardiovascular events (874 atrial fibrillation, 156 myocardial infarctions, 111 heart failure, 92 strokes).

People with lower cardiorespiratory fitness needed about 30–50 more minutes of weekly exercise than highly fit individuals for the same risk reduction — e.g., a 20% risk reduction required 370 minutes/week for the least-fit group versus 340 minutes/week for the most-fit group. Because the study is observational, it cannot prove causation, and cardiorespiratory fitness was estimated rather than directly measured. The researchers argue current guidelines remain a reliable minimum safety threshold, but suggest future guidance could set personalized, fitness-adjusted activity targets rather than a one-size-fits-all number.
