# FDA Approves Centanafadine for ADHD in Children, Adolescents, and Adults

source_url: https://www.psychiatrictimes.com/view/fda-approves-centanafadine-for-adhd-in-children-adolescents-and-adults

---

By Jessica Walters | Psychiatric Times | July 24, 2026

**Key Takeaways**
- FDA approval establishes centanafadine as the first-in-class NDSRI for ADHD, expanding nonstimulant options across adult and pediatric populations aged ≥6 years and >20 kg.
- Phase 3 pediatric and adolescent programs met week-6 efficacy endpoints on ADHD-RS-5 versus placebo in high-dose cohorts, supporting benefit across school-age developmental stages.
- Adult phase 3 trials demonstrated significant, clinically meaningful symptom reductions at both 200 mg and 400 mg versus placebo by week 6 on an investigator-rated scale.
- Post hoc analyses suggested improvements in executive function domains and emotional dysregulation, including time management, planning/prioritization, task initiation/completion, and working memory.
- Safety profiles were characterized mainly by decreased appetite and headache in adults, and decreased appetite, nausea, headache, and rash in younger populations; dependence and abuse potential were reported as low.

Centanafadine (Simtriyo) has been approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for treatment of attention-deficit/hyperactive disorder (ADHD) in adults and children aged 6 and older (>20 kg). The nonstimulant medication, developed by Otsuka Pharmaceutical, is the first FDA-approved norepinephrine, dopamine, and serotonin reuptake inhibitor (NDSRI) for ADHD.

The approval is based on positive phase 3 data from 4 trials (2 in adults, 1 in adolescents, 1 in children) and post hoc analyses which showed improvement in patient-reported executive function and emotional dysregulation. The most recent post hoc analysis reported improvement across measures of executive function, including time management, planning and prioritization, task initiation and completion, and working memory.

"The approval of SIMTRIYO marks an important milestone for people living with ADHD, as it introduces a novel treatment approach for this condition," said John Kraus, MD, PhD, executive vice president and chief medical officer of Otsuka. "ADHD is a complex and highly individualized disorder that can affect people throughout childhood, adolescence, and adulthood."

Development of centanafadine included phase 3 trials across age groups — children aged 4 to 12, adolescents aged 13 to 17, and adults aged 18 to 55. Trials in children and adolescents showed a statistically significant improvement in ADHD Rating Scale-5 score vs placebo at week 6 in the high-dose group; the most common adverse effects were decreased appetite, nausea, headache, and rash. Trials in adults showed improvement from baseline in ADHD Investigator Symptom Rating Scale score at week 6 compared with placebo, with both the low (200 mg) and high (400 mg) dose groups showing significant and clinically meaningful improvement; the most common adverse effects were decreased appetite and headache.

"ADHD can significantly affect many aspects of daily life across school, work, and relationships," said Lenard A. Adler, MD, director of the adult ADHD program at NYU Langone Health. "Even when on treatment, because of the heterogenic nature of ADHD, many patients continue to experience symptoms that can interfere with daily functioning. The approval of Simtriyo introduces a novel mechanism of action and expands the range of options available to healthcare professionals and patients."

Centanafadine is a triple reuptake inhibitor of norepinephrine, dopamine, and serotonin, and has been demonstrated to have low potential for dependence and abuse. A New Drug Application was submitted in November 2025 and was accepted for priority review in January 2026. Investigators will release full study results at an upcoming scientific meeting.
