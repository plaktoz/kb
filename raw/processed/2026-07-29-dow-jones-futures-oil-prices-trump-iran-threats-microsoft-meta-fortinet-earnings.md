# Dow Jones Futures: Stock Market Tumbles, Oil Prices Soar On Trump's Iran Threats; Microsoft, Meta, Fortinet Earnings Late

By Ed Carson, Updated 05:32 PM ET 07/29/2026

Dow Jones futures fell slightly after hours, along with S&P 500 futures and Nasdaq futures. Microsoft, Meta Platforms, Starbucks and Fortinet headlined another big night of earnings.

The stock market retreated as oil prices jumped on renewed U.S.-Iran hostilities. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq briefly turned positive as the Federal Reserve left interest rates steady and Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh said rising market rates have provided some tightening. But stocks returned lower as President Trump reiterated his Iran threats.

## Dow Jones Futures Today

Dow Jones futures lost 0.1% vs. fair value. S&P 500 futures declined 0.2%. Nasdaq-100 futures lost 0.4%.

Remember that overnight action in Dow futures and elsewhere doesn't necessarily translate into actual trading in the next regular stock market session.

## Microsoft Earnings

Microsoft (MSFT) reported Wednesday night. Fiscal Q4 earnings beat views, with Azure cloud-computing growth up 43% excluding currency impacts. The tech titan will likely give guidance, including for capital spending, on the upcoming conference call.

Microsoft stock rose modestly in overnight action. Shares edged lower in Wednesday's regular session after hitting resistance at the 50-day line on Tuesday.

## Meta Earnings

Meta Platforms (META) earnings missed while sales topped, but the Facebook parent guided slightly lower on Q3 revenue while raising its planned capital spending for the year.

Meta stock dived in extended trade. Shares dipped 1.3% on Wednesday, below all its key moving averages.

Fellow hyperscaler Amazon.com (AMZN) will report Thursday night.

## Fortinet, Samsung Electronics, Other Earnings

Fortinet (FTNT), Woodward (WWD), Glaukos (GKOS), Lam Research (LRCX), Starbucks (SBUX) and Robinhood (HOOD) reported after the close.

Fortinet, Glaukos and Starbucks were overnight big winners. Fortinet is set to gap up toward highs after reversing higher at the 50-day line. Starbucks also could offer entries on Thursday, while Glaukos already jumped above a buy point during the regular session.

Lam Research also rallied but has work to regain its 50-day line.

Woodward stock fell solidly after tumbling to below its 50-day line in Wednesday's session. Robinhood retreated solidly and is below key levels.

Samsung Electronics is set to report Q2 earnings Wednesday night New York time, after the South Korean memory giant released preliminary results earlier this month. Samsung does not trade in the U.S.

## Stock Market Wednesday

The stock market suffered broad losses amid rising oil prices and the ongoing sell-off in chip and AI names. Crude futures jumped as Iranian missiles attacked a U.S. base in Jordan. President Donald Trump vowed to hit Iran hard, both before the market open and shortly before the close. The latter threat snuffed out a tentative market bounce following the Fed announcement and Warsh's comments.

The Nasdaq composite tumbled to a fresh three-month low intraday, down 1.7% in Wednesday's stock market trading. The S&P 500 lost 1.5%, hitting a one-month low. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 2.2%, knifing through its 21-day line to just above its 50-day line. The small-cap Russell 2000 index slumped 1.6% and is now clearly below its 50-day for the first time since early April.

The Invesco S&P 500 Equal Weight ETF (RSP) fell 0.9% after hitting a new high on Tuesday.

Snowflake stock jumped 4.6%, breaking out of a cup-with-handle base to a four-year high. Datadog stock rose 5.3%, breaking the downtrend of its own cup-with-handle base.

U.S. crude oil prices shot up 6.6% to $84.46 a barrel. President Trump vowed to hit Iran hard after Tehran's surprise ballistic missile attack on a U.S. base in Jordan, ending several days of no hostilities.

The 10-year Treasury yield gained two basis points to 4.62%, but picked up to 4.67% in electronic trading after the 3 p.m. ET settlement. The 30-year yield gained five basis points to 5.14%, but after 3 p.m. ET shot up to 5.2%, the highest since 2007. Those moves appeared to reflect rising oil prices, not news from the Fed and Warsh. The two-year yield, more closely tied to Fed policy, fell four basis points to 4.235%.

The Industrial Select Sector SPDR Fund (XLI) tumbled 3.2%. The Financial Select SPDR ETF (XLF) sank 1.6%.
