# Shingrix shingles vaccine linked with lower heart disease risks

The article reports on a study suggesting the Shingrix shingles vaccine may offer cardiovascular benefits beyond its known effects, which researchers say may also include lowering risk of dementia and slowing biological aging.

The study, published in *Nature Medicine* on Aug. 26, 2026, examined records of more than 72,000 adults ages 60 and older to compare cardiovascular outcomes between people who received the older Zostavax vaccine versus the newer Shingrix vaccine, which uses recombinant DNA technology built from a single viral protein. Researchers took advantage of the U.S.'s rapid switch between vaccines in late 2017 to compare two similar patient groups.

Findings from the seven-year follow-up period showed that Shingrix recipients had a modestly reduced likelihood of developing cardiovascular conditions compared to Zostavax recipients. The reduction was described as 9% less likely for combined cardiovascular problems, with the strongest associations found for ischemic heart disease (10% lower risk) and heart failure (12% lower risk). Men who received Shingrix also showed a reduced risk of ischemic stroke. The protective effect appeared most pronounced in the earlier years following vaccination.

The piece notes the research was observational, so it cannot establish that switching vaccines directly caused the cardiovascular improvements. Researchers speculated that an immune-stimulating component in Shingrix could be a contributing factor, referencing prior research linking that ingredient to heart-related benefits. The article closes by reinforcing existing CDC guidance recommending the two-dose Shingrix series for adults 50 and older, including those previously vaccinated with Zostavax.
