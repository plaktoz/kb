# Nvidia Halts Server Shipments To 4 Southeast Asian Countries Amid New U.S. Export Curbs, Report Says

## Topline

Nvidia has instructed its manufacturing partners to halt shipments of servers equipped with its artificial intelligence chips to four Southeast Asian countries, the Financial Times reported Thursday, as the Trump administration moves to restrict Chinese AI companies' access to advanced American semiconductors amid heightened scrutiny of the chipmaker's business dealings in the region, following the controversy surrounding CEO Jensen Huang.

## Key Facts

Citing three unnamed sources, the FT reported that Nvidia's manufacturing partners, including Dell and Supermicro, have informed customers in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia that the chipmaking giant will not allow their servers to be shipped.

The report notes that it is unclear if the suspension is temporary to allow Nvidia and the server makers to address compliance with new U.S. export control rules.

The reported shipment freeze comes a week after new U.S. export restrictions went into effect, requiring companies to obtain licenses to ship advanced AI chips to the four Southeast Asian countries and Brazil, with exemptions for qualifying U.S.-based companies.

The move also follows the Nvidia board's decision to reinstate billionaire Jensen Huang as its CEO and the establishment of an independent National Security and Export Control Committee.

Bloomberg separately reported on Wednesday that the new restrictions have prompted lenders to pause billions in financing for Asian data center projects linked to Chinese technology giants, including ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent.

## Are The New Chip Restrictions Affecting Chinese AI Firms?

Reuters reported on Thursday that China's Z.AI could delay the launch of its next flagship model, GLM-5.5. The company originally planned to release GLM-5.5 this month and later shifted its target to year-end, but the launch could now slip into the first quarter of 2027. The report also noted that rival Moonshot AI believes its six-month development cycle for models could double to a year or longer if it loses access to Nvidia chips hosted by overseas cloud providers. Its current flagship Kimi K3 model launched in July this year and the restrictions could push back the launch of its successor K4 to mid-2027. The chip restrictions come as the companies also face increased efforts by OpenAI and Anthropic to prevent distillation of their cutting-edge models. Distillation is a process that lets companies train models using outputs from more advanced models at a fraction of the cost.

## Could Nvidia's Projected Revenue Take A Hit?

Despite the Commerce Department's announcement of new export restrictions, Nvidia raised its revenue growth forecast for fiscal 2028 from 70% to around 75% and projected revenue of around $130 billion for the current quarter. During the company's earnings call, Nvidia CFO and then-interim CEO Colette Kress acknowledged the new export restrictions, but said: "With demand far exceeding supply, we expect our performance to remain strong." Kress also said the company had assessed the new curbs while preparing its revenue forecast but was still studying their implications. It's unclear if the projection could be impacted by this reported freeze in shipments.

## Tangent

Nvidia's stock price fell 3.91% on Wednesday, ending the trading day at $149.30. Shares inched up around 1.3% to $151.23 in after-hours trading, but U.S. stock markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving. The chipmaker's shares surged more than 31% to close at $165.05 on Monday after the board announced Huang's return as CEO. Despite giving up some of those gains over the past two days, Nvidia's shares remain nearly 19% above Friday's closing price.
