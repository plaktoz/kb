# Itoki builds AI productivity model from 854 workers with Matsuo Institute

## KEY POINTS

- Itoki and Matsuo Institute build AI productivity model from 70 behavior data types covering 854 office workers
- Meeting frequency shows stronger next-day mental impact than meeting length, while uninterrupted 60-minute work blocks link to better condition
- Study began in 2025 to identify causal drivers of office productivity beyond conventional employee surveys

TOKYO, Oct 2 - Office furniture and workplace services company Itoki said on Friday it had built a data-driven productivity estimation model with Matsuo Institute after analyzing AI-processed behavioral data from 854 employees.

The joint research examined 70 types of daily work behavior data, including meetings, email and chat activity, calendars and movement within the office, to explore causal links affecting productivity. Data was collected from 854 people working at Itoki's headquarters office between Dec. 1 and Dec. 31, 2025.

The 70 indicators included 21 location-related data points, four schedule-related items and 45 online communication measures. The model also examined how behavior data and worker conditions such as mental and physical state and internal workplace relationships affect output, which Itoki defined as the degree to which each employee demonstrates individual capability.

The latest analysis found that the number of meetings, rather than their total duration, had a stronger effect on workers' mental condition the following day. Days with a higher number of meetings were associated with a decline in next-day mental condition across all three job categories studied: design, development and planning; sales and service; and administrative roles.

Itoki also found that on days when employees were able to work for more than 60 minutes without interruption from meetings or other tasks, 88% of workers across all job categories showed an improvement in next-day mental condition.

For email and chat activity, the research indicated that incoming messages had a greater effect on internal workplace relationships than outgoing communication. In sales and service roles, days with heavier email or chat inflows were linked to declines in multiple conditions, including internal relationships and physical condition. By contrast, self-initiated online communication was associated with improved internal relationship conditions.

The analysis did not find a uniform causal relationship between productivity and factors such as time spent in the office, the number of visits to break areas, response speed to email or chat, or the length of time workers remained digitally connected.

At the core of the model is VAR-LiNGAM, a time-series causal discovery method. Itoki said the research relied mainly on objective data rather than subjective surveys, and that all data was anonymized to prevent identification of individuals. The contents of emails and chats were not part of the analysis.

Itoki and Matsuo Institute began the joint productivity research in 2025 as companies in Japan increase investment in human capital and face pressure to show the returns from those investments. In Japan, human capital disclosures refer to corporate reporting on areas such as employee development, engagement and workplace conditions.

Itoki, founded in 1890, provides office furniture, office space design, workplace consulting and office data analysis services. Matsuo Institute works alongside the Matsuo-Iwasawa laboratory at the University of Tokyo's graduate school of engineering to develop and implement research for broader social use. The model can be applied to other companies, although the current findings reflect Itoki's own workplace data.
