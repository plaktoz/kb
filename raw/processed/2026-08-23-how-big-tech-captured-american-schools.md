# How Big Tech Captured American Schools

This article is an adapted excerpt from "Coding Kids: Big Tech's Battle to Remake Public Schools" by Natasha Singer for W.W. Norton & Company. Companies like Google and Microsoft have used their money, their might, and their massive reach to influence nearly every step of the education supply chain.

One afternoon in summer 2025, Microsoft representatives ran a workshop for roughly 200 teachers in Manhattan on AI chatbots in schools. The Microsoft team demonstrated their Copilot chatbot for tasks like generating parent emails and customizing lesson plans, and screened an AI explainer video produced by their Minecraft Education division, featuring a cheerful narrator and an animated duck. A Microsoft workshop leader argued that even schoolchildren too young to use AI directly could at least start learning about it.

The piece frames this as part of a broader pattern: over the last decade, schools have often embraced technology companies with few questions asked, in a way many parents would object to if the same access were granted to companies in other regulated industries, such as an oil major writing environmental science curricula or a pharmaceutical company training biology teachers.
