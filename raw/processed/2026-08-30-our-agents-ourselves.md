# Our Agents, Ourselves

A newsletter covering AI developments across knowledge work, mathematics, and tooling.

## ChatGPT for Knowledge Workers

Katie Parrott rebuilt Every's guide after OpenAI merged Codex into ChatGPT in mid-July, covering 16 workflows recast for the unified app.

## Executive AI Q&A

Natalia Quintero and Mike Taylor address 33 questions from ~400 executives on AI adoption: strategy, skeptic management, tool selection, governance, and team restructuring.

## KateBench & Model Evaluation

Every built a copyeditor trained on 30,000 edits to show how "a high acceptance rate can still hide the edits a human has to redo."

## Cloning Coworkers

Companies are capturing employee judgment as reusable AI skills — a practice Every is piloting internally.

## Deft Model Review

Katie Parrott tested Deft, a model designed to address AI prose sameness. Finding: less predictable output, but dense and prone to hallucination.

## Proof Indigestion (Alignment section)

Terence Tao identifies a new mathematical challenge: AI generates more apparently valid proofs than mathematicians can verify. He describes a backlog of results whose "central ideas have yet to be extracted." After working through a 90,000-line AI-generated proof of Sendov's conjecture, Tao produced a ~15,000-line human-readable version using surprisingly basic tools. The takeaway: humans may increasingly serve as translators between machine-generated results and usable knowledge.
