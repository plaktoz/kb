IBM is expanding its quantum computing presence by establishing a new innovation hub at ETH Zurich, partnering with Lockheed Martin. The system will be housed at Switzerland's National Supercomputing Centre in Lugano, powered by IBM's "Quantum Nighthawk processor."

Under a Swiss defense procurement offset agreement, the hub will support research in chemistry, materials science, optimization, and financial services. IBM also plans to deliver quantum education through "courses, certifications, workshops and hackathons."

The IBM-Lockheed Martin collaboration will explore aerospace and defense quantum applications, including "quantum sensing for navigation and improvements in additive manufacturing involving metallic alloys."

The venture also extends IBM's relationship with ETH Zurich into a 10-year effort focused on foundational AI and quantum algorithms.

## Competitive Landscape

Microsoft is pursuing quantum research targeting chemistry, materials science, and optimization problems. Alphabet's Google Quantum AI is focused on performance improvements and collaborating with universities on optimization and simulation applications.
