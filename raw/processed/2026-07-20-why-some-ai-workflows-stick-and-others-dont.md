# Why Some AI Workflows Stick—And Others Don't

**Author:** Katie Parrott
**Publication:** Working Overtime (Every)
**Date:** July 20, 2026

---

Parrott examines why certain AI workflows become indispensable while others get abandoned. Using her own failed experiments as case studies, she identifies four core reasons workflows fail:

## Four Reasons AI Workflows Fail

1. **Wrong problem** — She built "Attention Desk" (an inbox aggregator inspired by Every CEO Dan Shipper's "Tend" tool) for a workday she doesn't actually have — she only gets 6–8 Slack messages daily.

2. **Aspirational self-building** — Several tools were built for an idealized version of herself, touching on the psychological concept of "symbolic self-completion," where people reach for the symbols of an identity they aspire to rather than doing the actual work.

3. **Worth building, not worth keeping** — Her AI assistant "Margot" taught her valuable lessons about context and memory architecture, even though maintenance eventually exceeded its value.

4. **Immediate rewards drive persistence** — Her compound writing plugin succeeded because it paid off quickly. Research by Woolley and Fishbach supports this: "immediate rewards predicted persistence better than delayed rewards."

## Key Framework: Agent Ops Audit

She created an "Agent Ops" audit plugin using four decisions for each workflow:

- **Keep**
- **Redesign**
- **Revisit later**
- **Retire**

## Provisional Rules

- Run any automation manually three times before scheduling it.
- Output must require under five minutes of review.
- Four unused runs trigger a keep-or-retire decision.
