# How to Move Deep Work Off Monday Morning

Brooks kept losing Monday mornings to a lawn mower. Every Monday. Same noise. It showed up on a schedule. That's a known distraction. The fix was a schedule change. The deep work had to move to a different hour.

I call the same move the traffic jam rule. If you know a specific time or location is prone to interruptions, a noisy neighbor or a busy office area, don't schedule deep work there. Route your focus time onto a smoother route. A different room. A library. A different time of day.

Map your distraction zones. Then list your top 3 known distractions. Recurring noise. A specific app. A cluttered desk. Brooks's strategy is to identify and eliminate the known distractions first. Only after those are managed do you take on the unknown, internal ones. Trying to focus through known external noise is like running a marathon with a backpack of rocks.

## What a focused person can do

Brooks defines a focused person with three capabilities.

You can sit and grind for one hour without hacks.

You can say no to interruptions to protect the work you're in. Email counts. Chat counts. Both can wait until a fixed time you already chose.

You can prioritize what deserves attention in the first place. Which of these three is your weakest link?

## Here's the picture I use

A truly focused person is like a friend obsessed with fitness. They say no to the late-night party to protect the morning workout. They say yes to one committed goal, which makes it easier to say no to everything else. Prioritization is the hardest part of focus.

So name your yes before you touch the calendar. One committed goal. Once that yes is real, the no to a party, a chat, or an empty gap gets easier. The way I explain focus, it's knowing your priorities. You protect the time after you know what the time is for.

Brooks treats focus improvement like Maslow's hierarchy. The base is tactical distraction elimination. Plug the leaks. The middle is building habits. Time blocking. Pomodoro. The top is biological optimization. Exercise and sleep. You can't build the top without a solid base. People fail at focus when they skip that foundational removal of distractions. Are you trying to meditate while your phone is buzzing?

## Sixty days, four phases

Thirty days is often unrealistic for habit formation. The roadmap uses 60 days because that window allows for a tangible transformation. Four phases. Weeks 1-2, Eliminate Distractions. Weeks 3-4, Build Habits. Weeks 5-6, Upgrade Your Brain. Weeks 7-8, Reflect and Maintain. The first three follow the pyramid from the base up. Weeks 7-8 are where you reflect and maintain what actually held.

One uninterrupted 90-minute block of deep work a day is the benchmark. That's a realistic goal for most professionals. Most people struggle with even 1 hour, so 90 minutes is a strong, achievable target for the 60-day challenge. Two hours of deep work in a day is an Asian Efficiency level of high performance.

## Weeks 1-2: Eliminate Distractions

These two weeks are the dopamine reset. Aggressively reduce digital noise. Turn off all non-essential notifications. Limit screen time by 50%. Schedule fixed times to check email and your phone. You meet them at those times.

Someone I worked with reduced her screen time from about 5 hours to 2 hours. That cut was a key step in her focus recovery. Do a notification audit today. Write down every notification still allowed to reach you, and turn off whatever isn't essential. Then write down the fixed times for email and the fixed times for your phone. Those times are the only times they get you.

## Weeks 3-4: Build Habits

Never leave gaps in your calendar for open work. Schedule specific appointments with yourself for priority tasks. Showing up to a scheduled block with a specific intention, "Write newsletter," creates a different psychological commitment than showing up to an empty slot. Intention drives execution.

Look at your calendar for tomorrow. Do you have any empty gaps? Fill them with intention. If a gap sits in a distraction zone, move it. Different room. Library. Different hour.

## Weeks 5-6: Upgrade Your Brain

A team member was skeptical about drinking a large glass of water first thing. She tried it and found it significantly improved her energy and her focus.

To sustain long-term focus, optimize three physiological levers. Regular exercise, 3 to 4 times a week. Quality sleep. Hydration, specifically drinking 16 ounces of water first thing in the morning. This is the top of the pyramid. It belongs here, in weeks 5 and 6, after the leaks are plugged and the blocks have a name.

Start your morning with 500ml of water.

## Weeks 7-8: Reflect and Maintain

Go back to the known distractions you listed. Is the predictable noise still routed around, the way Brooks moved his deep work off the lawn mower hour? Are the blocks named, and did the 90 minutes hold? Is exercise still at 3 to 4 times a week, is sleep protected, and did the morning water stay? Keep what's in place.

What is the one thing you are saying yes to that requires you to say no to other things?

Tomorrow, protect 90 minutes for that yes. Put the block on a smoother route.
