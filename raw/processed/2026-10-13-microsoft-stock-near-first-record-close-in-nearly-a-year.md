# Microsoft stock near first record close in nearly a year

The software maker has gained more than 50% from a June low, adding roughly $1.5 trillion in market value

Microsoft $MSFT stock is closing in on its first record since October 2025, with shares climbing as high as $551.13 — a gain of 1.8% — during Tuesday morning trading, extending a multi-month advance.

Monday's close of $541.15 left the stock just short of its October 2025 peak. That recovery has restored roughly $1.5 trillion in market capitalization from a June trough, when a 17% monthly drop — the steepest since 2000 — wiped out close to $600 billion as investors questioned whether heavy AI infrastructure spending would generate sufficient returns, according to Bloomberg.

The turnaround began with a July 29 earnings report, according to Bloomberg. Azure posted 43% growth, topping the 40% consensus estimate, marking the division's strongest expansion rate in four years; the results also pushed Azure's annualized revenue above $100 billion for the first time. Shares surged 16% the following day, and Microsoft went on to post its best quarter since 1998.

At least two firms raised their ratings on Microsoft stock to buy following that earnings report, according to Bloomberg. Among the 72 Bloomberg-tracked analysts who follow Microsoft, 68 rate the stock a buy and not one has a sell. The consensus price target of $584 would represent roughly 8% above where the stock ended Monday.

The rally has not been enough to rescue Microsoft's full-year standing, which was badly damaged by its first-half decline. The stock is up about 14% in 2026, trailing the Nasdaq $NDAQ 100's 21% advance. Among the so-called Magnificent Seven tech giants, Microsoft's performance is roughly in line with Meta $META Platforms, Alphabet $GOOGL, and Amazon $AMZN, but lags Apple $AAPL and Nvidia $NVDA, each up more than 20%, according to Bloomberg.

The rebound comes as Microsoft has been reshaping its AI infrastructure. The company unveiled its third-generation Maia 300 AI accelerator on Monday and said it will use the homegrown chip as its primary architecture for running AI models — a shift away from longstanding reliance on Nvidia graphics processing units. The Maia 300 is designed to handle agentic workloads at cloud scale, and Microsoft said services including Microsoft 365 Copilot and GitHub Copilot will run on it. Production is set to begin in 2027.

Microsoft's stock rally has not resolved all of the company's legal exposure from the downturn. A Michigan police and fire pension fund filed suit in June claiming Microsoft hid from investors that Azure's expansion was decelerating while large AI expenditures loomed ahead, according to The Next Web. The case has not been resolved.
