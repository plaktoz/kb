# Bitcoin Is Soaring Again. Will It Hit $100,000 by the End of 2026?

The article examines Bitcoin's recent rally and questions whether it can reach $100,000 by the end of 2026.

Bitcoin has been climbing since mid-August, rising from around $65,000, where it had been trading sideways, to nearly $90,000 last week—levels not seen since earlier in the year. As of the article's writing, Bitcoin traded at $83,460, down 1.80% on the day, with a 52-week range of $57,945.16 to $126,079.89 and a market cap of $1.7 trillion.

The author notes that this rally is notable because it happened despite the Clarity Act—legislation meant to establish a regulatory framework for digital assets—failing to advance, even though such regulatory clarity could have been a huge catalyst for Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies.

One possible explanation offered is that investors are turning to Bitcoin as an alternative to stocks, given that equity valuations are historically high right now. Despite its recent gains, Bitcoin remains down roughly 26% over the past 12 months, making it look comparatively cheap. The author points out that even if Bitcoin hit $100,000, it would still be well below its all-time high of over $126,000 reached the previous year.

However, the author expresses skepticism about the rally's durability, cautioning that gains without a clear catalyst can just as quickly reverse. The piece challenges the notion of Bitcoin as a safe-haven hedge against stock market risk, citing 2022 as a counterexample: while the S&P 500 fell 19%, Bitcoin plummeted 65%, showing it tends to move with, not against, broader risk sentiment.

The author concludes that Bitcoin is unlikely to hit $100,000 by the end of 2026, and even if it does, broader market concerns could push it back down again.

Key points: Bitcoin's ascent since mid-August has occurred without Clarity Act passage; its weaker yearly performance may make it attractive relative to expensive stocks; and its history suggests it is not a reliable safe-haven asset during market downturns.
