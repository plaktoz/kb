# Higher Body Fat Linked to Low Sperm Count, Testosterone

source_url: https://www.healthline.com/health-news/higher-body-fat-percentage-lower-sperm-count-study

---

**Author:** David Mills
**Fact-checked by:** Jill Seladi-Schulman, Ph.D.
**Published:** July 31, 2026

A new study published in *Human Reproduction* (July 29, 2026) finds that body fat percentage is a stronger predictor of male reproductive health than BMI. Researchers studied 1,058 Danish men (~18 years old), measuring body fat via bioimpedance analysis alongside semen and blood samples.

**Key findings:**
- Men with high body fat (20–24%) had, on average, a **23% lower sperm count** than those with normal body fat (8–19%)
- Men with very high body fat (25%+) showed a **15% lower sperm count**
- Higher body fat correlated with lower testosterone, lower SHBG, and altered reproductive hormone balances
- Even men with *normal BMI* but elevated body fat showed reduced sperm counts and poorer sperm morphology

Lead researcher Nis Brix, MD, noted that measuring body fat percentage "may identify men at risk" who standard BMI screening would miss.

## Expert Context

Urologist Philip Werthman described the fertility decline as "a major public health signal," explaining that excess fat tissue converts testosterone into estrogen, directly impairing sperm production.

Urologist S. Adam Ramin noted that low sperm count may signal broader health concerns beyond fertility alone.

## Male Infertility Background

- Infertility affects roughly 15% of U.S. couples; men are solely responsible in ~20% of cases and contribute to 30–40% more
- Risk factors include obesity, STIs, chemical/radiation exposure, steroid use, and excessive alcohol use

## Lifestyle Strategies to Support Male Fertility

- Regular exercise
- Well-balanced diet and healthy body weight
- Antioxidant supplements (e.g., vitamin C)
- Stress reduction and quality sleep
- Quitting smoking; limiting alcohol
- Wearing loose-fitting clothing to support testicular circulation
- Avoiding prolonged exposure to heat (e.g., hot tubs)
