# Astra and Opus just passed Turing's other test

Alan Turing is widely remembered for his test of machine versus human intelligence, but his more consequential wartime challenge was breaking Nazi Germany's Enigma code. Two cryptanalysts now say they've used LLMs from OpenAI and Anthropic to solve two previously unbroken Enigma messages.

While Turing's team built the Bombe machine to decode Enigma during WWII, a small number of archival messages remained unsolved, often due to transcription errors made by original encoders.

Developer Carter Leffen asked OpenAI's new model, Astra, to search a database of Enigma messages for an unbroken message and decode it. The model conducted its own archival research, identified context clues, built a machine simulator, and recovered the plaintext of a message unsolved since 2005. Leffen also used Astra to build an interactive website explaining the solution.

Frode Weierud, a retired electrical engineer who runs the Crypto Cellar research site, validated Leffen's solution, saying it left him in "awe." The article notes that Astra's logs referenced archived messages in a "private collection" not hosted by Weierud, and he remains uncertain whether the model accessed these directly or found them through other public archives, such as German government records.

Weierud described the model's research capabilities: "GPT-6 Astra is behaving like a very professional cryptanalyst and archive researcher." He added that its two-day output would have taken a human researcher considerably longer, noting he personally spent weeks on related archival work.

Separately, cybersecurity executive Jack Willis contacted Weierud on September 21 to report using Anthropic's Claude Opus 5 to break a different unsolved message, providing more direct guidance than Leffen did. Willis's approach relied on a known officer's signature to crack the code.

The article closes by noting seven Enigma messages remain unbroken, plus one with known plaintext but unbroken code, suggesting further breakthroughs may follow.
