# White House Whipsaws Silicon Valley (and Itself) Over A.I. Rules

The Trump administration has struggled over how to approach "open source" AI models, which are freely available to download and favored by Chinese companies.

Trump administration officials have struggled over the past few weeks with what to do about artificial intelligence. A group that includes White House chief of staff Susie Wiles, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick have debated whether to take a more interventionist approach to "open source" AI models, which are freely available to download and modify, according to more than half a dozen current and former tech and government officials.

Senior officials considered a range of actions, including sanctions, a trade blacklist against Chinese companies that make open-source AI models, or even banning U.S. cloud companies from doing business with them. But after an outcry from Silicon Valley, they appear to have changed course and are instead focused on promoting American AI models to be more competitive.

The White House has invited U.S. tech companies to discuss a framework that would allow the government to review new AI models for cybersecurity issues before they are released publicly.

"This is all happening so fast," said Senator Jon Husted, Republican of Ohio, after meeting with the chief executives of Nvidia and OpenAI. "I don't know that I have any definite conclusions about any of it."

The whiplash reflects the Trump administration's conflicting impulses on AI and the difficulty of regulating a rapidly developing technology. The White House has aggressively intervened in swaths of the economy over the last 18 months, from media to chip manufacturing to mining, but AI's pace has left the government scrambling to decide how to put its thumb on the scale.
