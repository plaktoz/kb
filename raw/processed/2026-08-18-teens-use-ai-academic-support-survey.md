# Teens use AI for academic support even though they see the dangers, survey finds

Roughly 7 in 10 teens use AI for academic support, according to a new survey of 1,017 American teens ages 13-17 from The Youth AI Safety Institute at Common Sense Media. But 39% of teens feel they are missing out on learning when they seek AI's help on an assignment.

Most teens use AI for more than just completing assignments: 63% said they use it to get answers to schoolwork, and 25% said they use those answers as-is. Teens also reported using AI to brainstorm essay ideas, get writing feedback, and better understand schoolwork — two-thirds said AI had helped them better understand what they were learning. Nearly two-thirds said they first attempt to solve problems themselves or turn to another person before using AI.

Yvette Renteria, chief program officer at Common Sense Media, said that while AI can help students get through a difficult problem more quickly, it doesn't allow for the sharpening of critical thinking and problem-solving skills. Teens most commonly reported using general-purpose AI tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, or Grok; about 41% said they used AI services such as Grammarly.

Former U.S. Secretary of Education John King said schools face a moment where they must equip students with skills to navigate AI effectively while still ensuring students do the intellectual work required for learning — including rethinking how learning is assessed, such as blue-book exams, oral presentations, and cold-calling.

While most teens are using AI for academic support, 70% of survey respondents said their teachers had never discussed how to use the technology safely. Common Sense Media has introduced a free curriculum for high schools that includes AI lessons and educator professional development, starting with younger students by exploring how technology shapes human interactions before expanding to AI chatbot relationships as students get older.
