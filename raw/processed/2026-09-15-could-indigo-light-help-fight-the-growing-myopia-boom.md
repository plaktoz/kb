# Could Indigo Light Help Fight the Growing "Myopia Boom?"

Myopia affects roughly 2.8 billion people globally, with projections reaching 4.8 billion by 2050 — a trend researchers call the "myopia boom." A new study published in *Cell Reports Medicine* examined whether shortwave (indigo) light could suppress lens-induced myopia development in tree shrews, a diurnal mammal closely related to primates.

Researchers fitted tree shrews at 24 days old with spectacles — one lens as a control, one inducing myopia. After exposing the animals to various supplemental light wavelengths, they found that indigo light in the 419–446 nanometer range, added to warm white LED lighting, completely suppressed minus-lens-induced myopia.

## Key Findings

- Environmental factors may contribute to the myopia boom, partly due to the relative absence of indigo light indoors, where people spend roughly 86% of their time.
- A simulated classroom model suggested that combining warm white LEDs with indigo light "could better support eye health in indoor locations."
- Study author Rafael Grytz noted that "blue-blocking" lenses worn by children could "plausibly increase the risk of myopia" by reducing indigo light transmission.

## Expert Commentary

- Grytz stated the findings suggest "one contributing factor is the relative absence of indigo light in built environments."
- Optometrist Brenda Montecalvo noted the study supports outdoor exposure, which has shown to slow myopia progression, though safe duration remains unclear.
- Karen S. DeLoss cautioned that animal model research "has not yet been evaluated in human clinical trials" and that safety and dosing questions remain open.

## Next Steps

Researchers anticipate two potential strategies: supplemental indigo lighting in schools/daycares, and medical devices emitting controlled indigo light for treatment. Human trials to establish safety and efficacy are the recognized next step.
