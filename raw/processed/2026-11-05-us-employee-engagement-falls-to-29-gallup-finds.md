# US Employee Engagement Falls to 29%, Gallup Finds

U.S. employee engagement fell to 29% in the third quarter of 2026, reaching the lowest of lows since it last did so, notably in 2011. Considering some of the truly unrestful years we have had in between, it is concerning that the year's engagement numbers are trending downwards so dramatically.

U.S. employee engagement fell to 29% in the third quarter of 2026, reaching its lowest level since 2011, according to new findings from Gallup. The figure marks a two-percentage-point decline from the previous quarter and continue a downward trend that began after engagement peaked in 2020.

Gallup's Q3 2026 employee engagement report also found that 18% of U.S. employees were actively disengaged, up from 17%, while 53% were not engaged.

The latest results point to growing challenges around role clarity and workplace support. Gallup estimates that each percentage point in its engagement measure represents approximately 1.6 million U.S. employees. The two-point quarterly decline is therefore equivalent to around 3.2 million fewer engaged workers.

## Employee Engagement Has Fallen From Its 2020 Peak

Gallup began tracking U.S. employee engagement in 2000, when 26% of employees were engaged. The measure reached 36% in 2020 before declining to 31% in 2024. It remained at 31% through the first half of 2026 before falling further in the third quarter.

The latest 29% reading is the first time engagement has dropped below 30% since 2011.

The distinction between employees who are engaged, not engaged and actively disengaged is important. Employees who are not engaged may be psychologically disconnected from their work, while actively disengaged employees are more strongly disconnected from their workplace. Together, these groups account for 71% of the U.S. workforce in Gallup's latest findings.

## Role Clarity and Employee Support Also Decline

Gallup identified two aspects of the employee experience that weakened alongside engagement: knowing what is expected at work and feeling cared for by someone at the workplace.

In the first half of 2026, 49% of employees strongly agreed that they knew what was expected of them. That share fell to 46% in the third quarter. The figure is also well below the 61% recorded in 2015.

The percentage of employees who strongly agreed that their supervisor or someone at work cared about them as a person declined from 40% in the first half of 2026 to 37% in the third quarter. This is also below the 47% peak recorded in March 2020.

These findings suggest that employees are reporting weaker connections to their roles and workplaces. Unclear expectations can make it harder to understand priorities and performance standards, while a lack of perceived support can weaken employees' sense of belonging.

## Remote and Hybrid Employees Record Sharper Declines

The decline was particularly pronounced among employees in remote-capable roles. Gallup reported a five-percentage-point drop in engagement among fully remote employees and a two-point decline among hybrid employees between the second and third quarters.

Engagement among on-site employees fell by approximately one percentage point. Gallup noted that the changes among fully remote and hybrid employees exceeded the margin of error, while the on-site change did not.

The report also found that the engagement advantage previously seen among remote and hybrid employees at organisations adopting AI had narrowed. However, the findings do not establish that AI adoption or remote work caused the decline.

Instead, Gallup's analysis raises questions about whether communication, role clarity and manager support are keeping pace with changes in how work is organised and how new technology is introduced.

## What the Findings Mean for HR Teams

The latest figures put a renewed focus on the everyday factors that shape employee engagement. For HR teams and managers, the findings highlight the importance of making responsibilities clear, maintaining regular communication and ensuring employees feel supported.

Organisations may need to review how managers communicate expectations, particularly with remote and hybrid teams. Employees should understand their priorities, how their performance will be assessed and how changes to their responsibilities affect their work.

As companies introduce new technologies, including AI, managers also need to explain how these tools fit into employees' roles and provide practical support during implementation. Technology adoption alone does not guarantee a better employee experience.

Gallup's Q3 findings show that engagement is declining alongside employees' reported sense of clarity and support. Addressing these workplace conditions may be an important part of rebuilding stronger connections between employees, their managers and their organisations.
