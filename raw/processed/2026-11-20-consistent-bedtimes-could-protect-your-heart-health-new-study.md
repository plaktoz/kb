# Consistent bedtimes could protect your heart health, new study

(NewsNation) — The consistency of your sleep schedule may be more important than the number of hours slept in preventing adverse heart events, according to a new study.

The research, published in Circulation: Population Health and Outcomes, looked at over 10,000 participants who wore a Fitbit over the course of six months. Researchers broke down sleep data by quality, quantity, regularity, timing and number of sleep cycles.

Night-to-night changes in sleep timing were linked to a 20-23 percent increased risk of a serious heart event. These risks included heart failure, acute myocardial infarction, cardiomyopathy and ischemic strokes.

Each 29-minute increase in sleep length from night to night increased the likelihood of a serious heart event by 24 percent. Specifically, researchers observed a 20 percent increased risk of heart failure and a 29 percent increased risk of cardiomyopathy.

Surprisingly, sleep duration was not associated with any major heart-related risks.

These findings challenge the age-old notion that the most important factor in healthy sleep is how many hours you get.

“Our finding is surprising because we can see the dominance of regularity instead of quantity,” lead researcher Nguyen Minh Tuan Viet, MD, PhD said in a press release. “Adequate quantity of sleep had some protective effect, but lack of sleep did not have much harmful effect in the study.”

The study did not establish cause and effect, but they point out a key controllable aspect of sleep that people can use to protect their health. However, more research is needed to establish if intervention lowers the risk of heart-related issues.

“Sometimes we can’t control the total amount of sleep, but the regularity- that is the thing that we can actively control,” Nguyen said. “But the regularity- consistent bedtimes throughout the week and waking up at the same time every morning, is something some people can control.”
