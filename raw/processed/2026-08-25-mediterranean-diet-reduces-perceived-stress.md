# Want to Feel Less Stressed? Try the Mediterranean Diet

A new study, "Effects of Mediterranean and Western dietary patterns on perceived stress and mental distress," published in Nutrition and Health, found that people following a Mediterranean diet reported lower perceived stress tolerance thresholds than those following a Western diet.

Lina Begdache, associate professor of health and wellness studies at Binghamton University, and her team surveyed more than 1,500 people about the foods they ate and their levels of perceived stress. Using a machine learning model, the results showed that consuming components of the Mediterranean diet is associated with lower levels of perceived stress and mental distress, while consuming Western dietary components correlated with higher perceived stress and mental distress.

Begdache noted that the findings close a gap in the literature, since most prior studies on diet and stress focused on how stress affects dietary choices and quality, rather than the reverse. Her team is now examining other aspects of brain function and behavior in relation to dietary patterns.
