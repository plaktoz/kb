# Early Menopause May Lead to Faster Onset of Alzheimer's Disease, Cognitive Decline

## Key Findings

A study published in *JAMA Network Open* found significant links between early menopause and less favorable neurological outcomes — both functional and structural — with effects potentially persisting for decades.

Researchers drew on data from two aging cohorts (the Religious Orders Study and the Rush Memory and Aging Project), tracking participants across up to 18 years. Analysis covered cognitive trajectories, neuropathologic autopsy outcomes, and longitudinal MRI scans.

## What the Research Shows

Earlier menopause correlated with:
- Faster episodic memory decline
- Earlier Alzheimer's disease diagnosis
- Accelerated late-life brain atrophy
- Greater accumulation of white matter hyperintensities (areas of damaged brain tissue)

Women whose menopause began five years earlier showed **15% higher** white matter hyperintensity volume. Each decade of earlier menopause corresponded to roughly a **2% faster** Alzheimer's onset — about 1.8 fewer disease-free years for women entering menopause 10 years earlier than average.

Surgical menopause (involving sudden estrogen loss from ovary removal) was linked to faster memory decline than natural menopause, which involves more gradual hormonal changes.

## Why Estrogen Matters

Dr. Sherry Ross, OB/GYN at Providence Saint John's Health Center, explained that estrogen supports neuronal function, brain energy metabolism, and inflammation control. When menopause arrives early, "these protective effects are lost sooner."

She emphasized: "When you enter menopause matters when it comes to brain health. How menopause happens matters too."

## Protective Steps

Ross recommended that women with early or surgical menopause, or cardiovascular risk factors, have their cognitive function monitored over time. Broadly supportive habits include:

- Healthy diet
- Adequate sleep
- Regular exercise
- Stress management
- Gut health maintenance
- Keeping the mind active

Anyone with concerns about brain health following early menopause should consult a healthcare professional.
