# Crusoe abandons $1.25B plan to use Boom turbines at AI data centers

Crusoe, a Denver-based AI data center startup that recently raised $3.9 billion, has ended its agreement to purchase stationary power plants from Boom Supersonic, another Denver company.

Boom Supersonic — known primarily for developing the Overture supersonic passenger jet — launched a side business selling natural gas-fired stationary turbines called "Superpower," which share roughly 80% of components with its airborne Symphony engine. Crusoe had agreed to spend $1.25 billion on 29 of these 42-megawatt units, with first deliveries planned for 2027.

Boom CEO Blake Scholl announced the split publicly on X, noting that turbines are "no longer part of Crusoe's near term primary power mix." He added that Boom still has other customers and is targeting ~250MW of deliveries in 2027 and 1GW by 2028.

Crusoe confirmed the breakup, saying it stays "flexible, choosing the energy solutions that are right for each site" — including turbines, renewables, batteries, and grid power.

Crusoe's Abilene, Texas campus (serving OpenAI) runs on grid power, while a separate 900MW Microsoft facility there will use on-site gas turbines. The deal's collapse is a notable setback for Boom, which raised $300 million largely to fund the turbine business, intending those profits to support Overture's development.
