# MASLD: Eating tomatoes daily may help lower liver fat buildup

An exploratory randomized controlled trial (published in Nutrients) enrolled 79 adults with metabolic dysfunction-associated liver disease (MASLD), excluding those with heavy alcohol use or BMI over 30. Participants were split into a tomato-free control group and an intervention group eating 200g raw tomatoes plus 50g tomato sauce daily for 6 weeks; researchers measured body composition, hepatic steatosis (liver fat), liver stiffness, and cholesterol.

Hepatic steatosis declined in both groups, but declined more in the tomato-intervention group. No significant differences emerged between groups on liver stiffness, lipids, liver enzymes, or BMI after 6 weeks. Researchers concluded tomato consumption "may reduce hepatic fat accumulation independently of major changes in body weight, total and abdominal adiposity."

Limitations flagged by outside experts: the 6-week window is too short to assess long-term benefit; the study excluded people with BMI over 30, though most real-world MASLD patients have obesity, type 2 diabetes, or more advanced metabolic disease, limiting generalizability; the intervention combined raw tomatoes and tomato sauce, making it impossible to isolate which component (or whether lycopene/carotenoid content) drove the effect; and the study didn't show improvement in liver stiffness, fibrosis scores, liver enzymes, insulin resistance, inflammatory markers, or body composition — so it can't be concluded that tomato consumption meaningfully alters disease progression.

The authors describe the findings as hypothesis-generating rather than clinically actionable. Experts note dietary interventions should be viewed within the broader context of treating underlying metabolic disease — sustained weight loss of ~10%+ remains the intervention with the strongest evidence for improving steatohepatitis and fibrosis. Tomatoes can be part of a healthy Mediterranean diet but shouldn't distract from therapies with demonstrated outcomes; they may also be problematic for people with GERD, and tomato-based foods can be calorically dense.
