# IBM's Dividend Survived the Old IBM. Can It Survive the New One?

The piece examines whether IBM's 31-year dividend growth streak remains sustainable as the company transforms from a hardware-centric business into a software-driven enterprise.

**Shrinking Raises:** The article contrasts IBM's historical dividend growth with recent trends, noting that raises "have come one cent at a time, from $1.63 to today's $1.69" since 2021, compared to much larger increases in the 2013-2015 period. This is happening even as shares dropped "22.31% year to date."

**Software's Growing Role:** Software now makes up nearly 45% of revenue, with about 80% being recurring. CEO Arvind Krishna is quoted describing how the company has "transformed our business, improved the durability of our revenue growth" over five years.

**Cash Flow Coverage:** Free cash flow of $14.73 billion in 2025 comfortably covered $6.255 billion in dividend payments. CFO Jim Kavanaugh emphasized free cash flow as a core "leading indicator" of shareholder value creation.

**Competing Priorities:** The article highlights tension between dividend commitments and other capital uses — $8.29 billion in acquisitions, debt rising to $61.3 billion, and a pledge to invest over $10 billion in quantum computing over five years.

**Peer Comparison:** IBM is positioned between Oracle (prioritizing AI/cloud reinvestment over dividends) and Cisco (described as "the closest analog to IBM's path" in navigating a hardware-to-software shift).

**Conclusion:** The author's verdict is that the dividend "fits" IBM's new business model, since recurring software revenue offers more stability than legacy mainframe cycles did, but growth is the tradeoff — with acquisitions and quantum investments absorbing extra capital while shareholders receive only incremental raises.
