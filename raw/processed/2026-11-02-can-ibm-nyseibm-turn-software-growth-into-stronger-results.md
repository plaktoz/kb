# Can IBM (NYSE:IBM) Turn Software Growth Into Stronger Results?

### Highlights

International Business Machines beat revenue and earnings estimates as software growth picked up and free cash flow guidance rose, but a softer gross margin weighed on the stock.

International Business Machines (NYSE:IBM) posted revenue and adjusted earnings per share ahead of consensus expectations, and the company said software growth had picked up again compared with the prior quarter. Management also raised its full-year free cash flow guidance. The mixed reaction is a familiar pattern for a company that has spent several years reshaping itself.

## What did IBM actually post for the latest quarter?

According to the release, total revenue and adjusted earnings per share both exceeded the figures that research desks had expected. The strongest part of the narrative was software, where growth recovered after a period in which market watchers had questioned whether the segment was losing momentum. Management also lifted its full-year guidance for free cash flow, a metric that the company emphasizes because it funds both the dividend and its acquisition program. The raised guide signals that the cash conversion of the business, including the contribution from recent software deals, is tracking better than the company had earlier indicated. It does not change the fact that cash flow in this business is typically weighted toward the final quarter, when large infrastructure and software contracts tend to close.

## Why does the software rebound matter so much at IBM?

Software is the engine that International Business Machines wants to dominate the mix. It carries higher margins than consulting and more recurring revenue than hardware, and it includes Red Hat, the hybrid cloud platform built around the open-source operating system and OpenShift container tools, along with automation, data and security products. A rebound in software growth therefore speaks directly to the central claim of the transformation. The prior quarters had raised questions because some parts of the portfolio, including transaction processing and certain data products, grew more slowly than the headline software figure implied. Acquisitions such as HashiCorp, which brought infrastructure automation tools like Terraform and Vault, added revenue and widened the platform, but they also complicated the organic growth comparison.

## How do infrastructure and consulting fit into the picture?

The infrastructure segment centers on the Z mainframe family and related storage and power systems. When a new cycle is in its early phase, the segment often supports both revenue and cash flow, though it can fade as the cycle matures. The company has been repositioning the practice toward helping clients deploy generative artificial intelligence, and it has talked about a growing book of business tied to those projects. Utilization, pricing and wage costs all influence how that business affects consolidated gross margin. If infrastructure revenue is lower in a quarter while consulting remains a large share of sales, the blended gross margin can slip even when software is healthy.

## Where does IBM sit among its competitors?

In enterprise software and automation, rivals include Microsoft, Oracle, ServiceNow, Broadcom's VMware unit and a range of specialist vendors. In consulting, Accenture, Deloitte and Capgemini set the competitive bar. The artificial intelligence segment adds another layer. IBM positions watsonx as a platform for building and governing models inside corporate environments, and it emphasizes smaller, domain-specific models and governance features rather than competing head-on in the largest frontier model race. That stance is a deliberate choice about where the company believes it can earn attractive margins without the capital intensity that model training requires. IBM trades on the New York Stock Exchange and is a member of the S&P 500.

## What are the main sources of uncertainty after this release?

For International Business Machines, if the shortfall in gross margin reflects temporary mix and timing, it may reverse as the year closes. If it reflects structural costs, such as the expense of delivering new artificial intelligence services or the integration of acquisitions, the pressure could persist and weigh on earnings growth even as revenue stays firm. Research desks will want to see whether the rebound reflects broad strength across Red Hat, automation and data, or whether it was driven by a few large transactions. Consulting and infrastructure both depend on enterprise spending decisions, and software renewals can be delayed when budgets are reviewed. Currency swings also affect stated results because a large share of revenue is earned outside the United States.
