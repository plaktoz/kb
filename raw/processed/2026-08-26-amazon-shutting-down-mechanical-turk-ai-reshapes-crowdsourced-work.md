# Amazon is shutting down Mechanical Turk after 21 years as AI reshapes crowdsourced work

Amazon is shutting down Mechanical Turk, ending a 21-year experiment in crowdsourced digital labor that once put humans behind tasks computers could not handle. The timing carries a certain irony: artificial intelligence is getting better at many of the jobs that helped make Mechanical Turk useful in the first place.

AWS Mechanical Turk, better known as MTurk, will close on September 30, 2026, Amazon said. "We regularly evaluate our programs, tools and services and make adjustments based on those assessments," Amazon said in a notice posted on the Mechanical Turk website. "Following an assessment, we've made the decision to close AWS Mechanical Turk, effective September 30, 2026."

Launched in 2005, MTurk created a marketplace where companies and researchers could pay people to complete small online jobs known as Human Intelligence Tasks, or HITs — data validation, research, surveys, content moderation and data collection. Amazon described the premise as resting on a simple limitation of computing at the time: "there are still many things that human beings can do much more effectively than computers." Jeff Bezos famously described the concept as "artificial artificial intelligence."

Payments could amount to just pennies per task, but the platform grew into a global labor marketplace that at one point counted more than 500,000 workers, according to Amazon.

## AI changed the economics behind Mechanical Turk

Modern AI models can now handle many tasks that once required armies of online workers — image classification, transcription, text analysis and other forms of data processing have become standard AI capabilities. A new generation of companies, including Scale AI, Mercor and Prolific, has built businesses around supplying human expertise and training data to AI developers instead, with work extending far beyond basic image labeling to programmers, scientists and other skilled professionals who evaluate and improve increasingly capable models.

Krista Pawloski, a longtime data worker and organizer with worker advocacy group Turkopticon, described MTurk as "in decline," saying Amazon appeared to invest fewer resources in the service as competing data platforms gained ground. A 2023 study by researchers in Switzerland found that up to 46% of Mechanical Turk workers participating in one experiment appeared to use AI models to complete tasks, raising questions about data quality.

Signs of MTurk's approaching end became clearer last month when Amazon stopped accepting new customers. For some workers, the closure is more than the retirement of an aging Amazon service — MTurk provided flexible remote income to people who depended on work they could perform from home, and Pawloski said some workers still rely on the platform full time. "They're concerned now," she said.
