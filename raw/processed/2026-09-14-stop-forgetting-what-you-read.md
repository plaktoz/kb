# How to Stop Forgetting What You Read

Scott argues that prior knowledge—not intelligence—most determines how well we retain what we read.

## Reading More Enables Reading Better

He challenges the false choice between quantity and quality. "In reality, the best way to read harder books is to read more books," including less demanding works that build toward more complex ones.

## Prioritize Accuracy Over Sophistication

The real quality concern is accuracy. Starting with misleading books can create a "self-reinforcing cycle" of misinformation. Scott recommends textbooks and survey courses for grounding, particularly favoring Oxford's *Very Short Introductions* series for their density and accessibility.

## The Note-Taking Trap

Elaborate note-taking or flashcard systems can paradoxically become barriers to reading more. He notes that reading itself involves active retrieval—readers constantly integrate new text with existing knowledge.

## Core Takeaway

"The key to remembering more is knowing more, and the key to knowing more is reading more." Knowledge compounds; the flywheel starts with volume.
