# Anthropic Presses Ahead With Pre-Thanksgiving IPO at Lower Valuation

Anthropic is pushing ahead with its Nasdaq IPO before Thanksgiving despite a brutal tech selloff that has slashed expectations from $2 trillion to as low as $1.1 trillion. The Claude developer aims to raise $80‑100 billion at a $1.25‑1.75 trillion valuation, riding $81 billion in annualized revenue. But voter backlash against AI data centers and jittery public markets are testing whether the first pure‑play AI IPO can land cleanly.

## The Race to Thanksgiving

Anthropic is pressing ahead with its Nasdaq IPO before the Thanksgiving holiday, aiming to raise $80-100 billion at a valuation of $1.25-1.75 trillion, according to investors cited by the Financial Times. That marks a significant comedown from the $2 trillion figure the most optimistic investors had floated just weeks ago. Some rattled investors have discussed valuations as low as $1.1 trillion, Billionaires.Africa reported.

Anthropic filed its amended S-1 registration statement on November 2, 2026, confirming it had applied to list Class A shares on the Nasdaq Global Select Market. The filing disclosed approximately $895.4 billion in purchase commitments and lease agreements as of September 30, substantially all for compute and infrasructure — underscoring just how capital-intensive the AI business has become.

## Revenue Growth Meets Market Skepticism

Anthropic's annualized reported revenue now sits at about $81 billion, up from roughly $65 billion at the end of July, per The Decoder. The company primarily sells Claude products and services to other businesses, a focus that CNBC noted has helped it avoid becoming the public face of AI — a status that will change dramatically once quarterly earnings calls begin.

Georgetown finance professor Reena Aggarwal told CNBC that public-company status alone will push Anthropic into the spotlight, and AI's current political heat will intensify that attention. Renaissance Capital senior IPO strategist Matt Kennedy offered a counterpoint: public markets require greater transparency, the debut could attract users, and public status alone should not expose Anthropic to harsher criticism than a comparable private company like OpenAI.

## The Data Center Backlash

Voters rejected data center expansion in 40 of 41 local ballot measures across 10 states during the midterms, according to CNBC's analysis. A Gallup survey from March 2026 found 71% of Americans opposed an AI data center in their local area, with 48% strongly opposed. USC Annenberg's October 2026 survey of 2,980 Americans found 62% opposed local data center construction and 71% distrusted Big Tech companies on data center development.

Anthropic's own S-1 prospectus risk factors acknowledge that data-center opposition is "increasingly organized across jurisdictions" and warn that the company's public profile could attract particular scrutiny to associated projects, per Unite.AI. Gartner analyst Nicole Greene told CNBC that the combination of fear and excitement surrounding AI produces heightened scrutiny.

## The OpenAI Parallel: Both Giants Feel the Chill

Anthropic's IPO recalibration mirrors what happened across the aisle. OpenAI, its chief rival, just closed only $11 billion of a $30 billion funding target at a $1.25 trillion valuation — a raise that fell dramatically short of ambitions and sent the company scrambling to court wealthy individual investors, according to The Decoder.

The Nasdaq 100 dropped 6.4% on Thursday following the Democratic midterm sweep before recovering somewhat on Friday, according to The Decoder. Rising borrowing costs and heavy AI spending plans triggered the selloff, and the election results accelerated it.

## What Public Ownership Means for Claude Users

For the builders and developers who rely on Claude — particularly through Claude Code, Anthropic's dominant coding tool — the IPO changes the calculus in several ways:

Pricing transparency: Quarterly earnings reports will reveal Anthropic's revenue, margins, and customer metrics. Public pressure to grow revenue could accelerate enterprise pricing changes or modify API rate limits.

Product focus: Anthropic's stated strategy emphasizes expanding its own data-center footprint and securing compute beyond its $895.4 billion in commitments. How the company allocates IPO proceeds between infrastructure and product development will directly affect Claude's capabilities.

Competitive dynamics: If Anthropic's IPO lands cleanly, it becomes the first pure-play AI company on public markets — giving it a war chest that private rivals cannot match. If it stumbles, expect more aggressive moves from OpenAI, Google, and others to capture Claude's enterprise customers.

Regulatory spotlight: Public companies face SEC scrutiny, shareholder lawsuits, and activist pressure. Any safety incident involving Claude will be amplified in quarterly filings.

## The First Pure-Play AI IPO: A Market Test

CNBC characterized Anthropic as poised to become the first pure-play AI company to enter the public market, a milestone that will test whether public investors share private markets' enthusiasm for AI at trillion-dollar valuations. University of Virginia behavioral scientist Maura Austin told CNBC that a high valuation plays into an image of Big Tech wealth and billionaire power, which she expects the public to receive poorly.

"The midterms did not stop the AI buildout. They simply repriced it," Fortune wrote. "And OpenAI is the first to pay up." Anthropic may be next in line — but the bill is coming due for everyone.
