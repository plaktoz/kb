# Why International Business Machines (IBM) Is Getting Attention Today

International Business Machines (IBM) has drawn fresh investor attention after reporting a fiscal Q2 2026 revenue shortfall and cutting its 2026 revenue growth guidance, while keeping operating pretax margin and free cash flow commitments unchanged.

IBM's revenue guidance cut sits against a mixed price backdrop, with the share price up 11.34% over 30 days yet down 18.64% year to date. The 5-year total shareholder return of 114.96% reflects a much stronger longer-term picture.

According to the most-followed narrative on IBM, fair value sits at $256.08, above the last close of $237.16, framing the guidance cut against a valuation that still screens as discounted. That narrative describes IBM as a defensive growth technology investment transitioning into a software- and AI-led enterprise platform company: while topline growth remains moderate, improving software mix, strong margins and durable cash flows underpin a compelling long-term case, particularly for investors seeking enterprise AI exposure with lower volatility than pure-play SaaS peers. That narrative could be challenged if IBM stumbles on AI execution, or if quantum investments weigh more heavily on earnings than expected.
