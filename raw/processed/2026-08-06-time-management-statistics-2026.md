# 20+ Must-Know Time Management Statistics & Facts

A sourced roundup of time management data compiled by the founders of Lifehack Method, authors of the WSJ bestseller "Winning the Week."

## Top statistics

82% of people don't have a time management system in place (33% write to-do lists, 24% use their inbox, 12% schedule everything in a calendar, and 25% just deal with things as they come up). The average worker spends 51% of their workday on tasks of little to no value — over 20 hours a week — with unnecessary commuting (13%), meetings (16%), and emails (23%) as the biggest time sucks. Employees are interrupted about 60 times a day, and recovering from a single distraction takes an average of 25 minutes. Employee distractions cost businesses $588 billion each year.

Only about 20% of the average working day is spent on activities that matter; employees spend just 27% of their time on skill-based work they were actually hired to do, versus 28% managing email, 10% on social media, and 60% on coordination overall. Business owners spend about 6.8 hours a week on low-value activities that could be delegated — CEOs who delegate well see 33% higher revenue than those who don't.

## Meetings

Professionals spend over 10 hours per week on meeting-related activities (6 hours managing/scheduling, 5 in meetings, 4 preparing). MIT found the average employee spends 16% of a working lifetime in unimportant meetings, and 72% of meetings are considered unproductive, costing businesses 24 billion hours and $37 billion each year. Attention during a meeting declines from about 91% to 64% after the first 15 minutes. Meeting time as a share of the workday rises sharply with seniority: 6% for the average employee, 35% for middle management, 50% for upper management, and 72% for a CEO.

## Email and communication

The average office worker checks email 15 times a day and spends over two hours reading and replying — 1.8 of those hours are spent on entirely irrelevant messages. Overall, 57% of the average employee's time goes to communication: about 1 hour 12 minutes on email, 1 hour 31 minutes on chat, and 1 hour 50 minutes in meetings each day.

## Stress and benefits of managing time well

Only 1 in 5 employees feel they effectively manage their daily stress and workload; workplace stress costs American companies over $300 billion a year in health costs, absenteeism, and lost performance. On the upside, 90% of people say better time management would increase their productivity, and 91% say it would lower work-related stress while improving focus, goal achievement, decision-making, and confidence. Employees can save up to 40 hours a month by managing interruptions, and effective time tracking can reduce productivity leaks by 80% and boost revenue by 61%.
