# Nvidia Just Lined Up $500 Billion. Frank Holmes Says the Thing That's Scarce Isn't Money, It's Copper

On August 10, Nvidia signed a memorandum of understanding with six major asset managers — Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR — to mobilize over $500 billion in third-party capital for AI infrastructure. Each firm creates its own platform, allowing outside investors to fund data centers, power, and GPU clusters without burdening Nvidia's balance sheet.

Frank Holmes, executive chairman of HIVE, called the arrangement akin to "the Avengers" assembling. His core argument: solving the financing problem only reveals the next bottleneck — physical materials.

## The Real Constraint: Copper and Critical Minerals

Holmes argues capital is now abundant, but the raw materials underpinning AI infrastructure are not. Copper has hit new highs, trading around $6.67/lb — up over 16% since January 2026. A single gigawatt data center consumes roughly **50,000 tons of copper**.

He also flagged China's dominance over critical mineral supply chains as a geopolitical risk, warning of potential interference with strategic mineral flows. Holmes views the supply crunch as bullish — for those already holding the scarce inputs.

## The GPU Depreciation Argument

Holmes pushes back on fears that chips will become obsolete before infrastructure catches up. He describes a pricing curve where GPU rental rates decline gradually — from $1/hour down to $0.80, then $0.60, then $0.40 — but demand persists because the customer base shifts.

The entire $500 billion financing structure rests on GPUs earning revenue for roughly six years.

## Key Takeaway

The Nvidia-Wall Street capital mobilization addresses funding. It does nothing about copper. Holmes frames the AI buildout as fundamentally a **commodities story in tech clothing**.
