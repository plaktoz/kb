# Singapore stocks end higher on Friday; STI gains 0.4%

Singapore stocks ended higher on Friday (Aug 14). The benchmark Straits Times Index (STI) gained 0.4% (23.54 points) to finish at 5,743.59.

Genting Singapore led gainers on the blue-chip index, rising 6.4% (S$0.04) to S$0.665. The worst performer among STI constituents was UOL Group, down 2.8% (S$0.27) to S$9.28.

The three local banks ended mixed: OCBC rose 0.9% to S$31.79, UOB was up 0.2% at S$41.80, while DBS finished 1.3% lower at S$75.53. Within the iEdge Singapore Next 50 Index, First Resources was the top gainer (+7.6% to S$3.95), while Yanlord Land was the biggest decliner (-9.8% to S$0.595).

Across the broader market, losers outnumbered gainers 307 to 262, on 1.4 billion securities worth S$2.7 billion traded. Genting Singapore was most actively traded by volume (156.5 million shares); DBS was most actively traded by value (S$383.1 million).

Regional indices were mixed: Hong Kong's Hang Seng lost 1.1%, the FTSE Bursa Malaysia KLCI declined 0.4%, Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 0.6%, and South Korea's Kospi was up 2.4%.

Ipek Ozkardeskaya, senior analyst at Swissquote, said it's unclear whether the Fed's priority is "tame inflation" or helping the market absorb lower rates smoothly amid growing US debt interest payments. She noted Japanese PM Sanae Takaichi favors softer rates and robust spending, but persistent yen selling pressure may now push Japan toward a near-term rate hike, since currency weakness is "probably hurting the Japanese economy more than higher rates would."
