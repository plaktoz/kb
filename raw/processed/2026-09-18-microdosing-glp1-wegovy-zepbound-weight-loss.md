# Microdosing GLP-1s Like Wegovy, Zepbound May Still Lead to Weight Loss

Recent research published in *Biology Methods & Protocols* (August 31) suggests people remaining on the lowest starting doses of GLP-1 medications — sometimes called "microdosing" — may still experience meaningful weight loss.

The study drew from electronic health records of 814 individuals on sustained low-dose semaglutide (≥three 0.25 mg doses over six+ months) and 1,016 on sustained low-dose tirzepatide (2.5 mg over six+ months).

Key findings after one year:
- Low-dose tirzepatide: ~5.5% body weight reduction
- Low-dose semaglutide: ~2.2% body weight reduction
- High-dose semaglutide (matched comparison group): ~12% weight loss

Study co-author Venky Soundararajan described the findings as pointing toward a treatment approach for patients whose goal is "cardiometabolic health with lesser side effects" and "modest sustained weight loss in the 2-7% range."

Dr. Mir Ali, not involved in the study, confirmed seeing similar patterns clinically, though he cautioned that many patients eventually plateau at low doses and require escalation to meet their goals.

On side effects, sustained low-dose semaglutide showed notably lower 24-month rates of constipation (23.3% vs. 36.3%) and nausea (39.7% vs. 50.1%) compared to higher-dose users.

The researchers note this was not a study of intentional microdosing, and dosing outside approved schedules is not FDA-sanctioned.
