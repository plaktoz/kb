# Time Management Habits That Help Ambitious People Stay Ahead

When you set goals for yourself, do you feel you've failed if you don't achieve them by a certain deadline? If you do, you may need to shift your focus and analyze your time management skills instead of your general skills.

Ambitious people often take on multiple tasks, which can lead to burnout and a messy life. You may even know someone like that. Someone who is consistently occupied by email, meetings, managing family, etc. But they may not be the most successful person you know.

Unlike the myth, there may be no direct correlation between being busy and being successful. There's a difference between successful people and everyone else; successful people have made time management a priority and procrastination a taboo for themselves.

Let's see what those time management habits look like.

## Treat Productivity as a Long-Term Practice

We have this stereotype around productivity that whoever seems busier is more productive. Having a full agenda and endless things to cross off your to-do list is not a sign of being productive. In fact, the opposite might be more true. Being busy actually leads to the worst work-life balance.

Rather than thinking of each day as a sprint for tasks, or trying to maximize each day, draw a long-term plan and set goals that you can pursue over a period of 6 months to a year. Plan actions, break them into milestones for each day, and leave a buffer for unexpected happenings.

Planning for the long term may seem difficult, but here is a tip to motivate you: plan from the perspective of your future self.

You can ask yourself: 'What will be a good decision for these 6 months?'

## Protect Time for Deep, Focused Work

You can easily waste your whole day if you keep getting distracted by other things. There are always more emails, messages, meetings, and small requests that consume your time. Many people write 100 tasks per day, only to have their entire time eaten up by 2 of them, leaving them unable to make progress on their core tasks at all.

Even if you only set 1 hour of pure focused work per day, you may achieve much better output than hours and hours of distracted work done.

This strategy truly draws a clear line between 'appearing busy' and 'actually being productive'. Instead of making daily plans, list a small number of core priority tasks to achieve your long-term goals at the start of each week and reserve time for all those tasks that matter.

## Create Systems Instead of Relying on Motivation

We always give a lot of credit to motivation for getting things done. But who can honestly keep themselves motivated for an entire month? Motivation will ultimately die out, and discipline will be what takes you further along the path to progress.

You become better than most when you stop waiting for motivation and start forming systems. To begin with, develop three fixed habits:

- Set your priority tasks for the coming week on Sunday nights.
- Fix 2-3 hours each day at the same time for deep and focused work, and remove all kinds of distractions.
- Write down your goals for the next day before you go to bed.

When you have built a system, you don't have to spend extra energy figuring out how and what to do next. You simply follow the plan.

## Make Time for Continuous Learning and Growth

Most people think being productive means applying everything they've learned into action. But often forget that continuously learning and upgrading your knowledge to match the current fast-paced world is equally important.

No matter what industry you're in, there's progress to be made each day and at each stage. This is why people who want to improve their career opportunities often choose to complete programs like an EdD online in just two years to align their educational goals with their busy schedules.

The world is not stopping for you. Every day, a new tool pops up in your ads section, and you can't help noticing how fast the world is advancing. If you don't catch up with them, other people will outsmart you.

## Set Boundaries to Prevent Overwhelm

We are the most ambitious when we start our professional journey. We say yes to all those extra tasks, unreasonable requests, and more, without setting boundaries. It eventually makes us unable to make time for our own tasks.

Instead of somehow trying to create more time to get things done, we should protect the time we already have. That means saying no sometimes. Learn to reject all non-core tasks, every extra request, and anything that eats up your time and energy.

Every opportunity is not necessarily an opportunity you have to take. Being more selective about each opportunity helps you invest your time and energy where it'll bring the best results.

People who stay ahead of others don't always have more free time than they utilize.

Rather, they simply know what things require their attention and what matters more in terms of achieving their goals.
