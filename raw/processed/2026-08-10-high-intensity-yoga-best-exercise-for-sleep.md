# One Workout Is 'Significantly Superior' For Sleep, According to Research

Scientists know exercise helps sleep, but a recent meta-analysis set out to compare which types of exercise help most. Researchers at Harbin Sport University in China combined data from 30 randomized controlled trials involving over 2,500 participants with sleep disturbances across more than a dozen countries, and found regular yoga has the strongest association with sleep improvement — ahead of walking, combination exercise, aerobic exercise, and traditional Chinese exercises like qi gong and tai chi.

The researchers found that two high-intensity yoga sessions per week, lasting up to 30 minutes each, was the "optimal exercise prescription" for poor sleep, conducted over 8-10 weeks. High-intensity yoga is distinct from gentle, slow-flow yoga — it involves faster transitions between poses that use body weight for resistance. This yoga regimen had a "significantly superior" effect compared to aerobic exercise. Walking ranked second, followed by resistance training (including Pilates and weightlifting).

The finding contrasts with a 2023 review that found three sessions of aerobic or mid-intensity exercise weekly could be most effective — though even that review found yoga had more significant sleep effects than other exercise types. A separate 2025 study found tai chi was on par with cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) at improving sleep over a 15-month follow-up.

Researchers can't yet fully explain why yoga helps sleep most, but possibilities include breath control activating the parasympathetic nervous system via the vagus nerve, and yoga's effect on regulating brainwave activity to promote deeper sleep. The authors caution the evidence base is still limited and call for further high-quality research. The study was published in Sleep and Biological Rhythms.
