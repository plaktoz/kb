# Moderna's mRNA Flu Vaccine Approved by FDA. When Is It Available?

## Overview

The FDA has approved Moderna's mFLUSIVA (mRNA-1010), the first mRNA-based seasonal flu vaccine, for adults 50 and older. It uses technology similar to mRNA COVID-19 vaccines.

## Approval Basis

Approval came from a phase 3 trial of 40,000+ adults across 11 countries. Half received mFLUSIVA; the other half got a standard-dose flu vaccine. mFLUSIVA proved superior at preventing PCR-confirmed flu illness.

The vaccine also received **accelerated approval** for adults 65+ based on a separate 2,900-person U.S. trial measuring immune response versus high-dose inactivated flu shots.

Moderna expects availability for the **2026–2027 respiratory season**.

## Why It Matters

The CDC estimated ~45,000 flu-related U.S. deaths in the 2024–2025 season, with vaccination preventing roughly 12,000. mFLUSIVA showed approximately 27% greater relative efficacy versus standard-dose vaccines for ages 50–64.

Dr. Dung Trinh called it "an important addition to our influenza prevention toolkit," noting adults over 50 face rising complication risks.

Dr. David Cutler offered a measured view, noting the accelerated approval for the 65+ group means confirmatory real-world evidence is still required — precisely the population where preventing hospitalization and death matters most.

## mRNA Speed Advantage

Traditional egg-based flu vaccines take **at least 6 months** to manufacture. mRNA vaccines can be redesigned and produced faster, potentially improving strain-matching — a recurring challenge with seasonal flu.

## Effectiveness vs. Traditional Vaccines

The phase 3 trial demonstrated roughly 27% higher relative efficacy compared to standard-dose vaccines for the 50–64 age group. Experts suggest the technology could eventually reshape responses to both seasonal flu and future pandemics.

## Side Effects

Reported side effects include:
- Headache, fatigue
- Joint and muscle pain
- Nausea, vomiting
- Fever, chills
- Injection site reactions
- Rare: severe allergic reaction/anaphylaxis

### Precautions

Inform a healthcare provider if you have:
- Prior allergic reactions to vaccines
- Immunocompromised status
- History of Guillain-Barré syndrome after flu vaccination
- Pregnancy or nursing status

## Will It Replace Traditional Flu Vaccines?

No — experts frame mFLUSIVA as an additional option, not a replacement. Cutler advised that for 2026–2027, "the most important decision is still to get an appropriate flu vaccine rather than waiting for the 'perfect' vaccine."
