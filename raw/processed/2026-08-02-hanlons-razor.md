# Hanlon's Razor: Not Everyone is Out to Get You

Hanlon's Razor is a mental model stating: "Never attribute to malice that which can be adequately explained by neglect."

The core insight is that incompetence, inattention, and honest mistakes far outnumber deliberate bad acts in everyday life. People forget things, act on incomplete information, and speak without thinking — behaviors that can look malicious but usually aren't.

## Origins

The idea predates its naming. Goethe wrote in 1774 that "misunderstandings and neglect create more confusion...than trickery and malice." Napoleon made a nearly identical observation about incompetence versus ill intent.

## Key Applications

- **Relationships:** Most perceived slights stem from carelessness, not cruelty.
- **Media consumption:** Outlets profit from manufactured outrage; applying the razor helps cut through it.
- **Confirmation bias:** Expecting malice makes you find it everywhere.
- **Availability heuristic:** Vivid past incidents distort how we interpret new ones.

## Important Caveat

The razor has limits. The recommended default is to assume good faith and forgive broadly — but if malice is confirmed, be ruthless.
