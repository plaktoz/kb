# How GEO Is Changing the Role of Brand Manager

**Publication:** Harvard Business Review – *Cold Call* Podcast, Episode 291
**Date:** September 15, 2026
**Host:** Brian Kenny
**Guests:** Julian De Freitas (HBS Associate Professor) & Andrew Yan (CEO, AthenaHQ)

## Summary

This episode examines how generative engine optimization (GEO) is reshaping brand management as AI systems increasingly synthesize and deliver information to consumers instead of presenting traditional search results.

**Core Problem:** A global automaker discovered its newest vehicle had vanished from chatbot recommendations because AI was drawing from a 2021 article. This illustrates a fundamental shift: AI doesn't just surface content—it forms opinions about brands.

**Key Distinctions – SEO vs. GEO:**
- Traditional SEO optimized for rankings among "10 blue links"
- GEO requires optimizing for sentiment, factual accuracy, and share of voice across AI platforms
- As Yan explains, "AI has an opinion about your brand"—functioning more like a trusted friend recommendation than a neutral index

**The Dark Funnel:** AI is darkening the marketing funnel, particularly at discovery and consideration stages. Buyers arrive at brand sites having already researched via AI, showing higher conversion rates but less referral-traceable traffic. Evidence includes higher average order values from AI-referred shoppers.

**Fragmentation Challenge:** Different AI models pull from varying numbers of sources (Copilot ~7, Grok ~20+), and brands may be positioned differently across platforms roughly 55% of the time. Reddit, YouTube, and LinkedIn rank among the most-cited sources—not traditional authoritative outlets.

**Three Intervention Types (AthenaHQ's approach):**
1. Managing outdated "vestigial content"
2. Optimizing existing content
3. Creating net-new content to expand the "surface area of search"

**Organizational Implications:** De Freitas advises against creating a standalone "Head of GEO" role, warning it could silently excuse others from responsibility. Instead, existing cross-functional leaders should coordinate around a shared dashboard.

**Human-AI Balance:** Yan envisions "semi-autonomous marketing" where AI handles rote formatting and signal detection while humans retain strategic and judgment-level decisions—analogous to autonomous vehicle autonomy levels.

**Enduring Principle (De Freitas):** "Much of the traditional brand wisdom still applies"—consistent messaging, authorial control—but "the mechanisms by which you get there are changing."
