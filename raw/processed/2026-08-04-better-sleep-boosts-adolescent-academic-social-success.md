# Better sleep may boost academic and social success in adolescents

A new UC Davis Health study published in Psychological Medicine suggests that when it comes to helping adolescents succeed in school and maintain healthy friendships, sleep may matter more than screen time, exercise, or diet — particularly for youth experiencing anxiety, depression, or other mental health challenges.

Researchers examined how four lifestyle factors — sleep quality, screen time, physical activity, and diet — influenced the relationship between mental health symptoms and academic/social functioning in children ages 10 to 13, using data from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) Study. Sleep was the strongest factor associated with better grades and fewer social problems; screen time was second. "This finding surprised us. We expected sleep to be important, but we didn't expect it to stand out so clearly above other lifestyle factors, particularly screen time," said Jason Smucny, the study's first author.

Sleep was the strongest lifestyle factor linking mental health symptoms to lower grades, accounting for 18.5% of the association with depression, 36.3% with anxiety, and 8.3% with psychotic-like disturbances. Screen time accounted for smaller shares (5-6.3%) across the same conditions; exercise and diet had smaller effects still.

Globally, about one in seven adolescents experience a mental disorder, with depression and anxiety the leading causes of illness and disability among young people. Senior author Tara Niendam, executive director of the UC Davis Early Psychosis Programs, noted that while healthy lifestyle habits aren't a substitute for professional mental health care, "sleep is something parents and families can address... a practical way to support a child's emotional well-being and day-to-day functioning."
