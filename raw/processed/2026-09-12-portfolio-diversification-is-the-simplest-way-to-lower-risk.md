# Portfolio Diversification Is the Simplest Way to Lower Risk

The author, a dividend-focused investor, outlines how they built a ~34-investment portfolio balancing income and capital appreciation.

**Key approach:**

- **Outsource complexity** — Uses mutual funds, closed-end funds, and ETFs to cover growth stocks, healthcare, convertibles, and option-income strategies they can't manage alone
- **Focus on dividend stocks** personally — targeting companies with long histories of increases and historically high yields (e.g., Realty Income, PepsiCo, Black Hills, Enbridge)
- **Sector spread** — REITs, consumer staples, utilities, and energy pipelines feature prominently
- **Position discipline** — Builds to a set dollar amount per position, then stops adding
- **Cash patience** — Holds cash rather than forcing investments; only acts on "strong conviction"

The author notes that owning 50 stocks (as Motley Fool recommends) doesn't guarantee diversification if they're concentrated in one sector. True diversification spans "a wide range of sectors and asset classes."

The core takeaway: know your limitations, outsource what you can't do well, and don't force investments when nothing compelling is available.
