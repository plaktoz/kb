# Market Factors: Sectors with 'triple momentum'

BofA Securities quantitative strategist Nigel Tupper screens for stocks with triple momentum — rising earnings expectations, 12-month price increases, and positive news flow — and the technique has generated significant outperformance. Global stocks with positive triple momentum have outperformed those with negative triple momentum by 11.0 percentage points year to date, despite underperforming by 3.5 percentage points in July.

Over the last five years, the triple positives have outperformed the triple negatives by 60.2 percentage points cumulatively. The triple momentum stocks outperformed the MSCI All Country World Index (ACWI) by 7.3 percentage points over the past three months.

Investors concerned that global markets are peaking might be surprised that semiconductors and tech hardware are the sectors with the strongest average triple momentum. Global banks are next, thanks to a significant contribution from Canadian lenders, followed by diversified financials, industrials and insurance. The sectors with the weakest momentum are media and entertainment, consumer staples and software.

There is a country-level breakdown of sector momentum as well — Korean consumer staples shows strong price momentum, likely reflecting local investors rotating out of the country's giant, volatile semiconductor stocks that dominate the local benchmark.

## Quick hits

Datadog Inc. has become a poster child for AI-adjacent software, swinging wildly on sentiment changes and company-specific news. The stock more than doubled from April 4, 2026, rising from US$105 to US$288 on August 4, and is now 18 per cent below that peak. Management raised revenue growth guidance to 30 per cent year-over-year during its latest earnings report. BofA Securities has a US$305 target.

BMO senior economist Robert Kavcic thinks fears of a domestic recession can be put to bed — data flow indicates a significant bounce in GDP growth for the second quarter, with BMO estimating 3.8 per cent annualized. Kavcic cautions that above-trend growth might be fleeting: net imports have helped recent growth, but more tariffs could be on the horizon, and residential investment has merely gone from negative to neutral. He loosely forecasts a 1.5 per cent run rate for GDP.
