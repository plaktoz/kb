# What Is 'Biblical Eating'? Inside TikTok's Latest Whole-Food Obsession

"Biblical eating" is a growing social media trend in which Christian influencers encourage followers to base their diets primarily on foods mentioned in scripture. The trend discourages processed foods in favor of nutrient-rich whole foods and has amassed a following among Make America Healthy Again (MAHA) proponents. Also known as the "Bible diet," it claims to "strengthen faith," encourage healthier eating, and even reduce visceral fat.

Commonly promoted foods include raw milk, lamb, beef, pomegranate, bread (mostly sourdough), goat meat, figs, fish, raw honey, butter, salt, and olive oil. There is no universally accepted version — some followers focus strictly on scripture-mentioned foods, while others interpret it more broadly around whole, minimally processed foods.

Nutrition experts say many of the promoted foods can be part of a healthy diet. Registered dietitian Kristin Kirkpatrick noted that any trend encouraging avoidance of processed foods and mindful, plant-based eating is likely better than the standard American diet. Dr. Sari Eitches said the diet avoids common pitfalls of restrictive diets because it includes healthy fats, varied proteins, and high-fiber grains, fruits, and vegetables. Dr. Yoshua Quinones added that the faith-based framing may improve adherence and mindfulness around eating.

However, experts caution that Biblical eating is not a formally defined or scientifically studied diet. Josh Howard, a PhD candidate in American religious cultures at Emory University, warned that many influencers promoting it lack formal nutrition training, and that framing "good Christian influencer" against "bad health professional" can lead people away from trusting scientific expertise. Quinones and Eitches both cautioned against versions that become rigid, unnecessarily restrictive, or induce guilt about eating — noting the healthiest approach is flexible, nutritionally complete, and sustainable rather than dogmatic.
