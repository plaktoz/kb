# Testosterone Prescriptions for Women Surge Amid Pharmacy Access Barriers

Testosterone prescriptions for females rose 146% between January 2023 and July 2026, per a Truveta analysis reported by Reuters. Despite this surge, no FDA-approved testosterone products exist specifically for females, creating dosing complications and pharmacy access barriers.

The FDA responded on September 17 by calling for more data and encouraging manufacturers to pursue female-specific product studies.

## What evidence supports testosterone use?

The Menopause Society endorses testosterone for hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) in postmenopausal women. Dr. Stephanie Faubion of Mayo Clinic noted the evidence does not support testosterone for fatigue, bone density, muscle mass, or mood. She emphasized keeping hormone levels "in the normal range for a premenopausal woman."

HSDD affects roughly 1 in 8 females ages 45–65. Studies show testosterone can improve sexual desire, arousal, and satisfaction.

## Access challenges

Since no female-specific products exist, male-formulated products are prescribed at lower doses — creating pharmacy fulfillment issues. Some prescriptions have been rejected outright.

## Research pipeline

Over 30 trials are currently studying testosterone for various conditions in females. Company Avivo Bio is developing a female-specific therapy and studying breast tissue effects.

Dr. Sherry Ross argued testosterone "should no longer be an afterthought" in menopause care, while more research continues.
