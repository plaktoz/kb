# Microsoft (MSFT) Stock May Be 1% Undervalued On Fresh AI Revenue News

Microsoft has returned 69.3% over the past 5 years but only -4.0% over the last year. Current valuation checks send a mixed signal: Microsoft scores 3 out of 6 on Simply Wall St's valuation checks.

Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) view: Microsoft generates about $96.0 billion in trailing-twelve-month free cash flow. The DCF model's intrinsic value estimate is about $499/share, roughly 0.8% above the recent market price — essentially fairly valued, given uncertainty about how sustainable Microsoft's AI-driven revenue (much of it via OpenAI) will prove.

Earnings multiple view: Microsoft trades at a P/E of 27.5x, below the Software industry average of 31.4x but modestly above its direct peer average of 24.9x. A tailored "fair" P/E of 48.8x (based on growth, margins, scale, and risk profile) implies the stock is pricing in meaningfully less growth than the framework would suggest — reading as undervalued on this earnings-based approach.

Community narratives diverge sharply: the bull case (12% undervalued) centers on accelerating AI integration across Azure AI, Copilot, Dynamics 365, GitHub, and Fabric driving new revenue and usage intensity. The bear case (25% overvalued) points to free cash flow falling even as operating profit rose $26.7 billion over the same twelve months — a signal of surging AI infrastructure capex.

Bottom line: Microsoft is roughly fairly valued on DCF but appears undervalued on a P/E basis relative to industry and its own fundamentals-adjusted fair multiple. The key open question is whether AI/cloud spending converts into cash flow durable enough to justify both the current price and any further multiple expansion.
