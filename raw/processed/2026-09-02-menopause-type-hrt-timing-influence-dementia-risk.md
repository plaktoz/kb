# Menopause Type and HRT Timing Influence Dementia Risk, Large Study Suggests

A large observational study published in *Alzheimer's & Dementia* analyzed data from over 183,000 postmenopausal women in the UK Biobank, followed for an average of 13 years. Researchers found that women who used hormone replacement therapy (HRT) had roughly a 10% lower dementia risk and a 16% lower Alzheimer's risk compared to non-users.

Key findings include:
- Greatest risk reduction occurred when HRT was started between ages 46–56
- Women who underwent **surgical menopause** showed stronger protective associations than those with natural menopause
- **APOE4 gene carriers** also showed stronger associations

## Expert Commentary

Lead author Anne-Marie Minihane, PhD (University of East Anglia) noted that HRT's effects on brain health are "complex and influenced by individual biological factors," and that the findings suggest HRT "may also play a role in long-term cognitive health for some women."

OB/GYN Sherry Ross, MD explained that estrogen loss during menopause can negatively affect gray matter, which governs memory, cognition, and emotion regulation. Starting estrogen early in the menopausal transition may reduce those effects.

Internist Dung Trinh, MD offered an important caveat: this was observational research and "cannot prove that HRT itself prevented dementia," cautioning against starting HRT solely for dementia prevention based on these findings alone.

Trinh called for future randomized studies comparing different hormone formulations, delivery methods, doses, and patient subgroups — including APOE genotype stratification — to better define which women might benefit and when treatment should begin.
