# AI is killing student writing. Here's how Alpha is using AI to save it

As more students offload their writing to AI, their writing skills — and their love of writing — are declining. AlphaWrite is the AI tool Alpha School students use to learn how to write, built on the same evidence base as The Writing Revolution's Hochman Method: explicit instruction from sentences to paragraphs to essays, embedded in content that matters to students.

Students work through a structured, mastery-based progression:

1. **Sentence practice** — sentence-combining, expanding, and correcting exercises tied to topics of the student's choice. Research shows these sentence-level tasks are more effective than isolated grammar drills.
2. **Paragraph construction** — once fluent at sentences, students learn single-paragraph outlines (topic sentence, key details, closing sentence), organizing their own thoughts rather than filling in a template.
3. **Essay building** — only after paragraph-level mastery does the app unlock multi-paragraph essays, guiding planning, drafting, and revising in manageable chunks.

Because the app is adaptive and mastery-based, students get far more practice and feedback than a traditional "turn in your essay, wait two weeks" model, receiving at least 10x more writing reps with immediate, targeted corrections. The design reflects research consensus: explicit structured instruction, sentence-first complexity building, grammar taught in context rather than isolation, and deliberate practice with prompt feedback.

Results from this year's NWEA MAP scores across Alpha Schools: before AlphaWrite, students grew 2.1x typical growth in reading and 2.7x in language. This year, students using AlphaWrite grew 3.6x in language and 3.3x in reading, compared with 1.8x and 2.2x for students at the same campuses who didn't use it.

The author is not anti-generative-AI in general, but anti-gen-AI as a shortcut around thinking. AlphaWrite uses AI as a personalized tutor that scaffolds learning rather than an answer key: it surfaces targeted prompts requiring students to build, fix, or improve sentences themselves; diagnoses patterns like run-ons or weak topic sentences and coaches revisions instead of rewriting drafts; and keeps challenge level "just right" so effort turns into skill. AI is also used for personalization — letting students choose topics they care about so mechanics practice and creative application build a genuine love of writing.
