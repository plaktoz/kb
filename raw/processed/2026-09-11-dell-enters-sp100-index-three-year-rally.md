# Dell Enters the S&P 100 Index After Monstrous Three-Year Rally

Dell Technologies will join the S&P 100 index on September 21, 2026, following a roughly 700% stock surge over the prior three years. The same rebalancing adds Palo Alto Networks, Arista Networks, and SanDisk, while removing Nike, Simon Property Group, and Colgate-Palmolive.

The rally is rooted in AI infrastructure demand. In fiscal Q2 2027 (ending July 2026), Dell posted $47 billion in revenue — up 58% year-over-year — with earnings per share more than tripling to $7.04. The Infrastructure Solutions Group generated $31.8 billion in revenue, up 89%.

Dell recorded $60.9 billion in AI orders during the quarter, setting a fresh record, with a $95 billion AI server backlog. Over the trailing 12 months, AI server orders exceeded $131.7 billion.

CEO Michael Dell noted a structural chip shortage, suggesting conditions could worsen in 2027. The company raised full-year revenue guidance by $25 billion to $192 billion, with AI server revenue expected to triple to $74 billion. Full-year EPS guidance stands at $25.50, up roughly 150%.

COO Jeff Clarke projected the AI infrastructure market could exceed $1 trillion by 2030, with AI comprising 75% of data center demand by then. Dell also noted 1.2 million customer servers still running on 14th-generation hardware or older, representing a significant upgrade pipeline.

S&P 100 inclusion compels index-tracking funds to purchase shares, creating additional institutional demand. Of 21 analysts covering the stock, 14 recommend "Buy" and seven recommend "Hold," with an average price target of $595 against a then-current price of $535.
