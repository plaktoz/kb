# 20-Minute Workout or 90-Minute Nap May Offset Effects of a Sleepless Night

A McGill University study published in *PNAS* found that healthy young adults who either exercised or napped after 30 hours without sleep outperformed a control group on memory tests.

- **Exercise group:** 20 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous aerobic activity → ~21% better memory performance
- **Nap group:** 90-minute nap → ~23% better memory performance
- **Control group:** No intervention; average hit rate of 0.46 vs. 0.56–0.57 in intervention groups

The study focused on **episodic memory**, which is particularly vulnerable to sleep deprivation's effects on information encoding.

## Mechanism

Exercise and napping appeared to work through **different brain pathways**: napping restored a more learning-favorable brain state, while exercise improved encoding efficiency — both confirmed via EEG readings.

## Caveats

- Participants were healthy adults aged 18–35 with no sleep disorders
- Benefits were limited to memory hit rates, not broader cognitive performance
- Neither strategy replaces quality sleep; "the ideal nap is less than 30 minutes," per Stanford's Dr. Clete Kushida
- Real-world applicability requires further research

## Practical Implications

Findings may benefit students, healthcare workers, and shift workers. As one expert noted, workplace policies should consider "access to protected nap time and/or aerobic exercise" when sleep loss is unavoidable.
