# Early Menopause Linked to High Blood Pressure

source_url: https://www.healthline.com/health-news/early-menopause-may-increase-high-blood-pressure-risk

---

**Author:** Nancy Schimelpfening, MS
**Fact-checked by:** Jill Seladi-Schulman, Ph.D.
**Published:** July 31, 2026

A large study using UK Biobank data found that women experiencing menopause at younger ages face elevated hypertension risk later in life. The association was strongest for premature menopause (before 40), with peak risk among women who entered menopause between ages 25–35.

Participants were categorized as:
- **Normal onset** – after age 45
- **Early menopause** – ages 40–45
- **Premature menopause** – before age 40

Surgical menopause initially appeared linked to higher blood pressure, but that association disappeared after adjusting for lifestyle and health variables.

## Why Estrogen Matters

Dr. Heather Hirsch explained that estrogen supports nitric oxide production, helping vessels "relax and dilate," and preserves arterial flexibility. Losing ovarian estrogen early removes these protections "years or even decades" ahead of schedule. Indirect effects include worsened sleep, weight changes, and reduced insulin sensitivity.

## Management Tips (Dr. Jayne Morgan)

- Discuss hormone replacement therapy with your doctor
- Take prescribed blood pressure medications
- Consider statins for cholesterol
- Prioritize sleep, exercise, and a protein/fiber-rich diet
- Check vitamin D levels
- Monitor blood pressure at home regularly — it "often increases silently"
