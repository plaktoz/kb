# Graph Theory

Graph theory is the mathematical study of graphs—structures modeling pairwise relations between objects via **vertices** (nodes) connected by **edges** (arcs).

## Definition

A graph consists of vertices connected by edges. Graphs may be:
- **Undirected** or **directed** (edges have orientation/arrows)
- **Simple** or **multigraph** (allowing multiple edges between same vertices)
- **Weighted** (edges assigned numerical values)

## History

Leonhard Euler's 1736 paper on the Seven Bridges of Königsberg is considered the field's founding work. The term "graph" was coined by James Joseph Sylvester in 1878.

Key milestones include Arthur Cayley's study of trees, Kirchhoff's circuit laws (1845), the first textbook by Dénes Kőnig (1936), and the computer-aided proof of the four-color theorem by Appel and Haken (1976).

## Major Subareas

- **Topological** – graph embeddings, crossing numbers, map coloring
- **Algebraic** – spectral theory, symmetry via group theory
- **Geometric** – planar graphs, intersection graphs, visibility
- **Extremal** – maximum edge counts, Turán-type problems
- **Random graphs** – probabilistic methods, Erdős–Rényi models

## Applications

Graphs model systems across computer science, linguistics, chemistry, physics, sociology, and biology—including neural networks, molecular structures, and social networks.
