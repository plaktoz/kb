# What Sun Got Wrong

Cantrill reflects on Sun Microsystems' failures while celebrating Oxide Computer's OxCon meetup, where the team wore homage shirts to defunct computer companies, including a "Sun Microsystems"-styled Oxide design.

While acknowledging Sun's genuine legacy — Oxide's mission traces back to Scott McNealy's own words — Cantrill argues the core problem was that Sun had grown bored with the mechanics of running a business.

He illustrates this with a 2005 episode: a fast-growing startup (later revealed as Joyent) was already using OpenSolaris and wanted to buy Sun hardware. Sun couldn't close the deal. Dell, by contrast, had a rep calling the next morning after a late-night web form submission, handling nearly everything. The startup documented this in a post titled "The Sun Doesn't Shine on Me."

Cantrill was working on Fishworks when he read that post and recalls thinking that strategic success means nothing without operational execution. Sun ultimately couldn't right itself.

The coda is striking: Cantrill later joined Joyent, and "Steve from Dell" was also hired there. The two eventually co-founded Oxide together.

The homage shirts aren't pure nostalgia — they represent a commitment to studying what great companies got wrong, not just celebrating what they got right.
