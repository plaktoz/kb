# What to Expect in Markets This Week: Investors Prep for Earnings Season; Delta, Pepsi Report

It's the fourth quarter already? It sure is, as of last Thursday—and so investors are preparing for a flurry of new earnings reports. Analysts think there's a good chance they'll like what they see.

Third-quarter results start trickling in this week, with Delta, Pepsi and Levi Strauss due to report. Earnings season will begin in earnest next week, when numbers from big banks like JPMorgan Chase and Bank of America mark what's more or less considered the start of the next round.

Analysts have grown bullish about third-quarter results over the course of the quarter, raising earnings per share estimates 1.3% for companies in the S&P 500, according to a late-September analysis from FactSet. Analysts tend to lower their estimates as the quarter progresses, according to FactSet.

Upward revisions suggest earnings could "continue to have momentum," Wolfe Research said.

"Although another quarter of solid revisions creates a higher bar for companies reporting results over the coming weeks, we continue to have faith that the AI [buildout] remains strong, and this earnings season will likely act as a positive catalyst for stocks amidst a choppy macro backdrop," its analysts wrote last week.

Analysts expect S&P 500 constituents to report 12.1% year-over-year revenue growth in the third quarter, along with 29.1% earnings growth, per FactSet. If those estimates pan out, S&P 500 companies would deliver earnings growth above 25% for three quarters in a row, FactSet said.

## Market Recap

The Dow industrials and S&P 500 booked losses last week, falling 1.3% and 0.3%, respectively. The Nasdaq Composite rose 0.5%, on Friday touching an intraday record high. The Dow and S&P 500 ended last month down 4.3% and 0.5%, respectively, while the tech-heavy Nasdaq rose 1.9% in September. An inflation reading came in cooler than expected, fueling speculation that the Fed won't raise rates this month. Still, the yield on 10-year Treasury notes hit levels not seen for decades.

## This Week's Top Events

Here's a look at major events in the week ahead. TradingView publishes a more detailed calendar, but clicking the link will take you off the Investopedia site.

**Wednesday, Oct. 7:** The Fed is scheduled to publish minutes from September's Federal Open Market Committee meeting at 2 p.m. ET. The minutes may explain how monetary policymakers see the tightening cycle evolving after the mid-September rate hike. Four of 19 FOMC members indicated in September that they anticipate two more hikes in 2026, according to the central bank's "dot plot." A number of Fed officials then said the central bank must make headway on bringing down inflation in speeches. But New York Fed President John Williams said last week that another rate increase may not be necessary in October after an inflation reading came in cooler than expected.

**Wednesday:** Applied Digital (APLD) is set to publish its fiscal first-quarter results after the closing bell and hold a conference call at 5 p.m. ET. Company shares declined early this year when Nvidia (NVDA) sold its stake in the data center provider.

**Wednesday:** Levi Strauss (LEVI) is slated to host a conference call on its fiscal third-quarter results at 5 p.m. ET, with the figures expected earlier that day. The apparel company slightly outperformed expectations last quarter. The clothing company Aritzia (ATZ.TO) also reports this week.

**Thursday, Oct. 8:** Pepsi (PEP) is scheduled to release its fiscal third-quarter results at 6 a.m. ET and host a live question-and-answer session at 8:15 a.m. ET. The beverage and snack maker narrowly beat expectations last quarter. Lamb Weston Holdings (LW), which supplies restaurants with frozen fries, also reports this week.

**Friday, Oct. 9:** Delta Air Lines (DAL) is slated to hold a conference call on its "September quarter" at 10 a.m. ET, with figures for the July-to-September period expected ahead of the event. Delta topped estimates last quarter, with CEO Ed Bastian saying customers continue to pay for airfare, despite ticket prices rising in response to soaring fuel costs.

**Friday:** The University of Michigan is set to release the October Consumer Sentiment Index reading at 10 a.m. ET. Another consumer confidence index found sentiment plunged in September, hitting levels not seen since 2014.
