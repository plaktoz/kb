# Microsoft Just Ripped 28% in a Month. What Would It Take to Get MSFT Stock Up to $600?

Microsoft stock was up 28% over the past month to $490.39, while the State Street Technology Select Sector SPDR ETF (XLK) gained just 3% over the same span. Microsoft stock was down 3% over the past year through Monday's close, so this month's move looks more like a re-rating than a melt-up. The Wall Street consensus price target sits at $569.45, and the 52-week high is $549.20, meaning $600 would push shares into territory not touched in the past year. Analyst positioning is unusually clean: 14 strong buy, 40 buy, 3 hold, zero sell. Forward EPS sits at $19.97.

## Backlog Signals the Path Above the 52-Week High

Microsoft's July 29 earnings report delivered non-GAAP EPS of $4.74, beating consensus by 11.81%. Revenue came in at $90.01 billion, up 17.8% year over year — Microsoft's fifth consecutive quarter topping consensus EPS. Net income rose 31.33%, aided by a $3.2 billion gain from Microsoft's investment in Anthropic.

Commercial remaining performance obligations grew 84% to $678 billion — contracted revenue not yet recognized, arguing forward growth is visible rather than speculative. Azure grew 43% in the quarter and crossed $100 billion in annual revenue for the first time, while Microsoft 365 Copilot passed 30 million paid seats. Q1 FY2027 Azure guidance calls for 45% growth in constant currency.

## How the Hyperscaler Race Actually Stands

Azure runs neck-and-neck with Amazon Web Services on scale, while Alphabet has closed a real gap in enterprise cloud through Google Cloud and Gemini adoption. Oracle is the more disruptive question mark, running a multi-cloud database strategy that plants Oracle databases inside Amazon, Google, and Microsoft clouds while stacking up its own AI infrastructure contracts.

## The Bear Case Sitting Underneath the Rally

Microsoft's full-year capital expenditures reached $115.95 billion in FY2026, with fourth-quarter capex more than doubling year over year. Free cash flow fell 23.19% in the quarter even as operating cash flow rose 30%. If Azure decelerates before capex normalizes, the return-on-invested-capital math gets uncomfortable quickly. More Personal Computing segment revenue fell 4% in the quarter, and Windows OEM and devices are guided to a high-teens decline for the full fiscal year.

## What It Would Actually Take to Hit $600

Forward EPS of $19.97 needs to prove conservative through the next two or three quarters, backed by Azure holding its guided 45% constant-currency growth and Copilot seat additions continuing to accelerate. Free cash flow turning the corner as the capex cycle peaks would seal it. The company's next earnings report is the next scheduled catalyst.
