# Businesses looking to 2027 need to tackle the technology distractions draining focus

As businesses approach 2027, productivity plans are likely to contain plenty of discussion about artificial intelligence, automation and digital transformation. Yet a more fundamental issue may determine whether such investments work: *can people actually concentrate on the work that matters?*

[New research commissioned by Adobe Acrobat](https://www.adobe.com/uk/acrobat/resources/business-impact-of-focus.html) indicates that distraction is having measurable commercial consequences for businesses. The survey found that one in ten business leaders reported losing more than $100,000 in revenue because of a lack of focus. This comes against a still-challenging economic background. Such conditions give added context to Adobe Acrobat's finding that financial strain is the most commonly reported distraction for business leaders.

For companies planning their next phase of digital development, the research points towards three practical priorities: simplifying processes, reducing repetitive work through AI, and allowing work to move effectively between devices and locations.

## The price of distraction

The [Business Impact of Focus study](https://www.adobe.com/uk/acrobat/resources/business-impact-of-focus.html) was based on a survey of 350 adults conducted by 3 Gem on behalf of Adobe Acrobat between 14 and 19 February 2026. This methodological point matters since the findings are self-reported perceptions and experiences from a relatively small sample, rather than independently measured productivity or revenue data. Even with that caveat, the picture provides an interesting indication of the pressures facing business owners.

Financial issues, including inflation and wage demands, were identified by 51 percent of respondents as their leading distraction. Personal pressures were cited by 43 percent, while 39 percent pointed towards problems involving team members, including poor attendance.

The effects appear across organisations. Operations was the business area most commonly identified as suffering from distraction, at 25 percent, followed by finance at 21 percent. Some 28 percent of respondents reported reduced day-to-day efficiency and consistency when focus deteriorated.

The original research supplied to Digital Journal also records 24 percent reporting reduced productivity and 21 percent procrastination and wasted time. Furthermore, 17 percent cited team burnout associated with poor senior decision-making or inadequate work-life balance.

There is an important lesson here for technology strategies. Adding another application or AI service does not automatically make an organisation more productive. Digital technology has greatest value when it removes friction rather than adding another layer through which employees must navigate.

*Priority one: simplify the process*

The first 2027 consideration is therefore surprisingly straightforward: simplify. Complicated document processes can impose small interruptions repeatedly throughout a working day. Documents pass between people for alterations, feedback and approval, while multiple versions can generate additional administrative work. Adobe Acrobat's advice is understandably linked to its own products, so it should be regarded as vendor guidance rather than independent recommendation. Nevertheless, [the underlying principle applies](https://www.adobe.com/uk/acrobat/resources/business-impact-of-focus.html) much more widely. The company proposes bringing document editing, notes, feedback and communication together within a more streamlined workflow.

The wider objective for a business is less about one specific product and more about eliminating unnecessary stages. When management reviews its 2027 technology stack, a useful question is therefore not simply "what should we add?" but also "what can we remove?" Technology consolidation can be just as significant a digital strategy as technology acquisition.

*Priority two: make AI eliminate repetition*

The second area concerns artificial intelligence. The significant opportunity here is not necessarily to have employees perform more tasks *with* AI. Instead, businesses should examine whether AI can remove repetitive activities which otherwise compete for employees' attention.

Adobe Acrobat, for example, identifies generative AI applications capable of summarising information from files and assisting with presentation creation. Its focus research also points towards AI being used alongside activities such as PDF editing and conversion. This distinction matters as businesses plan investments. Introducing generative AI merely creates another source of work if employees continually have to decide when to use it, move information into it and verify what comes back.

The better question for 2027 is where automation can safely reduce low-value repetition while leaving human attention available for judgement, communication, creativity and decision-making. The word "safely" is important. Organisations still need appropriate oversight of AI output, particularly where generated material feeds into important business decisions. Automation should consequently be designed around reliable workflows rather than deployed simply because an AI function is available.

*Priority three: make workflows travel*

The third priority reflects the increasingly distributed nature of digital work: information needs to remain usable when people change devices or working locations. Adobe Acrobat highlights mobile applications as one approach, allowing documents to be created, viewed and shared from phones, with comments appearing across devices.

Again, the broader lesson goes beyond PDFs. A theoretically efficient workflow can become inefficient as soon as an employee has to switch platform, duplicate information, locate the latest version of a document or postpone an activity until they can access a different device. Cross-platform continuity should therefore become one of the tests companies apply to their 2027 digital systems.

There is also a more positive side to the study. Almost half of the business leaders surveyed said improving focus across their organisation had contributed to financial growth, including pay rises and promotions. The survey also found 60 percent saying a healthy work-life balance helps prevent unnecessary distractions, while just over half regarded building a reliable team as important to maintaining focus.
