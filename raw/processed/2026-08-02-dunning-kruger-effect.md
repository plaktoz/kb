# Unskilled and Unaware of It: How Difficulties in Recognizing One's Own Incompetence Lead to Inflated Self-Assessments

People consistently overestimate their own abilities — believing they're above-average drivers, investors, and decision-makers.

This stems from a dual burden: incompetent individuals not only draw wrong conclusions, but lack the metacognitive capacity to recognize their own errors.

Darwin's observation frames it well: "Ignorance more frequently begets confidence than does knowledge."

## Key Findings

- Success in most life domains requires knowledge, wisdom, and sound strategy.
- People vary widely in how effectively they apply such knowledge.
- Those who are incompetent in their chosen strategies suffer doubly — poor outcomes and an inability to perceive those outcomes as poor.

## Core Conclusion

The skills needed to perform competently in any domain are largely the same skills needed to evaluate that competence. As a result, incompetent individuals tend to inflate their self-assessments more than competent ones do.
