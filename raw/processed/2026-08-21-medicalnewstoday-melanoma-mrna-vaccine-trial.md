# Novel mRNA vaccine plus immunotherapy reduces melanoma relapse in phase 3 trial

Merck and Moderna announced phase 3 trial results (INTerpath-001, NCT05933577) on August 19 for an experimental therapy combining a personalized mRNA cancer vaccine (intismeran autogene) with the immunotherapy drug Keytruda (pembrolizumab), for patients with completely resected stage IIB–IV melanoma.

Melanoma recurrence risk is high even after complete surgical removal because microscopic cancer cells can remain undetected; stage III melanoma alone carries a 40–90% five-year recurrence risk in past studies. The mRNA vaccine works by analyzing DNA mutations unique to a patient's own resected tumor, then creating a personalized mRNA vaccine encoding those mutations — described by one oncologist as a "custom-built wanted poster" for the immune system. Once injected, the patient's cells produce these "flags," training the immune system to hunt down and destroy remaining microscopic cancer cells as long-term surveillance, potentially preventing recurrence. Pembrolizumab works alongside this by blocking the mechanism cancer cells use to hide from immune detection.

After 5-year follow-up, participants receiving the combination therapy had a 49% reduction in risk of recurrence or death, and a 59% lower risk of distant metastasis or death, compared with pembrolizumab alone. Side effects were reported as similar to pembrolizumab alone.

Melanoma experts interviewed noted this builds on, rather than replaces, existing effective adjuvant treatments like checkpoint inhibitors (pembrolizumab, nivolumab), which already reduce recurrence risk but don't eliminate it for all patients. Outstanding questions include the durability of the effect with longer follow-up, and whether patients who don't benefit have a different tumor mutational profile. Practical hurdles remain: manufacturing a personalized vaccine currently takes roughly six weeks from sample receipt per the company's stated target (with independent estimates of two to four months), so manufacturing turnaround, cost, and insurance coverage will matter for real-world adoption. Experts described the phase 3 result as a major step but not yet standard treatment, pending more detailed longer-term data.
