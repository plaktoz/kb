# Daily multivitamin may support healthy aging, suggests preliminary study

source_url: https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/daily-multivitamin-may-support-healthy-aging-preliminary-study

byline: Peter Morales-Brown (fact checked by Jill Seladi-Schulman, Ph.D.)

date: 2026-07-31

---

A new study suggests that older adults taking a daily multivitamin for three years reported significantly better functional health, including reductions in symptom burden and improvements in overall physical functioning. These benefits may support daily activities and everyday independence, such as walking, climbing stairs, dressing, bathing, and completing household chores. Although cocoa extract supplementation did not significantly improve functional health overall, it was associated with benefits in participants with congestive heart failure. The findings have not yet been published in a peer-reviewed journal and are preliminary; researchers emphasize multivitamins are not a substitute for a healthy diet and regular exercise.

The COSMOS trial (COcoa Supplement Multivitamins Outcomes Study) was a large, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial testing whether a commercially available daily multivitamin (Centrum Silver) or a daily cocoa flavanol supplement could reduce the risk of cancer, cardiovascular disease, and other age-related conditions in older adults. A secondary analysis, presented at NUTRITION 2026 (the annual meeting of the American Society for Nutrition), found that adults 60 and older taking a daily multivitamin reported better functional health than those on placebo.

## Multivitamins linked to better functional health

The analysis covered more than 16,000 COSMOS participants randomly assigned to a daily multivitamin, a cocoa extract supplement, both, or matching placebos. After three years, those taking a daily multivitamin had significantly better functional health scores than placebo, based on the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire, a validated tool measuring symptoms, physical limitations, and quality of life related to cardiovascular health. The largest differences were in symptom burden and the clinical summary score, driven by reductions in fatigue, shortness of breath, and swelling rather than improvements in physical ability alone.

Cardiologist Jennifer Wong, MD (MemorialCare Heart and Vascular Institute), who wasn't involved in the study, said: "Multivitamin-mineral supplementation may help preserve functional health in older adults with heart failure, particularly by reducing symptom burden such as fatigue, edema, and shortness of breath... While these findings are encouraging, additional research is needed to determine whether these benefits translate into improved long-term clinical outcomes."

## Could cocoa extract benefit heart health?

Unlike multivitamins, cocoa extract did not significantly improve functional health scores across the overall study population, though researchers observed a significant benefit among those living with congestive heart failure — a finding Wong called "an encouraging signal" that needs larger, well-designed trials before it could be recommended as routine care.

## What the findings mean

Functional health — the ability to perform routine daily activities independently — is considered an important component of healthy aging. Previous COSMOS analyses have suggested daily multivitamin use may support aspects of cognitive health in older adults, though other evidence on multivitamins remains mixed. Because this analysis hasn't undergone peer review, it's too early to conclude the benefits apply broadly.

Wong noted several potential mechanisms: certain vitamins and minerals may help regulate blood pressure, improve blood vessel function, reduce inflammation, support healthy cholesterol levels, and prevent deficiencies such as anemia — together possibly contributing to better cardiovascular health and energy levels that help preserve physical function. She emphasized that multivitamins should be viewed as a complement, not a substitute, for healthy lifestyle habits: staying physically active, strength and balance training, a heart-healthy diet, adequate sleep, and working with a healthcare provider to manage chronic conditions remain the cornerstones of healthy aging.
