# Thailand faces demand for 1.09 million skilled workers across 10 industries by 2029

Aviation and logistics account for 40.5% of projected demand as Thailand steps up efforts to align education and training with industry needs.

Thailand's 10 target industries are projected to require 1,087,548 highly skilled workers between 2025 and 2029, with aviation and logistics accounting for the largest share.

According to the Thailand Talent Landscape 2025–2029 survey by the Office of the National Higher Education, Science, Research and Innovation Policy Council (NXPO), the country is strengthening education and training to address the growing demand for specialised talent.

The Ministry of Higher Education, Science, Research and Innovation (MHESI) is promoting flexible university curricula, lifelong learning and private-sector participation in practical training to better align workforce skills with industry requirements.

## Aviation and logistics lead talent demand

Aviation and logistics account for 40.5% of projected demand, followed by smart electronics and industrial robotics. Together, the two sectors will require 666,996 skilled workers, representing 61.3% of the total.

The six industries with the highest projected demand are:

- Aviation and logistics: 440,573 workers
- Smart electronics and industrial robotics: 226,423 workers
- Digital industry: 87,568 workers
- Next-generation automotive: 77,652 workers
- Medical services: 71,207 workers
- High-value and wellness tourism: 60,004 workers

The survey identifies engineers, technicians, software developers, healthcare professionals and logistics specialists among the most sought-after occupations.

Dr Surachai Sathitkunarat, president of NXPO, outlined the workforce projections and policy measures on November 19, 2026.

## Universities align courses with industry needs

MHESI's Higher Education Sandbox allows universities to modify programme duration, academic credits and curriculum standards to respond to changing industry requirements.

Chulalongkorn University's Computer Engineering and Digital Technology (CEDT) programme demonstrates this approach. The three-and-a-half-year course works with more than 100 private-sector partners and provides students with practical workplace experience every year.

The initiative aims to bridge the gap between academic learning and employers' skill requirements.

## Training programmes connect talent with jobs

MHESI and NXPO are using the STEMPlus Platform and STEM One-Stop Service to connect educational institutions, employers and learners in science, technology, engineering and mathematics (STEM).

The platforms provide workforce demand information, facilitate course certification and connect trained candidates with employers.

NXPO reported that 603,243 people had completed 2,609 courses delivered by 219 training providers. The initiatives were linked to 16,490 new STEM jobs across 260 organisations, generating approximately 5.6 billion baht in combined annual income.

The New Breed Graduates programme and GenNX also involve employers in curriculum development and skills assessment.

NXPO reported an 86% employment rate among graduates of GenNX's intensive short courses, with incomes increasing two to sixfold within three months.

## Tax incentives support workforce development

The government is encouraging businesses to recruit STEM professionals and invest in employee training through the Thailand Plus Package and the Board of Investment's (BOI) STEM++ initiative.

Under the Thailand Plus Package's published rules for qualifying expenditure during 2023–2025, companies could claim corporate income tax deductions of 1.5 times eligible STEM salary costs and 2.5 times certified training expenses, subject to scheme conditions.

## Lifelong learning addresses workforce challenges

Surachai identified four challenges facing Thailand's economy and labour market: an ageing population, climate change, geopolitical volatility and international competition for skilled workers.

MHESI's lifelong learning policy covers early childhood, formal education, working life and older age. It promotes continuous learning, reskilling and upskilling to help workers adapt to technological and business changes.

The ministry is also developing flexible credit accumulation arrangements, allowing learners to build on previous education and training. More accessible learning finance is another part of the initiative.

## Three initiatives target advanced technology skills

Under its Thailand: Nation of Masters strategy, MHESI is advancing three initiatives to strengthen domestic expertise in deep technology.

- Siam Silica: Focuses on semiconductors and advanced electronics, with targets including eight integrated circuit design businesses and centres and at least one photonics or power-semiconductor fabrication facility by 2030.
- Siam Medica: Develops expertise in advanced medicine and wellness science.
- Siam Terra: Focuses on food technology and precision agriculture to strengthen the agricultural and food industries.

Surachai presented the initiatives at the first Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) Summit on Adult Learning and Education at HSE University in Moscow, Russia, with online participation.

His presentation, titled "Building a Lifelong Learning Ecosystem for a Resilient Future: Higher Education Innovation and Workforce Transformation", outlined the role of education reform, industry partnerships and continuous learning in preparing Thailand's future workforce.
