# Drinking Alcohol Raises Cancer Risk. Is Any Amount Safe for Health?

Three recent studies highlight alcohol's health dangers:

## 1. Doubled Cancer Deaths (The Lancet Regional Health – Americas)

Alcohol-attributable cancer deaths in the US rose from 11,361 in 1990 to 23,126 in 2023. Colorectal cancer led among men ages 20–54; breast cancer led among women in that group.

## 2. Risk Across 20 Health Conditions (Nature Health)

Analyzing 843 studies, researchers found moderate-to-strong evidence linking even low alcohol consumption to cancers of the lip, oral cavity, larynx, pharynx, and colorectum, plus liver disease and pancreatitis. Pharyngeal cancer risk rose by at least 105%.

## 3. Colorectal Cancer & Heavy Drinking (Cancer journal)

Among 88,092 participants over ~14.5 years, those averaging 14+ drinks/week showed a 25% higher colorectal cancer risk and a 95% higher rectal cancer risk versus light drinkers. Former heavy drinkers showed no elevated risk, suggesting cessation helps.

One expert noted: "abstinence is the safest and truest bet" for those asking about safe consumption levels.
