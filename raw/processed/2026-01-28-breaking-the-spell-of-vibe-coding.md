# Breaking the Spell of Vibe Coding

source_url: https://www.fast.ai/posts/2026-01-28-dark-flow/index.html

---

Author: Rachel Thomas
Published: January 28, 2026

Vibe coding — generating large amounts of AI-written code not intended for human review — has captured the tech industry's imagination, but the reality is far messier than promised.

Thomas draws a compelling parallel to gambling addiction. Psychologist Csikszentmihalyi's concept of flow requires skill-matched challenges and clear performance feedback. Vibe coding undermines both. Like multiline slot machines engineered to produce "losses disguised as wins," AI coding tools trigger feelings of productivity that may be illusory.

A key finding comes from a METR study: developers using AI believed they worked 20% faster, yet were actually 19% slower — nearly a 40% gap between perception and reality.

Thomas also warns about overconfident predictions. Geoffrey Hinton predicted AI would replace radiologists by 2021; Dario Amodei predicted AI would write "90% of all code" by late 2025. Neither materialized.

The core concern: people are halting their own skill development based on speculative forecasts. As Jeremy Howard noted, those who outsource all thinking to AI risk stopping "upskilling, learning, and becoming more competent."

Thomas concludes that AI handles syntax but not software engineering — grammar but not genuine thinking. Human creativity and judgment remain essential.

Key themes: vibe coding, AI productivity illusion, skill development, flow state, software engineering, METR study.
