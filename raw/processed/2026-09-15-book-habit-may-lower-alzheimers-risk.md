# Love to Read? Your Book Habit May Lower Alzheimer's Risk by 33%

A review published in *Psychological Medicine* examined reading for pleasure across all age groups, finding associations with improved cognitive health, mental well-being, and reduced dementia risk.

**For children (ages 3–10):** Data from the ABCD Study linked early reading to lasting cognitive and behavioral benefits in adolescence, including better attention, memory, and academic achievement — alongside fewer mental health problems. Brain imaging suggested larger cortical areas tied to language and emotional regulation. However, reading rates among children are declining, particularly among boys ages 11–16.

**For adults:** Even six minutes of reading was associated with stress reduction of up to 68%. Surveys found 74% of adult readers reported positive effects on mental health, and reading ranked as the top stress-relief method, chosen by 38% of respondents.

**For older adults:** Regular reading is associated with slower cognitive decline. Dr. Dung Trinh noted it may help "build cognitive reserve, giving the brain greater resilience as it ages," while cautioning that reading should be seen as "one promising component of a broader brain-healthy lifestyle."

Dr. Alex Dimitriu added that reading strengthens "sustained focus, patience, slowing down, impulse control, and imagination."
