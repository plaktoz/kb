# AI Expert Reveals the Jobs Most Likely to Survive Automation

A Pew Research Center survey (Sept. 17) found majorities in 34 of 37 countries expect AI to reduce jobs over the next two decades. Concern was highest in Australia, South Korea (76%), and the US (71%).

Bill Gates warned in late August that AI could rapidly reshape law, customer service, medicine, software, and manufacturing within a decade, potentially leaving far fewer jobs without proper policy responses.

Alex Svanevik, CEO of blockchain analytics firm Nansen, offers a counterpoint — some roles remain difficult to automate because they require **physical presence**:

- **Healthcare workers** (nurses, physiotherapists, massage therapists): AI can assist with diagnosis and planning, but cannot replace in-room human interaction
- **Education**: AI tutors will spread quickly, but human mentors remain essential — Svanevik envisions roughly *one mentor per five students* rather than one teacher per 25
- **Live entertainment** (theater, concerts, sports): robotic performers may emerge but are more likely to be *additive* than replacements — *"I don't think we're gonna see robot opera singers become very popular"*

His core argument: AI replaces tasks, not human presence.
