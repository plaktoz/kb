# Creating Pathways to Employment: New Vocational Training Units Open in the West Bank

UNESCO, in cooperation with the Ministry of Education and Higher Education and Palestine Technical University College - Kadoorie, has equipped and operationalized two vocational training units in the West Bank, expanding opportunities for young people to acquire practical skills and prepare for future employment.

Located at Deir Al-Ghusun School in Tulkarm and Jenin Secondary School for Girls in Jenin, the new units, whose establishment was made possible thanks to a generous support of the Government and People of Japan, specialize in beautification and fashion design. Together, they can accommodate up to 80 students in grades 11 and 12, providing hands-on training alongside formal education. The units were established under the UNESCO project *Supporting Livelihoods and Economic Recovery through Accelerated Skills Training for Palestinian Youth*, which aims to expand access to vocational training, strengthen resilience and wellbeing, and improve employment and livelihood opportunities for Palestinian youth.

The initiative represents an important step in strengthening technical and vocational education and training (TVET) opportunities in the West Bank. Through specialized learning environments and practical training, students can develop skills in fields with potential for employment and self-employment, while gaining greater awareness of future career pathways.

In addition to equipping the training units, the project supports the development of learning resources, capacity-building for instructors, and the delivery of practical, work-oriented training programmes. Recognizing the challenges many young people face in accessing education and training, including economic hardship, mobility restrictions and crisis-related disruptions, the project also works to strengthen institutional capacities and broaden access to skills development opportunities, particularly for young women and persons with disabilities.

Alongside its vocational training interventions, UNESCO integrates mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) activities for young people and caregivers in Gaza. The project is also establishing a Technical Learning Space in Gaza to expand access to skills development opportunities for crisis-affected youth aged 15 and above.

By combining practical skills training, strengthened institutional capacities and supportive learning environments, the initiative contributes to UNESCO's broader efforts to promote inclusive skills development, strengthen resilience and expand livelihood opportunities for Palestinian youth across the West Bank and Gaza.
