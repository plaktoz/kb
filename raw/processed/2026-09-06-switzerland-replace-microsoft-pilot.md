# Switzerland's Federal Government is Replacing Microsoft on 3,000 Computers

Switzerland's federal government has launched a pilot program to swap out Microsoft 365 for open-source alternatives on 3,000 workstations — roughly 7% of the federal workforce — with migration targeted for completion by end of 2027.

The initiative follows a proof-of-concept called "PoC BOSS," completed September 3, 2026, in which 172 federal employees tested the **openDesk** suite, a German open-source collaboration platform. Core tasks like document editing and email performed well; large-scale video conferencing still showed limitations.

The CHF 9 million pilot runs openDesk **in parallel** with Microsoft 365 rather than as an outright replacement. Success could trigger expansion to all 54,000 federal workstations.

**Three drivers cited by Prof. Matthias Stürmer (Bern University of Applied Sciences):**
- US cloud law could expose Swiss data to foreign authorities
- Single-vendor dependency creates service continuity risk
- Proprietary licensing costs are rising with no Swiss leverage

Switzerland's military Cyber Command is moving even faster, aiming to fully replace Microsoft 365 with openDesk by **October 2026**.

This effort aligns with the 2024 **EMBAG Law** requiring federal agencies to publish government software as open source, and a December 2025 federal declaration making digital sovereignty a primary policy focus. France and Germany have pursued similar initiatives.

Microsoft, meanwhile, is investing over CHF 325 million in Swiss AI and cloud infrastructure — partly to counter sovereignty concerns.
