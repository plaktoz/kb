# Why Stocks Haven't Tanked Despite Higher Bond Yields: Chart of the Day

With the S&P 500 approximately 2% below its all-time closing high, analysts are examining why elevated bond yields have not caused a steeper market decline.

Jeff Schulze of ClearBridge Investments attributes market resilience to earnings strength, noting S&P 500 profits climbed 52% year-over-year in Q2. He argues markets are "taking their cues from the…strong earnings environment."

Historical data supports continued optimism: when the S&P 500 gains more than 10% through August, it has advanced September–December in 25 of 28 historical instances — an 89% success rate.

The 10-year Treasury yield recently touched its highest point since 2023, reaching 4.93%, while the 30-year hovered near 5.33%. Schulze notes the yield rise stems primarily from higher **real rates** (up 50 basis points since late February), while inflation expectations rose only 15 basis points and term premium increased 17 basis points.

Because the move reflects stronger growth and AI infrastructure investment rather than inflation fears, Schulze argues: "If current yields were signaling a material threat to the economy, stocks would likely be much lower."

Markets were pricing roughly a 90% probability of a 25-basis-point Fed rate hike following an above-expected core inflation reading.
