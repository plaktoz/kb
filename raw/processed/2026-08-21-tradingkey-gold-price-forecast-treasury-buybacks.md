# Gold Price Forecast: US Treasury Expands Long-Bond Buybacks, Gold Benefits via Dual-Path Logic, May Test Interim High of $4,891

Spot gold (XAUUSD) broke through $4,600 on August 21 and was heading toward $4,700 — the first return to this level since May 15 — after the US Treasury unexpectedly expanded its long-term Treasury bond buyback program.

The move isn't Federal Reserve quantitative easing and won't reduce US government debt, but the policy signal reinforced market focus on fiscal pressure and long-end financing costs, creating a "dual-pathway" boost for gold:

1. **If the buyback works**: repurchasing 10–30 year Treasuries improves liquidity and eases upward pressure on yields. Falling long-term (especially real) yields lower the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding gold, and policy intervention may also weaken the dollar, making gold cheaper for non-dollar buyers.
2. **If the buyback's effect is limited or fails**: should long-term yields rise again due to fiscal deficits, debt expansion, and supply-demand imbalances, the market's focus shifts from the yield level itself to what's causing it — concerns over US fiscal sustainability, sovereign credit, and dollar purchasing power — which benefits gold as a hedge against sovereign credit risk.

In short: gold gains from falling opportunity costs when yields fall, and from credit-risk/safe-haven demand when yields rise for fiscal reasons. The Treasury's attempt to "rescue" long bonds instead exposed the market's reliance on official backstopping, reinforcing "fiscal dominance" and dollar-devaluation narratives. A rebound in energy prices lifting rate-hike expectations remains a periodic headwind for gold.

**Technical picture:** gold last traded at $4,610, having broken above the 0.618 Fibonacci retracement ($4,528.85) and its short/medium/long-term moving averages. It's now near the 0.786 Fibonacci level (~$4,688), a resistance-confirmation zone. Holding above the 5-day moving average ($4,503.67) and the $4,528.85 support keeps the bullish structure intact, opening the door toward the prior high of $4,891.54. A drop back below $4,528.85 would call the breakout into question; a further break below the 0.5 Fibonacci level ($4,416.82) and the 10-day moving average ($4,428.73) could turn the rebound into a range-bound pullback.
