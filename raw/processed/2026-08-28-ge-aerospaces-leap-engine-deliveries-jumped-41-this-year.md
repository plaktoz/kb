# GE Aerospace's LEAP Engine Deliveries Jumped 41% This Year. Here's Why Boeing and Airbus Both Need That Number to Keep Climbing.

CFM International — the GE Aerospace/Safran joint venture — produces the LEAP engine, the sole powerplant for Boeing's 737 MAX and one of two options for Airbus's A320neo family. Deliveries rose 41% in H1 2026 versus H1 2025.

## Why This Matters

Both manufacturers carry enormous backlogs:

- Boeing holds ~4,381 737 MAX orders (roughly 7.8 years of production at current rates)
- Airbus has ~7,500 A320neo orders (~9 years of production)

Engine supply is a core bottleneck. Without more LEAP deliveries, neither manufacturer can accelerate production to clear backlogs. There are also cash-flow implications — nearly finished aircraft awaiting engines tie up capital until delivery payment is received.

## Impact on GE Aerospace

The production ramp creates a short-term margin headwind. The industry model involves selling new engines at a loss while earning profits through long-term service agreements and spare-parts sales. As new-engine deliveries outpace aftermarket revenue, near-term profitability faces pressure — but long-term earnings potential grows alongside the expanding installed base.
