# How accurate have Ed Zitron's AI skeptic predictions been?

Dan Luu examines the prediction track record of Ed Zitron, a prominent AI skeptic, finding an extensive pattern of failed forecasts paired with flawed reasoning.

## Financial Claims About "Dying" Tech Companies

Zitron repeatedly argued that Meta, Google, and Microsoft were struggling and turning to AI out of desperation. The actual revenue and profit data show consistent double-digit growth across all three companies through 2026 — directly contradicting his thesis.

## Prediction Record

Luu catalogs roughly two dozen falsified predictions spanning 2024–2025, including:

- Multiple claims that AI had "peaked" and models couldn't improve further (starting February 2024, repeated through late 2025)
- That OpenAI's revenue forecasts were essentially fraudulent — forecasts OpenAI subsequently met or exceeded
- That Google's Gemini hitting 500M users was so implausible that "someone at Google should have been fired" — Gemini reached 750M
- That Cursor was dying and couldn't fetch even $10B — it sold for $60B
- That CoreWeave would collapse within six months — it trades at over double its IPO price

## Pattern of Reasoning

Luu argues Zitron's method involves numerical sleight-of-hand: citations that don't support stated conclusions, cherry-picked third-party metrics over primary data, and hero-villain narratives that oversimplify corporate dynamics. Juho Snellman is quoted observing that following sources "down to the primary source," what they say is "very different from what Zitron is implying."

Timothy B. Lee found spreadsheet errors in Zitron's Anthropic revenue projection, including counting March twice and including February 30th as a date.

## Gish Gallop Dynamic

Luu describes the structural problem critics face: Zitron produces high volumes of loosely connected claims cheaply, while thorough rebuttals are costly to produce. Defenders then argue that unanswered points validate the original claims.

## Comparison to Futurists

Luu compares Zitron to Ray Kurzweil — both deploy numbers for rhetorical credibility while the underlying logic breaks down on scrutiny. He also parallels Paul Ehrlich, who maintained apocalyptic population predictions for decades despite being consistently wrong, eventually saying his language "would be even more apocalyptic today."

## Confidence Calibration

A recurring theme is Zitron's extreme stated confidence on predictions that fail. Luu notes he has "never been wrong about a prediction" with anywhere near that level of certainty, and that a single such failure would indicate severe overconfidence — let alone a long string of them.
