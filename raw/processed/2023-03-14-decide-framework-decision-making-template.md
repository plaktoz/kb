# DECIDE framework & template

The DECIDE framework is a structured, six-step decision-making model for making tough, important decisions transparent, documented, and sequential: Define problem, Evaluate/Establish criteria, Create options, Identify solution, Decide and commit, Engage and execute.

When to use it: DECIDE is deliberately heavyweight and only appropriate for Type 1 (irreversible, "one-way door") decisions — e.g. launching in a new market, changing business model, a major system integration. Most decisions are Type 2 ("two-way doors," easily reversible) and are better handled with faster, lighter-weight decision-making rather than the full DECIDE process.

Benefits: transparency (a declared process reduces frustration around who is/isn't included), thoroughness (forces gathering diverse input and evaluating options against real criteria), and auditability (a written record that can be reviewed later to improve future judgment).

Step details:
- Define Problem: write a summary, context, scope/constraints, success measures (ideally a quantified metric), timeline, and stakeholders. Assign roles using the RAPID accountability framework — Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input, Decide (a single named decider, not a group) — since single-owner accountability increases both decision quality (real ownership) and speed (no need for unanimous buy-in).
- Establish Criteria: set criteria *before* generating options, in four tiers — fundamental principles (1-2 overarching, decision-ending if violated), major criteria (the real differentiators for this decision), minor criteria (noted but not decisive), and risks to avoid. Where possible, quantify impact to force objectivity, while remembering models are only directionally useful (good at "2x" comparisons, not reliable at "5% better").
- Create Options: a decision requires genuine alternatives, including the option of inaction/status quo; brainstorm with criticism deferred to a later session to avoid killing ideas prematurely.
- Identify Solution: the responsible team presents a recommendation and/or the decider privately consults stakeholders; the decider is not bound by the recommendation but should have full input. The final decision and reasoning must be written down to eliminate ambiguity, support internal comms, and create an audit trail.
- Declare Commitment: socialize the decision and get explicit, ideally public, commitment from key stakeholders — even those who disagree should "disagree and commit." Unaddressed dissent often shows up not as explicit blocking but as stakeholders quietly failing to prioritize their part of the execution.
- Engage and Execute: follow through on the committed decision.

The DECIDE model is contrasted with the lighter RAPID framework (used within DECIDE's "Define" step for role assignment) and with "High Velocity Decision Making" techniques recommended for the more common Type 2 decisions.
