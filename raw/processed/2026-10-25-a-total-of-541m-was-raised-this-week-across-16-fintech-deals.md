# A total of $541m was raised this week across 16 FinTech deals

It was a modest week for FinTech deals this week, with a total of $541m raised across 16 deals.

This represented a significant drop from the previous week, which saw $2.8bn raised across 17 deals. However, the lion share of the funding from that week came from the colossal $1.85bn investment secured by Ramp.

Despite the small total funding for the week, more than half was accounted for by just two deals.

The biggest deal of this week was secured by California-based infrastructure and enterprise software company Whop. It closed its Series D round at a $5.1bn valuation, coming just months after a $200m strategic investment from Tether had valued the business at $1.6bn.

Salmon, a philippine banking and lending platform, was the only other FinTech to break the $100m mark this week. The software developer netted $140m as it looks to expand its product line across the country.

The infrastructure and enterprise software was the strongest subsector of the week accounting for six of the 17 deals. These were Whop, Salmon, CapitalOS, Prism Data, ScribeUp and Vrtx.

Following behind were the RegTech and CyberTech sectors, which recorded three deals apiece. The RegTechs were Mural, Pennant and CoComply, while the CyberTechs were Opsin, Ultra Security and Asymptote Labs.

Rounding of the deals were marketplace lending and InsurTech with two deals each. The lending FinTechs were Cognitive Credit and Plinto, and the InsurTechs were Slingshot Insurance and Cachet.

The WealthTech subsector did not secure any deals this week, despite it having a very strong Q3. Research from FinTech Global recently noted that global WealthTech funding surged three-times QoQ in Q3. A total of $2.8bn was raised across 124 deals, compared to $932.2m across 151 deals in Q2 2026.

On the topic of WealthTech, FinTech Global recently spoke to several leaders in the space to get their thoughts on how the space will evolve in the coming years. As we edge closer to 2030, they were asked what the big shifts will be over the coming years, the biggest internal changes and what trends firms are currently underestimating. In terms of location the US continued to hold its crown as the biggest hub for FinTech. It was home to 10 of the 16 deals, including seven of the 10 biggest deals. The US-based FinTechs in this week’s roundup are Whop, Mural, Slingshot Insurance, CapitalOS, Ospin, Prism Data, ScribeUp, Ultra Security, Asymptote Labs and Pennant.

The UK was the only other country to record multiple FinTech deals this week. These were the funding rounds for Cognitive Credit and CoComply.

As for the remaining deals, the other countries represented this week were the Philippines (Salmon), Estonia (Cachet), Saudi Arabia (Vrtx) and Denmark (Plinto).

Despite the US maintaining its dominance in the number of deals, the sizable funding round secured by the Philippines-based Salmon show countries across the world are still building major FinTech players.

Recent research from FinTech Global highlighted this. In Q3 2026, the ten biggest deals of the quarter ranged across seven countries. While the US was the only one with multiple deals in the chart, including the biggest deal of the three months, the rest of the list was fairly diverse. The UK, Singapore, Saudi Arabia, Hong Kong, Indonesia and Italy were the other countries to feature in the top 10.

While on the topic of global FinTech funding during Q3, a total of $27.5bn was raised across 897 deals, representing a 35% increase in funding when compared to Q3 2025. The average deal size during the period was $30.6m, up from $21.2m in Q3 2025.

Here are the 16 FinTech funding rounds covered on FinTech Global this week

## Whop raises $200m Series D at $5.1bn valuation

Whop, a California-based technology firm aiming to give everyone access to a sustainable income, has closed a $200m Series D funding round that values the business at $5.1bn.

Sequoia and Shopify Ventures co-led the investment. Whop plans to use the fresh capital to scale its international presence and step up research and development, with a focus on making payments and advertising markets work far more efficiently. This is the second time Whop has raised money in 2026.

In February, Tether made a $200m strategic investment in the company at a $1.6bn valuation.

Around 25,000 business operators currently use Whop, collectively generating $550m every month. A further 50,000-plus people earn money each month by supplying services to those businesses.

Over the last three months, Whop has rolled out several new products. Business Blueprints allows newcomers to set up a business that is ready to start selling within a few clicks, and more than 21,000 businesses have been launched through it in the six weeks since its debut.

Whop serves business operators ranging from product leads at fast-growing startups to teenage first-time founders, offering them a gamified interface and a broad API that support distribution, company creation and payments. Most firms on the platform target narrow, specialised customer needs rather than mass-market audiences, taking advantage of the internet’s ability to reach niches once considered too small to sustain a business.

## Salmon raises $140m to deepen Philippine lending push

Salmon Group, a Philippine banking and lending platform built on technology, has raised a further $140m (PHP 8.8bn) as it concentrates its ambitions entirely on the Philippine market and prepares to take new credit products nationwide.

The new capital is earmarked for two priorities. First, Salmon plans to expand its existing profitable lending lines, namely the Salmon Credit Line and its in-store lending offer. Second, it will finance a national launch of fresh products, with motorcycle loans and lending to micro, small and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs) leading the pipeline.

The raise follows a period of rapid expansion. Salmon has been operating for under four years and in that time has positioned itself among the quickest-growing technology firms in the country. Its structure combines two regulated entities: Salmon Bank (Rural Bank), which holds a licence from the BSP, and Salmon Finance, a financing company licensed by the SEC.

Customer reach is delivered through the Salmon app, which serves millions of Filipinos. The app has recorded 9 million downloads and carries a 4.8 rating across both the App Store and Google Play. Salmon also reports 99.9% uptime and round-the-clock customer support.

Salmon Finance, which acts as the group’s financing arm, uses an AI-driven credit engine to assess applicants. This allows the company to lend to people with thin credit files and return decisions in a matter of minutes, a capability central to its strategy of widening access to credit.

## Cognitive Credit lands $40m as AI reshapes credit research

Cognitive Credit, a provider of corporate credit data, analytics and AI tools, has closed a $40m strategic growth investment as institutional investors push for verifiable data to underpin AI-driven credit analysis.

Summit Partners led the round, and existing backer ETFS Capital also took part. Chris Bon, a partner at Summit Partners, has taken a seat on the Cognitive Credit board of directors. The capital will fund the company’s commercial push in the US and the development of its next product generation.

Two launches sit at the centre of that roadmap. DealConnect, due imminently, will let users pull detailed credit models on demand for private issuers whose reporting is shared through secure data rooms.

The company is also preparing to deploy agentic credit analysis, which layers advanced AI over its verified data to automate in-depth fundamental research. Cognitive Credit plans to keep extending its issuer coverage into additional credit markets.

The firm operates a web application that supports analysts at each stage of credit research, pairing verified data with analytics and generative AI built for the credit domain. Its proprietary dataset, derived from the analysis of hundreds of thousands of financial reports, gives users audit-grade credit models and primary reporting libraries for more than 3,500 corporate bond and loan issuers across the US, Europe and emerging markets.

## Mural raises $28m to power FinTechs and agentic finance

Mural, a compliant system of record that FinTechs and platforms use to move, store and grow customer money, has brought its total funding to $28m after closing a fresh Series A round.

The $15m Series A was led by Tether, with North Island Ventures and the firm’s existing backers also taking part.

The company plans to put the capital towards strengthening its infrastructure for moving, holding and growing money, growing its team, widening its licensing and compliance coverage, and bringing additional markets onto what it describes as the Global Financial Internet.

The business was born out of a challenge its founders, Sinclair Toffa and Chris Fernandes, encountered while working as engineers at Palantir, where they connected fragmented systems for Fortune 100 firms. Mural says many global companies rely on a patchwork of financial tools, banking partners and compliance providers to deliver financial products, with no single layer tying them together. The company presents itself as a reimagining of that integration work, built specifically for the financial sector.

Mural offers a single platform covering the accounts, payments, identity and compliance functions that sit behind its customers’ financial products. It takes responsibility for the licences, banking relationships, and both local and blockchain payment rails needed to operate in multiple markets. Its forward deployed engineers work directly with clients in each market and support them through launch.

## Slingshot Insurance raises $27m for embedded InsurTech

Slingshot Insurance, an AI-native insurer focused on the embedded economy, has launched publicly after operating in stealth, revealing that it has secured $27m in seed capital.

Rally Ventures and Skyward Group jointly led the oversubscribed round. A number of property and casualty (P&C) insurance veterans also invested, among them Jim Hays, who is set to take a seat on the company’s board of directors.

The raise comes as embedded insurance gains momentum within the wider P&C landscape. According to industry projections cited by the company, embedded policies currently make up around 3% of worldwide P&C premiums, a share that could climb to close to 20% within ten years, growing considerably faster than the overall market.

Slingshot was co-founded by Rally Ventures and Ethan Genteman, who previously served as chief actuarial officer at Palomar Holdings. The business aims to support those operating in the embedded space, such as vertical software platforms, managing general agents (MGAs) and insurance distributors, by giving them the technology, capacity and pace needed to bring new products to market and grow them. Target areas include fast-moving niches like amateur sports, travel and the gig and independent-work economy.

The company’s platform is built specifically for the demands of embedded insurance, where large volumes of products generate significant transactional and data complexity. Rather than relying on spreadsheets, email chains and disjointed legacy systems, Slingshot offers one unified system that applies AI to automate intricate workflows, while APIs link it directly to MGAs, distribution partners and capital providers. Each decision made on the platform can be audited, reconciled and explained, offering greater visibility, speed and insight to MGAs and those supplying capital.

## CapitalOS bags $21.9m to turn software into finance teams

CapitalOS has secured $21.9m in Series A funding, a move that could reshape how B2B platforms compete for the businesses that rely on them.

LGVP led the round, with new backing from WEX Venture Capital, Group11, Great North Ventures and Night Capital. Existing investors also took part. CapitalOS plans to use the capital to substantially expand the infrastructure it has built. The raise follows an 18-month period in which volume processed through the platform rose more than twentyfold.

CapitalOS gives software companies the tools to roll out financial products within weeks or even days. It absorbs the work of compliance, risk management and underwriting, sparing platforms from recruiting specialists, building dedicated engineering teams or maintaining infrastructure for years. Its offering rests on four components. Bills handles supplier payments by assessing terms, payment options and timing.

Cards provides issuing with built-in spending limits and captures context at the point of purchase. Wallets holds business cash, moving idle funds into treasury for yield and ring-fencing reserves for payroll, tax and growth. Receivables manages invoicing, chases late payments and reconciles settled amounts.

Looking forward, the company expects platforms to differentiate by offering financial operations built around the industries they serve, giving smaller firms access to capabilities once limited to large enterprises.

## Opsin raises $21m as AI agents outpace legacy security

Opsin, a security company focused on governing enterprise AI agents, has secured $21m in a funding round co-led by SYN Ventures and Race Capital.

The company will put the capital towards accelerating product development, extending its reach among enterprises in regulated sectors, broadening its risk protection layer as agents gain more autonomy, and recruiting additional staff. Opsin views the investment as evidence that the market is moving towards a new security model designed specifically for agents.

According to Opsin, traditional enterprise defences were designed to watch endpoints, networks and the exchange of prompts and responses. Agents, by contrast, operate server-side, plug straight into corporate data and can take action on their own. Existing tools cannot assess whether an agent’s conduct remains consistent with the job it was built to do.

To address this, Opsin created a standardised agent model that brings consistency to how agents are classified across Microsoft 365 Copilot, Copilot Studio, Claude Enterprise, ChatGPT Enterprise and Gemini.

After identifying thousands of projects and recurring patterns of data oversharing, the firm made agent posture, covering connectors, tool calls and sharing scope, alongside runtime behaviour, the core inputs for understanding each agent. Runtime signals include an agent reaching beyond its role or passing sensitive data to users whose roles do not match.

This information feeds the Opsin Risk Graph, which learns continuously from agent activity to spot new threats. If an agent breaches policy, the platform can quarantine it, block the action or alert its owner with remediation guidance.

Opsin originally launched to help enterprises curb data oversharing as AI adoption grew. It has since widened its scope from securing data in motion to overseeing the identities, intents, connectors, tool calls and permissions of agents.

The platform connects through an API, requires no specialist training and produces an initial risk assessment of agents across approved AI platforms within 24 hours. Its customers range from mid-sized firms to Fortune 500 companies in healthcare, financial services, manufacturing and other regulated industries.

Looking three years ahead, Opsin expects agents to interact with one another far more than with people, with much of that exchange never seen by a human as it happens. The company argues this calls for governing agents as organisations govern employees: judging them on their actions and the requirements of their role, rather than restricting their speed.

## Prism Data raises $20m to scale cash flow underwriting

Prism Data, a US-based provider of cash flow underwriting and credit analytics technology, has secured $20m in Series A funding as banks and lenders turn to real-time banking data to judge creditworthiness.

Story Ventures led the round, which also drew backing from FINTOP, Core VC, Obvious Ventures, 1st & Main Growth Partners and Mana Ventures.

With the $5m seed round it disclosed in 2023, the business has now raised more than $25m in total.

Prism plans to use the money to keep investing in its technology, proprietary AI infrastructure and data science teams. That work includes building a new generation of its CashScore models. The company also intends to grow in consumer and small business lending, strengthen its position in the US and Canada, and eventually take its products to international markets.

Alongside the raise, Prism has launched REFRACT, an AI-driven engine for categorising transactions. It was trained on the company’s own dataset of more than 30 billion bank transactions.

Conventional tools label transactions using merchant names or keywords. REFRACT instead uses separate consumer and commercial models to work out what each payment is actually for. Drawing on contextual signals, it can tell apart activity that looks the same on a statement, such as earnings versus borrowed money, refunds versus income, and purchases versus transfers, even when one payment platform is involved in all of them.

According to the firm, benchmark tests show double-digit percentage improvements in accuracy over its earlier models. The engine can process data from any open banking aggregator, from banks’ own deposit accounts, and from PDF statements in the US and Canada. It also monitors for new kinds of transactions and counterparties, including those linked to newer products such as prediction markets.

Prism’s growth has been rapid. Lender requests to its API have risen more than 100-fold over two years, an average compounded increase of more than 20% each month.

## ScribeUp raises $13m as banks fight to own the bill layer

ScribeUp, an AI-driven provider of embedded recurring bill tools for financial institutions, has secured $13m in a Series A round as lenders face mounting pressure to keep control of everyday consumer spending.

Curql led the round, joined by Mucker Capital and Service Ventures. The financing combines $10m of equity with a $3m venture debt facility.

The platform sits inside a bank’s existing digital channels and pairs data on recurring payments with wider account context, such as balances, cash flow, the timing of payments and the institution’s own product range.

Rather than simply warning customers that a payment is approaching, it lets them act. A user can cancel a subscription they no longer use, switch the card or account linked to a biller, draw on an advance or move an outside loan to a new lender. Its agentic technology allows any supported biller action to be completed on any monitored bill without leaving the banking app.

ScribeUp counts over 60 banks, credit unions, FinTechs and neobanks among its clients and already connects to leading digital banking platforms. Institutions can roll out the service by SDK, iframe or API, which spares them from developing the underlying systems in-house.

The new capital will fund further work on AI-native intelligence and agentic execution, alongside hiring and go-to-market activity.

## Ultra Security raises $10m as AI agents outrun defences

Ultra Security, a developer of security tooling for autonomous AI agents, has emerged from stealth with $10m in seed capital, betting that firms can no longer afford to discover what their agents did only after the damage is done.

The round drew backing from Ludlow Ventures, Hidden Capital, Entrée Capital, Detroit Venture Partners, firedrop and FDVC. Angel investors drawn from the security and SaaS sectors also took part.

The company positions its platform as a checkpoint between AI agents and the MCP servers and connectors those agents rely on. Each prompt, data retrieval and tool invocation passes through Ultra, giving organisations live oversight and control of agent behaviour rather than a retrospective view.

Ultra argues that the timing is pressing. Agents have evolved from simple chatbots into software with genuine access to corporate systems, including email and code, and MCP is fast becoming the default route through which they reach tools, APIs and sensitive data. According to the firm, adoption is outpacing the monitoring tools designed to keep watch.

The platform combines four capabilities. Its guardrails convert plain-language policies into real-time enforcement, blocking credential access, redacting PII, protecting PHI, limiting abuse and preventing data moving between connectors, with rules applicable to an entire organisation, a team or one device.

## Asymptote Labs raises $5.5m to tame unchecked AI agents

Asymptote Labs, a developer of runtime infrastructure for AI agents, has secured $5.5m in an oversubscribed seed round, betting that companies deploying autonomous agents at scale will soon need a single system of record to monitor and control them.

Tower Research Ventures, the venture arm of trading firm Tower Research Capital, led the investment. Lytical Ventures, 1984 Ventures and South Park Commons also took part. The startup plans to direct the capital towards engineering, product development, recruitment and its commercial push.

The raise comes as enterprises roll out growing numbers of AI agents across staff workstations, cloud environments and business processes.

These agents can reach sensitive data, alter code and trigger actions within critical systems, yet security and engineering teams often have little insight into how they behave or how to contain the risks they introduce.

Central to the company’s offering is Beacon, an open-source telemetry layer released under the MIT licence. It records agent activity across Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and over 20 further agent harnesses, converting that activity into a standard runtime data model built on top of the OpenTelemetry standard.

Organisations gain a consolidated view of every agent while retaining ownership of the underlying data. Developers also benefit, as insights from earlier coding sessions persist instead of being lost when a session closes.

## Pennant secures $5m seed to modernise proxy voting with AI

Pennant, an AI-native platform designed to support corporate governance decisions, has closed a $5m seed round to develop what it describes as a world model for the governance of public companies.

Urban Innovation Fund led the investment, with Y Combinator also taking part. A number of strategic angel backers joined the round, among them people currently or previously connected with Marvell Technology, Legal & General, OpenAI, Kirkland & Ellis and NVIDIA.

The startup argues that while institutional investors control trillions of dollars of capital in public markets, and companies face mounting demands around governance and shareholder relations, the systems linking these groups have lagged behind. According to Pennant, research, voting and engagement still rely on scattered data, manual processes and a small number of established advisory firms, meaning governance tooling has failed to grow alongside the scale of ownership.

Proxy voting is the first area Pennant is tackling. Its platform brings together an institution’s own research, voting guidelines and historical decisions, allowing investors to apply independent judgement across large volumes of votes. Over time, the system builds up an institutional memory, becoming more effective with each vote cast and each engagement carried out.

The firm already counts US pension funds, institutional asset managers and prominent governance and legal advisers among its customers. Prior to entering Y Combinator, Pennant was chosen for the Palantir Startup Fellowship.

## Vrtx bags $3.6m to rebuild Saudi embedded finance rails

Vrtx, a Saudi-based embedded finance infrastructure provider, has closed a $3.6m pre-seed round as it seeks to remove the heavy build burden facing companies that want to offer financial services in the Kingdom.

SCV by Standard Chartered led the list of backers, joined by Sadu Capital, BYLD, Suhail Ventures, F6 Ventures, TAZ Investment and Palm Ventures.

The company has also secured In-Principle Approval (IPA) from the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) for an Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence. This regulatory milestone moves Vrtx closer to delivering its services within Saudi Arabia’s supervisory framework.

Launching products there exposed the founders to a recurring obstacle: even basic financial offerings demand that technology, operations, compliance and security all be developed simultaneously. They set up Vrtx to ease that burden.

The firm combines the infrastructure and operational tooling businesses need to embed financial services, covering wallets, payouts, card issuance, transfers and transaction processing. Rather than forcing firms to construct systems, link up payment flows and run operations independently, Vrtx wants to offer a faster, less complicated route to going live.

Its platform includes a secure sandbox, step-by-step workflows and an operational layer designed to scale. Vrtx hopes this combination will shorten the journey from concept to launch while giving clients tighter oversight of balances, transactions and financial rules, without burdening them with excess technical or operational work.

## Plinto secures €2.9m ($3.25m) to drive real-time finance with AI

Plinto, a FinTech company focused on bringing real-time finance to businesses, has closed a €2.9m pre-seed round backed by People Ventures and Emblem.

The two investors joined the round on the basis of a shared belief in where financial controlling is heading. Plinto argues that oversight of company finances is moving away from being the responsibility of a single dedicated team and towards a distributed model spanning the whole organisation, with agents coordinating the process.

The new capital will be put towards speeding up product development and entering new markets. Plinto said the investment is intended to help a wider range of companies tap into the potential of AI and transition towards finance that operates in real time.

People Ventures is an operationally focused early-stage fund headquartered in Copenhagen with a Nordic remit. It supports founders hands-on across areas including finance, sales and go-to-market strategy, and counts Pandektes, Hosted.ai and After Technologies among its portfolio companies.

Plinto was founded to address a problem in finance that the company says remained unresolved for decades because the available technology was not up to the task. Its platform now reviews thousands of transactions each day and takes action on them. The firm’s stated ambition is for finance to keep pace with the business itself, operating in real time.

## CoComply raises £1.9m ($2.5m) to expose hidden workforce risk

CoComply, a UK RegTech scale-up focused on external workforce compliance, has secured £1.9m to speed up product development and expand its presence both at home and abroad.

NPIF – Mercia Equity Finance led the round. The fund is run by Mercia Ventures within the Northern Powerhouse Investment Fund II (NPIF II). UK venture capital firm Haatch, which pairs capital with hands-on support for founders, also took part, alongside management and other investors.

Those backers include Richard Law, the former CEO of GBG and a technology investor with a portfolio that features Vypr, who will take on the role of chair. They also include Seb Timpson, co-founder of With Intelligence, a financial data and intelligence firm that S&P Global bought for $1.8bn in November 2025, and Stephen Cann, CEO of Bollin Group, owner of brands such as Mountain Equipment, Sprayway, Ronhill and Bridgedale.

CoComply serves mid-market and large organisations. Its platform surfaces external workers, including those concealed within supplier contracts, and maps how each person is engaged. It then sets out which compliance checks apply. The AI-native system pairs automation with specialist classification expertise and operates at the moment work first arrives in a business.

From there, it helps teams define the work required, choose a suitable engagement route, classify workers accurately and settle the approvals needed. The emphasis is on prevention. The goal is to catch exposure around worker classification and IR35, Right to Work and modern slavery before it becomes a liability.

The new capital will fund product work and a larger sales, marketing and delivery operation. This supports the company’s long-term aim of categorising and classifying the global workforce so that compliance holds everywhere.

## Cachet raises funding to expand adaptive insurance platform

Cachet, a Tallinn-based InsurTech broker specialising in insurance for Europe’s platform economy, has raised fresh capital led by Nordic investor Spintop Ventures, bringing its total funding to around €11m as it looks to expand its adaptive insurance offering.

Spintop Ventures led the round, with existing investors Truffle Capital and Icebreaker.vc also participating. Cachet will use the funding to develop its products and expand its sales team, while adapting its offering to new forms of platform-based work, including employer-of-record arrangements. The company is also preparing its platform for the arrival of autonomous vehicles on European roads.

The investment comes as ride-sharing, micromobility and gig economy platforms increasingly require insurance that reflects how their businesses operate. Traditional policies are often priced annually and arranged separately in each country, despite exposure changing with individual journeys and vehicle use.

Cachet provides insurance tailored to how people work and vehicles are used, integrating cover into platform operators’ systems and managing policy administration and claims. Established insurers continue to underwrite the risks.

The company has more than doubled in size year on year and recorded its first full year of positive EBITDA in 2025. It operates across multiple segments in the 16 European countries where it is licensed, with partners including Bolt, Voi, Ryde and Turo. Since launching, Cachet has covered close to 2bn minutes of mobility, with more than 310,000 assets on its platform.
