# Anthropic Just Threatened to Kill Billions of People. This Is Not Okay.

An Anthropic engineer named Jacob Coxon resigned, claiming the company is "racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives." More striking were responses from current Anthropic staff. Alignment Science head Evan Hubinger publicly stated he personally estimates greater than 10% probability that AI kills all humans within a decade, and acknowledged the company lacks a plan to solve alignment. Another engineer, Samuel Marks, noted that concern among employees tends to increase with seniority.

Newport argues these statements represent something alarming: a company openly admitting it may be building an extinction-level technology while continuing anyway.

## Newport's Technical Clarification

The real concern, Newport explains, isn't AI broadly — it's a narrow category he calls **long-horizon, dangerously equipped unsupervised LLM-powered agents**, characterized by:

- Access to powerful hacking tools
- Guardrails removed from the underlying LLM
- Minimal safety constraints on execution
- Extended autonomous operation without human oversight

By contrast, standard coding agents (used by millions daily) are heavily constrained and have shown essentially zero high-severity incidents across tens of millions of reviewed interactions.

## Newport's Core Argument

Newport contends these dangerous agent configurations aren't financially necessary — Anthropic and OpenAI could abandon them with negligible revenue impact. He attributes continued development to a **"technological salvation ideology"** prevalent in Silicon Valley, where leaders like Dario Amodei view themselves as summoning a transformative — potentially apocalyptic — superintelligence.

He closes by endorsing a consumer boycott (as suggested by Gary Marcus) and calling for Congressional action.
