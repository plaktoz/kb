# New Linux Foundation Research at Web Summit: 72% of Commercial Open Source Companies Report Net Positive AI Impact; Brand and Trust, Not Code, Ranks as Top Competitive Advantage

Second annual study of 225 founders and enterprise leaders reveals how companies combine revenue models and enterprise sales to commercialize open source

## Summary

- The Linux Foundation's State of Commercial Open Source 2026 report surveys commercial open source software (COSS) decision makers directly to better understand the strategic playbook for commercial open source companies and how they are adapting to new ecosystem developments.
- AI is a net positive: 72% of respondents report a net positive impact from AI, and nearly twice as many companies opened up their core product in response than closed them.
- Trust comes first: 57% name brand recognition and trust as their top competitive advantage, and most monetize it through two or more revenue models.
- Developers adopt, executives buy: companies layer enterprise sales on top of product-led growth as they scale, and building that capability is the top scaling challenge (47%).

LISBON, Nov. 10, 2026 – Web Summit 2026 – The Linux Foundation, the nonprofit organization enabling mass innovation through open source, today released The State of Commercial Open Source 2026, a study examining how commercial open source companies generate revenue, convert users into customers and build their businesses.

AI now writes code almost for free, and that raises a hard question for the whole software industry: if code costs next to nothing, where does the value of software lie? Commercial open source companies have been answering that question for more than 30 years. They have built large businesses on code they give away, which makes them a good guide to how software will make money in the age of AI.

Published by Linux Foundation Research in partnership with Serena, with sponsorship from AWS, Red Hat, Futurewei and CNCF, the second annual report draws on a survey of 225 commercial open source founders and enterprise leaders. Revealing findings on operating decisions across pricing, monetization, licensing, go-to-market strategies, conversion economics and AI, the report provides benchmarks for founders, investors and business leaders.

Matthieu Lavergne, Partner at Serena and co-author of the report, will present its key findings at Web Summit on Nov. 10, from 2:40 to 3:00 PM on Stage 5 (Venture Summit), and share how open source companies make money in the age of AI.

## AI is a net positive, and it's pushing companies to open up

Seventy-two percent of respondents report a net positive impact from AI, against 8% negative. So far, the gains show up mostly inside companies. Internal productivity is the most cited benefit (73%), ahead of market expansion (46%) and new monetization opportunities (39%). AI roles are the only hiring category growing on net. COSS strategy is mostly unchanged by AI. But when companies do adapt, they are nearly twice as likely to open more of their core product rather than become more closed.

## Brands sell trust, and charge for it in layers

When asked what sets them apart, 57% of companies name brand recognition and trust, well ahead of enterprise features, integrations and community size. They turn that trust into revenue through support and services (63%), managed cloud (49%) and open core (43%). Most combine these: 65% run two or more revenue models. Companies stack models as they grow, rather than switching between them: managed cloud rises from 35% of companies below $5 million ARR to 66% above $20 million. Venture-backed companies lean toward open core (53% vs. 38% for others).

## Developers adopt, executives buy, and sales becomes the bottleneck

Open source companies sell to two personas: practitioners use the product and executives buy it. CTOs or VPs of Engineering approve purchases in 46% of cases. That split holds at every revenue stage. Consequently, companies layer go-to-market motions as they grow: 57% of late stage companies run at least two (vs. 40% compared to early stage peers). Three quarters of late-stage companies run enterprise or channel sales. Building a mature enterprise sales capability is the most cited scaling challenge and the top priority for the next two years. The payoff is an efficient funnel: among companies that track it, 4 out of 5 recover their customer acquisition cost within 12 months.

"Commercial open source has matured into a sophisticated business model, but until now, founders have had limited empirical guidance on the strategic decisions that drive success," said Hilary Carter, SVP of research at the Linux Foundation. "This research moves beyond demonstrating the economic value of commercial open source to showing how companies actually build sustainable businesses. As AI creates new opportunities across the ecosystem, these insights provide a valuable foundation for open source entrepreneurs."
