# Estrogen-only hormone therapy tied to lower dementia risk

The article reports on a study published in *Neurology* on Sept. 8, 2026, suggesting menopausal women using estrogen-only hormone therapy may have a reduced risk of dementia or Alzheimer's-related brain changes.

Researchers examined two databases totaling over 20,000 women aged 50 and older. Estrogen-only therapy is advised only for women without a uterus, since progesterone is normally added to protect the uterine lining in women who still have one.

One database (2,959 women) involved post-mortem brain examinations for Alzheimer's markers, including amyloid protein; estrogen-only users were 35% less likely to show Alzheimer's-related brain changes than non-users. The second database (2,058 living women) involved cognitive testing and brain imaging; users showed 39% lower odds of a dementia diagnosis and performed better on memory tests.

The piece stresses limitations: the study was observational and cannot prove causation, did not include combination estrogen-progesterone therapy or vaginal estrogen users, and lacked data on treatment timing or duration. It concludes that while the findings are reassuring, they are not grounds to begin hormone therapy.
