# A Maryland Tech Company Just Launched a $200 Million Fund to Invest in AI Startups and Data Center Tech

Ciena plans to invest in startups in areas like AI networking and infrastructure supporting the data center boom.

A wide variety of companies are now looking to back AI startups. A Maryland-based tech company announced the launch of a new fund to invest in early-stage companies and tech-focused venture funds focusing on AI-driven networking and connectivity.

Ciena (NYSE: CIEN), a Howard County, Maryland-based networking, services, and software company, said it has committed $200 million initially to its investment arm, Ciena Ventures. (Ciena, whose stock hit a high in May but has since fallen, recently reported $1.67 billion in fiscal Q3 revenue, up 37 percent year over year.)

According to the company, Ciena Ventures will be led by the company's vice president of corporate development, Loai Louis. It says funds will go toward technologies that accelerate data center architectures, advance network and connectivity, and expand opportunities in related areas like computing, materials, and emerging communications technologies.

"As technologies such as AI reshape the networking landscape, Ciena Ventures provides another vehicle for executing on our long-term strategy and capital allocation priorities," said David Rothenstein, executive vice president and chief strategy officer of Ciena, in a press release.

The new fund comes as tech companies are ramping up AI spending, and AI startups are receiving a hefty slice of the venture capital pie. Last year, 41 percent of the companies that raised VC funding on the platform Carta were AI startups, according to the company. Earlier this year, Nvidia, the world's largest chip-maker, invested $5 billion in an AI startup and recently acquired AI startup Hugging Face for $12.9 billion. According to recent projections by Goldman Sachs, global investment in AI will top $1 trillion this year.

The number of data centers is growing rapidly, too. A Pew Research Center analysis earlier this year found that there are more than 3,000 operational data centers across the country with around 1,500 new data centers in development. According to Data Center Map, there are 58 data centers in various stages of development in Maryland, far behind Virginia (around 674) and Texas (around 537), which have the most.

In 2024, Maryland Governor Wes Moore appeared invested in making Maryland an AI leader. He signed the Critical Infrastructure Streamlining Act in 2024, which he said at the time would "supercharge the data center industry" by easing regulations. But since then, public concerns around data centers environmental strains and safety risks associated with AI have grown.

During a recent appearance on The Root podcast, Moore seemed more wary of the growing demands of data centers. "At least I can tell you in the state of Maryland, data centers do not get to write the rules. They do not get to override local opinion. We have to make sure that the power belongs to the people," he said.

The governor also says he plans to urge lawmakers to repeal a tax exemption for data centers, which was established in 2020. During a Sept. 23 news conference, Moore said, "They need to pay their own way, or they need to go away."
