# How Children Learn Best

Subtitle: Exploring the 2026 Gallup research on student engagement.

Healy opens by describing a conversation with a teacher about how kids learn best, concluding that "the best way for children to learn is when they don't even realize they're learning at all."

She argues that conventional schooling often fails students whose strengths lie outside standardized testing. She cites an example of a visual-artist student labeled a "failed math student," who later became a professional stained-glass artist, and an eighth-grader named Rosie who feels disconnected from Spanish class due to lack of real-world relevance.

As a solution, Healy points to project-based learning (PBL) and experiential learning as ways to increase engagement. She references PBLWorks research linking PBL to higher engagement, and cites the 2026 Gallup survey finding that "nearly half of Generation Z students found experiential learning boosted their engagement." She also describes visiting schools with hands-on programs, like a marine science track and electives such as Lego Engineering.

However, she notes the same Gallup survey found "over 49 percent of Generation Z students...felt that in school they don't get a chance to do what they do best."

She closes by quoting Plato ("All learning has an emotional base") to underscore that emotional connection and curiosity create optimal learning conditions.
