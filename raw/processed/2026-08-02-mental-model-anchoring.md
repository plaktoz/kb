# Mental Model: Anchoring

Anchoring is a cognitive bias where irrelevant numerical information shapes our estimates. First identified by psychologists Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman, it describes how an initial value — even an arbitrary one — pulls subsequent judgments toward it.

## The Basic Mechanism

When estimating, people latch onto an initial number and adjust insufficiently from there, gravitating toward anchor-consistent information while overlooking contradictory data.

## Tversky & Kahneman's Classic Experiment

Participants estimating the UN membership percentage of African nations gave dramatically different answers depending on whether they were first asked if the figure was above or below 35% vs. 65%.

## Why It Works

The selective accessibility account suggests anchors make hypothesis-consistent information more mentally available, rather than anchoring being purely a matter of insufficient adjustment.

## Who Is Affected

Experts and novices alike — though those with greater domain knowledge show somewhat less susceptibility to irrelevant anchors.

## Everyday Examples

- House listing prices in negotiations
- Credit card minimum payments
- Restaurant menu pricing
- First impressions
- School ability-tracking of children

## Investment Implications

As Russell Fuller notes, anchoring can cause investors to "under-react to new information" when prior valuations serve as reference points.
