# Diet Quality vs. Counting Calories: Is One Better For Weight Loss?

A two-year study published in *JAMA Network Open* compared a Mediterranean-style diet adapted for the Southern U.S. (Med-South) against a calorie-focused weight loss program (WeightWatchers). The trial followed 360 North Carolina adults with a BMI of 30+ and average age of 55, many with chronic conditions like diabetes and hypertension.

Both groups achieved nearly identical weight loss after 24 months (~3.4–3.5%). However, the Mediterranean diet group showed significantly greater improvements in diet quality — eating more nuts, whole grains, vegetables, and fruit — and 95% said they'd recommend the program, versus 68% in the control group.

Dietitian Kristin Kirkpatrick emphasized "the importance of quality over quantity," noting that calorie-only approaches may not sustain results long-term.

Stanford's Dr. Christopher Gardner cautioned that "it is possible to eat nothing but junk food and lose weight if the amount of total food consumed does not meet the caloric needs," but added that higher-quality foods support better long-term health outcomes.

A separate 2024 DIETFITS trial reinforced that both dietary adherence *and* diet quality together produce the greatest weight loss — neither factor alone is sufficient.
