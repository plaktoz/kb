# Why is the stock market down today? Surging bond yields, oil prices, and Fed anxiety halt Wall Street's record rally

U.S. equities retreated from their recent all-time highs on Wednesday as a cocktail of rising Treasury yields, soaring crude oil prices, and anticipation surrounding the Federal Reserve's meeting minutes pressured the market.

**U.S. stock indexes fell on Wednesday morning**, giving up some of their recent record gains as **a combination of surging bond yields, rising energy costs, and monetary policy** anxiety pressured Wall Street.

The **Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped roughly 1%**, or more than 500 points, while the **S&P 500 shed between 0.5% and 0.6%**, and the tech-heavy **Nasdaq Composite declined 0.7%**. The pullback comes just one day after bullish earnings estimates and a rally in tech giants like **Nvidia, Apple, and Microsoft pushed the S&P 500 to close above the 7,800 mark** for the first time in history.

Here is **a breakdown of the primary factors** driving the market down today:

## Bond Yields Hit 24-Year Highs

The most significant weight on equities is the surging cost of borrowing. The benchmark **10-year Treasury note yield climbed past 5.35%**, reaching its highest level since April 2002. The 30-year bond yield experienced a similar spike, **gaining more than 8 basis points to hit 5.725%**, its highest mark since May 2002.

Higher yields make borrowing substantially more expensive for both companies and consumers. Furthermore, **when government bonds pay high, guaranteed returns, riskier assets like stocks begin to look less attractive to investors**.

## A Spike in Oil Prices

Energy costs are climbing, reviving fears that inflation could remain stubbornly high. **International benchmark Brent crude futures rose above $101 per barrel**, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude neared the **$90 per barrel mark.**

The spike is being driven by geopolitical and environmental threats. In the Middle East, renewed **Houthi attacks targeting Saudi airports and the critical Strait of Hormuz waterway have kept the risk of large-scale crude supply disruptions high**. Domestically, a tropical storm nearing the **U.S. Gulf Coast is threatening to make landfall as a hurricane**, prompting major producers like **Chevron** to evacuate nonessential personnel from offshore platforms.

## Waiting on the Federal Reserve

Investors are acting cautiously ahead of **the 2 p.m. ET release of the Federal Reserve's September meeting minutes**. During that meeting, **policymakers opted to raise interest rates to combat inflation.**

Traders are scrutinizing the minutes for clues regarding the central bank's future trajectory. Markets are attempting to gauge whether the September rate hike was a one-off adjustment or a signal that more rate hikes are on the horizon.

## Normal Profit-Taking

Beyond macroeconomic pressures, **Wednesday's sell-off is also a byproduct of market mechanics**. Because the **S&P 500 and Nasdaq closed at all-time highs the day before**, traders are engaging in standard profit-taking—**selling off some shares to lock in the gains they accumulated during the massive tech-driven rally**.
