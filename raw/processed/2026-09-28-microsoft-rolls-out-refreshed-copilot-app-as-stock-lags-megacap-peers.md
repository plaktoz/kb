# Microsoft Rolls Out Refreshed Copilot App as Stock Lags Megacap Peers

Microsoft launched a redesigned Copilot app that merges its previously separate office-productivity and coding tools into a single platform, an idea CEO Satya Nadella had floated earlier in the year as a Copilot "super app." The new app has three components: "Code" for simple programming by non-technical users, "Cowork" for handling complex, multistep tasks, and "Autopilot" for building custom agents usable in Teams and Outlook.

Pricing is shifting as well — while Microsoft 365 Copilot remains priced at $30 per user per month, the new Cowork, Code, and Autopilot tools will instead be billed according to actual customer usage.

Copilot adoption has been modest so far, with fewer than 7% of Microsoft's over 450 million commercial Office 365 seats paying for the AI add-on, even as competitors like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta (with its Muse app) gain traction quickly. One unnamed Microsoft executive reportedly conceded that folding coding into the app represents the company playing catch-up in that space.

Despite robust underlying financials — including 18% revenue growth to $90 billion in the June quarter and 43% Azure growth (with about 45% Azure growth projected next quarter) — Microsoft's stock has risen only 9% year-to-date, trailing the Nasdaq and other megacap technology companies. Investor caution is attributed partly to heavy capital spending, citing $41 billion in capital expenditures last quarter and an anticipated roughly $175 billion for calendar 2026, with investors seeking clearer evidence that AI investments will generate returns.

Copilot's paid seat count topped 30 million last quarter with new seat additions more than doubling quarter-over-quarter, and GitHub Copilot has reached 50 million users. Nadella is quoted describing the combined seat-plus-usage pricing approach as making the market opportunity "much more expansive." Frontier program customers are expected to gain access to the new app in the coming weeks, and the article suggests that successful adoption could help the stock close its performance gap with peers.
