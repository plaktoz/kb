# Chan Zuckerberg Initiative's Learning Commons Launches Open AI Platform for K-12 Edtech

Learning Commons, a nonprofit backed by the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative, announced an open platform for K-12 edtech developers and educators on September 22, 2026, alongside six new partner organizations. The Learning Commons AI platform gives developers free access to state academic standards, curricula and evaluation tools intended to ground AI-generated classroom content in learning science, the organization said in its launch announcement.

Learning Commons was launched in 2025 to build on the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative's decade of work in learning science. Since then, its datasets have been downloaded roughly 20,000 times, according to Sandra Liu Huang, president of Learning Commons. The platform announced this week formalizes access to two core tools, the Knowledge Graph and the Evaluators, alongside a smaller set of "Agent Skills" built for teacher planning tasks.

## What the platform includes

The Knowledge Graph is a structured, machine-readable dataset covering academic standards from all 50 U.S. states, along with learning progressions, common student misconceptions, curricula and "durable skills" definitions such as critical thinking and collaboration. It is published on GitHub under an open license. The Evaluators are research-backed scoring tools that check whether AI-generated educational content is accurate, grade-appropriate, aligned to state standards and instructionally rigorous, covering dimensions including math standard alignment, writing feedback, grade-level appropriateness and text complexity.

A new addition, the Durable Skills Evaluator, assesses how well a tool supports critical thinking. The Knowledge Graph also now includes Eedi's Misconception Graph, an open-source dataset supported by the Gates Foundation that maps common student errors in math, contributed by the math-research company Eedi. Four Agent Skills co-developed with Anthropic — covering lesson plan creation, lesson differentiation, lesson prep and checking for understanding — are already available, with more due later this year.

Learning Commons' platform gives developers three connected components: a standards dataset, content evaluators and reusable teacher-planning workflows.

## New partners and reactions

Six organizations announced new integrations at launch: Canva Education, the Austin-based startup Level, MagicSchool, OKO Labs, Really Great Reading and TalkingPoints. Canva Education is using the Knowledge Graph to align instructional content with state standards; Really Great Reading is building its forthcoming Literacy Outcomes System around the dataset; and TalkingPoints uses it to generate lesson-aligned family activities translated into more than 150 languages. These partnerships add to existing integrations with OpenAI and Anthropic.

"We set out to build open, public infrastructure to ensure AI tools truly reflect the science behind student learning," said Sandra Liu Huang, president of Learning Commons, in the launch announcement. Karl Rectanus, chief executive of Really Great Reading, said in the same release that "shared public resources built on learning science like Knowledge Graph help us move from pockets of excellence to systems of achievement."

The launch has also drawn scrutiny over a philanthropy-backed nonprofit shaping shared AI infrastructure for schools. Helen Crompton, executive director of the Research Institute of Digital Innovation in Learning at Old Dominion University, told EdSurge she is "cautiously optimistic" about the platform but wants transparency in how Learning Commons decides which curricula, research and standards to include, noting that "those decisions are pedagogical decisions" that can shape what millions of teachers and students encounter. Tambra Clark, a technology integration facilitator for Birmingham City Schools in Alabama, told EdSurge that "whoever controls those underlying knowledge graphs and evaluators holds a massive amount of sway over what the system considers a 'valid' approach," while also calling the free, open datasets a time-saver for cash-strapped districts.

The platform launch follows a broader pattern of philanthropic and vendor efforts to bring evidence and evaluation standards to K-12 AI tools, including a nonprofit review that found mixed results for AI learning tools and the Gates Foundation's $1 billion commitment to equitable AI access in education.

## Background

Learning Commons is a nonprofit, private operating foundation backed by the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative, the philanthropic organization founded by Mark Zuckerberg and Priscilla Chan in 2015 to support education, science and community initiatives. Learning Commons launched in 2025 with the goal of building "public infrastructure" that connects proven teaching and learning research to the AI tools schools increasingly use, without relying on student data. Its Knowledge Graph and Evaluators tools were already used in beta by developers and school districts, including Lisle District 202 in Illinois, before the September 22, 2026, platform launch formalized public access and added the six new integration partners. Learning Commons says the launch is "a milestone, not an endpoint," with additional Agent Skills and dataset expansions planned for later this year.
