# $10,000 in Tesla When It Joined the S&P 500 Would Be About $15,700 Today. An Index Fund Would Have Done Better.

When Tesla joined the S&P 500 in December 2020, index funds were forced to buy shares at a record $695 closing price. Nearly six years later, that $10,000 investment grew to roughly $15,700 — while the same money in an S&P 500 index fund would have reached approximately $20,500 (before dividends).

The S&P 500 rose ~105% (~13%/year) over that period; Tesla gained only ~57% (~8%/year) from its split-adjusted entry price of $231.67.

Tesla's business did grow substantially — revenue tripled to over $100 billion, and quarterly deliveries now nearly match all of 2020's output. However, the valuation at entry was extreme: "paying more than 900 times the year's earnings." The P/E has since compressed to ~330x, meaning much of the real business growth simply justified the original premium rather than rewarding investors.

Today, Sparks argues the situation repeats itself — Tesla is investing heavily in autonomy and Robotaxi services, but operating margins have shrunk and free cash flow turned negative. He concludes: "even impressive growth can lose to an index fund when the starting price already reflects it."
