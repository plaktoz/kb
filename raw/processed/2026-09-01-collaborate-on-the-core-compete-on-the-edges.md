# Collaborate on the Core. Compete on the Edges.

The article opens with the Tour de France as a central metaphor. Rival cycling teams temporarily ride together in a peloton, sharing wind resistance benefits while each pursues their own competitive goals. The front rider absorbs the most effort while others conserve energy, positions rotate, and the collective moves faster than any individual could alone.

The core argument is that rival companies should apply similar logic: **collaborate on shared foundational infrastructure ("the core")** while **competing independently on differentiated offerings ("the edges")**. Companies neither win by isolating themselves nor by over-collaborating — the strategic advantage lies in knowing *what* to build together versus *what* to keep proprietary.

Nagle developed this framework during his time as advising chief economist for the Linux Foundation, where rival tech companies routinely co-invest in shared open-source infrastructure while fiercely competing on products built atop it.
