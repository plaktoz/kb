# A Simple Blood Test Catches Polyps, Early-Stage Colorectal Cancer

An experimental blood test called DENEB shows promise for detecting precancerous polyps and early-stage colorectal cancer noninvasively.

In a multicenter study published in *The Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology* (August 10), DENEB detected:
- **92%** of stage I–III colorectal cancers
- **81%** of advanced adenomas (precancerous polyps)

## Key Distinctions

What separates DENEB from existing blood tests is its polyp-detection capability. The currently FDA-approved blood test, Shield, detects only about **13%** of precancerous polyps, compared to DENEB's 81%.

Study co-author Ajay Goel noted that "many blood-based screening tests are very good at finding cancer, but have struggled to detect these precursor lesions."

## Important Context

- DENEB is **not** intended to replace colonoscopy, which remains the gold standard
- More than 1,500 adults across China, Japan, and Spain were tested
- Colorectal cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related death in Americans under 50
- A separate JAMA Network study found screening reduces mortality risk by up to **43%**

Colorectal surgeon Ketan Thanki emphasized the need for "cheaper, more accessible screening tests," calling each new study "progress."
