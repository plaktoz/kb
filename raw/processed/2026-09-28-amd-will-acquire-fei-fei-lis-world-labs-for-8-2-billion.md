# AMD will acquire Fei-Fei Li's World Labs for $8.2 billion

source_url: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/28/amd-will-acquire-fei-fei-lis-world-labs-for-8-2-billion/

---

By Tim Fernholz | September 28, 2026

AMD is acquiring World Labs, a company developing deep learning models focused on interpreting physical reality, in a deal valued at $8.2 billion, per the announcement from both companies.

World Labs framed the merger around the idea that advancing AI needs "close collaboration across model research, systems and compute." AMD noted that studying cutting-edge workloads like World Labs' will influence its future chip designs.

As part of the deal, Fei-Fei Li, World Labs' founder, will become AMD's executive vice president and chief scientist. The two companies had already been working together on inference and training optimization since the prior year, with Li even appearing at AMD's CES event earlier in the year.

Li, a Stanford professor, is known for pioneering work in computer vision, including creating the ImageNet database and related AI competitions. She launched World Labs in 2024 to build models with deeper understanding of physical space, contending that genuine general intelligence needs grounding in physics rather than just text-based reasoning.

In a Substack post about the acquisition, Li described the move as a way to expand her company's research beyond a lab setting, writing that she wants to "accelerate the future" now that there's "tangible proof of the possibilities." She said doing so means "scaling our efforts, widening our reach, and getting closer to the hardware."

The article notes that "world model" is an ambiguous industry term, covering everything from vision-enabled language models to full simulation systems. World Labs' debut product, Marble, is designed for entertainment applications as well as generating training environments for robots.

The acquisition is expected to strengthen AMD's position against Nvidia in building out AI chip ecosystems. Nvidia already offers open-weight world models such as Cosmos, while AMD's public offerings have been limited to text and video models.

The piece emphasizes that world models are considered essential for deploying AI in robotics — including self-driving cars and humanoid robots — since synthetic training data from these models can offset the shortage of real-world data needed by companies like Tesla and Figure.

The deal is anticipated to close by year's end, pending regulatory approval.
