# The AI Data Center Boom Is Colliding With Cities Scarred by Big Industry

Community resistance to AI data center construction has reached Philadelphia, where activists are pushing for a moratorium. City officials have identified two potential sites, one in the Grays Ferry neighborhood — already burdened by a now-closed oil refinery that exploded in 2019.

Shawmar Pitts, managing co-director of environmental justice group Philly Thrive, became an activist after recognizing that illnesses affecting his family and neighbors matched documented health impacts of refinery proximity. Philly Thrive board president Sonya Sanders, whose husband died of rare bone cancer in 2020, connected his death to refinery emissions.

Beyond local history, broader energy concerns drive the opposition. BloombergNEF projects U.S. data centers could consume more natural gas than Germany and Japan combined by 2035 — nearly double earlier estimates. Clean Air Council attorney Annie Fox noted that data centers in Pennsylvania typically include "hundreds of diesel engines that spew air pollution," with backup generators increasingly activated during grid stress events.

Philadelphia's opposition mirrors national trends. Moratoriums have been adopted in Denver, Indianapolis, Asheville, Charlotte, and Reno. New York Governor Hochul signed an executive order halting new large data center permits, stating: *"These data centers...should only be built in places that want them."*

Over 100 people attended the No Data Centers in Philly rally at City Hall on September 14, 2026.
