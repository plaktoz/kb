# Deep Dive: Why AI is booming, but productivity isn't

source_url: https://research.socialcapital.com/p/ai-roi

---

By Chamath Palihapitiya, Sep 25, 2026

The piece opens by referencing a prior claim that "vibe coding was dead" and that ROI-driven analysis of AI was becoming essential. It explains that evaluating AI's return requires looking at cost and value.

On cost, the author cites Ramp's AI Index, noting that the "median company spends $12.50 per employee a month," meaning AI needs to boost productivity by only a small fraction to pay for itself — roughly "three additional productive minutes a week."

On value, the piece uses examples like P&G, where one AI-assisted employee matched a two-person team's output, and MIT research suggesting automation works best when AI steps are grouped for review rather than checked individually.

The author argues humans may be the bottleneck to AI ROI, since freed-up time often gets absorbed by meetings or bureaucratic delays rather than converted into real output. This could explain uneven results: AI may amplify existing advantages rather than distribute gains evenly, citing a 2026 experiment with 515 startups where top performers who reorganized around AI saw revenue reach "1.9x the control group."

The article closes by invoking Bill Gates' 1995 observation that automation magnifies whatever efficiency (or inefficiency) already exists, Elon Musk's five-step process for improving before automating, and Robert Solow's 1987 productivity paradox quote, drawing a historical parallel to how computing-era productivity gains lagged until work was restructured.
