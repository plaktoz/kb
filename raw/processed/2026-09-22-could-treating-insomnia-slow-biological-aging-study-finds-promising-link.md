# Could Treating Insomnia Slow Biological Aging? Study Finds Promising Link

A UCLA Health study published in *The Lancet Healthy Longevity* examined whether cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) could influence biological aging in older adults.

## Study Design

A randomized controlled trial enrolled nearly 100 adults aged 60+ meeting DSM-IV insomnia criteria in Los Angeles. Participants received either CBT-I or a sleep education control. Blood samples were analyzed before the 8-week treatment and over 2-year follow-ups using three epigenetic clocks: DunedinPACE, GrimAge, and PC-PhenoAge.

## Key Findings

- CBT-I recipients were nearly 3 times more likely to achieve insomnia remission than the control group
- CBT-I was associated with a statistically significant slowing of biological aging per the DunedinPACE measure — roughly 2% slower aging per year
- GrimAge and PC-PhenoAge showed less sensitivity over the 2-year window

## Expert Perspective

Lead author Judith Carroll, PhD noted: "treat the insomnia, not just to help you sleep better but also to help protect your health."

Her explanation: disrupted sleep elevates the stress response, driving inflammation and cellular damage. CBT-I helps regulate the nervous system, allowing the body's repair processes to function more effectively.

## Important Caveats

- Small, single-site sample — replication in larger, more diverse populations is needed
- Epigenetic clock changes don't directly confirm longer lifespan or disease prevention
- Sex-based differences and comparisons with medication-based treatments remain unstudied
- The researchers noted growing difficulty securing NIH funding for follow-up work
