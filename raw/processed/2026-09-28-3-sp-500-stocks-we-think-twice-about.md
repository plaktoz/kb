# 3 S&P 500 Stocks We Think Twice About

The piece opens by noting that S&P 500 membership doesn't guarantee investment quality, since some companies "face significant challenges, whether it's stagnating growth, heavy debt, or disruptive new competitors." StockStory highlights three stocks it considers weak, along with better alternatives.

**Dollar General (DG)** — Market cap $27.54 billion. Described as a discount retailer selling household essentials, groceries, and seasonal goods. Concerns raised include limited growth potential due to scale, with the article noting "below-average annual revenue increases of 4% for the last three years." Gross margin is cited as "below its competitors," and return on capital is called "underwhelming" at 8.8%. The stock trades at $124.27 per share (15.3x forward P/E).

**IBM (IBM)** — Market cap $212.5 billion. Framed as a legacy tech giant now focused on hybrid cloud, AI, and consulting services. The article states annual revenue growth "over the last five years was below our standards for the business services sector," with estimated growth for the next year called "soft." EPS growth of 8.6% annually is described as underperforming the sector average. Trading at $225.61 (17.8x forward P/E).

**Equifax (EFX)** — Market cap $17.4 billion. Portrayed as a global data analytics firm with credit records on over 800 million consumers. The article notes rising costs have caused operating margin decline, and EPS growth of "just 1.1% annually" over five years is called sector-underperforming. Trading at $148.44 (16.3x forward P/E).

The article closes with a promotional pitch directing readers to StockStory's list of alternative "market-beating" stocks, citing past picks like Nvidia and Tecnoglass as examples of prior success.
