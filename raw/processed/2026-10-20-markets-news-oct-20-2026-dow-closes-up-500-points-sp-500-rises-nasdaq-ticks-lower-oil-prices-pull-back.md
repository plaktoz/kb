# Markets News, Oct. 20, 2026: Dow Closes Up 500 Points; S&P 500 Rises, Nasdaq Ticks Lower; Oil Prices Pull Back

The Dow Jones Industrial Average and S&P 500 closed higher on a big day of corporate earnings reports, while oil prices retreated on hopes of Middle East diplomacy.

The blue-chip Dow surged 1%, or more than 500 points, on Tuesday, while the benchmark S&P 500 rose 0.3%. The tech-focused Nasdaq Composite ended fractionally lower.

Yesterday, the Nasdaq jumped and the S&P 500 rose, as investors bought a dip in tech stocks. Last week, the Nasdaq had a four-week winning streak snapped, while the S&P 500 logged its fourth negative result in six weeks. The Dow ended Mnday down 0.1% after registering a second straight weekly gain.

The S&P 500 was lower in the opening minutes of trading Tuesday before reports that mediators were trying to bring the U.S. and Iran to discuss an end to the Middle East conflict. U.S. benchmark West Texas Intermediate crude futures, which had risen more than 20% over an eight-session winning streak, declined 2.5% to $101.10 a barrel around 4 p.m. ET. Brent crude futures, the international benchmark, slipped 0.2% to $109.30 after hitting their highest level since mid-May earlier Tuesday.

In post-earnings moves, General Motors (GM) and GE Aerospace (GE) ranked among the biggest S&P 500 decliners, falling roughly 7% and 3.5%, respectively. 3M (MMM) jumped almost 6% to lead the Dow, and defense contractors RTX (RTX) and Northrop Grumman (NOC) rose a respective 0.7% and 4.5%. SpaceX (SPCX) and Netflix (NFLX) declined less than 1% and 2%, respectively, ahead of their after-hours results.

Chip stocks ended lower, with the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) finishing down 0.4% and Roundhill's Memory ETF (DRAM) falling 1.3%. The Roundhill Magnificent Seven ETF (MAGS), which tracks the Magnificent Seven mega-cap tech stocks, ended 0.4% lower. Tesla (TSLA), which reports earnings tomorrow, led the group with a 1.5% advance.

CoreWeave (CRWV) shares, which plunged 25% last week amid concerns about the Nvidia-backed firm's debt, ended up 1.7% after dropping 5.5% early in the session.

The 10-year Treasury yield was below 5.12% in late-afternoon trading, down two basis points from Monday's close. The National Association of Realtors said pending home sales fell 7.3% month-over-month in September, a far bigger decline than the 0.4% expected.

The price of bitcoin was around $82,400, slightly higher over the past 24 hours. The U.S. dollar index, which tracks the value of the greenback against a basket of foreign currencies, slipped 0.3% to 101.73. Gold futures declined 0.5% to $4,140 an ounce.

## Yield On 'The Most Important Financial Asset In The World' Forecast To Stay High Through 2026

Economists expect yields on 10-year treasuries to stay over 5% well into 2027, in a sign that relief from high borrowing costs is nowhere on the horizon.

Out of 64 economists who responded to a poll question by The Wall Street Journal in October, only 17 expect the yield on 10-year treasuries to be under 5% by the end of 2026. More than half believe yields will stay above 5% at least through June 2027, according to survey data released Monday. That's a distinct change from the same survey taken in July, when none of the experts anticipated yields to top 5%.

The new forecast reflects the run-up in yields over the past month to levels not seen since the early 2000s. As of Tuesday, the 10-year Treasury yield was 5.12%, down from its recent peak of 5.3% earlier in the month, but well above its 4.2% to 4.3% range in February.

A sell-off in the global bond market is behind the surge in yields (which are inversely related to bond prices). Yields tend to track investor concerns about inflation, and the surge in fuel prices linked to the ongoing war in Iran has kept inflation stubbornly high.

## Hasbro Stock Soars as Toymaker Lifts Outlook on 'Magic: The Gathering' Success

Strong "Magic: The Gathering" results are aiding Hasbro stock today.

Hasbro (HAS) shares are surging 10% Tuesday after the maker of toys and games reported better-than-expected third-quarter results and raised various full-year projections.

Before the bell, the Pawtucket, R.I.-based company reported adjusted third-quarter earnings of $2.90 per share on revenue that jumped 15% year-over-year to nearly $1.60 billion. Analysts surveyed by Visible Alpha had expected $1.86 and $1.48 billion, respectively. The revenue surge was driven by 31% growth in its Wizards and Digital Gaming unit, including a 34% leap in "Magic: The Gathering" revenue.

As a result, Hasbro raised its fiscal 2026 forecasts for revenue, adjusted operating margin, and adjusted EBITDA, and doubled its share buyback target.

"Our asset-light licensing model lets great partners extend our brands while we focus on what we do best," CEO Chris Cocks said. "With a 'Magic' lineup that keeps building, we expect a strong finish to 2026 and continued growth in 2027."

With today's move higher, one of the biggest in the S&P 500, Hasbro shares have added nearly a third of their value this year.

## Walmart Launches $120 Walmart+ and Sam's Club Bundle—What You Need to Know

Walmart is launching a discounted bundle of the retail giant's two memberships as it looks to emphasize value for inflation-weary consumers.

Walmart (WMT) said Tuesday that it is now offering Walmart+ and Sam's Club Plus together for $120 a year. That's nearly $100 cheaper than subscribing to both separately, the company said in a release.

The bundle includes free delivery from Walmart and Sam's Club above a certain order size and a choice of an ad-supported version of either Peacock or Paramount+, along with gas discounts and 2% back on certain Sam's Club purchases. For customers who may already have both, the company said most would be eligible for a prorated refund by switching to the new plan.

The move comes as Walmart has looked to enhance its value proposition in recent months as consumers' budgets have been pressured by stubborn inflation. The retailer said it cut prices on thousands of items over the summer, and shares have surged this month as investors weary of the AI trade have rotated into more defensive stocks, including retailers. Walmart said the changes to the program shouldn't impact its third-quarter or full-year forecasts.

Walmart shares were little changed in recent trading, leaving them up about 6% for the year. This month's rally has carried the stock back into positive territory for the year, recovering losses suffered in the wake of Walmart's August report.

## 3M Shares Surge After Raised Profit Forecast

The top-performing stock in the Dow Jones Industrial Average today is the maker of Post-it notes and Scotch tape.

3M (MMM) shares are surging 6% to lead the blue-chip index Tuesday after the Saint Paul, Minn.-based company reported better-than-expected earnings and lifted its full-year profit forecast.

Before the bell, 3M reported third-quarter adjusted earnings of $2.50 per share on revenue that increased 1% year-over-year to $6.6 billion. Analysts polled by Visible Alpha had anticipated $2.38 and $6.6 billion, respectively.

3M raised its fiscal 2026 adjusted EPS projection to $9.10 to $9.15 from the prior range of $8.80 to $8.95. The Visible Alpha consensus forecast was for $8.93.

"We delivered another quarter of strong results, including solid sales growth, continued margin expansion, double-digit EPS growth, and robust free cash flow," 3M CEO William Brown said. "Improving productivity through structural transformation and a stronger pipeline of differentiated technologies reinforce our confidence in the full-year outlook and long-term growth and margin potential."

3M shares had entered the day up just 1% on the year.

## Braze Shares Skyrocket as Firm Agrees to Be Acquired by Salesforce for $5.66B

Braze stock entered today down by double digits this year. After the company agreed to be acquired by Salesforce, that is no longer the case.

## Today's Earnings Are an Opportunity for Netflix's 'Unloved Stock' to Win Back Fans

Netflix's next chance to win over investors comes today.

The streaming giant is due to release its third-quarter results after markets close, following a rough stretch for the company that led to big job cuts—and which has seen the stock drop, falling some 20% this year alone.

Netflix (NFLX) is expected to report revenue of $12.88 billion, up 12% year-over-year, along with earnings of 82 cents per share, up from 59 cents a year ago.

The stock has been pressured by investor worries about the company's recent engagement and revenue trends. Last week, Netflix cut 6% of its staff, equivalent to just under 1,000 employees.

The shares slumped to their lowest point in two years after Netflix's last report in July, which included a disappointing revenue forecast. Netflix shares, which have lost more than 40% of their value over the past year, were ticking higher in early trading today.

Wall Street analysts are broadly bullish, with a mean price target above $90; the shares closed Monday near $70. A rise to that target would still leave the shares shy of record highs above $130, though.

But Gabelli Funds Portfolio Manager John Belton in emailed comments Tuesday called Netflix an "unloved stock."

"Investors are concerned about soft engagement trends, decelerating revenue growth, a slower margin expansion outlook, and most recently content executive leadership changes," he wrote.

## General Motors Topped Earnings Estimates. The Stock Is Sinking Anyway

General Motors shares are tumbling after the automaker's latest earnings report.

Shares of GM (GM) shares were down 7% in premarket trading, threatening to drag them back into negative territory for the year. The stock was up 2% for 2026 through yesterday's close.

General Motors said this morning that it earned an adjusted $3.70 per share, growth of more than 30% year-over-year, on revenue of $50.02 billion that rose about 3% from a year ago. Analysts had been expecting adjusted earnings of $3.65 per share, and revenue of $48.69 billion, per Visible Alpha estimates.

The Chevrolet, Cadillac, Buick, and GMC maker narrowed its full-year adjusted EPS forecast to a range of $12.75 to $13.75, compared to a prior range of $12 to $14. The automaker also unveiled a new $20 billion stock buyback plan, after completing a $6 billion program GM announced in January.

JPMorgan analysts called the results an "in-line print with mixed quality," as improved sales volume was offset by lower prices. While the company looks to be heading towards a "better year" in 2027, the analysts said they see investors as being "likely to question conviction for now, given increasing commodity and logistics costs" along with uncertainty on pricing.

"Strong 3Q26 buybacks and a new $20 bn repo program are positives that should provide downside support," JPMorgan analysts wrote, adding that a meaningful change in valuation will likely have to wait until investors see "clear signs" of market share gains, along with software and autonomous driving progress.

## Trump Administration Bans China's DeepSeek From Government Use

The Trump administration has banned DeepSeek, a Chinese AI startup, from being used by federal agencies in the U.S.

The model, which made headlines for its performance being comparable to leading American models despite being trained on older hardware at a lower cost, had a significant impact on AI stocks when it was unveiled in early 2025.

On Monday, the Department of Homeland Security informed federal civilian agencies that they were required to remove DeepSeek from existing IT systems, per CNBC.

The ban is the latest step in a battle over control of AI between the U.S. and China, with the U.S. placing a number of trade restrictions in recent years on parts being exported to China that could be used to train AI models.

## Steel Dynamics Stock Drops After Q3 Profit Miss

Steel Dynamics reported record steel shipments and better-than-expected net sales for its third quarter. Its stock is dropping regardless.

Shares of Steel Dynamics (STLD) dropped more than 5% before the bell Tuesday, a day after the industrial metals company posted Q3 profit that came in below analysts' estimates.

However, Steel Dynamics' profit of $5.26 per share came in below the $5.49 consensus estimate, and its adjusted EBITDA of $1.2 billion and net cash provided by operating activities of $845.8 million also missed expectations.

The company was set to hold its earnings call at 11 a.m. ET Tuesday.

Entering today's session, Steel Dynamics shares were up about 45% since the start of the year.

## The 'No Kids' American Dream Is Still Expensive. Here's How Much It Costs

A couple avoiding the costs of childcare, years of summer camp, and college for two kids saves just under $1 million on the price of the American Dream.

Without kids, the lifetime tab runs $4.37 million, up 3.1% from a year ago, according to an Investopedia analysis. That total covers the milestones that have long defined the American Dream: a home, two cars, retirement, health care, a wedding, pets, and an annual vacation. That matches the 3.1% rise in private-sector wages and salaries over the 12 months through June, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics' Employment Cost Index.

The tab for families with two kids is $5.32 million, about 22% more. The difference covers childcare and other costs of raising two kids through age 17, plus four years at in-state public colleges.

Housing is the largest single cost, with or without kids. The analysis assumes a median-priced single-family home, a 20% down payment, and a 30-year fixed-rate mortgage. Add interest, property taxes, insurance, maintenance, and utilities, and housing totals nearly $1.3 million, or 29% of the child-free total.

## Don't Expect Housing to Get More Affordable Anytime Soon

If high home prices and mortgage rates have kept you from buying a home, you might have to stay on the outside of the housing market for the foreseeable future.

Forecasters see the housing market remaining frozen through 2027, with stagnant home sales, slowing new-home construction, and monthly payments out of reach for many would-be buyers. That's according to separate reports on Monday by economists at Goldman Sachs and Oxford Economics, who expect mortgage rates to stay high to the detriment of buyers, sellers, and builders of homes.

The pair of reports highlights the financial fallout from the war in Iran, tariffs, the AI boom, and other economic factors. Those forces have conspired to push average rates on 30-year fixed mortgages over the 7% mark to their highest in more than three years, with no relief on the horizon.

Surging mortgage rates have added $363 to monthly payments on a new mortgage for a typical home.
