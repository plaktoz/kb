# Cyclosporiasis Cases Approach 16,000 Across 47 States, CDC Says

The CDC has warned of rising cases of cyclosporiasis, a parasitic infection causing watery, often explosive diarrhea. The illness has been reported in at least 47 states (plus DC and Puerto Rico) as of August 17, has sickened thousands, and has been linked to two deaths in Michigan — both individuals had significant underlying health conditions worsened by the infection and dehydration; both illnesses developed before a related lettuce recall.

Cyclosporiasis is caused by the microscopic parasite *Cyclospora*, with symptoms typically starting within 7 days of exposure. The FDA initially linked an outbreak to iceberg lettuce supplied by Taylor Farms de Mexico (used by some Taco Bell locations in KY, IN, MI, OH, and WV) and Taylor Farms issued a voluntary recall, but the FDA later re-reviewed the sample and determined it was a false positive due to the complexity of detecting Cyclospora. The source of the current spread has not been identified.

Michigan has seen the largest cluster by far: from a typical ~50 cases/year, the state has reported 14,277 cases as of August 20, up from just 300 as of July 2. Nationally, ill individuals ranged from ages 1–99 (median 46), with about 57% female; none reported travel in the 14 days before symptom onset. A separate 1,681 lab-confirmed cases were acquired outside the US via travel.

Symptoms beyond watery/explosive diarrhea include abdominal cramping, nausea, bloating, headache, and loss of appetite. Untreated, illness can last weeks in an on-and-off pattern; the major hazard is dehydration, especially dangerous for the very young, very old, chronically ill, or immunocompromised (per Dr. William Schaffner, Vanderbilt University). Treatment typically involves antibiotics.

The parasite spreads via food or water contaminated with feces; it takes 1–2 weeks outside the body to become infectious, so person-to-person transmission is unlikely. It's not fully understood how Cyclospora gets into food and water, but recent US outbreaks have tied to contaminated fresh produce, especially in summer. Prevention: wash hands before/after handling produce, rinse all fruits/vegetables under running water before cutting or eating, scrub firm produce with a clean brush, cut away damaged/bruised areas, and refrigerate cut or cooked produce within 2 hours.
