# Berkshire Hathaway Has a New Top Holding, and It Dominates Its Market

Alphabet (GOOG/GOOGL) has broken into Berkshire Hathaway's top five holdings, displacing Chevron. Under CEO Greg Abel, Berkshire added 24.5 million Alphabet shares in Q2, boosting its stake by 45% to roughly 79 million shares valued at ~$28.2 billion — about 9.41% of the portfolio. Only Apple (22%), American Express (17%), and Coca-Cola (11%) rank higher.

Berkshire first acquired Alphabet shares just a year ago under Warren Buffett, purchasing ~18 million shares. Subsequent additions of 36 million (Q1) and 24.5 million (Q2) followed.

Alphabet posted strong Q2 results — revenue up 24% YoY to $120 billion, operating income up 30% to ~$41 billion — but investors are anxious about surging AI infrastructure spending. Capex guidance was raised to $195–$205 billion, and the company reported its first-ever negative free cash flow quarter of -$5.8 billion since going public.

CFO Anat Ashkenazi acknowledged that "free cash flow will remain under pressure" due to infrastructure investments. The article draws a parallel to Amazon's earlier heavy spending cycle, which ultimately paid off. Despite the recent ~7% monthly decline, Alphabet trades at just 16x earnings — a valuation profile consistent with Berkshire's value-oriented approach.
