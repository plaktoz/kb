# What is First Principles Thinking?

First principles thinking is a method for breaking down complex problems by separating confirmed truths from assumptions, leaving only the essential building blocks of knowledge.

## The Core Idea

Rather than accepting existing solutions, first principles thinking asks you to disassemble a problem to its irreducible elements and rebuild from there. Like breaking a Lego house into individual bricks, you move from "slightly improving what exists" to "building something entirely new."

> "I don't know what's the matter with people: they don't learn by understanding... Their knowledge is so fragile!"
> — Richard Feynman

First principles aren't a fixed checklist — they evolve as knowledge grows and differ by context. A refrigerator engineer might treat thermodynamic laws as foundational; a physicist might dig even deeper.

## Two Key Techniques

### Socratic Questioning
A disciplined process to surface foundational truths:
1. Clarify your thinking and trace its origins
2. Challenge assumptions
3. Seek evidence
4. Consider alternative viewpoints
5. Examine consequences of being wrong
6. Revisit the original questions

### The Five Whys
Children naturally use this approach — repeatedly asking "why" until they reach bedrock understanding. Adults tend to stop early, retreating to "because I said so" when their reasoning runs out.

## Examples in Practice

### Elon Musk & SpaceX
Musk found that rocket materials cost roughly 2% of a typical rocket's price on commodity markets. Rather than accepting that "rockets are expensive," he traced costs to their root and founded SpaceX to rebuild the pricing model from scratch.

> "You boil things down to the most fundamental truths... and then reason up from there. That takes a lot more mental energy."
> — Elon Musk

### BuzzFeed
Jonah Peretti identified wide distribution — not content quality — as the first principle of online success. BuzzFeed optimized for human sharing behavior rather than search engine algorithms.

### Derek Sivers & CD Baby
Sivers reduced business success to one principle: happy customers. By ignoring expensive overhead and focusing entirely on delighting buyers, he grew CD Baby to $4 million in monthly revenue.

## Overcoming Limiting Beliefs

First principles thinking dismantles common mental blocks:

- **"I have a bad memory"** — Evidence suggests humans can store far more than they assume; the real challenge is finding optimal storage strategies.
- **"All good ideas are taken"** — People have claimed this for centuries while new companies keep emerging with novel approaches.
- **"We need to move first"** — The iPhone wasn't first; it was better. Microsoft didn't invent operating systems; it had a superior business model.
- **"It's never been done before"** — This is often a reason *to* pursue something, not avoid it.

## Key Takeaway

> "The man who grasps principles can successfully select his own methods. The man who tries methods, ignoring principles, is sure to have trouble."
> — Harrington Emerson

First principles thinking is uncommon precisely because it demands effort. That difficulty is also what makes it a genuine competitive advantage — it moves thinking from incremental improvement into the realm of real possibility.
