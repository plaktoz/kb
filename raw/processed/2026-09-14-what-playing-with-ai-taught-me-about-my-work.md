# What Playing With AI Taught Me About My Work

Katie Parrott reflects on how AI reignited her childhood love of make-believe. Over Labor Day weekend, she used Astra to transform writing skills from her toolkit *Compound Writing* into illustrated creatures and fictional worlds — including a mossy tortoise named Save, whose "archive drawers" embodied her anxiety about misfiled AI instructions.

The playful detour kept bleeding into her workweek. She also procrastinated a serious essay by asking Astra to reimagine Every's columns as Disney characters — complete with villains, wants, and songs. But that game proved generative: developing character motivations prompted her to ask what *readers* want and need, leading her to add audience-guidance sections to her writing system.

Her key takeaway isn't to justify every detour as productive, but to extract something useful from it. She built a skill called **Is This Anything?** — a prompt that reviews an AI session against your priorities and surfaces up to three possible lessons, distinguishing what emerged organically from what the AI suggested.

Her advice: give the skill a conversation you already have, include your current priorities, and ask *"Is this anything?"* Then carry forward only what genuinely connects to your work — no need to finish everything a session made possible.
