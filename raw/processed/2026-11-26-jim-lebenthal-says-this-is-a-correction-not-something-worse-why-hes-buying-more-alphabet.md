# Jim Lebenthal Says This Is "a Correction, Not Something Worse." Why He's Buying More Alphabet

## Quick Read

Jim Lebenthal bought more Alphabet (GOOGL) after a 14% monthly slide, calling it "a correction, not something worse."

QQQ and SPY fell 11% and 6% in a month yet remain up for the year, so the correction label fits indexes better than Alphabet.

Cloud revenue doubled and powers the shots-on-goal case, but negative free cash flow, heavy borrowing and stock sales are Alphabet's weak spot.

Alphabet (NASDAQ:GOOGL) closed at $273.10 on Nov. 25, down 13.79% in a month. Jim Lebenthal used the drop to buy more. On CNBC's Halftime Report, the show's regular said: "The call is that this is a correction, not something worse. Not the AI bubble popping. And if that's the case, then this is the buying opportunity."

He also argued that Alphabet has many ways to win: "Whether it's the search, the chips, the Gemini model, whether it's YouTube, whether it's Waymo. I've got a lot of shots on goal here, so why not add to it?" Market data and Alphabet's filings test both claims.

## Correction Label Fits the Indexes Better Than Alphabet

A correction usually means a drop of 10% or more. The Invesco QQQ Trust (NASDAQ:QQQ) fell 10.99% over the past month. The SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (NYSEARCA:SPY) fell 6.36%. Both are still up for the year, at 3.19% and 5.1%. That looks like a correction.

Alphabet's own decline runs deeper. The stock is down 12.58% year to date and 15.34% over the past year. It also sits about 21.5% below the $348.10 it traded at when it filed its second-quarter results on July 22. Investors are marking down Alphabet more than the market.

## Shots on Goal Show Up in the Filings

Third-quarter revenue rose 24.6% to $127.58 billion. Google Cloud revenue rose 103% to $30.71 billion, helped by sales of Alphabet's TPU AI chip systems. Cloud operating income reached $11.12 billion, up from $3.59 billion a year earlier. Cloud growth has sped up every quarter this year, from 63% to 82% to 103%.

His other predictions hold up too. Search brought in $63.35 billion, and query growth hit its fastest pace in five years. YouTube ads added $11.21 billion. The Gemini app reached 1 billion users. Waymo passed 500,000 fully autonomous rides per week in the spring.

Earnings per share of $2.12 missed expectations, falling short of the $2.97 consensus. An $8.7 billion loss on equity securities cut EPS by $0.55. Operating income still grew 29.9%.

## What Would Turn a Correction Into Something Worse

Cash flow is the risk to watch. In the quarter, capital spending more than doubled, up 114% to $51.36 billion. Free cash flow was negative $9.07 billion, worse than the negative $5.86 billion in the second quarter. To pay for the expansion, Alphabet sold $28.7 billion in notes. It also sold stock through an at-the-market (ATM) program, which allows a company to sell new shares gradually into the market. That raised $4.3 billion in Class A shares and $8.1 billion in Class C shares, with $27.6 billion still available, as shown in the third-quarter earnings release.

The latest results show no buyback activity. Alphabet is issuing new shares, which dilutes existing holders. The quarterly dividend is $0.22, payable Dec. 14, for a yield of 0.62%.

Three things would break Lebenthal's thesis:

Cloud growth slows while spending stays near early-2026 capex guidance of $175 billion to $185 billion.

Search growth keeps slipping. It went from 19% to 17% to 12% this year.

Lenders pull back from AI borrowers.

A trailing P/E of 12 looks cheap. However, past earnings were boosted by equity gains of $36.9 billion and $99.0 billion in the first two quarters, so the stock is less cheap than that number suggests.

## Verdict for Retirement Investors

Lebenthal's correction call matches the indexes. The filings back his shots-on-goal case. Alphabet's businesses are growing, and Cloud is now a major profit engine. The weak spot is cash.

The company is borrowing and selling stock to fund AI, and its dividend is too small to rely on for retirement income. The data supports his case, though Alphabet carries more balance-sheet risk than the indexes right now. For retirement investors, the key signal is whether free cash flow turns positive. Alphabet's fourth-quarter report, expected in early February, will show whether spending starts producing cash.
