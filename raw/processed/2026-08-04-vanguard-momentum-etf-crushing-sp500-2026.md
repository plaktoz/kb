# Meet the Magnificent Vanguard ETF Crushing the S&P 500 in 2026, Thanks to Its Unique Momentum-Driven Strategy

The Vanguard U.S. Momentum Factor ETF (VFMO) exclusively invests in stocks with sustained upward momentum, using a mathematical model that weighs 12-month performance while checking the last six months to confirm the trend is intact, and verifying moves are driven by company fundamentals rather than a rising broader market.

The ETF is up 17% in 2026, versus 9.4% for the S&P 500 — and its outperformance isn't a one-off, since the fund has beaten the market on average every year since its 2018 launch, delivering a 14.8% compound annual return versus 14.1% for the S&P 500 over the same period, despite the COVID-19 crash, the 2022 inflation surge, and 2025 tariff volatility.

## Hundreds of winners packed into one ETF

The fund held 670 stocks across nine sectors as of July 31: Technology (25.6%), Industrials (19.7%), Healthcare (18.3%), Energy (12.8%) and Consumer discretionary (7.5%) were the top weightings. Applied Materials, Sandisk and Advanced Micro Devices were the top three holdings, all technology names benefiting from AI-driven data center demand. GE Vernova and Caterpillar, industrial names benefiting from AI-driven electricity demand, are also among the top 20 holdings.

The fund's holdings turn over almost entirely each year (a 99.9% turnover rate), meaning momentum is often fleeting — even Nvidia has fallen out of the ETF as it underperforms the S&P 500 this year. The mathematical models are designed to identify any change in market leadership and rotate holdings accordingly regardless of which sector is driving returns.

One downside is cost: VFMO's 0.13% expense ratio is roughly four times as costly as Vanguard's passive S&P 500 index funds, which charge around 0.03%.
