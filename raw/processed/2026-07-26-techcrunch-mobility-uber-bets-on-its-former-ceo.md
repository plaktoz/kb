# TechCrunch Mobility: Uber bets on its former CEO

source_url: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/26/techcrunch-mobility-uber-bets-on-its-former-ceo

---

By Kirsten Korosec, Transportation Editor — July 26, 2026

Tesla kicked off earnings season for this sector, and the shareholder letter, along with Elon Musk's remarks during the conference call, provided some incredible disclosures that likely concerned or puzzled investors.

Tesla has backed off previous promises to reach "volume production" of the Cybercab, Tesla Semi, and Megapack 3 in 2026. And while the company has publicly touted expansions of its Tesla Robotaxi service into new cities in Florida and Texas, the quarter-over-quarter data shows a drop in paid robotaxi miles.

Senior reporter Sean O'Kane took a closer look at a graph shared in Tesla's shareholder letter. At a passing glance, the chart appears to show steady growth in paid robotaxi rides between August 2025 and June 2026. But the numbers displayed are cumulative, and when broken down by quarter, they show that Tesla's Robotaxi fleet of Model Y SUVs carrying paying passengers covered around 1.1 million miles in the first quarter. That fell to roughly 700,000 miles in the second quarter, a decline of about 36%.

Musk also disclosed during the call that Tesla needs to accumulate driving data specific to the Cybercab before it can put large numbers of the vehicles on the road, because Tesla has to accumulate miles using Cybercabs retrofitted with steering wheels and pedals to calibrate to the Cybercab chassis — a change from the company's previous claims that its fleet of nearly 10 million customer cars has been collecting data usable for training future robotaxis.

On the financial front, Tesla's Q2 earnings show a company plowing money into its next generation of products (CapEx has doubled, and the company is back in negative free cash flow territory). Even though revenue is up, the boost wasn't enough to offset the cost of doing business, and net income fell 5% year over year.

## Deals

Travis Kalanick burst back onto the robotics and mobility scene earlier this year with Atoms — a rebranded holding company atop his ghost kitchen project — and a deal to acquire Anthony Levandowski's industrial automation startup, Pronto. Now the Uber co-founder and former CEO has $1.7 billion in capital to play with. VC giant Andreessen Horowitz led the round with participation from Bain Capital, Fifth Wall, and Uber. Ben Horowitz will join the company's board following the investment.

This may seem inconceivable for those who remember Kalanick's resignation from Uber's top leadership spot nearly a decade ago — and the string of scandals and lawsuits in the year leading up to his departure. What's even more incredible is that Uber participated in the funding round: The Information reported Uber invested $100 million into Atoms, made six months ago.

Reminder: In 2016, while Kalanick was CEO, Uber acquired Levandowski's self-driving trucks startup Otto. Less than a year later, Levandowski's former employer Waymo sued Uber for trade secret theft; the companies settled on the fifth day of the trial. There is a lot of messy history between Kalanick, Uber, and Levandowski — but the ride-hailing company is still willing to invest in them.

What is Atoms going to do with this capital? Details are vague, but a company email from Levandowski suggests Pronto will be a big part of those plans: "Atoms is investing heavily in Industrial AI and physical automation applied to mining and transport," and "Pronto is a core strategic priority for Atoms... scaling practical, OEM-agnostic autonomy."

Other deals that got attention: Einride agreed to acquire EV charging startup Flipturn in an all-stock deal worth $38 million. IBM agreed to buy HRL Laboratories, a quantum computing research lab jointly owned by Boeing and General Motors. Sila, the battery materials startup, raised $300 million in a round led by Atreides Management and Sutter Hill Ventures.

## Notable reads

Aurora launched its second-generation driverless trucks, with new hardware built for a million-mile service life, running an initial fleet on its Dallas-to-Houston route, growing to 200 driverless trucks by year end, without a human observer in the cab.

Ford is turning to Apple for its next generation of EVs, integrating Apple Maps navigation via a new MapKit for Automotive toolset starting with a $30,000 midsize truck in 2027.

The Insurance Institute for Highway Safety released a study finding Waymo's current robotaxis have a 68% lower crash involvement rate than human drivers when including police-reportable crashes, while noting national crash-data collection for Level 4 vehicles can be improved.

Mobileye founder and CEO Amnon Shashua plans to step down after nearly three decades, as the company pushes into robotaxis and humanoid robots.

Waymo is having internal discussions over how to end its Uber partnership, per the Financial Times; the existing contract covering Atlanta and Austin runs through May 2028.
