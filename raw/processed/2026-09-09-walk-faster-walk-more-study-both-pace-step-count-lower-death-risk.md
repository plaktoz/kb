# Walk faster or walk more? Study shows both pace and step count lower death risk

A study published in the *British Journal of Sports Medicine* analyzed 103,684 UK Biobank participants (ages 40–69) who wore activity trackers for 7 days. Researchers tracked all-cause and cardiovascular deaths over roughly 8 years.

Key findings:
- Walking 7,500–10,000 steps daily correlated with the lowest early-death risk
- Participants taking fewer than 5,000 steps daily but at a brisk pace (80+ steps/minute) showed mortality risk comparable to those doing 5,000–7,500 steps at a slower pace
- Intensity was measured using "peak 30-min cadence" — the average of the 30 highest-step minutes per day

Lead researcher Emmanuel Stamatakis emphasized "behavioral flexibility," noting the study is observational and shows association, not causation.

Cardiologist Kevin Shah, not involved in the research, noted that increased movement intensity can improve blood pressure, weight, and insulin sensitivity. He stressed that "the biggest health gains often come from going from no activity to some activity."

The core takeaway: if walking farther isn't feasible, walking faster may offer comparable longevity benefits.
