# What Even Is An OS Now?

Thomas Ptacek announces his departure from Fly.io to build a new phone with collaborator Kurt, using the occasion to argue that AI has fundamentally transformed computing in ways not yet fully absorbed.

He reflects on childhood disappointment with early computers — devices that required programming expertise just to do anything useful. Now, he argues, that barrier has collapsed.

The central thesis: software is shifting from mass-produced applications made by professional developers to personalized tools conjured by ordinary users. He gives examples of hyper-local, single-person-use-case apps — like routing around Chicago traffic or catching a missed meeting — that no commercial developer would build, but that AI makes trivially generatable.

This leads to a pointed question about operating systems: their core purpose has been isolating applications from *strangers'* software. In a world where most software is self-generated and malleable, that architecture makes less sense.

His conclusion: he's building a phone designed not around fixed-function apps, but around on-device AI that builds tools on demand. He acknowledges the announcement sounds grandiose, but frames it as a natural response to what he sees as an inevitable, already-occurring shift — one requiring people to "think like kids again."
