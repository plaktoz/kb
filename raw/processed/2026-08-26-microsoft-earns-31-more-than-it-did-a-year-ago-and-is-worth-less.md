# Microsoft Earns 31% More Than It Did a Year Ago and Is Worth Less

Microsoft posted remarkable fiscal 2026 results — revenue climbed 18% to $331.8 billion while net income jumped 31% to $133.7 billion. Yet the stock's market cap sits roughly 4.5% lower than a year prior.

The explanation lies in multiple compression: investors once paid ~37x earnings for MSFT; now they pay ~27x. The business grew by about a third, but the market's valuation of each earnings dollar shrank by a similar proportion.

Strong fundamentals weren't the issue. Azure surpassed $100 billion annually (up 41%), and the commercial backlog hit $678 billion — up 84% year-over-year. Excluding OpenAI investment gains, EPS still rose 22%.

The core concern is capital expenditure intensity. Microsoft spent $115.9 billion on property/equipment in fiscal 2026 (up 80%), with CFO Amy Hood guiding ~$175 billion in capex for calendar 2026 — exceeding the company's entire fiscal 2026 operating income of $155.2 billion. Free cash flow fell to ~$67 billion from ~$72 billion the prior year, even as earnings surged.

The Apple contrast is telling. Apple spent under $7 billion on capex in nine months, yet its market cap rose ~33% to $4.51 trillion, trading at ~35x earnings versus Microsoft's 27x. The market currently rewards asset-light customer-facing businesses over infrastructure builders.

Broader software sector fears about AI disruption compound the discount. The author concludes shares are "more of a hold than a buy."
