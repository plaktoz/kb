# FTSE 100 today: Stocks extend fall on U.S. chip curbs; UK jobless rate dips

Investing.com -- British stocks remained under pressure on Tuesday, weighed down by new U.S. curbs on China's access to AI chips and caution ahead of Nvidia's earnings, despite an unexpected drop in UK unemployment to 4.6%.

The FTSE 100 fell 1.8%. Germany's DAX lost 2% and France's CAC 40 fell 1.6%. Sterling rose 0.3% to $1.3225.

Brent January futures rose 0.8% to $93.18 a barrel and WTI January gained 0.6% to $84.96. Gold December futures rose 0.5% to $3,872.86 an ounce, while spot gold gained 0.3% to $3,870.

U.S. President Donald Trump warned Iran of severe consequences over reports of a possible dirty-bomb plot against Washington, telling reporters in Manila, Philippines, where he is attending the ASEAN-U.S. summit and a Quad meeting, that Tehran would pay "a price the likes of which no other country has ever paid before."

Beijing will take "necessary measures" to safeguard its interests, foreign ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun told a Tuesday press conference, calling the U.S. chip rule economic coercion.

The rule, published Monday, requires licences for advanced chip shipments to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil, while exempting U.S.-run data centres. Trump is due to meet Xi Jinping on Thursday in Shenzhen. MSCI's Asia-Pacific index fell about 1.2%, Investing.com reported.

Jefferies strategist Mohit Kumar said in a morning note the unwind of AI-related trades remained the market's key driver, though he called the sell-off a buying opportunity.

Britain's unemployment rate fell to 4.6% in the three months to September, the lowest since April 2025, the Office for National Statistics said, defying expectations for 4.7%.

Economist Sanjay Raja said in a note that payrolls fell by 34,000 and vacancies slid to 690,000, the lowest since 2014 outside the pandemic, adding that firms were likely to cut hiring.

Francesco Pesole said the data were unlikely to shift Bank of England tightening plans, with an 80% chance of a December hike priced in. UK inflation data are due on Wednesday.

Nvidia is set to report third-quarter results after market close, its first earnings announcement since the shock departure of co-founder and former CEO Jensen Huang last week.

Investors will look to interim CEO Colette Kress for clarity on the company's strategy and whether Nvidia plans to continue its circular financing arrangements with AI customers.

Analysts have flagged U.S.-China tensions, potential national security investigations and restrictions on access to high-end AI chips as key risks for Nvidia's outlook.
