# What To Expect For Microsoft Stock's 2026 Outlook, According To Experts

Microsoft shares fell significantly in early 2026 — dropping roughly 15% year-to-date as of April 23. After reaching a 52-week high of $555.45, shares hit a low in late March around $373. This marks the stock's worst quarterly performance since 2008.

## Financial performance

- Q2 2026: $81.3 billion in revenue (17% YoY increase), adjusted EPS $4.14
- Q3 2026 guidance: $80.7–$81.8 billion revenue
- Full-year 2026 consensus: $324–$327 billion revenue; EPS $16.46–$17.10
- Operating margins near 46.7%
- Contracted backlog: $625 billion (more than doubled in the last year)
- Annual Copilot (M365) revenue estimated at only $1.4–$3.2 billion vs. earlier $30 billion targets

## Analyst consensus: bullish

Roughly 97 analysts cover MSFT — most rate it "Strong Buy" or "Buy." Average 12-month price target: $589–$592. The current valuation (~21–22x NTM earnings) is the stock's cheapest forward multiple since 2023.

Key analyst views:
- **Wedbush (Dan Ives):** "Outperform," $625 target — "Wall Street underestimates Azure growth prospects"
- **Morgan Stanley:** "Top pick," raised target to $650 — Microsoft securing a central role in enterprise AI adoption
- **Bernstein:** Target $641 — "engine of growth is strong and getting stronger"
- **Benchmark:** "Buy" — current pullbacks are a long-term buying opportunity

## Bull case

- Azure cloud growth expected to reaccelerate as new AI capacity comes online H2 2026
- $625 billion contracted backlog provides revenue visibility
- Aggressive models project stock to $792+ by end of decade
- CapEx creates durable competitive moats in AI infrastructure

## Bear case

- $80–$146 billion in FY2026 CapEx growing faster than revenue; may compress free cash flow margins
- Copilot monetization failure: estimated $1.4–3.2 billion actual revenue vs. $30 billion analyst expectations
- OpenAI dependency: OpenAI projecting $600 billion in spending by 2030 while generating only $20 billion in 2025 revenue
- Vibe coding risk: generative AI could enable customers to write their own software, reducing Microsoft 365 seat demand
- UK antitrust probe into cloud licensing practices
- In April 2026, Microsoft offered voluntary buyouts to ~7% of U.S. employees; laid off 15,000+ in the prior year

## AI and Azure strategy

Satya Nadella stated Microsoft has already built an AI business larger than some of its legacy franchises. Azure AI infrastructure and Copilot adoption are the primary growth levers. The bears' central concern: capital expenditure is growing faster than AI monetization.
