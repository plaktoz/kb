# ADHD Linked to Higher Odds of Every Gastrointestinal Symptom Investigated

## Summary

A meta-analysis from Warwick Medical School examined data from 23 studies covering over 1.9 million participants and found a robust association between ADHD and gastrointestinal (GI) disorders across all symptom categories studied.

## Key Findings

People with ADHD showed a **48% overall increase in GI symptom odds** versus neurotypical peers. Specific elevated risks included:

- **97%** higher odds of constipation
- **88%** higher likelihood of abdominal pain
- **58%** higher odds of IBS
- **52%** higher odds of dyspepsia
- **2x+** higher odds of diarrhea
- **4x+** higher odds of encopresis

## Why the Link Exists

No single mechanism has been confirmed. Proposed contributors include:

- **Gut-brain axis disruption** and microbiome differences
- **Irregular eating habits** — skipping meals, impulsive eating, narrow dietary preferences
- **Stress and emotional dysregulation** affecting gut motility
- **Stimulant medications**, which can cause nausea, appetite suppression, and abdominal pain

Co-lead author Sarah Blake noted that "research into communication between the gut and brain...suggested this review may be worthwhile."

Consultant psychiatrist Sylvia Baker cautioned that "knowing what to do and being able to do it consistently are not always the same thing" for people with ADHD.

## Practical Guidance

Baker recommended:
- Recognizing the gut-ADHD connection rather than dismissing symptoms
- Regular meals, hydration, fiber intake, and physical activity
- Using reminders and routines to support consistency
- Discussing medication-related GI changes with a prescribing clinician
- Avoiding restrictive diets or supplements without specialist input

## What's Needed Next

Researchers called for longitudinal studies examining microbiome composition alongside clinical variables — medication, sleep, stress, and diet — to establish causality rather than correlation. Baker emphasized wanting to know "whether treating gastrointestinal problems has any meaningful effect on quality of life or ADHD symptoms, and vice versa."
