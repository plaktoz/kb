# AI Complements Radiologists to Help Improve Brain Aneurysm Detection

A real-world prospective study of 3,856 CT angiography (CTA) examinations across the Northwell Health system found that an FDA-cleared AI algorithm (Aidoc's aiOS) identified 55 brain aneurysms that radiologists had initially missed — a 39% relative increase in detection.

## Key Findings

- **Sensitivity:** AI outperformed radiologists (84.6% vs. 71.8%), catching more true aneurysms
- **Precision:** Radiologists were more accurate when flagging a finding as an aneurysm (positive predictive value: 92.7% vs. 78.2% for AI)
- **Agreement:** Radiologists and AI concurred on more than 96% of examinations
- **Missed findings:** AI caught 55 aneurysms radiologists missed; radiologists caught 30 the AI did not detect
- **False positives:** Of 101 AI-only findings, 46 were ultimately false positives

## Setting Matters

AI performance varied by care setting:
- **Inpatient:** Strongest results — 18 additional true aneurysms, 7 false positives
- **Emergency department:** Also favorable performance
- **Outpatient:** More limited benefit; false positives exceeded true positives

Researchers attributed this to higher case complexity in acute settings, offering more opportunity for AI to add value.

## Expert Perspective

Lead author Dr. Shlomit Stein noted that the AI "identified mostly small aneurysms missed by radiologists," adding that despite small size, these findings "may nevertheless be clinically important" given that rupture risk depends on shape, location, and patient factors — not size alone.

## Conclusions

The study, published in the *Journal of the American College of Radiology*, supports AI as a **complementary tool** rather than a replacement. Researchers emphasized that final interpretation and clinical decisions should remain with physicians. Importantly, the study does not yet establish whether increased detection translates to better patient outcomes — further research is needed.
