# A Qualitative Study of IT Students' Skill Development: Comparing Online and Face-to-Face Learning Environments

This qualitative study explores how IT students experience and assess skill development in online versus face-to-face (F2F) learning environments, using a grounded-theory approach based on semi-structured interviews with eight IT students across bachelor, master, and PhD programs at three Portuguese institutions.

## F2F Learning Environment

Key causal conditions were direct interaction with classmates and professors and access to physical resources (classrooms, libraries, offices). These fostered concentration and collaboration. F2F settings were most effective at developing non-technical skills: communication, collaboration, teamwork, idea exchange, brainstorming, and problem-solving, driven by real-time group dynamics. Reported downsides included poor time management (commuting, coordinating group schedules) and presentation anxiety.

## Online Learning Environment

Key causal conditions were access to online platforms/content, flexibility, and (historically) the pandemic. Online learning strengthened technical skills (particularly programming, via resources like Stack Overflow and GitHub), self-regulation, autonomy, discipline, time management, and digital literacy. Reported downsides included distraction, monotony, isolation, procrastination, and fatigue, along with weaker development of interpersonal and communication skills due to the lack of direct interaction.

## Comparison and Implications

F2F learning develops communication and collaborative skills more effectively through synchronous interaction; online learning strengthens self-regulation and adaptability through independence and flexibility. The authors suggest a hybrid learning model may combine the technical-skill and autonomy benefits of online learning with the interpersonal and collaborative benefits of F2F learning, offering the "best of both worlds" for IT education.
