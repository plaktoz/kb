# What You Eat and When You Eat It May Affect Your Dementia Risk

Over 57 million people worldwide live with dementia, and the WHO estimates that up to 45% of dementia risks are modifiable through lifestyle changes. Three recent studies shed light on how diet and timing may influence dementia risk.

## 1. Midlife Risk Factor Management

A study in *Neurology Open Access* tracked 12,409 participants over roughly 26 years, examining diabetes, hypertension, and smoking. Those with all three risk factors were 2.69 times more likely to develop dementia. Avoiding all three was associated with up to 13 additional dementia-free years.

Dr. Dung Trinh noted the findings "reinforce the close connection between cardiovascular health and brain health during midlife," while cautioning the observational design limits causal conclusions.

## 2. Early Life Sugar Restriction

A study in *npj Aging* analyzed 60,000+ UK Biobank participants born 1951–1956, exploiting the natural experiment of Britain's 1953 sugar rationing end. Those with sugar-restricted early life exposure showed:
- 46% lower Alzheimer's risk
- 27% lower all-cause dementia risk

Senior author Bing Zhang explained the first 1,000 days represent "a critical window for brain development." However, Dr. Peter Gliebus cautioned against over-interpreting results, noting the Alzheimer's finding relied on a relatively small case count.

## 3. Time-Restricted Eating

A trial of 47 women (ages 50–79, with overweight or obesity) found that those following a 9-hour eating window showed improvements in memory, learning, and problem-solving after 6 months, beyond what caloric restriction alone achieved.

Dr. Trinh summarized: "when people eat may matter for cognitive health, not just how much they eat," while describing findings as preliminary.
