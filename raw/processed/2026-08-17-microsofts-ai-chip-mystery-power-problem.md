# Microsoft's AI Chip Mystery May Become a Power Problem

A Guardian investigation into Microsoft's internal documents raised questions about whether the company has enough computing chips for its data centers, or sufficient power and finished facilities to use the hardware it already owns. Since 2022, Microsoft has spent $280 billion on AI infrastructure and added five gigawatts of data center capacity, but internal documents showed only 2.2 million AI chips installed — fewer than some experts expected, and well short of an earlier target of 1.8 million chips by the end of 2024 that should have scaled higher given the stated capacity growth.

CEO Satya Nadella has suggested electricity and construction, not chip supply, are the real constraint: "If you can't do that, you may actually have a bunch of chips sitting in inventory that I can't plug in. It's not a supply issue of chips. It's the fact that I don't have warm shells to plug into." Microsoft disputed the Guardian's calculations as "inaccurate, drawing the wrong conclusions from incorrect assumptions," without specifying which figures were wrong; the company's data centers mix Nvidia, AMD, Intel, and custom silicon, complicating exact estimates.

One Nvidia analyst told the Guardian the installed-chip figures were "less than I expected Microsoft would have." A UC Riverside professor, Shaolei Ren, questioned Microsoft's capacity claims given insufficient context on what "added data center capacity" means.

The piece frames this as a broader signal for the AI hardware market: processors deliver no value sitting in storage — they must be installed and connected to power and cooling before customers can use them. Microsoft's Fairwater project in Wisconsin illustrates the gap: Nadella said in April it "is going live," but Microsoft later acknowledged the site wasn't yet online. The debate isn't just how many chips Microsoft owns, but how many it can actually switch on — a dynamic that could open opportunities for competitors whose hardware better fits available power and cooling limits.
