# Expertise expert offers 8 tips for learning a new skill

UNC's Jeffrey A. Greene, drawing on K. Anders Ericsson's deliberate practice model, offers eight tips for learning a new skill:

1. Find a guide — seek a source (book, instructor, guide) that explicitly breaks down the steps.
2. Set up a distraction-free environment — it's easier to prevent distractions than resist them once present.
3. Build endurance — start with 15-30 minutes of practice and build up; even experts rarely exceed 4-5 hours a day.
4. Practice deliberately — be intentional and mindful, starting with a basic component of the skill; this can even improve automated skills like typing by "de-automatizing" them.
5. Find motivation — both intrinsic and extrinsic motivation are valid as long as you feel in control of the choice; extrinsic motivation only becomes harmful when coercive or manipulative.
6. Get feedback — outside perspective (coach, partner, or even a computer, e.g. code that won't run) accelerates learning; feedback should "feed forward" toward next steps, not just assess past performance.
7. Get the right kind of feedback — feedback should affirm your autonomy, your competence ("you're going to get there"), and that the person cares about your success.
8. Be your own coach — notice and internalize your own improvements over time.

Greene's overall takeaway: practice time matters, but it's only one of several factors — guidance, deliberate focus, motivation quality, and feedback quality all shape how effectively a skill is acquired.
