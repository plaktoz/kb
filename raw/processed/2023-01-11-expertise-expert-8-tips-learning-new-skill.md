# Expertise expert offers 8 tips for learning a new skill

UNC's Jeffrey A. Greene, who teaches a course on the science of expertise based on K. Anders Ericsson's deliberate practice model, says mastering a new skill takes a diligent approach — not just the popularized "10,000 hours." His eight tips:

1. **Find a guide.** Look for a source — book, online guide, or instructor — that breaks the skill into explicit steps.
2. **Set up a distraction-free environment.** "It's a lot easier to set up a context where distractions won't happen than it is to resist distractions when they happen." Put your phone in another room.
3. **Build endurance gradually.** Beginners should practice 15–30 minutes at a time across multiple days a week; even experts generally can't sustain more than four or five hours of practice a day.
4. **Practice deliberately.** Be intentional and mindful, starting with a basic component of the skill (e.g., your swing in pickleball). Deliberately "de-automatizing" and slowing down can even improve skills you've plateaued on, like typing.
5. **Find motivation, intrinsic or extrinsic.** Extrinsic motivation (wanting a better job, say) is fine as long as you feel in control of the choice to pursue it — it only becomes harmful when it's coercive.
6. **Get feedback** from a coach or partner, even a non-expert one, for outside perspective on your progress.
7. **Get the right kind of feedback** — assessments that make you feel in control of your choices, competent, and cared about strengthen both intrinsic and extrinsic motivation.
8. **Be your own coach.** Notice and internalize your improvements. Most people benefit from coaching regardless of skill level, but you can do much of that work yourself.

Greene's final tip: have fun.
