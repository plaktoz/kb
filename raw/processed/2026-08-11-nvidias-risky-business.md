# Nvidia's Risky Business

## Historical Parallel: Jay Cooke & the 1873 Railroad Crash

Thompson opens with Jay Cooke's 1870 deal to finance the Northern Pacific Railway. Unable to attract institutional buyers, Cooke sold bonds directly to retail investors through 1,500 salespeople and 1,300 funded newspapers — a funding innovation that ultimately collapsed in the Panic of 1873, triggering depression and deflation. The railway eventually became part of BNSF, now owned by Berkshire Hathaway.

The parallel to today: Liaquat Ahamed's book *1873* notes that annual U.S. railway bond investment during the 1870s boom, scaled to 2026 economies, approximates the ~$600 billion projected in tech infrastructure spending this year.

## Hyperscaler Debt Binge

Microsoft remains the only major hyperscaler with substantial free cash flow (~$19.6B last quarter) funding CapEx without debt. The others have aggressively tapped debt markets:

- Oracle, Meta, Alphabet, and Amazon issued ~$80B combined (Sep–Nov 2025)
- Combined 2025 issuance: ~$108B
- Through July 2026: ~$194B already raised
- Bond spreads are rising; 86% of 2026 issuances trade at higher yields than at issuance
- Cover for recent issuance has fallen from ~5x (February) to under 2x

In June 2026, Google raised $85B in equity, including a special $10B issuance to Berkshire Hathaway — signaling willingness to fund AI infrastructure via all capital sources available.

## DeepMind's Decline

SemiAnalysis declared Gemini "cooked," asserting DeepMind is "no longer a frontier lab" due to RL team departures and poor compute allocation. The new DeepMind CEO Koray Kavukcuoglu is reportedly aligned with Sergey Brin, and a pivot toward text/code-centered AI (closer to Anthropic's approach) appears underway.

Former DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis prioritized *world models*, which may have deprioritized coding and agentic workflows — areas where Anthropic and OpenAI have pulled ahead.

## Google as Infrastructure Play

Despite DeepMind's struggles, Google Cloud is thriving: 82% YoY growth, 36% margins. Anthropic is described as likely one of Google's largest cloud customers, and SemiAnalysis reports that over 20% of TPU shipments (3Q26–4Q27) are going directly to Anthropic — beyond what GCP already rents to them.

Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian frames Google explicitly as a "platform company," comfortable selling TPUs to competitors like Anthropic. TPUs are believed to offer a cost advantage over Nvidia GPUs, and Anthropic is now reportedly *buying* TPUs outright for its own data centers, converting marginal compute costs into capital costs.

Thompson's core thesis: Google may not win the model race, but it's positioning to dominate AI infrastructure — leveraging cash, debt, and now equity to become the capital-intensive backbone of AI, akin to how BNSF is a cash-generating infrastructure giant within Berkshire Hathaway.

## Nvidia's New Financing Gambit

Jensen Huang announced partnerships with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR to "mobilize over $500 billion of third-party capital" for AI infrastructure. The pitch: Nvidia-powered AI factories are now an investable infrastructure asset class — fungible, revenue-generating, and improvable over time via CUDA.

Key tension: Nvidia is backstopping investments with up to **25% residual-value financing**, putting its own profits on the line. This functions as an implicit price cut to reduce the cost of capital for Nvidia-based data centers — a defensive move against Google's TPUs and Amazon's Trainium chips gaining share.

Thompson's earlier concern (from 2024) remains: frontier labs like Anthropic have moved away from CUDA dependency, reducing Nvidia's software moat at precisely the moment competitive pressure is highest.

## The Risk

The closing argument mirrors the Cooke parallel: each successive funding mechanism — free cash flow → debt → equity → institutional/insurance capital — carries escalating systemic risk. Bringing in "safety-seeking assets" (pension funds, insurance floats) that don't mark risk to market is a qualitatively different level of exposure than equity dilution.

As Thompson puts it: *"AI better deliver before it's too late."*
