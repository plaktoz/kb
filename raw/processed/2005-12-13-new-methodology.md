# The New Methodology

Fowler examines the emergence of agile software methods as a reaction to rigid, plan-driven methodologies. He argues two fundamental distinctions separate agile from traditional approaches:

1. **Adaptive vs. Predictive** – Traditional methods attempt to plan extensively upfront, which fails when requirements shift. Agile embraces change through iterative development, delivering working software frequently to expose real problems early.

2. **People-first vs. Process-first** – Plan-driven methods treat developers as interchangeable resources. Agile recognizes that software is a creative, design-intensive discipline where talented individuals—not prescribed processes—drive success.

## Key Points

- Construction in software is nearly free; nearly all effort is *design*, making predictability elusive
- Requirements instability is the norm, not the exception, in business software
- Iterative cycles provide honest feedback and allow course correction before plans collapse
- Measurement-based management backfires in software because critical factors resist quantification
- Customers must engage continuously, not just at contract signing
- Self-adaptive teams regularly reflect and refine their own process each iteration

## Agile Flavors Covered

- **Agile Manifesto** (2001) – Unified umbrella values document
- **XP** – Technically rigorous; emphasizes TDD and continuous integration
- **Scrum** – Focuses on management cadence (sprints, daily standups)
- **Crystal** – Tailored by team size; prioritizes habitability
- **Context-Driven Testing** – Questions conformance-based testing norms
- **Lean Development** – Draws on Toyota Production System principles
- **RUP** – Flexible framework; risks becoming meaninglessly broad

## On Adoption

Fowler advises starting with willing teams on moderately critical projects, finding an experienced mentor, and committing genuinely to the approach before evaluating it. Imposing agile on reluctant teams contradicts its core philosophy.
