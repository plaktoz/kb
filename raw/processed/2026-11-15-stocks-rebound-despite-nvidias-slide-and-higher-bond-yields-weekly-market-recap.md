## 📊 Stocks rebound despite Nvidia’s slide and higher bond yields

## What happened?

Stocks bounced back this week, with AI optimism helping investors move past October’s selloff.

Strong earnings and demand updates from CoreWeave, TSMC and Applied Materials lifted AI-related stocks. Anthropic’s IPO, which was about six times oversubscribed, added to the buzz.

But the recovery hit a speed bump later in the week. Nvidia shares fell following the exit of CEO Jensen Huang.

The implications could extend beyond Nvidia. Huang has been a key backer of emerging AI companies, and his departure raises questions about leadership succession, corporate governance and funding for AI start-ups.

## What does this mean?

While enthusiasm for AI returned, inflation gave investors another reason to stay cautious. US core inflation rose 0.3% month-on-month in October, above market expectations of 0.2%.

The hotter-than-expected inflation report pushed the 10-year US Treasury yield above 5%.

For investors, this means higher interest rates could remain a headwind, even as company earnings offer reasons for optimism.

## Why should I care?

Despite the late-week jitters, the Nasdaq Composite gained 2.5%, while the S&P 500 rose 2.3%.

Closer to home, the Straits Times Index climbed 1.0%. SATS and Singapore Airlines were among the gainers, as weaker Brent crude oil prices helped keep a lid on fuel cost pressures across the aviation sector.

## 🚗 Moving This Week

Singtel’s 1H FY2027 underlying net profit rose 10% year on year to S$1.49 billion, supported by stronger contributions from NCS, Digital InfraCo, Optus, Airtel and AIS. Operating revenue increased 6% to S$7.29 billion, while reported earnings fell 55% due to the absence of large one-off gains recorded a year earlier. Singtel raised its interim dividend by 10% to 9 cents per share and has completed S$1 billion of its S$2 billion share buyback programme.

Singapore Airlines' 2QFY2027 net profit quadrupled to S$210 million, while revenue rose 14% to S$5.6 billion, supported by higher fares, resilient passenger demand and stronger AI-driven cargo volumes. Operating profit reached S$463 million, while losses from associates narrowed. However, jet fuel costs rose S$436 million year-on-year, weighing on profitability.

ST Engineering’s 3Q FY2026 revenue rose 9% year on year to S$3.4 billion, bringing 9M revenue to S$10.0 billion. Growth was broad-based, led by Urban Solutions & Satcom, where revenue increased 15%, while Defence & Public Security and Commercial Aerospace also posted higher revenue. ST Engineering will pay an interim dividend of 5 cents per share, in line with earlier guidance.

SATS' 2QFY2027 net profit rose 2.8% year-on-year to S$81.1 million, while revenue grew 9% to S$1.71 billion, driven by higher cargo and meal volumes. First-half earnings increased 4.3% to S$156.2 million. Cargo volumes outpaced industry growth for the 12th consecutive quarter, while SATS maintained its interim dividend at two cents per share.

City Developments Limited (CDL) expects significantly weaker 2HFY2026 earnings year-on-year, mainly due to the absence of a S$473.1 million gain from its South Beach divestment in 2025. The stronger Korean won has also resulted in substantial foreign exchange losses at its Korean subsidiary, while potential property impairment charges could further weigh on earnings.

Genting Singapore's 3Q2026 net profit fell 42% year-on-year to S$54.7 million, as revenue declined 13% to S$566.1 million. Gaming revenue dropped over 20% to S$315 million amid softer premium gaming activity, while non-gaming revenue remained stable. Ongoing upgrades at Resorts World Sentosa are expected to constrain operating capacity through FY2027.

ComfortDelGro's 9M2026 net profit fell 20% year-on-year to S$141.1 million, weighed down by higher operating costs and the absence of a S$25.9 million one-off disposal gain. Revenue rose 4.9% to S$3.94 billion, supported by improved London bus contract margins and stronger corporate taxi demand in Singapore and Australia. Overseas operations contributed 56.7% of revenue.

CSE Global's 3Q2026 revenue rose 25.4% year-on-year to S$323.2 million, driven by strong growth in its Americas electrification business, where revenue increased 41.2% to S$187.6 million. New orders surged 197.7% to S$434.9 million, lifting its order book by 55.8% to S$728.5 million. Expanded production capacity also supported higher project volumes.

NTT DC REIT reported 1HFY2027 DPU of 4.32 US cents, 10% above its IPO forecast. Net property income exceeded projections by 4.9% to US$55.8 million, supported by higher tenant fit-out activity, lower property taxes and favourable exchange rates. Portfolio occupancy stood at 97.5%, with positive rental reversion of 11.7% and gearing of 29.9%.

Centurion’s 9M2026 revenue rose 35% year on year to S$280.1 million, driven by new beds and portfolio additions across Singapore, Malaysia and Australia. 3Q revenue increased 41% to S$95.2 million. Centurion maintained its 2H2026 revenue guidance of about S$190 million and expects occupancy in Singapore and Malaysia to improve in 4Q.

UMS Integration's 3Q2026 net profit surged 102% year-on-year to S$21.1 million, as revenue rose 61% to S$95.4 million. Semiconductor revenue grew 65% to S$83.1 million, driven by strong system and component sales, while aerospace revenue increased 55%. The company declared a one-cent interim dividend, payable on 18 December.
