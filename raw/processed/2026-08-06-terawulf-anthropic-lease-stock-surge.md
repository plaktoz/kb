# TeraWulf (WULF) Is Up 19.7% After $19 Billion Anthropic AI Lease Deal - What's Changed

TeraWulf Inc. has now reported its second-quarter 2026 results, with revenue of US$44.77 million and a net loss of US$939.92 million, while simultaneously advancing its shift toward high-performance computing through expanded capacity and new data center assets.

A centerpiece of this update is a 20-year, roughly US$19 billion lease agreement with Anthropic, which materially reshapes TeraWulf's business mix toward long-duration AI infrastructure contracts backed by significant liquidity.

## TeraWulf Investment Narrative Recap

To own TeraWulf today, you have to believe its pivot from Bitcoin mining to long-duration AI and HPC leases can eventually support a sustainable business, despite very large current losses. The Anthropic deal and rising HPC mix sharpen the near term focus on execution risk and balance sheet resilience, especially after a Q2 net loss of about US$939.9 million. This news is material because it amplifies both the potential contract base and the consequences if projects slip.

The 20-year, roughly US$19 billion Anthropic lease stands out as the key announcement here, reinforcing the shift toward contracted AI infrastructure after Fluidstack and Google. While this adds long-term revenue visibility if fully delivered, it also increases exposure to a concentrated set of hyperscale tenants and multi-year build outs. That interplay between contract depth and counterparty or utilization risk now sits at the heart of the TeraWulf story.

Yet beneath the appeal of long-term AI leases, investors should be aware of how customer concentration and heavy capex could quickly become a problem if AI demand softens or key customers pull back.

Before this update, the most pessimistic analysts still assumed revenue could reach about US$1.8 billion by 2029, yet they highlighted how heavy capex and tenant concentration could backfire, underscoring how sharply opinions about TeraWulf's future can diverge.
