# Jevons Paradox: The Perils of Efficiency

## The Core Concept

Jevons Paradox comes from economist William Stanley Jevons's 1865 work on coal. It holds that efficiency improvements in resource use typically *increase* total consumption rather than reduce it — because lower costs justify new and expanded uses.

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella invoked the concept in January 2025 during the DeepSeek AI moment to calm market fears about AI cost reductions leading to reduced infrastructure spending. His point: cheaper AI means more AI usage, not less demand.

## Personal Applications

- Smartphones → 10x more emails sent
- Video calls → more meetings than ever
- AI tools → more job applications, fewer interviews

## Two Root Causes of the Trap

1. **Internal** – High achievers fill freed time with more work to avoid the guilt of rest
2. **External** – Demonstrated efficiency signals others that your time is now cheaper to consume, as Oliver Burkeman noted: *"people who transform themselves into 26-lane highways of productivity often find they're no less busy."*

## Bloom's Recommended Audit

- List 3-5 areas of recent efficiency gains
- Ask whether efficiency *created* or *consumed* time, and whether that was intentional
- For internal issues: pre-assign saved time before you save it
- For external issues: stop broadcasting the efficiency signal (email delays, phone lockboxes)

## Closing Thought

Efficiency only has value when it delivers more of what you actually want.
