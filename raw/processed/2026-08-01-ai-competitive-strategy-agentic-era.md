# AI Strategy is Business Strategy, Part 4: Competitive Strategy in the Agentic Era

For three decades, competitive strategy in technology-intensive industries followed a familiar pattern: scale conferred advantage, and fast followers could study leaders, replicate what worked, and enter markets at lower cost. Agentic AI is breaking that pattern — the source of competitive advantage is shifting from scale and access to learning velocity and orchestration capability, and competitive dynamics are shifting from linear to compounding.

**The learning flywheel as competitive moat.** Bain describes a "learning flywheel": data improves agents, agents improve people, people redesign work, and redesigned work generates better data. Because it compounds, the gap between leaders and followers gets structurally harder to close every quarter. As frontier AI models commoditize, proprietary operational data — generated only through real operational experience — becomes the durable differentiator; purchased data creates parity, but proprietary data creates advantage.

**The compounding divergence.** BCG found that "future-built" companies (roughly 5% of the global total) achieve 1.7x revenue growth, 3.6x three-year total shareholder return, and 1.6x EBIT margin versus AI laggards, while planning to spend more than twice as much on AI. Accenture found organizations with the greatest AI maturity growing 4.7x faster year-over-year than the least mature. McKinsey found the maturity gap between top and bottom performers jumped 60% between 2016-2019 and 2020-2022, and Bain's 2025 Technology Report found AI leaders delivering 10-25% EBITDA improvements while most organizations remain stuck in experimentation.

**Four first-mover advantages:** data advantage (proprietary operational data that can't be acquired by non-adopters), learning-curve advantage (institutional knowledge of orchestrating AI agents that can't be bought or quickly replicated), talent advantage (AI talent gravitates to serious AI deployers, and a reported 62% AI-skills wage premium with a 3.2-to-1 demand-to-supply ratio makes this zero-sum), and forgiveness advantage (early movers get more stakeholder tolerance for mistakes; that window is narrowing as AI-powered experiences become the norm).

**Market restructuring.** Gartner predicts that by 2028, 90% of B2B buying will be intermediated by AI agents, routing more than $15 trillion through machine-to-machine exchanges, with one in four enterprise software purchases made by agents with no human in the loop — favoring sellers with machine-readable catalogs and data over those relying on relationship-based selling. In SaaS, Bessemer Venture Partners identifies "Supernova" AI-native startups reaching $40M ARR in year one and $125M by year two; Bloomberg estimates subscription-based software pricing could fall from 60% to 30% of models over the next decade as outcome-based pricing rises from 10% to 60%. Median public SaaS companies trade at 3.4x revenue (a decade-plus low) while AI-native companies command 25-50x revenue multiples in private rounds.

**What's being commoditized vs. what stays defensible.** Commoditizing: routine processing, standard information synthesis, basic analysis, scripted customer interactions, template content, rules-based decisions. Defensible: proprietary data assets, deep domain expertise embedded in process and culture, trust-based customer relationships, regulatory licenses, creative/strategic judgment, and orchestration capability connecting agents, people and workflows into coherent systems.

**The fast-follower myth.** Unlike prior tech waves (e.g., cloud, where waiting two years to adopt was low-risk), AI's compounding dynamics mean the gap between early movers and followers widens rather than narrows over time — because data advantages are cumulative, organizational learning doesn't transfer via case studies or consulting, and talent flows toward leaders. The article argues "wait and see" is a high-risk strategy with compounding costs, not a risk-neutral one.

**Strategy playbook highlights:** score your organization against competitors on production deployment breadth, data asset depth, orchestration sophistication, talent density, and learning velocity (1-5 scale); audit your value chain to separate defensible activities from commoditizing ones; run an "agentic arbitrage exposure audit" asking whether purchasing agents could evaluate your offering without human interaction, whether an AI agent could replicate your core value, and whether your pricing model (seat-based vs. outcome-based) survives agent-mediated procurement; then choose to lead, fast-follow, or leapfrog for each market segment based on where you hold a genuine structural advantage.
