# GOOG Stock Reverses $300 Recovery as Google Gemini Launch and Anthropic Deal Raise Spending Questions

Alphabet shares have surrendered their recovery above $300, falling toward $290 despite new Gemini products and a major Anthropic investment, as investors question whether Google's expanding computing ambitions can generate sufficient returns to justify rising infrastructure costs.

## Quick overview

* Alphabet shares have fallen below $300, heading toward $290, raising concerns about the sustainability of its recovery.
* Google has launched new Gemini products and committed $10 billion to Anthropic, but investors are wary of rising infrastructure costs.
* Despite strong revenue growth in Google Cloud, Alphabet's earnings missed expectations, prompting questions about future profitability.
* The company's significant capital expenditures are a growing concern for shareholders, as they seek evidence of sustainable returns.

## Alphabet Stock Reverses Below $300

Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOG, GOOGL) shares have reversed lower after recovering above $300 following October's earnings-driven sell-off. The stock has now fallen back below this important psychological level and is heading toward $290, a negative technical development that raises the possibility that $300 has turned into resistance.

The recovery had been supported by improving sentiment across technology stocks following the U.S. midterm elections, alongside optimism surrounding Google Cloud and the company's expanding Gemini ecosystem. However, investors are increasingly focused on the cost of maintaining Google's competitive position, particularly as data-centre spending and financial commitments continue to grow.

## Gemini 4 Hydrogen and $395 Smart Glasses Launch

Google has introduced Gemini 4 Hydrogen as its new everyday model, claiming that it completes tasks approximately 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash. Its Terminal-Bench 4.1 score reportedly increased to 57%, compared with 18.2% for the previous model.

Hydrogen is available through the Gemini API, Gemini app and Google Search, with pricing set at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens across a one-million-token context window.

Google has also opened preorders for Gemini-powered smart glasses developed with Samsung, Warby Parker and Gentle Monster. Prices start at $395 for Warby Parker frames and $449 for Gentle Monster models, with shipments scheduled for December 10. The glasses feature a 12-megapixel camera, support real-time translation in more than 70 languages and offer approximately nine hours of battery life.

These launches expand Google's consumer and developer ecosystem, but the commercial challenge remains turning greater adoption into revenue that justifies the additional computing requirements.

## Google Commits Another $10 Billion to Anthropic

Google's financial relationship with Anthropic is also expanding. According to an amended filing, Google and Nvidia have agreed to purchase approximately $10 billion each in Anthropic shares as part of a planned $26 billion private placement involving seven buyers.

Anthropic plans to offer 825 million shares at between $70 and $78 each, with a proposed Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT. Google's allocation could amount to approximately 135 million shares at the midpoint price of $74.

The transaction exercises an option under Google's March 2026 financing agreement. The investment does not give Google control of Anthropic: the Class N shares generally carry no voting rights, and Google's ownership is capped at 14.99% under the agreement.

The deal reinforces Google's involvement in the competitive computing market, but it also highlights the scale of capital being committed to maintaining its position.

## Google Cloud Growth Overshadowed by Earnings Miss

Alphabet's third-quarter results showed strong operational growth but disappointed on earnings. Earnings per share came in at $2.12, below analyst expectations of $3.02, while revenue increased 25% year over year to $127.6 billion.

Operating income climbed 30% to $40.6 billion. Google Cloud was the standout performer, generating $30.7 billion in revenue, up 103%, while operating income surged to $11.1 billion from $3.6 billion a year earlier.

Despite these figures, investors are questioning how much additional investment will be required to sustain this expansion and whether profitability can keep pace with infrastructure costs.

## Technical Levels in Focus

From a chart perspective, Alphabet stock printed a new high in May this year, but it has reversed lower and has slipped below its 50-week moving average (yellow) near $320 which was acting as support for some time. Last week we saw a break below $300, however the 100 SMA (green) acted as support last week and we saw a rebound to $305 this week, however the price has returned below the $300 level today.

## $200 Billion Spending Forecast Raises Concerns

Alphabet's third-quarter capital expenditure reached approximately $51.4 billion, compared with $24 billion a year earlier. Year-to-date spending totalled around $132 billion as the company expanded its data centres, servers and networking infrastructure.

Alphabet previously indicated 2026 capital expenditure of $175 billion to $185 billion. However, some external estimates suggest spending could approach $200 billion this year and potentially reach $300 billion in 2027. These projections are not confirmed company guidance.

The growing investment burden is becoming a central concern for shareholders. Rapid cloud growth and expanding Gemini usage are positive, but investors want evidence that revenue and operating cash flow can justify the scale of spending.

Political opposition to data-centre expansion adds another risk. Communities are increasingly concerned about electricity prices, water consumption and pressure on local power grids. Greater scrutiny could delay projects or increase infrastructure obligations, although tighter spending discipline could ultimately support free cash flow.

## Alphabet Stock Outlook: $300 Turns Into Resistance

Alphabet's latest reversal below $300 weakens the recovery and puts the $290 area in focus. A sustained move lower could deepen the correction, while buyers would need to reclaim $300 to improve the technical picture.

The company's cloud growth, Gemini expansion and Anthropic investment demonstrate its ambitions, but the market is demanding more than growth announcements. Until Alphabet shows that its massive infrastructure commitments can deliver sustainable returns, free cash flow and capital allocation will remain central to the stock's valuation.
