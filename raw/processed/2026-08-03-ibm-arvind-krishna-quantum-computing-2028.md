# IBM's Arvind Krishna Predicts Quantum Computing Will Become a Meaningful Business by 2028

IBM CEO Arvind Krishna said quantum computing could begin making a measurable contribution to the company's revenue and profit by 2028 or 2029. Speaking on CNBC's "Mad Money," Krishna said investors may not have to wait much longer for quantum computing to become a meaningful business for IBM: "I think that in 2028 or 2029, you'll see it have a measurable impact on our top line and bottom line." He added that "by the end of the 2030s, we are now pretty convinced this is a trillion dollars of value."

IBM and quantum startup Algorithmiq on Thursday unveiled research they said demonstrated quantum advantage — the ability of a quantum computer to solve certain problems more efficiently than leading classical systems — and validated the results. Krishna said IBM's quantum technology revealed previously unobserved material behaviors that conventional computing had been unable to capture, with potential applications in battery technology, new materials, fusion energy, and drug development.

Quantum computing still faces significant obstacles, including error rates, hardware complexity, and the challenge of scaling systems for broad commercial use. IBM is nonetheless increasing its investment: in May the company announced plans to build a standalone quantum chip foundry backed by a $1 billion commitment from the U.S. Department of Commerce through CHIPS incentives, matched by another $1 billion from IBM. IBM is competing with Alphabet and Rigetti Computing to bring quantum technology into commercial use.

Krishna also addressed IBM's worst single-day stock decline on record, when shares fell 25% on July 14 after the company said some customers had postponed capital spending projects. He said the delayed deals had not been abandoned: "about 40% of them have already closed in three to four weeks... that's a good signal that it is a deferral, not a destruction." IBM posted second-quarter revenue of $17.16 billion, surpassing the consensus estimate of $16.86 billion, with adjusted earnings of $2.93 per share versus expectations of $2.86.
