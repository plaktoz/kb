# The Top Time Management Statistics in 2026 for Workplace Productivity

U.S. companies lose $468B annually to productivity loss, with employees spending 51% of their workday on low-value tasks — making structured time management a business-critical priority.

Notification overload, excessive meetings, and repetitive tasks cause employee burnout and disengagement.

Time management tools give leaders the visibility to identify where productivity breaks down and support team members in managing their time more effectively.

We all have the same hours in the day. But when employees struggle to focus, those hours likely won’t be used effectively.

Without proper workplace time management, teams get stuck playing catch-up. Communication breaks down. Deadlines get pushed. It’s a recipe for burnout and missed opportunities.

These time management statistics shed light on what keeps employees from being their most productive and offers insights into what leaders can do to increase efficiency for distributed teams.

## What is time management?

Time management is the practice of prioritizing tasks and planning time to be as efficient as possible during working hours. Poor time management can lead to employee burnout, low-quality work, and higher operating costs. Organizations with strong time management techniques reduce costs and keep employees motivated.

## Top 10 time management statistics for 2026

U.S. companies could gain an additional $468 billion a year by reducing how much focus time is lost due to distractions (Economist Impact).

In 2025, only 20% of employees worldwide were engaged in their work—the lowest rate since 2020 (Gallup).

69% of managers say that hybrid or remote work increases employee productivity (Owl Labs).

Employees estimate that they lose 51% of their average workday to low-value tasks (Talker Research).

80% of global knowledge workers don’t feel like they have enough time or energy to get everything done (Microsoft).

85% of employees cite repetitive tasks as a top cause of burnout (Talker Research).

60% of employees have a hard time focusing on work because of how many notifications they get (Grammarly).

Business leaders say that better communication increases productivity by 64% (Grammarly).

Employees have about 25 meetings every month on average (Hubstaff).

The average employee spends about 39% of their time in deep focus (Hubstaff).

## How do teams manage their time?

There are plenty of time management practices, from time blocking to the Eisenhower Matrix to SMART goals. Still, many employees use basic time management tools such as to-do lists, calendars, or email inboxes to plan their day.

Many companies are opting for a time tracking or productivity measurement tool. These platforms help managers see how employees spend their time so they can improve capacity planning, understand performance, and clear up inefficiencies.

## What prevents effective time management?

Many employees feel stretched for time without enough hours to perform tasks. Here are some of the top issues that get in the way of good time management.

## Notification overload

60% of employees report getting so many notifications that it’s hard to concentrate on work. That’s understandable given that work is interrupted every two minutes by a meeting, message, or notification. Employees get the most messages around 11am—which also happens to be when many people are at their most productive.

With all these notifications popping up, employees often find themselves multitasking or switching between tasks, which disrupts focus and eats up time. Switching between tasks, for instance, can take up to 40% of an employee’s day.

## Communication problems

Just sending and receiving messages takes up a good chunk of time, with knowledge workers spending 88% of their work week on communication. Teams are also communicating across more platforms—i.e., Slack, Teams, email, Asana—but that communication isn’t always effective.

According to a 2025 report from Grammarly, more than 80% of employees say that communication issues at work reduce productivity and increase stress. The opposite—strong communication—saves employees an estimated 21 hours a week.

## Excessive meetings

Meetings take up a significant amount of the workday. The average company has almost six times as many meetings as they did two years ago, with individual employees averaging about 25 meetings a month.

Unfortunately, these meetings often aren’t the best use of employee time. Employees view 58% of meetings as unwarranted, and 66% don’t have a clear sense of next steps. Plus, 77% of workers say that time is wasted due to technical difficulties during meetings, and 60% of meetings are impromptu instead of scheduled ahead of time.

It’s no wonder that 87% of employees dread meetings.

To top it all off, 50% of meetings take place either between 9am to 11am or 1pm to 3pm—right when people are naturally at their most productive.

When meetings impact productivity, businesses lose money. According to a global study by Jabra, excessive meetings can cost businesses over $130 million a year. Laxis puts the cost of unnecessary meetings at $399 billion a year for US companies. Regardless, the takeaway is clear: unproductive meetings are costly.

## After-hours work

After-hours activity is up: 71% of employees feel like they have to respond to emails outside of working hours, while meetings after 8pm have increased 16% year over year. It’s becoming more common to work 50+ hour weeks, especially for managers, sales, and support roles.

## Irrelevant tasks

One of the biggest productivity drains is tasks that don’t produce meaningful results. “Work about work”—such as meetings, information finding, and updates—can account for 20% of employee time. According to Grammarly, that means 8+ hours a week are spent on tasks that don’t drive real value.

## How poor time management affects teams

For employees, poor time management increases stress while decreasing engagement:

32% of employees have participated in “quiet quitting”— a practice where a disengaged employee does only the bare minimum to cope with strain at work.

85% of employees report that repetitive tasks lead to burnout.

Only 10% are fully invested in internal communications.

Just one out of four employees feel truly appreciated at work.

When employees aren’t engaged, the company suffers, too; in 2025, low employee engagement cost the world economy approximately $10 trillion in lost productivity.

## Does time management work?

Yes, time management practices work to improve productivity.

A 2025 research study used a time management scoring system to look at the connection between time management and productivity. It found a clear pattern: participants with higher time management scores were more productive. Similarly, a 2021 meta-analysis of nearly 54,000 people notes that time management practices moderately improve job performance, sense of well-being, and academic achievements.

## Benefits of time management in the workplace

When employees have enough time to get everything done, the quality of work increases and engagement goes up. Employees enjoy better work-life balance and are less likely to become burned out or unhappy with their jobs.

For businesses, this means better progress towards goals, improved employee retention, and less internal friction. Economist Impact found that better productivity could earn U.S. companies an additional $468 billion a year.

## How to improve employee time management

Modern teams face a difficult dilemma: 53% of leaders want to increase productivity, but 80% of employees don’t think they have the time or energy to get everything done.

While individual team members are responsible for organizing their own time, managers also play a big role in setting up their employees for success. Here are some time management strategies leaders can use to increase efficiency:

## Communicate clearly

Business leaders report that effective communication increases productivity 64%, and that starts with leadership. Be transparent about goals, deadlines, expectations, and strategies. At the same time, be mindful about the amount of communications being sent out; minimize excessive pings and encourage employees to turn off notifications after hours or during deep focus time.

## Reduce meetings

While meetings are inevitable and some roles have to be in frequent meetings, it’s best to be thoughtful about how many meetings are being scheduled. Here are some meeting best practices to keep in mind:

Track meeting time vs. focus time to minimize excessive or unproductive meetings.

Consider whether a meeting is really needed—maybe it could indeed be an email.

Mark attendance as optional for employees who don’t have to attend.

Consider how time zones overlap when scheduling to make sure meetings don’t interrupt employees during their most productive hours.

Come prepared with an agenda to avoid wasting time.

Ensure meetings end on time and with clear action items.

Share a recap, recording, or transcript after the meeting so that no one forgets what was discussed.

## Integrate AI thoughtfully

Many organizations are implementing AI tools to improve productivity, with 85% of professionals using AI to some degree. There is a clear connection to productivity: 77% of employees say AI helps them complete tasks faster while 70% say it makes it easier to focus.

The key is to implement AI tools intentionally, with defined goals and clear training on use. With distributed teams already using so many tools and platforms, adding too many new tools without a clear purpose could cause more overwhelm.

## Use time tracking software

Time tracking software with productivity metrics makes it easy to get an accurate picture of how employees are spending their time. But the best time tracking platforms go beyond simple time tracking to provide deeper insights into activity levels and productivity patterns.

For example, Hubstaff is a time tracking platform that captures productivity metrics such as app and URL usage, idle time, and unusual activity. This data helps managers make better decisions around capacity planning, flag issues proactively, and understand employee performance.

When implementing time tracking, be transparent with employees about its purpose and how privacy will be respected. 85% of employees say that employers should tell them if they are using a tracking tool.

## Focus on focus time

Most employees only get about 2-3 hours a day of “deep work”—that is, uninterrupted time spent on tasks that require concentration. Deep work is usually when employees get the most done, so focus time is one of the most important Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) leaders should pay attention to. You can calculate a blended productivity score by looking at focus time and time spent on core tasks.

When tracking focus time, keep in mind that different roles need different amounts of focus time. For a project manager, just 20% of focus time might be enough, but an analyst might need 40%.

## Track productivity metrics

Tracking productivity metrics helps leaders spot when employees are struggling to manage their time. This gives them the opportunity to step in early, providing coaching to get their time management back on track.

On the flip side, productivity measurement shows when employees are highly efficient. In 2026, Gallup found that only 29% of employees in the US reported getting praise in the last week. Keep teams feeling engaged and motivated by recognizing productive employees.

## Be flexible with schedules

63% of employees want their employers to offer more flexible options for managing their time between work and personal responsibilities. Give employees options (rather than limiting them to a traditional 9-5 schedule) so they can adjust their workday to match the needs of their role and their personal preferences. For example, flexible hours allow employees who feel more productive in the evening to take a longer afternoon break and then finish work at night.

## Better time management starts with better visibility

Looking at time management statistics paints a clear picture of the modern global workforce: with teams more distracted and overwhelmed than ever, focus time has become a precious commodity. Leaders can support their employees by creating clear systems that make it easier for employees to do their best work.

Using the right tools is critical, too. A time tracking and productivity monitoring platform empowers leaders with insights into where productivity breaks down and which employees are using their time most efficiently. With this data, they can optimize processes and offer support to ensure every team member can manage their time most effectively.
