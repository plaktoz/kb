Alphabet (GOOGL, GOOG) shares have reversed below $300 and fallen toward $285 despite new Gemini products, strong Google Cloud growth and another $10 billion Anthropic investment, as investors question whether rising infrastructure costs can generate sufficient returns.

#### Alphabet Stock Reverses Below $300

Alphabet shares have surrendered their recovery above $300 following October’s earnings-driven sell-off, falling below $290 and toward $285. The reversal raises the possibility that $300 has become resistance, weakening the stock’s technical recovery.

Despite improving sentiment across technology stocks following the U.S. midterm elections, investors are increasingly focused on Google’s expanding infrastructure commitments and the profitability of its investments.

Regulatory scrutiny remains another headwind. Alphabet continues to face antitrust pressure surrounding its search dominance, digital advertising business and distribution agreements. Potential restrictions on default search agreements across consumer devices and browsers could threaten profitable revenue streams, adding uncertainty to the company’s valuation.

However, capital expenditure is becoming an equally important concern. Alphabet is expanding its data-centre footprint, developing custom silicon and investing in computing capacity to compete against other major cloud providers. Although these investments are intended to protect its search business and accelerate Google Cloud growth, shareholders are becoming increasingly sensitive to operating margin pressure and declining free cash flow.

#### Gemini 4 Hydrogen and $395 Smart Glasses Launch

Google continues expanding its Gemini ecosystem with new developer and consumer products.

The company has introduced Gemini 4 Hydrogen as its new everyday model, claiming it completes tasks approximately 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash. Its reported Terminal-Bench 4.1 score increased to 57%, compared with 18.2% for its predecessor.

Hydrogen is available through the Gemini API, Gemini app and Google Search, with pricing of $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens.

Google has also opened preorders for Gemini-powered smart glasses developed with Samsung, Warby Parker and Gentle Monster. Prices start at $395 and $449, respectively, with shipments scheduled for December 10. Features include a 12-megapixel camera, real-time translation in more than 70 languages and approximately nine hours of battery life.

These launches strengthen Google’s consumer and developer ecosystem, but the challenge remains converting adoption into revenue that justifies the additional computing requirements.

#### Google Commits Another $10 Billion to Anthropic

Alphabet’s financial relationship with Anthropic is also expanding. According to an amended filing, Google and Nvidia have agreed to purchase approximately $10 billion each in Anthropic shares as part of a planned $26 billion private placement involving seven buyers.

Anthropic plans to offer 825 million shares at between $70 and $78 each, with a proposed Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT. Google’s allocation could reach approximately 135 million shares at the midpoint price of $74.

The transaction exercises an option under Google’s March 2026 financing agreement. However, the investment does not give Google control of Anthropic. The Class N shares generally carry no voting rights, and Google’s ownership is capped at 14.99%.

The deal reinforces Google’s exposure to the computing market but also highlights the enormous financial commitments involved in maintaining its competitive position.

#### Technical Levels in Focus

From a chart perspective, Alphabet stock printed a new high in May this year, but it has reversed lower and has slipped below its 50-week moving average (yellow) near $320 which was acting as support for some time. Last week we saw a break below $300, however the 100 SMA (green) acted as support last week and we saw a rebound to $305 this week, however the price has returned below the $290 level today.

#### Google Cloud Growth Overshadowed by Earnings Miss

Alphabet’s third-quarter results showed strong operational growth but disappointed on earnings. Earnings per share reached $2.12, below analyst expectations of $3.02, while revenue increased 25% year over year to $127.6 billion. Operating income climbed 30% to $40.6 billion.

Google Cloud was the standout performer, generating $30.7 billion in revenue, up 103%. Operating income surged to $11.1 billion from $3.6 billion a year earlier.

Despite these figures, investors are questioning whether profitability can continue growing fast enough to offset the cost of expanding infrastructure.

#### $200 Billion Spending Forecast Raises Concerns

Alphabet’s third-quarter capital expenditure reached approximately $51.4 billion, compared with $24 billion a year earlier. Year-to-date spending totalled around $132 billion as the company expanded its data centres, servers and networking infrastructure.

Alphabet previously indicated 2026 capital expenditure of $175 billion to $185 billion. However, external estimates suggest spending could approach $200 billion this year and potentially reach $300 billion in 2027. These remain projections rather than confirmed company guidance.

Political opposition to data-centre expansion adds another risk, with communities increasingly concerned about electricity prices, water consumption and pressure on local power grids. Greater scrutiny could delay projects or increase infrastructure obligations.

#### Alphabet Stock Outlook: $300 Turns Into Resistance

Alphabet’s latest reversal below $300 weakens the recovery and brings the $285–$290 area into focus. A sustained move lower could deepen the correction, while buyers would need to reclaim $300 to improve the technical outlook.

Google’s cloud growth, Gemini expansion and Anthropic investment demonstrate its ambitions, but investors are demanding evidence that these commitments can generate sustainable returns.

Until Alphabet demonstrates that its infrastructure spending can translate into stronger profitability and free cash flow, rising capital requirements and regulatory uncertainty could continue limiting the stock’s upside.
