# Educators Are Racing to Shape AI Before It Shapes Education

Newswise — PHILADELPHIA, PA — [October 6, 2026] — As artificial intelligence rapidly reshapes teaching and learning, educators, schools, and policymakers are grappling with how to harness its benefits while protecting students and preserving what matters most about education. To help answer those questions, the University of Pennsylvania Graduate School of Education (Penn GSE) is launching the Penn GSE Living Laboratory for Human Agency and AI (Living Lab).

Building on Penn GSE's leadership in AI and education, the initiative brings together researchers, educators, school systems, and industry partners to develop approaches to AI in real-world educational settings. Designed as a network rather than a standalone center, the Living Lab will serve as a hub for rapid evidence-based innovation and collaboration on the future of learning–-while helping educators and policymakers address emerging questions about the safe and responsible use of AI in education.

"Artificial intelligence presents tremendous opportunities for learning, but it also raises important questions about safety, trust, and how these tools should be used," said Katharine O. Strunk, Dean of Penn GSE and George and Diane Weiss Professor of Education. "Too often, education systems and educators are asked to respond to technological change after the fact. The education community has a critical role to play in determining how artificial intelligence develops and how it is used. The decisions being made today will influence how students learn, how teachers work, and how opportunity is distributed for years to come."

## A Vision for the Future of Learning

"Educators are experimenting with AI every day, often without clear guidance about what works, for whom, and under what conditions," said Associate Professor Bodong Chen, Director of the Knowledge Building Innovation Network at Penn GSE and founding Co-Director of the Living Lab. "Schools need more than predictions about what AI might do. We want educators and students to be the designers of AI in learning, not just its users, and to build the evidence of what their designs make possible. That is how we keep learning human."

The Living Lab focuses on five priorities:

- **People First:** Ensuring AI enhances human agency and learning.
- **Trust, Safety, and Ethics:** Advancing responsible AI practices that protect privacy, security, and learner well-being.
- **Practice-Driven Innovation:** Co-designing and testing AI tools in authentic educational settings.
- **AI Literacy and Pedagogy:** Advancing effective and trustworthy AI use among educators and students.
- **Infrastructure for Scale:** Building the partnerships and systems needed to sustain innovation.

## Driving Impact Through Collaboration and Innovation

The new hub builds on Penn GSE's growing portfolio of AI-related teaching, research, and innovation, creating an interdisciplinary foundation for the initiative. Across the School, faculty are developing and studying approaches that put educators and learners at the center of how AI is designed, used, and evaluated. These efforts include CORAL, Shiyan Jiang's project that enables young children to design humanoid robots and see themselves as people who not only understand AI but can shape it; CraftPad, Bodong Chen's AI-assisted workspace that supports teachers' lesson design while centering their professional expertise and judgment; and Yasmin Kafai's algorithm auditing project (in partnership with Penn Engineering), which teaches high school students to investigate bias in AI systems. The School is also home to the Learning Analytics and Artificial Intelligence master's program, which prepares educators and leaders to apply data science, learning analytics, and AI to improve educational outcomes. Through its three-state PASS initiative, Penn GSE is also helping school systems explore how AI and emerging technologies can support student outcomes while addressing questions of governance, equity, and safe deployment.

"For the first time, we have the ability to understand and support learning at a level of scale and personalization that was previously impossible. The challenge is ensuring these advances are grounded in learning sciences and designed to serve learners," said Associate Professor Seiji Isotani, Faculty Director of the Learning Analytics and Artificial Intelligence Program and founding Co-Director of the Living Lab. "By combining expertise in AI, learning analytics, and learning sciences, we can help unlock that potential while addressing the challenges that come with it."

Alongside Penn AI and the University's strengths in medicine, engineering, business, and the social sciences, Penn GSE is helping ensure that education plays a central role in shaping the future of artificial intelligence. The Living Lab helps translate emerging research into practical tools, partnerships, and insights for schools.

## Investing in Real-World Solutions

Through three years of philanthropic support, the Living Lab will help launch new research, deepen partnerships with schools, and create opportunities for students to work on pressing questions. Potential projects could include evaluating AI tutoring tools, developing resources to help educators use AI responsibly, studying the impact of AI on teaching and learning, or testing strategies to improve AI literacy among students and teachers.

In addition, the Living Laboratory will host a Google Public Sector-supported speaker series, bringing leading experts from industry, education, and the nonprofit sector to campus to share and interrogate emerging ideas and best practices. While these opportunities will focus primarily on Penn GSE students, they will also be open to students across the University of Pennsylvania, reflecting the Living Laboratory's commitment to interdisciplinary collaboration and broad engagement.
