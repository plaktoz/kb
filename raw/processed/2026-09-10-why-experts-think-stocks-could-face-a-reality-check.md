# Why Some Experts Think Stocks Could Face a Reality Check

Despite the Dow Jones hitting an all-time record close of 54,085.88 on Aug. 4, several financial experts are warning that a market correction may be approaching.

## Bond Market Troubles

University of Michigan finance professor Paolo Pasquariello believes bond market stress signals problems for equities. He cites rising interest rates driven by the ongoing Iran conflict, tariff-fueled inflation, and AI infrastructure spending competing for debt capital. He warns of a "double whammy" — rising rates combined with falling business cash flow.

## National Debt

Loyola Marymount professor Sung Won Sohn notes U.S. debt crossed $40 trillion in August 2026, growing ~$2 trillion annually. He warns: "Heavy Treasury issuance, budget battles and no credible fiscal plan lead investors to demand higher yields." Treasury yields above 5% make stocks comparatively less attractive.

## Fed Outlook

- Sohn: Fed will hold rates steady in September due to soft job market and slowing growth
- KPMG's Yelena Maleyev: Expects two quarter-point hikes — one in September, one in December
- Fed Chair Kevin Warsh signaled at Jackson Hole that summer inflation improvements don't reflect meaningful underlying progress, reaffirming a "firm, fixed" 2% PCE target

## Investor Guidance

Morningstar's Christine Benz recommends:
- Under 50: Maintain broad stock exposure with an emergency fund outside retirement accounts
- Over 50: Shift meaningfully toward fixed income as a buffer against equity downturns
- Use a "bucket strategy" — cash for 1–2 years of spending, bonds for 5–8 years, remainder in stocks
- Consider TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities) as an inflation hedge
- Avoid high-yield or emerging market bond funds; favor short-to-intermediate high-quality bonds

Benz cautions that stagflation — sluggish growth paired with persistent inflation — could hurt both stocks and bonds simultaneously.
