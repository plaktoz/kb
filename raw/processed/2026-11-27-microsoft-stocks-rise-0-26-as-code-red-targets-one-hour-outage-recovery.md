# Microsoft Stocks Rise 0.26% as Code Red Targets One-Hour Outage Recovery

Microsoft (NASDAQ:MSFT) shares rose 0.26% to $491.18 at 10:55 a.m. ET on Friday, November 27, in the shortened regular session, as investors weighed an internal Code Red push to fix Microsoft 365 reliability. Memos reviewed by Reuters show the company wants to resolve future global incidents within an hour, a goal Microsoft 365 Core president Jason Henderson acknowledged it does not yet meet.

The trigger was a nearly 24-hour outage on November 12 and 13 that knocked out Exchange Online, Outlook, Teams and Copilot. Microsoft's preliminary review traced it to a July fix for networking gateway servers and lock contention in an internal Windows process, leaving more than 80% of servers unresponsive at the worst point. Outage reports peaked at about 130,000 across 74 countries, TweakTown reported, and Satya Nadella apologized.

The GuruFocus chart shows a perfect GF Score of 100/100, with elite profitability, a strong balance sheet and top-tier momentum. The GF Value of $606.32 implies a 19.0% discount, which GuruFocus rates modestly undervalued, so the market is not treating the outage as lasting damage. The commercial risk is real, though: Copilot is sold on top of Microsoft 365, and every hour of downtime weakens the case for charging enterprises more per seat. The next test is Microsoft's final post-incident report and whether commercial bookings hold up next quarter.
