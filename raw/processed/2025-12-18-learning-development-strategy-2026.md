# How to create a learning and development strategy for 2026

A learning and development (L&D) strategy is a simple plan for how knowledge will move through an organization, connecting employee growth to business goals so learning supports the work rather than sitting on the sidelines. Without one, training becomes reactive — someone requests a course, someone builds it, and no one checks whether it supports business strategy.

## Why you need one

Opportunities to learn and grow are the top driver of retention and talent attraction, per LinkedIn's Workplace Learning Report. IBM's Global Skills Study found more than 45% of organizations say skill gaps prevent them from achieving business goals. A TalentLMS survey found 66% of employees say they need new skills to stay effective in their roles.

## Seven steps to build a strategy

1. **Identify business goals**: Start with what the company needs to achieve, not with learning — ask what outcomes matter most and which skill gaps slow the work today.
2. **Define key stakeholders**: Involve leadership, managers, employees and HR/talent development owners; short conversations are usually enough for smaller teams.
3. **Conduct a skills gap analysis**: Use manager insights, employee feedback, performance data and a knowledge audit to find where work slows or knowledge is inconsistent.
4. **Set L&D goals with KPIs**: Use SMART goals tied to real outcomes — e.g. "cut onboarding time from 3 months to 6 weeks" rather than "offer more training." Track engagement, knowledge and business metrics.
5. **Develop learning plans and experiences**: Pick formats that match how the team already works — short digital lessons, scenario practice, peer coaching — and share training where people already work.
6. **Choose tools**: Look for ease of use, speed, flexibility, predictable cost and analytics.
7. **Write the strategy to share**: Treat it as a working guide, not a document that sits unread — an executive summary, business alignment, skill gaps, KPIs, initiatives, timeline, roles and budget.

A strong L&D strategy transforms scattered learning into something purposeful, predictable and tied directly to organizational success.
