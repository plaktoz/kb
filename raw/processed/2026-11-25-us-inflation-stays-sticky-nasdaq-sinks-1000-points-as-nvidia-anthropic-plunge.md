# US inflation stays sticky, Nasdaq sinks 1,000 points as Nvidia, Anthropic plunge

The U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) has released the October PCE price index data. This would be the last major inflation report with complete year-over-year data before the Federal Reserve's FOMC meeting scheduled for December 8-9.

From the preceding month, the PCE price index for October increased 0.3 percent. Excluding food and energy, the PCE price index also increased 0.3 percent.

From the same month one year ago, the PCE price index for October increased 3.8 percent. Excluding food and energy, the PCE price index increased 3.3 percent from one year ago.

The US PCE price index increased 0.3% month-over-month in October 2026, as expected, following a 0.5% rise in September which marked the biggest gain in six months.

The core PCE price index, which excludes food and energy, also rose 0.3%, the same as in the previous month and in line with market expectations.

On an annual basis, headline PCE inflation accelerated to 3.8% from 3.6% and the core rate edged up to 3.3% from 3.1%, above forecasts of 3.2%.

The personal consumption expenditures price index is the primary inflation gauge for the Fed and has been above the 2% target since 2021.

The US core Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index, the Federal Reserve's preferred measure of underlying inflation, rose 0.3% month over month in October 2026, matching both September's increase and market expectations.

On an annual basis, core PCE inflation accelerated to 3.3% from 3.1% in the previous month, slightly above market forecasts of 3.2% and marking its highest level since November 2023. The reading remains well above the Fed's 2% target, reinforcing concerns that rising energy costs could be contributing to broader inflationary pressures.

The US economy expanded an annualized 4.3% in Q3 2026, much higher than the 3.4% reported in the advance estimate, and marking the fastest growth rate in three years. The contributors to the increase were investment, consumer spending, and government spending that were partly offset by a decrease in exports. Imports also increased.

## US Markets

Wall Street opened in the red on Wednesday. The Dow Jones Industrial Average is down over 800 points, and the S&P 500 fell nearly 2.5%. Technology stocks have been badly hit. The Nasdaq is down nearly 900 points, or over 3.76%, just after the opening bell.

The pressure continues on the two key AI stocks, Nvidia and Anthropic. On November 25, Anthropic opened at around $75, down more than 8%, while Nvidia was near $142, down almost 9%. Nvidia has lost nearly 34% over the past month and is down 21% over the last 12 months.

Meanwhile, the US has proposed new tariffs on 14 trading partners, including India, China, Japan and the European Union, as President Donald Trump continues his push for higher import duties.

The new tariffs will not be added to existing duties, except for China. Chinese goods will face an additional 20% tariff on top of the existing 25% duty imposed in 2018. This could rise to 40% if China fails to honour its trade agreement with the US.

The tariff proposal could put pressure on global markets as investors worry about trade tensions and higher costs for businesses.

## All eyes on US Fed

Inflation remains sticky in the US. It seems the Fed now has no choice but to raise U.S. interest rates. Markets are currently assigning roughly a 97.8% probability to a 25-basis-point rate increase in December, and also anticipate another hike in January. That will be enough for the AI stocks and the broader market to remain under pressure.

On September 16, the Fed increased the Federal Funds Rate target range by 25 basis points to 3.75%-4.00%. It maintained this rate during the October 28 meeting with a 10-2 vote. A further quarter-point hike in December is expected, raising the range to 4.00%-4.25%.

Hawkish remarks from Fed officials in the last few days have strengthened expectations of additional rate hikes. Signs of a strengthening labour market are also likely to clear the way for the Fed to raise rates again beyond December's anticipated hike, as it tries to bring inflation under control. Tuesday's stronger consumer confidence figures reinforced expectations that policymakers will stay focused on persistent inflation rather than near-term economic weakness.

The dollar index regaining strength could also be a reason for the precious metals to come under pressure again. The US dollar index traded over 104 after briefly slipping to 103.8 on Wednesday.
