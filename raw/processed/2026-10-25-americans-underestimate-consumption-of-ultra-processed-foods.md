# Americans Underestimate Consumption of Ultra-Processed Foods

A recent survey reveals that Americans significantly underestimate their intake of ultra-processed foods, with reality showing these foods make up over half of their diets. Only 9% of respondents could accurately identify ultra-processed items, highlighting a widespread misconception about food processing.

## Survey Highlights Misconception About Food Consumption

Conducted by Talker Research, the survey involved 2,000 participants from the general population and aimed to compare their perceptions of ultra-processed foods with actual dietary trends. Respondents estimated that their diets contain only 16% ultra-processed foods. However, data from the CDC indicate that these foods account for 53% of the average American's diet, revealing a disconnect of more than threefold.

The findings also highlighted a common difficulty in identifying ultra-processed items. While most individuals consume such foods at least once weekly, only 9% correctly recognized commonly purchased items like sliced bread (42%), breakfast cereal (35%), and deli meats (33%) as ultra-processed.

## Financial Implications of Food Choices

The survey also explored the economic aspects of dietary choices. Participants estimated their weekly spending on 'real' foods at $52 and on ultra-processed foods at $40, totaling around $128,149 over a lifetime. However, Dr. Lindsay Allen, a health economist, emphasized that the hidden costs associated with a diet high in ultra-processed foods could exceed $350,000 over a lifetime. These costs include healthcare expenditures and lost productivity, which often go unnoticed by consumers.

As people only consider immediate expenses, they may overlook the long-term health implications of their food choices. Respondents recognized potential future costs, with 34% linking health conditions to ultra-processed food consumption and 21% associating it with reduced work productivity.

## Public Attitudes and Willingness to Change

The survey revealed a growing awareness among consumers regarding ultra-processed foods. Although 71% of participants considered the proportion of ultra-processed items in their diets, 61% felt that avoiding them was nearly impossible due to their prevalence. Interestingly, 42% indicated that knowledge about long-term costs associated with ultra-processed foods might motivate them to reduce their intake.

Additionally, the willingness to invest more in higher-quality groceries was expressed, with 69% stating they would pay nearly $1,200 extra annually to mitigate long-term health risks. However, 28% felt the primary responsibility for reducing these costs lay with food manufacturers, suggesting a demand for greater transparency regarding food processing.

## The CNP Takeaway

Understanding the extent of ultra-processed food consumption can inform healthier choices. While many individuals express a desire to avoid these foods, a lack of awareness and confusion persists. Being mindful of both immediate and long-term costs associated with food choices is crucial for better health outcomes.
