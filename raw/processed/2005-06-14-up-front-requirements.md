# Up-Front Requirements

Shore recounts a project where stakeholders signed off on detailed requirements without truly understanding them. Despite thorough documentation — prototypes, screenshots, prioritized features — the team delivered the wrong product. Critical features like access control and reporting were deprioritized, and one stakeholder expected a web app, not a Windows app.

His diagnosis: most customers lack the ability to mentally simulate software from written descriptions. He compares it to nodding along in a foreign country — "you smile and nod and hope it wasn't important." Stakeholders *appeared* engaged but couldn't genuinely evaluate what they were approving.

His conclusion: up-front requirements processes, however rigorous, have inherent limits. The more reliable path is delivering "real, working, non-prototype software" early, with an explicit expectation of iteration and change.
