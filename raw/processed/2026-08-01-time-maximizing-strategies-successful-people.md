# Time-Maximizing Strategies of Highly-Successful People

"Regardless of location, industry, or occupation, productivity is a challenge faced by every professional," says Bob Pozen, Senior Lecturer at MIT Sloan and author of "Extreme Productivity: Boost Your Results, Reduce Your Hours." Pozen was Executive Chairman of MFS Investment Management and previously President of Fidelity Management & Research Company. Below are tips from Pozen and other highly successful people on managing time well.

## Get your priorities straight

"Most professionals have not taken the time to write down their goals and prioritize them," says Pozen. "Without a specific set of goals to pursue, many ambitious people devote insufficient time to activities that actually support their highest professional priorities." Pozen also notes that time itself is not the best measure of productivity: "The key metric is what you get accomplished, not how many hours you're in [the office]."

## Manage your energy level — and get more sleep

Kat Cole, President of Cinnabon, says she is "pretty religious" about sleep because she is "so much less effective at time management" without it. MIT Sloan neuroscientist Tara Swart notes that 98–99% of people physically require the seven to nine hours of sleep doctors recommend, and testing shows most people lose five to eight IQ points after one bad night's sleep — sleep is an active cleansing process for the brain, not lost productive time.

## Schedule a morning power hour

Author Laura Vanderkam suggests scheduling a "power hour" first thing every morning focused on a single top-priority task, avoiding the email inbox until it's over. She recommends dedicating Monday mornings to bigger, speculative tasks requiring deep thought.

## Do more of what you love

Thomas Davies, a Director at Google for Work, says prioritizing tasks he loves energizes him for the rest of his schedule — when those tasks disappear from his calendar for a week, he knows it's time to build them back in.

## Handle your email deliberately

Pozen recommends checking email only every hour or two and skipping anything clearly unimportant from the subject line, but handling important messages immediately and only once. Tim Ferriss advises checking email just twice a day (e.g., 11 a.m. and 4 p.m.) with an autoresponder set accordingly.

## Make a "To-Don't" list

Jocelyn K. Glei, author of "Unsubscribe," suggests listing six to ten things you commit to not doing because they keep you from your best work — such as sleeping next to your phone or opening email first thing in the morning. Pozen suggests focusing only on tasks that truly require you, and delegating the rest.

## Protect family time

Pozen makes it a priority to be home by 7:00 p.m. for dinner with his family when not traveling, using the dinner table as a hard stop that forces him to shut off work.
