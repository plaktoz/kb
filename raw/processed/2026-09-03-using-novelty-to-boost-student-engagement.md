# 15 Ways to Use Novelty to Boost Student Engagement

Novelty in the classroom must trigger what Professor Susan Engel calls "epistemic curiosity" to genuinely deepen learning. Brain research supports that this curiosity enhances learning and memory retention.

## Retrieval & Assessment Routines

1. **Unfair Game** — Competitive quiz review where point values are revealed only after answering
2. **Diagram Labeling** — Students fill in whited-out labels on diagrams before new content is taught
3. **Blackboard Knowledge Audit** — Silent individual brain dump of everything students know about a topic
4. **Crack the Redaction** — Pairs reconstruct blacked-out key terms in a passage

## Analysis Routines With a Twist

5. **Classroom CSI** — Students act as detectives to find and correct content errors
6. **Spot the Difference** — Compare two nearly identical examples to find distinctions
7. **Slow Reveal** — Progressively disclose a text or image to reshape interpretation
8. **Faulty Rules** — Present incorrect rules for students to identify and rewrite correctly
9. **Cognitive Conflict** — Open with a misconception or startling fact to prompt investigation

## General Engagement Hooks

10. **Genius Questions** — Provocative philosophical questions to refocus flagging energy
11. **Micro-Museum** — Four related items (quote, image, question, detail) students connect to the lesson
12. **Ignite Talks** — Five-minute student presentations using 20 auto-advancing slides
13. **Would You Bet?** — Teams wager play money on predictions, then learn outcomes
14. **Sensory Anchor** — A sound, scent, or object tied to a concept aids recall
15. **Literary Text Blasts** — After-school messages sent from fictional characters via a school-approved app

## The JOLT Framework

Effective novelty delivers a **JOLT**:
- **J**arring — surprise or contradiction
- **O**pen-ended — unresolved or incomplete
- **L**augh-inducing — playful or absurd
- **T**ense — dilemma or unresolved problem
