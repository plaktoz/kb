# Can Humans Live Forever? Study Puts a Cap on Life Span at 194 Years

A study published in *npj Aging* used a mathematical model to estimate human longevity if nearly all reversible aging hallmarks were eliminated, leaving only somatic mutations — DNA copying errors that accumulate throughout life. Under those idealized conditions, researchers estimated a median lifespan of **146–194 years**, with a maximum range of 210–557 years.

Key findings:
- Brain neurons and heart muscle cells were identified as primary longevity bottlenecks, as they rarely regenerate
- Regenerative organs like the liver showed far greater resilience in simulations
- The model did *not* account for mitochondrial dysfunction, chronic inflammation, or future mutation-reducing therapies

Experts noted the study supports the view that aging is multifactorial — "no single hallmark of aging is likely to account for normal human aging on its own."

For practical longevity, specialists emphasized exercise (particularly VO2 max), sleep, diet, blood pressure, and social connection over supplements or peptides. As one sports medicine specialist put it: "It's what you eat, drink, think, and do."
