# Microsoft's Biggest AI Customer Is Also Its Biggest Risk

Microsoft's fiscal 2026 annual report disclosed $24.1 billion in revenue from commercial arrangements with OpenAI — roughly 7% of Microsoft's total $331.8 billion revenue (up 18% YoY).

OpenAI is the only customer named in the filing, making it uniquely significant among Microsoft's client base.

## Azure Dependency

Azure surpassed $100 billion in revenue for the first time in FY2026, growing 41% YoY. OpenAI's arrangements alone represented approximately a quarter of that figure.

Microsoft's commercial backlog reached $678 billion (up 84% YoY), but CFO Amy Hood confirmed on the earnings call that excluding OpenAI, backlog grew only 25% — meaning the majority of backlog growth stemmed from a single customer, largely tied to OpenAI's October commitment to purchase an additional $250 billion of Azure services.

## The Risk Side

OpenAI reportedly burned $3.7 billion in Q1 2026, and the Financial Times reported expected cash burn of nearly $280 billion from 2026–2030.

Microsoft's capital expenditures on property and equipment totaled $115.9 billion in FY2026, up ~80% from the prior year.

A revised April agreement loosened terms: OpenAI's revenue-sharing payments to Microsoft now carry a total cap through 2030, and OpenAI gained flexibility to use other cloud providers.

## The Upside

Microsoft holds roughly 27% of OpenAI, valued at ~$135 billion, meaning it benefits both as infrastructure provider and part-owner if OpenAI continues scaling.

## Analyst Takeaway

The article characterizes the situation as "a reason for attention, not alarm," noting Microsoft's broader business remains strong, but concludes the stock is a hold given growth concentration risk around one customer.
