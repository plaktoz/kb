# Most Men Unaware That Dairy Intake Is Linked to Prostate Cancer Risk

A survey commissioned by the Physicians Committee for Responsible Medicine found that only 1 in 5 U.S. men knows about the potential connection between dairy consumption and prostate cancer risk. The online poll of 1,102 men, conducted by Morning Consult, also revealed that just 20% had received nutritional guidance from a healthcare provider regarding prostate cancer.

Once informed, roughly two-thirds said they'd reduce dairy intake, and about 76% favored warning labels on dairy products.

However, independent experts cautioned against overinterpreting the link. Ying Wang, PhD, of the American Cancer Society, noted the evidence is graded "limited-suggestive" — two tiers below "convincing." Urologist S. Adam Ramin, MD, said dairy alone is not a "major or definitive risk factor."

Key research cited includes:
- A 2015 meta-analysis linking higher dairy consumption to elevated prostate cancer risk
- A 2016 meta-analysis suggesting men with the highest whole milk intake had a "50% higher risk of death from prostate cancer"
- A 2016 study finding vegan men had roughly 35% lower prostate cancer risk

Ying Wang emphasized that body fat remains the only modifiable factor with strong evidence linking it to prostate cancer risk. Both experts recommended maintaining healthy weight, regular exercise, balanced diet, and PSA screening — especially for Black men and those with family history.
