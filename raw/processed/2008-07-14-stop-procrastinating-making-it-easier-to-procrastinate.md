# Monday Master Class: Stop Procrastinating by Making it Easier to Procrastinate

There are two types of procrastination. Light procrastination is the everyday resistance to shutting down email or turning off the TV — simple tricks like working on a regular schedule, starting early rather than late, and staying well-fed handle most of it. The real problem is deep procrastination: a state where the pressure of starting at the absolute last minute becomes necessary just to motivate any work at all, leading to frequent all-nighters and constant requests for extensions.

The common explanation is that the work itself is unpleasant. But imagine being told you could complete the same assignment at your own pace, with no other courses competing for your time — for most people, that same work would actually be enjoyable, because mastering material over time satisfies a basic need for competence.

The real root of deep procrastination is that the overall schedule is too demanding. Too much work and not enough time forces people into working late night after night, and this constant unpleasantness breeds resentment toward the work itself. Once someone resents their work — and gets none of the satisfaction of competence or mastery — their mind starts finding ways to avoid starting at all.

Stricter schedules and more intense productivity rules don't fix this, because the problem isn't disorganization — it's a deep-seated antipathy toward the work in general. The fix is removing the source of resentment: doing much less. Counterintuitively, a lighter schedule makes it easier to procrastinate in principle (there's more slack to get away with a last-minute scramble), but for most people the opposite happens — the reduced fatigue lets genuine interest in the work return, and interested people don't struggle to get started.

If procrastination is affecting your grades or forcing frequent late-night deadline scrambles, it's worth examining whether your schedule is simply carrying too much, rather than assuming you just need more discipline.
