# Common Antibiotic Associated with Increased Death Risk, Review Finds

A review published in *JAMA Network Open* found that cefepime, a widely used cephalosporin antibiotic, was linked to higher mortality rates compared with other β-lactam antibiotics.

## Key Findings

- Of 11,726 cefepime recipients, 6.6% died, versus 6.2% among those on other β-lactams — a difference of roughly 4 deaths per 1,000 patients.
- Adults taking cefepime faced an **18% higher mortality risk**; children showed no significant difference.
- Elevated mortality risk by condition:
  - Febrile neutropenia: ~19%
  - Severe bacterial infections: ~19%
  - Pneumonia: ~11%
  - Meningitis: ~10%
  - Urinary tract infection: ~33%

## Expert Commentary

Dr. David Michalik, a pediatric infectious disease specialist, noted that cefepime "remains a viable choice when a patient needs broad antibacterial treatment" for life-threatening illness, but acknowledged the study's findings warrant caution.

He cautioned that the broad, multinational scope of the review — spanning varied ages and conditions — makes it difficult to draw firm conclusions. He also noted that category labels like "severe bacterial infection" are subjective, complicating interpretation.

Michalik emphasized that antibiotics should only be prescribed when necessary, given the growing threat of multi-drug-resistant pathogens.

## Clinical Implications

The review authors recommend that "the potential mortality signal associated with cefepime should be considered when framing its role in clinical practice guidance." Cefepime remains one of few broad-spectrum antibacterials available for complex, serious infections, making judicious use especially important.
