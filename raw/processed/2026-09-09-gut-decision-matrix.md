# The Gut Decision Matrix: When to Trust Instinct and Intuition

The article distinguishes between two types of "gut feelings" that are frequently conflated:

- **Instinct** — evolutionary, biological, rooted in brain structures like the amygdala; optimized for survival speed over accuracy
- **Intuition** — built from experience; essentially rapid pattern recognition developed through domain expertise

Psychologist Gary Klein's research on firefighters showed that seasoned professionals could sense danger before consciously explaining why — their brains were matching cues to learned patterns, not tapping some mysterious force.

The author proposes a **Gut Decision Matrix** using two questions:
1. Is this instinct or intuition?
2. Is the situation immediate danger or more complex?

The four resulting scenarios:
- **(a) Instinct + immediate danger** → act quickly
- **(b) Instinct + complex situation** → slow down and question the response
- **(c) Intuition + fast-moving situation with expertise** → reasonable to trust it
- **(d) Intuition + complex/slower situation** → treat as a hypothesis; test before committing

The core reframe: rather than asking *whether* to trust your gut, ask *what kind* of gut feeling it is.
