# 3 Lessons I Learned From Watching Warren Buffett for 30 Years

The article reflects on Warren Buffett stepping down as Berkshire Hathaway's chairman, after previously handing CEO duties to Greg Abel. The author shares three key lessons drawn from decades of observing Buffett:

**1. Temperament beats intelligence.**
Buffett's view is that average intelligence suffices for investing — what separates good investors is emotional control. The author re-reads *The Little Book of Behavioral Investing* annually to stay aware of common cognitive traps and keep emotions in check.

**2. Buy the business, not just the stock.**
Buffett seeks "well-run companies while they are attractively priced" for long-term holds. Thinking like a part-owner rather than a stock trader also helps investors avoid emotional decision-making tied to short-term price swings.

**3. Define your own "enough."**
Buffett famously lived modestly despite enormous wealth. The author credits this example with helping his family avoid lifestyle inflation — redirecting unspent money into investments, allowing compounding to build long-term financial freedom. The takeaway: personal benchmarks for success matter far more than comparisons to others.
