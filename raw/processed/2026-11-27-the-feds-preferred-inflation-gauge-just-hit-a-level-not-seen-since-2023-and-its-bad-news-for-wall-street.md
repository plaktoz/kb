# The Fed’s Preferred Inflation Gauge Just Hit a Level Not Seen Since 2023, and It’s Bad News for Wall Street

## Quick Read

Core PCE inflation hit 3%, its fastest annual pace since late 2023, as energy, tariffs and AI-driven demand push prices further from the Fed's 2% target.

Markets price a near-certain quarter-point Fed hike on December 8-9, with traders still expecting two more increases in 2027.

Heather Long told CNBC, "America's inflation problem is bigger than an energy shock."

Core PCE, the version of the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge that strips out food and energy, rose 3.3% in the 12 months through October 2026. That is its fastest annual rate since November 2023 and up from 3.14% in September, with a monthly gain of 0.29%. The Commerce Department's Bureau of Economic Analysis published the October figures on November 25, 2026, alongside a revised third-quarter GDP estimate.

Core PCE came in 0.1 percentage point above the Dow Jones consensus, CNBC reported, while headline PCE matched it. CNBC noted headline PCE rose 3.8%, essentially matching May's 3.82%. Per CNBC, it moved further from the Fed's 2% target.

## Core Inflation Climbs Above Every Reading Since Late 2023

Core and headline annual rates over the past 13 months are graphed below, with October as the newest point.

| Month | Core PCE YoY | Headline PCE YoY |
|---|---|---|
| Oct 2025 | 2.7% | 2.66% |
| Dec 2025 | 2.87% | 2.78% |
| Feb 2026 | 2.96% | 2.8% |
| Mar 2026 | 3.11% | 3.37% |
| May 2026 | 3.16% | 3.82% |
| Jul 2026 | 3% | 3.37% |
| Sep 2026 | 3.14% | 3.67% |
| Oct 2026 | 3.26% | 3.84% |

On an unrounded basis, October's core reading of 3.26% tops December 2023's 3.23% and January 2024's 3.22%. The last higher reading was November 2023 at 3.44%. The low in the two-year history is 2.56% in March 2025.

| Component | October 2026 YoY | September 2026 YoY |
|---|---|---|
| Goods | 4.45% | 3.95% |
| Services | 3.58% | 3.54% |
| Food | 1.92% | 1.49% |
| Energy | 22.45% | 21.29% |

CNBC and ABC News linked the acceleration to energy prices since the late-February attacks on Iran, tariffs including a fresh round the White House announced Wednesday, and strong demand tied to the AI buildout. Energy PCE went from -0.09% in February to 12.97% in March. Goods inflation running higher than services points beyond fuel. Heather Long, chief economist at Navy Federal Credit Union, told CNBC: "America's inflation problem is bigger than an energy shock."

## Rate Hike Path Puts Pressure on Stocks

CNBC reported that, per CME FedWatch, markets are fully pricing a quarter percentage point rise at the Fed's December 8-9 meeting, and ABC News put those odds at about 97%. CNBC added that traders still price in two more increases in 2027. The Fed already raised its upper target bound from 3.75% to 4.00% on September 17, 2026. At his press conference after the Fed's last meeting, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said, as quoted by ABC News: "It's our responsibility to get inflation down."

Growth gives the Fed room to keep going. Revised third-quarter GDP grew at a 4.3% annualized rate, with gross private investment up 22.4%. An economy expanding that fast suggests current policy is barely holding back demand, which supports further reducing and higher discount rates for equity valuations.

Still, Treasury yields extended their fall after the data flurry, and the 10-year yield stood at 4.91% on November 24, below its 5.31% peak on October 5. CNBC reported that odds of a third 2027 rise slid to about 1-in-3 from 50-50 on Tuesday. Stock futures added to losses right after the release, and the VIX sat at 26.36 on November 24, inside its elevated-uncertainty band.

## Who Pays for a Longer Hiking Cycle

Borrowers: Variable-rate debt tends to reprice with Fed increases. The average credit card APR was already 21.19% as of August 2026.

Savers: Higher policy rates support yields on cash, money market funds and short-term Treasuries.

Retirees: Income from cash and short-duration bonds holds up, while faster price growth erodes fixed payments and rising rates weigh on existing bond prices.

Stock investors: Rate-sensitive and growth stocks face the most valuation pressure when the expected path of policy rates rises.

## What Could Shift the Fed Before 2027

The Fed's December 8-9 decision is the next event, followed by the Bureau of Economic Analysis's November PCE report, which has no confirmed release date yet. To ease pressure, monthly core gains would need to fall back toward the 0.13% and 0.14% readings from June and July 2026. Another month at or above October's 0.29% would keep the annual rate climbing. Consumer sentiment at 48.0 in October shows households already feel the strain. Anyone holding variable-rate balances has the most reason to review their costs before the December meeting.
