# Ultra-Processed Foods Linked to Higher Risk of Multiple Diseases, Death

A large analysis combining 51 studies with nearly 9 million participants found that people eating the most ultra-processed foods (UPFs) faced significantly higher risks of chronic disease and death compared to those eating the least. The study was published September 22 in *Family Medicine and Community Health*.

Key findings comparing highest vs. lowest UPF consumers:

- 24% higher risk of cardiovascular events
- 23% higher risk of obesity/overweight
- 24% higher risk of metabolic syndrome or diabetes
- 27% higher risk of depression and anxiety
- 31% higher risk of digestive diseases (including Crohn's disease)
- 12% higher risk of cancer
- 18% higher risk of death from any cause

The researchers also found dose-response relationships — for each additional 100 grams of UPFs eaten daily, risks rose incrementally (e.g., 14% higher cardiovascular risk, 4% higher cancer risk, 3% higher risk of death from any cause). The link to high blood pressure was less clear in the overall comparison but appeared in the dose-response data.

UPFs were defined using the NOVA classification system, described as containing "industrial formulations with five or more ingredients" not typically used in home cooking.

The article notes that these diseases often occur together (multimorbidity), suggesting cutting UPF intake could help prevent several conditions simultaneously.

Outside experts weighed in on practical takeaways:

- Dietitian Johannah Katz noted there's no firm "cutoff" for harm, describing it instead as "a dose-response relationship" where risk climbs with intake, and recommended keeping whole foods as the diet's foundation rather than eliminating UPFs entirely.
- Dietitian and chef Vahista Ussery recommended a gradual approach — swapping in healthier packaged options, cooking more at home, and cutting back on fast food, such as choosing "a small milkshake" and adding a side salad instead of ordering large portions.
