# Execution to Orchestration: Upskilling Your Workforce for Agentic AI

The main topic that has dominated workforce discussions since generative AI became a widespread technology can be summed up in a single question: will AI take my job? It’s certainly a valid concern but, in my opinion, approaches the challenge from the wrong direction. Because while nobody can say for certain what jobs AI will eliminate, we do know what’s happening to the people who keep them.

They’re transforming. Agentic AI, a new wave of systems that can reason, adapt, act autonomously, and accomplish goals, is redefining not just the nature of work but what it means to be a worker. Rather than serve as reactive tools prompted by humans, these systems can operate as automated workers alongside us. But as organizations look to scale agentic AI from experimentation to full production, they’re realizing that while they may not need as many workers to do routine tasks, they do need more people who can configure, manage, and extract value from these intelligent systems.

Digitate’s December 2025 report, Agentic AI and the Future of Enterprise IT, predicted as much. When we surveyed 600 IT decision-makers at North American enterprises, we found that while 61 percent are beginning to treat agentic systems as “extensions of human teams,” an intelligent workforce that can effectively partner with AI is hard to come by. In fact, respondents cited lack of skill and expertise as the number one challenge holding them back from adopting more autonomous technologies at work (33 percent), well ahead of budget, legacy tech, and resistance to change.

If there’s one universal truth about AI in the workplace, it’s this: technology is developing faster than people are able to learn how to work with it, and that’s where learning and development leaders come in.

## Upskill Your Workforce for Partnership with AI

Aligning your workforce to work with – and not just for – agentic AI starts with four key steps:

## Redefine “Upskilling” for Agentic Workloads

Preparing your employees to work alongside AI starts with redefining what “work” looks like in an agentic world. Where traditional automation efforts focused on offloading manual work to machines, AI systems complete tasks by piggybacking on human intent. Instead of executing routine tasks, workers orchestrate agents to do that work for them.

Upskilling programs should reflect this reality by focusing on training workers to collaborate with AI effectively, starting with systems thinking and workflow design. Technical skills will always be important, but they’re only one piece of the puzzle. Employees should understand how agentic systems are architected, how to communicate their goals with clarity, and how to measure whether an AI system’s output meets the quality standards they’ve set.

In practice, this means less emphasis on developing the skills needed to perform routine work, and more focus on teaching employees how to orchestrate teams of agents that do it instead. For example, insurance claims agents who configure AI agents to review documents; Finance analysts who create automated workflows for routine reporting; IT operations leaders who auto-triage and diagnose system alerts surfaced by AIOps platforms. Each one is designing a workflow, delegating work to an AI system, and monitoring progress.

## Learn to Train AI…Not Just Use It

The proliferation of agentic AI systems is also creating an entirely new job: the AI trainer. Unlike data scientists, AI trainers are domain experts responsible for teaching agentic systems how to do their jobs.

In practice, this means annotating training data, testing and validating outputs, and creating rules that teach autonomous systems how to mimic – or at least complement – human decision-making. But for all the overlap, training an AI system to do your job is not the same thing as doing it yourself.

While most employees aren’t expected to know how to build an AI system from scratch, they should know how to train one. That means L&D programs should cover how employees can apply their industry expertise to evaluate model performance, identify common failure modes, document (and format) their knowledge for machine consumption, and establish outcome expectations clearly. L&D professionals have the skills to teach these skills – they just need to know where to start.

## Don’t Forget Governance

As AI agents take on more responsibility for decision-making at work, employees who manage and interact with them share in that liability. But while most organizations understand how their AI systems make decisions at a technical level, very few workers understand how to manage them at scale.

49 percent of survey respondents told us that one of their primary pain points with implementing autonomous systems was just how often humans need to intervene. But that’s not an AI problem – that’s an employee readiness issue. Without clear governance frameworks and guardrails, employees tend to swing to extremes: either refusing to trust the technology or accepting counterproductive, risky agent behavior.

Governance training starts with defining, documenting, and sharing your governance model internally. The same principles that apply to testing and validating AI systems apply to workforce training. AI decisions should be auditable by employees, employees should know how to escalate exceptions, and there should be consequences for keeping bad data in your systems. Most importantly, workers across your organization should know where to draw the line between trusting AI output and questioning it.

## Work alongside AI…Not against It

Humans and machines aren’t in competition. At least not in the enterprises that are winning with AI today. The secret to building an AI-ready workforce is designing your L&D programs around collaboration, not replacement.

With each wave of new technology, roles and workloads change in form, not in existence. By focusing on building a workforce that knows how to adapt, teach, manage, and govern agentic systems, forward-thinking L&D leaders will ensure that their employees aren’t just ready for the AI era. They’ll own it
