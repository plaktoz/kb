# Trouble Falling or Staying Asleep? Try Eating This Nut, Says New Research

A team of six researchers in India found that a handful of almonds could improve "sleep latency" (how fast you fall asleep) and "sleep efficiency" (how much time in bed is actually spent sleeping). "Sleep disturbances are associated with adverse metabolic, neurocognitive, and inflammatory outcomes," the research team said.

Publishing their work in July 2026 in the journal Frontiers in Nutrition, the team highlighted that almonds have "micronutrients known to support sleep via muscle relaxation and neuroendocrine regulation." The trial was supported by the Almond Board of California.

The study involved 175 participants with poor sleep quality, ages 21-55. Eighty-eight engaged in regular almond consumption while 87 ate a non-almond snack with the same calories, over a five-month intervention. Researchers measured outcomes via brain-wave recording, blood tests, and self-reported sleep scores.

By the end of the study, the almond group reported fewer sleep disturbances and an easier time falling asleep, and blood testing showed significantly higher melatonin concentrations. The clearest benefits were in how participants perceived their sleep quality. Harvard's The Nutrition Source notes almonds are calorie-dense — about 23 almonds (one ounce) is a single serving.
