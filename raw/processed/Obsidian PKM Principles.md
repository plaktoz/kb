# Obsidian PKM Principles and PARA Workflow

## Overview
This note captures the shared advice from the articles about using Obsidian as a personal knowledge system. The goal is to make capturing, connecting, and reviewing ideas feel simple and sustainable.

### 💡 Core Maxim
> Build a lightweight, connected note system that captures ideas quickly, links them meaningfully, and revisits them regularly so your knowledge compounds over time.

## Practical Workflow
### 1. Capture
- Use an Inbox or capture note for new ideas, snippets, and half-formed thoughts.
- Keep the barrier to entry very low so nothing gets lost.

### 2. Organize
- Move notes into the right bucket when needed: Projects, Areas, Resources, or Archives.
- Use a simple PARA structure instead of over-engineering the system.

### 3. Connect
- Link notes to related concepts and source notes.
- Prefer a network of ideas over a pile of isolated files.

### 4. Review
- Revisit old notes regularly to refine, connect, and archive them.
- Treat your vault as a living system, not a static archive.

## Actionable Next Steps
- [ ] **Create a simple intake path**: keep an Inbox or capture note for fresh ideas and process it weekly.
- [ ] **Link new notes to related concepts**: add at least three meaningful links when creating a new note.
- [ ] **Separate sources from insights**: keep source material as anchors and write your own ideas in linked notes.
- [ ] **Use templates and frontmatter**: standardize note types, tags, and properties for easier retrieval.
- [ ] **Review and refine regularly**: revisit old notes, connect ideas, and prune or archive what is no longer useful.

## Key Lessons & Insights
* **Knowledge is a network, not a filing cabinet**: Obsidian works best when notes are interconnected through links, allowing ideas to surface through exploration rather than rigid categorization.
* **Capture first, organize later**: The fastest way to avoid losing ideas is to create a low-friction intake system and process it deliberately on a schedule.
* **Your system should fit your behavior**: The most effective vaults are shaped by your actual workflow, not by rigid dogma; templates, folders, and tags should serve your thinking rather than control it.
* **Notes become more valuable when they are used**: Reviewing, linking, and rewriting notes turns them from archives into active tools for thinking, writing, and synthesis.
