# How to Learn Creative Skills

Creative skills aren't mysterious or innate — they're learnable. Young breaks them into two components:

## 1. Technical Skills

These involve methods, concepts, and practice. Expertise begins explicitly, then becomes automated intuition over time. Both *precision* (accuracy) and *fluency* (natural movement) must be developed — they're complementary, not opposing.

## 2. Ideational Skills (Creativity)

This combines:

- **Knowledge** — immersion in a field sharpens the filter for recognizing which ideas are actually good. Most successful creatives are insiders, not outsiders.
- **Judgment/Disposition** — original work requires risk-taking. As Young quotes Csikszentmihalyi, limits on creativity are more about "demand-side" resistance to new ideas than lack of supply.

Creative ideas thrive in a "Goldilocks zone" — novel enough to attract interest, but still comprehensible within existing taste frameworks.

Technical mastery supports creativity but doesn't replace it. Both dimensions require deliberate development.
