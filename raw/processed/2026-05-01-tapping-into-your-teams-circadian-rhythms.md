# Tapping into Your Team's Circadian Rhythms

source_url: https://hbr.org/2026/05/tapping-into-your-teams-circadian-rhythms

---

Author: Stefan Volk
Published: May 2026 (Harvard Business Review)

Internal biological clocks drive daily fluctuations in energy, focus, and emotion. People fall into distinct chronotypes — morning-oriented "larks," evening-oriented "night owls," and an afternoon-peaking type researchers have called the "vulnerable lark" or "intermediate finch."

The article's core argument is that these rhythms are not adjustable habits or preferences — they are stable biological dispositions. Corporate culture has historically favored morningness, but effective managers recognize and work with the diverse chronotypes on their teams rather than against them.

Scheduling high-stakes meetings, creative sessions, or performance-critical work against an employee's natural energy peak actively degrades output quality and increases error rates. The research suggests that aligning task demands with chronotype can improve individual performance and reduce inter-team friction caused by misaligned expectations.

Stefan Volk is a professor of management at the University of Sydney Business School and co-director of its Body, Heart and Mind in Business Research Group.

Key themes: chronobiology, workplace performance, scheduling, team management, biological clocks.
