# 10-year Treasury yield briefly tops 5% for the first time since 2023

The 10-year Treasury yield briefly surpassed 5% on Monday — its first time at that level since October 2023. The 30-year Treasury yield also touched 5.38% before retreating.

The primary driver was rising oil prices, with Brent crude hitting a session high above $109/barrel, fueling inflation concerns and expectations of Federal Reserve rate hikes.

Goldman Sachs revised its forecast to anticipate a rate hike at the upcoming Fed meeting, noting that market pricing of a hike had risen to nearly 90% — "high enough that the FOMC will likely want to avoid the market reaction" from staying put, according to chief economist David Mericle.

Strategist Ed Yardeni suggested a rate hike could actually ease long-term yield pressure by restoring Fed credibility. He also noted that despite rising yields globally — including in Australia and the UK — stock markets have remained resilient, supported by climbing corporate earnings.

Yardeni also pointed to an unwinding of the yen carry trade as a contributing factor to the global bond selloff, along with increased debt issuance by governments and corporations funding AI infrastructure.

Polymarket bettors placed the probability of a September hike at 80%.
