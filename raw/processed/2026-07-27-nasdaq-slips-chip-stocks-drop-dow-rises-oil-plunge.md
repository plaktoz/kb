# Markets News, July 27, 2026: Nasdaq Slips as Chip Stocks Drop, Dow Rises; Oil Prices Plunge on Pause in US-Iran Hostilities

---

By Aaron Rennie, with contributions from Kara Greenberg, Aaron McDade, Peter Gratton and Sarina Trangle

Major indexes logged mixed results to start the busiest week of the corporate earnings season, as chip stocks weighed on the Nasdaq Composite.

The blue-chip Dow Jones Industrial Average and benchmark S&P 500 ended a respective 0.5% and fractionally higher, while the tech-focused Nasdaq pulled back 0.2%. Major stock indexes are coming off two straight weeks of declines. On Friday, the Nasdaq fell as chip stocks sank, while the Dow rose and S&P 500 edged higher.

Oil prices dropped Monday, with U.S. benchmark West Texas Intermediate futures sinking 7.8% to $82.30 a barrel and global benchmark Brent crude futures plunging 9.1% to $88 at 4 p.m. ET. The sharp pullback followed a Reuters report that Iran would pause military attacks if the U.S. continued its own pause and another from The Wall Street Journal over the weekend saying President Donald Trump called off an escalation of the conflict. Shares of energy giants Chevron (CVX) and ExxonMobil (XOM) declined roughly 2.5% and 1.5%, respectively.

Roughly a third of S&P 500-listed companies are slated to report quarterly results this week, including Magnificent Seven members Meta Platforms (META) and Microsoft (MSFT) on Wednesday and Amazon (AMZN) and Apple (AAPL) on Thursday. A slight majority of the septet finished lower today, with Nvidia (NVDA) dropping 5% to pace Dow decliners following a Journal report that it is in talks with OpenAI to guarantee $250 billion in financing for a massive Ohio data center project. Nvidia lost its crown of the largest market capitalization to Apple, which closed at a record high.

The Roundhill Memory ETF (DRAM) and broader iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) reversed early gains and closed down about 1.5% and 2%, respectively. Memory stocks Sandisk (SNDK), Western Digital (WDC), and Seagate Technology (STX) were down between 4% and 11%.

The 10-year Treasury yield, which influences interest rates on a variety of consumer loans including mortgages, pulled back more than four basis points from Friday's close to about 4.64% in recent trading. The yield had hit its highest level since January 2025 at nearly 4.72% Thursday amid inflation fears as gasoline prices have moved back above $4 a gallon. According to the CME Group's FedWatch tool, traders are pricing in a 38% likelihood of a Federal Reserve rate hike at its meeting this week, up from about 16% one week ago, and an 82% chance of at least a quarter-percentage-point rate hike at its September meeting, up from 64% a week ago.

"This is a week with more than its fair share of potential surprises, good and bad," said Chris Larkin, E*TRADE from Morgan Stanley's Managing Director, Trading and Investing, in written commentary. "Geopolitics and oil prices may be the biggest wild cards, but a bullish response to strong Mag-7 earnings isn't a given, especially if AI spending levels continue to raise eyebrows. And even though the Fed is expected to leave rates unchanged this week, the possibility of a rate hike is higher than it's been in a while. Choppy trading could persist, even if the ultimate direction is to the upside."

Bitcoin was trading around $64,900, up slightly over the past 24 hours. The U.S. dollar index, which tracks the value of the greenback against a basket of foreign currencies, was near flat at 101.50. Gold futures were 0.4% higher at $4,090 an ounce.

## Quantum Stocks Rise After D-Wave Inks Deal With AT&T

D-Wave Quantum (QBTS) signed a deal with AT&T to help the telecom giant improve its network operations. Shares of quantum firms Rigetti Computing (RGTI) and IonQ (IONQ) are surging a respective 9% and 7% in the final hour of trading Monday. D-Wave Quantum stock is up 18%. AT&T said it "used D-Wave's technology to reduce the processing time for a network optimization workload from approximately one hour to less than 15 seconds," and intends to use it across a broader set of applications. Despite today's gains, all three quantum stocks are down between roughly 20% and 30% this year.

## Apple Stock Hits Latest All-Time High; Poised to Close Above Nvidia for Top Market Cap

Shares of the iPhone maker rose to a fresh all-time high of $339.57 Monday, giving it an intraday market cap of about $4.94 trillion. Nvidia (NVDA) shares, meanwhile, sank nearly 5.5% to a market cap of about $4.74 trillion.

## What to Expect in Markets This Week: Amazon, Apple, Meta and Microsoft Earnings; Fed Interest Rate Decision

America's AI buildout will be in the spotlight this week, with the Federal Open Markets Committee meeting Tuesday and Wednesday. The central bank is unlikely to adjust interest rates this week, but rate hikes may be coming later in the year. Last week, Alphabet beat expectations, but its stock sank after the parent company of Google and YouTube raised its projected expenditures on AI infrastructure. Tech companies are selling stock and using debt to finance the construction and operation of AI data centers, leaving investors concerned about whether these investments will pay off. Meta, which reports Wednesday, is reportedly looking to launch a cloud computing business. Amazon, reporting Thursday, is charging customers more to rent hardware needed to train and run AI models.

## Household Wealth Has Never Leaned This Hard on the Stock Market

Goldman Sachs told clients last week that American, Australian and Swedish households hold more of their financial assets in stocks than households anywhere else. According to Federal Reserve data, Americans are the most exposed, with stocks making up 32% of everything American households own — the highest in quarterly Fed records dating to the early 1950s and well above the 27% peak during the dot-com era. "Equity gains have been the dominant driver of household wealth accumulation," Goldman said.
