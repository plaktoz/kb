# Markets News, July 28, 2026: Major Indexes End Mostly Higher on Strong Earnings as Oil Prices Sink; Dow Adds Nearly 550 Points; Chip Stocks Weigh on Nasdaq

source_url: https://www.investopedia.com/stock-market-today-dow-jones-s-and-p-500-07282026-12028107

---

Major stock indexes closed mostly higher Tuesday, aided by several strong corporate earnings reports, although chip stocks continued to weigh on the Nasdaq Composite.

The blue-chip Dow Jones Industrial Average and benchmark S&P 500 finished up a respective 1% and 0.2%, with the Dow adding nearly 550 points. The tech-focused Nasdaq Composite ended down 0.2%, the start of the busiest week of the earnings season.

The Roundhill Memory ETF (DRAM) and broader iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) closed down about 9% and 5%, respectively. SK Hynix, Sandisk, Western Digital, and Seagate Technology fell between 7.5% and 14%. Micron Technology and Intel retreated 9% and 5%, respectively.

Shares of chipmaking giant Nvidia, which lost its crown of the largest market capitalization to Apple following a 5% drop the prior day, edged higher. Apple shares set a fresh record high and hit $5 trillion in market cap before closing up about 1%. The other Magnificent Seven mega-cap tech stocks ended mostly higher.

In post-earnings moves, Sherwin-Williams, Coca-Cola, and Boeing shares powered the Dow, rising roughly 8%, 5%, and 4.5%, respectively, while Corning and United Parcel Service sank 12% and 6.5%, respectively. Visa and Ford Motor were set to report after markets closed.

Oil prices fell further as a pause in hostilities between the U.S. and Iran continued. U.S. benchmark West Texas Intermediate futures declined 4.3% to about $79 a barrel, while global benchmark Brent crude futures fell 5.1% to $83.80.

A two-day Fed meeting kicked off Tuesday. Traders are pricing in a 31% likelihood of an interest-rate hike by the Federal Open Market Committee, up from about 26% one week ago, and a 75% chance of at least a quarter-point rate hike at the September meeting. The 10-year Treasury yield was above 4.60%.

## Big Tech Earnings Week

This week, 169 companies of the S&P 500 are slated to report quarterly results, including Magnificent 7 members Microsoft, Meta, Apple and Amazon. That's after investors sent Tesla shares tumbling after the EV maker revealed its investments in AI and R&D squeezed profits. Alphabet's stock also took a hit despite earnings that topped estimates, after the company warned it plans to boost spending on its AI buildout. Intel saw its stock slide too, even after posting blowout results.

Of the less than 30% of S&P 500 companies that have already reported earnings so far, nearly 90% have reported better-than-expected earnings, according to FactSet, with the tech sector among the strongest contributors. However, companies that beat profit and sales estimates recently have only outperformed the S&P 500 by an average of 1.2 percentage points the day after their results, a bit below the historical average of 1.4 points, analysts at Bank of America found. Tech stocks have lagged by about 2.6 points, amid growing unease around the sustainability of AI spending.

## Other Notable Items

Fitch, one of America's big three credit rating agencies, warned that the possibility of an AI-related market correction, along with geopolitical uncertainty in the Middle East, could pose a "major credit risk." Bank of America's latest survey of fund managers found close to half of respondents identified AI spending by America's biggest tech giants as the most likely source of a systemic credit event, up from just around a third two months ago.

A report Thursday from the Bureau of Economic Analysis is expected to show second-quarter GDP grew at an annualized rate of 1.8%, down from 2.1% in the first quarter, with much of the growth attributable to the tech industry's spending spree on AI infrastructure and data centers. "Capital spending by hyperscalers – like Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, and Oracle – is projected to be almost $700 billion in 2026, which is $300 billion higher in 2026 than in 2025. That alone would account for a full one percentage point of GDP growth," Brian Wesbury, chief economist at First Trust, wrote.
