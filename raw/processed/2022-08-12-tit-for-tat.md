# Tit For Tat

Tit for tat is a game theory strategy considered most effective for iterated (repeated) games involving mutual cooperation or defection. The core mechanic: start by cooperating, then mirror whatever your opponent did in the prior round.

Codified by mathematical psychologist Anatol Rapoport, the strategy reflects our instinctive sense of reciprocity — we help those who help us and withdraw from those who don't. This dynamic encourages cooperative behavior broadly, since cooperation tends to benefit groups over time.

A refinement called "tit for tat with forgiveness" improves on the basic version by occasionally cooperating even after the opponent defects. This prevents both parties from getting locked into endless cycles of mutual defection.

Key takeaway: life is iterative, so as Peter Kaufman advises, it pays to "go positive and go first" — and when mistakes happen without malice, forgiveness is the strategically sound response.
