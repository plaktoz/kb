# Women, Young Adults Experience Fatigue More Often, Says CDC

A CDC National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS) report, published September 3, found that 71.5% of U.S. adults experience fatigue on at least some days, with 16.6% reporting it frequently (most or every day).

## Who's Most Affected

- Women: 20% report frequent fatigue
- Adults ages 18–34: 19.5%
- American Indian/Alaska Native adults: 25.5%
- Rural residents: 20.5% vs. 14.5% in urban areas
- Southern and Midwestern regions showed elevated rates

## Associated Conditions Among Frequent Fatigue Sufferers

- 69.6% reported anxiety symptoms
- 46.1% reported cognitive difficulty
- 45% reported depression symptoms
- 36% reported work limitations

## Expert Perspective

Specialists attribute higher rates in women and young adults to caregiving burdens, life transitions, and financial pressures. Chronic stress disrupts cortisol and adrenaline levels, potentially driving fatigue. Menopause is also a significant factor — one study found over 85% of postmenopausal women reported exhaustion.

Experts recommend consulting a doctor if fatigue persists beyond a few weeks or disrupts daily functioning.
