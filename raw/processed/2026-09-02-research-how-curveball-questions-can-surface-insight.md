# Research: How Curveball Questions Can Surface the Insight You're Looking For

**Authors:** Nandil Bhatia, Wei Cai, and Sameer B. Srivastava
**Date:** September 2, 2026

**Author Affiliations:**
- Bhatia: PhD candidate, Strategy, Columbia Business School
- Cai: Associate professor, Columbia Business School
- Srivastava: Associate professor, Harold Furst Chair, UC Berkeley's Haas School of Business

---

Conventional tough interview questions tend to elicit rehearsed, polished responses rather than genuine candor. The authors argue for a different approach — "curveball" questions — which are described as being closely tied to what the interviewer is actually trying to learn, yet unexpected enough to bypass prepared talking points.

Professionals across many fields — interviewers, managers, journalists, and negotiators — all share the same underlying goal: to uncover information the other person may be reluctant to reveal. Curveball questions are designed to surface this kind of insight by catching the respondent off guard in a targeted, purposeful way.
