# Mental health: Low-calorie diets may increase depression

A new cross-sectional study led by researchers from Toronto, Canada, examined how dieting affects depression symptoms, finding that participants who restricted calories were more likely to have more depressive symptoms. The findings appear in BMJ Nutrition Prevention & Health.

The researchers used data from 28,525 adults from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) and the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9), categorizing participants into calorie-restricted (8%), nutrient-restricted (3%), established medical dietary pattern such as DASH (2%), and no diet (87%) groups.

Overall, 7.79% of participants reported depressive symptoms. The calorie-restriction group had PHQ-9 scores 0.29 points higher than other groups; among people with higher BMI in that group, the increase was 0.46 points. Overweight people in the nutrient-restriction group had PHQ-9 scores 0.61 points higher. In biological men, all three dietary patterns were associated with higher somatic symptom scores, and men on nutrient-restricted diets were more likely to show increased cognitive-affective symptoms.

Dietitian Amy Roethler, who was not involved in the research, said the study "provides additional support" for the long-suspected link between significant calorie restriction and depressive symptoms, noting patients tend to focus on restricting calories rather than nutrient intake. Neuronutritionist Timothy Frie explained that cutting calories or entire food groups can lead to deficits in B vitamins (B12, B6, folate), iron, omega-3 fatty acids, and complex carbohydrates — all essential for the neurochemical processes that regulate mood and emotions. The study authors hope to further research which specific nutritional deficits from dieting most affect depressive symptoms.
