# The S&P 500 Is Forecast to Climb as Earnings Growth Powers Stocks Higher

The US stock market's strong rally in 2026 has been powered entirely by corporate profit growth rather than rising stock valuations, according to Goldman Sachs Research, a dynamic expected to continue through the rest of the year and into 2027.

Goldman Sachs Research raised its S&P 500 forecast for year-end 2026 to 8000, up from an earlier projection of 7600 — a 6% gain as of May 26. The team, led by chief US equity strategist Ben Snider, projects S&P 500 earnings per share of $340 in 2026 (24% annual growth) and $385 in 2027 (13% growth). First-quarter earnings were "exceptionally strong," increasing 18% year over year, with the median company on track for its strongest quarterly growth rate in the past decade outside the 2018 tax-cut and post-pandemic reopening periods.

The investment boom in AI infrastructure is a core factor behind the upgraded outlook. Analyst consensus estimates that the largest hyperscale tech companies will spend $754 billion on capital expenditures this year — an 83% increase from 2025 — and $905 billion in 2027. Snider expects beneficiaries of that spending to account for roughly half of total S&P 500 earnings growth in 2026 and next year, with semiconductor companies the primary direct beneficiaries alongside tech hardware, industrials and utilities.

However, the broader economy presents a more mixed picture: softening consumer spending, elevated input costs and fading fiscal stimulus are expected to weigh on companies not benefiting from AI investment. The team's forecasts embed a 0.4 percentage point boost to S&P 500 EPS growth from AI productivity this year and a 1.5 point boost in 2027.

## Are US stocks overvalued?

The S&P 500 trades at about 21 times forward earnings, in the 88th percentile relative to the past 40 years. Despite that historically elevated reading, Goldman Sachs Research's base case is for the multiple to remain roughly flat through year-end, as modest declines in Treasury yields offset decelerating growth, geopolitical uncertainty and skepticism about AI-related profits' durability.

## What are the signs a bull market is fading?

Conditions that have historically marked the end of major bull markets are mostly absent, but some cautionary signals have appeared: retail trading and Goldman's speculative trading indicator have increased, though both remain below historical highs. Energy prices stemming from the closure of the Strait of Hormuz are expected to result in weaker consumer spending and higher inflation. The AI trade's narrowing of market breadth and sharp rise in momentum — investing in stocks that have performed well — has historically signaled elevated market risk.

## What should investors expect for the rest of 2026?

Several factors point to more moderate near-term returns: a historical pattern of seasonal weakness ahead of midterm elections, and a high bar for sustaining the pace of upward AI capex revisions. Aggregate earnings growth will increasingly depend on hyperscale tech companies' ability to produce returns on AI investment that justify enterprise spending on AI applications. Snider suggests investors diversify beyond AI-infrastructure stocks given narrow market breadth and elevated return dispersion among individual stocks.
