# 10-year Treasury yield hovers above 5% after strong GDP report, rising inflation data

The 10-year U.S. Treasury yield hovered above 5% Thursday morning following the latest inflation data and a strong reading on economic growth.

The yield on the 10-year Treasury — used as a benchmark for borrowing costs ranging from mortgages to credit cards — was 3 basis points lower at 5.011%.

The 2-year Treasury yield, which more closely tracks short-term Federal Reserve policy, lost 3 basis points to trade at 4.693%, while the yield on the 30-year Treasury was down by 2 basis points at 5.334%.

One basis point equals 0.01%, or 1/100th of 1%, and yields and prices move inversely to one another.

Third-quarter gross domestic product increased at an annual rate of 3.4%, better than the 3.1% expected by economists, while inflation accelerated in September. The personal consumption expenditures price index — closely watched by Federal Reserve policymakers — increased a seasonally adjusted 0.5% compared with August, in line with the Dow Jones consensus estimate. It rose 3.6% year-over-year, slightly less than the 3.7% expected but still higher than August's reading and above the Fed's 2% target.

The data comes one day after the Federal Reserve's October meeting that saw the central bank hold its key interest rate in a range between 3.75% and 4%. But the post-meeting statement acknowledged that inflation remains elevated.

"The accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to the Committee's 2 percent goal," the Fed said Wednesday.

Speaking to reporters after the meeting, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said policymakers were not on a set path.

"If we had a firm conclusion about the conduct of monetary policy, we'd announce it to much fanfare today," he said.

Markets are currently pricing in an 83% chance of a quarter-point hike from the Fed in December, according to the CME's FedWatch tool.

Ali Hassan, a portfolio manager at Thornburg Investment Management, said in a note on Wednesday that markets appeared to see the Fed updates as "a softer policy path, not an inflation scare."

"The market trimmed hikes further out: it still expects a December move but is less convinced of the follow-through," Hassan said. "The test comes on Friday, when the blackout lifts [on Fed policymakers' additional commentary]. If Fed speakers put more hikes back on the table, they could claw back the front-end easing and blunt the self-defeating dynamic."
