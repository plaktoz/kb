# Charlie Munger: How To Use The Lattice Of Mental Models To Make Better Decisions

Charlie Munger developed a practical discipline he called the latticework of mental models — a connected web of thinking tools drawn from psychology, economics, physics, biology, and business. It is not a checklist to memorize but a flexible framework for seeing hidden risks, identifying genuine opportunities, and avoiding repeating the same expensive mistakes. The article rewrites a 129-model version of the latticework for solopreneurs, in plain English and illustrated with business scenarios.

Two models highlighted: "Technology as Friend vs. Competitor" — AI tools can either accelerate your output or replace it, depending on where you position; at the level of execution, AI competes, but at the level of judgment and strategy, it assists, so you should position accordingly. "Lean Operations" — a solo operation with clean processes and no overhead is structurally more agile than a small agency with fixed costs and coordination overhead, and that agility is itself a competitive asset.

Another model, the "Cancer-Surgery Formula," argues that sometimes removing a significant but toxic element — a difficult client, an unprofitable service line, a distracting side project — is necessary to protect the health of the broader business.
