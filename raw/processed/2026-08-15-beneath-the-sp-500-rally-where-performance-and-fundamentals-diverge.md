# Beneath the S&P 500 rally: Where performance and fundamentals diverge

U.S. equities have rallied strongly, but the gains are increasingly showing signs of disconnecting from underlying fundamentals, Barclays analysts said, as investors chase upside and demand for stocks remains high.

The S&P 500 is up 13.1% year-to-date, compared with a 17.9% gain for the Nasdaq and a 21% rise for the Russell 2000, according to Barclays' equity derivatives strategy team.

The bank said small-cap outperformance appears particularly difficult to justify given weaker earnings revisions than large caps and rising sensitivity to interest rates.

Barclays analysts said the broader rally has been supported by resilient earnings, improving macroeconomic momentum and fading concerns over an Iran-driven stagflation shock. But they said the key question is shifting from market direction to whether recent gains are fundamentally justified.

The disconnect is also evident in the second-quarter earnings season. Most S&P 500 companies have beaten consensus expectations, but both earnings beats and misses have generated negative average share-price reactions — the first such pattern since the fourth quarter of 2024, Barclays said.

Options markets point to elevated expectations, with implied earnings moves exceeding realized moves across sectors, particularly technology and utilities. Barclays said this suggests investors had limited tolerance for anything short of a clean earnings beat accompanied by stronger guidance, amid stretched positioning and growing scrutiny of artificial-intelligence capital spending.

Big Tech remains a key support for earnings, with Barclays noting strong results from Amazon and Alphabet and continued upward revisions to fiscal 2026 earnings-per-share estimates.

Against this backdrop, Barclays reiterated its preference for hedging small-cap exposure, arguing that the Russell 2000's gains are not fully explained by AI-related strength. The bank favors Russell 2000 put spreads as a hedge against a reversal in the rally.
