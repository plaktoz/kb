# The Market Is Doing Something It's Only Done Once Before. Here's What History Says Happens Next.

The S&P 500 is up 14% year to date, and if 2026 follows the pattern of recent years it will end even higher — a fourth straight year of double-digit gains, which hasn't happened since the late 1990s. The cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings ratio (CAPE ratio) has surpassed 40 for only the second time ever, the first being January 1999, the fifth year of double-digit market gains at the time. Today the CAPE ratio sits near 41, after crossing 40 in May. In January 2000 the CAPE ratio reached 44, preceding three years of market losses.

The market has enjoyed a strong bull run over the past three and a half years, doubling since the beginning of 2023, driven by AI infrastructure spending. Amazon and Alphabet are planning to spend about $700 billion this year on AI infrastructure, and JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Dimon expects hyperscaler spending to reach $1 trillion next year. Much of that spend is flowing to infrastructure companies providing data centers, energy, and memory products, which are seeing outsized gains.

The parallels to the dot-com bubble are notable: extraordinary sums spent on a new category of business, years of market gains, and a rising CAPE ratio. Whether today is different remains to be seen.

The article recommends maintaining a diversified portfolio with both growth stocks (to benefit from AI trends) and defensive/dividend stocks (in case of a downturn), noting the S&P 500 has always eventually rebounded — even after the 2000-2002 slide, it gained 782% from 2003 onward.

Key points:
- The market hasn't had back-to-back years of double-digit gains since the late 1990s.
- The CAPE ratio is at its second-highest level ever.
- Jamie Dimon expects hyperscalers to spend $1 trillion on AI next year.
