# Philadelphia Fed president says she's keeping an 'open mind' on rates, watching inflation

Philadelphia Federal Reserve president Anna Paulson said Tuesday that she supported last week's decision to hold rates steady and is keeping an open mind about next steps, depending on where inflation moves. "The recent improvement in some inflation data is welcome... It is a step in the right direction, but it is only one step," she said in a speech titled "Keeping an Open Mind."

Paulson estimates underlying inflation (stripping out tariffs and energy shocks) is running between 2.4% and 2.8%. She said inflation has been elevated for a long time, and that persistence is what she's most focused on in evaluating a path back to the Fed's 2% goal.

She outlined two scenarios for current policy, which holds the federal funds rate at 3.5%–3.75%: either the policy is mildly restrictive and will bring inflation to 2% in an acceptable time frame, or current policy isn't restrictive enough given that inflation has been above target for more than five years — in which case "persistently elevated inflation suggests more restrictive policy may be needed."

She cited anecdotal evidence of pressure across the economy: a large consumer-goods CEO said his firm is holding prices fixed despite rising costs because shoppers are price-sensitive, while small businesses — particularly those serving real estate — report growing challenges. She said AI build-out is creating some price pressure but that this should stay contained if policy is well calibrated.

In a 9-3 decision last Wednesday, the Fed held rates steady, but three regional presidents (Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari, and Dallas's Lorie Logan) dissented in favor of a quarter-point hike. The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, core PCE, came in at 3.3% year-over-year in June, down a tenth of a point from May.
