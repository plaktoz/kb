# Founder Traits And One Big AI Test: How Former NEA Partner Vanessa Larco Picks Winners

source_url: https://news.crunchbase.com/seed/vanessa-larco-nea-premise-vc-investment-thesis-seed-ai

---

By Mary Ann Azevedo, July 30, 2026

In early 2025, Vanessa Larco teamed up with Mercedes Bent to found Premise VC, a firm focused on backing early-stage technical founders building durable, high-growth software. Before that, Larco spent nearly eight years as a partner at New Enterprise Associates (NEA), where she served on the firm's investment committee and led investments across enterprise software, developer tools, and consumer technology, including Evident, Kindred, Cleo, Greenlight, and Mejuri. She also served as a board observer at Robinhood leading up to its 2021 IPO.

Crunchbase News sat down with Larco to discuss how changing founder preferences and the Silicon Valley Bank (SVB) collapse drove her to launch a specialized pre-seed and seed fund.

**Why did you start your own firm?**

"At a multi-billion-dollar fund, writing $2 million checks is never going to be a top priority... Founders have gotten wiser to what it feels like when they are a top priority versus when they aren't." Larco said founder preferences shifted after the SVB collapse: when the bank was going under, founders learned quickly where they sat on their investors' priority list. That shift benefited emerging, specialized funds. "Emerging funds over the last three to four years are winning very competitive deals, securing lead slots against more established, bigger firms. This was virtually unheard of before."

**What does Premise invest in?**

Strictly pre-seed and seed, with check sizes from $500,000 to $3 million. The firm spends one to three days in deep diligence conversations with founders before backing them, plus extensive reference checks, concentrated in cities where the partners have strong networks: SF, New York, and Atlanta.

**How does Premise evaluate founders versus ideas?**

Larco and Bent identified seven core founder attributes based on the best founders they backed at their previous firms. They look for founders who are world-class in at least two of the seven. "The likelihood that [the pitched idea] is what the company looks like in five to ten years is very slim. A lot of it is gauging the potential of the person to find the right market and product fit."

One surprising attribute: "urgently dissatisfied." These founders can seem disagreeable — more focused on the goal than on making people feel good — but people who've worked with them say the founder pushed them to accomplish things they didn't think were possible.

**On AI wrappers and defensibility**

Larco isn't opposed to "wrapper" startups built on top of existing AI models, comparing the dynamic to early skepticism about AWS: critics said anyone could start a company over a weekend on AWS with no moat, yet many great companies were built on it. Her hesitation is when founders don't understand the underlying mechanics well enough to keep innovating if a model gets too expensive or degrades. "What I look for in any company to be competitive is whether it is faster, cheaper, or easier than existing options... The bar now is enabling people to do things they couldn't do before."

**On AI agents moving from advice to execution**

"Few people want 'Do it entirely for me, and I have no idea what you did or how you did it' right now... It's not just a trust problem, but more that people still want to make the decisions themselves — just not do all the research."

**On the consumer AI pullback**

Larco called the broader VC retreat from consumer tech toward B2B enterprise AI "short-sighted," noting that consumer software has historically produced some of the most important companies ever built. "If everyone is running from a category, that's usually worth paying attention to in case new tailwinds emerge."
