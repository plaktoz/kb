# Women With Enlarged Breasts More Likely to Experience Migraine, Sleep Apnea

A new cross-sectional study published in *Headache: The Journal of Head and Face Pain* found that women with macromastia (enlarged breasts) face significantly higher odds of chronic migraine, neck pain, cervical radiculopathy, and obstructive sleep apnea compared to women without the condition.

Researchers reviewed medical records from 687 females at Stanford-affiliated neurology and headache clinics (2017–2024). After controlling for age, BMI, depression, and anxiety, women with macromastia were:
- 40% more likely to have chronic migraine
- ~2x more likely to experience neck pain
- 54% more likely to have obstructive sleep apnea
- ~2x more likely to have cervical radiculopathy

Lead author Dr. Kristyn Pocock noted that macromastia is often viewed as cosmetic, but the findings suggest it may be linked to "a broader range of women's health concerns."

The study is observational and does not establish causation. Proposed mechanisms include mechanical strain on the neck and shoulders, inflammation, hormonal effects, and disrupted sleep.

Neck pain was the symptom most associated with patients choosing breast reduction surgery. Preliminary small-scale studies suggest surgery may reduce headache frequency, but larger trials are needed before it can be recommended as a headache treatment.

Non-surgical management options include properly fitted bras, physical therapy, NSAIDs, low-impact exercise (swimming, cycling, walking, strength training), and maintaining a healthy weight.
