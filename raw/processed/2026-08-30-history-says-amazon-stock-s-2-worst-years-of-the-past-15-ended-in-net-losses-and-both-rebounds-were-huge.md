# History Says Amazon Stock's 2 Worst Years of the Past 15 Ended in Net Losses, and Both Rebounds Were Huge

Amazon is executing its largest-ever capital investment cycle, with roughly $220 billion in projected capex for 2026 — a figure CEO Andy Jassy raised from $200 billion mid-year. AWS and Nvidia also announced plans for 2 million additional GPUs in AWS infrastructure through 2027–2028.

Examining Amazon's 15-year track record, the two worst years for the stock coincided with net losses during heavy investment periods:

- **2014:** Capex hit $4.9B (+42% YoY); net loss of $241M; stock fell 22%
- **2015 rebound:** Operating income surged tenfold; stock rose 118%

- **2022:** Capex reached $58.3B; net loss of $2.7B (partly from a Rivian write-down); stock lost ~50%
- **2023 rebound:** Net income hit $30.4B; stock rose 81%

Crucially, spending alone wasn't the trigger. In 2012, Amazon posted a small net loss yet the stock gained 45%. Other heavy-spend years (2021, 2024, 2025) produced modest but positive returns. What distinguished 2014 and 2022 was that operating profit collapsed *alongside* the spending surge.

**Current picture:** Free cash flow has turned negative (outflow of $7.6B trailing 12 months), and capex represents ~22% of trailing revenue. However, operating income rose 43% YoY to $27.5B in Q2 2026, and AWS revenue grew 37% YoY — its fastest since 2021. The stock trades near $266, up ~15% in 2026.

The author's conclusion: the key metric to watch isn't capex size, but whether operating income continues growing through the build-out. "The number to watch from here isn't the size of the capital budget."
