# Living near a dry cleaner may come with an unexpected Parkinson's disease link

A nationwide Medicare study found a clear distance-response pattern between residential proximity to dry cleaners and Parkinson's disease, raising new questions about the potential role of long-lasting chlorinated solvent contamination.

In a recent study accepted for publication in the journal npj Parkinson's Disease, researchers examined whether residential proximity to dry cleaners is associated with the risk of developing Parkinson's disease among older United States Medicare beneficiaries.

## Background

Genetic factors are thought to play a role in fewer than 10% of people with Parkinson's disease, increasing interest in environmental factors that may contribute to the illness. Among these, chlorinated solvents such as trichloroethylene and perchloroethylene have neurotoxic properties and long-term environmental persistence, and have been used historically in the dry-cleaning industry, raising concerns regarding soil and groundwater contamination in surrounding areas.

## About the study

The researchers conducted a nationwide population-based case-control study using Medicare research data collected between 2014 and 2018. The study sample consisted of Medicare recipients aged 67 years or older. Incident Parkinson's disease cases were identified using ICD-10 diagnosis code G20. Locations of 32,723 dry-cleaning establishments in 2013 were determined using Esri Business Analyst data, and distance was measured from the nearest dry-cleaning location to each participant's residential Zip+4 location two years before diagnosis or the control reference date.

Statistical analyses evaluated the relationship between residential distance and Parkinson's disease while adjusting for age, sex, race, smoking probability, healthcare utilization, neighborhood income, Rural-Urban Commuting Area classification, and area-level PM2.5.

## Study results

The analysis included 180,982 individuals with incident Parkinson's disease and 18,848,268 control participants living within five miles of a dry cleaner. A clear nationwide distance-response pattern emerged: people living closest to a dry cleaner had the highest odds of Parkinson's disease, and the association gradually weakened as residential distance increased.

Compared with people living four to five miles away, those residing within 500 feet (0.10 miles) of a dry cleaner had 14% higher odds of developing Parkinson's disease. The strongest association was observed among individuals with prior gait disturbance codes: those living within 0.10 miles of a dry cleaner had 23% higher odds of Parkinson's disease with gait disturbance compared with those living four to five miles away. The odds were 25% higher for cases with dementia or cognitive-decline codes and 19% higher for cases with prior falls among people living within 0.10 miles of a dry cleaner.

No significant association was identified for the claims-defined tremor group, although the researchers cautioned that tremor may have been incompletely captured in administrative claims.

Sex-stratified analyses demonstrated that the relationship between residential proximity and Parkinson's disease was present in both men and women, although it remained stronger and extended across greater distances in men. Exploratory geographic mapping indicated that the relationship was not limited to a single area but showed a broadly positive pattern across much of the United States, with stronger associations in the Dallas–Fort Worth and Detroit metropolitan areas, southern Illinois, the Tennessee-Kentucky border, central Pennsylvania, north-central Indiana, parts of southern California, and the New York City-New Jersey region.

## Limitations

Dry-cleaner proximity was only a proxy for potential solvent exposure; dry-cleaner locations came from 2013, and longer-term residential stability had to be assumed. The researchers also could not exclude unmeasured neighborhood confounding or co-exposures, and the findings among older Medicare beneficiaries may not generalize to younger populations.

## Conclusion

Living closer to dry cleaners was associated with higher odds of developing Parkinson's disease among older Medicare beneficiaries in the United States, following a distance-response pattern. Stronger associations were identified for Parkinson's disease with gait disturbance, dementia or cognitive decline, and prior falls. The findings suggest that residential proximity to dry cleaners, potentially reflecting community-level exposure to dry-cleaning solvents, may be associated with Parkinson's disease risk, warranting further environmental investigation.
