# My productivity system in 2026

This piece is a follow-up to a 2022 article on the author's productivity system, noting the coincidental "strange 4-year cycle" between updates. The author reflects on how AI has changed personal and professional workflows over the past six months, describing themselves as bullish on AI but selective about its use.

The author identifies as a long-time GTD (Getting Things Done) practitioner and former teacher of the method, explaining that they tighten their GTD practice during hectic life periods and relax it during calmer ones. Currently, they're in a structured phase, relying heavily on an "end-of-day brain dump" for relief from mental clutter.

The core of the update describes using **Goose**, an open-source AI agent harness, for personal (non-work, non-coding) tasks. Key features include:

- **Read-only calendar access** (personal, kids', work)
- **A dedicated agent email mailbox** for forwarding information and sending messages

Three custom "skills" were built:

1. **GTD skill** — manages simple markdown-based lists for tasks, projects, and pending items, with logic to move items between lists.
2. **End-of-day skill** — takes a free-form brain dump and cross-references it with GTD data, adding tasks, checking off completed items, flagging emails, and proactively researching info (e.g., phone numbers) for upcoming tasks.
3. **Start-of-day skill** — reviews the previous night's dump, calendar, reminders, and emails to generate a daily briefing of priorities and commitments, suggesting possible actions.

The author emphasizes the system's simplicity and independence from cloud services, noting it also works with a local AI model. They close with reflections on sustainable productivity design, citing David Allen's principle that systems should support low-energy moments, and invoking Saint-Exupéry's famous idea about achieving simplicity through subtraction. The article ends with the phrase "KISS always prevails."
