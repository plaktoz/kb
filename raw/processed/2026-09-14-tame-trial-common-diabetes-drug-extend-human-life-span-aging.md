# The TAME Trial: Can a Common Diabetes Drug Extend Human Life Span?

Fact-checked by Jill Seladi-Schulman, Ph.D.

## Summary

Metformin, a decades-old generic drug for type 2 diabetes, is being studied for potential anti-aging effects through the **Targeting Aging with Metformin (TAME) trial**, led by the American Federation for Aging Research (AFAR).

## The Trial

The nationwide, multi-center study will involve 3,000 participants aged 65–79 without diabetes, across 14 U.S. research institutions. It is structured as a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial. Half will receive 1,500 mg/day of metformin; the other half a placebo. The trial spans 6 years, with multi-disease delay as its primary outcome. A review paper appears in the journal *Aging*.

**Note:** Metformin use for longevity is off-label and not FDA-approved for anti-aging purposes.

## How Metformin Might Influence Aging

Metformin works by improving insulin sensitivity, reducing liver glucose production, and activating the AMPK cellular pathway, which helps regulate energy and metabolism.

Proposed longevity mechanisms include:

- **AMPK activation** — may improve mitochondrial health and enhance autophagy (cellular cleanup)
- **Gut microbiome modulation** — may increase beneficial bacteria like *Akkermansia muciniphila* and reduce inflammation
- **Epigenetic effects** — may alter gene expression patterns associated with aging

A 2024 *Cell* study found metformin slowed brain aging in male monkeys by roughly 6 years over a 40-month period.

Dr. David Cutler noted that metformin "acts on a biochemical and cellular level to alter mitochondrial function, inflammation, insulin action, and epigenetic factors."

Longevity scientist Diogo Barardo explained that metformin may activate AMPK and reduce mTOR signaling, "potentially favoring maintenance processes such as autophagy."

## Limitations of Current Evidence

Both experts cautioned that existing evidence is largely observational or from animal models.

Cutler stated the case for metformin slowing aging is "increasingly interesting," but not yet strong enough to claim it extends human lifespan.

Barardo noted: "Changing a pathway involved in aging is not the same as proving that a treatment slows aging in a person."

For people with diabetes, metformin shows clear benefits — reduced heart attacks, strokes, and death — but whether this stems from diabetes control or anti-aging effects remains unclear.

## Expert Recommendations on Use

Neither expert recommends metformin for healthy individuals seeking longevity benefits outside a clinical trial.

Barardo stated he would not suggest a healthy person start metformin solely for longevity, adding: "even a generally safe medicine needs to show enough benefit to justify taking it for years."

Cutler warned that "due to its known risks and the unknown benefits in those without diabetes, it is not advisable for healthy people to take metformin" for longevity purposes.

### Known Side Effects
- Common: nausea, diarrhea
- Less common: vitamin B12 deficiency (may cause anemia, neuropathy, cognitive decline)
- Rare but serious: lactic acidosis
- Contraindicated in significant kidney or liver dysfunction

## Proven Longevity Habits

Both experts emphasized evidence-based lifestyle measures over unproven drugs:

- Regular aerobic and strength training exercise
- Not smoking
- Healthy diet and body weight
- Adequate sleep (7–9 hours)
- Blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood glucose management
- Avoiding excessive alcohol and illegal drugs
- Social engagement
- Recommended vaccines and cancer screenings
- Fall prevention

The WHO recommends 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity weekly, plus muscle-strengthening on at least two days.

Cutler concluded that given the safe and effective lifestyle options available, experimenting with a drug lacking proven longevity benefit "seems foolish."
