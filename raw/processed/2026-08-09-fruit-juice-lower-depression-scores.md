# A daily glass of fruit juice was linked to lower depression scores

A randomized controlled trial from Newcastle University found that adults who added a daily glass of 100% fruit juice or a smoothie to their diet, on top of increasing fruit and vegetable intake, showed significantly lower depression scores than a control group after four weeks. The peer-reviewed findings, published in the British Journal of Nutrition and funded by the Fruit Juice Science Centre, add to evidence that simple dietary changes may support mental health.

The 4-week trial included 42 adults who ate two or fewer portions of fruit and vegetables per day at baseline, randomly split into three groups of 14: a control group asked to continue their usual diet; a group given support to reach 5-a-day using whole fruits and vegetables only; and a group that also added one daily glass of fruit juice or a smoothie. All groups received £10/week, and the two intervention groups received educational materials.

Mood was assessed with validated questionnaires — GAD-7 for anxiety and PHQ-9 for depression. The fruit juice/smoothie group scored 2.52 points lower on the 27-point depression scale than the control group (adjusted mean of 2.93 vs. 5.45) — a difference researchers called modest but statistically significant. Both intervention groups increased estimated daily fiber intake by 8–10g, indicating juice did not crowd out other fiber-rich foods. Despite concerns about juice sugar content, researchers found no adverse effects on metabolic health markers after four weeks.

Dr. Courtney Neal, who completed the study at Newcastle University: "Our trial showed that when people with low fruit and vegetable intake were provided with targeted financial and educational support... simple, cost-effective solutions, like drinking a small glass of 100% fruit juice or a smoothie daily, could help people reach their 5-a-day, with potential benefits for mental well-being." Dr. Oliver Shannon, a senior author, noted the finding "backs up studies which report improved brain blood flow and cognitive function after people drink citrus juices."

Fewer than 1 in 5 UK adults (17%) and 1 in 10 teenagers currently meet the 5-a-day recommendation.
