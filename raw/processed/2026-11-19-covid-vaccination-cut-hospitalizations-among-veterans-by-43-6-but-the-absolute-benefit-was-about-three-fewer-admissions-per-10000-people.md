# COVID vaccination cut hospitalizations among veterans by 43.6%—but the absolute benefit was about three fewer admissions per 10,000 people

Veterans who got last season's updated COVID-19 vaccine along with a flu shot had 43.6% fewer COVID-related hospitalizations over six months than those who got only a flu shot, according to a study of 931,943 U.S. veterans published in the journal Vaccine and reported by CIDRAP. COVID-related emergency visits were 31.7% lower.

In absolute terms, the difference was small, as the authors noted. Vaccination was linked to about seven fewer emergency visits and about three fewer hospitalizations for every 10,000 people over 180 days, during a season when COVID was less common than in earlier years.

For families deciding whether an older parent needs a COVID shot this winter, both numbers matter. The relative drop shows the vaccine still protected against severe illness, while the absolute figures show that in a quieter season, many people would need to be vaccinated to prevent one hospital stay.

## Inside the Two VA Studies

The first study, by researchers at the VA St. Louis Health Care System, followed veterans who received a seasonal flu vaccine between Sept. 21, 2025, and Jan. 31, 2026. Of those, 114,911 also received the 2025-2026 COVID vaccine on the same day, and 817,032 received only the flu shot.

Comparing two groups that both chose to get vaccinated helps reduce one common bias, since people who seek out flu shots tend to have similar health habits. The authors wrote that their results "quantify the benefit of vaccination in a lower-incidence season."

A second VA study, from the VA Providence Healthcare System and published in JAMA Network Open, compared two COVID vaccines among 294,499 veterans 65 and older. Moderna's mRNA-1283, sold as mNexspike, was linked to a 35.9% relative advantage against medically attended COVID and a 47.0% advantage against hospitalization compared with Pfizer-BioNTech's vaccine.

The absolute differences were again modest: about 10 fewer medically attended cases and two fewer hospitalizations per 10,000 people vaccinated. The hospitalization estimate was also imprecise, with a 95% confidence interval of 5.4% to 74.8%, according to Moderna's announcement of the publication. That study was partly funded by Moderna.

## Evidence Check: Real Protection, Modest Numbers

MedicalDaily Evidence Check: These are observational studies using VA health records, one with nearly 932,000 veterans and one with about 294,000. They found lower rates of COVID emergency visits and hospitalizations among vaccinated veterans. They cannot prove the vaccine caused every difference, since vaccinated and comparison groups may differ in ways records do not capture. Neither study's reported results included deaths.

Veterans in VA care are mostly older men, so the results may not apply equally to women or younger adults. The studies also looked at the 2025-2026 vaccine, not the updated shots offered this fall.

The size of the absolute benefit depends on how much COVID is circulating, which is why the same shot can look more or less valuable from one winter to the next. In a high-transmission winter, the same relative protection would prevent more hospital stays.

The findings fit earlier VA research. A study of the 2024-2025 vaccine published in the New England Journal of Medicine linked vaccination to a 39.2% lower risk of COVID hospitalization and a 64% lower risk of COVID death over six months, Medical Dialogues reported.

## Older Adults and High-Risk Patients Stand to Gain Most

The benefit of vaccination is greatest for people most likely to be hospitalized. That includes adults 65 and older, people with heart or lung disease, diabetes or kidney disease, and people with weakened immune systems. For a healthy 30-year-old, the absolute reduction in hospital risk is likely smaller than in these studies.

The CDC's current COVID vaccination guidance, last updated Sept. 23, recommends a 2026-2027 COVID vaccine for all adults 18 and older. Adults 65 and older are advised to get two doses six months apart, though the second dose is off-label because FDA approval for that age group covers a single dose. Children with moderate or severe immune compromise should be vaccinated, and other children can get a shot after a discussion between parents and a clinician.

Getting COVID and flu shots at the same visit appears safe. A separate VA study of about 2.5 million adults published in Annals of Internal Medicine found no added risk across 46 adverse reactions studied, with the authors writing that the findings "support the short-term safety of coadministration in older adults," CIDRAP reported.

## Planning Shots for the Winter Season

Adult children helping an older parent can check when the parent last had a COVID shot and whether a fall dose is due. Pharmacies and primary care offices can look up vaccination records, and Medicare Part B generally covers COVID vaccines without a copay.

People with chronic conditions can ask their clinician whether one product is preferred for them. Those who had COVID recently can also ask how long to wait before getting a dose. The Providence study suggests mNexspike may offer somewhat more protection in older adults, but the evidence is observational and partly industry-funded, and any licensed COVID vaccine reduces the risk of severe illness.

COVID often begins with fever, cough, sore throat, fatigue or loss of taste or smell. Older adults and high-risk patients who test positive can ask quickly about antiviral treatment, which works best when started within days of symptoms.

Households can keep a few home tests on hand before holiday gatherings, since testing a person with symptoms helps protect older relatives and babies at the table. People who are sick should stay home and away from high-risk family members until they feel better and have been fever-free.

Trouble breathing, chest pain, confusion, bluish lips, or inability to stay awake need emergency care. MedicalDaily will report on studies of this season's updated COVID vaccines as data become available.

## Key Questions Answered

What did the VA study find? Veterans who got last season's COVID shot had 43.6% fewer COVID hospitalizations and 31.7% fewer COVID ER visits over six months.

How big was the absolute benefit? About three fewer hospitalizations and seven fewer ER visits per 10,000 people.

Why was the absolute benefit small? COVID was less common last season, so fewer people were at risk of hospitalization.

Is one vaccine better for older adults? A second VA study linked Moderna's mNexspike to fewer cases than Pfizer's vaccine. It was observational and partly funded by Moderna.

Who should get a COVID vaccine this season? The CDC recommends a 2026-2027 COVID vaccine for all adults 18 and older.

Can I get COVID and flu shots together? Yes. A large VA study found same-day vaccination did not raise the risk of adverse reactions.
