# Banks Have Profited from Idle Cash for Years. AI Agents Could Change That.

Wall Street is growing concerned that AI agents may help bank customers optimize their idle cash balances, threatening a key profit source for financial institutions.

Meta's newly released **Muse AI agent** went viral this week, triggering sell-offs in financial stocks including Charles Schwab, JPMorgan Chase, Booking Holdings, Expedia, and Arthur J. Gallagher. The KBW Nasdaq Bank Index dropped roughly 2.6% on Tuesday before partially recovering.

Muse can link to users' financial accounts, track balances and investments, make recommendations, and act autonomously on users' behalf — a direct threat to banks that benefit from customers leaving cash in low-yield accounts.

Bank of America analyst Ebrahim Poonawala noted the app "establishes the opportunity" for margin compression, but cautioned that "until deposit costs rise faster than can be explained by rates or competition, the disruption thesis remains conceptual."

The threat echoes Charles Schwab's 2023 cash-sorting crisis, when rising rates drove customers to move billions into higher-yielding money market funds, forcing Schwab to tap expensive short-term funding.

Banks are responding: Citigroup launched a new savings rate initiative, while JPMorgan's Jamie Dimon previewed a product called **Smart Cash**, and Bank of America offers **CashPro** for corporate treasury management.
