# "Everything Has Been Positive": Analyst Sees a Strong S&P 500 Rally Ahead

The S&P 500 Index is rising today, Nov. 27, as American investors return to equities at the start of the shopping season. The rebound may continue, according to Bloomberg analyst Mark Cudmore, who has identified several potential catalysts for the market.

## Analyst Flashes Potential Catalysts for S&P 500 Index

In a Bloomberg interview, Cudmore identified several bullish catalysts that will boost the S&P 500 and the US stock market.

He believes fundamentals have improved over the past few months. As an example, he cited the ongoing earnings season, which has been relatively strong. FactSet (NYSE:FDS) data shows that S&P 500 earnings growth came in at 32.9%, slightly above analyst expectations. It was the third consecutive quarter in which earnings growth exceeded 25%.

Cudmore also noted that oil prices and bond yields were coming down. Indeed, Brent and the West Texas Intermediate (WTI) have dropped by over 22% from their highest levels in October, leading to lower inflation expectations. Gasoline and diesel prices have dropped substantially in the past few weeks.

US bond yields have turned lower, with the ten-year yield falling to 4.89%, while the five-year yield fell to 4.70%. Historically, the stock market tends to do well in these market conditions.

Further, he pointed to the recent macro data, which show that the economy is doing well. A report released on Wednesday showed that the economy expanded by 4.3% in the third quarter, much higher than the previous estimate of 3.4%. US growth forecasts for next year have also been hiked, with **Goldman Sachs** (NYSE:GS) expecting it to grow by 2.4% in 2027.

## Fear and Greed Index Has Moved to Extreme Fear

Meanwhile, the closely watched Fear and Greed Index has plunged into the extreme fear zone, with most of its sub-indexes in the red. For example, market momentum, stock price breadth, put and call options, and safe-haven demand are all in extreme fear.

Periods of extreme fear can lead to panic selling among investors, which helps explain why the stock market has dropped recently. However, bull markets have often started in such conditions. Cudmore added:

> "Almost everything fundamentally looks positive. And yet, we saw $20 trillion wiped out in global stocks since September."

Cudmore believes that investors returning from the Thanksgiving holiday will find the stock market higher and decide to chase the move. Indeed, data shows that the Invesco QQQ and Vanguard's VOO have had substantial inflows this month.
