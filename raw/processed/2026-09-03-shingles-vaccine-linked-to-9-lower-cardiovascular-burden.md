# Shingles Vaccine Linked to 9% Lower Cardiovascular Burden in New Study

**Author:** Corrie Pelc
**Date:** September 2, 2026
**Fact-Checked By:** Jill Seladi-Schulman, Ph.D.

## Background

The FDA approved Shingrix (recombinant zoster vaccine) in 2017 to replace the older live-virus Zostavax vaccine. The CDC recommends two doses for adults 50 and older.

## Study Details

- Published in *Nature Medicine*
- Analyzed 72,920 people (36,460 Shingrix recipients vs. equal number of Zostavax recipients)
- Data sourced from USA TriNetX electronic health records
- Focused on adults 60+ over a 7-year post-vaccination period

## Key Findings

- 9% reduction in overall cardiovascular burden
- 10% lower ischemic heart disease burden
- 12% lower heart failure burden
- 12% lower stroke burden in men
- 7% lower atrial fibrillation burden in both sexes

## Potential Mechanisms

Shingles causes significant inflammation previously linked to elevated cardiovascular risk. Shingrix's adjuvant (AS01) may also modify inflammatory pathways, particularly involving interleukin-6 (IL-6), a marker associated with cardiovascular disease.

## Limitations

Senior author Max Taquet noted the study is observational, stating findings require confirmation through randomized clinical trials, with "two...underway."
