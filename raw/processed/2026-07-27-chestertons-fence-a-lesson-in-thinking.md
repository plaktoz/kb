# Chesterton's Fence: A Lesson in Thinking

source_url: https://fs.blog/chestertons-fence/

---

Author: Shane Parrish / Farnam Street
Published: Unknown

Chesterton's Fence is a principle urging understanding before action — specifically, don't remove a fence until you know why it was built.

The idea originates from G.K. Chesterton's parable: a reformer wants to clear away a gate, and the wiser response is, "If you don't see the use of it, I certainly won't let you clear it away."

The core lesson: things that exist usually serve purposes that aren't immediately obvious. Intervening without understanding risks unintended consequences.

Chesterton warned against dismissing earlier generations as foolish. Will and Ariel Durant reinforced this — "out of every hundred new ideas ninety-nine or more will probably be inferior to the traditional responses" they aim to replace.

Examples explored include:
- Hierarchy-free companies — removing hierarchy often just creates invisible, harder-to-navigate power structures
- Habits — bad habits fulfill unmet needs; eliminating them without addressing those needs invites replacement behaviors
- Social norms and laws — each arose to solve a problem; sweeping them away invites the return of those problems

The principle isn't a defense of the status quo — it's an argument that reform should be preceded by understanding. Approach what already exists "with humility and curiosity."
