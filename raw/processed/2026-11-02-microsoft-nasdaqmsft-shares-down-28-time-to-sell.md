# Microsoft (NASDAQ:MSFT) Shares Down 2.8% - Time to Sell?

## Key Points

Microsoft shares fell 2.8% to about $507, despite reporting better-than-expected fiscal first-quarter results: revenue rose 18.8% year over year to $92.28 billion, while adjusted EPS reached $4.89.

Azure revenue surged 48% and Copilot adoption accelerated, supporting optimism about Microsoft's long-term AI and cloud growth. Analysts remain broadly bullish, with a "Moderate Buy" consensus and an average price target of $609.54.

Key risks include rising AI infrastructure spending, which reduced free cash flow to $18.6 billion, as well as a planned $25 billion bond offering to help fund additional capacity and continued insider selling.

## Microsoft News Summary

Positive Sentiment: Microsoft reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of $92.28 billion and adjusted EPS of $4.89, exceeding analyst estimates. Revenue increased 18.8% year over year, reinforcing confidence in demand for its cloud and AI services.

Positive Sentiment: Azure revenue grew 48%, ahead of management's 44%–45% forecast, while Copilot adoption accelerated. Usage-based Copilot customers increased more than 75% last quarter and daily users rose fivefold year over year, suggesting Microsoft may be expanding its addressable AI market.

Positive Sentiment: Several investment analyses describe Microsoft's scale, customer trust and Azure position as competitive advantages in AI. Reports also point to roughly $677 billion in signed contracts or committed demand, potentially supporting long-term growth if the company can add capacity.

Neutral Sentiment: Microsoft's approximately $25 billion bond offering is intended partly to fund AI infrastructure. Supporters view the borrowing as disciplined financing for contracted demand, but investors are monitoring whether debt and capital spending generate adequate returns.

Negative Sentiment: Free cash flow fell to $18.6 billion as capital expenditures climbed, raising concerns that AI investment could pressure margins and delay returns. Comparisons with Apple also highlight Microsoft's heavy infrastructure spending.

Negative Sentiment: A new scam impersonating Microsoft technical support could create limited reputational risk, although it does not appear to affect the company's financial outlook.
