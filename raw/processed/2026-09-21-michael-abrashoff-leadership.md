# Lessons on Leadership: Michael Abrashoff's Turnaround Playbook

**Author:** Shane Parrish | **Published:** October 23, 2022 | **Source:** Farnam Street (fs.blog)

---

## Overview

Michael Abrashoff took command of the USS Benfold—one of the Navy's worst-performing ships—in his mid-thirties. Within three years, without replacing a single crew member, he transformed it into the highest-performing vessel in the fleet.

His lessons are drawn from his book *It's Your Ship*.

---

## Core Leadership Principles

### Know Yourself First
Abrashoff argues that most obstacles limiting people stem from the leader's own fears, ego, and habits. Self-understanding shifts how a leader engages with everyone around them, making them appear more authentic.

### Ditch Command-and-Control
> "The command-and-control approach is far from the most efficient way to tap people's intelligence and skills."

Micromanagement disempowers subordinates and eliminates ownership and accountability.

### See Through Others' Eyes
One of his most powerful insights: view the organization from the crew's perspective. This reveals problems that top-down vision misses entirely.

### Why People Really Leave
Exit surveys revealed the top reasons weren't pay (ranked fifth). The actual top four:
1. Not treated with respect or dignity
2. Prevented from making an impact
3. Not being listened to
4. Not given more responsibility

---

## Owners vs. Renters

A central theme: ownership drives performance. Owners demand autonomy; renters avoid the responsibility that comes with it. Great organizations use **principles** rather than rigid rules, allowing judgment and exceptions.

> "Show me an organization in which employees take ownership, and I will show you one that beats its competitors."

---

## On Innovation and Stasis

> "Organizations should reward risk-takers, even if they fall short once in a while."

Standing still is organizational death. Rules that once made sense may now be obsolete—and should be retired accordingly.

---

## Key Takeaway

Abrashoff's transformation came not from new personnel or technology, but from shifting the culture toward **autonomy, dignity, and genuine ownership**—proving that how people are treated determines what they're capable of achieving.
