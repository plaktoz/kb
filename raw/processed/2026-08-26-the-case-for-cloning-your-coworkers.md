# The Case for Cloning Your Coworkers

Every CEO Dan Shipper is building AI "clones" of team members by packaging their expertise into self-improving skills.

## KateBench

A copyediting agent trained on 30,000+ historical edits from editor Kate Lee. Writers run drafts through it before human Kate reviews, and tracked changes feed back into the system via Codex, which rewrites the skill to improve over time.

## DanLens

The next experiment: cloning Dan's marketing instincts so colleagues can apply them without consuming his calendar.

## Key Stories

**Druckenmiller & AI Authorship:** Investor Stanley Druckenmiller confirmed AI assistance on a WSJ op-ed. The WSJ defended him, stating the relevant question is whether a piece "reflects an author's original argument." The FT has taken the opposite stance, banning AI-assisted columns.

**Arielle's Self-Improve Skill:** When Codex errs, she documents the failure, runs a self-improve skill, and gets a targeted fix suggestion — turning mistakes into training data.

**Open-Weight Models:** Frontier model Fable captures only ~6% of Anthropic business tokens one month post-launch. Dan notes: "I get no relative gain from Fable on 80 percent of my knowledge work tasks." Open-weight models jumped from 11% to 29% of Vercel AI Gateway tokens (April–June), at under 4% of spend.

**Walleye Capital:** The $10B hedge fund mandates AI use for all 400 employees. CEO Will England: "Results are what matter" — effort alone doesn't.
