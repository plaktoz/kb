# Q3 2026 investment outlook: Solid growth endures as policy and geopolitics shift

The third quarter begins with a familiar but evolving backdrop. Markets have absorbed meaningful uncertainty this year, yet the core growth supports remain in place: consumers are still spending, businesses are still investing and growing earnings, and companies continue to build the infrastructure needed for a more data-driven economy.

## Global economy

The U.S. economy enters the quarter from a position of resilience. Labor conditions remain steady enough to support consumer spending, while earlier income gains still provide some cushion against higher prices. Business investment offers another source of strength — AI-related spending is broadening beyond large technology companies into systems, energy and industrial capacity needed to support long-term productivity.

**Key risks:**
- The Iran conflict remains the most fluid risk. Diplomacy has improved and shipping activity through the Strait of Hormuz has started to recover, but the reopening remains fragile
- Fed policy: at Kevin Warsh's first meeting as chair, the Fed held rates steady while signaling a renewed focus on price stability. Markets price at least one 0.25% interest rate hike by year-end
- Public listings pipeline reopening — potential offerings from Anthropic and OpenAI by year-end

**Key points on the global economy:**
- U.S. employers added an average of 114,000 jobs per month through May
- Wage growth up 4.1% year-over-year in May
- Analysts expect large U.S. companies to increase capital spending by about 40% over the next 12 months, led by AI

## Equity market

Earnings growth endures as the clearest support for stocks. Analysts continue to raise earnings estimates for 2026 and 2027, supporting market valuations despite above-average large-company multiples.

- Technology investment continues as a major growth engine
- Foreign equities: analysts forecast **11% earnings growth in foreign developed stocks** in 2026
- Emerging markets: analysts project more than **50% earnings growth** — a high bar
- Higher-income consumers remain resilient; middle-income households more cautious

## Bond markets

Treasury yields between 4% and 5% provide a stronger starting point for investors than in the previous decade. Corporate bonds offer attractive income (yields 5–7%), though selectivity is important. Municipal bonds remain competitive for top-bracket investors.

## Real assets

Broad REIT valuations somewhat elevated vs. bonds. Utilities make up nearly half of broad infrastructure indexes; rising electricity demand (including from data centers and AI) supports revenue growth with utility earnings growth forecast to be strong in 2026.

## Private markets

The public listing market is reopening. SpaceX's public debut drew attention; expected listings from Anthropic and OpenAI by year-end. Some large private credit funds continue to manage investor withdrawal requests. Default rates around 6%, in line with long-term averages.

## Alternative investments

Hedge fund managers enter the second half with more willingness to take risk. Focused on semiconductor, technology hardware and power generation companies benefiting from AI investment. Strong company-level dispersion creates opportunities for skilled stock pickers.
