# The Moral of Fable

Shipper argues that Claude Fable 5 (Anthropic's newest model) represents a significant leap—but mainly for those already operating at advanced levels of AI adoption.

## Who Feels the Difference?

Most knowledge workers find Fable incremental and expensive (2x the token cost of prior models). But users like Kieran Klaassen, who run full agent-driven workflows, are seeing dramatic results. Klaassen's team set a goal of fixing any reported bug within 24 hours; the actual median turnaround has been **five hours**.

## The Gardening Metaphor

Shipper revisits his earlier framing: advanced AI use looks like gardening—setting conditions for growth rather than doing direct work. The developer's role becomes:

- Collecting user feedback
- Turning it into actionable plans
- Letting AI execute
- Reviewing, merging, and feeding lessons back into the next cycle

"The process becomes the product."

## Expanding Individual Capability

Fable enables individuals and small teams to tackle projects once requiring entire organizations. Klaassen reportedly built a full email inbox (web + iOS) in roughly two months—a feat that took a well-funded competitor over two years pre-AI.

## The Access Problem

Fable is not only more expensive per token, it's also **token-hungry**, often spinning up dozens of subagents automatically. This creates a new asymmetry: frontier capability increasingly requires meaningful capital, resembling labor-market inequalities more than the relatively flat technology access of recent decades.

## Conclusion

Shipper leaves the moral open-ended: developer workflows are spreading to all knowledge work, individual power is expanding, but so is the potential gap between those who can afford continuous frontier access and those who cannot. "The moral for Anthropic's Fable is still being written."
