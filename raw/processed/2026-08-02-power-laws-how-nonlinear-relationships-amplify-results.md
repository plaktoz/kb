# Power Laws: How Nonlinear Relationships Amplify Results

From Farnam Street (fs.blog). Category: Mental Models / Mathematics.

A power law describes a nonlinear relationship between two things, expressed as **Y = MX^B**, where:
- Y = output/result
- X = the variable you control
- B = the exponent (order of scaling)
- M = a constant

Small input changes can produce dramatically large output changes when B is greater than 1.

## Key Types of Power Laws

| Exponent (B) | Relationship | Example |
|---|---|---|
| B = 1 | Linear | Doubling a recipe needs 2x flour |
| B = 2 | Second-order | Kinetic energy (½mv²) |
| B = 3 | Third-order | Wind power conversion |
| B = 4 | Fourth-order | Heat radiation law |
| B = negative | Inverse | Gravity weakening with distance |
| Variable n | Compounding | Investment growth over time |

## Major Applications

**Compounding:** The only power law with a variable exponent, described as potentially limitless in growth. Small early investments vastly outperform larger later ones due to exponential accumulation over time.

**Zipf's Law (Language Learning):** In language learning, a tiny fraction of words comprises the majority of usage. Roughly 135 words can form half of typical everyday speech, making early vocabulary learning extraordinarily high-yield. This same distribution appears in word frequency rankings across languages.

**Pareto Principle:** "20% of the people owned 80% of the land." Severely unequal distributions appear across natural and social systems — wealth, productivity, website traffic, earthquake magnitudes.

**Venture Capital (Peter Thiel):** Returns are heavily skewed — the single best investment in a fund can outperform all others combined. *"The best investment in a successful fund equals or outperforms the entire rest of the fund combined."* This means portfolio construction should concentrate on finding outliers, not avoiding them.

**Business Strategy:** Within any company, one revenue source typically dominates all others. Entrepreneurs should identify and concentrate on that primary driver rather than spreading effort evenly.

**Biology — Kleiber's Law:** Metabolic rate scales at three-fourths the power of body mass. Larger animals are proportionally more energy-efficient than smaller ones, which also explains lifespan differences across species — larger animals live longer partly because they metabolize more slowly.

## Diminishing Returns

Past a certain threshold, positive exponents can turn negative — increasing input yields less output. Examples:
- Excessive training causing injury rather than improvement
- Income beyond ~$75,000/year showing negligible happiness gains
- Study time with exponentially diminishing marginal returns past a saturation point

## Core Insight

Human intuition defaults to linear thinking, making power-law dynamics consistently surprising. As Peter Thiel observed, *"we vastly underestimate exponential things"* because everyday life experience is predominantly linear. Recognizing where power laws operate — compounding, network effects, skill accumulation — allows better allocation of time and resources toward high-leverage activities.
