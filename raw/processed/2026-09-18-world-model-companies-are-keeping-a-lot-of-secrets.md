# World Model Companies Are Keeping a Lot of Secrets

TechCrunch's AI Editor moderated a world models panel at the All In conference, probing one of AI's most secretive sectors. The two dominant players — Yann LeCun's AMI Labs and Fei-Fei Li's World Labs — have raised substantial funding but remain tight-lipped about commercialization plans.

AMI co-founder Michael Rabbat offered little, saying only: "We'll talk about it when we're ready to talk about it," and later clarifying via email that the company remains in a "research and building phase."

World Labs' most developed offering, Marble, demos capabilities across media creation, game environments, and CGI — though it reads more as a capability showcase than a product.

The secrecy extends even to suppliers. Alex de Vigan, CEO of data provider Physicl, noted he remains unclear on what his clients are actually building: "I wish they would tell us more. We could build more useful data if we knew what they were working on."

The author argues companies have strategic incentive to stay quiet — announcing a clear direction would attract competition from rivals, neolabs, and giants like OpenAI and Anthropic. He frames this as a "dark forest scenario": in an uncertain competitive landscape, silence is protection.
