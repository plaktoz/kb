# Daily Summary: Tech Stock Selloff Weighs on Markets, Fed Outlines AI Scenarios

Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh avoided giving rate guidance while highlighting AI economic impact scenarios.

Nvidia shares fell 3.82% to 156.56 USD amid a governance crisis following the departure of CEO Jensen Huang.

European natural gas contracts dropped over 6% intraday due to forecasts of mild temperatures and record US exports.

Gold retreated 0.54% to 3 862 USD and silver fell 3.20% to 47.12 USD amid a stronger dollar and high real interest rates.

Bitcoin declined 0.41% to 64 835 USD as the largest cryptocurrency tracked losses in global tech stocks.

## Macroeconomics and Monetary Policy

Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh spoke at the opening of the Productivity and Jobs Task Force conference hosted by the San Francisco Fed. In line with his communication approach, Warsh deliberately refrained from any direct guidance regarding the near-term path of interest rates and monetary policy. The Fed Chair revealed that central bank analysts are sketching out contingency scenarios (noting that these are not forecasts and that the Fed's role is neither to advocate for nor oppose the technology) regarding the impact of artificial intelligence (referred to in official US administration parlance as Super Intelligence - SI) on the economy:

Optimistic productivity boom: AI accelerates economic growth and labor productivity, with gains shared between employers and workers.

Jobless growth: A pickup in economic activity comes at the cost of massive job losses.

Disappointment scenario: Following a wave of massive capital expenditures and stock market gains, expected returns disappoint, triggering an equity selloff, an economic slowdown, and a more notable rise in unemployment (with a subsequent recovery occurring without a lasting jump in productivity).

External expert task forces established by Warsh in June will submit recommendations to the FOMC around the end of the year. Although the Fed Chair hopes a productivity boom will ultimately slow inflation, he previously acknowledged that surging prices for raw materials and AI infrastructure hit the demand side of the economy first, boosting price pressures.

Weak data from Asia and geopolitical tensions: Disappointing macroeconomic releases from China (a slowdown in industry and consumption) and Europe dampened sentiment at the start of the week, amplifying concerns over demand for commodities and exports. In Japan, a weaker Q3 GDP reading failed to quell speculation about further rate hikes by the Bank of Japan due to inflationary pressures and yen weakness. Investors' attention is also focused on tensions in the Strait of Hormuz, Canadian inflation readings, and Fitch's warning regarding deteriorating outlooks for North American sovereign issuers due to fiscal risks and polarization in Washington.

## Equity Markets and the Technology Sector

Pressure on Wall Street: A selloff in tech stocks continues on Wall Street, triggered by a governance crisis at Nvidia following the resignation of CEO Jensen Huang (the board confirmed it is not considering his return, despite appeals from industry leaders, including Sam Altman). Nervousness is compounded by tightened US Commerce Department regulations closing export loopholes to Chinese data centers, Nvidia's upcoming quarterly earnings report, and Anthropic's impending stock market debut.

Major indices in Asia, Europe, and the US: The Asian session saw steep losses across semiconductor-linked markets (Taiwan, South Korea, Japan), while European equities ended the day with moderate declines, and US indices are deepening losses led by the tech-heavy Nasdaq.

## Corporate news:

Nvidia: shares fall 3.82% to $156.56 ahead of upcoming quarterly results and amid turmoil following the departure of CEO Jensen Huang, despite Donald Trump's backing for his reinstatement.

Alphabet: plans to participate in two prepaid energy transactions totaling $3 billion.

Tesla: loses 2.77% to $378.51, despite announcing a €3 billion investment plan to expand its factory near Berlin for the production of autonomous Cybercab vehicles.

Apple: declines 0.90% to $353.09; company veteran Corey Fugman took over leadership of the Apple Pay and digital wallet division.

Eli Lilly: acquires Calytrix Bio in a deal worth up to $1.5 billion, continuing this year's $32 billion wave of acquisitions in oncology and immunology.

Johnson & Johnson: agreed to acquire Ollin Biosciences for $2.2 billion upfront (plus up to $1.13 billion in milestone payments) to expand its ophthalmology segment.

## Commodities

Natural gas: European gas contracts came under heavy selling pressure, losing over 6% intraday toward key support levels due to forecasts of mild temperatures and record US exports of the commodity. US natural gas (as of 18:41) is down 3.55% to 3.1230.

Silver: falls 3.20% to $47.12, sliding toward annual lows under pressure from a stronger dollar and weakening demand from the photovoltaic and electronics sectors.

Gold: retreats 0.54% to $3,862, moving away from all-time highs amid high real interest rates.

Copper: declines 1.09% to $13,201, reacting to weaker macroeconomic readings from China.

## FX Market

Defensive currencies in demand: The Japanese yen is gaining support as a safe haven amid the equity selloff, with USDJPY upside further capped by the risk of currency intervention from the Ministry of Finance in Tokyo (as of 18:41, the rate stands at 159.53, change -0.08%). The Swiss franc is also strengthening moderately (USDCHF trading at 0.8393, +0.23%).

## Cryptocurrencies

Bitcoin under pressure from risk aversion: The largest cryptocurrency is tracking declines in tech stocks, testing key demand barriers (as of 18:41, BTC is priced at $64,835, representing a drop of 0.41%).
