# Why SPMO Stands Out in a Strong Momentum Year

Momentum has been one of the strongest-performing equity factors in 2026, as persistent uncertainty around inflation, rates, valuations, and earnings pushes more investors toward factor-based investing. The Invesco S&P 500 Momentum ETF (SPMO) has drawn particular attention, pulling in about $1.02 billion in net inflows over the past month and $3.59 billion over the past three months.

SPMO tracks the S&P 500 Momentum Index, selecting and weighting stocks by risk-adjusted price momentum rather than market cap, and — unlike broader momentum funds that draw from large- and mid-cap stocks — it selects exclusively from S&P 500 constituents. That construction lets it capture more concentrated exposure when a small group of companies (this year, alongside AI leaders, leadership broadened into industrials, financials, and cyclicals) drives market returns.

Over the trailing 12 months, SPMO returned 26.89% versus 26.18% for the iShares MSCI USA Momentum Factor ETF (MTUM) and 17.43% for the S&P 500 Total Return Index — meaningfully ahead of the broad market. MTUM uses a broader large- and mid-cap universe with a risk-adjusted momentum approach, while SPMO's S&P 500-only, momentum-weighted construction and semiannual rebalancing keep it more concentrated in stocks with persistent momentum characteristics as leadership shifts, reducing emotional decision-making through a transparent index methodology. SPMO carries a 0.13% expense ratio.
