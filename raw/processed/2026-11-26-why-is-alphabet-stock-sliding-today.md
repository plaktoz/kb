# Why is Alphabet stock sliding today?

Investing.com -- Shares of [Alphabet](https://www.investing.com/equities/google-inc) fell 4.2% in morning trading after Malaysia's communications regulator filed two lawsuits against Google today seeking combined damages of roughly $146 million, alleging the company failed to adequately address harmful content — including online gambling, scams, and fraud — on its YouTube platform. The legal action compounds an already difficult backdrop for the stock, which has been under sustained pressure since a disappointing third-quarter earnings report in late October that saw search revenue growth slow to 12% and EPS miss expectations due to a large equity investment loss.

Adding to the pressure, an unconfirmed supply-chain report suggesting Google could reduce its 2027 orders for custom AI chips (TPU-8i) by roughly 20% has unsettled investors focused on the company's AI capital allocation strategy. A securities class action lawsuit — with a lead-plaintiff deadline of December 1 — alleging Alphabet misled investors about the timing of its Gemini 3.5 Pro launch also continues to weigh on sentiment, as does a report that Google cancelled a publisher funding deal, signaling a tightening of its media partnerships.

The broader market provided no shelter today. The [Nasdaq](https://www.investing.com/indices/nasdaq-composite) dropped more than 3%, the [S&P 500](https://www.investing.com/indices/us-spx-500) fell more than 2%, and the [Dow Jones](https://www.investing.com/indices/us-30) declined more than 1% as Wall Street absorbed hotter-than-expected PCE inflation data and fresh Trump administration tariff threats, both of which intensified concerns about the Federal Reserve's path ahead of its December meeting. The Software & IT Services sector broadly underperformed, with credit default swap spreads on major AI infrastructure names hitting record levels.

The combination of fresh regulatory risk, unresolved legal proceedings, AI chip order uncertainty, and a hostile macro environment pushed Alphabet to trade at its lowest level in over a year, touching a new 52-week low of $262.73 intraday — underscoring how multiple converging headwinds have eroded investor confidence in the stock well beyond the sector-wide selloff.
