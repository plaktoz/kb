# Bug Blindness

Dan Luu explores why most people don't notice software bugs despite encountering them constantly. His core argument: people aren't hitting *fewer* bugs — they're hitting the same bugs but have developed unconscious workarounds that mask the problems.

## The Phenomenon

Luu notices hundreds to thousands of bugs weekly. When products he evaluates as severely broken later launch and fail publicly, it confirms he's not triggering unusual edge cases — the bugs are real and widespread.

## Bug Blindness in Practice

Internal teams often believe their broken products are excellent. Examples include:

- **Blackboard**: Widely considered among the most disliked software ever, yet one employee he met believed users *loved* it.
- **Search engines**: Users defend spam-filled results as high quality.
- **Discourse**: Had code that actively slowed real performance to game benchmark metrics.

## Habitual Workarounds

A childhood anecdote illustrates the concept: his mouse had accumulated debris, causing erratic tracking. He'd unconsciously learned to compensate with equally erratic hand movements — and couldn't notice he was doing it.

Modern equivalent: he waits before typing a Google Doc title because immediate typing gets overwritten. These micro-habits accumulate into what we call "computer literacy."

> "A large fraction of computer literacy... is developing a large library of these habits that you just do at a non-conscious level."

## Why Dogfooding Fails

Having developers use their own software helps, but programmers are *especially* skilled at developing workarounds — which means they adapt to problems rather than perceiving them as problems.

## The Fix

Luu believes quality blindness is curable by simply pointing out bugs to receptive people. After a few weeks, they start noticing independently. Years later, some report they "see bugs everywhere now."

## Notable Observations

- The reflexive developer response to usability complaints is typically: *"It's easy, just do [complex unintuitive sequence]"*
- Fans systematically ignore flaws in things they love (Volvo reliability forums, sports team fans defending dirty players)
- With LLMs, it's now easier than ever to both *produce* low-quality software AND *improve* quality — but only if you notice quality is improvable in the first place

## Appendix Highlights

**Advertising blindness** (from Michael Malis): Many people don't realize hospitals advertise heavily, despite bus/billboard ads being everywhere. Geo-segmented A/B tests at major companies showed strong, measurable ad effectiveness — contradicting the common programmer belief that ads don't work.

**Mac laptop bug** (from Em Chu): Waking a MacBook sometimes produces an unusable black screen, fixable only by closing and reopening the lid — requiring a habitual workaround most users develop subconsciously.

**German word for this:** *Betriebsblindheit* — operational blindness to familiar problems.
