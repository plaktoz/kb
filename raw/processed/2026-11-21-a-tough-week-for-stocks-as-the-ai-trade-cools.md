# A Tough Week for Stocks as the AI Trade Cools

Technology shares led a broad pullback that pushed the S&P 500 into correction territory, while high-quality bonds helped cushion the decline.

#### The Week in Brief

- The S&P 500 Index fell 8.6% this week, its worst week since April 2025, and ended the week more than 10% below its October record, a level investors commonly call a "correction."
- Technology and AI-related companies drove most of the decline, even though NVIDIA reported quarterly results that beat Wall Street expectations.
- Bond prices rose as investors looked for safety. The 10-year Treasury yield, a key benchmark for mortgage and borrowing rates, fell to 4.92% from 5.15%.
- The Federal Reserve signaled it may raise interest rates again in December, with inflation still running above its 2% goal.

#### Stocks

This was one of the more difficult weeks for stocks in recent memory. The S&P 500 Index fell 8.6%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 8.0%, the Nasdaq Composite fell 10.0%, and the small-company Russell 2000 Index fell 7.5%. The S&P 500 declined all five days and closed Friday at 6,910, which leaves it up 0.9% for the year.

The selling centered on artificial intelligence. NVIDIA, the largest AI chipmaker, reported quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion and an outlook above analyst estimates, but its shares still fell about 23% for the week. On Friday, the shares fell about 14% after reports that regulators were looking into the company. Other chip and large technology stocks also fell, and the technology sector dropped about 12.5% for the week.

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI assistant, began trading Thursday after a closely watched initial public offering (IPO). Its shares opened well above the offering price but gave back part of those gains by Friday. Many investors appeared to treat the event as a reason to take profits in AI stocks rather than add to them.

There were bright spots. Defensive areas such as consumer staples and utilities held up much better, and several major retailers, including Walmart, Target, and TJX, reported solid results that pointed to steady consumer spending.

#### Bonds & Interest Rates

High-quality bonds did what they are designed to do in a diversified portfolio. As stocks fell, investors moved toward Treasuries, pushing bond prices up and yields down. The 10-year Treasury yield fell 0.23 percentage points to 4.92%, and the 2-year yield fell to 4.63% from 4.88%.

Investment-grade corporate bonds and long-term Treasuries gained for the week, while high-yield bonds dipped modestly. Even after this week's decline, yields remain well above where they started the year, so new bond purchases continue to offer meaningful income.

#### The Economy & the Fed

Economic reports this week were mixed but generally steady. Retail sales rose 0.2% in October, and a key measure that excludes volatile categories rose 0.6%. Weekly jobless claims stayed low at 203,000. Manufacturing surveys from New York and Philadelphia softened, and permits for new homes fell 7.4%, which reflects the impact of higher mortgage rates.

On the inflation front, a University of Michigan survey showed that consumers' expectations for inflation over the next year fell to 3.7% from 4.7%. That is an encouraging sign, though inflation itself remains above the Fed's target.

Minutes from the Fed's October meeting showed that almost all officials see further rate increases as likely appropriate. Several Fed officials said this week they are open to raising rates at the December 8-9 meeting, and markets are pricing in a strong likelihood of an increase.

#### What We're Watching This Week

- Wednesday, Nov. 25: the October Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index, the Fed's preferred inflation measure, along with an updated estimate of third-quarter economic growth, durable goods orders, and the Fed's Beige Book survey of regional business conditions.
- Tuesday, Nov. 24: consumer confidence and earnings from Dell and other technology companies.
- U.S. markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving and close early, at 1:00 p.m. ET, on Friday.

#### Our Perspective

Weeks like this one can be unsettling, especially after a strong run for technology stocks. It is worth remembering that pullbacks of 10% or more are a normal part of investing and have happened many times over the past several decades. Markets have historically recovered from them over time, though the timing is never predictable and past results do not guarantee future outcomes.

This week also showed the value of diversification. While technology shares fell sharply, high-quality bonds rose and defensive stock sectors held up comparatively well. Portfolios built across different asset classes are designed with weeks like this in mind.

Rather than reacting to short-term headlines, we continue to focus on your long-term plan, your time horizon, and your comfort with risk. Periods of volatility can also create opportunities to rebalance or harvest tax losses where appropriate.
