# Markets News, July 30, 2026: Indexes End Sharply Higher, Tech Shares Soar; Apple, Amazon Report

source_url: https://www.investopedia.com/stock-market-today-dow-jones-s-and-p-500-07302026-12030671

---

By Aaron Rennie and Investopedia staff. July 30, 2026.

Major stock indexes staged a big rebound Thursday. One day after plunging, the tech-focused Nasdaq Composite, benchmark S&P 500, and blue-chip Dow Jones Industrial Average closed a respective 2.8%, 1.7%, and 1.2% higher, with the Dow adding 600 points.

Microsoft and Meta Platforms issued highly anticipated earnings after Wednesday's close, and investors reacted differently: Microsoft's results beat expectations and shares surged 16%, pushing the company's market value over $3.5 trillion, while Meta's profit missed as costs rose and shares sank 8%.

The Roundhill Memory ETF (DRAM) and iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) jumped 16% and 8.5% respectively after dropping more than 6% and 5% the prior day. Memory stocks Sandisk, Micron, Seagate, and Western Digital soared between 12% and 26%. Apple and Amazon were due to report results after the close; Apple shares slipped about 1.5% ahead of the report while Amazon rose 4%.

Wednesday, the indexes fell between 1.5% and 2.2%, with the Dow shedding more than 1,150 points for its worst day in 15 months, after the Fed's 9-3 decision to hold rates steady; Chair Kevin Warsh said the central bank would be resolute on inflation. Traders were pricing in a 63% chance of at least a quarter-point rate hike at the September meeting, per CME's FedWatch tool, down from 82% a week earlier.

Before the bell Thursday, monthly PCE inflation readings matched expectations: prices rose 3.7% over 12 months in June, down from 4.1% in May; core PCE rose 3.3%, down from 3.4%. Second-quarter GDP growth came in at 1.5%, below the 1.8% expected and down from 2.1% in Q1. The 10-year Treasury yield was below 4.67% at 4 p.m. ET.

Oil prices declined as traders assessed US retaliatory strikes against Iran; WTI was 1.1% lower at $83.50 a barrel. Gold futures rose 3.4% to $4,170 an ounce.

"These data confirm what we already knew — but for the energy shock, inflation would be heading lower," said Jamie Cox, managing partner for Harris Financial Group. "The Fed made the right call yesterday to stand pat on rates."

## Majority of S&P 500 sectors in red despite huge overall gains

With less than an hour to go in the trading day, six of the 11 S&P 500 sectors were in negative territory, led by Communication Services (down 2.6%) and Consumer Staples (down 2.2%). The index's overall gain was driven almost entirely by Information Technology, up 5.1%.

## Microsoft stock soars on solid Q4 results

Microsoft shares rose nearly 10% (later extending to roughly 16%) a day after the company topped estimates: adjusted EPS of $4.74 on $90.01 billion in revenue, versus $4.25 and $87.7 billion expected. Intelligent Cloud revenue was $39.3 billion, about $1 billion above estimates, as Azure and other cloud services revenue rose 43%. JPMorgan called the results a standout for balancing revenue growth with margin management; Citi called it a "solid rebuttal to the bear case" for Microsoft.

## Meta's stock tumbles over 10% after profits disappoint

Meta posted Q2 EPS of $6.18, below the $7.19 expected, despite record revenue of $60.80 billion. Costs surged 55% on AI infrastructure investment; the company also booked $2.40 billion in legal charges and $1.18 billion in severance after layoffs of 8,000 employees in May. Goldman Sachs called the results a "solid indicator" of AI's application to Meta's core business but not the outsized returns some expected.

## Other notes

Second-quarter GDP growth came in below expectations, driven partly by rising imports (a spending spree on imported computer equipment tied to the AI investment boom) subtracting from the GDP calculation. Starbucks shares rose on a stronger-than-expected turnaround quarter; Stellantis reaffirmed full-year guidance despite solid first-half results, sending shares lower; AB InBev shares fell despite beating estimates, as it only affirmed (rather than raised) full-year guidance.
