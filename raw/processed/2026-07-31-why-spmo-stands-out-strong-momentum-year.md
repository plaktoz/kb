# Why SPMO Stands Out in a Strong Momentum Year

Momentum has emerged as one of the strongest-performing equity factors this year. As investors continue rewarding companies with sustained relative strength despite persistent macroeconomic uncertainty, ETFs such as the Invesco S&P 500 Momentum ETF (SPMO) are drawing investor attention.

## How momentum investing follows market leadership

Momentum investing isn't about predicting the next winner — it's about identifying companies already outperforming peers with characteristics to sustain that performance. While the AI theme continues to drive markets, leadership has broadened into industrials, financials, and cyclical sectors, and investors are rewarding stocks with strong relative performance. SPMO has attracted significant investor interest, gathering approximately $1.02 billion in net inflows over the past month and $3.59 billion over the past three months.

## How SPMO works

SPMO tracks the S&P 500 Momentum Index, which selects and weights stocks based on risk-adjusted price momentum rather than market capitalization. Unlike broader momentum strategies drawing from large- and midcap stocks, SPMO focuses exclusively on S&P 500 companies and weights holdings according to momentum characteristics rather than size — meaning it can capture more exposure to market leaders when returns are concentrated among a small group of companies, as has been the case with high-performing technology and growth names.

## SPMO vs. MTUM vs. the broader S&P 500

Over the past 12 months, SPMO delivered a 26.89% return versus 26.18% for the iShares MSCI USA Momentum Factor ETF (MTUM), and both outperformed the S&P 500 Total Return Index's 17.43%. The difference highlights how momentum strategies vary depending on underlying index methodology and portfolio construction — SPMO focuses exclusively on the S&P 500 with momentum-based weighting, while MTUM uses a broader large- and midcap universe with a risk-adjusted momentum approach.

## Why semiannual rebalancing sets SPMO apart

SPMO's recent edge has been supported by higher exposure to some of the market's strongest-performing companies. Because the index rebalances semiannually, the portfolio refreshes as market leadership evolves, helping maintain exposure to stocks with persistent momentum characteristics while following a transparent, systematic methodology that reduces emotional decision-making. SPMO carries an expense ratio of 0.13%.
