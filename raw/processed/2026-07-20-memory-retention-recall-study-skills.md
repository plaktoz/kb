# Study Skills: Memory, Retention & Recall

Your brain is constantly recording new information, and it frequently needs to discard information that is not being used. Some studies suggest humans forget approximately 50% of new information within an hour of learning it. Within 24 hours, nearly 80% is forgotten, and without revisiting information, only about 5% is recalled 30 days later.

## The Forgetting Curve
Hermann Ebbinghaus, a 19th century psychologist, tested his own memory over various time periods and found memories decay rapidly hour by hour and day by day. Key findings from his Forgetting Curve research:
- Memories disappear with time — retention drops most dramatically immediately after learning.
- It's easier to remember things that have meaning; content with little relevance to the learner disappears fastest.
- The way something is presented affects learning — logically organized, clearly presented material is easier to remember.
- Energy and emotion affect retention — physiological factors like stress, diet, and sleep are important.
- Active learning and critical thinking help retention — there is a measurable difference between passive and active learning in how much students retain. Recall is reinforced by higher-order techniques such as applying, explaining, evaluating, and creating.

## Improving Retention through Spacing
Spaced (distributed) learning — practicing a skill or retrieving information spread over time rather than repeated in a short period — has been proven to slow the forgetting process and is critical to consolidating memories.

## The Role of Self-Care in Retention and Recall
Academic success ties closely to retention and recall, both of which are influenced by physiological factors:
- **Sleep**: Sleep amount is one of the strongest predictors of academic success. Sleep helps fix and consolidate memories and prevents their decay. A study of nurses found people who slept five hours or less, or nine hours or more, performed worse on cognitive tests.
- **Diet**: Energy foods like nuts and berries help sustain focus; processed foods, refined sugar, and caffeine can cause dehydration and fuel anxiety, undermining focus.
- **Exercise**: Moderate to vigorous exercise releases dopamine, sharpening the ability to learn and think quickly; done before an assessment, it can boost academic performance.

## The Power of Previewing
Previewing course materials (chapters, articles, videos) before a lecture increases retention and comprehension and prepares learners to more actively engage in class.
