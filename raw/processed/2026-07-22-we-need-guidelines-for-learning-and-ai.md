# We Need Guidelines for Learning and AI

source_url: https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2026/07/22/we-need-guidelines-for-learning-and-ai/

---

Author: Scott H. Young
Published: July 22, 2026

Young argues that AI presents both enormous opportunity and serious risk for learners. Drawing on Carl Hendrick's essay, he uses the autopilot analogy: the danger isn't automation itself, but over-dependency on it — especially for novices.

He questions whether there's an optimal amount of AI assistance. His view evolved between his 2019 book (Ultralearning, favoring struggle) and 2024 (Get Better at Anything), where he acknowledges that excessive cognitive load can slow learning, making timely help beneficial.

## Five Tentative Guidelines

1. **You can't learn from work you don't do yourself** — AI completing tasks in toto produces no skill growth.
2. **Don't use AI on your first attempt** — especially in creative work, where early AI input shapes thinking and bypasses judgment-building.
3. **Don't use AI on your last attempt** — after failure, AI help is useful, but must be followed by an unaided retry.
4. **Do use AI to surface alternatives** — its breadth can reveal overlooked methods, papers, or ideas.
5. **Do use AI to clarify confusion** — post-attempt, AI feedback on mistakes mirrors expert review.

## Learning Loop Framework

He frames learning as a See → Do → Feedback loop: AI can assist the first and third steps, but cannot replace doing the work yourself.
