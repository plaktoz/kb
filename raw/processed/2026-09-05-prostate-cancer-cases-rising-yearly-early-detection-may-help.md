# Prostate Cancer Cases Rising 3% Yearly, Early Detection May Help

The American Cancer Society (ACS) projects 333,830 new prostate cancer cases in 2026, with 36,320 deaths. Cases rose roughly 3% annually from 2014–2022, with the sharpest increases in advanced-stage diagnoses.

ACS Chief Scientific Officer William Dahut noted that screening conversations are happening "too late for too many men, particularly those at high risk."

## Key Statistics

- 1 in 8 men will be diagnosed in their lifetime
- Over 99% five-year survival rate when caught before distant spread
- Black men face nearly 70% higher incidence than white men

Urologic oncologist S. Adam Ramin emphasized that early detection yields more treatment options, higher cure rates, and less aggressive interventions. Localized prostate cancer carries a nearly 100% five-year survival rate.

## Screening Guidelines (ACS)

- Age 50: average-risk males
- Age 45: high-risk males
- Age 40: highest-risk males (e.g., multiple affected relatives)

Screening typically begins with a PSA blood test, potentially followed by DRE, MRI, or biopsy if results are abnormal.
