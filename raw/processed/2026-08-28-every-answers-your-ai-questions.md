# 33 Questions Executives Ask About AI—Answered

Every's consulting team addressed 400 executives on AI adoption strategy, covering skeptics, governance, tool selection, and organizational restructuring.

## Key Themes & Takeaways

**Strategy**
- AI is described as "a tool, not a strategy" — it should serve your existing roadmap, not replace it.
- Focus on AI champions rather than convincing skeptics; peer demonstration drives adoption organically.
- Two productivity types matter: doing existing work faster, and pursuing previously impossible work.

**Fluency & Training**
- Eight levels of AI adoption exist; most workers remain at levels 1–2 (basic ChatGPT use).
- Best early wins are "boring and frequent": meeting prep, summaries, inbox triage, structured tables.
- Avoid abstract AI training — have people build something useful with their *real* work immediately.

**Tools & Platforms**
- Recommend selecting one platform; switching costs include retraining staff and rewriting skills.
- Novices don't need cutting-edge tools — "being a month or two behind the frontier is absolutely fine."
- Surprise insight: code generation advanced faster than writing quality, contrary to earlier expectations.

**Governance**
- AI can *increase* transparency in regulated environments via activity logs and automated reporting.
- Keep IT as a design partner; distinguish between low-risk writing tools and high-risk database-access systems.
- For token economics: use frontier models for new/complex tasks; optimize to smaller models for repetitive ones.

**Measuring Impact**
- No single metric works; measure cycle time, error rates, rework frequency, NPS, and skill reuse rates.
- Establish a pre-AI baseline before deployment, then track actual improvement.

**Knowledge Management**
- AI "exposes knowledge debt" but doesn't fix it. Start with 10–20 high-priority sources, assign owners, add freshness dates.
- Context is not a "warehouse" — every document must justify its inclusion.

**Ownership**
- Both a central AI team and distributed business-team ownership are necessary; neither alone works.
- For a 200–500 person org, start with executives, then target 2–3 teams with painful, high-value repetitive work.

## Notable Quotes

- On vision: *"AI is a tool, not a strategy."*
- On knowledge debt: *"AI exposes knowledge debt, and it does not magically fix it."*
- On external consultants: deliverables should include "skill files, the evals, the decision log, the operating instructions"—not just a working demo.
