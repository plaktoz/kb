# Sierra raises $1.75B, doubling valuation to $30B in six months

**What's the deal?** Sierra, the enterprise customer-service AI startup co-founded by former Salesforce co-CEO Bret Taylor, has raised $1.75 billion in a Series F round at a $30 billion valuation. Greenoaks and Lightspeed Venture Partners led the round, with Benchmark, Sequoia, GV, and Tiger Global — all prior backers — participating.

**Why now?** The raise lands less than seven months after a $950 million round that valued Sierra at $15.8 billion. The new deal doubles that figure.

**By the numbers:** Sierra said its annualised recurring revenue topped $400 million in October, adding more than $150 million in the third quarter alone. At the new valuation, that implies roughly 75 times revenue — though the figure is not audited full-year revenue.

**What's the endgame?** Sierra builds AI agents it calls a "digital front door" for companies, charging for outcomes rather than usage. It says it serves nearly half of Fortune 50 companies, including BNY, HSBC, Wells Fargo, and JPMorganChase. The new cash will fund its technology stack and expand sales to sign enterprise contracts faster.

**What could go wrong?** The $30 billion price tag sets a high bar for sustaining growth and proving the economics of automated service. Sierra competes with a crowded field of well-funded rivals.

**The signal:** Rivals are drawing heavy capital too — ElevenLabs is valued at $22 billion, Wonderful at $5 billion, and Decagon at $4.5 billion. Sierra's round ranks among the largest Series F deals ever for a US machine-learning company, a clear marker of investor conviction in enterprise AI agents.
