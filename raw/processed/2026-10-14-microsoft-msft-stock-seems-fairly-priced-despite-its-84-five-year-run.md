# Microsoft (MSFT) Stock Seems Fairly Priced Despite Its 84% Five Year Run

Microsoft has ridden the AI buildout and cloud story to a share price of US$543.45, which puts the focus squarely on a simple question for investors: Are today’s cash flows, and what the business can realistically earn in the years ahead, enough to justify that price tag?

Over the past 5 years, Microsoft has delivered a total return of 83.7%, so the cash flow case now needs to support gains that are already substantial.

The plan to roll out the Maia 300 AI chip at multi gigawatt scale and the report of more than US$130b in recent data center lease commitments can reshape future cash generation by changing both AI infrastructure costs and capital needs.

The issue now is whether the Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) view of Microsoft’s future cash streams lines up with where the stock trades today.

## Where Does Microsoft Sit on Cash Flow?

The Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) model here values Microsoft by projecting what cash it can return to shareholders over time and then discounting those streams back to today. On this view, the latest twelve month free cash flow sits at about $96.0b, and the forecast path assumes that figure grows rather than shrinks from current levels.

Those projections build in a sizeable ramp in future free cash flows through the early 2030s, which implies Microsoft is expected to keep funding heavy AI and data center spending while still returning rising cash to equity holders. Because the DCF projections put Microsoft's estimated intrinsic value broadly in line with the current share price of $543.45, the model suggests investors are already paying up for that cash flow profile. The plan to deploy the Maia 300 AI chip at multi gigawatt scale, aimed at tightening control over AI infrastructure costs, helps explain why the market is comfortable valuing those future cash flows where it does today.
