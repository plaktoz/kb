# Daily whole grains could support heart health, weight loss

A meta-analysis published in the *European Heart Journal* examined over 6,500 participants across randomized controlled trials, finding that consuming 60–100 grams (4–6 servings) of whole grains daily was linked to improvements in multiple cardiovascular risk factors.

**High-certainty evidence** showed reductions in:
- Body weight
- Waist circumference
- Total cholesterol
- Interleukin-6 (IL-6), an inflammation marker

**Moderate-certainty evidence** showed improvements in LDL cholesterol, triglycerides, systolic blood pressure, and fasting plasma glucose.

Lead researcher Helda Tutunchi, PhD, described it as "the largest and most comprehensive meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials on wholegrain intake."

Cardiologist Joseph You noted the findings show a possibly dose-dependent correlation, suggesting whole grain consumption may reflect "an overall healthier and balanced lifestyle."

**Limitations:**
- Median trial duration was only 8 weeks — long-term effects remain unclear
- Few studies examined intakes above 140g/day

**Recommended whole grain sources:** Brown rice, quinoa, whole grain pasta, whole grain pita, and cereals made with buckwheat or spelt.
