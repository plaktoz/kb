# AI Apps Shift to Specialized Niches Amid Big Tech Dominance

*As Google, Canva Integrate AI, Startups Target Adult Content and Professional Fields*

The era where simply creating a single AI app capable of summarizing documents and generating images could attract users has passed. The competitive dynamics of the AI service market are shifting toward an era where only companies with proprietary technology or specialized expertise can survive. This change is driven by the integration of AI features into core services by big tech firms like Google and existing software companies such as Canva and Notion, reducing the need for consumers to install separate apps. New AI startups are now seeking business opportunities in niches underserved by major platforms, including adult content, security, and professional fields.

## Rising Barriers for Newcomers to Climb the Ranks

According to the tech industry, only 11 services newly entered the "Top 100 Generative AI Consumer Apps" report recently published by U.S. venture capital firm Andreessen Horowitz (a16z). This is the lowest figure since the survey began in September 2023. a16z noted, "After tracking consumer AI for three years, the rankings have stabilized." This does not mean fewer AI apps are being launched, but rather that established services with existing user bases are maintaining their top positions, making it harder for newcomers to break into the popular app rankings.

Notably, existing software companies are showing significant growth. Design platform Canva and task management service Notion ranked in the top 10 for web traffic, while design collaboration tool Figma entered the top 15. These companies, which had already secured user bases before the rise of AI, are now adding AI features to their document creation, design, and organization tools. For users, this reduces the need to switch to separate AI apps beyond their usual programs.

Google also placed five services—Gemini, Gemini Notebook (NotebookLM), AI Studio, Labs, and AntiGravity—within the top 50 for web traffic. As big tech directly provides AI services spanning search, document creation, and development tools, the scope of competition for startups is expanding.

Major AI companies are expanding their service areas by targeting different customer segments. According to a16z, OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic collectively launched 127 new products and features over the past six months. Anthropic strengthened capabilities for professional knowledge work like coding and research, Google expanded into creative fields such as music and image generation, and OpenAI broadened its reach into enterprise tools and consumer services like health, finance, and job searches.

## Targeting Niche Markets Resistant to Replacement

Startups are focusing on specialized markets that are difficult for large AI companies to replicate. OpenEvidence, which provides medical information to healthcare professionals, newly entered the rankings at 47th place in web traffic. Venice, an AI service emphasizing personal data protection, rose to 43rd.

Adult AI content is also forming a separate market. a16z excluded NSFW (Not Safe For Work) AI services from this survey, but noted that such services would have occupied over 20% of the top 50 list if included. While major AI companies restrict adult content due to policy constraints, independent services targeting this demand are gaining users.

Industry experts predict that as AI becomes a standard feature of existing software, startups will face increasing pressure to differentiate. Rather than simply adding features to chatbots, proprietary models, specialized data, and services optimized for specific professions will become critical. A tech industry source stated, "As entry barriers in the AI app market rise, a structure is forming where big tech's general-purpose services and startups' specialized services compete in distinct domains."
