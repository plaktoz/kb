# Factor Views 3Q 2026

Factors posted a positive second quarter on average, extending a strong run alongside risk assets' strong performance. Equity momentum stood out, driving equity markets higher despite challenging conditions for value and quality. Macro factors gained, led by carry (FX and commodity) with momentum also positive in aggregate. J.P. Morgan Asset Management maintains a positive outlook on factors overall, flags equity value and quality as historically inexpensive, and recommends diversification given elevated factor volatility.

## Equity momentum became the central market story

Momentum extended its best multi-year run since the dot-com bubble, the only factor delivering positive performance across all global developed and emerging markets. Global equity markets rose about 15% in Q2 — the strongest quarter since the 2020 COVID recovery — with roughly two-thirds of gains from the tech sector; some semiconductor and memory chip makers gained 100%–200%. Momentum leadership has rotated from hyperscalers (2024) to memory chip makers (2026) as AI capex beneficiaries. J.P. Morgan is developing a proprietary "Momentum Fragility Index" (incorporating options market data) to assess crash risk; momentum factor volatility has risen to six-year highs and factor dispersion is high relative to history, raising reversal risk. Recommendation: trim momentum overweights but don't abandon the factor, given its diversification value versus value and quality.

## Equity value and equity quality both cheap but underperforming

Equity value stumbled globally after three positive quarters, with year-to-date returns negative outside the U.S. The global value factor is nearly one standard deviation cheap versus history (sector-neutral); in the U.S., more than one standard deviation cheap, near record lows outside the dot-com and post-COVID bubbles. Two paths could normalize valuations: value stocks outperforming (the historically more common pattern following wide valuation spreads) or value companies' earnings deteriorating into "value traps." J.P. Morgan stays humble but maintains value exposure, just not as overweight as valuations alone would suggest.

Equity quality posted another weak quarter, its worst stretch since the post-COVID bubble, declining in all major regions except the UK and Japan — now about one standard deviation cheap globally, more so in the U.S. Elevated dispersion in profitability metrics may be making high-quality companies AI-disruption targets, pressuring their prices. J.P. Morgan remains optimistic on quality given attractive valuations and strong fundamentals broadly, despite acknowledging some companies will be disrupted.

## Macro factors: carry led, momentum also positive

FX carry was the top macro performer, driven by G10 positioning (Australian dollar, British pound gains; Japanese yen at a 40-year low) and EM FX carry (Colombian and Mexican peso gains, Korean won weak on foreign capital outflows). Commodity carry was strong in June, led by livestock (cattle import suspension from Mexico due to screwworm cases; lean hog short covering), while negative-carry crops (corn, wheat, sugar) underperformed on favorable harvests and weak demand. Macro momentum recovered from a difficult March but ended June lower after a hawkish press conference from new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh disrupted markets; time-series momentum rebuilt equity exposure and moved to neutral on commodities.

## Outlook

J.P. Morgan sees generally attractive prospects for equity factors, led by value and quality valuations, but flags elevated risk in momentum (and equity factors broadly), favoring diversification over outsized positions. Macro factors are rated broadly neutral, also favoring diversification. The firm maintains a pro-risk posture given an intact global expansion, viewing factors as an attractive source of diversifying returns — directly relevant to momentum-factor holdings like SPMO and IWMO, and broad-market holdings like CSPX and VWRA.
