# Satya Nadella's Microsoft Stock Jumped 18% in a Week After Azure's Annual Revenue Topped $100 Billion for the First Time. Is It Still a Buy?

Microsoft (MSFT) converted massive AI spending into impressive AI revenue where some peers have struggled. The company reported its Azure cloud business topped $100 billion in annual sales for the first time in fiscal 2026, a 41% jump from 2025, outpacing expectations and boosting investor optimism about Microsoft's AI strategy. Shares popped 18% in a week and are up about 29% over the past month.

Investors had grown skeptical that tech giants — Microsoft, Meta, Alphabet, and others — were overspending on AI infrastructure with little to show for it. Microsoft spent $175 billion (adjusted from $190 billion due to an accounting change) in capex for fiscal 2026, part of an estimated $750 billion industry-wide AI capex this year. For many peers, high capex has hurt free cash flow: Alphabet's free cash flow fell to negative $5.9 billion in its most recent quarter (from +$25 billion a year prior), and Meta's free cash flow fell 91% to just $784 million in Q2 2026. Microsoft's capex rose just 23%, and it still posted positive free cash flow of $19.6 billion in fiscal Q4 2026 — the financial stability AI investors are looking for.

Microsoft's stock has a trailing P/E of 28, below the technology sector average of 35, making it relatively inexpensive despite recent gains. The author's take: Microsoft isn't a buy just because it's cheap, but because it has demonstrated it can invest heavily in AI while actually generating revenue without destroying free cash flow — a playbook other tech companies are still trying to match.

Key data (as of Aug 16, 2026): MSFT $495.40, market cap $3.7T, 52-week range $349.20–$553.72, gross margin 67.94%, dividend yield 0.72%.
