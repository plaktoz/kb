# The Questions You Should Be Asking About the AI Bubble

The article's central premise challenges readers to look beyond the common question of whether an AI capital expenditure bubble exists, suggesting the more critical issue is whether such a bubble poses genuine macroeconomic danger.

Executives globally are debating AI infrastructure spending, with data center investment estimates ranging from "hundreds of billions to lately several trillion dollars." This has fueled anxiety around compute overcapacity, poor returns, and potential recession.

The piece redirects focus from bubble *existence* to bubble *consequence* — essentially arguing that the framing of the debate itself may be flawed. The distinction matters: a bubble that bursts without systemic financial contagion (e.g., the dot-com bust's limited banking exposure) differs fundamentally from one with broad economic fallout.

Both authors are BCG economists and co-wrote *Shocks, Crises, and False Alarms* (HBR Press, 2024).
