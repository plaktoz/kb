# Can an AI-Powered Scribe Curb Physician Burnout?

Harvard Business Review / Cold Call Podcast, Episode 289. Guest: Professor Susanna Gallani, Harvard Business School.

Mass General Brigham (MGB), Massachusetts' largest health system with ~12,000 physicians, piloted an AI ambient note-taking program to address clinician burnout driven by electronic health record documentation burdens.

**How it works:** A phone app records physician-patient conversations, which an AI engine transcribes and converts into clinical notes, billing codes, and prescription suggestions — all pending physician sign-off. The audio is then deleted.

**Key findings from the pilot (~800 self-selected clinicians):**
- Significant drop in burnout measures
- More family time; less "pajama time" documentation after hours
- Improved physician-patient eye contact and engagement

**Challenges identified:**
- Physicians must verbalize clinical thinking aloud, changing workflow
- AI-generated notes don't always match individual physician "voice"
- Patient privacy concerns about recorded conversations
- Fear that reclaimed time will simply be filled with more patient volume
- Low adoption among non-self-selected users, even with licenses

**Broader lessons Gallani emphasized:**
- Lead with the human problem you're solving, not the technology
- Build trust before scaling
- Consider how AI affects trainee development — in medicine and law
- Avoid assuming enthusiasm will be universal; adoption is highly variable

As Gallani noted, this may be "the first innovation in healthcare that is improving care without asking physicians to do more."
