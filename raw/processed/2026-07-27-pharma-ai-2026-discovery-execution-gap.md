# Q&A: Pharma AI in 2026: Discovery Solved, Execution Still Manual

**Source:** https://www.pharmtech.com/view/pharma-ai-in-2026-discovery-solved-execution-still-manual  
**Author:** Megha Sinha (Kolter AI), Zachary Zubulake (PharmTech)  
**Date:** 2026-07-27

---

Megha Sinha, founder and CEO of Kolter AI, reviews her 2026 pharma AI predictions at the Q2 midpoint.

**On real-time tariff decision engines:**  
Half the prediction came true — the easier half. Companies like Merck, Novartis, and BMS run real-time what-if simulations for supply chain planning; the digital twin market exceeded $1 billion. But these systems model shipments, inventory, and capacity — they are not living tariff decision engines. Tariff planning still happens in quarterly reviews after disruption hits. None reach the regulatory consequences: label updates, grace periods, and country-by-country submission waves a tariff change triggers.

**On manufacturing AI:**  
Agentic AI inside plants is delivering. Systems detect an out-of-spec result, pull batch records, cross-reference historical deviations, draft a root cause analysis, and route it to the right reviewer — cutting investigation time by 50% or more in validated GxP environments. But even the best deployments reason locally. They translate a deviation into its financial and supply impact, but none recognize that a proposed "minor" change might be a major variation requiring prior approval in Brazil or a six-month grace period in Germany.

**What surprised Sinha most in 2026:**  
"The bottleneck moved. For three years, the assumed constraint was model capability. This year, the technology has arrived, and the binding constraint is organizational readiness, employee sentiment, and data." The shift from "if AI can do it" to "ROI math after 18 months of hype" happened fast. Investment poured into AI drug discovery and planning pilots, while the hardest, most valuable problem — computing execution of a geo expansion or lifecycle change across functions and markets — drew almost no capital.

**Prediction for 2030:**  
By 2030, the operational landscape can't stay fragmented. A "connective layer" — a nervous system carrying a single change from one function to the next and out across every market it touches — is needed. New tariffs and reshoring incentives are already forcing network redesign faster than teams can absorb. "Strategy moves at the speed of the boardroom; execution still moves at the speed of a spreadsheet."

**Key insight:**  
The industry proved AI can design a molecule and draft a deviation report — and left compliant execution across 60 markets exactly where it's always been: manual, tribal, reinvented on every program.
