## Summary

Twenty-five Fields Medal recipients co-signed an open letter at [mathandai.org](https://mathandai.org) expressing concern that AI labs are undermining mathematical intellectual work through competitive proof-racing.

## Key Developments

- NYU professor Tristan Buckmaster accused OpenAI of pressuring him to exclude an Anthropic collaborator from credit on a significant math problem, and raised suspicions that their Codex work may have informed OpenAI's own [Navier-Stokes proof](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/)
- OpenAI pulled its sponsorship of a CalTech math event after facing researcher criticism
- OpenAI's proof remains **unverified**

## Core Concerns from the Open Letter

The mathematicians warn that AI-driven proofs are being rushed out, leaving no time for proper writeups, attribution, or integration of prior work. They argue:

> "Without the willing mathematicians who must take care of their development...AI-conceived ideas would never become fully alive."

They also caution that the broader pattern mirrors challenges facing all creative and scientific fields — the risk of losing sight of *why* the work matters in the first place.

## Broader Context

- The **Leiden Declaration** (June 2026) offered related guidance for mathematicians, institutions, and policymakers
- Researchers fear that big labs' ability to spend tens of millions outpacing individual researchers will push academia toward secrecy
- The mathematicians frame this as a warning to all professions: their field is simply experiencing first what others will face next
