# Should You Learn Physics Like Newton? Contrasting Expert and Beginner Learning Strategies

Discovery is different from learning, and therefore requires different skills — and learning at different levels of expertise requires different skills too. Expert learners and beginners face very different challenges and should use different strategies.

## Mastery and the First 20 Hours

Josh Kaufman's book The First 20 Hours documents getting past the initial frustration barrier with a new skill — in contrast to the ten thousand hours supposedly required for expertise, comfort can come after just twenty. But mastery isn't just repeating the first twenty hours 500 times; mastering a skill requires different strategies than beginner skill acquisition.

## Learning as a Beginner

Beginners have advantages: a glut of learning opportunities (it's easy to find something you haven't learned when you know nothing), and the biggest opportunities for performance improvement haven't been used up yet. Disadvantages include a lack of context for where knowledge fits into a bigger picture, low meta-knowledge on how to learn successfully in the domain, and negative feedback overwhelming motivation to practice. These disadvantages are more easily hacked away since they involve finding good teachers, material, and learning environments rather than structural difficulties.

Strategies: (1) Use the skill as early as possible — get positive feedback and higher context by actually using it in a practical environment. (2) Slow down and break down anything you don't understand into its components. (3) Set up regular habits for practice — even a suboptimal approach yields enormous growth early on.

## Advanced Learning

Advanced learners face structural difficulties: context is known, feedback is generally positive, and meta-learning strategies are already established. Complacency is the biggest problem — simple participation stops ensuring growth; it takes deliberate practice to shift to the next level.

Strategies: (1) Separate work from practice — most applications of your skill are no longer "practice"; do side projects or change your environment to force yourself back to a beginner phase. (2) Set a "level up" goal to force uncomfortable habit changes. (3) Learn complementary skills — expertise is thousands of microskills, so shifting to a highly related skill can improve the core one, though skills too far afield or with contradicting rules and conditions can backfire.

## Should You Become an Expert?

It's a mistake to commit to mastering every skill you start. Core career skills reap the highest rewards from mastery, but many others are still rewarding at post-beginner levels. Mastery takes 10-1000 times as much time as intermediacy, so most people will only truly master a handful of skills in a lifetime — recognizing which learning obstacles apply, beginner or advanced, is key to choosing the right strategy.
