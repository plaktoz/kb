# Stocks surge in best day since 2025, adding trillions in value after Jensen Huang returns to Nvidia

Major U.S. stock indexes soared Monday to their best day since April 2025, as Wall Street cheered Jensen Huang’s return to Nvidia.

The S&P 500 rallied 4.7%, while the Nasdaq 100 index surged almost 7%. The Dow, of which Nvidia is a large component, soared about 1,450 points.

The broad moves higher also came as markets sought a rebound after AI-linked stocks dragged major indexes into correction territory Friday.

The surprise announcement from Nvidia caused its stock to close up 31.5%, which added about $950 billion of value to the company, the largest one-day gain in market history.

For perspective, the $950 billion in value Nvidia shares gained Monday is equivalent to more than twice the size of Coca-Cola, and just slightly less than the entire market capitalization of Walmart. It’s also the size of five Walt Disney’s, or three times the value of Bank of America.

Nvidia’s surge also pushed the entire tech sector higher. Shares of companies in the sector collectively gained roughly $2.7 trillion in market value on Monday alone.

Even with the day’s jump higher, Nvidia shares are still down 30% from their record high on Oct. 6. That drop leaves the company about $1.7 trillion smaller today than it was in early October.

Huang’s reinstatement at Nvidia came at the urging of President Donald Trump, who took credit for his return in an interview earlier on Monday morning.

In an interview on CNBC, Trump said the market’s recent slump, led by major tech stocks, “probably” impacted his decision to get involved.

Evercore ISI technical analyst Rich Ross wrote, “The return of Jensen Huang removes the single biggest factor weighing on AI, Semis, Tech, Growth and Risk,” in a note Monday.

Analysts at Stifel viewed Huang’s reinstatement as “removing a key-person overhang on the AI infrastructure roadmap.”

It wasn’t just tech stocks that rallied, however. Every sector of the S&P 500, save for energy and consumer staples, rose Monday.

Twenty-six of the 30 companies that make up the Dow also ended the day higher.

Despite Monday’s exceptionally rare rally, the Nasdaq 100 remains in correction territory, down almost 15% from its last record high.

This was not the case for the S&P 500, however, whose gains Monday helped to pull it out of correction, which is defined as a drop of more than 10% from the most recent high.

The Philadelphia Semiconductor index rose 14%, while memory stocks spiked higher by 18%. Dell Technologies, a primary supplier of Nvidia-chip powered servers for data centers, soared 21%.

Other stocks heavily linked to the AI trade, such as Oracle, CoreWeave, Nebius and industrial construction firm Caterpillar also rose sharply, on the perception that Huang’s return would reignite optimism for the sector.

The moves higher also marks a sharp recovery from significant selling seen Friday, driven largely by AI-linked stocks.

On Friday, the Nasdaq 100 suffered its worst one-week drop since 2020, wiping out its gains for the year. Meanwhile, the S&P 500 and Dow fell into correction territory, a 10% drop from their most recent record highs.

Before Monday, the S&P 500 had seen little upward momentum recently, having fallen for seven straight days, its longest run of down days since the Covid pandemic began in February 2020, Deutsche Bank’s Jim Reid noted.

The broad selling started in earnest Thursday once shares of frontier AI developer Anthropic made their market debut. While the company’s shares posted a 17% jump in its first day, shares closed less than $6 away from its IPO price Friday.

Anthropic shares bucked the positive market momentum Monday, ending the day lower by 1.4%.

“Last week was brutal,” said Ed Yardeni, a Wall Street watcher and founder of Yardeni Research.

He said that he had “expected a pullback” but got more than he bargained for. Yardeni sounded a note of optimism though, writing that he doesn’t think last week’s moves into correction territory will become a “bear market, simply because we don’t see an impending recession.” A bear market is when an index or stock falls 20% from its last record high.

Still, Yardeni, one of the most optimistic analysts on Wall Street this year, cut his year-end S&P 500 target for the second time since September. He now sees the benchmark index ending 2026 at 7,500, which would be a gain of more than 8.5% from current levels. As of Friday, the S&P 500 is holding on to a less than 1% gain for the year.

Some analysts are warning that even if this is ultimately a correction and doesn’t lead to a more dramatic bear market or worse, “the process may still take time,” as Reid wrote Monday morning. “Similar volatility shocks have typically taken 6-8 weeks to subside,” he wrote, adding that “history would argue that further downside and volatility remain plausible before a durable bottom forms.”

Jonathan Krinsky, chief market technician at BTIG, wrote that “extreme one-day gains like today are more often associated with markets that are trending down, not up.”

Until Monday’s announcement, Nvidia had also been providing significant headwinds to markets, after the brief removal of Huang, after admitting that he was unfaithful to his wife. One of the women who Huang allegedly had a relationship with was a suspected Chinese asset, which has thrown the company whose products effectively underpin the entire artificial intelligence boom into turmoil.

Nvidia shares sank almost 23% last week, deepening its losses for the year to 33%.

The revelations related to Huang have reportedly sparked an investigation of the company by the Securities and Exchange Commission, according to multiple reports. Nvidia did not respond to requests for comment, while the SEC declined to comment Friday regarding the reports.

The selling in Nvidia shares have also driven its peers lower. “The fear is that tighter export controls will lower the demand for chips and depress their prices,” Yardeni said of the drop in chip stocks.

One of the potential candidates to replace Huang at Nvidia took himself out of the running Monday shortly before Nvidia’s own announcement. Arm Holdings announced early Monday that CEO Rene Haas signed a new contract that runs through 2033. Shares of Arm, one of Nvidia’s largest peers, jumped 20% in early trading.

After falling at the end of last week, U.S. Treasury yields also rose early Monday. Treasuries are often seen by investors as a safe haven in times of market turmoil, leading to higher demand and lower yields.
