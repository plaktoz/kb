# Wall St ends sharply lower on AI sell-off, S&P 500, Dow in correction

NEW YORK – US stocks extended their rout on Nov 20, and the S&P 500 and the Dow both confirmed corrections after Anthropic's momentous debut fell flat, exacerbating worries about the AI trade that has driven this year's stock market rally.

All three major US equity indexes ended sharply lower.

The Dow and the S&P 500 are now more than 10% below their record closing highs, confirming both indexes have entered a correction.

The bellwether S&P 500 notched its seventh consecutive drop, its longest losing streak since February 2020.

The S&P 500 and the Dow logged their largest weekly percentage losses since April 2025, when US President Donald Trump announced his market-rattling "liberation day" tariff package.

The Nasdaq's weekly drop was its largest since March 2020. The tech-heavy index has dipped into negative territory for the year.

"This is clearly a challenging day for capital markets, and as we head into a Friday close, appears to be accelerating to a certain degree," said Bill Northey, senior investment director at US Bank Wealth Management in Billings, Montana.

"But as we step back and evaluate fundamentals and sentiment, sentiment is clearly winning the day today."

"Sentiment is always the more powerful force in the immediate space, whereas fundamentals tend to win out over time," Northey added, referring to economic strength and solid corporate earnings, among other things.

Anthropic's supersized debut as a publicly held company, which was intended to raise as much as US$2 trillion (S$2.5 trillion) in market cap, failed to catch fire on Nov 19, closing 17.1% above its US$83 offer price as enthusiasm for the stock faded throughout the session.

In its second day as a publicly held company, Anthropic's shares fell 9.1%. Nvidia's shares tumbled 13.8%.

The Anthropic IPO, combined with Nvidia's quarterly earnings report, failed to dispel fears over risks associated with artificial intelligence and the massive expenditures on the nascent technology.

"Nvidia reported what might have been one of the greatest quarters in corporate earnings history; they did everything right," said Chuck Carlson, chief executive officer at Horizon Investment Services in Hammond, Indiana. "A lot of people, myself included, were looking to the Anthropic debut to some extent as a clearing event, and it wasn't."

The Philadelphia SE Semiconductor Index, which has powered much of the broader stock market's gains in recent months, was up as much as 106.6% in mid-June. That lustre has faded, with the index's year-to-date advance having shrunk to 18.7%.

Among hyperscalers, Amazon fell 3.9%, while Meta dropped 5.9%.

Neocloud firm CoreWeave fell 12.1% and its peer Nebius slid 16.1%.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 949.73 points, or 1.93%, to 48,147.33, the S&P 500 lost 265.41 points, or 3.70%, to 6,910.42 and the Nasdaq Composite lost 1,261.47 points, or 5.28%, to 22,617.73.

The CBOE Market Volatility Index, often referred to as Wall Street's "fear gauge," rose to its highest close since April 7, 2025.

## Inflation, US Fed in focus

Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook said the central bank's policy rate is not high enough to deal with inflation levels.

Markets are pricing a near-certain likelihood of a 25-basis-point interest rate hike at the Fed's next meeting in December, according to CME Group's FedWatch tool.

Next week's personal consumption expenditures (PCE) figures will provide further clarity on the Fed's monetary policy path after a hotter-than-expected October inflation report last week.

Among other movers, Intuit shares fell 12.8% after the company cut its annual revenue growth forecast and agreed to sell Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for about US$3.68 billion, five years after acquiring it for nearly three times as much.

UnitedHealth rose 2.8% after TD Cowen upgraded its shares to a "buy" from a "hold" rating.

The AI sell-off also sent Caterpillar 5% lower on worries over weakening demand for its power-generating and backup power equipment.

Cryptocurrency firms including Coinbase, Strategy and Robinhood tumbled between 6.3% and 9.6% after bitcoin dropped below the $60,000 level.

Declining issues outnumbered advancers by a 3.77-to-1 ratio on the NYSE. There were 38 new highs and 1,199 new lows on the NYSE.

On the Nasdaq, 928 stocks rose and 3,964 fell as declining issues outnumbered advancers by a 4.27-to-1 ratio.

The S&P 500 posted 3 new 52-week highs and 28 new lows while the Nasdaq Composite recorded 21 new highs and 826 new lows.

Volume on US exchanges was 25.19 billion shares, compared with the 19.50 billion average for the full session over the last 20 trading days. REUTERS
