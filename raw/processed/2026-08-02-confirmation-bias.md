# Confirmation Bias And the Power of Disconfirming Evidence

Confirmation bias is our tendency to seek information that validates existing beliefs while discounting contradictory evidence. It is most pronounced in emotionally or ideologically charged views.

Quine and Ullian distinguished two impulses: wanting to be right (truth-seeking) versus wanting to have been right (ego protection) — the latter being the obstacle to genuine learning.

Warren Buffett observed that humans excel at interpreting new information so prior conclusions stay intact.

The bias persists even against strong counterevidence — Festinger noted that people confronted with undeniable disproof often emerge "more convinced...than ever before."

## Examples

- Creationism vs. evolutionary biology
- Doomsday prophecy movements
- Music (where predictive pattern-matching is pleasurable)
- Homeopathy (mass-scale motivated reasoning)
- Scientific peer review

## Why It Happens

Mental shortcuts conserve cognitive energy. Lerner and Tetlock found critical thinking sharpens mainly when people must justify their views to others.

## Antidote

Actively seek disconfirming evidence. Ask which parts of any argument you skimmed, ignored, or immediately accepted without scrutiny.
