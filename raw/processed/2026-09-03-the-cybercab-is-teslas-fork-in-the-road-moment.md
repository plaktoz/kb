# The Cybercab is Tesla's 'fork in the road' moment

Tesla is formally launching the Cybercab — a small, gold, two-seater autonomous vehicle with no steering wheel or pedals — in Austin, Texas. The company hasn't detailed the launch's exact scope, but has staged hundreds of Cybercabs across multiple U.S. cities in anticipation. Texas state records showed 420 autonomous vehicles registered to Tesla at publication time.

Tesla AI head Ashok Elluswamy declared that "streets won't be the same anymore," while Musk confirmed plans to flood Austin with the vehicles.

The Cybercab grew from Tesla's effort to build a cheaper EV platform — smaller, lighter, and less battery-intensive than existing models. Tesla bulls argue its cost-per-unit advantage over competitors like Waymo is decisive.

However, challenges remain. Tesla's robotaxi pilot (using modified Model Y SUVs since June 2025) saw paid miles actually declining by mid-2026. The network has operated only within geofences — something Musk himself once said disqualified a system from being "real self-driving."

The Cybercab launch represents a defining moment: either Tesla begins transforming urban transportation, or it confirms it remains, fundamentally, just an automaker.
