# Wall Street Sees U.S. Stocks Rising, with Divergence Across Sectors... Treasury Yields Uncertain [U.S. Blue Tsunami]

## U.S. Stocks Close Sharply Higher Across the BoardKOSPI Sell-Sidecar TriggeredWall Street Sees "Medium- to Long-Term Gains"Iran War Key to Oil Prices

After it became clear that Democrats would take control of both chambers of Congress in the U.S. midterm elections (a "blue tsunami"), the New York stock market rose by more than 3% at its peak on Nov. 4 (local time). Wall Street was particularly confident that the artificial intelligence (AI) investment cycle itself would not turn downward, and expected stocks to rise over the medium to long term.

However, the KOSPI opened at 5,580.09 on Nov. 5, down 103.71 points (1.82%) from the previous day. It later fell below the 5,500 mark, triggering a sell-sidecar measure that temporarily halted order quotations. The decline reflected concerns that tighter regulation under Democrats could delay some AI infrastructure investment, as well as expectations that data center construction would slow amid Democratic opposition.

## U.S. Stocks Expected to Rise Over the Medium to Long Term... AI Regulations to Drive Divergence Across Sectors

In a report released that day, Morgan Stanley maintained its overweight recommendation on global equities, citing solid U.S. economic growth, improving corporate earnings, and expanding AI investment. It said that while tighter regulation under Democrats could delay some AI infrastructure investment, the overall investment cycle would not turn downward.

Morgan Stanley also cited historical data showing that since 1982, following U.S. midterm elections, large-cap stocks had risen by an average of 15-16% over six months and 22% over a year. Standard Chartered also expected the U.S. stock market rally to continue, forecasting annual returns of 8-10% going forward. Unlike in the past, it expected a gradual climb rather than a sharp surge.

That day, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 3.03%, its biggest gain in about 19 months. The S&P 500 rose 1.58%, while the Nasdaq Composite gained 1.44%.

Wall Street expects sector performance to diverge markedly after Democrats take control of Congress. Investors are closely watching whether regulations on data centers will tighten, particularly in the AI sector. During the campaign, Democrats raised concerns about the burden on local residents' electricity bills stemming from data centers' enormous consumption of power and water.

In a report released that day, JPMorgan Asset Management said, "The AI industry's boom will continue, but costs could rise, growth could slow, and industry concentration could intensify over the next few years." Democrat Gina Hinojosa, elected governor of Texas, known as a "data center hub," had pledged to immediately suspend approvals for all new data centers across the state.

By contrast, healthcare and consumer-related sectors could benefit from Democratic control of Congress. Morgan Stanley said household spending capacity could improve if Democrats delay or ease budget cuts to Medicaid, the health insurance program for low-income people, and the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP). However, it remains uncertain whether these policies will actually be implemented, as President Donald Trump could veto them.

## Direction of Treasury Yields Uncertain... Iran War Holds the Key for Oil Prices

In the bond market, some expect a Democratic landslide to ease upward pressure on Treasury yields, but analysts generally agree that it is unclear whether this would lead to a long-term decline in yields.

JPMorgan Asset Management said the government and Congress would be divided, making a new large-scale fiscal stimulus package unlikely over the next two years. However, it expected slower economic growth and easing inflation to limit upward pressure on Treasury yields.

It expects inflation to peak at the end of this year and approach 2% around May next year. JPMorgan Asset Management also identified the passage of the federal budget and negotiations over the national debt ceiling as major sources of financial market uncertainty over the next two years.

In commodity markets, the course of the Iran war, which President Trump began in February, was seen as the biggest variable. JPMorgan Asset Management expected the Trump Administration to seek an exit as Democratic control of Congress increases political pressure over the war in Iran.

However, it said the possibility that President Trump could intensify military pressure on Iran to overcome a political crisis could not be ruled out.

The dollar and gold are expected to be influenced by the Federal Reserve's (Fed) monetary policy and geopolitical risks. Standard Chartered forecast that the dollar would trade within a certain range in the short term, then weaken as expectations for Fed rate cuts grow in the second half of next year. It set a 12-month target of 98.5 for the U.S. Dollar Index.
