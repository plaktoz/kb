# Keto Diet May Offer Benefits Beyond Weight Loss

A ketogenic (keto) diet may provide greater health benefits for people with obesity than the Mediterranean or low-fat, plant-forward diets, according to a study published in *Cell Metabolism*.

The clinical trial enrolled 42 participants with obesity, prediabetes, and fatty liver disease, randomly assigned to one of three diets. All food was provided by researchers, and participants met weekly with a dietitian.

- **Keto:** 4% carbohydrates, 73% fat
- **Mediterranean:** 50% carbohydrates, 35% fat
- **Plant-forward:** 70% carbohydrates, 15% fat

Calorie intake was adjusted to achieve roughly 10% body weight loss over four to five months. All three diets reduced body fat and improved insulin sensitivity. However, the keto group showed liver fat reduction averaging 67%, versus 45% in other groups. About half of keto participants no longer met prediabetes criteria, compared to 29% (Mediterranean) and 7% (plant-forward).

Lead author Dr. Max Petersen noted that keto participants did not experience rises in blood fat or cholesterol despite high fat intake.

Endocrinologist Dr. Yesika Garcia, not involved in the study, cautioned that the trial was small and short, and that "the real star in metabolic health continues to be weight loss." She also noted the Mediterranean diet has a stronger long-term track record for preventing diabetes and heart disease, and that adherence matters most when choosing a dietary approach.
