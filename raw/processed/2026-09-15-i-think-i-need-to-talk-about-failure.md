# I Think I Need to Talk About Failure

Bloom had trained all summer for a sub-2:50 marathon but DNF'd at mile 21.45 due to dehydration on an unexpectedly hot, humid day (70°F+, 90% humidity). His heart rate spiked from 170 to 190+ bpm, his mouth went dry, and his hamstring locked — so he walked off the course.

He draws three lessons from the experience:

## 1. Stay in the Game

Pushing through injury for short-term toughness often leads to long-term sideline time. Life compounds — but only if you remain a participant. His mantra: "Survive and advance."

## 2. Three Types of Failure

- **Failures of Nature** — uncontrollable; don't lose sleep
- **Failures of Effort** — intolerable; never acceptable
- **Failures of Execution** — learnable; a gift in disguise

His DNF was a Failure of Execution: he knew the forecast, didn't adjust hydration, and missed his water bottle twice. That's fixable.

## 3. Identity vs. Achievement

Friends assumed he was devastated. He wasn't. He no longer ties his identity to reaching "arbitrary summits." Quoting *Cool Runnings*: *"A gold medal is a wonderful thing, but if you're not enough without it, you'll never be enough with it."*

The person formed during training retains value regardless of race outcome.

Closing call to action: Get in the arena. Fail often. You define yourself by the journey, not the finish line.
