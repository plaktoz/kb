# I Think I Need to Talk About Failure

Bloom shares a vulnerable account of DNF-ing (Did Not Finish) a marathon he'd trained extensively for, having stopped at mile 21.45 despite running a strong 6:24 average pace. The culprit: dehydration on an unexpectedly hot, humid day (70°F+, 90% humidity), where his heart rate spiked to 190 and his hamstring locked up.

Rather than the planned success-story newsletter, he pivots to three lessons about failure:

## Three Core Lessons

**1. Always Stay in the Game**

Prioritizing long-term availability over short-term toughness. He argues that compounding — in fitness or life — requires staying healthy and active. "Survive and advance."

**2. Three Types of Failure**

- *Failures of Nature* – uncontrollable external forces
- *Failures of Effort* – lack of energy or commitment
- *Failures of Execution* – gaps in planning or follow-through

He classifies his DNF as a Failure of Execution (missed hydration adjustments for conditions), which he views as learnable. "Be grateful for the failures that forge you."

**3. Identity Beyond Achievement**

Quoting *Cool Runnings*: "A gold medal is a wonderful thing, but if you're not enough without it, you'll never be enough with it."

He argues that growth happens *during* the pursuit, not at the finish line — and that his lack of post-race devastation is itself a sign of maturity.
