# S&P 500 forecast: tech rally loses momentum as Nvidia doubts linger

US equity futures turned lower overnight, leading to a lower open on Wall Street today, as Monday's relief rally, triggered by Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia chief executive, lost momentum. The S&P 500 was down around 1% at the time of writing, after running into resistance at its old support zone, while Anthropic has slipped below its IPO price. Until the index reclaims that zone, the near-term S&P 500 forecast hangs in the balance, with the burden of proof firmly on the bulls.

- The S&P 500 is down around 1% after failing at former support between 7,224 and 7,298.
- Nvidia shares are down around 5%, while Anthropic trades below its $83 IPO price.
- A close above the 200-day moving average and 7,421 would signal that the market has bottomed.

Before turning to what has soured the mood towards technology, let's have a quick look at the chart first.

## Technical S&P 500 forecast and key levels to watch

From a technical analysis point of view, the S&P 500 forecast hinges on the 7,224 to 7,298 zone. The index bounced from this area on multiple occasions, notably in June, at the end of July and twice more this month, before the big drop that was eventually bought around 7,000. That level, previously resistance earlier this year, was briefly breached on Friday amid the sell-off in AI names, but the index recovered strongly on Monday, even before the Nvidia news was out. In that sense, the downside objective has been met, and the strength of the rebound suggests a bottom could be in place.

But it is early days, and our US SP 500 index, which is based on the S&P 500 futures, needs to reclaim 7,224 to 7,298 to inspire confidence that the market has bottomed out. Short-term support comes in around 7,150, where dip buyers could step in. For confirmation, I would want to see a closing break above the 200-day moving average and, ideally, a move above 7,421, the high of last Thursday's thrust candle that led to the more recent sell-off.

## Nvidia leadership change fails to settle nerves

Optimism has faded quickly. Nvidia shares are down around 5%, as Jensen Huang's return has done little to silence questions over the company's corporate governance and the strength of its board oversight. Anthropic has meanwhile slipped below its $83 IPO price, wiping out the gains from its record-breaking debut.

There are broader reasons for caution. China has signalled little expectation that US restrictions on semiconductor exports will ease, which is a reminder that the geopolitical risks facing chipmakers have not gone away. That leaves the AI trade vulnerable to further bouts of profit-taking, particularly with valuations and AI-related investment already under scrutiny.

## Risk appetite struggles to gain traction across markets

The weakness is not confined to Wall Street. European equities have followed suit, with the Stoxx 600 down around 0.2% as it gives back some of Monday's Nvidia-related gains. Silver was down 2.5%, bitcoin also down with a similar percentage drop and copper was off more than 3%.

The broader picture therefore suggests investors remain reluctant to embrace a sustained recovery in risk appetite. From a macro point of view, not a lot has changed since last week's sell-off, and most of the concerns that were prevalent then are still in place.

## Fed and year-end seasonality now in focus

Monetary policy remains the other big hurdle. The Federal Reserve has strongly signalled a rate hike in December, with the possibility of more in early 2027, as policymakers try to get inflation back down to their 2% target. A firm October core PCE reading on Wednesday could reinforce that message, while today's US calendar includes September house price data and November consumer confidence, which is expected to edge higher despite the recent correction.

Yet seasonality offers some hope. Equity markets typically stage a rally towards the end of the year, the so-called Santa rally, and the same period is usually soft for the US dollar. It may be a little early for that, especially in a holiday-thinned week, but it is something to keep an eye on over the next couple of weeks as we head into year-end.

## Frequently asked questions

### Has the S&P 500 bottomed?

Not yet confirmed. The bounce from 7,000 is encouraging, but a close above the 200-day moving average and 7,421 is needed to confirm a bottom.

### Why did the tech rally lose momentum so quickly?

Questions over Nvidia's governance, Anthropic's slide below its IPO price and China's stance on chip export restrictions have all weighed on sentiment.

### Is a Santa rally still possible this year?

It is. Equity markets typically rally into year-end, although it may be a little early to expect that in a holiday-thinned week.
