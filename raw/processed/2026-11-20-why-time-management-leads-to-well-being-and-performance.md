When the mechanism is left unspecified, scholarship and practice incur three predictable errors. First, theory collapses distinct processes into a single latent "time management effect," making it impossible to explain why some practices (e.g., scheduling) can feel empowering in one study yet constraining in another. Second, empirical work strings together heterogeneous behaviors under one scale, inflating measurement noise and yielding the very pattern of inconsistent coefficients that recent meta-analyses report (Bedi & Sass, 2023). Third, managers extrapolate from those noisy averages, pouring resources into one-size-fits-all training or tracking apps that can relieve overload for some employees while intensifying it for others, making it an expensive misfire for firms' investment in time management training.

## An Expanded Conceptualization of Time Management

*the deliberate planning, sequencing, and regulation of one's activities and temporal boundaries to achieve personally and contextually valued goals*. The construct has two layers: practices and skills. Time management practices are the observable behaviors that distribute time (e.g., daily prioritization, fixed "email-only" windows, time-boxing). Time management skills, on the other hand, are the meta-cognitive capabilities that enable those behaviors, including temporal foresight, monitoring, and goal setting (Mudrack, 1997). For example, reserving a 90-minute "deep-work" block each morning, batching email replies into two set windows, and reviewing one's checklist are practices that succeed when paired with skills such as forecasting task duration, recognizing personal peak-energy periods, and flexibly re-sequencing plans when interruptions arise. For brevity, the term "time management practices" is used throughout this paper to encompass both the behavioral practices themselves and the underlying skills that enable them.

### Structure

#### Sense of control

*Proposition 1a*: Time management practices that provide temporal structure will be positively associated with employee well-being through an increased sense of agency.

*Proposition 1b*: Time management practices that provide temporal structure will be positively associated with productivity through an increased sense of agency over time use, which reduces procrastination and enables efficient task sequencing.

#### Predictability

*Proposition 2a*: Time management practices that create a predictable temporal framework will be positively associated with employee well-being through improved temporal decision-making and reduced cognitive load.

*Proposition 2b*: Time management practices that create a predictable temporal framework will be positively associated with productivity, owing to reduced cognitive load and coordination conflict.

### Obstacles to Structure

*Proposition 3*: The positive relationships between time management practices and (a) well-being and (b) productivity will be moderated by temporal fragmentation, with stronger fragmentation weakening both effects.

### Synchrony

#### Conflict reduction

*Proposition 4a*: Time management practices that enhance synchrony with social and natural rhythms will be positively associated with employee well-being through reduced time-based conflict.

*Proposition 4b*: Time management practices that enhance synchrony will be positively associated with employee productivity, because lower time-based conflict frees cognitive resources for task execution.

#### Social cohesion

*Proposition 5a*: Time management practices that facilitate alignment with social and organizational temporal norms will be positively associated with employee well-being through increased social cohesion.

*Proposition 5b*: Time management practices that facilitate alignment with social and organizational temporal norms will be positively associated with employee productivity through improved coordination and knowledge sharing.

### Concordance

#### Meaning

*Proposition 7a*: Time management practices that foster concordance between daily activities and personal values will be positively associated with employee well-being through an increased sense of meaning and purpose in work.

*Proposition 7b*: Time management practices that foster concordance will be positively associated with productivity because self-concordant goal pursuit elicits more autonomous motivation and consistent effort, increasing day-to-day task completion.

#### Self-continuity

*Proposition 8a*: Time management practices that strengthen temporal self-continuity will be positively associated with employee well-being through enhanced perseverance in the face of challenges.

*Proposition 8b*: Time management practices that strengthen temporal self-continuity will be positively associated with employee productivity by sustaining engagement and follow-through on complex, long-horizon tasks.

### Obstacles to Concordance

*Proposition 9*: The positive relationships between time management practices and (a) well-being and (b) productivity via concordance will be moderated by organizational temporal myopia, such that both relationships are weaker when myopia is high.
