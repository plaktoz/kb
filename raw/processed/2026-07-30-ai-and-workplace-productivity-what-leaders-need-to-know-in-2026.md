# AI and Workplace Productivity: What Leaders Need to Know in 2026

source_url: https://www.gallup.com/workplace/713063/ai-workplace-productivity.aspx

---

Gallup Workplace research, 2026.

AI tools are now present across a majority of US workplaces, but widespread availability has not produced widespread organizational productivity gains. Within organizations that have implemented AI, 65% of employees say it has improved their productivity and efficiency, but only 12% strongly agree that AI has transformed how work gets done in their organization. Gallup attributes the gap to task-level gains (drafting, summarizing, idea generation) without workflow or role redesign around AI.

## Adoption depth matters more than breadth

Frequent AI users report significantly stronger productivity gains than occasional users. Among employees whose AI integrates well with existing workflows, 88% are frequent users, versus 55% of those who don't feel it integrates well. Only 13% of US employees use AI daily; another 15% use it a few times a week; 49% never use it in their role. The gap between frequent and occasional users is largest for coding assistants (22% vs. 8%) and data/analytics tools (18% vs. 8%).

## Who reports the strongest gains

21% of leaders say AI's impact has been extremely positive, versus 13% of individual contributors. Frequent AI use among leaders rose from 17% to 44% since Q2 2023; among managers, from 15% to 30%. Adoption is highly uneven by industry: Technology leads with 77% total adoption and 57% frequent use; Retail sits lowest at 33% total and 19% frequent. Remote-capable roles show far higher adoption than non-remote-capable roles (66% vs. 32%).

23% of employees don't know whether their organization has implemented AI at all — individual contributors (26%) are far more likely to be unaware than leaders (7%).

## The manager is the missing link

Manager support and workflow integration are the two strongest drivers of frequent AI use, yet only 21% of employees strongly agree their manager actively supports their team's AI use. Employees whose managers actively support AI use: 79% use it frequently, versus 46% among those without that support. Supported employees are 8.7x as likely to say AI transformed how work gets done, and 7.4x as likely to say AI gives them more opportunity to do their best work.

A 2025 MIT study found only 5% of organizations report measurable ROI from generative AI investment. Only 25% of US employees say their organization has communicated a clear AI adoption strategy.

## AI and employee wellbeing

18% of US employees say it's somewhat or very likely their job will be eliminated within five years due to AI, up from 15% two years ago; that rises to 23% in organizations that have already implemented AI, and 31–32% in finance, insurance, and technology. Yet only 1% of laid-off workers specifically cite AI as the primary layoff reason — most cite restructuring or cost-cutting. Workers who use AI at least monthly appear more insulated from layoffs than those who don't.

## What leaders should do

Gallup identifies four actions: equip managers to champion AI at the team level (47% of AI users report no training on how to use it in their job); communicate a clear AI strategy (employees who strongly agree their org has one are 2.9x as likely to report AI readiness); measure adoption depth (frequent/daily use) rather than breadth of access; and address job-security concerns directly rather than assuming tool availability will drive adoption.
