# Coffee: What the Research Says About Your Daily Caffeine Intake

Roughly two-thirds of American adults drink coffee daily. Recent research points to potential benefits for heart and liver health when consumed in moderation.

## Heart Health: Up to 5 Cups Per Day

The American Heart Association published a scientific statement in *Circulation* indicating that 3–5 cups (8 oz each), or up to 400mg of caffeine daily, is generally safe for most adults and may reduce risk of:
- Atrial fibrillation (AFib)
- Coronary heart disease
- Heart failure
- Stroke

Nutrition expert Michelle Routhenstein cautioned it's "evidence" of compatibility with cardiovascular health, not a target intake. Nutritional therapist Claire Cohen noted individual genetic differences affect caffeine metabolism and tolerance.

## Liver Health: 5+ Cups Per Day

A *Clinical Gastroenterology and Hepatology* study of 354,000+ UK Biobank participants found that drinking 5 or more daily cups was linked — after a median 13-year follow-up — to reduced risk of cirrhosis, hepatocellular carcinoma, and liver-related mortality. Dietitian Meridan Zerner attributed possible mechanisms to coffee's antioxidants, improved insulin sensitivity, and lower liver fat accumulation.

## AFib Recurrence: 1 Cup Per Day

A 2025 clinical trial (published in *JAMA*) randomized 200 adults with persistent AFib to either consume at least one daily cup of caffeinated coffee or abstain. Coffee drinkers showed a **39% lower risk** of recurrent atrial flutter. Lead author Dr. Gregory Marcus stated the findings "should certainly reassure" AFib patients who enjoy coffee and suggested caffeine prohibition may be unwarranted.

## Key Caveats

- Individual responses vary based on genetics
- Recommendations apply to generally healthy adults
- Black or lightly sweetened coffee appears most beneficial
- Consult a healthcare provider before changing consumption habits
