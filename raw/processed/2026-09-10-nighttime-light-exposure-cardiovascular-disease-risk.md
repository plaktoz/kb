# Even Low Light Exposure During Sleep May Raise Your Risk of Heart Failure, AFib

New research published in the *European Heart Journal* found that nighttime light exposure — even at levels as low as 3 lux — is linked to cardiac remodeling, including thicker heart muscle walls and reduced heart flexibility, potentially increasing cardiovascular risk.

Researchers analyzed over 11,000 UK Biobank participants who wore wrist-based light monitors. Follow-up cardiac MRI scans revealed that those with higher nighttime light exposure showed a 2.4% greater left ventricular mass, 1.5% thicker heart walls, and 1.9% lower myocardial contraction fraction.

Lead author Lu Qi described the findings as showing "exposure to higher levels of light at nighttime is linked to cardiac remodeling," noting this weakens the heart over time.

A related 2025 *JAMA Network Open* study found nighttime light exposure correlated with 32–56% higher risks of various cardiovascular conditions.

Cardiologist Cheng-Han Chen noted disruption of the body's circadian rhythm may impair blood pressure regulation, metabolism, and sleep. Shorter sleep duration accounted for 24–49% of the light-cardiac associations observed.

Practical recommendations include: blackout curtains, keeping phones face-down, using dim warm-toned nightlights, maintaining consistent sleep schedules, and getting morning sunlight to reinforce circadian rhythm.
