# I Don't Want a Learning Dashboard for My Child

source_url: https://www.fast.ai/posts/2026-02-17-education/index.html

---

Author: Rachel Thomas
Published: February 17, 2026

Rachel Thomas, a data/ML professional who homeschools her daughter, argues that both traditional and AI-based education share a common flaw: over-reliance on quantification and metric-driven learning.

She observes that dominant language arts curricula have eliminated whole novels, replacing them with short passages to test isolated skills — contributing to a steep decline in children reading for pleasure (9-year-olds reading daily for fun dropped from 53% to 39% between 2012–2022).

Thomas traces this problem to Texas's high-stakes testing policies under Governor Bush, which became the blueprint for No Child Left Behind — later widely criticized and debunked.

She invokes Goodhart's Law: when a measure becomes a target, it stops being a useful measure. AI amplifies this, being "too effective at optimizing metrics," leading to narrowed curricula, teacher burnout, and administrator cheating scandals.

She also emphasizes that meaningful teacher-student relationships — impossible to quantify — are being crowded out by assessment demands.

Her conclusion: analog school isn't automatically better, and AI ed-tech often doubles down on the same failures. The solution lies in combining technology, creativity, and genuine human connection.

Key themes: education, Goodhart's Law, AI in education, quantification, homeschooling, learning metrics.
