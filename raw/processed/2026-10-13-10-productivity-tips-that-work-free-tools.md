# 10 Productivity Tips that Work + Free Tools

Staying productive can feel like a daunting state only achieved by people who have it all together. Yet, productivity is attainable to anyone. You just need to find the right strategies and mindset shifts that work for you and your needs.

It's all about understanding your way of working, cutting down on overwhelm, and implementing the right time-saving tools. This guide covers 10 helpful productivity tips that are bound to make accomplishing your goals much easier.

- **Prioritize your most important task:** Start with the task that would make your day successful, ideally when your energy and focus are highest, rather than trying to tackle everything at once.
- **Structure your time and tasks:** Use time blocking, group similar tasks, and keep your to-do list streamlined to reduce overwhelm and make focused work easier.
- **Protect your focus:** Limit distractions such as your phone, social media, and clutter, while taking short breaks to maintain your attention and energy throughout the day.
- **Build sustainable productivity habits:** Plan ahead, automate repetitive tasks, use accountability when helpful, and focus on consistent progress rather than pushing yourself toward burnout.

## 1. Pick one task that would make your day successful

It's easy to get overwhelmed by your own sprawling to-do list before you even start, and this can result in productivity paralysis. Instead, try picking one main task that would make the day a success if you completed it.

This could be finishing a product commission that's attached to a deadline, finalizing a business plan, getting clarity on your content strategy or anything that gives you a twinge of anxiety when you think about it stalling out.

Dedicating your day to a single, high-priority task will build momentum across your week. Who knows, that feeling of accomplishment might even motivate you to check a few more items off your list while you're at it.

## 2. Manage your energy, not just your time

Effective time management is often synonymous with productivity success, yet if it's something you struggle with, then you might already feel discouraged before you even start. That's why managing your energy instead can be a helpful reframe.

Start by being realistic with yourself about your capacities and natural daily rhythms. Maybe you notice that you are your freshest and most focused self in the morning, but your attention span naturally wanes by late afternoon. Rather than trying to force yourself to produce equal output across the day, dedicate your mornings to your most pressing tasks, and schedule shorter to-do items that require less mental energy for the afternoon.

## 3. Address procrastination head on

Let's face it, we can all be guilty of procrastination from time to time. It often amounts to anxiety about the task at hand, perfectionism, or overwhelm about where to start. It helps to identify what is behind your procrastination so you can address it head on.

There are also some practical steps you can take to mitigate these unhelpful tendencies:

- **Use cues to start:** Train your brain to transition into focused time with simple starting cues like playing a favorite upbeat song, taking a quick walk around the block, repeating an encouraging affirmation, or simply tying your hair back before you start reviewing your to-do list. These tips can be especially helpful for remote workers who lack a dedicated office space to help distinguish between leisure and work.
- **Establish routines:** Get into a daily rhythm that makes procrastination feel out of place in your schedule. For example, maybe you start every morning with setting an intention for the day while you drink your coffee, followed by your daily focused block. After a screen-free lunch hour, you then do a quick sprint dedicated to simpler tasks before signing off.
- **Use time blocking:** Sometimes setting finite time blocks to complete specific tasks helps to cut down on the overwhelm that naturally comes with contemplating the vast expanse of time before you. After all, the time it takes to do a given task usually expands or contracts to the amount of time you have to do it. So quell your procrastination anxiety by segmenting structured blocks of time to work on it.
- **Start small:** If you're still struggling to get started—and the idea of beginning with your most pressing or high-stakes task feels too daunting—you can always take the opposite approach. Build productivity momentum by completing the shortest and simplest tasks on your list, such as paying a bill, watering your plants, replying to an email, or scheduling an appointment online. The dopamine of completing these smaller things might be all you need to tackle the bigger stuff.

## 4. Streamline task management

There are tons of gadgets and gizmos out there promising to be the productivity hack you need to succeed. Yet, if you adopt too many tools and approaches at once, you'll simply bog yourself down instead of getting ahead. Instead, it's best to pick a single tool for managing your tasks—whether it's a user-friendly digital workspace, collaborative team calendar, a spreadsheet, or simply your favorite bullet journal.

The most important thing is to keep all your to-dos in one place where you can easily track progress, so you can focus your time on actually getting things done.

## 5. Automate time-consuming tasks

Speaking of focusing your time on what actually matters, automating time-consuming, manual tasks can also do wonders for your productivity. If you need help discerning which daily tasks suck your time most, spend a few days auditing what drains you. It could be managing your inbox, updating your calendar, or sifting through large documents for their most salient points.

There are many effective automation tools that can take over these mundane tasks so you can focus your mental energies on the important things—like building your business, developing your next creative endeavor, or studying smarter.

## 6. Group similar tasks together

Before we know it, tomorrow's to-do list becomes today's—and suddenly you're facing all those tasks you keep putting off. Before the overwhelm-induced procrastination sets in, try grouping similar tasks together. This will help reduce mental load and make things feel more manageable.

Some examples include:

- **Online banking:** Do all of your online banking in one go—such as paying bills, checking your budgets, and reviewing last month's statement.
- **Responding to messages:** Block out half an hour to respond to important texts, Slack messages, LinkedIn inquiries, and time-sensitive emails.
- **Doing house chores:** Group things like taking out the trash, loading the dishwasher, and watering your plants so you can complete them in one swoop.
- **Completing admin and paperwork:** Tackle invoicing, organizing your mail, or filing documents all at once.

## 7. Wrangle your distractions

Trying to be productive against a backdrop of chaos is likely to yield subpar results. Take an honest look at where your mind wanders when you should be working—maybe it's scrolling on social media, doing house chores that can wait, or socializing with friends and colleagues.

Once you have a sense of your biggest daily distractions, it's time to reorganize your environment accordingly. Reserve that focus room at the office, put your phone in airplane mode while you're working, and keep your desk free of clutter that can lure you off course.

## 8. Get an accountability buddy

We all have different working styles, and productivity doesn't have to happen in a silo. Whether it's a fellow freelancer, a friend, or colleague, having an accountability buddy can help keep you on track.

The role of your accountability buddy can also look different depending on your needs. Maybe you co-work together on a Zoom call or in a cafe. Perhaps you simply report to each other at the end of each day about what you've accomplished and what you'll do tomorrow.

## 9. Set yourself up for success the night before

Spending your mornings organizing your tasks and goals for the day is a waste of precious focus time. Instead, cap off each day by prepping your to-do list and making it easy to dive right in tomorrow.

Taking just a few minutes to set your priorities for the next day will also prime your brain to process these objectives overnight. This way, you can hit the ground running in the morning.

## 10. Take a long-term approach to productivity

In an effort to boost productivity, it can be tempting to go from 0 to 100 by adopting a bunch of tips and tools at once. Maybe you manage to work long, focused hours for a few weeks at this pace. Yet, if you fail to take a slower and more balanced approach to productivity, you're bound to burn yourself out before you build meaningful habits.

Some tips that will help you effective take a long-term approach to productivity include:

- **Set priorities:** Much like we did in step one, highlight the day's most pressing task. Sometimes you'll have to let the nice-to-haves on your to-do list take a back seat for now.
- **Plan breaks:** Schedule time to pause and recharge—throughout the day and in the form of time off such as on weekends or the occasional vacation.
- **Work with ebbs and flows:** Recognize that your energy is finite and your productivity may look a bit different from one day to the next. Long term consistency matters most.

## Frequently asked questions about productivity tips

Productivity tips for a fast-paced work environment include:

- **Triaging priorities:** At the start and end of each day, take 10–15 minutes to review your tasks and identify the highest priority items to focus on.
- **Doing focused work:** Set time blocks in your schedule for putting your head down and getting pressing tasks done. Turn off notifications and remove distractions.
- **Setting boundaries:** Once you know where your priorities lie, protect your focus time by setting boundaries. Let colleagues know your capacities and make your availability clear via calendar blockers and pausing notifications.

Productivity strategies for academics include:

- **Using task decomposition:** Break down vague and unwieldy tasks like writing a chapter or conducting a literature review into smaller, more tangible subtasks that you can allocate to specific timeslots in your day.
- **Protecting focused work time:** Dedicate focused blocks to tasks that require your sharpest mental energy. Hit pause on your notifications, respond to emails later, and limit interruptions as much as possible.
- **Minding your work environment:** Whether you prefer the total silence of a library or the hum of a cozy cafe, find which kinds of environments are most conducive to your productivity.
- **Taking breaks to prevent burnout:** At first glance, this may not seem like it has anything to do with productivity. However, the only way to make the demands of academic life sustainable is by taking intentional breaks for rest and wellbeing.

Some of the best productivity techniques are surprisingly simple and don't cost any money.

Free productivity tips include:

- **Extended pomodoros:** Try implementing 90 minute focused work blocks followed by 15 minute breaks.
- **3-2-1 to-do list method:** Each day choose three work tasks, two health-related activities, and one fun thing you'll do. Consider completing this list a productive day.
- **Airplane mode:** There are tons of fancy products, apps, and gadgets promising to free you from distractions, but simply putting your phone in airplane mode while you work is usually enough to do the trick by reducing unnecessary screen time.

Productivity tips for people who work from home include:

- **Creating rituals** for signing on and off from work, even if you don't have a designated home office space. These can include writing a to-do list, crossing off the date on your calendar, or doing a short meditation.
- **Using an accountability buddy.** Find a friend or colleague who also works from home and have periodic check-ins, co-working sessions over video call, or share weekly wins. They can help you stay focused and keep you in check on goals like reducing screen time.
- **Mixing up your environment.** Consider working part time in a local library or favorite coffee shop if working at home is too distracting.
