# On Deep Breaks

Deep work requires focusing intensely on a demanding task, but few people can maintain peak cognitive intensity for more than an hour or so without some sort of relief. This relief is necessary — but also dangerous. Most types of breaks wrench your attention away from the task at hand and leave you with a thick slather of "attention residue." If you're careful, however, it's possible to take a so-called deep break, which allows your mind to regroup and recharge without impeding your ability to quickly ramp back up your concentration. Anyone who regularly succeeds in long deep work sessions is almost certainly someone skilled at deploying deep breaks to keep the session going without burning out or losing focus.

## Heuristics for a Deep Break

Deep breaks should not turn your attention to:
- A target that might generate a professional or social obligation you cannot completely fulfill during the break (e.g., glancing at an email inbox or social media feed).
- A target your mind associates with time-consuming distraction rituals (many people have a set "cycle" of distracting websites they visit that becomes so ingrained that looking at one sends the mind the message it's time to look at them all).
- A related but different professional task (e.g., editing an unrelated report while trying to write your own).
- A topic that is complicated, stressful, or will soon need a lot of your attention.

Deep breaks should also not usually last more than 10-15 minutes, with exceptions such as for meals.

## Example Deep Break Activities

- Taking a short walk to get water or coffee while just observing your surroundings.
- Day dreaming about the good things that could come from succeeding at the task at hand.
- Summarizing to yourself what you already know about the task and what you're trying to accomplish.
- Reading a book chapter or magazine article unrelated to the deep task at hand.
- Doing something fun with family.
- Completing a household task or short errand.

The key message: you don't need to maintain peak concentration for hours on end — if you try, you'll fail. But be mindful about how you take your cognitive breathers, since they play a key role in whether the deep work session as a whole succeeds. Deep breaks are implicit in scheduled deep work sessions — if you block out four hours for deep work, that time will be punctuated, as needed, by deep breaks; you don't need to separately schedule them.
