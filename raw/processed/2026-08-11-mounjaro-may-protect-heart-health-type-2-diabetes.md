# Mounjaro May Help Protect Heart Health in People With Type 2 Diabetes

New research suggests the GLP-1 medication Mounjaro (tirzepatide) may lower the risk of major cardiovascular events, including heart attack, in people with type 2 diabetes and established heart disease. Researchers estimated that for every 70 people who started tirzepatide, one major cardiovascular event could be prevented.

Researchers analyzed health insurance data from nearly 53,000 people with type 2 diabetes, overweight or obesity, and established heart disease, comparing cardiovascular outcomes among people taking tirzepatide versus sitagliptin (used as a placebo proxy, based on prior studies showing no cardiovascular effect). Results were published August 5, 2026 in The BMJ.

The analysis included 52,971 individuals with an average age of 70 (35,353 on tirzepatide, 17,618 on sitagliptin), tracked for one year or until treatment stopped/switched or disenrollment, adjusting for age, sex, race, BMI, prior heart issues, other chronic conditions, and medication use.

At one year, major cardiovascular event risk was 2.9% in the tirzepatide group versus 4.4% in the sitagliptin group. Tirzepatide was associated with a 33% reduction in heart attacks specifically, though no meaningful difference for ischemic stroke. The tirzepatide group also saw fewer infections requiring hospitalization (1 prevented per 48 people), fewer deaths from infection (1 per 200), and fewer deaths from any cause (1 per 122).

"These findings build on a growing body of research showing that GLP-1 medications can benefit more than just blood sugar; they may also support heart health and help manage conditions like diabetes and cardiovascular disease," said Dr. Cheng-Han Chen, an interventional cardiologist not involved in the study. The study was observational with a relatively short follow-up period, so researchers say more research is needed to confirm the findings.

Tirzepatide (as Mounjaro) is currently FDA-approved only for type 2 diabetes treatment, not specifically for cardiac risk reduction — coverage and usage remain limited to patients with type 2 diabetes, obesity, and obstructive sleep apnea, according to Dr. David Rhee, a cardiologist not involved in the study. The same active ingredient is sold as Zepbound for weight loss. GLP-1s like Mounjaro may support heart health both indirectly (managing blood sugar, weight, blood pressure) and directly (reducing inflammation, improving circulation), though most data on the GIP component's cardiovascular role remains preclinical.
