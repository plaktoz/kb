# Painless Functional Specifications – Part 1: Why Bother?

Spolsky argues that skipping functional specs is "the *single biggest unnecessary risk* you take in a software project." Despite near-universal acknowledgment that specs matter, most teams avoid writing them.

## Three Core Arguments for Writing Specs

**Design Force Multiplier**

Designing in human language takes minutes to iterate; designing in code takes weeks. A programmer invested in written code resists changing it, leading to compromised architecture rather than optimal design.

**Communication Efficiency**

A spec lets QA, marketing, developers, tech writers, and managers all get aligned from one document. Without it, ad-hoc communication fills the gap — poorly. Tech writers end up producing circular help text because they were never told what features actually *do*.

**Scheduling Prerequisite**

Without a detailed spec, meaningful schedules are impossible. Unresolved design debates get quietly deferred, and "the most likely projects to fail" are those that never force decisions.

## Why People Skip Specs

Spolsky's diagnosis: most people simply dislike writing. His prescription — treat writing as a skill built through practice: journaling, blogging, or creative writing courses.
