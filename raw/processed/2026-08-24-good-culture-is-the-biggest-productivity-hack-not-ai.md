# Good Culture is the Biggest Productivity Hack, Not AI

Ojstersek argues that organizational culture outweighs AI tooling as a productivity driver. While AI tools are valuable, they amplify whatever already exists — meaning bad culture, poor architecture, and weak communication get *worse* with AI adoption, not better.

## Culture as Foundation

The author invokes Conway's Law — systems mirror organizational communication structures — to argue that productivity and product quality reflect culture first, tooling second.

## Executives Misreading AI Claims

Many CEOs panic over competitor claims of "10x productivity gains," not recognizing these are often marketing narratives tied to product partnerships. This FOMO-driven pressure damages psychological safety.

## AI Amplifies Existing Conditions

"AI makes bad communication even worse, it also makes bad architecture even worse as well." Conversely, good culture + good architecture = compounding gains.

## Bottom-Up Adoption Only

Forcing AI top-down fails because the landscape changes too rapidly. Knowledge must flow laterally across teams.

## The Real Leadership Question

> "How do we build an organization where great people can do their best work, and then use AI to multiply them?"

## Culture Health Check Questions

- Do people know their responsibilities and have decision-making authority?
- Is psychological safety present?
- Are priorities clear and outcomes rewarded?
- Do teams learn from failure rather than assign blame?
