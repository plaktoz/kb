# FDA Recalls IV Fluids Over Fiberglass and Other Contaminants

Two major IV fluid manufacturers — Baxter International and B. Braun Medical — issued five recalls over a 10-day span in August 2026, after discovering hazardous particles in saline and other intravenous solutions distributed to hospitals and healthcare facilities nationwide.

Contaminants identified across the recalls include fiberglass, stainless steel, iron oxide (rust), cellulose cotton and wood fibers, and polystyrene. Neither company reported injuries, deaths, or adverse events linked to the affected products at the time of publication.

## Recalled Products

| Product | Manufacturer | Contaminant | Recall Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.9% Sodium Chloride 500 mL | Baxter | Fiberglass | Aug 25, 2026 |
| 70% Dextrose 2000 mL | Baxter | Stainless steel | Aug 25, 2026 |
| Anticoagulant Sodium Citrate 250 mL | Baxter | Fiberglass | Aug 25, 2026 |
| 0.9% Sodium Chloride 100 mL | B. Braun | Iron oxide | Aug 27, 2026 |
| Excel Lactated Ringer's 1000 mL | B. Braun | Cotton/wood fiber, polystyrene | Aug 19, 2026 |

Baxter identified the fiberglass contamination through its own particulate testing using filtration and microscopic analysis.

## Health Risks

According to FDA draft guidance and medical experts, particles introduced intravenously can cause:

- Vein inflammation and blockages
- Blood clots, including potentially fatal pulmonary embolism
- Organ damage or death in severe cases
- Allergic reactions

Emergency physician Dr. Robert Glatter emphasized that foreign particles "should never be introduced directly into the bloodstream," and that depending on size and number, they could "obstruct blood vessels" or trigger "inflammation or an allergic response."

Higher-risk populations include people with clotting history, recent surgery, cancer, active infection, autoimmune disease, diabetes-related vascular disease, and the elderly or very young.

## What Patients Should Do

- Contact the facility where the IV was given to confirm whether a recalled lot was used
- Seek medical attention for IV site symptoms: redness, swelling, warmth, pain, fever, or chills
- Seek emergency care for chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, or sudden neurological symptoms
