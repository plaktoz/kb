# AI Is Revolutionizing Strategic Decision-Making

**Author:** Felipe A. Csaszar (Alexander M. Nick Professor, strategy chair, University of Michigan's Ross School of Business)
**Publication:** Harvard Business Review, September–October 2026

The article opens by prompting readers to reflect on a typical strategy offsite — the weeks of preparation, travel, facilitation costs — and asks how many genuinely distinct strategic options emerged. The author poses whether the limited output (typically three or four options) reflects actual scarcity of good ideas, or rather the constraints of human time and cognitive bandwidth.

The core argument is that AI tools can overcome longstanding human limitations in strategic decision-making by continuously generating, evaluating, and synthesizing insights in ways that individual teams simply cannot match.

The piece covers how traditional strategy frameworks (such as SWOT analyses and portfolio tools) were bounded by these human limitations, and how AI now offers the potential to expand the scope of strategic consideration significantly.

> "New tools can improve human judgment by tirelessly generating, evaluating, and synthesizing insights."

The article is categorized under Corporate Strategy, with related topics including AI and machine learning, strategy formulation, and organizational decision-making.
