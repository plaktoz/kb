# AI is changing the rules of career readiness

Artificial intelligence is changing what it means to be workforce ready. McKinsey & Co.'s "State of Organizations 2026" found 88% of organizations use AI in at least one business function. Samuel Jay, executive director of Digital Learning and AI Strategy at MSU Denver, says the shift increases the value of both technical fluency and distinctly human abilities: "What will remain valuable in the workplace is what has always been valuable: the human qualities machines can't replace. The humanities teach empathy, perspective-taking and ethical reasoning."

Emerging research suggests AI is changing the nature of jobs rather than simply eliminating them. The Strada Education Foundation found senior talent leaders are nearly three times more likely to say AI will increase entry-level hiring than reduce it, with more than 40% reporting AI has shifted new employees from routine admin work toward more analytical responsibilities. Industrial Design student William Block said using AI in coursework changed how he imagines his career: "The more I use it, the less I think AI is going to take my job. It changes what I think my job is going to be. I become a manager of the AI."

Computer Sciences professor Steve Geinitz said graduates who can identify where AI improves a process — and where human expertise remains essential — will be best positioned to help organizations use the technology strategically: "You need to know what problem you want developers to solve." Students don't necessarily need to code, but should understand their field well enough to identify bottlenecks and shape effective technical solutions.

MSU Denver is launching "AI Fundamentals" this fall, an elective open to any major requiring no coding experience, covering machine learning, generative AI, ethics, bias, privacy, and decision-making through hands-on case studies. Geinitz hopes students leave understanding that "AI isn't magic. It's just data, math and human choices" — but that the stakes of using it responsibly are real.
