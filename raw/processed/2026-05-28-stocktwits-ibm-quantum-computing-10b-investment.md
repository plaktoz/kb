# IBM Stock Gains Amid Plan To Invest $10B For Large-Scale Quantum Computer By 2029

IBM shares rose 3% after the company unveiled a plan to spend more than $10 billion over five years on quantum computing, aiming to bring a commercial, large-scale system to market by 2029. Per an SEC filing, the spending covers R&D, capital expenditures, manufacturing expansion, and targeted acquisitions, aimed at engineering a "fault-tolerant" quantum computer — hardware that can automatically correct its own computational errors, a longstanding barrier to commercial quantum applications.

IBM, which already operates more than 90 quantum installations globally, plans to deploy a machine capable of 100 million quantum gate operations, which would exceed standard supercomputers for certain complex calculations.

The announcement follows a federal funding infusion one week earlier: on May 21, the Trump administration announced a $2 billion federal grant package (via the 2022 CHIPS and Science Act) distributed among nine quantum technology firms, with the Department of Commerce taking minority equity stakes — an unconventional industrial-policy move Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick framed as protecting US dominance in national security and advanced research. IBM captured half that package ($1 billion) and is matching it with $1 billion of its own capital to establish Anderon, a new Albany, NY-based subsidiary that will operate as the first purpose-built, pure-play quantum semiconductor foundry focused on superconducting wafers. Other developers — D-Wave Quantum, Rigetti Computing, IonQ, Atom Computing, Infleqtion, PsiQuantum, and Quantinuum — received letters of intent for smaller individual grants (mostly ~$100 million each).

Analyst sentiment: of 21 analysts, 13 rate IBM "buy," seven "hold," one "sell." Retail sentiment on Stocktwits was "extremely bullish" with "extremely high" message volumes. IBM stock had gained 3% over the trailing 12 months as of this report.
