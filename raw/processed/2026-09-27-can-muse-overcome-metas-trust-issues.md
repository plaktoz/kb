# Can Muse overcome Meta's trust issues?

Meta's new AI agent, Muse, drew significant attention at the company's Connect event, where CEO Mark Zuckerberg signaled that Meta intends to expand AI features broadly across its products. On TechCrunch's Equity podcast, hosts Anthony Ha, Kirsten Korosec, and Sean O'Kane discussed how Meta's AI news seemed to overshadow recent model releases from OpenAI and Anthropic.

The discussion noted that while competitors have focused on coding and enterprise tools, Meta instead emphasized consumer applications, including a Tamagotchi-style AI wearable that the company describes as intended for adults only.

O'Kane, who tested Muse himself, said the app helped him discover unclaimed funds owed to him, calling it a positive but limited experience — something he described as more of a "party-trick type thing" rather than a tool for daily engagement.

The hosts also discussed trust concerns tied to Meta's advertising-based business model. O'Kane remarked skeptically that Meta might argue more personal data leads to better ads, saying: "wake me up when we get to that fever dream."

He added that Muse initially interacted with him without pulling in his existing Meta app data, which increased his comfort using it, though he observed the app gradually encourages deeper data-sharing over time. O'Kane compared this cautiously to Apple's Siri, stating he trusts Apple more with sensitive data since its business is not centered on selling ads.

Korosec countered that Meta has an established history of embedding itself in everyday consumer life through Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp, suggesting this consumer focus could work in Meta's favor even as rivals pivot toward enterprise clients.

Ha noted that OpenAI and Anthropic's shift toward enterprise may be driven by pressure to generate revenue ahead of potential public offerings, while Meta may see an opening to double down on consumer AI instead.
