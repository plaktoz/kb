# International Business Machines (IBM) Opens Lightwell Access On Microsoft Marketplace, Is It Still Undervalued?

International Business Machines (IBM) is back in the headlines after IBM and Red Hat opened up wider access to their Lightwell Network security offering through Microsoft's cloud marketplace, putting open source software supply chain risk in sharper focus for investors.

For International Business Machines, the headlines have not translated into short term gains. The 7 day share price return is down 9.18% and the 30 day share price return is down 10.75%. However, the 3 year total shareholder return of 57.95% and 5 year total shareholder return of 133.17% point to momentum that has built over a longer horizon.

International Business Machines is coming off a sharp pullback after a long winning streak, which puts a simple tension in front of you. Has the bulk of the rerating already happened, or does the current price still leave meaningful upside on the table once valuation is stacked against IBM's cash flows and peers?

## Most Popular Narrative: 12% Undervalued

International Business Machines closed at $224.44 while the most followed narrative on Simply Wall St pegs fair value at $256.08, so the narrative implies meaningful upside when that estimate is discounted at 9.55%.

According to kapirey, an investor in the Simply Wall St Community who wrote the narrative, the long term thesis rests on IBM's shift toward hybrid cloud and AI as a software led platform business with strong cash generation and a broad enterprise footprint.

IBM represents a defensive growth technology investment transitioning into a software and AI led enterprise platform company. While topline growth remains moderate, improving mix (software), strong margins, and durable cash flows underpin a compelling long term investment case, particularly for investors seeking exposure to enterprise AI with lower volatility than pure play SaaS peers.

Result: Fair Value of $256.08 (UNDERVALUED)

However, International Business Machines still faces real swing factors if AI execution stalls, legacy infrastructure drags on profitability, or investor confidence shifts quickly.
