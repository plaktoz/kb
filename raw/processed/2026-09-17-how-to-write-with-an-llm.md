# How To Write With An LLM

Two rules for using LLMs as copyeditors without letting them homogenize your writing voice.

## Rule 1: Never Use an LLM's Suggested Words

Frontier models excel at generating appealing phrases, but those phrases tend toward a kind of polished, headline-y uniformity. The author's prescription is strict: any specific phrasing an LLM offers is disqualified, even if you prefer it. You won't reliably catch all the subtle ways a model will push your prose toward processed blandness.

## Rule 2: Suppress Encouragement

LLMs default to praising your drafts, which causes you to double down on first-draft instincts rather than genuinely rethinking. The author recommends explicitly forbidding the model from offering encouragement, then staying skeptical of any praise that slips through anyway.

## What LLMs Are Actually Good For

- Spotting overuse of passive voice, filler words ("very," "actually," "really"), and repeated phrasing
- Identifying paragraphs that belong elsewhere in the piece
- Running systematic editing passes based on a personal checklist

The recommended workflow: ask the model to *flag problems*, rewrite sections yourself, then have a **fresh model instance** (without editing context) evaluate old vs. new — avoiding the sycophancy trap.
