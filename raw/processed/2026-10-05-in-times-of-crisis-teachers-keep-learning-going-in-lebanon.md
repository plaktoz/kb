# In times of crisis, teachers keep learning going in Lebanon

When a crisis disrupts everyday life, teachers do more than keep lessons going. They help students find their way back to a sense of safety, routine and hope.

In Kafrreman, South Lebanon, teachers are back in their classrooms with one shared message: every child belongs in school, learning alongside their teachers and peers.

The 2025-26 school year was a challenging one across Lebanon. Yet, even amid uncertainty and displacement, teachers and students held on to education. Learning continued keeping the school community connected because for Lebanese citizens education remains key to building Lebanon back better.

At Kafrreman Public School, teachers are now back with their students in the classrooms. While life is not back to normal, their commitment remains clear: to provide quality education to every student and to help students feel safe, supported and ready to learn.

For many teachers across Lebanon, the crisis affected them directly. Some were displaced, some continued to teaching from collective shelters or from temporary locations. Others continued connecting with students online or on WhatsApp, checking in on their wellbeing and maintaining a sense of continuity, while they themselves were in need of support.

> During the crisis, I was supposed to be strong and committed. We used to be in our classrooms and hear the bombing around us. Our role was to maintain a calm environment, while maintaining our own wellbeing, supporting our colleagues and our students. We used to sing the national anthem to soothe the fear,
>
> I hugged another teacher as if it was the last time I would see her. But we stayed connected online. We really cared about the psychosocial wellbeing of our students, not only their education. We worked closely with parents and continued to be there for our students.
>
> — Nada Khamis, teacher at Kafrreman Public School

For Fadia Ramadan, another teacher at the school, continuing to teach meant staying connected despite her own challenges.

> We faced a lot of challenges trying to reach our students, especially as we ourselves were displaced and faced many challenges ourselves. But we continued to connect with our students. Today, we are back with them in our classroom in Nabatieh. Life is not normal, but we are committed to making our students feel safe.
>
> — Fadia Ramadan, teacher at Kafrreman Public School

> We are challenging the crisis. Today, we have one message: to raise equip citizens to be intellectually curious, innovative and a lifelong learners
>
> — Fatima Rizk, Principal, Kfarreman Public School

Across Lebanon, teachers have continued to adapt, teach and care for their students through periods of disruption. Their experience shows that during crises, education is not only about maintaining learning. It is also about preserving connection, routine, dignity and a sense of belonging.

UNESCO remains committed to working with partners to support teachers and education systems through crisis, including by strengthening teachers' capacities in digital learning, crisis-sensitive education and approaches that respond to the psychosocial needs of learners and educators.

Because classrooms are more than spaces for learning. They are places where children reconnect, teachers continue their mission, and communities hold on to their future.
