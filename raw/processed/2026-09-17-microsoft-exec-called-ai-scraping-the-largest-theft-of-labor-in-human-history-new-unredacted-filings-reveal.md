# Microsoft Exec Called AI Scraping 'the Largest Theft of Labor in Human History'

Newly unsealed court filings in the NYT v. OpenAI/Microsoft copyright lawsuit reveal that Microsoft and OpenAI executives privately acknowledged serious ethical and legal problems with their AI training data practices.

A top Microsoft executive (Director of Applied Science Brent Hecht) internally described AI scraping as "the largest theft of labor in human history" in a January 2023 memo.

Microsoft's own data showed Copilot caused NYT click-through rates to drop as much as 93% compared to traditional Bing search — described internally as a "doom loop."

Satya Nadella testified that paywalled content "should be licensed" and said he would have required OpenAI to retrain models had he known about paywall scraping.

OpenAI's head of ChatGPT, Nick Turley, wrote internally that publishers face an "existential threat" from products that are "largely substitutive."

Training datasets allegedly contained over 91,692 copies of NYT, Daily News, and CIR works; a Common Crawl dataset had 2 million+ nytimes.com documents.

Employees allegedly devised methods to bypass paywalls undetected and stripped copyright notices from training data before model ingestion.

Microsoft and OpenAI shared training data through initiatives called Project Taxi and Project Mango, the latter containing copies of at least 160,903 unique publisher works.

The article notes that much new information comes from The Times' own brief, with underlying exhibits still sealed, and that quotes appear without their original context.
