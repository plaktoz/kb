# The S&P 500 Has Only Grown Earnings This Fast Twice Before. History Says This Is What Happens Next

source_url: https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/p-500-only-grown-earnings-210501186.html

---

By Jeremy Bowman, The Motley Fool | Published: Tuesday, September 29, 2026

2026 stands out as a year for S&P 500 earnings growth rather than price appreciation: the index is "up 12.6% year-to-date" while analysts project EPS growth of "24%-32% this year." Growth is attributed to AI-related stocks and the Magnificent Seven, which "now makes up about a third of the index."

Specific company examples: Nvidia is "expected to grow EPS by 95% this year," Amazon is "on track to grow EPS by 79%" partly due to a one-time equity gain tied to Anthropic, and Alphabet is expected to see GAAP earnings grow "by 90%," also benefiting from Anthropic and SpaceX-related gains. Micron is mentioned as posting EPS growth rates "above 1,000%."

This level of earnings growth outside a recession recovery has occurred only twice before — in 1994 and 1999:

- **1994:** Despite EPS jumping "39.8%," the S&P 500 "actually fell slightly for the year" due to Fed rate hikes under Alan Greenspan. Growth was driven by early internet-related demand for chips and telecom hardware, along with corporate cost-cutting (citing Procter & Gamble) and margin expansion, plus GDP growth of "4.1%."

- **1999:** Described as one of the best market years historically, with earnings up "27.7%," partly aided by Y2K-related tech spending. Established companies like Microsoft, IBM, and Intel led gains. The author notes this "would prove to be the last hurrah" before the dot-com crash in 2000.

Comparing 2026 to these precedents, the author argues today's market shares traits with both years — rate hikes and a memory shortage — but leans toward it resembling 1999 more due to the ongoing AI bull market's maturity. However, mitigating differences are highlighted: leading AI stocks like Nvidia "trade at reasonable valuations," there's no comparable "IPO mania," and the AI boom is concentrated among fewer, more financially sound companies (noting Anthropic is "profitable on an adjusted basis" and SpaceX is "on the verge of profitability").

The author concludes that despite some parallels to 1999, similarities to 1994 suggest "we're not yet at the stock market peak," citing reasonable valuations relative to growth expectations and lower risk of a broad startup collapse.
