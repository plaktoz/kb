# The week the AI trade cracked: Anthropic's debut, Nvidia's slide and a global sell-off

- The S&P 500 fell 8.6% for the week, its worst since April 2025; the Dow fell 8%, its worst since March 2020; and the Nasdaq about 10%; the S&P 500 and Dow closed Friday in correction [1]
- Anthropic priced its IPO at $83, opened at $111.50, closed its first day at $97.17 and its second at $88.30, after trading as low as $83.05 [6][7][8]
- Nvidia fell about 14% on Friday after Reuters reported SEC enforcement staff are examining its accounting, slipped below Alphabet in market value and posted its longest weekly losing streak since 2008 [1][13][14]
- Taiwan's Taiex lost a record 17.5% for the week, Japan's Nikkei 7.7% and the STOXX 600 5.8%, its worst week since March [16][17][18][19]
- Gold headed for its worst week in eight months and bitcoin fell below $60,000 for the first time since July, as Fed officials including Cook, Goolsbee and Paulson argued for more rate increases [1][20][22][23][24][25]

Anthropic's record stock market debut was supposed to show that investors still wanted to own artificial intelligence. Instead, the week of Nov. 16 to 20 ended with the S&P 500 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average in correction territory, 10% below their closing records, and the Nasdaq Composite down 2.7% for 2026, CNBC reported [1]. The S&P 500 fell 8.6% over the week, its worst week since April 2025; the Dow fell 8%, its worst since March 2020; and the Nasdaq lost about 10%, its largest one-week pullback in more than six years [1].

The selling ran from Taipei to Frankfurt to New York and spread to gold and bitcoin. This analysis pulls together MLQ.ai's coverage of the week, from Anthropic's $83 pricing and Nvidia's turmoil after Jensen Huang's exit to the Federal Reserve officials who spent the week talking about more rate increases. It uses only figures reported by the sources cited, and views are those of the people quoted.

## Five days, five losses

The week opened with a sell-off in chip stocks before Nvidia's results. On Monday the Nasdaq fell 2.49% to 24,509.76, the S&P 500 1.92% to 7,416.59 and the Dow 739.38 points to 51,608.17, with Nvidia down 4.1% (Monday's close) [5]. On Tuesday the Dow lost another 848.75 points and the S&P 500 closed at its lowest since late July, while software stocks led the declines before Anthropic's pricing; Salesforce fell 9.45% and ServiceNow 6.51% (Tuesday's close) [4]. Europe had its worst day in eight months the same day.

Nvidia's results on Tuesday evening briefly lifted futures, but the S&P 500 fell on Wednesday for a fifth straight session, its longest losing streak since June, and Nvidia closed down 2.89% at $150.96 (Wednesday's close) [3]. On Thursday, the day Anthropic began trading, the Dow fell 1,101.11 points, or 2.19%, to 49,097.06, its first close below 50,000 since June, and the Nasdaq fell 2.25% (Thursday's close) [2]. Friday was the worst day: the S&P 500 fell 3.7% to 6,910.39, the Nasdaq 5.28% to 22,617.73 and the Dow 949.73 points to 48,147.33, and the Nasdaq-100 entered a bear market on an intraday basis, 20% below its recent high (Friday's close) [1].

Wall Street's fear gauge rose with the losses. The Cboe Volatility Index closed at 25.46 on Tuesday, at 28.44 on Thursday, and rose above 30 early on Friday for the first time since March, CNBC reported [4][2][1]. Defensive stocks held up on Friday: Walmart rose more than 1%, and Costco, Johnson & Johnson and Procter & Gamble each gained about 1% [1].

## Anthropic's debut did not turn the market

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share on Wednesday night, above its marketed $70 to $78 range, selling 980 million shares in an $81.34 billion deal, its final prospectus shows (our pricing report) [6]. Its stock opened on Thursday at $111.50, touched $115.10 and closed at $97.17, up 17.1% from the IPO price, Nasdaq data show (our day-one report) [7]. The market averages hit their session lows just after Anthropic began trading, CNBC reported [2]. "Today doesn't matter," Baird tech strategist Ted Mortonson told CNBC. "It's a blink of an eye of their journey" [2].

On Friday the shares closed at $88.30, down 9.1% from Thursday's close and 6.4% above the IPO price, after falling as low as $83.05, StockAnalysis data show (our day-two report) [8]. Anthropic said the underwriters had bought all 147 million extra shares under their option, bringing gross IPO proceeds to about $93.5 billion [9]. Reuters reported that Morgan Stanley, the stabilization agent, had closed the position meant to support the stock by exercising that option, removing a buyer of last resort below $83 [10]. MLQ.ai's earlier coverage looked at who is getting rich from the IPO and at its path into the Nasdaq-100.

The cooling appetite for AI listings showed elsewhere: Singapore's DayOne put its US IPO of up to $5 billion on hold amid the sell-off, Bloomberg reported [31] (our report).

## Nvidia without Huang

Nvidia started the week without Jensen Huang, who resigned as chief executive on Nov. 12; finance chief Colette Kress became interim CEO, the company's filing shows [11]. Its third-quarter revenue rose 103% from a year earlier to $115.8 billion and it guided to $130.0 billion for the fourth quarter, plus or minus 3% (our results report) [12]. On the call, chair Harvey Jones said the board does not intend to reinstate Huang (our report).

The results did not stop the slide. After the Wall Street Journal reported on Tuesday that the SEC was investigating Nvidia (our report), Reuters reported on Friday, citing two people briefed on the matter, that SEC enforcement staff are scrutinizing potential accounting issues at the company; the SEC declined to comment (our report) [13]. Nvidia fell about 14% on Friday, with selling increasing after the Reuters report, and posted its longest weekly losing streak since 2008, CNBC reported [1]. Earlier in the day it slipped behind Alphabet to become the fourth-most valuable US company, Reuters reported, citing LSEG data (our report) [14]. MarketWatch said Nvidia had erased $811 billion in market value in seven days [15].

Huang will testify publicly before the Senate Banking Committee, a spokesperson for Chairman Tim Scott said on Friday, though no date is set, and he has agreed to a closed-door interview with three House committees on Dec. 1 (our report) [29]. WIRED reported on the board's concerns before his resignation (our report).

## Asia, Europe, gold and bitcoin

The sharpest losses were in Asia's chip markets. Taiwan's Taiex fell 6.23% on Friday to 36,033.29 and lost 17.5% over the week, its biggest weekly loss on record, Focus Taiwan reported (our Asia close) [16]. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 5.06% on Friday and South Korea's Kospi 5.21% [32][33]; the Nikkei lost 7.7% for the week and the Shanghai Composite 2.8%, RTHK reported [17]. Taiwan had already tightened short-selling curbs after a 4.4% fall on Thursday (our report).

In Europe, the STOXX 600 closed down 2.0% on Friday at 625.65 and fell 5.8% over the week, its worst week since March, as ASML fell 8.7%; Germany's DAX lost more than 7% on the week (our Europe close) [18][19].

Assets that often rise in a stock sell-off did not. Spot gold was heading for a 9% weekly loss on Friday morning, Reuters reported, and Euronews said it was on course for its worst week in eight months, as bets on a December Fed rate increase kept the dollar near a 19-month high (our report) [20][21][22]. Bitcoin fell below $60,000 on Friday for the first time since July, trading at $58,966.58 at 1:29 p.m. ET, CNBC reported (our report) [1], a day after falling to $61,800.

## The Fed kept talking about rate increases

The sell-off came as central bankers argued for tighter policy. Minutes of the Fed's October meeting, released on Wednesday, showed almost all officials saw further rate increases as likely appropriate (our report) [28], and Governor Christopher Waller said the Fed may need to "shock people" with a half-point increase in December (our report) [34]. JPMorgan now expects increases at the Fed's next three meetings (our report) [35].

Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said "the AI boom is contributing to the potential of overheating in the economy" and that "the evidence does not suggest we are on a path back to 2%" (our report) [23]. Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson said the Fed has "more tightening to do" (our report) [25][26]. On Friday Governor Lisa Cook said waiting longer to raise rates risks getting behind the curve (our report) [24]. In Europe, Bundesbank President Joachim Nagel said a December ECB increase would send a clear signal (our report) [27]. The FOMC next meets Dec. 8-9 [23].

Policy risk also came from the White House. On Friday afternoon President Trump said he wants to stop US companies from building data centers in other countries and that they would face "tremendous penalties," without describing a legal mechanism or timeline (our report) [1].

## How investors described it

"The stock market is going through an AI repricing, reflecting an environment of slowing data center buildouts, reduced funding sources for AI-related projects and a slower trajectory of technology-related earnings growth," Richard Saperstein of Treasury Partners told CNBC [1]. "This is not just a valuation-driven correction. It's becoming broader... Right now, it's definitely risk-off," said Massimo Santicchia of Procyon [1].
