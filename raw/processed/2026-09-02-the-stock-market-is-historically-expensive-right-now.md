# The Stock Market Is Historically Expensive Right Now. Here's Why I'm Still Investing.

The S&P 500's CAPE ratio has reached its highest level since 2000, prompting investor anxiety about a potential downturn. Despite this, long-term investors have good reasons to stay the course.

**Time horizon matters.** The S&P 500 has averaged approximately 10% annual returns since 1928, through every major crisis. Short-term volatility is noise for patient investors.

**Diversification reduces concentration risk.** Beyond the S&P 500, owning broader U.S. funds (like VTI's 3,500+ stocks) and international ETFs spreads exposure away from richly-valued mega-cap tech.

**Dollar-cost averaging works in downturns too.** Regular fixed investments mean that market dips automatically buy more shares — counterintuitive, but it's true: stock market downturns can actually be "good news" for long-term investors who are still in the accumulation phase.

While uncertainty remains, a diversified, low-cost index fund portfolio is expected to reward patience over the next decade and beyond.
