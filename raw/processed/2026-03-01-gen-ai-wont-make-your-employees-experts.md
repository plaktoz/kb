# Gen AI Won't Make Your Employees Experts

source_url: https://hbr.org/2026/03/gen-ai-wont-make-your-employees-experts

---

Published: March 2026 (Harvard Business Review)

The article examines whether generative AI can bridge the performance gap between novices and experts. While prior research — including work by MIT's David Autor — shows gen AI "shortens the time it takes novices to gain competence at new tasks," the core question explored is whether it enables novices to perform as well as experts.

The finding: gen AI "can help workers perform unfamiliar tasks more quickly, but it doesn't eliminate the performance gap between novices and experts."

This is directly relevant to leaders in fields like copywriting and software development who are betting that gen AI can help employees take on more-advanced responsibilities. The research challenges the assumption that an AI-assisted junior employee is equivalent to an experienced senior: the underlying judgment, pattern recognition, and error-detection abilities that define expertise are not transferred by AI tooling.

The implication is that organizations which eliminate expert roles in favor of AI-augmented novices may be trading hard-to-recover capabilities for short-term cost savings. Deliberate skill development and mentorship remain essential even in AI-augmented workflows.

Key themes: expertise, skill development, generative AI, knowledge work, workforce strategy.
