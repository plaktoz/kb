# Coca-Cola Hasn't Sold Much More Soda in a Decade. Its Dividend Has Gone Up Every Year Anyway.

Coca-Cola's beverage system sold roughly 33.8 billion unit cases in 2025, only about 16% more than a decade earlier (~1.5%/year growth). Yet the dividend climbed from $1.32/share in 2015 to $2.12 today — a 61% increase — with raises every single year, including a ~4% boost in February marking the company's 64th consecutive annual increase.

## What Funded the Raises?

The key driver was a structural business transformation, not volume growth. Between 2016–2018, Coca-Cola refranchised most of its low-margin bottling operations, shedding billions in revenue but dramatically improving margins.

- Revenue dropped from $44.3B (2015) to $31.9B (2018), then recovered to $47.9B (2025)
- Operating income rose from $8.7B to $13.8B over that period
- Operating margin expanded from ~20% to ~29%

Pricing also contributed heavily. In 2025, organic revenue grew 5% while unit case volume was flat — four points of that growth came from price and mix, with just one point from concentrate sales.

## Can the Streak Continue?

The math looks supportive. Management expects ~$12.4B in free cash flow in 2026, while the dividend costs just over $9B — roughly three-quarters of projected cash flow. Volume has also recently accelerated; Q2 unit case volume grew 5% year-over-year, and management raised organic revenue guidance to ~5% for the year.

## Valuation Concern

Despite the strong streak, the author cautions that "safety and value aren't the same thing." At ~$88/share, KO trades at roughly 25x earnings with a 2.4% dividend yield. The author concludes the dividend will likely keep growing but wouldn't pay the current premium price for it.
