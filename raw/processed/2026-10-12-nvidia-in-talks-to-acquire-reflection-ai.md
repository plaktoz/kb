# Nvidia in Talks to Acquire Reflection AI

Nvidia no longer wants to just build the chips that A.I. models run on; it apparently wants to own the models, too: The chip giant is in talks to acquire Reflection AI or significantly increase its stake in the start-up. That is according to the Financial Times, as summarized by Reuters. The talks are at an early stage, and a deal could come within weeks, but the discussions could still fall apart. Reflection declined to comment.

## Acquisition, Acqui-Hire or More Money

According to the FT, three options are on the table: a full acquisition, a so-called acqui-hire or a larger investment. In an acqui-hire, Nvidia would hire Reflection’s employees and license its technology without buying the company. That structure could avoid a lengthy regulatory review. Nvidia has used it before: In December 2025, the company brought on the team of the chip start-up Groq and licensed its technology, a deal CNBC valued at about $20 billion (about €18 billion).

Nvidia is already one of Reflection’s biggest backers and has committed $800 million (about €712 million), according to the FT. In April, Misha Laskin, the chief executive, told CNBC that Reflection was raising new money at a $25 billion (about €22 billion) pre-money valuation. In total, the start-up has raised close to $2.6 billion from investors including Nvidia, Sequoia Capital and Lightspeed.

## America’s Answer to DeepSeek

Reflection was founded in 2024 by Misha Laskin and Ioannis Antonoglou, two former Google DeepMind researchers. The start-up recently released Beam, its first open-weight model, which is built for coding and A.I. agents and aims to compete with low-cost Chinese models such as DeepSeek and Kimi. An acquisition would expand Nvidia’s model business, which already includes its open Nemotron models.

Competition in the open-weight segment is fierce. Besides the Chinese providers, Mistral recently unveiled Large 4, a model with one trillion parameters whose weights are set to be released. Mistral raised $3.5 billion in the third quarter, the largest venture round ever for a European company.

## Antitrust Questions

Nvidia could easily afford the deal: Over the past four quarters, the company generated about $127 billion in free cash flow. Market power is the more sensitive issue. Nvidia dominates the market for A.I. chips while investing in a growing number of companies that buy those chips. According to Yahoo Finance, an acqui-hire structure may avoid a classic merger review, but it does not shield a deal from antitrust scrutiny.
