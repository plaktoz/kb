# AI Makes Building Easy. Choosing What to Build Is Harder.

AI is making it possible for individuals with little technical expertise to build sophisticated products, raising questions about whether collaboration is becoming less important. Observations from an AI-enabled innovation challenge suggest the opposite. As AI accelerates research, coding, and prototyping, teams create the most value during problem definition and solution design, where diverse perspectives help challenge assumptions and refine ideas. Fast prototypes become tools for learning rather than finished products, making debates more concrete and exposing weaknesses early.

AI tools are powerful enough to make solo-founding increasingly viable, allowing innovators with no technical skills to create functional products. The solo builder concept is playing out not only in startups but also in larger organizations as AI adoption grows: AI has commoditized execution across the innovation lifecycle, letting product managers build working prototypes without touching a line of code and developers accelerate software development, testing, documentation, and data manipulation.

The authors' recommendation: managers should protect time for defining problems, use prototypes to improve thinking rather than simply validate solutions, and build teams around complementary perspectives rather than technical specialties. As AI commoditizes execution, competitive advantage increasingly comes from deciding what is worth building and why.

J.P. Eggers is the Catherine & Peter Kellner Professor of Entrepreneurship at NYU Stern School of Business. Sarah Ryan is Director, Venture of Acceleration at NYU Stern School of Business. Asha Dinesh is Marketing Insights Lead at Miro.
