# Stock Market Today (Oct. 12, 2026): Dow futures climb as Wall Street gears up for busy week

Investors are monitoring escalating tensions in the Middle East, which threaten further disruptions to global energy supplies.

*This live blog is refreshed throughout the day with the latest updates from the market.*

**Happy Monday.** Stock futures were mixed and oil prices were rising as Wall Street geared up for a week of earnings and economic reports.

Markets closed higher on Friday, driven by a broad-based rebound as early-week jitters over technology and artificial intelligence stocks subsided.

JPMorgan (JPM), Goldman Sachs (GS) and Citi (C) are among the companies reporting results on Tuesday.

The September consumer and producer price indexes are due Wednesday and Thursday, respectively.

"Wall Street edged higher to round out the week, but persistent geopolitical risk and uncertainty about global monetary policy continue to plague the market," said Kyle Rodda, senior financial market analyst at Capital.com.

"The uncertainty has bled into the new week, especially as escalating tensions in the Middle East over the weekend risk further supply disruptions in global energy markets."

Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia's main international airport killed at least 13 people over the weekend, including a U.S. national, and injured hundreds more, prompting President Donald Trump to consider joining the Saudi-led campaign against the Iran-backed group in Yemen, according to media reports.

The escalation threatens to further disrupt global energy supplies, with the Houthis also posing a potential threat to shipping through the strategically important Bab el-Mandeb Strait.

Meanwhile, Trump announced an agreement with Russian President Vladimir Putin to import millions of tons of Russian diesel fuel in an effort to lower U.S. and global fuel prices ahead of the midterm elections.

A bipartisan group of six senators on Sunday called on the Trump administration to "immediately reverse course" on the deal, arguing that the move conflicts with U.S. sanctions policy toward Russia.

"Despite the geopolitical risk and interest rate uncertainty, Wall Street remains around record highs thanks to extraordinary (albeit highly concentrated) earnings growth," Rodda said. "Analysts tip 30% EPS growth for the upcoming quarter.

"And although the all-important Magnificent Seven don't report for several weeks still, the kickoff bank earnings will provide color on whether corporate fundamentals remain strong enough to keep driving stocks higher."

## SpaceX Climbs in Early Trading as Australia, NZ Telecoms Fall

SpaceX (SPCX) shares gained 1.06% to $164.30 ahead of the opening bell.

Elon Musk's aerospace and artificial intelligence company announced plans to acquire Grain Management's nationwide 800 MHz low-band wireless spectrum portfolio for about $8 billion.

Telecom stocks in the U.S. and Europe tumbled amid concerns about growing competition from satellite-based mobile services. Telecom shares in Australia and New Zealand also fell Monday, Reuters reported.

In addition, the Federal Communications Commission officially approved SpaceX's plan to deploy 15,000 next-generation direct-to-device (D2D) satellites for its Starlink Mobile network.

SpaceX shares debuted at $135 apiece on June 12 and climbed to a record $225.64 on June 16.

## Iran War News: Iran to Reply to US on Hormuz Proposal After Review

Iran is still reviewing a U.S. response to its proposal for reopening the Strait of Hormuz and will reply to Washington through intermediaries once the review is complete, Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei said Monday, Reuters reported.

Baghaei told a news conference that Tehran continues to exchange messages through intermediaries but provided no further details.

Qatar and Pakistan, among others, have served as mediators in efforts to end the war, which began with U.S. and Israeli strikes on Iran on Feb. 28.

A central point of disagreement is Washington's insistence on addressing Iran's nuclear program from the outset of negotiations.

Tehran says talks should initially focus on reopening the Strait of Hormuz and restoring security in the waterway, which before the war carried about one-fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas trade.

On the escalating conflict between Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Houthis, an Iranian ally, Baghaei reiterated Tehran's willingness to help resolve the crisis through dialogue.

"The issue of Yemen should have been resolved a long time ago with the (UN) roadmap, and we had said we would support any agreement reached by Yemenis and Saudis," Baghaei said. "Our request is that this situation be resolved through dialogue."

## Oil Market Update: Crude Rises on Mideast Strikes, Russian Diesel Deal

Oil prices climbed as markets weighed escalating tensions in the Middle East against President Donald Trump's announced deal to import Russian diesel.

West Texas Intermediate crude rose 1.13% to $92.89 per barrel, while Brent crude gained 1.17% to $106 per barrel.

The Iran-backed Houthis claimed responsibility for a Saturday strike on Riyadh's international airport that killed 12 people and wounded more than 300. A second attack on the airport on Sunday killed another person and injured 11, Reuters reported.

Separately, two tankers were attacked over the weekend, raising concerns about further disruptions to Middle East crude exports.

One tanker was struck by a drone near Oman's Sohar, while another was damaged while transiting the Strait of Hormuz, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations reported.

Trump announced the Russian diesel deal on social media Friday, saying Russia would supply fuel to U.S. and global markets to help lower prices.

Trump also said Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an "energy ceasefire" in their 4 1/2-year-old war. He provided no details, and Ukraine reported continued Russian attacks on energy facilities after the announcement.

The deal runs counter to U.S. efforts to restrict the revenue Russia earns from oil and gas sales, which helps fund its war against Ukraine.
