# Google stock keeps falling after 12% plunge on Q3 earnings miss

Google (GOOG, GOOGL) stock continued its steep declines on Friday, falling another 1% after missing Q3 earnings expectations this week, despite Google Cloud growth of 103%. The stock just had its worst day since it went public in 2004, falling nearly 13% on Thursday.

Google reported earnings per share (EPS) of $2.12 on revenue of 127.58 billion. Analysts were looking for EPS of $3.01 on revenue of $127.93 billion. Revenue excluding traffic acquisition costs came in at $111.21 billion. Wall Street was anticipating $111.29 billion.

Google's advertising segment saw sales of $82.01 billion versus expectations of $83.5 billion, while its cloud unit brought in $30.71 billion. Analysts were projecting revenue of $29.1 billion.

Google is also burning through its free cash flow, declining from negative $5.85 billion in Q2 to negative $9.06 billion in Q3.

The results come just hours after the company debuted its latest frontier AI model.

Google said the new model, Gemini 4 Carbon, outpaces even its Gemini 4 Argon, which it released just two weeks ago. The company claimed Carbon also beats out competing models, including OpenAI's (OPAI.PVT) GPT-6 Astra and Anthropic's (ANTH.PVT) Claude Opus 5.5.

According to Google CEO Sundar Pichai, the Gemini app now has more than 1 billion users, making it the fastest-growing app in the company's history.

Investors on Wall Street, meanwhile, have been vacillating between concern and exuberance about the future of AI and whether hyperscalers' vast investments in the technology will pay off.

Those fears came into focus last week as traders dumped CoreWeave stock amid questions about the company's massive debt load, sending large swaths of the market lower.

On Tuesday, Oracle announced that it will be the first company to purchase Google's AI processors for use in its own data centers.

Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives expressed a bullish take on an otherwise bearish market reaction.

"Cloud doubling on contracted demand with margin expanding at the same time settles the demand side of the capex debate for this quarter, and TPU systems contributing now rather than next year puts a second growth engine on the field the other hyperscalers do not have."

But Google is also facing increasing competition from Meta, thanks to its new Muse AI agent. According to Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg, 10 million people have set up Muse, with the majority of users accessing the app for shopping.

If Muse continues to expand its user base and gain ground in online sales, it could prove to be a long-term threat to Google.

"Search usage [and] growth is back in focus given Muse launch and challenges Google could face in migrating users to agentic while maintaining strong monetization," Post wrote.

Pichai made the case on the earnings call that Google's agentic tools add to the search business, addressing long-standing concerns about AI offerings cannibalizing the company's core revenue streams. "I would view it as an expansion opportunity," Pichai said of agents.

"Just like a few years ago, being able to incorporate AI chat use cases in search allowed people to ask more and do more in search, which is what is playing out in our usage."
