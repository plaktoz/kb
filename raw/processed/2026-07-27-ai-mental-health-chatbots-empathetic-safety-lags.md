# AI Mental Health Chatbots Feel Empathetic, But Safety Evidence Still Lags

source_url: https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260727/AI-mental-health-chatbots-feel-empathetic-but-safety-evidence-still-lags.aspx

---

Author: Tarun Sai Lomte
Published: July 27, 2026 — News-Medical.net

A new scoping review in npj Digital Medicine finds that AI-powered mental health chatbots can feel accessible, personal, and empathetic, but the evidence still leaves key questions about safety, sustained use, and real-world clinical value.

## Background

Around 25% of people worldwide experience a mental health problem, yet approximately 85% do not receive adequate treatment due to barriers such as stigma, costs, professional shortages, and geographic distance. Traditional mental health chatbots deliver pre-scripted therapeutic content, but are limited in their ability to personalize support. Unlike traditional chatbots, large language model (LLM)-based chatbots can simulate core aspects of therapeutic encounters, including personalized suggestions and empathetic reflections.

## The Study

Researchers reviewed 21 studies conducted in 11 countries from 2023 to 2025. Interventions ranged from early-stage prototype evaluations to clinical trials. Most studies included general or clinical adult populations; sample sizes ranged from 5 to 527 participants.

## Intervention Design

Chatbot interventions most often targeted depression and anxiety, using shared therapeutic mechanisms including mindfulness, emotion regulation, and cognitive restructuring. Most interventions were based on CBT principles and were short-term (2–8 weeks). About 67% of interventions were text-based chatbots; others used voice, avatar-based, or augmented reality interactions.

## User Experience Findings

- Participants described interventions as convenient and accessible with generally moderate-to-high acceptability and high reported satisfaction.
- Users preferred free-flowing chat interfaces and customizable features over predefined options.
- Most chatbots featured emotion detection with adaptive interaction for personalized, empathetic reflections.
- However, attrition patterns emerged over time — high initial uptake in multi-week interventions declined over the course of the intervention.

## Key Safety Concerns

- Personalization and empathy did not consistently translate into stronger clinical outcomes or sustained use.
- Some users reported repetitive, generic, or contextually misaligned responses.
- Users raised concerns about over-reliance on chatbots, reduced human contact, data privacy, and the ability to respond appropriately during crises.
- Inaccurate or clinically misaligned outputs were linked to erosion of trust and disengagement.

## Concluding Remarks

GenAI chatbots have meaningful potential to deliver tailored, empathetic mental health support and show promising acceptability. Nevertheless, standardizing UX assessment, sustaining engagement, and ensuring safety remain major challenges. Addressing them will require co-design with experts and users, validated UX metrics in long-term studies, transparent reporting standards, and stronger attention to safety, equity, and crisis-response limits.
