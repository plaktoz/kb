# Superintelligence is a Fairy Tale. But Chasing It Can Still Cause Harm.

Newport argues that while true superintelligent AI remains impossible with current technology, the obsessive pursuit of it by labs like Anthropic and OpenAI creates real, near-term dangers.

## Three Core Points

**1. Superintelligent AI is not achievable by current labs.**
Newport states they have "no idea how to create such machines" and that scaling LLMs is insufficient. He dismisses recursive self-improvement arguments as decades-old Silicon Valley mythology.

**2. The obsession itself causes harm.**
Believing they're racing toward transformative power leads these companies to cut corners. He cites reckless OpenAI agent experiments tied to a HuggingFace hacking incident as an example — dangerous configurations deployed carelessly in pursuit of benchmark milestones.

**3. Responsible AI progress is possible.**
Newport stresses that "AI is not the same thing as LLMs." Many impressive AI achievements — protein research, autonomous vehicles, game-playing systems — are largely unrelated to LLMs. Slowing these specific labs wouldn't halt broader AI advancement.

## Conclusion

Newport calls for regulatory intervention, arguing the labs are too ideologically committed to self-correct. He explicitly frames this not as opposition to AI generally, but to a narrow group's reckless approach.
