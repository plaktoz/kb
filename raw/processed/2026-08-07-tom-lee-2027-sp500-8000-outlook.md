# Tom Lee Thinks 2027 Will Be "One of the Best Years" for Stocks—Why He's Got a Strong Case

Fundstrat managing partner and head of research Tom Lee, a prominent and frequently-right Wall Street bull, believes a phenomenal 2027 could follow a solid 2026, targeting the S&P 500 at 8,000. His case rests on rising economic growth from AI productivity gains, America's position as an "AI export superpower," a rotation of capital from private to public markets, and a large pool of sidelined Millennial wealth that could flow into equities.

The bullish case comes with serious counterweights. Dr. Michael Burry has drawn comparisons to 1987 — one of the steepest market crashes in history — citing toppiness in the market, and remains short the semiconductor and tech trade via put options. JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon has separately warned about high levels of market leverage, noting retail margin debt is near its highest level in a long while, a risk the article argues has been heavily discounted by the retail investing crowd even as SpaceX and SK Hynix both began trading on the Nasdaq during a hot summer for trading. Investor Bill Ackman has also warned that investors are chasing "new new stocks" over quality names trading at reasonable multiples, suggesting a stock picker's market has quietly returned.

The article's author notes the market has so far proven resilient in the face of geopolitical conflict, oil-shock fears, and other unknowns. Whether 2027 becomes one of the market's best years, the piece argues, will likely hinge on whether AI monetization lives up to expectations and inflation moderates enough that the Federal Reserve doesn't need to raise rates — and cautions that elevated margin levels may need to unwind before the market can move meaningfully higher, even as sidelined capital and AI productivity gains provide a real tailwind.
