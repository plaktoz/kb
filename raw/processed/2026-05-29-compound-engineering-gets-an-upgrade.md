# Compound Engineering Gets an Upgrade

source_url: https://every.to/p/compound-engineering-gets-an-upgrade

---

By Kieran Klaassen, May 29, 2026

Subtitle: The AI-native engineering philosophy has expanded from four steps to eight.

Klaassen explains that compound engineering, the AI-assisted development approach he coined roughly a year prior, began as a four-step cycle: "Brainstorm → work → review → compound → repeat." That loop still underlies how he builds Cora, Every's email product.

He notes that as AI models improved, the "work" phase became less demanding of human attention, since a well-context agent typically writes code and fixes issues correctly on its own. This shift raised the question of where human involvement remains most valuable. His collaborator Trevin Chow offered a "sandwich" analogy — AI handles the middle, while humans provide the "bread" on either end.

Klaassen describes his own role as occurring at the start (deciding what's worth building, understanding users and edge cases) and at the end (checking design, copy, and overall feel of the product, even when it "technically works").

Because of this, he expanded the original loop into eight steps: "Ideate → brainstorm → plan → work → review → polish → compound → repeat." He emphasizes that "ideate" and "brainstorm" now form the front end, while "polish" is a new final stage, and that "compound" remains central since each feature should make future work easier.

He also mentions updating the compound engineering guide to reflect this fuller system, suggesting the framework extends beyond coding to knowledge work generally — arguing that even as automation handles more of the "middle," human involvement at the beginning and end keeps the work original and high-quality.
