# MARKETS LIVE: Wall Street closes lower as Alphabet sell-off revives AI spending concerns

Last updated: 07:25 30 Oct 2026 AEDT, First published: 22:00 29 Oct 2026 AEDT

## 4:20pm: Wall Street closes lower

US stocks ended lower on Thursday as a sharp sell-off in Alphabet reignited concerns about the returns on heavy artificial intelligence investment, while stronger economic growth and accelerating inflation added to the market's uncertainty.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 133 points, or 0.3%, to 52,343, while the S&P 500 shed 24 points, or 0.3%, to 7,720. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 365 points, or 1.4%, to 26,185.

Alphabet shares closed around 12% lower after the Google parent reported disappointing third-quarter revenue, weighing on the broader technology sector and prompting renewed scrutiny of the capital spending required to build AI infrastructure.

The declines were concentrated in large technology stocks, with around 390 S&P 500 constituents advancing despite the benchmark's fall, suggesting investors were rotating into other parts of the market.

Economic data offered a mixed picture for investors. US gross domestic product expanded at an annualized rate of 3.4%, its fastest pace in a year and ahead of expectations, pointing to continued economic resilience.

However, core personal consumption expenditures inflation, the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation measure, rose 0.5% in September. The combination of stronger growth and persistent price pressures could complicate the outlook for interest rates.

Attention now turns to a busy slate of earnings after the closing bell, with Amazon, Intel, Gilead Sciences, SanDisk, Starbucks and Strategy among the companies due to report.

## 1:25pm: Demand outpacing economy's capacity: Adams

Fifth Third chief economist Bill Adams said rising prices, rather than increased purchasing power, accounted for last month’s growth in consumer spending, while falling real incomes pushed the saving rate to a multiyear low.

Adams noted that the saving rate does not reflect Americans’ income from capital gains, but offers insight into financial pressures on low- and middle-income households and helps explain weak consumer sentiment.

“Real GDP growth picked up in the third quarter, supported by solid growth of consumer spending and a surge of business capex,” Adams said. He added that core domestic demand grew at its fastest pace since 2021, while the widening trade deficit could reinforce the Federal Reserve’s concerns that demand is outpacing the economy’s capacity, strengthening the case for further interest rate increases.

On a monthly basis, core PCE rose 0.3%, matching forecasts and up from 0.2% previously. Headline PCE increased 0.5%, also in line with estimates, compared with 0.3% in the prior reading.

US gross domestic product (GDP) grew at an annualized rate of 3.4% in the third quarter, beating expectations for 3.2% and accelerating from 2.2% in the previous quarter.

However, real consumption was flat against expectations for 0.1% growth, slowing sharply from 0.6% previously.

## 11:10am: Alphabet wipes $500B in valuation

Alphabet shares are down almost 12% after Wall Street digested a disappointing earnings report.

XTB’s Kathleen Brooks said Alphabet’s results have exposed growing investor concerns about the cost of the AI boom, with the company reporting two consecutive quarters of negative cash flow as it invests heavily in AI infrastructure.

“The big problem for investors when it comes to AI is the cost of the payoff,” Brooks said, adding that Alphabet’s cash flow performance was unacceptable to investors and had contributed to a roughly 10% pre-market decline in its shares.

Brooks said the AI trade was becoming increasingly selective rather than moving as a unified group. Microsoft’s shares rose 3.5% after results impressed investors, while upcoming earnings from Amazon and Apple would provide further tests of sentiment towards the sector.

## 10:00 am ET: AI spending fears back

As indicated in pre-market trade, US stocks opened lower on Thursday as Alphabet’s results reignited concerns about soaring AI costs, while investors assessed fresh inflation data following the Federal Reserve’s decision to hold interest rates steady.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 389p, or 0.74%, to 52,086.13 at the open, while the S&P 500 dropped 44.4p, or 0.57%, to 7,699.61.

Wall Street is trying to understand the inflation outlook and what it means for the Federal Reserve’s next move on interest rates.

The US economy grew at an annualised rate of 3.4% in the third quarter, accelerating from 2.2% in the previous quarter and beating Wall Street’s 3.2% forecast, as the AI spending boom continued to support growth.

Real final sales to private domestic purchasers, a closely watched measure of underlying demand, rose 4.9%, up from 4.6% in the second quarter.

Higher government spending also supported growth, partly offset by increased imports, which weigh on GDP.

US inflation accelerated in September as higher gasoline and goods prices pushed up consumer costs, reinforcing expectations that the Federal Reserve could raise interest rates in December.

The Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index rose 0.5% in September, following a 0.3% increase in August, according to data released by the Commerce Department’s Bureau of Economic Analysis on Thursday, matching economists’ forecasts.

Annual PCE inflation increased to 3.6% from 3.4% in August.
