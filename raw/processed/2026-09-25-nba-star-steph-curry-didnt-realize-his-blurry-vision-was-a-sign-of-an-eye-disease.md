# NBA Star Steph Curry Didn't Realize His Blurry Vision Was a Sign of an Eye Disease

Twelve-time NBA All-Star Stephen Curry has opened up about his keratoconus (KC) diagnosis — a progressive eye condition where the cornea thins and bulges into an irregular cone shape, causing blurred or distorted vision, light sensitivity, and frequent prescription changes.

Curry was unaware anything was wrong until Warriors GM Bob Myers texted him a photo of himself squinting on a TV broadcast and urged him to get checked. At 29, Curry was diagnosed and began treatment with corrective lenses. He described the transformation as "a whole new world," noting he'd simply been adapting to distorted sight without realizing it.

Dr. Randy McLaughlin of Ohio State noted KC is typically diagnosed during adolescence and can worsen without treatment, potentially threatening vision permanently. Rigid gas permeable or scleral contact lenses are common solutions, alongside corneal cross-linking therapy to slow progression.

Curry now gets biannual eye screenings and has partnered with Glaukos on the "Could it be KC?" awareness campaign, noting KC disproportionately affects Black and Hispanic communities. He also introduced a children's book, *Eye on the Ball*, at a community school event in Oakland to make eye health conversations more accessible for young people.
