# IBM stock steadies after sharp July selloff as investors digest Q2 2026 fallout

IBM (ISIN US4592001014) stock traded at $236.71 as of August 13, 2026, following a volatile July in which shares dropped 25% — the company's worst single-day performance since 1968 — after a disappointing Q2 revenue update on July 14 that missed estimates by $0.7 billion, with the company noting some customers were reallocating budgets toward AI hardware rather than IBM's software.

Q2 2026 revenue was $17.16 billion, up 1.1% year-over-year but below the $17.46 billion consensus. EPS came in at $2.93, matching estimates and up from $2.80 a year earlier. Net margin was 15.52% and return on equity 35.65%. Within software, growth slowed to 5% (down from a 14% peak in Q4 2025): Transaction Processing revenue tied to IBM's Z mainframe line declined, while Data software rose 19% and Hybrid Cloud (including Red Hat) grew 11%. Roughly 80% of the software business is recurring, providing revenue visibility even as legacy license revenue softens.

IBM reiterated guidance for 4%–5% full-year growth; analyst consensus expects full-year 2026 EPS of 12.33. The July selloff also coincided with the opening of a securities fraud investigation related to the Q2 communication and subsequent price collapse, adding legal overhang alongside the fundamental growth-mix questions. IBM continues to pay a quarterly dividend of $1.69/share (2.86% yield), which remains well covered by expected earnings.

On August 14, IBM declined 1.89% even as the S&P 500 reached a record 7,799, meaning the stock lagged a strong broader-market backdrop as investors continued digesting company-specific news. IBM's next earnings release is expected October 21, 2026.
