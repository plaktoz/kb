# Can Microsoft Stock Keep Earning Like This?

Microsoft (MSFT) trades near $493.95, roughly 92% of its 52-week high, following its most profitable stretch in years. The concern isn't operational failure — it's quieter.

## Record Margins, But At a Cost

Trailing twelve-month net margin hit 40.3%, the highest in at least five years, versus a 36.8% three-year average. Operating margin reached 46.8%. Revenue of $331.8 billion grew ~18% year over year.

However, peak margins rarely persist, particularly when tied to an expensive infrastructure buildout.

## Azure's Growing Bill

Azure revenue surged 43% in fiscal Q4 2026, with demand still exceeding capacity. Capital expenditure reached $41 billion in that single quarter, roughly two-thirds directed toward short-lived assets like CPUs and GPUs. Free cash flow came in at $19.6 billion against $55.4 billion in operating cash flow.

Gross margin dipped to 67% in Q4, declining year over year, with management attributing this to the Azure sales mix shift and AI infrastructure costs.

## Valuation Risk

Management guides fiscal 2027 operating margins down less than one point, while still projecting double-digit revenue growth. The stock trades at 11.1x price-to-sales — about 71% up its 10-year range.

Notably, Microsoft extended assumed useful lives of data centers from 15 to 25 years, which softens reported capex growth without changing actual build-out pace.

The stock returned 20.2% over the past three months but only 0.6% over twelve months, compared to 19.3% for the S&P 500. Its worst peak-to-trough drawdown within that year was 34.5%.

## Key Metric to Watch

The Microsoft Cloud gross margin — 65% in Q4, guided roughly stable — is the leading indicator. If it continues declining while capex rises, the margin peak is likely behind us. Implied volatility sits near the 44th percentile of its 52-week range, suggesting options markets aren't yet pricing elevated downside risk.
