# Could IBM and Lockheed Martin's Quantum Bet Face a Long Road to Returns?

IBM and Lockheed Martin have partnered to establish a quantum innovation hub at ETH Zurich, created through an offset agreement with armasuisse (Switzerland's Federal Office for Defence Procurement). The facility will host Switzerland's first IBM Quantum System Two, running IBM's Quantum Nighthawk processor, at the Swiss National Supercomputing Center in Lugano.

## Strategic Value

For IBM, the hub validates its hardware/software roadmap and expands its European research presence. Its hybrid cloud foundation and recurring software revenue provide funding capacity for quantum R&D.

For Lockheed Martin, the collaboration integrates quantum sensing and advanced manufacturing into defense applications, supported by its record backlog and multi-year revenue visibility.

## Key Risks

- IBM carries high debt levels that "limit overall financial flexibility," and capital allocation toward early-stage quantum may strain resources if software momentum slows.
- Lockheed faces margin pressure from program ramp costs, and committing resources to speculative quantum platforms could "amplify balance-sheet risks" if supply chain issues emerge.

## Conclusion

While the partnership offers strategic upside, near-term financial impact remains conditional on translating research milestones into commercial returns.
