# FDA Clears 2 New Alzheimer's Blood Tests, Including One for Adults as Young as 40

The FDA cleared two new blood tests in August 2026 to help clinicians assess Alzheimer's-related brain changes, bringing the total number of FDA-cleared Alzheimer's blood tests to four.

## The Two New Tests

**PrecivityAD2** (C2N Diagnostics) is the first FDA-cleared Alzheimer's blood test indicated for symptomatic adults aged 40 and older. It measures amyloid-beta 42/40 ratio and p-tau217/np-tau217 ratio, combining them into an Amyloid Probability Score 2 (APS2). In a validation study of 1,142 symptomatic participants, it showed a positive predictive value of 97.6% and negative predictive value of 93.1% against PET scans and CSF biomarkers. The FDA-cleared version is expected to launch later in 2026.

**Elecsys pTau217** (Roche/Eli Lilly) targets adults 55 and older with cognitive symptoms. It measures a single biomarker — phosphorylated tau 217 — and delivers positive, intermediate, or negative results. A study of 2,100+ adults found roughly 88–91% agreement with amyloid PET results. It runs on Roche's existing cobas lab infrastructure, with over 4,500 instruments already installed in U.S. labs.

## Clinical Context

Neither test is a standalone diagnostic tool. Both are designed to inform — not replace — comprehensive clinical evaluation. They complement existing assessments including neurological exams, cognitive testing, brain imaging, and, when warranted, PET or cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) testing.

Physician Dung Trinh, MD, of MemorialCare Medical Group, noted that younger adults with cognitive symptoms face wide-ranging alternative explanations, including sleep disorders, mood conditions, and metabolic issues. He emphasized that a positive result "can have major implications for work, family, finances, and emotional well-being," requiring careful patient selection and counseling.

Regarding the Elecsys test, Trinh called it potentially significant for enabling "meaningful biomarker assessment much earlier into the diagnostic process and into primary care," allowing physicians to triage patients before specialist referral.

## Broader Implications

These clearances follow two earlier approvals: the Lumipulse G pTau217/ß-Amyloid 1-42 Plasma Ratio (May 2025) and the Elecsys pTau181 plasma test (October 2025). Together, the four tests represent a shift toward more accessible, less invasive Alzheimer's assessment — though clinical evaluation, specialist input, and confirmatory testing remain essential components of diagnosis.
