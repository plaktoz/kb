# The missing piece in productivity: a proactive approach to employee workplace physical wellbeing

Employee physical health shapes energy, focus and emotional resilience, making it the foundation of workplace performance and productivity. Workers in the UK dissatisfied with their physical health lose 58.4 working days of productivity per year, compared to 35.5 days among satisfied workers, according to the TELUS Mental Health Index. Deloitte estimates presenteeism costs UK employers around £24 billion of the £51 billion lost to poor mental health each year.

Physical health is more than a personal lifestyle choice — it powers an employee's ability to think clearly, manage stress and cope with pressure. Regular moderate physical activity significantly reduces workplace burnout and emotional exhaustion, particularly among desk-based employees.

## The connection between physical and mental health

Employees who stay active show up for work both physically and mentally: greater creativity and productivity, more active problem-solving and stronger workplace cohesion. Regular activity can reduce stress, improve mood and lower the risk of mental ill health. Conversely, poor mental health often leads to poor physical health — one study found people with depression were more likely to be physically inactive and rate their own health as worse.

## The impact of physical health on employee productivity

Harvard Business Review research found that daily physical activity brings physical, affective and cognitive improvements through mutually reinforcing next-day benefits researchers call "resource caravans" — better sleep quality and protein synthesis translate into more energy, vigour, focus and stamina at work.

The pan-European TELUS Mental Health Index found workers dissatisfied with their physical health experienced a productivity loss of 77.8 workdays annually, over 25 days more than satisfied colleagues. Workers who rate employer support for physical wellbeing as poor score 16 points lower on the index and lose an additional 28 working days annually.

## Why companies need to advocate for employee physical health

It can be difficult for employees to maintain an active lifestyle while balancing work and other commitments, giving employers both a responsibility and a vested interest in making space for physical health. A European study found 85% of businesses reported increased productivity with flexible work arrangements, such as flexible start times or extended lunch breaks for fitness classes or medical appointments. One study found employees with higher physical activity levels had a 15% lower risk of workplace injuries.

## Matching support to the wellbeing continuum

Self-service perks tend to engage people who are already thriving. Employees who are struggling or unwell rarely self-initiate care, so employer-led outreach and manager capability matter — training managers to spot exhaustion and burnout early is one of the most practical investments an organisation can make.

A proactive approach — including health risk assessments, direct access to support, and integrated wellbeing solutions connecting digital tools, counselling and coaching — pays off: workers who describe their organisation's culture as supportive of wellbeing lose 24 fewer days of productivity per year than those who describe it as unsupportive.
