# Alison Unveils Free Course About Data Visualisation for Lean Six Sigma

## New CPD-Accredited Certificate Course Teaches Professionals How to Turn Operational Data Into Visual Insights That Drive Improvement

Lean Six Sigma's power lies in its insistence on data. From DMAIC's Measure and Analyse phases to control charts and process capability studies, the methodology is built on the principle that decisions should be evidence-based. Yet one of the most persistent challenges facing process improvement professionals is not the absence of data — it is the difficulty of communicating what that data means. Spreadsheets full of numbers do not drive action. Clear, well-constructed visuals do. As organisations across manufacturing, healthcare, financial services, and the public sector embed Lean Six Sigma into their operations, the ability to translate analytical findings into compelling, accessible visuals has become an increasingly valued — and frequently underdeveloped — skill.

Alison, one of the world's largest free online learning platforms, is responding with the launch of its new Data Visualisation for Lean Six Sigma course. Free, CPD-accredited, and pitched at Intermediate level, the course equips Lean Six Sigma practitioners with the practical skills to build charts, dashboards, and reports that make process insights impossible to ignore.

## From Raw Data to Reliable Insight

The course begins at the foundation: understanding where operational data comes from, how to structure it into clear and usable tables, and how to assess and correct data quality before any analysis begins. Learners work in Excel to calculate baseline process measures and compare results across different groups to identify variation — the starting point for any meaningful improvement.

## Choosing and Building the Right Visualisations

With clean data in hand, the course turns to the question of which visual tells which story. Learners select chart types suited to specific process questions and build the visuals most commonly used in Lean Six Sigma work: Pareto charts for prioritising problems, trend charts for tracking performance over time, histograms for understanding distribution, and scatter charts for exploring relationships between variables. The course also covers how to set KPI targets and status thresholds clearly, and how to arrange dashboards and reports so that key findings surface immediately rather than getting buried.

## Proving Improvement and Sustaining It

The final section addresses one of the most important moments in any improvement project: demonstrating that the change worked. Learners apply before-and-after visuals to show the impact of an intervention and build monitoring tools that keep that improvement visible over time — ensuring that process gains are sustained rather than quietly reversed.
