# Google DeepMind launches institute to widen the AGI debate

Google and Google DeepMind researchers launched the DeepMind Institute to advance AGI discourse. Directors include DeepMind co-founder Shane Legg (managing editor), Google executive James Manyika, and DeepMind chair Demis Hassabis.

The institute aims to surface differing perspectives across Google, Google DeepMind, and the broader research community — acknowledging views will evolve as the field advances.

Four inaugural essays address:
- Economic policies for potential AGI disruption
- Preserving human-readable model reasoning
- Principles for human flourishing
- A framework for evaluating frontier AI models

Safety researchers Rohin Shah and Anca Dragan argue that shrinking AI transparency is not inevitable, suggesting limits on "opaque serial depth" or requiring developers to show less transparent systems remain monitorable.

Hassabis proposes a U.S.-led frontier AI standards body where developers would initially submit models voluntarily up to 30 days pre-release. Eventually, independent "held-out" tests would prevent labs from gaming known evaluations. The framework could be "ratcheted up if the seriousness of the situation demands," potentially including coordinated developer slowdowns.

The essays arrive as the industry safety debate shifts toward concrete proposals, accelerated by Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's recent call to "pace" frontier AI development.
