# Microsoft drops $101.9 billion surprise as AI reshapes company

Microsoft revealed quarterly Azure revenue figures for the first time, disclosing $29.4 billion in its most recent quarter and $101.9 billion for the fiscal year ended June 30. The disclosure gives investors a clearer view of where Azure stands relative to competitors — behind Amazon Web Services but ahead of Google Cloud.

Amazon Web Services generated $42.2 billion in its latest quarter, versus $24.8 billion for Google Cloud. On an annual basis, AWS produced $128.7 billion in calendar 2025 sales, compared to $85.8 billion for Azure over the same period.

Azure and other cloud services revenue grew 43% year over year in Microsoft's fiscal fourth quarter. Company-wide revenue climbed 18% to $90 billion, with operating income also up 18% to $40.6 billion. Full-year revenue reached $331.8 billion.

Beyond the revenue figures, Microsoft is restructuring its reporting segments from three to two. The new "Agents and Infra" segment will combine cloud computing, AI software, and conventional business software. The second segment, "Devices and Consumer," will include Windows, Xbox, Bing advertising, and LinkedIn advertising.

CEO Satya Nadella noted that AI is "blurring the boundaries between our products and reshaping our business models." The restructuring reflects how AI increasingly connects infrastructure, software, and applications that were once reported separately.

Microsoft also slightly revised its Azure revenue forecast — not due to weakened demand, but because certain GitHub sales were reclassified into Microsoft 365 Cloud businesses. Overall quarterly expectations remain unchanged.

A key concern for investors is margin pressure. Cloud gross margin percentages declined year over year, partly due to the sales mix shifting toward Azure and sustained investment in AI infrastructure. Microsoft must demonstrate that AI-driven revenue growth can outpace the substantial costs of building and maintaining that infrastructure.

The company's broader thesis is that Azure infrastructure spending drives adoption of AI software tools — Copilot products, developer tools, and AI agents — creating a reinforcing cycle of infrastructure and software revenue growth.
