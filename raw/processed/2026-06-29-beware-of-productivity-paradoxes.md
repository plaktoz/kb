# Beware of Productivity Paradoxes

source_url: https://calnewport.com/beware-of-productivity-paradoxes/

---

Newport draws a parallel between the personal computer revolution and today's AI boom. Despite enormous promise, PCs failed to deliver the expected productivity gains — a 1991 NYT economist noted that "white-collar productivity has stagnated" despite heavy computer spending. A study (1987–1993) found computers added only 0.2 percentage points annually to business output growth, prompting what Edward Tenner called a "productivity paradox" in his 1997 book Why Things Bite Back.

Newport argues AI faces similar dynamics. While conveniences are real and unlikely to be abandoned — much as PCs were — the leap to transformative productivity gains is far from guaranteed. He notes that prior industrial innovations like the steam engine had far clearer impacts than digital tools did.

His closing caution: "in the digital world, productivity doesn't always match our expectations."

He references his book A World Without Email for deeper exploration of computing's uneven impact on knowledge work.
