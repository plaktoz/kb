# Students increasingly turn to AI for mental health support

New research finds students are using AI for emotional support, including when distressed and as a personal therapist or coach.

One in four students responding to a University of Bath survey said they had used generative AI for mental health support in the previous six months, according to a new peer-reviewed study in JMIR Mental Health.

Students used AI for advice when distressed, to understand their mental health and as a personal therapist or coach.

Use was highest among students with very low wellbeing, with more than a third reporting using AI for mental health support.

The findings come amid growing interest in how generative AI might shape mental health support, particularly among young adults who are already familiar with digital technologies.

Generative AI is already changing how people seek support for their mental health. Our findings suggest that many students are turning to tools such as ChatGPT when they are struggling, including some experiencing particularly poor wellbeing."

There is enormous potential for AI to make mental health information and support more accessible, personalized and engaging. But we need to distinguish between what these tools could offer and what we know about their safety and effectiveness.

The reality is that people are already using AI for their mental health, whether or not universities and healthcare services are ready for it. Rather than ignoring this, we need to understand how these tools are being used, where they might help, and where they could cause harm."

Dr. Jeffrey Lambert, lead author, University of Bath

The study surveyed 2,020 students from the University of Bath. It also found students with a declared disability were more likely to report using AI for mental health support.

The researchers say AI could widen access to support, but there are still questions around its safety and effectiveness. They highlight risks including inaccurate information, privacy concerns and poor responses when someone is in crisis.

Nic Streatfield, Director of Student Support & Safeguarding at the University of Bath said:

"Students are already using AI for wellbeing support, given this reality, we have developed guidance to help students understand the benefits and risks, what to think about before using AI, and when to seek further help

"AI can be useful, but it is not a replacement for professional or crisis support. Our guidance helps students understand its limits and know where to turn when they need more support."

The University of Bath has published dedicated guidance for students: Thinking about using GenAI for wellbeing support? What you need to know first.

The researchers say universities need to recognize that students are already turning to AI and help them understand its safety, privacy and limits.

Although more than 2,000 students participated, this represented 9.1% of those invited. The researchers caution that the study was conducted at a single university and may not be representative of students across the UK.

The researchers are now expanding the work through a national, multi-university study to better understand how students across the UK use AI for mental health support, including the potential benefits, risks and implications for student support services.

Source: University of Bath
