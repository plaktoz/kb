# Monday Master Class: A Crash Course in Student Time Management

Cal Newport rounds up his best time-management essays into a single crash course, arguing that time management is the "productivity glue" underlying all other study strategies — without control of your time, even the smartest study techniques won't help.

Key pieces in the roundup:

- **Getting Things Done for College Students**: Adapts David Allen's GTD system to student life; forms the backbone of a durable task-management system.
- **The Visual Panic Schedule**: A technique for turbo-charging time management during high-intensity crunch periods.
- **The Auto-Pilot Schedule**: The cornerstone technique — fixing regular work to a regular schedule removes the need to constantly decide when (or if) to do it, substantially reducing stress.
- **Fixed-Schedule Productivity**: Start with the workload you want, then ruthlessly cut, reduce, and cancel until your schedule matches it. Newport credits this with letting him keep a 9-to-5, weekday-only schedule during an intense graduate program.
- **Time Arbitrage**: Not all time is equal — rearrange hours so demanding tasks get the time slots best suited to them, reducing hours worked without reducing output.
- **Pseudo-Work Does Not Equal Work**: Hours alone mean nothing; intensity of focus matters. Long, fatigue-saturated study marathons without managed energy accomplish little.
- **Don't Use a Daily To-Do List**: To-do lists alone are weak time-management tools; time-blocking is presented as the more reliable way to plan a day.
- **Apply the Weakest Link Theory to Time Management**: Highlights how difficult it is to restart a system after falling off it, an often-overlooked failure point.
