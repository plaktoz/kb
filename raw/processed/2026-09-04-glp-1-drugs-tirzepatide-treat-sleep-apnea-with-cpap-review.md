# GLP-1 Drugs Like Zepbound Effectively Treat Sleep Apnea Alongside CPAP

A review published in *JAMA Otolaryngology* found that GLP-1 receptor agonist medications may significantly improve obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) linked to obesity. Tirzepatide (Zepbound/Mounjaro), which targets both GLP-1 and GIP receptors, showed the strongest results and is already FDA-approved for OSA treatment.

## Key Findings

Researchers searched databases including PubMed, Cochrane Library, and ClinicalTrials.gov for studies published between 2016 and 2025. They analyzed randomized trials, meta-analyses, and observational studies measuring polysomnographic outcomes.

The primary mechanism of benefit is weight loss — specifically fat reduction around the neck, tongue, and throat. Losing 10% of body weight correlates with roughly a 26% drop in the apnea-hypopnea index (AHI).

In the SURMOUNT-OSA phase 3 trials, tirzepatide reduced AHI by 20–24 events per hour, with 42–50% of participants reaching full remission. Notably, improvements appeared within four weeks — before maximum weight loss occurred — suggesting possible benefits beyond fat reduction alone, potentially including effects on respiratory neuromuscular control and inflammation.

Despite these results, CPAP remained the superior treatment.

## Expert Perspectives

Sleep medicine specialist Ruchir P. Patel, MD, cautioned that GLP-1s address only one OSA risk factor. "There are other factors involved in the development of OSA, including just structural anatomy unrelated to fat deposits," he noted. Drawing on bariatric surgery data, he emphasized that substantial weight loss does not always resolve OSA entirely.

Weight loss surgeon James J. Chao, MD, advised patients to continue CPAP during GLP-1 therapy and undergo a repeat sleep study after 12 months to assess progress. He also flagged cost as a barrier, with treatment running approximately $1,000 monthly, though insurance coverage may apply for qualifying conditions.

## Bottom Line

GLP-1 therapies represent a useful complement — not a replacement — for existing OSA treatments. Combining them with approaches like hypoglossal nerve stimulation may further improve outcomes. Patients should consult their physician before adjusting any current sleep apnea treatment plan.
