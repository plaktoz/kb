# Financial stocks are crushing it. These charts show why the 'breakout' rally may have just begun.

The financial sector has rallied as strong earnings, fading concerns about private credit, and improving capital-markets activity draw investors rotating out of previous highfliers like chip stocks and AI hyperscalers. The State Street Financial Select Sector SPDR ETF (XLF) is up 4% in 2026 through the article's date (versus the S&P 500's 9.4% gain), but gained 6.2% in July alone — its best month since January 2025 — and hit a record closing high on July 28. "The move higher that we've seen this month is well justified," said Edward Jones investment-strategy analyst Brock Weimer, citing strong earnings across the board and fading private-credit and AI-competition concerns.

Financials are the ninth-cheapest of the 11 S&P 500 sectors on forward P/E, according to FactSet, and saw the largest year-over-year revenue increase in July of any sector. State Street Investment Management's Michael Arone said the sector still trades at more than a 30% discount to the broader market despite the rally. The financial sector broke out of a 15-month downtrend relative to the S&P 500, and the bank subsector (State Street SPDR S&P Bank ETF, KBE) rallied 9.7% over two months to a record high — easing fears that prior bank underperformance signaled broader market trouble. XLF has also broken its long downtrend against the PHLX Semiconductor Index (SOX), suggesting a rotation from chips into value sectors like financials may continue.

Also noted: the week's economic calendar includes a manufacturing PMI reading (expected to show a seventh straight month of expansion) and Friday's July jobs report; Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said "job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has changed little." Earnings due include AMD, SpaceX's first public report since its June IPO, and McDonald's.
