# Startup ARR is less secure than ever, new research shows

AI has fundamentally disrupted enterprise IT purchasing behavior. IDC projects ~$4.25 trillion in enterprise tech spending for 2026, nearly all AI-driven.

Madrona VC research (150 enterprise IT professionals surveyed) found 74% plan to expand AI budgets in the next 12 months, yet fewer than half of AI pilots reach full production — an improvement over MIT's prior finding that roughly 95% of enterprise AI projects failed on ROI.

The critical finding: 77% of enterprises reevaluate AI vendors every six months or on a rolling basis. Madrona describes this as a "fast in, fast out" dynamic contrasting sharply with traditional SaaS, where multi-year contracts created switching-cost moats.

This undermines the revenue security that powered AI startups' explosive ARR growth — including the trend of reaching $10M ARR in three months.

Andreessen Horowitz research (50 technical AI buyers) found a majority prefer pricing tied to outcomes — reports processed, tickets closed, leads generated — rather than usage metrics like tokens. A16z argues pricing "around the recognizable work" makes AI economically defensible for both parties.

Enterprise contracts no longer guarantee long-term revenue. The AI era has lowered switching costs and accelerated vendor re-evaluation cycles, leaving startup ARR more fragile than ever.
