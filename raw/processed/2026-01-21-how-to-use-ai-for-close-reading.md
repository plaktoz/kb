# How To Use AI for the Ancient Art of Close Reading

source_url: https://www.fast.ai/posts/2026-01-21-reading-LLMs/index.html

---

Author: Rachel Thomas
Published: January 21, 2026

Close reading — careful analysis of a text's language, structure, and meaning — is an ancient practice now finding new utility through LLMs. Rachel Thomas describes experiments from the fast.ai course How to Solve It With Code, where participants used LLMs as interactive reading companions.

Jeremy Howard read Eric Ries's book Incorruptible using the SolveIt platform, asking clarifying questions, pursuing tangents, and personalizing content to his own context. One rabbit hole revealed that four of Jack Welch's thirteen mentees served as Boeing CEOs during its safety scandals. He called it one of his "absolute best reading experiences."

A second example involved researcher Johno Whitaker working through a dense academic paper from Yann LeCun, exploring math, code, and building visual intuitions along the way.

The general workflow involves converting PDFs to Markdown, generating chapter summaries for LLM context, asking questions while reading, and optionally creating Anki flashcards via the fastanki library.

Challenges remain: setup requires coding ability, and the tooling is still rough. Hallucination risks were minimized through grounding and web search integration. Johno compared the preparation to an architect sharpening pencils — a small upfront investment that transforms the experience.

Key themes: close reading, LLMs, knowledge retention, active reading, Anki, fast.ai, SolveIt platform.
