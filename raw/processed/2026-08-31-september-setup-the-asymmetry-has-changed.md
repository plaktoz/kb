# September Setup: The Asymmetry Has Changed

We remain constructive on US equities. But the setup that carried the market through August is changing, and the near-term asymmetry has shifted.

Since the March 30 low, the S&P 500 has rallied approximately 22%, adding roughly $12 trillion in market capitalization in just five months. **September presents a different setup.**

## 1. The tailwinds are fading

**Earnings are in the rearview.** With 93% of the S&P 500 by weight having reported, 88% beat EPS estimates by a median of 7%. Q2 S&P 500 EPS growth tracked around 33% — the strongest pace outside post-recession recoveries, while estimates followed the steepest upward revision path since at least 2000.

**Retail demand slows seasonally.** September has historically been the weakest month of the year for retail demand, recording both the lowest proportion of annual retail net notional and the lowest directional skew. Retail buying on S&P 500 down days in September has been roughly half the all-month average — the lowest of any month.

**The corporate buyback bid fades.** More than $1.1 trillion of announced buyback authorizations moved back into an open window through August. That support fades as companies enter pre-earnings blackout periods — the blackout window accelerates around September 12.

## 2. Protection is cheap

SPX 1-month 25-delta put skew is trading near its flattest level of the past year, ranking in the 1st percentile. On Friday, 1-month 25-delta downside protection in SPX fell to its cheapest level since December 2024, while the VIX closed at 14.4.

Leveraged ETF AUM fell nearly $70 billion (31%) from the June peak, including declines of 38% across Technology and 50% across Semiconductors. Average 1-month ATM implied volatility across the 15 largest SOX constituents collapsed from 77.2 to 46.0 in just 20 sessions — already below where the run-up began.

## 3. Exposure has rebuilt, equities face competition

CTA, Vol-Control, and Risk-Parity strategies have all rebuilt exposure from the July lows. The market no longer has the same reservoir of unused systematic buying capacity.

Top 100 US pension plans are approximately 112% funded — their highest funding levels since 2001 — incentivizing de-gliding and equity selling into bonds at quarter-end.

## 4. The calendar turns back to macro

NFP September 4, PPI September 10, CPI September 11, FOMC September 16. Unlike earnings, the macro calendar presents a two-sided catalyst set.

**September is the only month in which the S&P 500 has finished lower more often than higher** (since 1928): lower 55% of years, average monthly return of -1.1%, average selloff of -4.7%.

The September 18 options expiration is shaping up to surpass the June record $7.7 trillion triple-witching. Approximately $6.2 trillion expires on that date — 23% of total US options exposure — creating another potential technical reset as supportive long gamma dealer positioning fades.

## GMI Bottom Line

None of these factors changes the longer-term equity story. But collectively, they change the near-term asymmetry. The upside catalysts are becoming less obvious just as the downside catalysts are becoming more numerous.

**View September as a tactical downside window, not the beginning of a broader bearish turn.** Use strength to reduce some exposure and add inexpensive protection. A potential entry point at a more constructive setup may emerge beginning around mid-October.
