# How AI Is Changing the Way Employees Spend Time at Work

## Story Highlights

- Frequent workplace AI use rose to 32% of U.S. employees in Q3 2026
- 52% of frequent users spend less time gathering information since using AI
- 29% of employees in AI-adopting organizations say AI raised expectations

Nearly nine in 10 frequent workplace AI users (88%) say AI has changed how they spend their time. Across 12 work activities, frequent users most often report spending less time gathering information and writing. Time spent collaborating with coworkers and working with customers or clients mostly stays the same.

While some organizations have begun to react to how AI is changing work, employee performance expectations largely remain the same. In Gallup's Aug. 5-19, 2026, survey of 21,648 U.S. employees, 29% of those in AI-adopting organizations agree that AI has raised expectations for daily output, and few feel pressure to use AI to keep up with expectations.

## AI Use at Work Continues to Edge Higher

AI use at work continues to rise gradually throughout 2026. In the third quarter, 53% of U.S. employees say they use AI in their role at least a few times a year. That includes 32% of employees who use it frequently, meaning a few times a week or more, a two-percentage-point increase from the previous quarter. Half of frequent users use AI daily, which equals 16% of all U.S. employees.

Employees in remote-capable roles, generally desk- or computer-based jobs that workers could do remotely regardless of where they actually work, are among the most frequent AI users. Nearly half (49%) use AI at least a few times a week, including 26% who use it daily. By comparison, 22% of employees in jobs that require on-site work use AI frequently, and 10% use it daily.

Leaders use AI more often than individual contributors do. Among remote-capable workers, leaders are 11 points more likely to use AI frequently (57% versus 46%) and are seven points more likely to use it daily (32% versus 25%).

Leaders are also more likely to hold remote-capable jobs: 64% do, compared with 46% of individual contributors. These differences suggest that employees in the same organization can have fundamentally different experiences with AI, depending on their type of work and their role level.

## Frequent AI Users Spend Less Time on Some Tasks, but Most Tasks Stay the Same

As AI use becomes more common at work, frequent users, those who use it daily or a few times a week, report changes in how they spend their time. Since they began using AI, 52% spend less time searching for or gathering information, and 47% spend less time writing or drafting.

Frequent AI users also spend less time on other information-based and routine tasks. Nearly four in 10 (38%) spend less time analyzing data, and 29% say the same about manual or administrative tasks. Still, for most of the work activities Gallup measured, spending about the same amount of time is the most common response, and no more than about one in five report spending more time on any of them.

Activities involving other people stay particularly stable. More than eight in 10 frequent users (82%) spend about the same time working with customers or clients, and 78% say the same about collaborating with coworkers. Similarly, 83% report no change in time spent attending meetings.

Some activities move in both directions. About one in five frequent users spend more time reviewing and correcting work (21%) or learning new skills and tools (21%), while similar shares spend less time on these activities, at 23% and 18%, respectively.

Even as AI changes how employees spend their time, most AI users say their work requires about the same mental effort as before. Among frequent users, nearly two-thirds (65%) report no change in mental effort since they began using AI, while 21% report less effort and 14% more.

Among infrequent users, who use AI a few times a month or a few times a year, 77% report no change in their mental effort, while 13% report less effort and 10% more.

## Most Don't Say AI Has Increased Expectations for Daily Output

Even in organizations that have adopted AI, fewer than one in three employees (29%) agree that AI has raised expectations for how much work they can complete in a day.

Expectations rise with frequency of use. Across all employees, regardless of whether their organization has adopted AI, more than one-third of frequent users (36%) agree, compared with 16% of infrequent users.

Pressure to use AI follows a similar pattern. Among frequent users, 33% agree that they feel pressure to use AI to keep up with expectations, compared with 17% of infrequent users who say the same.

## Implications

AI is beginning to change how employees divide their time across their jobs. Frequent users report spending less time gathering information and writing. Similar shares report spending more and less time reviewing work and acquiring new skills, and time spent collaborating with coworkers and interacting with customers or clients stays about the same.

As work activities shift, the skills employees rely on may shift too, with more emphasis on reviewing AI-assisted work, applying information in context and working well with others. Understanding these changes has implications beyond AI training, including the skills organizations prioritize in hiring and employee development and how responsibilities are divided across roles.

Evidence remains limited that AI is changing expectations for how much employees should accomplish. Even in organizations that have adopted AI, fewer than one in three employees agree that AI has raised expectations for how much work they can complete in a day. How organizations turn greater AI use into employee performance expectations is still taking shape.
