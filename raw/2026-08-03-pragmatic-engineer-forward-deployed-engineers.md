# What are Forward Deployed Engineers, and why are they so in demand?

**Author:** Gergely Orosz
**Date:** August 12, 2025
**Source:** The Pragmatic Engineer

## Overview

The Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role has emerged as one of tech's most sought-after positions in 2025, particularly within AI startups. Described by a16z as "the hottest job in tech," FDEs blend software engineering, sales support, and platform work — helping customers integrate complex products while also feeding insights back into core product development.

---

## 1. What Is a Forward Deployed Engineer?

FDEs are software engineers who alternate between embedding with customer teams and contributing to their employer's core product. Key characteristics include:

- **Strong engineering fundamentals** — most roles require real-world shipping experience
- **Sales collaboration** — FDEs help close deals by demonstrating that a product can be successfully integrated
- **Customer embedding** — traveling onsite to understand and solve customer problems directly
- **Product contribution** — feeding field learnings back into the core platform

Travel expectations vary: Palantir targets ~25% onsite time; healthcare AI firm Commure estimates up to 50%. Some environments are unusual — factory floors, airgapped networks, and farms have all been FDE workplaces.

Palantir's job description captures the scope well: "FDEs responsibilities look similar to those of a startup CTO: you'll work in small teams and own end-to-end execution of high-stakes projects."

---

## 2. Origins: Palantir's "Delta" Role

Palantir created the "Forward Deployed Software Engineer" role — internally called "Delta" — in the early 2010s. Remarkably, until around 2016, Palantir had *more* FDEs than traditional software engineers. When Palantir Foundry launched that year, many FDEs transitioned back to core product work, bringing deep field experience with them.

Palantir distinguishes the two roles simply: a Dev's focus is "one capability, many customers," while a Delta's is "one customer, many capabilities."

FDEs differ from consultants in that they work with customers long-term rather than making one-off recommendations, and they actively contribute to the product itself.

### Typical Weekly Breakdown at Palantir
1. Customer project work (primary focus)
2. Improving Palantir's platform based on field needs
3. Internal communications, standups, shared projects

## 3. The FDE Role at OpenAI

Colin Jarvis, Head of Forward Deployed Engineering at OpenAI, built the team from two people at the start of 2025 to over 10 members across eight cities on three continents (New York, San Francisco, Dublin, London, Munich, Paris, Tokyo, Singapore).

OpenAI distinguishes FDEs from Solutions Architects (SAs):
- **SAs** are advisory; they rarely write production code and typically work with anonymized/offline data
- **FDEs** write code directly on customer infrastructure, work with greater ambiguity, and align with OpenAI's research objectives

### Customer Project Phases at OpenAI

**Phase 1 — Scoping:** A few days onsite to map processes, identify high-value areas, and prototype with synthetic data.

**Phase 2 — Validation:** Confirming the scoped solution is truly the most valuable option, then building evals, labeling data, and hill-climbing on model performance.

Validation happens before delivery because, as Jarvis explains, "what the customer describes in scoping doesn't match the data/system reality on the ground."

**Phase 3 — Delivery:** Onsite multiple days per week — gathering data, building solutions, demoing, and iterating toward the smallest complete end-to-end solution.

### Real-World Example: John Deere
OpenAI FDEs traveled to Iowa to work with John Deere on scaling personalized farmer outreach. Previously, staff manually called farmers with equipment tips. The FDE-built solution delivered automated, personalized insights for optimizing weed-control technology — reducing pesticide use and costs — delivered in time for the planting season.

### Internal Knowledge Sharing
- Bi-weekly sessions with the Research team
- Fortnightly readouts with the Head of Product
- An internal "FDE Field Notes" Slack channel
- Quarterly in-person bootcamps for the globally distributed team

The FDE and Solutions Architecture teams also contribute to OpenAI's **Agents SDK**, which evolved from the earlier Swarm framework built to simplify agent network handoffs to customers.

## Key Themes

- **Why now?** Integrating LLMs and AI products is a natural fit for the FDE model — the technology is complex, customer needs are highly variable, and early deployments benefit enormously from embedded engineering expertise
- **Palantir's influence** remains central; no other company has done more to define and scale this role
- **Related roles** — Solutions Engineers, Agent Engineers, Sales Engineers — overlap with FDEs, but FDEs are generally expected to contribute more substantively to the core product
