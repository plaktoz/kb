# Humans could survive up to 194 years without major diseases, study suggests

source_url: https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/humans-could-survive-194-years-without-major-diseases-study-longevity-aging

---

A study published in NPJ Aging used mathematical modeling to estimate human lifespan limits when aging is driven solely by somatic mutations — genetic changes in non-reproductive cells that accumulate throughout life.

Key findings:
- If all aging hallmarks (including somatic mutations) were eliminated, median lifespan could reach ~1,759 years
- With only somatic mutations retained, median lifespan would fall to 146–194 years — roughly double today's ~79 years
- Heart muscle cells and neurons are identified as "critical lifespan bottlenecks" because they don't regenerate after mutation-driven damage
- Liver cells, by contrast, showed resilience potentially lasting 100,000+ years

Outside experts noted the study is purely theoretical, built on simplified assumptions and estimated mutation rates, and doesn't capture how aging hallmarks interact.

Practical longevity advice from experts remains unchanged: avoid smoking, exercise, eat whole foods, sleep well, maintain social ties, and prevent disease — potentially adding 10–20 healthy years.
