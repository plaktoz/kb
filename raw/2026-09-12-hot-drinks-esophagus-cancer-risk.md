# Love Your Coffee Piping Hot? It May Raise Your Esophageal Cancer Risk

**Author:** Nancy Schimelpfening, MS
**Date:** September 11, 2026
**Fact-checked by:** Jill Seladi-Schulman, Ph.D.
**Source:** Healthline
**Source URL:** https://www.healthline.com/health-news/very-hot-drinks-may-damage-esophagus-raise-cancer-risk

---

## Key Takeaways

- Very hot beverages may triple the risk of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (SCC)
- Drinking 6+ hot beverages daily raises SCC risk by ~71%
- Letting drinks cool before sipping may significantly reduce risk
- Smoking and heavy alcohol use remain the most critical risk factors overall

---

## Study Overview

A large UK study of nearly 1 million adults examined the link between hot beverage consumption and esophageal cancer. Participants from the Million Women Study and UK Biobank (ages 37–73) reported preferred drink temperatures and daily consumption, then were tracked for over a decade.

Two cancer types were analyzed: squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) and adenocarcinoma (AC). Over the follow-up period, 2,348 new esophageal cancer cases emerged — 1,045 SCC and 1,303 AC.

---

## Findings

- **"Very hot" drinkers** had **3x the SCC risk** compared to "warm" drinkers (pooled relative risk: 3.17)
- **"Hot" drinkers** had nearly **double** the SCC risk of warm drinkers
- Drink temperature showed **no significant effect** on adenocarcinoma risk
- Drinking **6+ cups daily** correlated with **71% higher SCC risk**
- Researchers estimated ~14% of UK SCC cases — roughly **440 per year** — could be prevented by lowering drink temperature

---

## Expert Commentary

Radiation oncologist Dr. Sridhar Papaiah Susheela explained that unlike the mouth, the esophagus has fewer pain receptors, so tissue damage from hot liquids often goes unnoticed. Repeated heat exposure triggers chronic inflammation, which can promote cancer development.

The WHO's IARC classifies beverages above **65°C (149°F)** as "probably carcinogenic." A practical tip: allow hot drinks to cool **3–5 minutes** before drinking.

> "If the beverage is hot enough to numb or burn the tip of your tongue, it is actively damaging your esophagus."

Nutrition researcher Sylvia Crowder, PhD (Moffitt Cancer Center) agreed but noted that **tobacco use, heavy alcohol, obesity, and chronic acid reflux** likely matter more for most individuals.
