# Here are the 3 big things we're watching in the stock market in the week ahead

source_url: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/26/here-are-the-3-big-things-were-watching-in-the-stock-market-in-the-week-ahead.html

---

By Kevin Stankiewicz — July 26, 2026

Amazon, Meta Platforms, Microsoft, and Apple headline the busiest week of this summer's earnings season, with the Federal Reserve's July policy meeting adding intrigue following rekindled Middle East tensions.

## 1. Earnings

Boeing kicks off the week Tuesday morning; the biggest metric to watch is free cash flow, both for the quarter and full-year guidance (currently $1B–$3B), plus any update to 2026 delivery targets (currently 500 planes). Corning, also reporting Tuesday, needs to reaffirm its role as a data-center infrastructure beneficiary and update its shift from copper wiring to fiber optics. Procter & Gamble (Wednesday) faces softening U.S. consumer trends and oil-driven input costs. Starbucks (Wednesday night) needs confirmation that CEO Brian Niccol's turnaround is sustaining, measured by same-store sales.

Meta (Wednesday night) will be watched for AI capex guidance and plans for a cloud business to rent excess compute; the stock is 11% below its pre-April earnings level, lowering the bar. Microsoft (Wednesday night) needs to justify its capex given Azure growth, Copilot seat growth, and its OpenAI partnership; investors also want CEO Satya Nadella's take on cheaper, open-weight Chinese models. Amazon (Thursday night) will be judged on AWS growth relative to Azure and Google Cloud. Apple (Thursday night) should see limited surprises given the iPhone 17 cycle's tail end, but its Services business and reaction to Mac/iPad price hikes from rising memory costs will get scrutiny — this is also Tim Cook's last call as CEO before transitioning to executive chairman in September.

Linde (Friday) will be watched for North American manufacturing volumes and semiconductor/electronics demand; Eaton (Friday) stands to benefit from AI-driven data-center buildout following its Boyd Thermal liquid-cooling acquisition.

## 2. Two-day Fed meeting

The Fed issues its rate decision Wednesday afternoon, followed by Chairman Kevin Warsh's press conference. Resurgent oil prices have added pressure on inflation expectations; markets are pricing in a one-third chance of a quarter-point hike this meeting and a 95% chance of a hike by September. A live debate: whether the AI capex boom is causing a generalized rise in prices — Fed Governor Lisa Cook flagged this as a risk in a mid-July speech, while Warsh has said he believes AI will be deflationary over the long run.

## 3. Economic data

The most influential report is Thursday's PCE price index for June, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge — economists expect it to decline 0.07% month-over-month and rise 3.7% year-over-year, with core PCE up 0.2% month-over-month and 3.3% annually. Bank of America argues underlying inflation trends remain above the Fed's 2% target even excluding temporary factors. Thursday also brings the first reading of second-quarter U.S. GDP.

## Week ahead highlights

Monday: durable goods orders. Tuesday: Corning, Boeing, PayPal, Coca-Cola, UPS report; Visa, Ford, Teradyne after the bell. Wednesday: Fed decision at 2 p.m. ET, Warsh press conference 2:30 p.m. ET; Starbucks, Microsoft, Meta, Procter & Gamble, Qualcomm report after the bell. Thursday: jobless claims, PCE index, Q2 GDP preliminary reading; Apple, Amazon, Roblox, Reddit report after the bell. Friday: Linde, Eaton, Exxon Mobil, Chevron, AbbVie report before the bell.
