# Pancreatic Cancer Drug May Also Help Fight Treatment-Resistant Lung Cancer

**Author:** Erika Watts
**Date:** September 11, 2026
**Fact-checked by:** Jill Seladi-Schulman, Ph.D.
**Source:** Medical News Today
**Source URL:** https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/pancreatic-cancer-drug-may-also-help-fight-treatment-resistant-lung-cancer

---

## Key Findings

- Daraxonrasib (Rasonque), FDA-approved for metastatic pancreatic cancer, is being studied for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)
- In a phase 1-2 trial, tumors shrank in over 30% of participants
- A smaller subgroup saw a 42% tumor shrinkage rate
- A phase 3 randomized trial is planned

---

## Background

NSCLC accounts for ~85% of lung cancer cases, and roughly 30% of NSCLC patients carry *RAS* mutations. These mutations leave cell-growth signals permanently "on," driving cancer progression. RAS proteins have historically been difficult to target therapeutically.

Daraxonrasib is a RAS(ON) multiselective inhibitor — unlike drugs targeting a single inactive RAS form, it blocks active RAS across multiple proteins and mutations.

---

## Trial Details

Researchers enrolled adults with previously treated, advanced *RAS*-mutant NSCLC. Doses ranged from 10–400 mg daily; the main analysis covered 136 participants receiving ≤300 mg.

**Tumor response rates by dose:**
- ≤120 mg: 31%
- 160–220 mg: 34%
- 300 mg: 37%

In an exploratory subgroup (n=38, receiving 160–220 mg, no prior docetaxel):
- Tumor shrinkage: **42%**
- Disease control rate: **89%**
- Median response duration: **11.5 months**
- Median progression-free survival: **8.3 months**
- Median overall survival: **16 months**

---

## Safety

Nearly all participants experienced at least one adverse event. Over half had grade 3+ events including pneumonia, diarrhea, rash, and anemia. Common side effects included rash, mouth sores, nausea, and vomiting, often managed through dose adjustments.

---

## Expert Commentary

Oncologist Nilesh Vora, MD, called the results encouraging, noting they "could potentially give us another tool to improve both the quality and length of life" for cancer patients, while emphasizing the need for head-to-head phase 3 comparison.

Clinical pharmacist Sonia Thomas, PharmD, explained that rather than targeting one specific mutation, daraxonrasib may "interfere with several different forms of RAS-driven cancer" — potentially expanding targeted therapy access to patients with currently untreatable RAS variants.
