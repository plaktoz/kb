# International Business Machines (IBM), What Is Driving Fresh Attention Today?

IBM has completed a CAD 997.11 million fixed income offering of 4.750% notes due August 15, 2034, giving investors fresh insight into the company's funding costs and capital structure. The bond issuance comes as IBM shares trade at US$236.31, with the 7-day share price return up 4.42% but the 30-day return down 17.82%. The 5-year total shareholder return stands at 108.63%.

The most-followed valuation narrative on IBM anchors fair value at $256.08 versus the $236.31 close — about 7.7% undervalued. That narrative frames IBM as a defensive growth technology investment transitioning from a traditional IT services provider into a software- and AI-led hybrid cloud enterprise platform company, with moderate topline growth offset by improving software mix, strong margins, and durable cash flows — a way to gain AI exposure with lower volatility than pure-play SaaS peers. The narrative applies a 9.55% discount rate and treats IBM more like a compounder than a legacy slow-grower. The thesis would be tested if AI/hybrid cloud adoption slows or higher-margin software growth falls short.

A market-multiples view offers a different picture: IBM's current P/E of 20.8x sits above the US IT industry average (18.7x) and above peer average (13.8x), though still below what would be considered a "fair" ratio of 35.3x for its business mix — meaning the market has priced in a fair amount of quality already, with room to move either toward the fair ratio (if sentiment improves) or down toward peers (if expectations cool).
