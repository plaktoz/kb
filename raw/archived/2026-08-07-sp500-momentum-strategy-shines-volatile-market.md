# S&P 500 Momentum Strategy Shines in Volatile Market, Up 22.95% Year-to-Date

■KIWOOM US S&P500 Momentum ETF Selects 100 Top-Performing S&P 500 Stocks More Than 10 Percentage Points of Excess Return Over Index

Finance|
|

By Yoon Min-hyuk

||

![Traders monitor market activity at the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) in Manhattan on Nov. 4 (local time). Reuters-Yonhap News - Seoul Economic Daily Finance News from South Korea](https://wimg.sedaily.com/news/cms/2026/08/07/news-p.v1.20260807.8bb347efd6dc468e90cf4c8789e2c363_P1.jpg "S&P 500 Momentum Strategy Shines in Volatile Market, Up 22.95% Year-to-Date")

Traders monitor market activity at the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) in Manhattan on Nov. 4 (local time). Reuters-Yonhap News

As hopes and concerns over artificial intelligence (AI) and semiconductors have diverged in domestic and overseas stock markets recently, increasing volatility among individual stocks, the "momentum strategy" of concentrating investments in leading stocks on an upward trend is shining. An exchange-traded fund (ETF) that incorporates a large number of top-performing stocks within the Standard & Poor's (S&P) 500 according to set rules is recording solid returns, outperforming the Nasdaq 100.

According to Koscom ETF CHECK on the 7th, the "KIWOOM US S&P500 Momentum" ETF recorded a return of 22.95% year-to-date as of the previous day. This figure is more than 10 percentage points ahead of the S&P 500 index (12.42%) over the same period, and 6.42 percentage points higher than the Nasdaq 100 index (16.53%), a leading growth stock index. The product has also maintained an upward trend in period-based returns, with 6.51% over the past three months and 25.21% over six months.

This ETF tracks the same "S&P 500 Momentum Index" as the "Invesco S&P 500 Momentum ETF (SPMO)," which is popular in the U.S. market, earning it the nickname "Korea's SPMO." The method involves compressing and incorporating only the top 100 stocks with superior returns relative to volatility over the past year among S&P 500 constituents. It is designed to pursue aggressive excess returns compared to ordinary S&P 500 ETFs, which diversify investments across all 500 stocks.

Regular rebalancing (portfolio reconstruction) in step with market changes is also a key factor driving performance. The product conducts rebalancing twice a year, in March and September. During the March rebalancing, the turnover rate on a stock basis reached 54.5%. The weighting of the information technology (IT) sector was raised from 33.2% to 44.4%, while the weighting of the financial sector, affected by interest rate volatility, was sharply reduced from 19.5% to 6.3%. Micron, buoyed by expectations of a recovery in the memory chip industry, was newly incorporated as the top holding, while Meta, whose valuation burden has grown, and financial stocks (JPMorgan and Visa) were reduced or removed.

The financial investment industry is paying attention to the regular rebalancing scheduled for September. Lee Kyung-jun, head of the ETF management division at Kiwoom Asset Management, said, "The momentum strategy is a smart beta strategy whose long-term excess returns have been proven both theoretically and empirically." He added, "In the AI era, as winners keep changing, we can continuously select and invest in new winning companies, making it an index investment method optimized for the AI era."
