# Hybrid Work and Productivity: What the Data Actually Show

source_url: https://www.gallup.com/workplace/713093/hybrid-work-productivity.aspx

---

By Gallup, Workplace

The debate over hybrid work has generated strong opinions on both sides. Gallup's research cuts through the noise with a consistent finding: Work location is not the primary driver of productivity. Management quality is.

## Where the U.S. Hybrid Workforce Stands Today

Among remote-capable U.S. employees — roughly half of the total workforce — hybrid work is the dominant arrangement. As of Q2 2026, 52% of remote-capable workers are hybrid, 26% are fully remote, and 22% are fully on-site. These figures have held relatively stable since 2022.

Hybrid workers currently spend 46% of their workweek on-site (about 2.3 days), up from 42% in 2022, with little movement since. Hybrid schedules are now divided roughly equally: 34% self-determined, 35% set by manager/team, 31% set by employer/leadership.

Two notable exceptions: the federal government, where hybrid work collapsed from 61% to 28% after the Trump administration ended remote work for most federal employees in 2025, and the technology sector, which has trended more on-site than other industries.

## What Gallup's Research Shows About Hybrid Work Productivity

**KEY INSIGHT:** Hybrid work, when managed well, does not hurt employee productivity. Manager effectiveness — not work location — is the primary determinant of whether hybrid teams achieve strong team performance.

Gallup's research among 8,090 remote-capable employees finds the top advantages of hybrid work are improved work-life balance, more efficient use of time, control over work hours and location, burnout mitigation, and higher perceived remote productivity. Seventy-six percent of hybrid workers cite improved work-life balance as a top benefit.

Teams with a formal hybrid collaboration plan are 66% more likely to be engaged and 29% less likely to experience burnout than teams without one.

## Where Hybrid Work Creates Productivity Risk

**KEY INSIGHT:** Giving employees full autonomy over their hybrid schedule increases burnout risk. Employees with fully self-determined schedules are 76% more likely to say burnout or fatigue is their greatest challenge than those whose schedule is set by their manager or team.

Employees with fully self-determined hybrid schedules are also 57% more likely to report reduced work-life balance and 52% more likely to say they struggle to meet customer needs, compared with employees whose schedule is set by their manager or team.

Only 54% of managers who supervise remote workers strongly agree that they trust their teams to be productive when working remotely, and just 57% of employees strongly agree that they feel trusted by their manager. Nearly half of hybrid workers report that their team has never discussed a formal plan for collaborating effectively.

## Why Mandates Aren't the Answer

Blanket return-to-office mandates have not moved the needle on hybrid work patterns. Despite sustained employer pressure, hybrid workers spend the same proportion of time in the office as they did in 2023. Six in 10 exclusively remote workers say they would be extremely likely to look for a new job if remote flexibility were removed.

## What "Intentional Hybrid" Looks Like in Practice

The best hybrid teams create a team charter — a documented agreement covering the team's mission, goals, strengths and ways of working together across locations. Only 11% of employees currently work on a team that has set its hybrid policy together, the arrangement most strongly associated with fairness and positive collaboration outcomes.

Gallup identifies four reasons that justify in-office time: Connection, Collaboration, Creativity, and Culture.

Employees who strongly agree that they know what is expected of them are 47% less likely to experience frequent burnout.

## Hybrid Work as a Retention and Talent Strategy

Hybrid is the most preferred work arrangement across every generation. Only 23% of remote-capable Gen Z employees prefer fully remote work, the lowest of any generation. Fully on-site work remains the least popular option across all age groups.

## Key Stats (FAQ Summary)

- Hybrid workers average 2.3 days per week on-site; employees generally prefer two to three days.
- Gallup's four primary hybrid challenges: trust gaps, burnout risk from unstructured autonomy, declining role clarity, and feedback deficits.
- Teams with a formal hybrid collaboration plan are 66% more likely to be engaged and 29% less likely to burn out.
