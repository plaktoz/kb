# Organizational AI Adoption Jumps Six Points

source_url: https://www.gallup.com/workplace/712736/organizational-adoption-jumps-six-points.aspx

---

Author: Andy Kemp, Gallup
Published: 2026 Q2

Reported organizational adoption of AI grew sharply in Q2 2026. Forty-seven percent of U.S. employees now say their organization has integrated AI tools to improve productivity, efficiency or quality, up from 41% in the last quarter. One in five employees remain unsure as to whether their organization has integrated AI tools.

Individual AI use at work has also risen steadily. More than half of U.S. workers (52%) now use AI in their role, with 30% using it frequently (a few times a week or more). Fifteen percent use it daily.

## Most Common Uses of AI at Work

Among AI users, the most common uses of AI are:
- Writing and editing (51%)
- Search or research (49%)
- General assistance or problem-solving (39%)

More technical applications are reported less often: coding assistance and automation (each 16%), data science/analytics (18%), and presentation creation (17%).

## Productivity Gains Highest for Task-Specific Uses

The AI applications used most often are not the ones most strongly associated with productivity improvements. The highest productivity ratings come from:
- Coding assistance (77% report extremely/somewhat positive productivity impact)
- Automation or process automation (77%)
- Presentation/slide deck creation (76%)
- Data science or analytics (75%)

Compared to writing (68%) and research (65%) which are used more frequently but generate lower productivity gains.

## Greater Variety of AI Use Linked to More Productivity

Employees who use AI for more purposes report far higher productivity gains:
- 1–2 purposes: 45% report positive impact
- 3–4 purposes: 66%
- 5–6 purposes: 78%
- 7+ purposes: 90%

## Implications

Organizations may get more out of AI when employees are supported in applying it across a wider range of job-relevant tasks, rather than treating it only as a general-purpose writing or search tool. The data suggests that breadth of AI adoption, not just access, drives measurable value.
