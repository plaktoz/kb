# S&P Gains as Earnings Strength Offsets Fed Uncertainty

The S&P 500 finished the week of July 27–31, 2026 higher, in choppy trading, as investors digested the Fed's decision to hold rates steady, strong earnings, and AI capex projections from hyperscalers. The Dow added 1.0%, the S&P 500 gained 1.1%, and the Nasdaq finished up 1.6% for the week. Sector breadth was negative, with seven of 11 sectors closing lower; consumer discretionary (+8.3%) was the best performer while utilities (-4.2%) was the weakest.

Through July 31, 305 S&P 500 companies had released Q2 results, with over 86% beating expectations; aggregate earnings for reporting companies were up 57% year over year, well ahead of the 24% growth projected at the end of June. Full-year expectations call for S&P 500 earnings to grow nearly 27% this year and almost 17% in 2027.

The FOMC left rates unchanged in a fractured 9-3 vote, with Fed presidents Logan, Hammack, and Kashkari dissenting in favor of a quarter-point hike. Fed Chair Warsh said policymakers "won't hesitate" to act and that "there is no soft inflation target." U.S. GDP grew an annualized 1.5% in Q2, a deceleration driven partly by a surge in imports, while consumer spending rose a stronger-than-expected 3.2%. Consumer confidence fell in July amid elevated gas and food prices.

Guggenheim's outlook: while favorable through year-end, turbulence is likely through the summer given AI buildout worries, tech valuations, and Iran-related headline risk — though the macro environment still offers a solid risk/reward profile for longer-term investors on pullbacks. The week ahead centers on the July jobs report (consensus: +85,000 payrolls, 4.2% unemployment) plus JOLTS, ADP payrolls, and ISM manufacturing/services data.
