# MSCI World ETF: Index Provider Rewrites the Rulebook for Fast-Moving Stocks

MSCI has scrapped its cooling-off period for stocks that have soared too quickly, a quiet methodological shift that could accelerate the entry of high-flying technology names into the MSCI World index and the iShares MSCI World ETF just as the fund flirts with record territory. The "Extreme Price Increase" (EPI) mechanism previously forced a mandatory waiting period on stocks with outsized gains; that brake is now lifted for companies with a free-float factor of at least 0.75, meaning only names with substantial international share turnover benefit from the fast-track admission. MSCI confirmed on August 5 that results of its August index review will publish August 12, with implementation at the close of trading August 31.

The rule change lands in an index already leaning heavily on tech: the iShares MSCI World ETF holds roughly $8.18 billion in assets across 1,284 holdings, allocates about 72.45% to US equities, and 30.27% to information technology alone. Recent earnings illustrated why the EPI rule mattered — Palantir surged around 30% after revenue jumped 93% to $1.94 billion, AMD beat expectations, and Microsoft's Azure cloud division reported annual sales exceeding $100 billion. Under the old rule, such explosive moves might have triggered a cooling-off delay to index inclusion; the relaxation suggests MSCI wants its rules to reflect market dynamics rather than restrain them.

The ETF closed around $208.63–$208.86 (sources vary slightly), down about 0.15% on the day, but up 12.31% since January and just 1.63% below its 52-week high of $212.08 reached in June. Over the past 30 days it added 3.25%, with an RSI of 64.4 — approaching but not yet in overbought territory. Microsoft's fiscal Q4 delivered $90.01 billion in revenue on 43% Azure growth; Palantir and AMD provided additional ballast offsetting softer results from other "Magnificent Seven" members.

Market participants are watching the August review for semiconductor and AI names that previously tripped the old waiting-period threshold — if technology momentum persists, the rebalancing could bring more turnover than usual, further strengthening the dominant US technology names already driving the fund's performance. The article notes this doesn't change the ETF's underlying concentration risk in a handful of sectors and a single country's equity market — it just makes the index quicker to reflect that reality.
