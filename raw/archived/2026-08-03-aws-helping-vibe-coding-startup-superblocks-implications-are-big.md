# AWS is helping vibe-coding startup Superblocks, and the implications are big

Superblocks announced a multiyear joint marketing deal with AWS, allowing its vibe-coding tool to be embedded within AWS customers' private clouds. Enterprise subscribers can offer vibe coding to business users without data leaving their environment — apps spin up Amazon Aurora databases internally and integrate with Amazon Bedrock, keeping everything under IT governance.

CEO Brad Menezes explained the core value: "data never leaves… It's their AWS account and basically secure with all of the auditing, all of the encryption, all of the network controls."

AWS doesn't yet have its own vibe-coding agent for business users — it has Kiro (developer-focused) and Quick (an AI assistant) — making Superblocks a complementary fit. The startup has 50 employees and raised $60M through its Series A, backed by Spark Capital, Kleiner Perkins, Meritech, and Greenoaks.

The broader significance: hyperscalers are pushing enterprises to decouple AI models from surrounding infrastructure, keeping orchestration and app layers on their clouds. Microsoft's Satya Nadella has similarly urged enterprises toward multi-model strategies. Menezes noted that enterprise preference has shifted dramatically, predicting "any enterprise that is betting on a single model provider, that executive will be fired."

Open models now account for roughly 29% of traffic through Vercel's AI gateway, reflecting this multi-model momentum.
