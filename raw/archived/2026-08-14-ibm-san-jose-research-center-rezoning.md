# IBM Research Center in San Jose might land updated city-crafted zoning

The future of IBM's 687-acre Almaden Research Center in South San Jose may start to take shape this month if the City Council updates zoning for the tech titan's scenic office hub, per a staff report for an Aug. 18 San Jose City Council meeting.

The entire site at 650 Harry Road currently bears a Planned Development Zoning District designation. Under the proposal, roughly 35 acres (including the research buildings) would become Industrial Park Zoning District, and an estimated 652 acres would become Open Space Zoning District. IBM still owns the entire property. The San Jose Planning Department: the Industrial Park designation is "intended for a wide variety of industrial users such as research and development, manufacturing, assembly, testing, and offices," while Open Space lands are "typically devoted to open space, parks, recreation areas, trails, habitat buffers, nature preserves."

The property became available after IBM closed the research hub and shifted workers to its Silicon Valley Lab at 555 Bailey Road near Coyote Valley. The rezoning is on the City Council's consent calendar, meaning a speedy vote with limited public debate — a point of contention. Resident Jessica Vernon wrote to city leaders that "this request is far more complicated and nuanced than it appears on the surface" and that the consent calendar is an inappropriate forum. Resident Abby Simons urged the council to remove the item from the consent calendar for open discussion.

Commercial real estate firm Cushman & Wakefield, marketing the 544,500-square-foot property, says it offers investors "the unique opportunity to reposition or redevelop a property of unique scale within Silicon Valley," suited to data centers, industrial, R&D, advanced manufacturing, or general office use. Land-use consultant Erik Schoennauer, representing a potential buyer group, called the current zoning "an antiquated custom zoning from 1980 specifically for the IBM Research Center operation" and described the rezoning as simply conforming the site to existing general-plan designations.

The rezoning itself does not authorize redevelopment or approve any project — any specific proposal would face further public scrutiny.
