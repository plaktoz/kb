# IBM Has Fallen 33% From Its High and Yields 3%. Here's What That Dividend Actually Costs the Company.

IBM trades at about $224, roughly 33% below its 52-week high of $332.46, pushing its dividend yield to about 3%. The dividend costs IBM about $6.4 billion a year ($3.2 billion paid in the first half of 2026); at $1.69 per quarter ($6.76/year), the payout consumes about 60% of trailing EPS of just over $11 — but only about 40% of the $15.7 billion of free cash flow management expects for 2026 (IBM generated $14.7 billion of free cash flow in 2025, up $2 billion year over year). IBM has raised its dividend for 31 consecutive years, though April's increase was a single penny per quarter.

The 33% drawdown reflects a repriced growth story, not the dividend. Q2 revenue rose just 1% year-over-year to $17.2 billion, and management trimmed full-year guidance to 4–5% constant-currency revenue growth, down from over 5%. Software (the growth segment) grew 5% with Red Hat up 11%, but decelerated from 11% growth the prior quarter; consulting was flat, and infrastructure revenue fell 7%, dragged by a 42% decline in the IBM Z mainframe line — likely a cyclical dip tied to product-launch timing rather than a permanent shift. At roughly 17x forward earnings, the stock is now valued like a slow-growth company, because in Q2 it was one.

CEO Arvind Krishna said IBM is in the "early innings" of a structural shift for business, and a new mainframe cycle could eventually reset the growth comparison. The dividend is covered about 2.5 times by expected 2026 free cash flow, but the author concludes he'd wait for software growth to reaccelerate before buying the drawdown: "The dividend is safe. That alone doesn't get me there."
