# MSFT Stock Gains After Microsoft Opens Its Biggest India Data Center Yet

Shares of Microsoft (MSFT) edged higher Thursday morning after the company announced its fourth cloud region in India and its largest data center deployment in the country to date, intensifying an infrastructure race with Alphabet's Google Cloud and Amazon Web Services. The new India South Central cloud region, located in Hyderabad, is part of Microsoft's broader $20.5 billion investment in India's cloud and AI ecosystem — a $3 billion commitment announced in January 2025 plus an additional $17.5 billion later that year. MSFT stock gained 1.7% in morning trade, while retail sentiment on Stocktwits dipped to "bullish" from "extremely bullish."

Microsoft said the Hyderabad buildout gives it the "largest hyperscale cloud presence in India," spanning three availability zones roughly the size of two Eden Gardens cricket stadiums combined, with early customer adoption from Adani Group, Bajaj Finserv, HDFC Bank, and PB Pay. Azure has posted strong double-digit growth in India over the past two years, with demand expected to rise further once the region is fully operational. Puneet Chandok, President of Microsoft India and South Asia, said customers are increasingly moving from AI experimentation to production-scale deployment, and that the new region is key to providing the infrastructure, security, and data sovereignty enterprise AI adoption requires.

The Hyderabad launch lands in the middle of a broader hyperscaler buildout across India: Google confirmed a $15 billion data center project in Andhra Pradesh in October 2025, while AWS pledged $8.3 billion in India in January 2026, on top of an earlier $4.4 billion commitment for its second Indian cloud region, also in Hyderabad. Microsoft now operates four Azure regions in India directly plus two more (Jio India West and Jio India Central) run with Reliance Jio, and more than 80 cloud regions globally across 34 countries.

In the June quarter, AWS generated $42.2 billion in revenue (a $169 billion annualized run rate), growing 37% year-over-year. Microsoft's Intelligent Cloud segment, including Azure, produced $39.3 billion in quarterly revenue ($157 billion annualized), with Azure itself growing 43% year-over-year. Google Cloud posted $24.8 billion in quarterly revenue ($99 billion annualized), with growth accelerating sharply to 82% year-over-year from 63% the prior quarter. Globally, AWS remains the largest cloud provider with roughly 28–31% market share per Synergy Research Group, versus about 21% for Azure and 14% for Google Cloud.
