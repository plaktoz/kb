# Summer reading for teaching and school improvement: Harnessing the Science of Learning

source_url: https://www.eschoolnews.com/educational-leadership/2026/07/24/summer-reading-science-of-learning

---

By Miriam Williams, M.Ed., 3P Learning — eSchool News, July 24, 2026

Every summer the author finds herself in conversations with teachers and administrators trying to figure out why current pedagogical practices are or aren't working — questions like "I have kids at different learning levels, how do I help them all grow?" and "how do I help my teachers succeed and reduce their load?" Nathaniel Swain's book *Harnessing the Science of Learning* (4.5/5 on Amazon) provides research-based answers, translating explicit instruction, cognitive load theory, retrieval practice, spaced repetition, knowledge-rich curriculum design, and whole-class responsive teaching into practical strategies aimed at educators specifically (versus books like *Make It Stick*, aimed at the learner's own perspective).

## Cognitive load

Working memory is very small; when students learn something new, visual clutter or anything else competing for that limited working memory impedes learning. This matters especially for young learners and students with learning differences who may already carry extra cognitive demands. Distraction-free environments and explicit, systematic instruction ("no bells and whistles") support this.

## "I do, we do, you do"

The gradual-release framework: the teacher explicitly demonstrates a concept (I do), works through it with the class with feedback and scaffolding (we do), then observes independent practice (you do). This maps onto skill-acquisition science — learners need clear demonstration and guided practice before independent practice, otherwise practice can build confidence in wrong approaches that must later be unlearned. The author notes professional development itself rarely follows this same model — PD is usually delivered as information transfer, then teachers are sent back to implement alone.

## Applying it to school and PD design

The author is now auditing her own professional-development design against three Science of Learning principles: small, manageable learning increments; modeling before practice; and connecting to existing knowledge rather than layering on new content. The framework also gives a consistent set of questions to apply to any classroom technology: does it reduce cognitive load or add to it? Does it support explicit instruction or substitute passive exposure? Does it extend or merely duplicate what a teacher can do? "When implemented thoughtfully, these evidence-based practices reduce unnecessary complexity and help educators focus their energy on what moves the needle for student learning."
