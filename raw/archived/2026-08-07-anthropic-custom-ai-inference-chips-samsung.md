# Anthropic co-designing custom AI inference chips to bypass costly Nvidia GPUs — Samsung reported as manufacturing partner for Claude maker

Anthropic has announced it's building an in-house chip development team to co-design its own custom ASIC processors for handling AI inferencing workloads. As reported by Business Insider, Anthropic is hiring engineers to design the chips with an unspecified partner, working to an aggressive schedule.

Anthropic joins Google, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, and OpenAI in building custom hardware for inference. Training advanced AI models still requires Nvidia GPUs, but inferencing workloads can run on a much wider array of hardware. Custom silicon can offer up to 65% lower total cost of ownership than Nvidia GPUs and is less power-hungry.

Google has built its Tensor Processing Units for 12 years with Broadcom. Amazon has Trainium and Inferentia chips. Meta recently announced new MTIA designs for deployment through 2027. Microsoft has its Maia line, and Tesla pivoted to AI5 and AI6 chip designs after years developing Dojo.

Anthropic told Business Insider it is co-designing chips so Claude can run faster and more efficiently at the scale its customers need, citing explosive growth in the consumer space, significant government contracts, and agentic AI workloads that require far more tokens than traditional single-prompt interactions.

Anthropic hasn't revealed its chip design partner. Broadcom and Marvell represent roughly 95% of the ASIC co-design market, but The Information reported last month that Anthropic was in talks with Samsung for manufacturing. Broadcom already has a $73 billion order backlog and expects over $100 billion in annual AI chip revenue by the end of 2027; Marvell is expected to make upwards of $11 billion from co-design work in 2026. TSMC remains the biggest beneficiary overall, producing the majority of the world's cutting-edge silicon and handling advanced packaging (CoWoS) for HBM integration across nearly all these chips.

No AI company is likely to become 100% reliant on its own chips given the scale of AI demand and Nvidia's continued dominance of training workloads, but every custom inference chip installed reduces reliance on Nvidia GPUs for that specific purpose.
