# Two new compounds could reveal hidden drivers of Alzheimer's disease

Vanderbilt researchers at the Warren Center for Neuroscience Drug Discovery (WCNDD) developed the first selective inhibitor of TAOK-1, a protein linked to Alzheimer's disease that has remained poorly understood due to a lack of research tools. The work was published in ACS Chemical Neuroscience.

Alzheimer's affects more than seven million people in the United States; available treatments can slow the disease but none cure it, partly because the underlying biology is still not fully understood. Researchers use "tool compounds" — chemicals that raise or lower a protein's activity — to investigate what a poorly understood target does, even when the compounds themselves aren't viable as medicines.

Led by postdoctoral fellow Daniel Schultz and PhD student Lauren Parr, the WCNDD team built a large library of related molecules and evaluated each for its effect on TAOK-1 and its drug-like properties. This produced VU6083859, the first selective TAOK-1 inhibitor, which could serve as a starting point for future Alzheimer's drug development.

A second compound, VU6080195, produced an unexpected result: rather than inhibiting TAOK proteins, it activated all three members of the TAOK family. Schultz said the team is excited to study the neurological effects of increasing TAOK activity, since research on the family has mostly focused on inhibition. The WCNDD — a clinical-stage biotech startup within Vanderbilt with five compounds already in Phase I trials — is a founding pillar of the new Vanderbilt Institute for Therapeutic Advances. Researchers hope the two compounds will draw more attention to the understudied TAOK protein family and its links to Alzheimer's and other neurological diseases.
