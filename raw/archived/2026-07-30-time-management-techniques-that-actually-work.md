# Time management techniques that actually work

source_url: https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/time-management-techniques-that-actually

---

By Lenny Rachitsky, Lenny's Newsletter.

A reader asked for productivity tips to stay above water. Ten techniques that have stuck, used daily:

1. **Use your calendar for to-dos.** Turn a to-do into a calendar event instead of an app entry — your calendar is your personal roadmap. (From Nir Eyal's *Indistractable* and Jake Knapp/John Zeratsky's *Make Time*.) Go further: plan next week's most important to-dos into your calendar every Sunday evening.

2. **If it takes less than two minutes, just do it.** From David Allen's *Getting Things Done*: "it's more or less the point where it starts taking longer to store and track an item than to deal with it the first time it's in your hands."

3. **Keep a "waiting for" list.** Track everything you've delegated or are waiting on from someone else, alongside your to-do list, so nothing depends on memory.

4. **Each morning, write down just 1–3 things you have to accomplish that day.** Keeping the list short makes it far more likely you'll actually finish them. Inspired by Matt Mochary's Top Goal approach and the "highlight" concept from *Make Time*. Go further: "eat the frog" — do the hardest thing first.

5. **Set up, and fiercely protect, regular deep work time.** From Cal Newport's *Deep Work*: focused, uninterrupted, cognitively demanding work versus "shallow work." It takes 30–60 minutes to enter a deep-work state, so create 2–3 recurring weekly blocks and never let anyone schedule over them. Tools: kill distracting apps, try binaural beats (e.g. Brain.fm), encourage a no-meeting day, use Google Calendar's Focus Time feature.

6. **Make a rule: no meetings before [as late as possible].** The author uses no meetings before 3 p.m., protecting the freshest mental hours for generative, creative work. Even a rule of no meetings before 10 or 11 a.m. on some days can help.

7. **Keep Do Not Disturb mode on.** Built into your OS, free, and used constantly by the most productive people the author knows. For deep focus, physically remove your phone from the room.

8. **Keep work async.** Pivot meeting requests into email conversations — most become five-minute tasks instead of 30–60 minute meetings. Template: "Happy to help! Could we start async over email? What are you most looking for my input on?"

9. **Get a virtual assistant to take on low-impact work.** Using Shreyas Doshi's LNO framework (Leverage / Neutral / Overhead) to identify tasks to delegate; an EA that starts small often grows into 20–30 hours/week of high-ROI delegated work.

10. **Say no more often, even when it's hard.** Most burnout comes from taking on too much, not from a lack of productivity tools. Tactics: filter asks through your top 2–3 priorities; prioritize *and* communicate tradeoffs to your manager rather than silently absorbing work; imagine the decision without the emotional weight of disappointing someone; ask "would I be excited about this if it were tomorrow?"
