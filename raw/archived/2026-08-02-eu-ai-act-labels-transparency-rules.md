# EU AI Act Labels Start Aug 2. AI Transparency Rules Explained

Starting August 2, 2026, providers and deployers of certain AI systems must comply with the transparency obligations set out in Article 50 of the EU Artificial Intelligence Act. The rules require companies to transparently declare AI use, particularly for AI-generated content, even if those companies are based outside the EU but serve EU users.

Chatbots and other interactive AI systems must now tell users they are dealing with AI, not a human. Deepfakes — images, videos, or audio that have been edited or generated using AI — must be labelled. AI-generated or altered content must also carry machine-readable marks so it can be detected more easily.

The disclosure obligation does not apply where AI-generated text has undergone human review and editorial control, and where a natural or legal person holds editorial responsibility for the publication. Where deepfake content is evidently artistic, creative, satirical, or fictional, the transparency obligations are limited to disclosure in a manner that does not hamper enjoyment of the work.

Noncompliance can trigger fines of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. The EU AI Office has published a voluntary Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content, offering providers a recognized path to demonstrate compliance.
