# Amazon and Apple just told us more about their AI plans - here are three things we learned

source_url: https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cp87m46g392o

date: 2026-07-31

---

The world's biggest technology companies - including Microsoft, Meta, Google, Apple and Amazon - updated Wall Street this week on their finances. One common thread emerged: they are all planning to continue spending massive amounts of money on artificial intelligence (AI).

The reaction from investors was that they need to see more tangible results to show for the $1tn (£743bn) and growing investment in things like computer chips, data centres, and even technical staff. That sent some tech stocks on a wild ride in recent days.

## AI tools and chatbots still do not make much money

OpenAI's release of ChatGPT in late 2022 kicked off the ongoing AI investment race, and every major tech company has since launched a consumer-facing AI chatbot of their own. Meta has Meta AI, Google has Gemini, Amazon has Rufus, Apple even relaunched Siri.

Yet none of the chatbots or the related tools in and of themselves clearly provide a meaningful amount of revenue for the companies, despite being costly to create.

This batch of earnings results made clear that companies like Google (owned by Alphabet) and Meta are currently spending much more money related to AI tools than they bring in. Both companies reported some of their lowest ever amounts of free cash flow. Google spent so much on AI that Alphabet's free cash flow was negative on revenue of $118bn, meaning it spent more than it brought in for the first time in the company's history as a public company. Meta's free cash was just $784m on $61bn of revenue. Meta's Reality Labs, responsible for its AI work, lost nearly $9bn in the first half of this year.

## Wall Street wants results, not more ideas

Investors are no longer placated with executives' claims that AI investment will turn out to be worth it at some unknown point in the future. Shares of Meta plunged to its second lowest level in a year after CEO Mark Zuckerberg said Meta was working on its own AI agent and planning an operation to sell an AI tool directly to other firms — neither of which currently exists in a way that could make Meta money, with no timeline given. Still, Meta increased the low end of its planned AI spending, likely pouring more than $140bn into AI this year alone.

Meanwhile, shares in Microsoft soared to a six-month high. Despite plans to effectively match this financial year the $190bn it spent on AI over the last 12 months, it showed strong revenue growth and more adoption of its core AI tool. Tracy Woo, an analyst with Forrester, said Microsoft was a tech company showing that its massive AI investments were "beginning to deliver returns."

The market reaction to Amazon was almost identical. Despite negative cash flow and plans to spend $220bn on AI this year, the success of its other businesses drove its stock to its highest price in two months.

## There is still huge demand for new tech

AI tools may not have yet proven to be a consumer tech revolution on the scale of the internet or even electricity, but there is still huge demand from people for new technology. Google said last week that 950 million people are using its Gemini chatbot at least once a month, three times the users it had a year ago.

Apple said new versions of its core products — Mac, iPhone and iPad — have been selling better this year than the company planned for or expected, so much so that it warned investors sales of such products would slow because Apple is unable to get enough of the microchips required to meet buyer demand.

The company is anticipating a lot of excitement from Apple users for its impending update of Siri, its AI voice assistant getting an overhaul with the help of Google's Gemini chatbot. Outgoing chief executive Tim Cook said Apple already has plans to charge users who wish to make heavier use of the new Siri, given feedback received from user testing so far. "We're off-the-charts excited about Siri AI," Cook said. "We do believe there will be people who want to use it — a lot."
