# Expertise expert offers 8 tips for learning a new skill

If you want to master a musical instrument, sport, or software program, follow this advice from UNC School of Education's Jeffrey Greene.

The key to learning a new skill is a diligent, deliberate approach, not simply the "10,000 hours" often cited. Practice time is important, said Jeffrey A. Greene, McMichael Family Professor in the School of Education, but it's only one of several factors that help you succeed. Greene bases his advice on the deliberate practice model developed by the late cognitive psychologist K. Anders Ericsson, author of "The Making of an Expert."

## 1. Find a guide
People need explicit directions to begin. Look for a source that breaks down the steps — a book, online guide, or instructor.

## 2. Set up a distraction-free environment
"Research shows that it's a lot easier to set up a context where distractions won't happen than it is to resist distractions when they happen," Greene said. Find a place and time for focusing, and put your phone in another room.

## 3. Build endurance
Practice multiple days each week, starting with a few minutes of focused practice and building up. "Beginners usually spend only 15 to 30 minutes practicing because it's tiring," Greene said. "Even experts generally can't practice more than four or five hours a day."

## 4. Practice deliberately
Be intentional and mindful while practicing. Begin with a basic part of the skill and consciously pay attention to it. Paying attention can also improve already-automated skills: "De-automatize it. Concentrate, slow down and focus," Greene said. "That can make you better even at things you've plateaued on."

## 5. Find motivation
Both intrinsic and extrinsic motivation are valid. "It's okay to do something because you want to obtain a goal or get something of value," Greene said, "as long as you feel like you're in charge and it's your choice to do it. Where extrinsic motivation gets bad is when it's coercive or manipulative."

## 6. Get feedback
A coach or partner's outside perspective can enhance learning. Feedback should "feed forward" — asking questions like "What's the next goal? What's the next step?" Some technologies, like a coding compiler, give feedback automatically.

## 7. Get the right kind of feedback
Good feedback should make you feel: you are in control and can make good choices about how you practice; you have sufficient competence ("I know you can do it"); and the coach genuinely cares about your performance. These three things strengthen intrinsic motivation and complement extrinsic motivation.

## 8. Be your own coach
Notice your improvements and think about what brought them about — write them down and internalize the skills and confidence gained. "Most people benefit from coaching no matter how advanced they are. But you can do a lot of that work yourself." Lastly, Greene said: have fun.
