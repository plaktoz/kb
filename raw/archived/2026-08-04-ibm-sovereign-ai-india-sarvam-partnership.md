# Can IBM's Sovereign AI Solutions for India Propel the Stock Higher?

IBM has partnered with India-based AI company Sarvam to develop and test "sovereign AI" technologies for government agencies and regulated industries in India — systems that give organizations greater control over data, governance, and operations while supporting public-sector digital transformation. IBM and Sarvam will jointly pilot AI applications for citizen services, grievance redressal, document processing, and multilingual/voice-enabled administrative workflows, combining IBM's enterprise AI infrastructure (its watsonx platform) with Sarvam's locally developed AI models.

IBM's watsonx platform underpins the effort: watsonx.ai for foundation models and generative AI, watsonx.data for an open lakehouse-based data store, and watsonx.governance for responsible, transparent AI workflow oversight. IBM is also benefiting from solid hybrid cloud demand as enterprises manage increasingly complex, heterogeneous multi-cloud environments; its HashiCorp acquisition has strengthened Red Hat's cloud infrastructure management capabilities.

Despite this, IBM shares have fallen 9.7% over the past year against 182.8% industry growth, lagging Microsoft (-7.6%) and Amazon (+32.9%) over the same period. Earnings estimate revisions are trending positive, though: 2026 EPS estimates rose 3.4% to $12.34 over the past year, and 2027 estimates rose 4.3% to $13.23.

IBM faces stiff competition and pricing pressure from AWS and Microsoft Azure, eroding margins. It also faces a specific threat from Anthropic: Claude Code's ability to modernize legacy COBOL systems — a language deeply embedded in IBM's mainframe ecosystem — threatens to reduce enterprise reliance on specialized legacy service providers like IBM by automating code exploration, documentation, refactoring, and security analysis. Zacks currently rates IBM a Hold (Zacks Rank #3), suggesting new investors trade with caution despite the positive estimate-revision trend.
