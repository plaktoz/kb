# Inside the UAE's Push Towards a Healthier Food System

source_url: https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/07/27/inside-the-uaes-push-towards-a-healthier-food-system/

---

Published: July 27, 2026 — FoodNavigator

The UAE's National Strategy for Healthy Nutrition 2031 has been given cabinet approval and is positioned as a comprehensive framework to improve the nation's overall nutritional health, reduce diet-related disease, and promote a more resilient population.

## Alarming Survey Findings

The strategy was motivated by the UAE's first National Nutrition Survey, which revealed:
- 54% of adults have high blood cholesterol
- 26% have high blood pressure
- 22% have obesity
- 12% have high blood sugar
- 16% of children suffer from obesity
- 96% of the population exceeds WHO recommended sodium intake (consuming 5,000mg/day vs the recommended 2,000mg)
- 56% exceed recommended fat consumption
- 27% exceed recommended sugar intake
- 60% are not performing at least 150 minutes of physical activity weekly
- Average daily energy intake: 2,800 kcal

55% of deaths in the UAE are accounted for by non-communicable diseases (NCDs); 34% of these are due to cardiovascular disease alone. The healthcare cost: over AED 40bn ($10.9bn) spent treating these health issues annually.

## Three Pillars of the Strategy

**1. Shift the food sector towards healthier alternatives:**
- Ban Partially Hydrogenated Oils (PHOs) in food
- Major food reformulation project
- Second phase of sugar taxation on sugary drinks and energy drinks (started early 2026)
- National Food and Beverage Pledge
- Regulate marketing of unhealthy foods to children

**2. Raise awareness of appropriate dietary patterns:**
- Health-promoting retail environments
- Nutrition information on food menus
- Healthy nutrition guidelines reflecting UAE needs
- Healthy restaurant initiative

**3. Strengthen preventive health through nutrition:**
- Early discovery of health issues in children under five
- Supplements to address vitamin and mineral deficiencies (based on National Nutrition Survey results)
- Food fortification with vitamins and minerals to address micronutrient deficiencies

## Economic Case

Government investments in evidence-based interventions to reduce salt consumption, improve dietary patterns and increase physical activity are projected to yield AED 1.45bn ($394.8m) in savings over 15 years — a compelling return on public health investment.
