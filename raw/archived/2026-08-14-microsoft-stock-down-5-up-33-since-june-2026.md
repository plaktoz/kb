# Microsoft Stock Is Down 5% This Year But It's Up 33% Since June. Here's Where Shares Could Go in 2026.

**Key takeaways**: MSFT has climbed 33% since its June 30 close of $373, even though shares still trade 4.6% below where they stood a year ago. Azure grew 43% in fiscal Q4, guided toward 45% for Q1 FY2027. Of 53 analyst price targets: 39 buys, 14 outperforms, 3 holds, 1 sell — the $570 mean target sits 14% above the current $497 price. TIKR's mid-case valuation model puts Microsoft at $1,159 by mid-2031, implying 133% total return (19% annualized).

**The rebound**: Microsoft closed at $497 on August 13, still 4.6% below a year ago despite a 33% climb since June 30. The rebound followed a July 29 fiscal Q4 report that beat estimates across the board: $90 billion revenue (+18%), diluted EPS of $4.74 (+23% adjusted). Azure did the heavy lifting — revenue grew 43%, guided ~45% growth in constant currency for Q1 FY2027. Commercial remaining performance obligation (contracted, unrecognized revenue) jumped 84% YoY to $678 billion. CFO Amy Hood: "Currently, the situation is obviously that demand exceeds available supply in a sort of relatively extreme moment." Microsoft was among the megacaps (with Meta, Tesla, Nvidia) that pushed the S&P 500 to a record close on August 13. Shares traded near $520 last October before an AI-capex-anxiety slide to the $370s by spring; FY2027 capex guidance is rising again, with Q1 alone expected to top $50 billion.

**AI stakes swinging earnings**: The July 29 results included a $3.2 billion gain tied to Microsoft's Anthropic stake (+$0.27 EPS), while its OpenAI stake subtracted $0.07/share the same quarter. This landed the same week Bloomberg reported OpenAI is on track for $40B+ annualized revenue, and IBM said it would join OpenAI's elite partner tier.

**Analyst targets catching up**: The 14% gap between price and mean target has narrowed sharply from a 59% high in March 2026 (stock at $370, mean target ~$587) — analysts trimmed targets only modestly (from $622 in December to $561 by June) as the wide gap was mostly a function of price falling faster than estimates.

**Valuation model**: TIKR's mid-case model rests on Azure holding its acceleration path past the 43% posted in fiscal Q4 and ~45% guided for Q1 FY2027 — sustained double-digit revenue growth well beyond near-term guidance.
