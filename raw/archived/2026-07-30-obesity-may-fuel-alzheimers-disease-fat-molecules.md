# Scientists discover how obesity may fuel Alzheimer's disease

Houston Methodist researchers have uncovered a possible biological pathway connecting obesity to Alzheimer's disease. The study, co-led by Stephen Wong and Li Yang and published in Molecular Neurodegeneration, examined how changes in body fat associated with obesity may send damaging signals to the brain that interfere with the brain's immune system and contribute to Alzheimer's-related biological damage.

The researchers identified phosphatidylethanolamines (PEs) — a class of lipid found in cell membranes throughout the body — as a potentially important link. Obesity raises the amount of PEs in body tissue; these molecules are then loaded into tiny particles that travel through the body and reach the brain. Once inside the brain, the particles can interfere with communication between brain cells, weaken immune protection, and encourage amyloid protein accumulation, a hallmark of Alzheimer's disease.

"Obesity can change how signals travel to the brain," Wong said. "The good news is that this may be something we can treat. Instead of looking at Alzheimer's risk tied to obesity as just a metabolic problem, this research suggests we may be able to target the process that connects those changes to the brain."

When researchers restored a healthier balance of PEs in Alzheimer's disease models, they observed less disruption in lipid regulation, along with improved brain function and cognitive performance (learning, memory, attention, and problem solving).

More than 6.5 million Americans are living with Alzheimer's, a total expected to rise to nearly 14 million by 2060. Yang emphasized more research is needed before PE-targeted treatments can be tested in people, but the findings introduce a possible strategy for intervening earlier in individuals whose metabolic health places them at greater Alzheimer's risk.
