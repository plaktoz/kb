# Only 3 in 10 workers reach deep focus every day. Here's what's getting in the way.

Only about one-third of U.S. workers said they reach a state of deep focus every day, according to a new survey (Resume Now's "Fleeting Flow Report," based on 1,012 employed men and women) suggesting that meetings, busy work, and constant interruptions are making uninterrupted concentration increasingly difficult. The findings align with Microsoft's 2025 Work Trend Index, which found 80% of employees lack the time or energy to do their jobs, and that the average knowledge worker is interrupted by a meeting, email, or notification every two minutes — a pattern Microsoft calls the "infinite workday."

The survey found 31% of workers reach a state of "flow" every day, 46% achieve it several times a week, 15% only occasionally, and 8% rarely or never. More than three-quarters (77%) said repetitive or low-value work occupies a meaningful portion of their week, with 40% estimating at least a quarter of their hours go to "busy work." Nearly two-thirds (64%) said only half or fewer of their meetings are productive, and 38% spend at least five hours weekly in meetings.

Forty-one percent identified burnout or fatigue as their biggest barrier to deep focus, followed by personal distractions and coworker interruptions (both 35%). Nearly 8 in 10 workers (79%) reported some level of "Zoom fatigue." The survey also found 58% feel pressure to remain available outside normal hours, and 44% work extra hours because they can't finish their workload during the regular day.

Resume Now's career expert Keith Spencer said the findings suggest organizations may see greater productivity gains from reducing unnecessary meetings and protecting uninterrupted blocks of time than from adding more collaboration software — employers may not have a productivity problem so much as an attention problem.
