# What Lies Beneath: An Updated Outlook on the Economy and Investing

The summer of 2026 has been dominated by the AI boom. Some fear AI is advancing too quickly for thoughtful regulation; others worry end-user revenues will lag massive capital spending and fast-growing debt. From an economic perspective, AI is pure momentum — generating profit gains, adding to investment spending, boosting upper-income consumer spending via a wealth effect, and coinciding with solid productivity gains.

Beneath the AI economy there is plenty of weakness, confirmed in the latest employment report: job growth over the past year is barely positive per the payroll survey and negative per the household survey, wage growth continues sliding below CPI inflation, homebuilding is stagnant, and government employment and non-tech exports remain soft.

**Headwinds and tailwinds:** A negotiated resolution to the Iran conflict is likely, giving Iran some control of the Strait of Hormuz in exchange for safe passage guarantees — this could stabilize oil prices for the rest of the year. New tariffs are expected to be less onerous than the temporary IEEPA tariffs they replace (~7.5% of imports vs. 11.5%). Immigration policy remains restrictive, contributing to a shrinking working-age population. The federal deficit will approach $2 trillion this fiscal year with no further stimulus likely. The AI capital spending boom should remain enduring, with business fixed investment up 6.7% over the past year.

**Economic vital signs:** GDP growth is expected to rise to 2.8% in Q3 (from 1.5% in Q2) before decelerating to roughly 2.0% in 2027. Payroll growth should stay anemic (50,000–100,000/month) even as unemployment falls toward 4.0% by Q4 2026. Headline CPI inflation is expected at 3.4% y/y for July, easing toward the Fed's 2.0% target by spring 2027. S&P 500 second-quarter earnings season has been strong — proforma EPS up 50% y/y, though roughly half of that is inflated by unrealized capital gains at two large tech companies; underlying growth is closer to 20%. The Fed is expected to hold rates at the September FOMC meeting.

**Investment implications:** The S&P 500 is up 13.3% year-to-date, leaving the forward P/E at 20.2x — elevated but down from the 2025 peak, and potentially misleading given analyst expectations rest heavily on continued mega-cap tech capex. International stocks look cheaper than U.S. counterparts and can diversify away from a concentrated U.S. AI bet; U.S. fixed income looks fairly priced; alternatives like infrastructure and real estate offer further diversification. The market value of all U.S. corporate equity is now over 400% of GDP, versus 244% before the pandemic and 74% before the 1987 crash — a reminder that stock prices ultimately depend on the fortunes of American workers and consumers, not just AI capex.
