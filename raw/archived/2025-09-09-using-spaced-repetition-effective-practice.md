# Using Spaced Repetition to Achieve Effective Practice

Too much repetition in a single sitting can cause regression rather than improvement. Spaced repetition — practicing something over time instead of in one block — encourages stronger memory formation because revisiting material right around the time you'd forget it triggers a neural reconstruction that leaves a deeper impression.

## How to implement spaced repetition

1. **Begin practicing a problem area, committing it to short-term memory.** Analyze what you want to improve and practice deliberately, actively supervising your performance in real time and via recordings.
2. **Repeat within the range of short-term memory.** Revisit material right around when you'd naturally forget it — this is more mentally effortful and involves more frequent failure, but the rewards are worth it. Skill drills can create an illusion of competence: performing something error-free once doesn't mean it's fully learned.
3. **Wait 24 hours before practicing it again.** Mindlessly repeating a section in one sitting doesn't help; instead, practice something a limited number of times, move on, and return the next day. After two or three days, mastery improves. Recommended gaps for revisiting material you're committing to repertoire: first repetition after 1 day, second after 7 days, third after 16 days, fourth after 35 days. Interleaving — alternating between multiple passages within a single practice session — is another way to apply spaced repetition within a shorter time frame.
4. **Repeat steps 2 and 3.** Per the "Forget to Learn" theory, the greater the drop in retrieval strength before a review, the greater the learning gain when the memory is accessed again. For committing a piece to repertoire over time: revisit within 1–2 days for material learned a week ago, after 1 week for a month-old piece, after 2 weeks for 3 months, after 3 weeks for 6 months, and after 1 month for a year.

The efficacy of spaced repetition comes from deliberately waiting and then repeating within the right time frame — consistent practice this way leads to solidification within days and, over time, full mastery.
