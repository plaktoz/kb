# How to Plan Deep Work Sessions With Claude (2026)

source_url: https://www.asianefficiency.com/productivity/claude-deep-work-planning

---

Asian Efficiency, by Thanh Pham

Deep work blocks fail more often from a missing plan than from a lack of time. A 5-minute pre-block conversation with Claude turns "I have 3 hours to work on the business" into a specific, timed checklist.

## The 5-minute pre-block planning session

**Prompt 1 — the brain dump:** list everything competing for your attention today (projects, deadlines, stressors), and ask Claude to pick the highest-leverage use of the block.

**Prompt 2 — prioritization:** Claude recommends one primary focus (and maybe a secondary task) based on deadlines, dependencies, and energy. Push back if needed; usually takes 2–3 exchanges.

**Prompt 3 — breakdown:** ask Claude to break the focus into specific timed steps, each with what you're producing, how long it should take, and what "done" looks like.

An advanced version — a weekly deep-work map done during a Sunday review — has Claude map each priority to a specific block for the week, flagging anything that won't fit given available deep-work time.

## Why Claude over ChatGPT for this

The author argues Claude is better for three reasons: it pushes back on overambitious plans ("that's 8 hours of work compressed into 3 — here's a realistic scope") rather than enthusiastically planning the impossible; it asks clarifying questions to force specificity on vague asks like "work on the business"; and with persistent Projects, it retains context across planning sessions (deadlines mentioned previously, progress checks).

## The rules that make it work

Never skip the planning session — five minutes of planning saves 30–60 minutes of wandering. Define "done" before starting. One primary focus per block, not two or three. Match tasks to energy (creative work in high-energy morning blocks, analytical work in lower-energy afternoon blocks). Plan for about 80% of the block's time and leave 20% as buffer, since tasks run long. Common mistakes: planning during the block instead of before it, overloading the block with two ambitious deliverables, and not protecting the block from interruptions like Slack.
