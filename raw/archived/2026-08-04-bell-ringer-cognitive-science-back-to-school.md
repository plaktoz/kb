# The Bell Ringer back-to-school companion

With the entire Southeast headed back to school, this roundup collects the most significant reporting on cognitive science and what it means for teaching and schools, pulling together the research, books, and evidence-based practices shaping classrooms today.

## Spotlight on cognitive science: The Four Big Ideas series

From working memory to retrieval practice, the series covers the basics of how cognitive science applies to teaching and learning, explained by leading experts and educators — including why working memory is the most important thing for teachers to understand, how to use the science of learning to become a more effective teacher, the nitty-gritty of explicit instruction, getting retrieval practice right at home and at school, how asking the right questions teaches "effortful thinking," and writing instruction informed by evidence.

## The "science of math," explained

As the math wars rage on, coverage examines why "productive struggle" is falling out of favor with some teachers, schools and even states; why successful problem-solving is like the TV show "Chopped"; why every math teacher should know the evidence-based "instructional hierarchy"; how to 3X students' daily math practice; why most teachers aren't actually using "inquiry" or "explicit" math instruction (they're using lectures and worksheets); and why math needs knowledge-building, too.

## The latest in the science of reading

The newsletter has broken key science-of-reading stories now shaping the conversation, including the rise of linguistic phonics ("phonics 2.0"), why the science of reading needs cognitive and instructional science to be effective, how schools teach reading comprehension in ways that don't line up with cognitive science, a survey finding students read few or no whole books at school, and a new study finding classrooms teaching too much phonics decoding and not enough "real reading."

## How to translate evidence into practice

In June, the author helped launch the Teaching That Succeeds Symposium, where K-12 teachers across subjects shared routines and techniques that incorporate this research into actionable, data-tested practices. All 18 presentations are available free on YouTube, including sessions on cracking the code of academic vocabulary (linking sound, spelling and meaning for word mastery), an 8-week fluency intervention that improved student reading, word blending for early learners, and incorporating schemas into word problem solving.

## Plus

Additional resources cover helping students build strong learning habits with the science of learning, understanding executive functions to help early learners, and helping students harness and sharpen their attention.
