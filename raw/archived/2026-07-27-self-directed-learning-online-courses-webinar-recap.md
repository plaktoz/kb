# Strategies to Support Self-Directed Learning in Online College Courses: Webinar Recap

Online and hybrid courses expand access to higher education but require students to manage time, motivation, and study strategies with less face-to-face contact. In a July 16 webinar, researchers from the Postsecondary Teaching with Technology Collaborative presented a new Self-Directed Learning (SDL) Instructional Model, developed and refined over five years of research including real-world pilot studies.

The model has three strategies that can be used individually or together: self-directed learning videos, reflective prompts, and student-peer interaction and networking (SPIN), each reinforcing motivational, metacognitive, and applied learning processes. Researcher Amy E. Brown said the pilots showed high student participation and gave instructors actionable insight into what students understood or struggled with, informing real-time course adjustments. A Course Implementation Guide and Campus Resource Guide provide ready-to-use text, pacing guides, and slide decks. Researcher Hannah Cheever noted the model doesn't require adding new assignments — it mainly reframes assignments instructors already use.

Odessa College math professor Nikki Handley, who piloted the strategies in an eight-week developmental math course, said implementation was "extremely easy" — largely copy-and-paste — and she has continued using it in subsequent semesters (introductory prompts, assessment reflections, advice-sharing with future classes). Student Cheyenne Natividad, returning to college after years away, said the strategies helped her "set my goals and actually reach them" and better plan and manage her time in an online environment where she had previously felt disorganized.
