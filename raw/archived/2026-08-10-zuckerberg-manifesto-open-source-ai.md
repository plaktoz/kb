# Zuckerberg manifesto pushes open-source approach on AI as Meta releases latest model

Meta Platforms on Monday announced the release of a new artificial intelligence model open for other developers to use, as CEO Mark Zuckerberg warned of the risks if control of advanced AI is concentrated under a select few companies, institutions or governments.

The company included a "permissive" open source license for Muse Glimmer, a lightweight AI model that can run on a personal computer, and said it would provide developer access to a more powerful model, Muse Spark 1.2. Meta has long championed open-sourcing its AI models but has struggled to keep pace with rivals including OpenAI, Anthropic and Google that have taken a more closed approach.

In a lengthy essay, Zuckerberg laid out his vision for AI's future and argued open source is a fairer path for development, along with policy recommendations for keeping the U.S. ahead of rivals like China. He warned that if AI "superintelligence" ends up controlled by a select few individuals, businesses, governments, or even "AI itself," that will naturally lead to outcomes less favorable for everyone else. The key, he wrote, is finding a balance that favors individuals and ensures advanced AI is "broadly distributed" to empower billions of people.

In an apparent veiled reference to rivals such as OpenAI and Anthropic, Zuckerberg wrote: "Most other labs are focused on building AI for companies, governments, or other institutions, so if those labs lead, then the balance of power will favor larger institutions over individuals."

He said the U.S. and its allies need to speed up building energy capacity and infrastructure to stay competitive with China, and argued authorities should reconsider their stance on "distillation" — training a less capable model on a stronger one's outputs. The Trump administration has vowed to crack down on Chinese AI firms using distillation to extract technical features from U.S. models; Anthropic earlier this year accused China's DeepSeek of the practice. "Some have tried to frame distillation as harmful, but I think it is important to protect the principle that you can learn from anything you can observe," Zuckerberg said. "This is how the world works, and the US will not be able to lead if we restrict ourselves on this front."
