# VC-backed startups commit more fraud, and researchers think they know why

source_url: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/31/vc-backed-startups-commit-more-fraud-and-researchers-think-they-know-why/

---

By Dominic-Madori Davis — July 31, 2026

Research from Imperial College (UK) and Emlyon Business School (France) examined how VC-backed founders commit fraud and the role investors play. The researchers built a database of tech founders facing SEC and DOJ securities fraud prosecutions between 2000–2023.

A separate University of Toronto study of 654 fraud cases found that VC-backed companies face fraud charges more often than non-VC-backed ones, and startups launched during overheated markets are **19% more likely** to later commit fraud.

Researcher Tim Weiss described three escalating stages of dishonesty he calls "façading":

- **Surface** — exaggerating company success beyond aspirational pitching
- **Reinforced** — fabricating evidence (fake contracts, invoices, revenue) to support earlier lies
- **Deep** — manufacturing false technical capabilities and entire "parallel realities"

Weiss noted that investors can "co-create fraud" by continuing to back founders with prior misconduct allegations. The UT study found that alleged fraud does little to block future fundraising, which it called consistent with a culture that "embraces failure regardless of the cause."

Key findings also include:

- Founder-controlled boards were **twice as likely** to commit fraud vs. investor-controlled boards
- Post-IPO, VC-backed companies face more securities lawsuits than PE-backed peers within two years
- Longer private company timelines reduce scrutiny

Weiss proposes routine SEC audits once startups hit a significant investment threshold, and argues investors should face liability for governance failures and for pressuring founders toward unrealistic growth targets.
