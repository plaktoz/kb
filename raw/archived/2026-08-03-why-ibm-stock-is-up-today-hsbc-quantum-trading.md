# Why IBM (IBM) Stock Is Up Today

Shares of IBM jumped 5.6% in the morning session after the company, in partnership with HSBC, announced the world's first-known successful trial of quantum-enabled algorithmic trading. The collaboration showed that using IBM's quantum systems improved the prediction of bond trade outcomes by up to 34% compared to traditional computing methods. The trial focused on the European corporate bond market, where the technology helped better price customer inquiries in a competitive bidding process — a significant milestone in finding practical uses for quantum computers to solve real-world financial problems. Separately, IBM Japan revealed a team-up with Fujitsu to advance innovation in AI and healthcare.

IBM's shares are not very volatile and have only had 7 moves greater than 5% over the last year, so today's move indicates the market considers the quantum trading news meaningful. The biggest move in the past year was two months earlier, when the stock dropped 7.8% after a second-quarter earnings report showed revenue from its software division missed analyst expectations, overshadowing an otherwise strong quarter — the company had surpassed Wall Street's forecasts for overall revenue and profit, but underperformance in the high-growth software segment, combined with cautious commentary from CEO Arvind Krishna, prompted a sell-off.

IBM is up 28.6% since the beginning of the year, trading at $282.74 per share, close to its 52-week high of $294.78 from June 2025. Investors who bought $1,000 worth of IBM's shares 5 years ago would now be looking at an investment worth $2,377.
