# The Art of Decision-Making: Mental Models vs. First Principles

Indecision leads to missed opportunities, wasted resources, and stagnation. Two competing approaches to problem-solving — Mental Models and First Principles Thinking — can be chosen between using a simple 2x2 matrix crossing Impact against Time required.

**High impact, low time:** lean on Mental Models. Quick wins with high stakes, like a marketing strategy for a product launch, benefit from tried-and-true frameworks that avoid wasting precious time (example: investing, M&A decisions).

**High impact, high time:** switch to First Principles Thinking. When stakes are high and the problem is genuinely complex, like building a new product from scratch, breaking the problem down to fundamental truths produces more innovative, foundational solutions even though it takes longer. Pairing this with second-order thinking helps manage the resulting risks. Elon Musk's approach to cutting battery costs — rethinking the entire production process from raw materials up — is the canonical example.

**Low impact, low time:** Mental Models again, but looser — familiar frameworks or gut feeling are enough for something like choosing a project management tool for a small team.

**Low impact, high time:** First Principles Thinking still applies when there's slack to explore a lower-stakes problem, such as re-evaluating a team's workflow by questioning every step, producing fresh insight without real downside risk.

Mapping a decision onto this Impact/Time matrix first — before reaching for either tool by habit — is what makes the choice between "fast, proven framework" and "slow, from-scratch reasoning" deliberate rather than automatic.
