# ADKAR Model for Change Management: Stages & Steps

## Overview

The ADKAR model is a change management framework centered on individuals rather than processes. Developed by Jeff Hiatt of Prosci in 2003 after analyzing over 700 organizations, it identifies five sequential conditions every person must meet for organizational change to stick. The acronym stands for **Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, and Reinforcement.**

Prosci research indicates that roughly 70% of change initiatives fail due to people-related factors, not technical or budget issues.

---

## The 5 Stages

| Stage | Practical Meaning | Blocking Signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Employee understands why change is necessary | Resistance narratives like "why fix what isn't broken?" |
| **Desire** | Employee voluntarily wants to support the change | Passive resistance, low voluntary engagement |
| **Knowledge** | Employee knows how to implement the change | Frequent errors, excessive questions to managers |
| **Ability** | Employee applies new knowledge independently | Knows theory but can't execute without support |
| **Reinforcement** | New behaviors are sustained long-term | Regression to old habits 2–3 months post-launch |

### Stage Details

**1. Awareness** — More than announcing a change is coming; employees must grasp the risk of *not* changing and how it affects them personally. Tools: segmented internal communications, leadership videos, informational sessions.

**2. Desire** — The hardest stage because it hinges on personal values, fears, and interests. Leaders act as change agents here. Tools: mentoring, executive Q&A sessions, social learning content.

**3. Knowledge** — Where e-learning and LMS platforms become central. A common mistake is creating one generic module for everyone, when different roles need different depth. Personalized learning paths by profile are recommended.

**4. Ability** — Bridges the gap between knowing and doing. Experiential learning, immediate feedback, and simulated scenarios are key. Gamification and branching scenarios are particularly effective here.

**5. Reinforcement** — Without this, Ebbinghaus's forgetting curve reverses the change. Reinforcement is an ongoing system: recognition, review microlearning, tracking dashboards, and communicating results.

---

## Advantages & Disadvantages

### Advantages
- Individual and measurable focus — pinpoints exactly where each person is blocked
- Clear sequential structure prevents jumping to training before building buy-in
- Directly aligns with L&D actions
- Reduces resistance at its root causes
- Flexible and scalable across project sizes

### Disadvantages
- Managing individual diagnostics at scale is resource-intensive without digital tools
- Doesn't directly address collective dynamics like team culture
- Requires methodological discipline — skipping early stages is a common failure point
- Not a project management replacement; must complement frameworks like Agile or PMBOK

---

## Implementation Steps

1. **Prior ADKAR Assessment** — Diagnose which stage each employee group is blocked at using Prosci's scoring tool (1–5 per stage per person)
2. **Design stage-specific actions** — Communication for Awareness; leadership engagement for Desire; training for Knowledge; practice for Ability; monitoring for Reinforcement
3. **Involve line managers** — Middle managers are often the primary success/failure factor, especially in early stages
4. **Activate digital learning tools** — LMS platforms support Knowledge through Reinforcement stages with personalized paths and analytics
5. **Measure and adjust continuously** — Treat ADKAR as an iterative system, not a one-time sequence

## Comparison With Other Models

| Model | Main Focus | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| **ADKAR** | Individual — 5 sequential stages | Tool/process adoption requiring individual change |
| **Kotter 8 Steps** | Organizational — collective urgency and leadership | Large-scale cultural transformations from the top |
| **Lewin (3 Stages)** | Structural — unfreeze/change/refreeze | Planned, controlled changes with a defined end state |
| **McKinsey 7-S** | Systemic — 7 organizational elements simultaneously | Complex organizational diagnosis with multiple dimensions |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Skipping Awareness** — Assuming "they already know" leads employees to fill information gaps with fear-based narratives
2. **Confusing Knowledge with Ability** — Course completion doesn't equal execution capacity; transfer should be evaluated at 30, 60, and 90 days
3. **Abandoning Reinforcement after launch** — Without ongoing systems, regression within 90 days is nearly inevitable
4. **Applying ADKAR rigidly across the whole organization** — Different people are at different stages simultaneously; targeted diagnosis is essential

---

## 6 Strategies for Better Results

1. Run a prior ADKAR assessment before designing any action
2. Involve line managers from the start
3. Personalize learning paths by role
4. Combine formats in the Knowledge stage (e-learning, video, simulations)
5. Measure job transfer, not just course completion
6. Design Reinforcement as an ongoing system, not a single event

---

## Real-World Example

Grupo AKRON trained 700+ professionals across multiple countries using role-specific learning paths that combined a course catalog with custom content. The outcome was a 35% increase in completion rates and half the previous production time.

---

## Timeline Expectations

According to Prosci, mid-scale change projects typically require **3–12 months** to complete all stages. Large-scale technological or cultural transformations can extend to **18–24 months**. Organizations that bypass Desire or Reinforcement stages show significantly higher failure rates.
