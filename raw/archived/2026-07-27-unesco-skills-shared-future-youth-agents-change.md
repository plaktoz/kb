# Skills for a Shared Future: Young People as Agents of Change

source_url: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/skills-shared-future-young-people-agents-change

---

Published: July 15, 2026 — UNESCO / World Youth Skills Day 2026

Young leaders, Member States, employers, workers, civil society and United Nations partners gathered in New York on July 15, 2026 to mark World Youth Skills Day under the theme "Skills for a Shared Future." The event explored how digital and AI literacy can be combined with creativity, critical thinking, empathy and other human-centred skills, while making education and training systems more inclusive, resilient and responsive to change through meaningful youth participation.

## Key Themes

**AI Literacy as a Core Skill:** The event emphasized that digital and AI literacy must be built into education systems globally. However, AI skills alone are insufficient — they must be combined with creativity, critical thinking, and empathy.

**Youth Voice in Policy:** Mr. Minchenberg emphasized that young people must have a seat at the table and that their voices, ideas and experiences should inform decisions about education and the future of work.

**Skills for Economic Transition:** UNESCO's Global Youth and Student Consultations on Education beyond 2030 were highlighted as an opportunity for young people to share their aspirations, priorities and proposed actions for the future of education.

## Broader Context

UNESCO's framing reflects a growing global consensus: the most valuable skills in an AI-driven economy are not pure technical skills, but a combination of AI fluency with the distinctly human capacities — judgment, empathy, creativity, ethical reasoning — that AI cannot replicate.

This mirrors findings from workforce research (Gallup 2026) showing that AI's highest productivity gains come not from replacing human judgment, but from augmenting task-specific human work with AI tools across a broader range of applications.
