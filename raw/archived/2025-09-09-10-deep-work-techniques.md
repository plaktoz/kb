# 10 Deep Work Techniques To Help You Focus

Deep work is a state of concentration where a person works on a complex task without distraction, distinct from shallow work — the easy, interruptible tasks handled between meetings. Deep work matters because complex, strategic work is what moves a project or organization forward; without the ability to focus, output gets delayed.

## Four types of deep work (Cal Newport)

**Monastic** — multiple days of near-complete focus, rarely realistic for most jobs but powerful when blocked between projects. **Bimodal** — some days reserved fully for deep work, others for everything else. **Rhythmic** — the day split into blocks (often ~90 minutes) for deep work, with the rest left open. **Journalistic** — squeezing deep work in wherever time allows, common for people juggling many smaller tasks.

## 10 techniques

1. **Prioritize** — before each session, ask "what single task will move the needle most?"
2. **Create a routine and rituals** — a consistent way to transition into deep work (do-not-disturb signals, defined "emergency" criteria, a reward ritual afterward).
3. **Schedule your days and block time** — put deep work sessions on the calendar as protected blocks; mark open blocks "ask before scheduling" to prevent meetings from consuming them.
4. **Track your time** — measure how long specific tasks actually take to spot whether shorter fragmented sessions are hurting output on cognitively demanding work.
5. **Limit context switching and multitasking** — task-switching prevents the flow state deep work requires and often means completing several things poorly instead of one thing well.
6. **Minimize distractions** — clear the physical and audio environment (music, clutter, notifications) before starting.
7. **Set and track goals** — set realistic weekly targets, e.g. three 90-minute sessions, and adjust as the habit builds.
8. **Make time to rest** — deep work without recovery leads to burnout, which erodes the very focus deep work depends on.
9. **Set a timer** — a Pomodoro-style timer (or longer, ~90-minute blocks for genuine deep work) keeps sessions bounded and focused.
10. **Turn off notifications** — put all devices on do-not-disturb and tell colleagues or family you're unavailable except for genuine emergencies.
