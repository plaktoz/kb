# No Hangover: Revisiting Microsoft One Week After Earnings

Microsoft reported earnings on July 29, and the stock has been on a tear since, up more than 25% since the report and turning positive for the year on August 5. The rally is a relief for shareholders who watched MSFT drop nearly 30% between October 2025 and March 2026 amid concerns over hyperscaler spending (both too much and not enough), the "SaaS-pocalypse" narrative around AI, and questions about AI monetization.

Microsoft posted fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $90 billion, up 18% year over year, with Azure revenue climbing 43%. Full fiscal 2026 revenue topped $331 billion. Microsoft 365 Copilot crossed 30 million paid seats, and commercial remaining performance obligation (contracted backlog) grew sharply — giving investors proof that AI spending is translating into revenue.

The stock's 25%+ move in about a week is an outlier for MSFT, which typically takes a month or longer to make moves of that size, raising pullback concerns; based on RSI, however, the stock isn't yet technically overbought.

**Locking in AI infrastructure power.** Chevron's July 31 earnings highlighted Project Kilby, a 20-year take-or-pay power agreement with Microsoft covering 2.67 gigawatts of behind-the-meter capacity in West Texas, moving toward a final investment decision later this year with expected mid-teens returns. Constellation Energy has a separate long-term power arrangement tied to Microsoft, anchored by the restart of the Crane Clean Energy Center under a 20-year supply agreement. Between Chevron's gas-fired capacity and Constellation's nuclear restart, Microsoft is diversifying its AI power supply across very different parts of the energy sector.

**Free cash flow trade-off.** Microsoft delivered $19.6 billion in free cash flow, down 23% year over year, repeating a pattern from the prior quarter. Microsoft still maintains positive FCF, unlike Oracle (taking on debt for AI infrastructure) or Alphabet (negative FCF), but the decline makes MSFT expensive on some discounted-cash-flow models that prioritize FCF — the opposite of what fueled the stock's 2025 rally.

Management now expects roughly $175 billion in fiscal 2027 capital expenditures, and is extending depreciation schedules for data centers and office buildings from 15 to 25 years while booking more future leases as operating rather than finance leases — an accounting shift that will affect the FCF picture going forward. The article concludes both the AI demand story (real, multi-year, evidenced by the Chevron/Constellation deals) and the cost story (real, ongoing) are true simultaneously, and balancing the two will likely define MSFT's trading over the next two quarters. At time of writing MSFT traded at $499.86 (+2.5%), with a Moderate Buy consensus rating and $558.87 average price target.
