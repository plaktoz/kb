# Are brain waves the next unlock for physical AI?

source_url: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/26/are-brain-waves-the-next-unlock-for-physical-ai/

---

Author: Tim Fernholz
Published: July 26, 2026

Encord, a data tooling company based in San Leandro, California, is experimenting with brain wave measurements as a new modality for generating robot training data. Workers called "pilots" perform physical tasks — like playing Jenga or stacking poker chips — while wearing headsets from German startup Zander Labs that capture both egocentric video and EEG-style brain activity. The goal is to detect mental states like error, intent, and surprise, helping models know when to deploy higher-effort processing.

The core problem driving this work: physical AI faces a severe training data shortage. Encord's head of robot learning, Vineeth Velmurugan (formerly of OpenAI's robot lab), states plainly that "the data simply does not exist." He estimates a dataset roughly "five times the size of YouTube's video corpus" would be needed to make a meaningful breakthrough.

Encord collects two main data types: egocentric video from factory workers globally, and data from remotely operated "leader-follower" robotic arms. They also use forearm muscle-signal sensors to reconstruct 3D hand position — compensating for the fact that standard hand video rarely captures the full hand.

Dense annotation — labeling actions like "right hand tightens bolt" — is described as worth "100 times as much as 'junky ego data'" for task-specific training, at only 20x the cost. However, unlike scraping internet text, physical data must be actively manufactured, fundamentally changing the economics of building physical AI models.
