# The Stock Market Is Doing Something Observed Only 6 Times Since 1871 — and the Previous 5 Occurrences Ended in Disaster for Wall Street

The Dow, S&P 500, and Nasdaq Composite have hit record highs since early June, but the market has also reached a rare valuation mark achieved only six times since January 1871, based on the S&P 500's Shiller (Cyclically Adjusted) P/E Ratio, which averages 17.4 over that 155-year span. As of July 27's close, the ratio stood near 40.5.

The six instances where the CAPE Ratio exceeded 30 during a continuous bull market for at least two months: August–September 1929 (Dow later lost 89% peak to trough); June 1997–August 2001, peaking at a record 44.19 in December 1999 just before the dot-com crash (S&P 500 lost 49%, Nasdaq lost 78%); September 2017–November 2018 (S&P 500 fell 20% in Q4 2018); December 2019–February 2020, ahead of the COVID crash (S&P 500 lost 34% in 33 days); August 2020–May 2022, crossing 40 in January 2022 just before a nine-month bear market that cut the Nasdaq by a third; and November 2023–present, peaking at 42.84 in early June 2026 — the second-priciest multiple in 155 years.

Despite the historically dire short-term signal, the article notes that bear markets have historically been much shorter than bull markets: since the Great Depression, the average S&P 500 bear market lasted about 286 days (9.5 months) versus an average bull market of 1,023 days (roughly 3.6x longer), with 14 of 27 bull markets exceeding the length of the longest bear market. The author's framing: valuations argue for caution near-term, but a long-term diversified investor has historically been rewarded for staying invested through cycles.
