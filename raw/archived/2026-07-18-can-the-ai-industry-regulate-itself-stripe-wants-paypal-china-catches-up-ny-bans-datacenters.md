# Can the AI Industry Regulate Itself? Stripe Wants PayPal, China Catches Up, NY Bans Datacenters

**Publication:** All-In with Chamath, Jason, Sacks & Friedberg (Episode 281)
**Duration:** 1:29:54

## AI Self-Regulation (SRO Proposal)

DeepMind's Demis Hassabis proposed a US-led, FINRA-modeled self-regulatory body for AI. It would be industry-funded, expert-run, with federal oversight. Frontier labs would submit models 30 days pre-release for assessment on cyber, biosecurity, and national security risks.

Sacks outlined five conditions for the SRO to work:
1. Broad industry representation including startups and open source
2. Only review true frontier models
3. Focus solely on catastrophic risks (cyber, CBRN)
4. Start voluntary, become mandatory only after proving effectiveness
5. Must substitute existing regulatory structures, not add to them

Sacks contrasted this with the "FAA for AI" approach, noting new aircraft type certifications take "5 to 9 years," which would cripple AI development pace.

Friedberg called the SRO concept "elegant," noting it keeps domain experts in control rather than government agencies that can't keep pace with the technology.

Chamath warned of regulatory capture: "The faster we avoid that off-ramp by actually establishing a set of rules... is a really important thing."

Sacks criticized Anthropic's strategy, citing a Politico report describing their "state-by-state plan" encouraging progressively stricter AI rules across blue states rather than a unified national framework.

## Stripe/Block Bid for PayPal

Stripe and private equity firm Advent jointly offered ~$53B (~$60/share) for PayPal. Block (Jack Dorsey) is also reportedly involved, contributing ~$17B in equity.

PayPal metrics: 439M consumer accounts, ~$1.7T annual transaction volume, peaked at ~$322B market cap, was trading near $30–40B before the offer.

Chamath's thesis: combining Stripe's merchant relationships, PayPal's consumer base, Block's point-of-sale infrastructure, and stablecoin assets (PayPal's PYUSD, Stripe's Bridge acquisition) could create "a competitor to Visa and MasterCard."

Friedberg predicted this is part of a broader wave of AI-native operators acquiring stalled "first-generation digital native businesses," citing Ryan Cohen's eBay bid and Bending Spoons' roll-up of Web 2.0 assets (Evernote, Vimeo, Eventbrite, etc.).

Sacks noted PayPal's stagnation stems from losing founder DNA post-eBay acquisition: "as soon as it was acquired... they basically blew out all the founders."

## Apple Sues OpenAI

Apple filed a 41-page lawsuit alleging OpenAI stole trade secrets during hardware development. Key allegations:
- Tang Tan (former Apple VP, now OpenAI's chief hardware officer) allegedly directed job candidates to bring "actual parts" to interviews
- A former Apple engineer reportedly texted a still-employed colleague: "LOL, I found out I can access the network storage."
- OpenAI has poached 400+ Apple employees

Chamath: "Nobody should be stealing things from their former employer." Sacks offered a simple rule: "The only thing you can bring to your new job is what's in your head."

## Grok Build Data Leak & AI Cost Dynamics

Grok Build was inadvertently uploading entire user codebases — including passwords and API keys — to xAI servers despite privacy settings claiming otherwise. xAI disabled the upload and open-sourced the Grok Build harness.

Chamath warned: "Privacy in AI is very fragile... you are leaking information where you don't know it" and called for independent third-party layers between enterprises and model providers.

Token pricing comparison:
- Claude/GPT-4 class: ~$26–56/million input tokens
- Grok, Meta: ~$1.50
- Chinese models (Qwen, etc.): ~$0.50

Ramp CEO Eric Gleiman noted enterprise token spend grew 21x in one year, with CFOs often blindsided by bills.

Mira Murati's new company Thinking Machines launched "Inkling," positioning below frontier intelligence but optimized for fine-tuning open models at dramatically lower cost.

## NY Data Center Moratorium

NY Governor Hochul signed the "nation's first ever statewide moratorium on hyperscale data centers," citing power costs, water use, land use, and noise pollution.

The besties pushed back on each claim:
- **Power:** Behind-the-meter solutions let data centers generate their own power
- **Water:** Modern facilities use closed-loop systems
- **Land:** Upstate New York is "70 to 80% undeveloped"
- **Jobs/taxes:** Data centers generate substantial tax revenue and construction employment

Sacks argued the moratorium may be a political holding pattern. Friedberg drew a parallel to anti-GMO sentiment, suggesting foreign influence campaigns may be seeding anti-data center narratives — citing OpenAI's blog post identifying "PRC-linked influence operations targeting AI debates in the US."

Chamath noted ~40% of planned data center projects are being cancelled or paused, creating supply shortfalls.

## Science Corner (Friedberg)

A joint paper from Calico (Google's longevity lab) and Revel Pharma described using AlphaFold and directed evolution to engineer a novel enzyme that degrades CML (a key advanced glycation end product that accumulates in the extracellular matrix with age).

Results: the enzyme eliminated "52 to 97% of CML" in lab tests and reduced CML on donated elderly human skin by 55%, effectively reverting skin biological age to ~31 years old from 70+.

Friedberg called the cosmetic applications alone a potential "$2 trillion" market, with longer-term potential for injectable or RNA-based delivery to address systemic aging, joint inflammation, and more.
