# Time Management Strategies: 12 Methods Ranked by Effectiveness

You do not need more time. You need a system for spending the time you have on the things that actually matter. This guide ranks 12 time management methods from simplest to most comprehensive.

**Tier 1 — Simple methods:**
1. **2-Minute Rule**: If a task takes under 2 minutes, do it immediately instead of listing it. Eliminates mental overhead from small tasks.
2. **Ivy Lee Method**: Each evening, write the 6 most important tasks for tomorrow, ranked by priority; complete #1 before moving to #2.
3. **1-3-5 Rule**: Plan 1 big, 3 medium, and 5 small tasks each day for realistic daily planning.

**Tier 2 — Intermediate methods:**
4. **Time Blocking**: Assign every hour to a specific task/category; deep work gets 2-3 hour blocks. Most recommended by productivity researchers.
5. **Pomodoro Technique**: 25 minutes focused work, 5-minute break, repeat 4x then longer break. Best for procrastinators; adjust to 50/10 for deep work.
6. **Eisenhower Matrix**: Categorize tasks by urgent/important quadrants — do, schedule, delegate, or eliminate.
7. **Eat the Frog**: Do your most dreaded task first thing each morning, when willpower and cognition peak.

**Tier 3 — Comprehensive systems:**
8. **Getting Things Done (GTD)**: David Allen's 5 steps — Capture, Clarify, Organize, Reflect, Engage. The weekly review is non-negotiable.
9. **ABCDE Method**: Rank tasks A (must do) through E (eliminate); never work on B while A is incomplete.
10. **Themed Days**: Assign each weekday a single focus area to eliminate context-switching.
11. **80/20 Rule (Pareto)**: 80% of results come from 20% of activities — identify and double down on the high-leverage 20%.
12. **Weekly Review + Daily Planning Hybrid**: Weekly review of accomplishments/unfinished tasks plus daily task selection from the weekly plan.

**Choosing a method** depends on your biggest problem: procrastination → Pomodoro/Eat the Frog; can't prioritize → Eisenhower/ABCDE; overcommitting → 1-3-5/Ivy Lee; context-switching → Time Blocking/Themed Days; complex projects → GTD/Weekly Review Hybrid.

**Common mistakes**: confusing busy with productive; skipping planning (15 min planning saves 2 hours of reactive work); multitasking (Stanford research shows it cuts productivity up to 40%); ignoring energy management; never reviewing.

Time management is not about squeezing more into your day — it's about making sure the right things get your best attention.
