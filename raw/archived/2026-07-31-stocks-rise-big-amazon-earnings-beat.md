# Stocks Rise on Big Amazon Earnings Beat: Stock Market Today

source_url: https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/stocks/stocks-rise-on-big-amazon-earnings-beat-stock-market-today

byline: Karee Venema

date: 2026-07-31

---

Stocks were volatile Friday as market participants weighed mixed reactions to a pair of Big Tech earnings reports and surging Treasury yields. The main equity indexes finished the session higher, though, and snapped their weekly losing streaks.

At the close, the Dow Jones Industrial Average was up 0.5% at 52,485, the broader S&P 500 was 0.7% higher at 7,489, and the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite had gained 1.0% to 25,373.

But stocks' gains were contained as borrowing costs continued to climb. The 2-year Treasury yield jumped 4.1 basis points to 4.27%, while yields on the 10-year Treasury (+4.9 basis points to 4.712%) and 30-year bond (+4.6 basis points to 5.253%) hit their highest levels since 2007.

Today's rise in Treasury yields came after Cleveland Federal Reserve President Beth Hammack, who voted for a quarter-point rate hike at the July Fed meeting, said in a statement that "now is the time" for the central bank to act to bring inflation down. Minneapolis Fed President Neel Kashkari, who also dissented in this week's Fed decision to hold interest rates steady in favor of a quarter-percentage-point hike, said he'd prefer to raise rates now than to allow high inflation to become entrenched.

"The fixed-income complex is moving further away from the central bank's current midpoint of 3.63% as bond vigilantes protest [Fed] Chair Kevin Warsh's overly patient posture and dial up inflation expectations, amid a monetary policy institution that isn't following through on its hawkish rhetoric at this juncture," said José Torres, senior economist at Interactive Brokers.

## Amazon, Apple chart different paths after earnings

Amazon emerged as a clear winner on Friday, surging 15.3% after the e-commerce and cloud giant reported a second-quarter beat on strong revenue growth in its cloud segment. Amazon also gave soft third-quarter revenue guidance due to Prime Day occurring in June this year, and lifted its full-year capital expenditures budget to $220 billion from $200 billion on higher costs for memory chips.

Bill Birmingham, managing director at REX Financial, said Amazon's quarter was "exceptional," with evidence that the company's custom silicon and AI commercialization are gaining traction. He said the print "provides real evidence that AI capex is being monetized."

Apple, on the other hand, slumped 7.4%, as weak current-quarter revenue guidance overshadowed higher-than-expected fiscal third-quarter earnings and revenue. "We paid more per memory in the March quarter than the December quarter," said Tim Cook on the earnings call, his last as CEO of Apple, adding the company expects to pay even more for memory costs in its September quarter. Argus Research analyst Jim Kelleher reiterated his Buy rating and $375 price target on Apple after earnings.

## Roblox has its worst day ever after earnings

Roblox was one of the worst stocks period, with shares plunging 26.9% — their biggest one-day decline ever — after the video-game company reported a second-quarter bookings miss and forecast lower-than-expected third-quarter bookings, without offering a full-year forecast. Oppenheimer analyst Martin Yang maintained an Outperform rating, saying "retention remains stable and content is diversifying," though it's uncertain when monetization will catch up to retention. Of 34 analysts covering Roblox, 19 rate it a Buy, 13 Hold, and two Sell.
