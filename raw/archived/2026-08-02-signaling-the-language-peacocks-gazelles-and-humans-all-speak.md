# Signaling: The Language Peacocks, Gazelles, and Humans All Speak

Signaling is how we communicate qualities about ourselves through costly actions rather than words. Because "talk is cheap," believable signals require real expenditure of time, energy, or resources.

## Honest vs. Dishonest Signaling

Signals can be genuine or faked. Easy-to-fake signals lose value quickly. Experience is notably hard to counterfeit.

## Countersignaling

This is signaling *by not signaling* — the billionaire eating fast food, the CEO in a hoodie. Those with established status can afford to skip the performance. As Tyler Cowen notes, sharing good news too eagerly can backfire: "reporting good news can make a person look bad."

## Biological Origins

- Peacock tails demonstrate fitness through costly handicaps
- Gazelle "stotting" signals strength to predators
- Aposematism (bright warning colors) signals danger

## Information Asymmetry

Akerlof's "Market for Lemons" shows how poor signaling degrades entire markets. *Screening* is how uninformed parties prompt informed ones to reveal hidden information.

## Conspicuous Consumption

Thorstein Veblen identified how purchases signal status beyond utility. "Veblen goods" perversely gain desirability as prices rise.

## Workplace Signaling

Open offices, packed calendars, and physical presence often signal productivity while undermining actual output.
