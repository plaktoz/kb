# Why the stock market's red-hot momentum trade might be headed for a violent unwind this month

U.S. stocks were set up for potentially dramatic rotations beneath the S&P 500's surface, with rumblings already underway following a stratospheric rise in semiconductor shares. An astounding rally in momentum stocks — captured by the performance of the Invesco S&P 500 Momentum ETF (SPMO) — helped drive major indexes like the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite to their biggest quarterly gain in six years, according to Dow Jones Market Data.

Warren Pies, co-founder and strategist at 3Fourteen Research, said investors could see a "violent rotation" away from the momentum factor in the coming weeks, noting momentum stocks have sold off during each of the past five Julys: "My view is that this is going to be an especially volatile July for the momentum trade." According to 3Fourteen's analysis (calculated as the top 10% of S&P 500 stocks by momentum divided by the bottom 10%), July shows an average five-year return of about negative 5% for the momentum factor.

Pies expects investors taking profits from the chip-stock run-up may find opportunities in oversold "quality factor" stocks — particularly a "disjointed" pocket of beaten-down names with momentum scores in the bottom 10% of the S&P 500 — suggesting incremental buying into such pockets rather than aggressive portfolio rotation. This is directly relevant to momentum-tilted holdings like SPMO and iShares MSCI World Momentum Factor ETF (IWMO), which could see outsized volatility if the rotation materializes.
