# Sleeping longer than 8.5 hours a night may be a sign of Alzheimer's

source_url: https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/sleeping-longer-than-8-5-hours-a-night-may-be-a-sign-of-alzheimers

---

By Katharine Lang — Medical News Today, July 23, 2026

A new study published in *Alzheimer's & Dementia* found that people who slept an average of more than 8.5 hours a night had higher levels of the blood biomarker p-tau181, suggesting long sleep may be an early sign of neurodegenerative change rather than simply a consequence of aging.

Researchers analyzed data from 2,410 participants in the Framingham Heart Study (mean age 70, 55.2% female), all with self-reported sleep duration and at least one of four blood biomarkers (p-tau181, total tau, neurofilament light chain, or GFAP), adjusting for age, APOE e4 status, sex, sleep apnea, depression, and kidney function. Of the cohort, 23.9% reported short sleep (≤6 hours), 61.2% average sleep (6–9 hours), and 14.9% long sleep (>9 hours); those with longer sleep were older and more likely to have depression, use antidepressants, and have dementia. All biomarkers tended to rise with longer sleep, but only p-tau181's increase was statistically significant, following a J-shaped curve — elevated in short sleepers, lowest around average sleep, then rising steadily past 8.5 hours and sharply past 10 hours.

Neurologist Steven Allder (Re:Cognition Health, not involved in the study) told MNT the findings strengthen evidence that sleep changes may reflect the earliest biological processes of Alzheimer's rather than just aging, and noted sleep duration alone says little about sleep quality — someone spending nine or ten hours in bed may have fragmented, non-restorative sleep that impairs the glymphatic system's clearance of amyloid proteins. Corresponding author Vanessa M. Young (UT Health San Antonio) cautioned this is a cross-sectional snapshot, not proof that long sleep causes Alzheimer's — it may instead be an early symptom, since Alzheimer's pathology can begin affecting sleep-regulating brain regions 15–20 years before memory symptoms appear. Allder: "maintaining good sleep hygiene, alongside routine physical activity, cardiovascular risk reduction and cognitive stimulation, remains one of the most sensible strategies for promoting long-term brain health."
