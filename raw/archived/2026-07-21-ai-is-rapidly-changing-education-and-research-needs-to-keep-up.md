# AI is rapidly changing education and research needs to keep up

source_url: https://www.brookings.edu/articles/ai-is-rapidly-changing-education-and-research-needs-to-keep-up

---

By Stacey Alicea (Executive Director, Research Partnership for Professional Learning) and Meghan McCormick (Senior Officer of Research and Impact, Overdeck Family Foundation) | July 21, 2026 | Brookings, Brown Center on Education Policy

Generative AI is already part of everyday classroom practice, with nearly two-thirds of teachers now reporting that they use AI in their work. But the way we evaluate the effectiveness of AI-based tools has not kept pace with adoption.

Conversations about AI in education often start with the right question: Is there evidence of the effects of this technology? Too often, though, the next question gets very narrow: What have we learned from randomized controlled trials (RCTs)? RCTs are hard to come by, so the pursuit of evidence tends to stop there — perhaps explaining why only 1 in 5 edtech products in classrooms have any evidence that they improve teaching and learning outcomes.

RCTs have played essential roles in education research for decades, helping the field build evidence across topics such as class-size reductions, the science of reading, high-impact tutoring, and teacher coaching. But AI is a different type of intervention — developers constantly update models and refine prompts, while users evolve their interactions as they learn what works. AI-based tools are not the stable, well-defined interventions that RCTs were designed to evaluate.

## Where RCTs break down for AI and how implementation R&D can help

RCTs assume a stable intervention applied consistently across contexts. That assumption does not hold for most early-stage AI tools. Implementation research and development (implementation R&D) is better suited to the evidence needs of AI technology — it helps refine how a tool is designed, delivered, and used in real settings before a large-scale evaluation, and provides initial evidence on a product's effects on teaching and learning.

Implementation R&D is not a substitute for RCTs; it is the foundation on which to build research that proves cause and effect. The Institute of Education Sciences (IES) has funded generative AI R&D centers that emphasize iterative development and pilot testing before formal trials. Partnerships such as Leanlab Education and Boston University's EVAL initiative support technology through cycles of real-world testing and refinement.

Because AI tools often capture real-time data on how educators use them, researchers can study which specific design choices are driving changes in real time — for example, an ongoing study of an AI coaching tool from Teaching Lab compares a standard version to one that emphasizes student-centered instruction, enabling faster, more responsive research through iterative A/B testing.

## Guidelines for building evidence for AI tools

Three principles can help the field move beyond an "RCT or nothing" mindset:

1. **Build evidence in stages** — early-stage tools should focus on feasibility and rapid-cycle testing; larger RCTs should come later, once the intervention is stable and there is initial evidence that target outcomes are improving.
2. **Ask how it works before asking whether it works** — before asking whether a tool improves student achievement, ask whether it changes the behaviors it's designed to change.
3. **Let the questions drive the method** — leverage A/B testing and continuous evaluation rather than relying solely on methods designed for static programs.

## Why this matters for decisionmakers

A state investing in AI today cannot wait years for evidence from a study on a version of an intervention that no longer exists. Many organizations reach significant scale without even basic outcome data on teaching and learning. Implementation R&D offers a more practical path to ensuring tools are effective before they are taken to scale.

The Research Partnership for Professional Learning's (RPPL) Shared Measures Toolkit is one example of implementation R&D in practice — RPPL worked with professional learning organizations and researchers to identify the aspects of high-quality instructional materials and curriculum-based professional learning implementation practitioners most needed to understand and improve.

AI tools should be held to high standards. But to make those standards achievable, the education sector must move toward a new paradigm for research focused on producing iterative evidence and proof of impact on the outcomes that matter most.
