# Markets News, July 29, 2026: Dow Closes Down 1,150 Points After Fed Holds Interest Rates Steady; Oil Jumps on Surprise Iran Attack

source_url: https://www.investopedia.com/stock-market-today-dow-jones-s-and-p-500-07292026-12029108

---

By Aaron Rennie, and contributors (Aaron McDade, Kara Greenberg, Taylor Tompkins, Diccon Hyatt, David Marino-Nachison)

Major stock indexes closed sharply lower Wednesday, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average shedding more than 1,150 points to log its worst day in 15 months, after the Federal Reserve kept interest rates intact. Meanwhile, oil prices surged after President Donald Trump threatened Iran for launching a surprise missile attack on U.S. forces.

The blue-chip Dow, tech-focused Nasdaq Composite, and benchmark S&P 500 finished down a respective 2.2%, 1.7%, and 1.5% after the Fed's 9-3 decision to keep rates steady as Chair Kevin Warsh said the central bank would be resolute in getting inflation down to its 2% target. The Dow posted its biggest one-day point decline since the sell-off that followed President Donald Trump's tariff announcements in April 2025. It was the Nasdaq's sixth straight session in the red.

The 10-year Treasury yield, which influences interest rates on a variety of consumer loans including mortgages, was around 4.67% at 4 p.m. ET, up six basis points from yesterday's close and 4.64% immediately before the decision.

"For now, it's likely that market pricing for a hike has simply been pushed forward," Ellen Zentner, Chief Economic Strategist for Morgan Stanley Wealth Management, said in written commentary. "September remains a live meeting, and the incoming inflation data between now and then will be all that matters."

According to the CME Group's FedWatch tool, traders are pricing in a 54% chance of at least a quarter-percentage-point rate hike at the Fed's September meeting, down from 79% before today's meeting.

Oil prices jumped early Wednesday after U.S. Central Command said Tuesday night that Iran had "launched multiple ballistic missiles ... in an attempted surprise attack on U.S. forces based in the Middle East." Trump later told Fox News that "we'll be hitting them hard. They're going to get a beating." U.S. benchmark West Texas Intermediate futures jumped 7.4% to $85.10 a barrel in recent trading, while global benchmark Brent crude futures surged 8.2% to $91.

Microsoft (MSFT) and Meta Platforms (META) shares declined 0.7% and 1.3%, respectively, before they reported results after the bell. The other Magnificent Seven mega-cap tech stocks closed lower—with Alphabet (GOOGL) the exception.

The Roundhill Memory ETF (DRAM) and broader iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) closed down more than 6% and 5%, respectively, after finishing about 9% and 5% lower yesterday. South Korean memory chipmaker SK Hynix (SKHY) stock fell 2.5% and Seagate Technology (STX) shares rose more than 2%.

In other post-earnings moves, GE HealthCare Technologies (GEHC) jumped 12%, Ford Motor (F) rose 2%, Visa (V) added less than 1%, Vertiv Holdings (VRT) dropped 17%, and Procter & Gamble (PG) fell about 2%.

Bitcoin was trading around $63,800, little changed over the past 24 hours. The U.S. dollar index, which tracks the value of the greenback against a basket of foreign currencies, was down 0.5% at 100.90. Gold futures rose 0.8% to $4,070 an ounce.

## Investors Haven't Sold Off Stocks Like This Since COVID

Retail investors on Tuesday recorded the "largest net selling of individual stocks since the Covid crash," according to Vanda Research, as worries about protracted tensions in the Middle East and an AI bubble weighed on sentiment.

Shares of Micron Technology (MU), Sandisk (SNDK), Seagate (STX) and Western Digital (WDC) accounted for the lion's share of yesterday's retail sell-off, according to Vanda, though hard-drive maker Seagate's stock was up some 6% in recent trading after a better-than-expected earnings report. Western Digital (WDC) was up some 4%.

Shares of all four stocks remain among the S&P 500's top performers for 2026 so far, with Sandisk more than quadrupling year-to-date. Seagate, Western Digital, and Micron have nearly tripled.

## Tech Earnings Today: Meta, Microsoft Set to Report Latest Results

Investors are awaiting the latest quarterly earnings—as well as their outlooks and other details—from Microsoft (MSFT) and Meta Platforms (META), both due after the close today. Tech shares have been under pressure lately, with the Magnificent 7 as a group, as measured by the Roundhill Magnificent 7 ETF (MAGS), down slightly this year while the S&P 500 has risen.

Today's results are part of a busy stretch for the companies that are among the top funders of the AI buildout—and the biggest publicly traded businesses on Earth. Two more U.S. tech stalwarts, Apple (AAPL) and Amazon.com (AMZN), are due to announce their results after the close Thursday.

Alphabet (GOOG, GOOGL) reported last week, turning in numbers that exceeded Wall Street's expectations. Tesla's (TSLA) earnings are also in, with profits that were lower than expected and revenue that came in stronger than predicted. Its sort-of-sister company SpaceX (SPCX) will deliver its first quarterly results as a public company next week. Nvidia (NVDA) will round out the Magnificent 7's second-quarter earnings in late August.

## Lennox International Leads S&P 500 Industrials Sector Lower

The S&P 500 Industrials Sector is the worst performer of the 11 industries tracked by the benchmark index. Lennox International (LII) shares tumbled 20%, the worst individual performance in the S&P 500. Industrials shares are down some 2.2%, more than twice the decline of any other sector. Five of the 11 sectors are in negative territory.

## Procter & Gamble's Stock Drops After Sales Miss Wall Street's Projections

Shares of Procter & Gamble (PG) were down 3% in recent trading after the maker of Tide, Old Spice, Pampers, and dozens of other consumer brands posted fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $21.20 billion, slightly below the $21.22 billion analysts had expected. The company's adjusted earnings per share of $1.43 was 2 cents ahead of expectations.

CEO Shailesh Jejurikar, who took over Procter & Gamble at the start of the year, pointed to a "very challenging geopolitical and economic environment" for the results.

Procter & Gamble said it now expects sales to rise 1% to 3% in fiscal 2027, with flat to 3% growth in adjusted EPS. The company also said that in the upcoming fiscal year, it will take on the remainder of the $1 billion to $1.6 billion in restructuring costs under a plan announced last year that included layoffs of about 7,000 employees.

Wednesday's decline erased much of Procter & Gamble's stock gains for the year, leaving it up just 1% for 2026 so far.

## GE HealthCare Stock Leads S&P 500 and Nasdaq Gainers After Strong Earnings

Shares of GE HealthCare (GEHC) were up about 12%, one of the best performers in the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Wednesday. The company reported second-quarter revenue of $5.3 billion, up about 6% year-over-year and just above the $5.27 billion analyst consensus. Adjusted earnings per share came in at $1.13, up from $1.06 a year ago.

The former General Electric division said its profits were boosted by tariff refunds, though the company said it doesn't expect to see any more refunds this year. GE HealthCare affirmed its full-year forecasts of organic revenue growth of 3% to 4% and adjusted EPS of $4.80 to $5.00.

Even with GE HealthCare's post-earnings rally, the stock is still down more than 10% since the start of the year.

## VF Corp. Stock Nosedives After Poor Vans Performance, CFO Departure

Shares of VF (VFC), the parent company of outdoor and active brands including The North Face and Timberland, sank 17% Wednesday after it reported a bigger loss and Vans sales decline than analysts had expected, and announced the departure of its finance chief.

VF registered Q1 revenue that fell 5% year-over-year to $1.67 billion, while analysts had called for $1.64 billion. The company raised its fiscal 2027 revenue outlook to 2% or better growth on a constant-currency basis. However, VF posted an adjusted loss of $0.27 per share, wider than analysts' consensus forecast of $0.23 per share. Vans revenue fell 8% from last year.

VF also announced that Paul Vogel will cease serving as CFO effective Aug. 1, when COO Abhishek Dalmia will take over those roles.

## Teradyne Stock Soars After Profit, Revenue Easily Top Analysts' Estimates

Shares of Teradyne (TER) jumped 8% after the automated test equipment and advanced robotics systems firm reported second-quarter results that easily topped analysts' estimates: adjusted earnings of $2.47 per share on revenue that more than doubled year-over-year to $1.32 billion, versus expectations of $2.05 per share and $1.22 billion.

The company expects third-quarter adjusted EPS of $1.85 to $2.15 on revenue of $1.20 billion to $1.30 billion. "Our Q3 guidance reflects robust AI-related demand," CEO Greg Smith said. "Looking further ahead, rapid increase in wafer fab equipment investment sets the stage for continued growth in 2027 and beyond."

## Ford Stock Jumps on Solid Earnings, Outlook

Ford (F) shares surged after the automaker reported a higher-than-expected profit and lifted its full-year forecast. The company generated $48.3 billion in revenue in the second quarter, more than $1 billion shy of the analyst consensus, but its adjusted earnings of 42 cents per share topped estimates.

Ford also raised its full-year adjusted earnings before interest and taxes (EBIT) and adjusted free cash flow outlooks, in part because of tariff refunds the company expects to get from the federal government.

## Apple Set to Report Earnings Thursday

Apple is set to report earnings after the closing bell Thursday, with traders expecting the iPhone maker's stock could extend its record-setting rally following the results. Based on current options pricing, Apple (AAPL) shares are seen swinging up to about 4% in either direction by the end of the week.

After overtaking Nvidia (NVDA) as the world's most valuable company and watching its market capitalization briefly cross the $5 trillion threshold, Apple saw its shares finish Tuesday's session 1% higher at a closing record of $340. Morgan Stanley analysts lifted their price target to $364 from $360.
