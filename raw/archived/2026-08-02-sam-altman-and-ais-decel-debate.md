# Sam Altman and AI's decel debate

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman recently said that it may be time to "pace the rate of AI development" so that society can "harden around some of these new capability levels."

On the latest episode of TechCrunch's Equity podcast, Kirsten Korosec, Sean O'Kane, and Anthony Ha discussed how Altman's comments were probably prompted by a recent hack in which an OpenAI agent breached Hugging Face's systems. Sean noted that while a hack performed by an AI agent is novel, the hack itself was not "some new advanced thing."

"It was more like Nixon's people breaking into Watergate than some real stealthy cyber-op, because it didn't need to be, and it wasn't instructed to be," Sean said. "Hopefully, this is a sign that these companies will take this forward and be more careful about that stuff."

Altman's comments also raised questions about the usefulness of the whole accelerationist versus decelerationist framing, since it "kind of suggests that there's only one path" and "all we get to decide — inasmuch as we get to decide at all — is, do we speed up or do we slow down?"

Kirsten Korosec noted that although Altman was careful with his words, OpenAI and Anthropic did support a petition that reflects the same caution he expressed. She said much of this was likely triggered by the Hugging Face breach, which "probably spooked him and certainly a lot of people in the industry." The hard question is how OpenAI can thread the needle between continuing to generate revenue, raise money, or pursue a successful IPO, while also "pacing" development.

The panel also discussed how the specific hack, while caused by an OpenAI model, stemmed largely from the testing site not being properly secured — the model should not have been able to get online at all. This raises the stakes of human error: a more powerful, misaligned AI model would make similar mistakes far more dangerous.

On the IPO angle, Sean noted it may be strategically smart for Altman to publicly advocate pacing development now, since OpenAI is not close to going to market — he has floated 2027 for an IPO and has only filed confidentially to keep the option open. Anthropic, by contrast, is already in conversations with bankers ahead of a nearer-term IPO, which constrains what it can say publicly about slowing down.
