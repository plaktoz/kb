# Microsoft signals AI bet is paying off with blowout Azure growth

Microsoft's fiscal Q4 results (quarter ended June 30) surpassed Wall Street's cloud growth estimates and beat forecasts, sending shares up more than 8% in extended trading. Azure revenue grew 43% in the quarter, and Microsoft projected 45% constant-currency growth for the next quarter, above the 40.92% analysts expected; overall revenue guidance landed at a $90.4 billion midpoint versus $89.66 billion consensus. The results came days after Google Cloud reported 82% revenue growth, which had fueled concern about market-share erosion — concern the Azure numbers largely dispelled. Microsoft remains the No. 2 cloud infrastructure provider behind AWS.

## Strategic shift and capex

CEO Satya Nadella described a shift away from Microsoft's earlier heavy reliance on OpenAI toward designing its own models alongside custom silicon, saying the approach is delivering efficiency gains of up to 40%: "That's really the enterprise design architecture that we are going to evangelize. We ourselves are using it." Microsoft also changed how it accounts for data center leases — spreading them over 25 years instead of 15 — which mechanically lowered reported capex without changing underlying spending. The company still guided to $50 billion capex for fiscal Q1 2027 and $175 billion for calendar 2026, both below prior Wall Street and company estimates. Q2 capex hit $41 billion, up more than 70% year-over-year but slightly below the $42.37 billion consensus; free cash flow was $19.6 billion, well above the $13.44 billion estimate though down 23% year-over-year.

## Demand signals

Microsoft's SEC filing disclosed $329.1 billion in data center leases not yet commenced, spanning fiscal 2027 through 2033. Cloud contracted backlog reached $678 billion, up from $627 billion, with the roughly $50 billion sequential increase coming entirely from companies outside the leading US AI model makers — signaling broad-based enterprise adoption rather than concentration among a few hyperscalers. Microsoft 365 Copilot paid seats surpassed 30 million, up from 20 million the prior quarter and ahead of the 26.9 million analysts forecast.

## Full-year FY2026 results

Revenue was $331.8 billion, up 18% (16% constant currency). Operating income was $155.2 billion, up 21%. Net income was $133.7 billion, up 31% GAAP. Diluted EPS was $17.95, up 32% GAAP.
