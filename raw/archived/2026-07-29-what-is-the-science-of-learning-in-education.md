# What Is the Science of Learning in Education?

Posted on July 29, 2026 by College Communications

The science of learning draws on cognitive and neuroscience research to improve teaching and leadership. This article explains what it studies and why it matters.

Every day, educators make decisions that influence how students learn. They choose instructional strategies, evaluate curriculum materials, design interventions, and determine how to support learners with different strengths and needs. Yet many educational practices have historically been shaped by tradition, intuition, or popular trends rather than research about how learning actually works.

The science of learning seeks to change that. Drawing from cognitive science, neuroscience, psychology, and education, this growing field provides evidence-based insights into how people learn, retain information, and develop new skills. Initiatives such as the Learning Policy Institute's Science of Learning and Development have helped translate decades of research into practical guidance for educators and school leaders.

## What is the science of learning?

The science of learning is an interdisciplinary field that studies how people learn. Rather than relying on educational trends or long-held assumptions, it draws from research across multiple disciplines — including cognitive science, neuroscience, developmental psychology, and education — to better understand how knowledge is acquired, retained, and applied.

At its core, the science of learning asks a simple question: What does research tell us about effective teaching and learning?

Decades of research have helped educators better understand:
- How memory supports learning
- How attention affects student performance
- What motivates learners to persist
- How relationships influence academic growth
- Which instructional practices are supported by evidence

## Why are more schools embracing evidence-based instruction?

### The science of reading

Perhaps the most visible example is the science of reading movement. Research on how children learn to read has led many states and districts to adopt structured literacy approaches that emphasize oral language development, phonemic awareness, phonics, vocabulary, fluency, and comprehension.

### Rethinking learning styles

For years, many educators believed students learned best when instruction matched a preferred learning style, such as visual, auditory, or kinesthetic learning. Research has challenged this assumption. Instructional methods should align with research-supported practices, such as explicit and systematic instruction with intentional practice opportunities, rather than tailoring instruction to a "learning style."

### Retrieval practice and spaced learning

Research has shown that students retain information more effectively when they are required to retrieve knowledge over time rather than repeatedly reviewing the same material. Strategies such as low-stakes quizzes, cumulative review, and spaced repetition can strengthen long-term retention and support deeper understanding.

### Supporting the whole learner

Learning is influenced by more than academics alone. Research increasingly points to the importance of social, emotional, and developmental factors in student success, recognizing the connections between academic achievement, well-being, relationships, and belonging.

## Why does the science of learning matter for education leaders?

School leaders make decisions that affect instructional quality across entire classrooms, schools, and districts — curriculum adoption, professional development, intervention systems, assessment practices, school improvement initiatives, and resource allocation. The science of learning provides a framework for evaluating whether an initiative is likely to improve student outcomes, instead of relying on trends, assumptions, or vendor claims.

## What does evidence-based leadership look like in practice?

Education leaders grounded in learning science often ask: What research supports this initiative? Has the evidence been replicated? How will success be measured? What implementation support is needed? Does this align with what we know about how students learn?

Sustainable improvement requires ongoing learning, thoughtful implementation, and a commitment to continuous evaluation.

## How does the science of learning promote educational opportunity?

The science of learning helps educators create more equitable learning environments by aligning instruction with research about how students learn best. Research-informed instruction can help educators identify student needs earlier, choose evidence-based interventions, support language and literacy development, reduce gaps in access to strong reading instruction, and evaluate whether systems are working for all learners.

This is especially important in literacy. Students who do not receive effective reading instruction early often face barriers across subjects and grade levels.

## Leading with evidence and purpose

The science of learning is ultimately about helping educators make better decisions on behalf of students. By understanding how people learn, leaders can evaluate practices more effectively, support teachers more intentionally, and create learning environments where students can thrive.
