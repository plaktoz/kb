# Is it time to double your protein intake? Experts weigh in on longevity benefits

source_url: https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/time-double-protein-intake-experts-weigh-longevity-benefits

---

By Yasemin Nicola Sakay — Medical News Today, July 26, 2026

A perspective paper published June 16, 2026 in *Frontiers in Nutrition* argues current official protein-intake guidance focuses on preventing deficiency, not maximizing long-term health. Chris Macdonald, PhD, of the University of Cambridge, draws attention to the fact certain groups — physically active adults, older adults, and pregnant people — may benefit from protein intakes up to roughly twice the current baseline recommendation of 0.75–0.8 g/kg/day (about 0.34–0.36 g/lb), reaching up to 1 g/lb of body weight. Benefits cited include faster muscle recovery, better weight management and fat loss, and reduced fracture risk.

Two dietitians gave Medical News Today more grounded, individualized targets. Michelle Routhenstein (EntirelyNourished) said the current RDA "was established to prevent protein deficiency and not necessarily to optimize muscle health, healthy aging, or cardiometabolic resilience," and recommends a practical range of about 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day depending on exercise level for preserving lean muscle and metabolic health. Kristin Kirkpatrick (Cleveland Clinic) agreed guidelines are a good starting point but not personalized — protein needs vary by age, activity, health status, and goals, and can usually be met through whole foods (eggs, Greek yogurt, chicken, fish, beans, lentils, cottage cheese) spread across meals.

Both cautioned against protein tunnel vision: over-focusing on protein can crowd out fiber, vitamin C, potassium, magnesium, and folate, so it should sit within a full, heart-healthy dietary pattern. Routhenstein flagged an upper safe limit of roughly 2 g/kg/day for healthy adults with normal kidney function, with people who have chronic kidney disease needing individualized, lower targets, since excess protein can accelerate disease progression. High intakes of red and processed meat specifically, rather than protein generally, are linked to elevated TMAO and cardiovascular risk, and animal-protein-heavy, fiber-poor diets can disrupt the gut microbiome. Kirkpatrick added that any longevity plan built on protein alone, without resistance training and regular physical activity, is "missing one of its most critical pieces."
