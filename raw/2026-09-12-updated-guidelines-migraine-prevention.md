# Migraine: New Guidelines Expand Options for Preventing Episodes

**Author:** Peter Morales-Brown
**Date:** September 8, 2026
**Fact-checked by:** Jennifer Chesak, MSJ
**Source:** Medical News Today
**Source URL:** https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/updated-guidelines-offer-adults-more-options-preventing-migraine

---

## Overview

The American Academy of Neurology (AAN) and American Headache Society (AHS) have jointly updated their migraine prevention guidelines, replacing the previous 2012 recommendations. The updated guidance appears in *Neurology* and *Headache* journals and is also endorsed by the American Academy of Family Physicians.

Migraine affects roughly 12–15% of Americans (~40 million people), causing severe head pain, nausea, light/sound sensitivity, dizziness, and cognitive difficulties lasting hours to days.

---

## Who Should Consider Preventive Treatment

Clinicians are advised to discuss preventive therapy with adults who have:

- 4+ migraine days per month
- 4+ moderate-to-severe headache days per month
- Migraine that significantly disrupts work or daily life

**Chronic migraine** is defined as headaches on 15+ days/month for over 3 months, with migraine features on at least 8 of those days.

---

## Key Changes from 2012 Guidelines

The major update reflects availability of newer therapies — particularly those targeting the **calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) pathway** — that didn't exist when previous guidelines were issued. The new framework covers both episodic and chronic migraine, accounts for medication overuse, and addresses special populations including pregnant individuals and those with comorbidities.

AHS President Matthew Robbins noted the guideline "stratifies recommendations by episodic and chronic migraine, the presence of acute medication overuse, special populations."

---

## Individualized Treatment Approach

Rather than a one-size-fits-all protocol, clinicians are encouraged to weigh:

- Strength of evidence for a given medication
- Side effects and tolerability
- Cost and accessibility
- Method of administration (oral daily vs. injectable monthly/quarterly)
- Coexisting health conditions
- Impact on quality of life

---

## Monitoring and Follow-Up

The guideline recommends reassessing patients after starting treatment within specified timeframes. Keeping a **headache diary** is encouraged to help clinicians evaluate whether a therapy is working.

Robbins described once-per-week tracking as "a very patient-centered, easy-to-encapsulate recommendation," noting it marks a key threshold for migraine-related disability risk.

---

## Broader Implications

Robbins emphasized that "migraine is woefully undertreated and millions of people in the U.S. who deserve better care...do not receive it." The updated guidelines aim to close that gap by expanding evidence-based options.

Anyone considering changes to migraine medication should consult a healthcare professional first.
