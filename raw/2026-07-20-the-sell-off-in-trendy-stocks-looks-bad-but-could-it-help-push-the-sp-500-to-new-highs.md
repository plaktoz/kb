# The Sell-Off in Trendy Stocks Looks Bad—But Could It Help Push the S&P 500 to New Highs?

source_url: https://www.investopedia.com/could-sell-off-in-trendy-stock-propel-s-and-p-500-to-new-highs-12022534

---

By Crystal Kim, Investopedia — Published July 20, 2026

"Buy high, and sell higher" used to be a working investment strategy, until it wasn't. The momentum trade — buying recent winners — has unwound, with the sell-off intensifying around chip stocks. The iShares MSCI USA Momentum Factor ETF (MTUM) declined 12% since the start of July through the most recent Friday's close, while the S&P 500 was roughly flat over the same period.

"Painful volatility in popular AI infrastructure stocks has renewed investor interest [in] investment themes outside of AI," Goldman Sachs' Ben Snider wrote, observing that the momentum factor has erased gains since the end of April as its volatility surged to the highest level of its 45-year history excluding recession years.

## Why this matters

Though the decline in high-flying chip stocks might worry investors about the AI trade and the broader market, strategists think the S&P 500 could go higher on the back of less trendy names. Snider said "history, positioning, and lack of a favorable catalyst point to continued near-term challenges" for the AI infrastructure momentum trade specifically.

Morgan Stanley's "broadening" thesis — that the S&P 500 will be driven higher by more than the AI trade — is playing out, according to equity strategist Michael Wilson: consumer discretionary and transports have outpaced the S&P 500 by 12% over the past two months as earnings-per-share estimates get raised in those areas. The broadening also shows in the Invesco S&P 500 Equal Weight ETF (RSP), up 11% year-to-date versus the S&P's 9%.

If the decline in AI stocks spills over into other categories, the S&P could move down to 7000, Wilson said — but after that, the bull market returns "in earnest," with the firm's year-end target of 8000 remaining "very achievable," implying about 7% upside from recent levels.
