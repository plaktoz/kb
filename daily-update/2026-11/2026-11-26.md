## 🧠 Emerging Connections
**The handoff tax is what Claude Ship tries to automate away.** Supercut's research finds workers spend about 30% of their week briefing and reviewing agents, with background knowledge the hardest thing to pass on ([AI agent handoff tax](../../wiki/productivity/ai-agent-handoff-tax-12-hours-week-briefing-supercut-uctoday.md)). Anthropic's [Claude Ship](../../wiki/technology/claude-ship-ai-agents-review-fix-approve-code-changes.md) aims at the same bottleneck from the other side, letting agents review and approve routine code changes under team-written policies. Both point to the same constraint: the scarce resource is no longer generating output but capturing the context that lets someone else, human or agent, trust it. The edcircuit tutoring note ([AI tutoring](../../wiki/learning/ai-tutoring-when-does-it-help-students-learn-edcircuit.md)) shows the human-side risk: assisted performance can rise while unassisted skill falls.
