## ⚡ The Big Picture (TL;DR)
Today's notes keep returning to one question: **is the measure showing real value, or just a convenient proxy?** AI tutors lift homework grades while unaided exam scores fall, a brain-blood-flow result doesn't become a cognitive gain, and a 32% stock drop gets read as a buy without checking the cash-flow cost. Finance dominates the batch, with the Fed's likely December hike shaping most of the market reads.

### 🧠 Learning
- **[AI makes kids lazier or sharper, depending on the task](../../wiki/learning/ai-making-kids-lazier-or-smarter-luckin-uk-evidence.md)**: Rose Luckin argues AI weakens learning when it does the thinking and strengthens it when students must explain their reasoning. A China study of 26,000+ students found homework improved while unaided exam scores fell up to 24%, which makes "think first, then challenge the AI" a practical rule.
- **[Better grades are not better learning](../../wiki/learning/arthur-d-little-ai-gcc-schools-teachers-students-report-nov2026.md)**: An Arthur D. Little report on GCC schools says AI raised grades 48%, but students scored 17% worse once it was withdrawn. Assessment has to test mastery independent of the tool, the same proxy trap that [[Goodhart's Law]] describes.
- **[Tutors should scaffold, not answer](../../wiki/learning/penn-state-alaaska-ai-tutor-reasoning-gaps-nsf-grant.md)**: Penn State's ALAASKA tutor returns hints and knowledge checks instead of solved answers. An early look found 21 of 119 chat interactions showed reasoning missing from the submitted work, and an NSF-funded comparison is now testing it. The analysis is early and not peer reviewed.

### ❤️ Health
- **[Blood flow is not a brain benefit](../../wiki/health/urolithin-a-brain-blood-flow-memory-task-obesity-trial.md)**: A University of Oklahoma trial found urolithin A (1,000 mg daily for four weeks) raised brain blood flow during a memory task in adults with obesity. Memory and attention did not improve, a useful reminder that a physiological marker is not a functional outcome.
- **[Childhood diet tracks into adolescence](../../wiki/health/childhood-diet-quality-tracks-adolescence-metabolic-health-helix.md)**: The HELIX study of 1,663 mother-child pairs found childhood diet quality predicted adolescent metabolic risk more strongly than diet during pregnancy. The design is observational and the questionnaires were unvalidated, so read it as an association.
- **[Eating disorders go unseen in pregnancy](../../wiki/health/perinatal-eating-disorders-screening-treatment-gaps-stat-nov27-2026.md)**: About 4.3% of pregnant women and over 9% of new mothers have eating disorders, yet ACOG guidance omits them and coverage is thin. Screening only pays off when treatment capacity exists to act on it.

### 🎯 Strategy

Secondary data becomes a depreciating asset, so the question is where your unique informational advantage comes from.

The excerpt covers only the first of four scenarios.

## 🧠 Emerging Connections
Urolithin A's blood-flow result and Arthur D. Little's grade gains are the same mistake in different clothes: both report a measure that moves without the outcome moving. The [urolithin trial](../../wiki/health/urolithin-a-brain-blood-flow-memory-task-obesity-trial.md) shows it in the brain, and the [AI-in-schools report](../../wiki/learning/arthur-d-little-ai-gcc-schools-teachers-students-report-nov2026.md) shows it in exam results. Checking the outcome you actually care about is the common fix.
