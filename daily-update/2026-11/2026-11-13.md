### ❤️ Health
- **[GLP-1s and cancer: lower rates against some drugs, not all](../../wiki/health/glp1-cancer-risk-359k-observational-study.md)**: Across 359,595 adults with type 2 diabetes, GLP-1 starters had lower obesity-related cancer rates than insulin or sulfonylurea starters but not metformin, SGLT2 or DPP-4 users. Observational, with confounding by disease severity, so it doesn't show prevention.
- **[Continuing antidepressants in pregnancy tied to lower postpartum depression risk](../../wiki/health/antidepressant-continuation-pregnancy-lower-postpartum-depression-risk.md)**: In 6,552 Kaiser Permanente patients, stopping medication carried a 14% higher adjusted risk, and 78% higher for those with early symptoms who stopped. Framed as input to shared decision-making.
- **[Warmer-than-usual bedrooms nudge sleep and next-day focus down](../../wiki/health/warm-bedroom-nights-poorer-sleep-lower-next-day-productivity.md)**: Over 1,189 nights, each extra degree Celsius cost about 0.5 sleep-score points and 0.04 productivity points on a five-point scale. Tiny, self-reported and not shown to run through sleep, so a hint rather than a prescription.
- **[Pilot study links long-term vaping to FEV1 loss](../../wiki/health/long-term-vaping-persistent-lung-function-decline-pilot-study.md)**: Among 22 young adults, FEV1 fell 100 to 240 ml with more daily use, though the dose trend wasn't statistically significant. Small, no tobacco-free control group, so preliminary.

## 🧠 Emerging Connections
**Three health notes repeat a caution the vault already holds.** Today's [GLP-1 cancer study](../../wiki/health/glp1-cancer-risk-359k-observational-study.md) (confounding by disease severity) and the [warm-bedroom sleep study](../../wiki/health/warm-bedroom-nights-poorer-sleep-lower-next-day-productivity.md) (each link tested separately, sleep as the route unproven) both stop short of cause, as the [vaping pilot](../../wiki/health/long-term-vaping-persistent-lung-function-decline-pilot-study.md) does with its non-significant dose trend. That is the same discipline as the earlier note on [AI users' longer workdays](../../wiki/productivity/ai-users-longer-workdays-association-not-causation-tech-insider.md), where the link was real but reverse or third-factor explanations fit equally well. Across domains, the same question applies: did the exposure change the outcome, or did the people who got the exposure differ to begin with?
