### 🧠 Learning
- **[ChatGPT boosted practice scores and hurt test scores](../../wiki/learning/students-using-chatgpt-did-worse-on-math-tests-turkey.md)**: In a Penn experiment with ~1,000 Turkish high schoolers, plain ChatGPT raised correct practice answers by 48%, then **cut test scores by 17%**. Students who practiced alone performed the same on practice and tests. This is the canonical evidence for **cognitive offloading** as a learning risk.
- **[Simulating students to train better AI tutors](../../wiki/learning/studentsim-personalized-student-simulators-ai-tutoring.md)**: Microsoft Research's StudentSim builds per-student simulators. They're scored on **behavioral fidelity** (acting like that student) and **guidance responsiveness** (improving after help), two dimensions grounded in Vygotsky's zone of proximal development. Used as an RL reward model, it lifted tutor accuracy to 90.5%. It explains why role-play LLM "students" give falsely flattering feedback.

## 🧠 Emerging Connections
**Activity is not outcome, whether in a math class or an inbox.** The [ChatGPT math experiment](../../wiki/learning/students-using-chatgpt-did-worse-on-math-tests-turkey.md) found AI help inflated practice scores (+48%) while test performance fell (−17%). The appearance of progress replaced the learning itself. The [digital-habits note](../../wiki/productivity/workday-focus-understanding-digital-habits.md) makes the same diagnosis at work: **busy ≠ focused**, and the [Hubstaff data](../../wiki/productivity/hubstaff-time-management-statistics-2026-workplace.md) estimates 51% of the workday goes to low-value tasks that feel like work. [StudentSim](../../wiki/learning/studentsim-personalized-student-simulators-ai-tutoring.md) shows the same fallacy one level up: simulators that are too capable make tutors look effective when they aren't. In each case the fix is the same. **Measure performance where the support is removed** (the unaided test, the shipped priority, the real student) instead of the assisted, visible activity.
