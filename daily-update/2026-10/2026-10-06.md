### ⚡ Productivity
- **[AI makes workers faster and reviewers busier](../../wiki/productivity/ai-makes-workers-faster-reviewers-busier-measure-handoffs.md)**: Linthicum argues enterprises measure the **person using AI** rather than the **system absorbing its output**, missing the verification and exception work pushed to legal, QA and senior staff. His practical metric is **"what happened at the next handoff?"**: acceptance without material correction, rework, escalations and queue time.
- **[AI's work benefits go to those who already have good jobs](../../wiki/productivity/gallup-ajqs-ai-benefits-unevenly-distributed-2026.md)**: In Gallup's American Job Quality Study, AI users mostly report gains in **speed (63%) and creativity (56%)**. Regular use, and even the benefits among users, skew toward graduates, managers, younger and higher-paid workers, which is key evidence for the debate over whether AI narrows or widens job-quality gaps.
- **[NHS productivity: fewer targets, more trust](../../wiki/productivity/nhs-productivity-commission-breaking-the-cycle.md)**: The NHS Productivity Commission's *Breaking the cycle* report makes **10 implementable recommendations**, diagnosing a low-trust, target-heavy culture as the main barrier rather than any shortage of capable staff. **Devolved accountability**, trusting teams instead of multiplying metrics, is the hardest shift and the one the commission sees as central.

### ❤️ Health
- **[Heat therapy as a depression treatment](../../wiki/health/heat-therapy-sauna-mental-health-depression-research.md)**: In a small randomized trial, **whole-body heating halved depression symptoms within a week** for most treated patients, with effects lasting six weeks. One proposed mechanism is a **"thermoregulatory workout"** that corrects the impaired cooling seen in depression, though the evidence is still preliminary.
- **[GLP-1 use in kids aged 8–11 jumps from 0.3% to 9.3%](../../wiki/health/glp1-use-children-8-11-pediatrics-study.md)**: A *Pediatrics* study of 3.5M+ children reports the sharp rise among non-diabetic children since 2019. Specialists frame **pediatric obesity as a chronic, multi-organ disease** but insist medication follow thorough evaluation and intensive lifestyle treatment, with close monitoring of growth, puberty and mental health.
- **[Long-term Food Is Medicine programs show dose-dependent gains](../../wiki/health/food-is-medicine-long-term-plant-based-groceries-cardiometabolic.md)**: Adults receiving weekly plant-based groceries for an average of **2.7 years** showed **dose-dependent drops** in diastolic blood pressure, weight and BMI. Most prior research covered only weeks to months, so this supports the authors' call for **"high-potency"** programs: frequent, high-quality food delivered over long periods.

## 🧠 Emerging Connections
**The NHS and the AI-enabled enterprise share the same bottleneck.** [Linthicum's AI-handoff argument](../../wiki/productivity/ai-makes-workers-faster-reviewers-busier-measure-handoffs.md) and the [NHS Productivity Commission](../../wiki/productivity/nhs-productivity-commission-breaking-the-cycle.md) come from entirely different worlds, yet both rest on the **Theory of Constraints**. Speeding up AI generation only grows the queue at review and verification, just as bottlenecks elsewhere in the health system force extra resources into A&E. Both also blame **measurement at the wrong level**: counting AI-assisted work products in one case, a proliferation of targets in the other, either of which can show a local win while the system absorbs the cost. The shared lesson for any productivity program is to **measure the flow at the constraint, not the effort at any single point**. It echoes the vault's earlier warning in [empty calendars and fake work](../../wiki/productivity/empty-calendar-fake-work-constraints.md) that "AI will help you do the wrong things faster."
