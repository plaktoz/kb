### ⚡ Productivity
- **[AI gives time back, and work expands to fill it](../../wiki/productivity/jabra-worklife-2026-ai-productivity-paradox.md)**: In Jabra's survey of 6,000+ workers, 85% report *more* work in at least one area. Frequent AI users collaborate far more (64% vs. 25%). The gains concentrate among senior leaders and "superusers," and an **AI-capable manager makes daily AI use 2.4x likelier**. This is the productivity trap from SAP's research, now with numbers.
- **[Presenteeism costs 54 days a year; absence data misses most of it](../../wiki/productivity/presenteeism-absence-data-misses-most-health-productivity-loss.md)**: Vitality/RAND estimates 63.4 days lost per US worker each year to poor health, and **85% of that happens at work**. Absence records capture only about one lost day in seven. Workers with caring managers lose 9 fewer days. Note the source caveat: Vitality sells health programs.
- **[Workday: leaders expect AI to boost output, not cut headcount](../../wiki/productivity/workday-2026-global-workforce-report-ai-productivity-over-cuts.md)**: 40% of leaders expect higher output from existing staff and 28% expect headcount cuts. Demand for basic AI skills has fallen 25% from its January peak while **applied AI-building skills are up 51%**. Internal mobility has stalled: "Employees may not be changing jobs, but their jobs are changing around them."

### 🧠 Learning
- **[LLMs can't measure reading levels](../../wiki/learning/llms-inaccurate-lexile-text-complexity-measurement.md)**: Across 20,000+ analyses, only **3% of LLM Lexile estimates matched official measures**. One passage's estimates spanned 3–4 grade levels. The lesson generalizes: next-token prediction produces plausible numbers, not validated measurements. Errors compound when you then ask the model to rewrite text "to level."
- **[Principals: "people over paperwork"](../../wiki/learning/principals-using-ai-absenteeism-teacher-goals-student-tracking.md)**: School leaders are building no-code AI tools: an earn-back absenteeism tracker, prompts for measurable teacher goals, and needs trackers for teachers with 90+ online students. Each comes with explicit **student-data guardrails**. Practical examples of AI offloading clerical work so staff have more time with students.
- **[TED and Google want educators leading the AI conversation](../../wiki/learning/ted-google-educator-ai-ideas-initiative-ted2027.md)**: A free Idea Workshop leads to proposals due Nov. 15, and five educators will speak at TED2027. It deliberately contrasts with the $23M AFT, Microsoft and OpenAI program that trains educators. TED's framing: **outsider-led AI-in-education debate is "backwards."**

### ❤️ Health
- **[Retatrutide breaks the 15% weight-loss ceiling](../../wiki/health/retatrutide-triumph-2-obesity-type-2-diabetes-phase-3.md)**: In the Phase III TRIUMPH-2 trial, Lilly's triple agonist (GLP-1, GIP and glucagon) produced up to **18.8% weight loss** in people with type 2 diabetes, and nearly a third lost ≥25%. Semaglutide and tirzepatide rarely exceed 15% in this group. The glucagon pathway may explain the difference, though GI side effects are substantial.
- **[Schizophrenia gut microbes change mouse behavior](../../wiki/health/schizophrenia-gut-microbes-mouse-behavior-immune-axis.md)**: Mice that received stool from people with schizophrenia showed working-memory and social deficits, gut inflammation and reactive prefrontal microglia. It's early **gut-brain-immune axis** evidence: tiny samples and no proven causation, but a testable pathway.
