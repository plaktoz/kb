The AI-tutoring piece shows struggling students were never limited by content availability — stability, motivation, and accountability were the binding constraint, so pouring in more AI-delivered content optimizes a variable that wasn't broken. The **[PwC workload survey](../../wiki/productivity/three-in-10-workers-workload-productivity-pwc-survey.md)** makes the identical argument for the workplace: only 13% of workers blame a skills gap for lost productivity, while 28% point to workload and job design. Both notes independently invoke Theory-of-Constraints logic — education and management keep reaching for the same wrong lever (more content, more training) instead of fixing the actual bottleneck (accountability, job design).
