## ⚡ The Big Picture (TL;DR)
The AI build-out is now **big enough to move the bond market**: tech firms' data-center debt is crowding into a shrinking pool of savings, and the AI trade lurches on a single OpenAI revenue number. At the same time, Amazon and Nvidia are buying their way out of single-layer dependence. On the people side, the same lesson keeps coming up: **mandate, measurement and environment design** decide outcomes. Owning the program, raw willpower and efficiency metrics alone don't.

Only ~1 in 5 hypertensive adults is currently controlled. The result is preliminary, but it frames prevention as risk prevalence × coverage gap.

It's a useful caution that risk profiles don't automatically carry over between disease subtypes.
- **[Radon's reach beyond the lungs](../../wiki/health/radon-exposure-brain-cancer-risk-womens-health-initiative.md)**: In 159,391 postmenopausal women, the absolute risk of malignant CNS cancer rose with residential radon concentration. Actionable takeaway: **cheap passive detectors** and basement ventilation, with the EPA's 4 pCi/L action threshold.

## 🧠 Emerging Connections
**AI capex is now a bond-market variable, and that feeds back into AI equities.** UHERO's [bond sell-off analysis](../../wiki/finance/global-bond-selloff-2026-causes-consequences-hawaii-uhero.md) names tech firms' AI data-center bond sales (more than doubled to $200B+) as one force **crowding out** a finite pool of savings and pushing real yields up. Read alongside [Alphabet's scenario note](../../wiki/finance/alphabet-10000-double-2031-scenarios-cloud-backlog.md) (debt up from ~$16B to ~$100B, FCF negative) and the [OpenAI run-rate selloff](../../wiki/finance/openai-50bn-run-rate-circular-financing-ai-selloff-oct2026.md) (Oracle and CoreWeave carrying heavy net debt at 8.5%+ coupons), it forms a loop. Debt-funded AI spending helps raise the rates at which that same spending must be refinanced and discounted. That makes the AI trade more sensitive to **any** demand wobble, like a single run-rate headline.
