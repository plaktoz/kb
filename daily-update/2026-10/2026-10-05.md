## 🧠 Emerging Connections
**Hiring and heart health have the same problem: the standard proxy misses what it's meant to measure.** The [REACT study](../../wiki/health/silent-atherosclerosis-young-adults-react-study.md) shows that cardiovascular risk scores, estimated from age, blood pressure and cholesterol, flagged only a small share of people whose arteries already had plaque, a **map vs. territory** gap that imaging closes. [Handshake's AI Skills Studio](../../wiki/learning/handshake-ai-skills-studio-project-based-ai-badges.md) attacks the same gap in hiring: a self-reported "AI skill" is a cheap proxy that is everywhere (9x more common) and tells employers little, so Handshake replaces it with verified evidence of finished work. In both fields, the fix is to **observe the underlying thing directly**, a published project or an artery scan, instead of inferring it from easy inputs. And in both, the open question is the same: does better detection actually lead to better outcomes, whether that's fewer heart attacks or better hires?
