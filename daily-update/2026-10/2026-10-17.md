### ⚡ Productivity
- **[The "3 productive hours a day" myth, debunked](../../wiki/productivity/productive-hours-per-day-benchmarks-by-role.md)**: The famous figure merges a 2013 self-report survey with 2019 app telemetry, and neither measures output. Realistic benchmarks are **2–4 focused hours** for knowledge workers and 1–2 for managers, whose job is meetings. The note warns that focused-hour targets lead to calendar hoarding (Goodhart's law).
- **[10 online-meeting mistakes that waste time](../../wiki/productivity/online-meetings-10-mistakes-that-waste-time.md)**: Start by asking whether people need a discussion or just the same information. Write outcome-based invites ("Choose the launch date"), mark each invitee as required, optional or for awareness, and end with **one named owner per action**. A 30-minute call with eight people costs four person-hours.

## 🧠 Emerging Connections
**The vault's measurement theme reaches the AI frontier.** The [productive-hours note](../../wiki/productivity/productive-hours-per-day-benchmarks-by-role.md) shows that "time in productive apps" and self-reports both fail to measure output. [Anthropic's employment report](../../wiki/technology/how-employment-is-changing-at-anthropic-ai-agents-junior-hiring.md) shows the same failure at a new scale. When agents work 6x as many hours as their handlers, human hours stop meaning much, and the real constraint becomes **human attention**: sessions sit idle ~80% of the time waiting on a person, and reading all agent output would take more than half a work week. This matches [Wharton's finding](../../wiki/productivity/wharton-survey-managers-ai-excitement-fades-verification-shift.md) that 72% of managers now mainly manage AI outputs. Taken together, the useful productivity metric is shifting from hours worked to **how fast a person can verify agent output and make decisions**.
