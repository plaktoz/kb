## ⚡ The Big Picture (TL;DR)
**The AI story has moved from "wow" to "prove it."** Markets now sell off on record AI results (TSMC, Micron) because they want proof of return on capex and of financing that holds up. CoreWeave's bonds yield ~16%, and September PPI jumped 1.3%. Inside companies, Wharton finds managers using AI more but enjoying it less, as the job shifts to **verifying AI output**. Classrooms show the same shift, with assignments being redesigned so learning is visible rather than just produced.

## 🧠 Emerging Connections
**The doing-vs-verifying gap shows up in three very different places today.** [Wharton](../../wiki/productivity/wharton-survey-managers-ai-excitement-fades-verification-shift.md) finds that an agent doing a task is not the same as anyone checking it was done right. 72% of managers now mostly manage AI output, and many reviewers may just sign off (what the researchers call "cognitive surrender"). [SMU Libraries](../../wiki/learning/smu-libraries-research-assignment-design-strategies-amid-ai.md) makes the same diagnosis in the classroom: a polished paper proves nothing, so instructors should grade checkpoints and **require students to show one instance of checking AI output**. And [Saxo's investor Q&A](../../wiki/finance/saxo-investor-qa-ai-selloff-hot-cpi-portfolio-balance-oct2026.md) shows markets doing the same thing to the AI build-out: headline demand no longer counts on its own, and investors want evidence of payback on capex. At work, in school and in markets, **AI makes output cheap, so value moves to verifying it**.
