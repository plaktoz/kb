### ⚡ Productivity
- **[Why AI Is Making Your Workday Worse, Not Better](../../wiki/productivity/why-hasnt-ai-made-work-easier.md)**: An ActivTrak study of 164,000 workers found AI adoption doubled time on email and messaging and increased business-tool usage 94%, while uninterrupted deep work fell 9%. The mechanism is Jevons Paradox: lowering the friction on low-value tasks multiplies them rather than freeing up capacity for high-value work.

- **[The Bottleneck You're Ignoring Is Why Your Tools Don't Work](../../wiki/productivity/bottlenecks-and-productivity-theory-of-constraints-newport.md)**: Applying Goldratt's Theory of Constraints to personal productivity: a system's output is capped by its slowest step, so speeding up email and drafts with AI just piles up unfinished work at the real bottleneck — the deep, high-value cognitive work you're trying to avoid.

- **[Three Structural Fixes to Stop Digital Tools From Making You Busier](../../wiki/productivity/avoiding-digital-productivity-traps.md)**: Newport's framework: measure a scoreboard of real outcomes (not task speed), direct tools at your actual bottleneck, and protect deep work time in the calendar before adopting any new tool. The same 164,000-person study: AI raised admin tasks 90% and cut deep work 10%.

### 🧠 Learning
- **[Retrieval Practice Beats Mind-Mapping by 50% — Here's the Mechanism](../../wiki/learning/retrieval-practice-beats-mind-mapping.md)**: A head-to-head study showed active recall outperforms open-book concept mapping on both retention and inference questions. The key finding: combining concept mapping with retrieval adds no benefit over retrieval alone, confirming recall — not visual organization — is the active learning ingredient.

- **[Deliberate Practice Is Real, But It Has Two Blind Spots](../../wiki/learning/deliberate-practice-career-application-scott-young.md)**: Ericsson's framework was built for domains with clear objectives and feedback — most careers have neither. Scott Young adds two missing layers: figuring out *what* to practice (career landscape mapping) and making competence visible to others (signaling). The fix is augmenting real-world work to function more like deliberate practice.

- **[The Feynman Technique: Why Teaching Forces Real Understanding](../../wiki/learning/feynman-learning-technique.md)**: The four-step method (choose → teach simply → identify gaps → simplify further) works because attempting to explain a concept in plain language immediately surfaces the gaps between familiarity and genuine understanding — and forces you to return to the source rather than bluff past confusion.
