### 🎯 Strategy
- **[Jevons Paradox: efficiency gains don't free up time — they expand demand for it](../../wiki/strategy/jevons-paradox-the-perils-of-efficiency.md)**: Lower cost per unit of a resource reliably grows total consumption — Jevons proved it with coal in 1865; smartphones and video calls proved it again; AI is proving it now. The fix is pre-assigning saved time *before* you save it, and stopping the broadcast of efficiency signals to others.

## 🧠 Emerging Connections
**Jevons Paradox meets the AI slowdown accountability gap.** Today's [AI governance note](../../wiki/technology/ai-slowdown-commitment-accountability-gap.md) argues that voluntary slowdown pledges lack enforceability because market incentives eliminate accountability. [Jevons Paradox](../../wiki/strategy/jevons-paradox-the-perils-of-efficiency.md) explains *why* this is structurally inevitable: as AI becomes cheaper and more capable, demand for its use expands — labs that slow down cede market share to those that don't, creating a coordination trap identical to the coal overconsumption Jevons documented in 1865. The paradox also surfaces in today's [Codex Agents Dashboard](../../wiki/technology/codex-agents-dashboard-multi-agent-workflow.md) note: multi-agent tooling makes individual developers more efficient, which — per Jevons — doesn't free up developer time but instead generates more concurrent agent sessions competing for human attention.
