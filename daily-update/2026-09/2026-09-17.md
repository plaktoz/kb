- **[Ternary LLMs Get Smaller: Breaking the 1.58-Bit Assumption](../../wiki/technology/breaking-the-1-58-bit-barrier-for-ternary-llms.md)**: The standard five-trit-per-byte ternary weight packing assumes uniform {-1, 0, +1} distribution, but zeros exceed 50% of weights in many real ternary models. BITCOS exploits this: a bitmap + sign vector layout costs `2 − z` bits/weight (where `z` = zero density), achieving as low as 1.485 bits/weight and up to 1.28x faster matrix-vector multiplication on CPUs and GPUs. Outperforms five-trit packing in 26 of 29 ternary models tested.

- **[Backups Are More Complicated Than You Think](../../wiki/technology/backup-strategy-3-2-1-borg-restic.md)**: A personal data-loss near-miss prompted a rigorous examination of why naive file copies fail: RAID mirrors replicate deletions in real time, cloud storage strips file metadata, and databases require proper dumps before backup. The conclusion is that rolling a custom solution hits a complexity ceiling fast — encryption, deduplication, checksums, and GFS rotation compound non-linearly. Use [[Borg]] or [[Restic]], and test restores every six months.
