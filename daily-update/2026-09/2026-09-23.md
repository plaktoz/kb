- **[Human language is inferential, not coded — word order is half the meaning](../../wiki/learning/language-inferential-meaning-beyond-words.md)**: Wilson and Sperber's relevance theory shows that listeners reconstruct intended meaning from context, tone, and implication — not literal word definitions. Practical upshot: taking language literally misses most of what is actually communicated.

### 🎯 Strategy

- **[AI investment is booming; AI financial returns are not — the gap is opportunity selection](../../wiki/strategy/closing-the-gap-ai-investment-financial-return.md)**: HBR's Rogers & Sankaranarayanan argue companies are pursuing the wrong AI opportunities — features customers don't value and productivity tools whose time savings never flow to measurable outcomes — leaving CFOs unable to connect AI spend to bottom-line impact. The bottleneck is not AI capability or budget. It is the discipline to select opportunities where AI spend has a direct, traceable line to financial return.

- **[Post-incident reviews: shift from "why did this happen" to "what structurally changes"](../../wiki/strategy/i-dont-want-the-details.md)**: An SVP's blunt redirect — "I don't want the details, I want to know what we're changing" — captures an important insight: compelling explanations reduce urgency to fix anything. Once behavior is deemed reasonable, the system that produced the failure goes unchanged. The right post-mortem question is not why the incident happened, but whether the fix survives full team turnover.

- **[Strategic plan calibration: neither too cautious nor overextended](../../wiki/strategy/strategic-plan-ambition-calibration.md)**: HBR frames ambition calibration as the core strategic leadership dilemma — under-ambitious plans leave competitive opportunity on the table while over-ambitious ones overwhelm execution capacity. Ambition must be sized to the organization's genuine change-absorption bandwidth, not an abstract ideal. The Goldilocks zone is real and organization-specific.

- **[Active travel as cognitive growth — passive tourism just fades](../../wiki/strategy/travel-mental-models-transformative-thinking.md)**: Shane Parrish argues that most travel evaporates because it is experienced as passive entertainment rather than active participation. Drawing on Mary Wollstonecraft's reflective Scandinavian journals, he derives four goals: engage actively, investigate the *why*, notice internal shifts, resist over-planning. The mental model: travel is a low-cost way to expand the sample size of lived experience and disrupt confirmation bias.

## 🧠 Emerging Connections

Today's AI ROI gap note — [[closing-the-gap-ai-investment-financial-return]] — and the focus/saying-no note — [[focus-saying-no-ultimate-productivity-hack]] — are the same insight at two different scales. Parrish's thesis is that focus means saying no to *phenomenal* ideas to protect what matters most. Rogers & Sankaranarayanan's finding is that corporate AI investment fails precisely because companies say yes to too many low-value AI opportunities instead of selecting the few that produce traceable financial impact. The failure mode is identical: diffuse effort on genuinely-good-looking alternatives, at the expense of focused execution on the ones that compound.
