- **[The AI productivity paradox mirrors the PC era](../../wiki/productivity/ai-productivity-paradox-pc-revolution-parallel.md)**: Cal Newport draws the parallel: PCs added only 0.2 percentage points/year to business output despite enormous expectations. His prediction — AI will be useful but fall short of its sweeping productivity transformation narrative — is grounded in a consistent pattern of digital tool adoption.

- **[AI saves 11 hrs/week — but only 13% see better performance](../../wiki/productivity/ai-magnifies-broken-work-not-breaks-it.md)**: Newport's case that "botsitting," tool-switching overhead, and workplace theater are pre-existing problems AI is amplifying, not creating. The real opportunity is the executive attention AI's novelty is drawing to workflow dysfunctions that email and Slack created but never forced anyone to fix.

- **[AI isn't taking jobs — it's reshaping them](../../wiki/productivity/ai-job-displacement-measured-reality-2026.md)**: Altman and Amodei are walking back their strongest displacement predictions. On the ground, Newport found AI functioning as a "resourceful intern" — building internal tools, augmenting roles — not replacing workers wholesale. His "freestyle work" model describes the adaptation pattern.
