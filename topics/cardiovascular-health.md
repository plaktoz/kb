# Cardiovascular Health

The vault treats cardiovascular health as a lifelong process rather than an event of old age, covering silent plaque, undetected hypertension, rising heart failure in young adults, the modifiable and inflammatory drivers of arterial damage, the exercise, diet and drug interventions that change the course of disease, and newer anatomical routes to stroke, and its latest addition, observational data on GLP-1 drugs and elective surgery, offers a hypothesis about drug-linked protection rather than a proven effect.

## Plaque Vulnerability and Early-Onset Risk

Coronary imaging separates two processes that are often lumped together: in 131 patients, a higher count of the five modifiable factors (dyslipidemia, hypertension, diabetes, obesity and smoking) was linked to rupture-prone plaques, while age, sex and family history tracked mostly with stable plaque, and at four or more modifiable factors half of plaques were thin-cap fibroatheromas [[modifiable-risk-factors-coronary-plaque-heart-attack]]. Population imaging shows the process starts far earlier than clinics usually look: REACT found plaque in 57.1% of more than 16,800 adults aged 18–70 with no known heart disease, including about 1 in 13 under 30, and standard risk scores flagged only a small share of those already affected [[silent-atherosclerosis-young-adults-react-study]]. Young adults show the same early drift in kidney and metabolic markers, since roughly 79% of 1,283 young adults averaging 23 already show cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic syndrome, with higher stages carrying arterial wall thickening normally seen in much older people [[ckm-syndrome-young-adults-cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic]].

## Hypertension and the Leaky Care Cascade

Hypertension, one of the five plaque-linked factors, shows how much modifiable risk goes unmanaged: among 1.4 million English adults in Our Future Health, 37% had hypertension, 59% of them did not know it, and half of those on medication still read at or above 140/90 mmHg [[undiagnosed-hypertension-england-our-future-health]]. The silence starts young, since more than 15% of 18–39-year-olds have it yet are not eligible for NHS Health Checks, and early menopause is linked to blood pressure that rises unnoticed [[early-menopause-linked-to-high-blood-pressure]], just as roughly 62% of AFib patients are unaware of their condition before diagnosis [[eye-scan-retinal-imaging-afib-risk-prediction]]. The Oxford authors argue that NICE's multi-reading, lifestyle-first route delays control, and they call for proactive, population-wide detection and treatment based on predicted cardiovascular risk [[undiagnosed-hypertension-england-our-future-health]].

## Heart Failure's Rising Toll and the Therapy Gap

US age-adjusted heart failure mortality rose about 37% from 2010 to 2023, with HF contributing to 452,573 deaths in 2023, and the burden is moving younger, as hospitalizations rose among adults under 65 and crude mortality rose 121% among ages 25–44 from 2001 to 2020 [[hf-stats-2026-heart-failure-mortality-rising-younger-adults]]. The report ties the projected rise from 7.7 to 11.4 million patients by 2050 to obesity, hypertension and other cardiometabolic risks, and notes that about a third of adults without symptoms already meet stage A criteria, which matches the early-onset picture in [[ckm-syndrome-young-adults-cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic]]. The problem is an implementation gap rather than a lack of proven treatment, since only 18% of patients with reduced ejection fraction receive quadruple guideline-directed therapy [[hf-stats-2026-heart-failure-mortality-rising-younger-adults]], the same underuse that the cardiac rehab review describes [[at-home-cardiac-rehab-as-effective-as-in-person-programs]].

## Exercise Dose, Volume and Intensity

Meeting the 150-minute weekly guideline lowers cardiovascular risk by only 8–9%, while cutting it by more than 30% takes roughly 560–610 minutes a week, a level only 12% of the 17,088 UK Biobank adults reached, and less-fit people need more minutes for the same benefit [[exercise-volume-heart-protection-study]]. Intensity can stand in for volume: brisk walkers (80+ steps per minute at peak 30-minute cadence) taking fewer than 5,000 daily steps had mortality risk similar to slower walkers taking 5,000–7,500 [[walk-pace-step-count-mortality-risk]]. In nearly 100,000 wearable-tracked adults, the highest share of vigorous, slightly breathless activity, sometimes only 15–20 minutes a week, went with 46% lower all-cause mortality and 63% lower dementia risk than none [[exercise-intensity-beats-duration-disease-risk-study]]. Taken together, these notes treat the guideline as a floor rather than a ceiling, point to effort and pace as levers for people who cannot add hours, and are all observational, so the gains are associations rather than proven causes.

## Structured Exercise as Treatment: AFib and Cardiac Rehabilitation

Exercise also works as therapy for people who already have heart disease: in the NEXAF trial, a year-long individualized program cut time spent in AFib by 45% (from 7.1% to 3.9%) and AFib-related hospitalizations by 46%, and experts suggested it could count as a primary treatment used alongside medication, not instead of it [[tailored-exercise-intervention-reduces-afib-recurrence]]. An updated Cochrane review of 107 trials extends the benefit to coronary heart disease, where exercise-based cardiac rehab cut heart attack risk by 28% and all-cause admissions by about 35%, preventing one heart attack per 71 participants within 6–12 months [[at-home-cardiac-rehab-as-effective-as-in-person-programs]]. Both bodies of evidence point toward remote delivery: NEXAF moved patients from eight supervised sessions to home exercise tracked by smartwatch and insertable monitor, and a 27-study companion analysis found home and digitally supported rehab matched facility programs, which suggests access rather than efficacy has limited reach [[at-home-cardiac-rehab-as-effective-as-in-person-programs]]. Only 17% of rehab trial participants were women [[at-home-cardiac-rehab-as-effective-as-in-person-programs]], a blind spot that parallels how exercise and brain-health trials often overlook menopause status [[aerobic-exercise-brain-health-postmenopausal-women]].

## Aerobic Exercise Beyond the Heart

Aerobic exercise also shows brain and cancer associations, with 93 sedentary postmenopausal women doing 45 minutes of moderate activity four times a week improving executive function within three months, although this small secondary analysis still needs larger confirmatory trials [[aerobic-exercise-brain-health-postmenopausal-women]]. After cancer treatment, a meta-analysis of 21 RCTs (8,449 participants) linked aerobic exercise alone to a 29% lower relative risk of death and structured exercise to a 17% higher chance of recurrence-free survival, while resistance training alone was not significant [[aerobic-exercise-cancer-recurrence-survival-meta-analysis]]. Combined aerobic and resistance training still reached about 25% lower death risk, and the cardiac rehab review calls strength training an underused complement [[at-home-cardiac-rehab-as-effective-as-in-person-programs]], so resistance work reads as an addition to aerobic training rather than a substitute [[aerobic-exercise-cancer-recurrence-survival-meta-analysis]].

## Beverages and Sweetener Substitutions

Coffee's benefit depends on the outcome: three to five cups a day is generally considered safe for most adults, one daily cup cut recurrent atrial fibrillation or flutter by 39% in a 2025 JAMA trial, and five or more cups a day is linked to lower cirrhosis and liver cancer risk [[coffee-research-daily-intake-heart-liver-health]]. Xylitol points the other way: in conference data from more than 17,000 people, not yet peer-reviewed, the highest blood levels carried 57% (Canada) and 18% (UK) higher major-event risk even after adjusting for hypertension, BMI, smoking and lipids [[xylitol-cardiovascular-risk-study-2026]]. That result shows how swapping in a substitute to fix one variable, such as blood sugar, can carry its own cardiovascular cost, which is why experts rank overall patterns such as the DASH diet above removing any single sweetener [[xylitol-cardiovascular-risk-study-2026]].

## Dietary Patterns: Whole Grains, Plant-Based Groceries and Ultra-Processed Food

The largest RCT meta-analysis of whole grains (87 trials) found that 4–6 daily servings lower body weight, waist size, total cholesterol and IL-6 with high certainty, with moderate-certainty improvements in LDL, triglycerides, systolic pressure and fasting glucose and benefits starting around three servings [[whole-grains-daily-servings-cardiovascular-risk-reduction]]. Meanwhile, a meta-analysis of 51 cohorts (about 8.8 million people) linked each extra 100g a day of ultra-processed food to 14% higher cardiovascular-event risk, with no safe threshold identified [[ultra-processed-foods-disease-risk-meta-analysis]]. A Mass General Brigham Food Is Medicine program adds the long-duration view that short trials lack: among 68 adults who received an average of 97 plant-based grocery packages over 2.7 years, every 100 packages went with 3.21 mmHg lower diastolic pressure and 2.6 kg less weight, though A1c did not improve [[food-is-medicine-long-term-plant-based-groceries-cardiometabolic]]. None of this fits single-nutrient thinking, since whole grains act through fiber, minerals, polyphenols and phytosterols at once and the ultra-processed "cocktail effect" frames harm as coming from combined additives and processing, yet 8-week trials, self-reported diets and a before-and-after design without a described control group limit causal claims.

## Inflammation as the Connecting Mechanism

Systemic inflammation is the mechanism that recurs most often across the vault: about 34% of U.S. adults have it, and in 488,079 UK Biobank participants the top GlycA quartile carried 43% higher major-event risk alongside smaller ventricles, thicker walls and weaker diastolic function [[systemic-inflammation-glyca-cardiovascular-risk]]. Smoking, a GlycA driver, is also one of the five modifiable factors that the plaque study links to vulnerable plaque through a proposed vascular inflammation pathway [[modifiable-risk-factors-coronary-plaque-heart-attack]], and GlycA itself is meant to be read alongside ApoB, LDL, blood pressure and glucose rather than on its own [[systemic-inflammation-glyca-cardiovascular-risk]]. The same pathway appears in the IL-6 reduction seen with whole grains [[whole-grains-daily-servings-cardiovascular-risk-reduction]], in tirzepatide's proposed plaque-stabilizing effect [[mounjaro-heart-health-type-2-diabetes]], in the shingles vaccine's proposed AS01 effect on IL-6 pathways [[shingles-vaccine-linked-to-lower-cardiovascular-burden]], and in the link between vigorous exercise and lower systemic inflammation [[exercise-intensity-beats-duration-disease-risk-study]]. That spread suggests lowering inflammation, more than any particular food, drug or workout, may be what many of the strongest findings share, though each link remains a proposed mechanism rather than a demonstrated one.

## Pharmacological and Vaccine-Based Interventions

The FDA's August 28, 2026 approval of Mounjaro to reduce major cardiovascular events in high-risk type 2 diabetes rests on SURPASS-CVOT, where it carried 8% lower relative risk than dulaglutide across more than 13,000 patients, and on BMJ observational data showing 33% lower heart-attack risk than sitagliptin in nearly 53,000 people, although its very high-risk trial population argues against broad generalization [[mounjaro-heart-health-type-2-diabetes]]. Shingrix recipients aged 60 and over had 9% lower overall cardiovascular burden than Zostavax recipients across seven years, including 12% less heart failure, 10% less ischemic heart disease and 7% less AFib, a finding from an observational study whose authors tie it possibly to the AS01 adjuvant's effect on IL-6 pathways while two randomized trials are under way [[shingles-vaccine-linked-to-lower-cardiovascular-burden]]. An EPIC COSMOS analysis of 2,028,601 elective surgery patients, 145,610 of them on GLP-1 receptor agonists, found 11% lower odds of major cardiovascular events and 8% lower odds of postoperative infection among users [[glp1-surgery-outcomes-cardiovascular-infection-observational-study]]. Because that design is observational, causation is not established, aspiration risk was not assessed, and the researchers call the results hypothesis-generating rather than a reason to start or stop GLP-1 drugs, with the proposed anti-inflammatory mechanism still a hypothesis [[glp1-surgery-outcomes-cardiovascular-infection-observational-study]].

## Hormonal Risk and the HRT Timing Window

Menopause before 45, and especially before 40, raises later hypertension risk: estrogen supports nitric oxide production and arterial flexibility, and losing it early removes that protection, with sleep disruption, weight gain and worse insulin sensitivity adding to the effect [[early-menopause-linked-to-high-blood-pressure]]. Twenty years of SWAN data on more than 2,700 women shows the other side, with HRT started in perimenopause or early postmenopause associated with about 22% lower CVD risk, 27% when started within 10 years of menopause, and roughly 49% among Black women, though the observational design cannot establish causation [[menopausal-hormone-therapy-early-timing-heart-disease-risk]]. The proposed estradiol mechanism (lower LDL, including plaque-linked subparticles, plus lower blood sugar and inflammation) lines up with the plaque and inflammation findings above, and REACT's finding that women's plaque rises most steeply between 40 and 60 places that window in the same decades [[silent-atherosclerosis-young-adults-react-study]]. Hot flashes and night sweats that occur alongside high blood pressure or abnormal cholesterol may mark a moment for an individualized discussion of hormone therapy [[menopausal-hormone-therapy-early-timing-heart-disease-risk]].

## Circadian Disruption and Cardiac Remodeling

Even 3 lux of nighttime light was linked in more than 11,000 UK Biobank participants to 2.4% greater left ventricular mass, 1.5% thicker heart walls and 1.9% lower contraction fraction, and a 2025 JAMA Network Open study tied nighttime light to 32–56% higher risk of various cardiovascular conditions [[nighttime-light-exposure-cardiovascular-disease-risk]]. Shorter sleep accounted for 24–49% of the association, which points to circadian disruption affecting blood pressure and metabolism rather than direct damage to the heart [[nighttime-light-exposure-cardiovascular-disease-risk]]. The thicker-wall pattern resembles the remodeling seen with high GlycA [[systemic-inflammation-glyca-cardiovascular-risk]], and the source lists concrete mitigations (blackout curtains, face-down phones, dim warm nightlights and morning sunlight) that target the exposure directly [[nighttime-light-exposure-cardiovascular-disease-risk]].

## Stroke Pathways: Arrhythmia, Anatomy, and the Brain

The vault describes two routes by which clots can reach the brain: through the left atrial appendage, where clots usually need AFib to form, and through the left atrial septal pouch (LASP), an 8–11mm pocket in the atrial septum found in about one in three adults, where blood can stagnate and clot even in normal rhythm after 16 years of observational links to strokes with no other identified cause [[left-atrial-septal-pouch-heart-discovery]]. A 2026 Fuwai Hospital trial, the first randomized test of catheter-delivered LASP closure, found the procedure feasible and markedly fewer recurrent MRI-detected ischemic brain lesions than with medication alone [[left-atrial-septal-pouch-heart-discovery]], while on the AFib route retinal thinning visible on routine eye scans appeared about four years before diagnosis [[eye-scan-retinal-imaging-afib-risk-prediction]]. Catching these routes early matters because TIAs carry a 34% higher long-term dementia risk, with the first year at 75% higher, and dementia follows about three times as often as a second stroke [[mini-strokes-tia-dementia-risk-34-percent]]. Cryptogenic strokes, which have no other identified cause, are linked to this anatomical structure, which is not one of the modifiable factors discussed above [[left-atrial-septal-pouch-heart-discovery]].
