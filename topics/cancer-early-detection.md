# Cancer Early Detection and Risk Signals

This topic tracks how the vault's health notes approach catching cancer before it becomes symptomatic or advanced. It covers new blood and microbiome biomarkers, risk-stratified screening, the rise of early-onset colorectal cancer, and the persistent human problem of getting eligible people tested at all.

## Why Stage at Diagnosis Dominates Outcomes
Across cancer types, the notes tie survival closely to how early the disease is found. Localized prostate cancer has close to 100% five-year survival [[prostate-cancer-cases-rising-early-detection]], stage 1 breast cancer has a 99% five-year relative survival rate [[katherine-lanasa-breast-cancer-early-detection-mammogram]], and pancreatic cancer drops from about 44% five-year survival when localized to about 3% once it has metastasized [[panxeon-blood-test-pancreatic-cancer-early-detection]]. Finding cancer early also means lighter treatment: surgery alone cured Marisa Stachelski's stage 1 colon tumor, with no chemotherapy or radiation [[brca2-mutation-early-onset-colon-cancer-marisa-stachelski]]. The opposite case is a review of 84 colorectal cancer patients aged 21 or younger, most of whom were diagnosed at Stage III or IV; more than a third relapsed and nearly half died [[early-onset-colorectal-cancer-adolescents-late-diagnosis]].

## Reading Trends Instead of Snapshots
A PLOS Medicine study of 275,205 patients with unexplained weight loss treats routine bloodwork as a risk signal. Changes across 26 common markers appeared 3 to 5 years before the weight loss, and models adjusted for age and sex reached an AUC of 0.82 [[blood-test-trends-predict-cancer-risk-before-symptoms]]. Many of those changes stayed within normal reference ranges, so the signal comes from each patient's own trend over time, and different patterns pointed to different cancers. PANXEON uses a similar idea of combining weak signals: it merges circulating and exosomal microRNAs with CA19-9 into a single AI-generated probability, and it caught 87% of stage 1 and 2 pancreatic cancers among nearly 1,800 participants [[panxeon-blood-test-pancreatic-cancer-early-detection]].

## Liquid Biopsy and the Shift From Detection to Interception
Blood-based screening is most advanced for colorectal cancer. Freenome's SimpleScreen CRC became the second FDA-approved blood test in July 2026, after Shield in 2024. It detects CpG methylation, with about 80% sensitivity and 90% specificity in the PREEMPT CRC trial of more than 48,000 people [[fda-approves-simplescreen-crc-blood-test-colorectal-cancer]]. The experimental DENEB test aims earlier in the disease: it caught 92% of stage I to III cancers and 81% of advanced adenomas, compared with about 13% polyp detection for Shield [[deneb-blood-test-polyp-colorectal-cancer-detection]]. PANXEON has the same goal in the pancreas, flagging high-grade dysplasia in more than 64% of high-risk cyst patients [[panxeon-blood-test-pancreatic-cancer-early-detection]], which suggests that a test able to find precancerous lesions works as prevention, not just diagnosis.

## The Early-Onset Colorectal Puzzle
Bowel cancer before 50 is still rare, as more than 9 in 10 cases occur in older people. But it is rising worldwide: rates went up in 27 of 50 countries studied, England had the fourth-fastest rise globally from 2007 to 2017, and UK rates among 25- to 49-year-olds are up more than 50% since the early 1990s [[prospect-uk-study-early-onset-bowel-cancer-diet-lifestyle]]. The colorectal blood-test notes also describe it as the leading cancer killer of Americans under 50 [[fda-approves-simplescreen-crc-blood-test-colorectal-cancer]] [[deneb-blood-test-polyp-colorectal-cancer-detection]]. The Mayo Clinic cohort of 1,364 patients found higher lactate and more lactate-feeding *Veillonella parvula* in colorectal patients aged 50 and under [[mayo-clinic-gut-microbiome-cancer-signatures-1300-patients]]. To look for causes, PROSPECT-UK aims to recruit 250,000 adults aged 18 to 50 and link brand-level food data to their health records, because the processed foods behind about 60% of UK calorie intake differ too much to study under one broad label [[prospect-uk-study-early-onset-bowel-cancer-diet-lifestyle]].

## When Age Masks Risk
Because colorectal cancer is uncommon in young people, its warning signs are easy to dismiss. In the adolescent review, rectal bleeding, unexplained weight loss, and changes in bowel habits were often overlooked until the disease was advanced, and the study author describes early-onset colorectal cancer as "a different disease" from the one seen in older adults [[early-onset-colorectal-cancer-adolescents-late-diagnosis]]. Stachelski's experience at 37 followed the same pattern on a smaller scale: months of bloating and diet changes came before the stool test that found hidden blood and led to her colonoscopy [[brca2-mutation-early-onset-colon-cancer-marisa-stachelski]]. Both notes suggest that judging a symptom against the patient's age group delays diagnosis exactly when that group's risk is rising, which is why the adolescent study calls for coordinated pediatric-adult care.

## Hereditary and Personal Risk Stratification
Risk-based screening moves high-risk people to earlier screening. ACS guidance starts PSA testing at 50 for average-risk men, 45 for higher-risk men, and 40 for those with multiple affected relatives, and Black men have about 70% higher prostate cancer incidence [[prostate-cancer-cases-rising-early-detection]]. Family history is an incomplete filter, though. Stachelski's BRCA2 mutation was found only through genetic testing after her early-onset diagnosis, with no obvious family history [[brca2-mutation-early-onset-colon-cancer-marisa-stachelski]], and only 20% of the young colorectal patients had a first- or second-degree relative with the disease [[early-onset-colorectal-cancer-adolescents-late-diagnosis]]. New tools such as PANXEON are still aimed at defined high-risk groups such as cyst or chronic pancreatitis patients [[panxeon-blood-test-pancreatic-cancer-early-detection]], and LaNasa's case presents personal and family history as the basis for a tailored mammography schedule [[katherine-lanasa-breast-cancer-early-detection-mammogram]].

## The Participation Gap
Better tests help little if people skip screening, and the notes show that gap growing. The share of women aged 21 to 29 who had never been screened for cervical cancer rose from 12.5% in 2005 to about 32% in 2023, while regional- and distant-stage cervical cancer increased in women aged 30 to 64 [[cervical-screening-decline-women-20s]]. Only about half of eligible women get annual mammograms because of cost, time, transportation, and fear [[katherine-lanasa-breast-cancer-early-detection-mammogram]], and about 60 million eligible Americans have not been screened for colorectal cancer [[fda-approves-simplescreen-crc-blood-test-colorectal-cancer]]. Blood tests are offered as a low-friction alternative with possible real-world adherence near 95%, but both colorectal notes present them as a complement to colonoscopy, not a replacement [[fda-approves-simplescreen-crc-blood-test-colorectal-cancer]] [[deneb-blood-test-polyp-colorectal-cancer-detection]].

## Validation, False Alarms, and Correlation
Nearly every new signal in these notes comes with a caveat about evidence. The blood-trend model still needs external validation and could trigger unnecessary investigations [[blood-test-trends-predict-cancer-risk-before-symptoms]]. PANXEON is still investigational, with false-positive rates near 3% in low-risk and 16% in high-risk people [[panxeon-blood-test-pancreatic-cancer-early-detection]], and SimpleScreen's lower accuracy than colonoscopy raises concern that focusing on completion rates could let more cancers go undetected [[fda-approves-simplescreen-crc-blood-test-colorectal-cancer]]. Causal claims are also uncertain: the Mayo researchers note that tumors may produce the lactate the bacteria feed on [[mayo-clinic-gut-microbiome-cancer-signatures-1300-patients]], and PROSPECT-UK's observational design can narrow down likely causes but cannot prove them. No single diet or lifestyle factor has yet been shown to drive the early-onset rise [[prospect-uk-study-early-onset-bowel-cancer-diet-lifestyle]].
