# Cancer Early Detection and Risk Signals

This topic tracks how the vault's health notes approach catching cancer before it turns symptomatic or advanced, spanning new blood and microbiome biomarkers, risk-stratified screening, and the persistent human problem of getting eligible people tested at all.

## Why Stage at Diagnosis Dominates Outcomes
Across cancer types, the notes tie survival tightly to how early the disease is found: localized prostate cancer approaches 100% five-year survival [[prostate-cancer-cases-rising-early-detection]], stage 1 breast cancer carries a 99% five-year relative survival rate [[katherine-lanasa-breast-cancer-early-detection-mammogram]], and pancreatic cancer falls from roughly 44% five-year survival when localized to about 3% once metastatic [[panxeon-blood-test-pancreatic-cancer-early-detection]]. Early detection also lightens the treatment burden, not just the odds, as when Marisa Stachelski's stage 1 colon tumor was cured by surgery alone with no chemotherapy or radiation [[brca2-mutation-early-onset-colon-cancer-marisa-stachelski]]. That asymmetry is why so many of these notes frame screening through a prevention-versus-treatment lens.

## Reading Trends Instead of Snapshots
A PLOS Medicine study of 275,205 patients with unexplained weight loss reframes routine bloodwork as a risk signal: shifts across 26 common markers surfaced 3 to 5 years before the weight loss, and age- and sex-adjusted models reached an AUC of 0.82 [[blood-test-trends-predict-cancer-risk-before-symptoms]]. Because many of those shifts stayed inside normal reference ranges, the signal lives in each patient's own trajectory, and distinct patterns pointed toward distinct cancers, such as white cell and neutrophil trends for lung cancer and mean cell volume for bowel cancer and lymphoma. PANXEON applies a parallel logic of pooling weak signals, fusing circulating and exosomal microRNAs with CA19-9 into a single AI-generated probability that caught 87% of stage 1 and 2 pancreatic cancers among nearly 1,800 participants [[panxeon-blood-test-pancreatic-cancer-early-detection]]. Together they suggest that composite or longitudinal readings of familiar measurements can outperform any single marker read in isolation.

## Liquid Biopsy and the Shift From Detection to Interception
Colorectal cancer is where blood-based screening is most mature: Freenome's SimpleScreen CRC became the second FDA-approved blood test in July 2026, following Shield in 2024, detecting CpG methylation with roughly 80% sensitivity and 90% specificity in the 48,000-plus-person PREEMPT CRC trial [[fda-approves-simplescreen-crc-blood-test-colorectal-cancer]]. The experimental DENEB test moves the target upstream, catching 92% of stage I to III cancers and 81% of advanced adenomas versus about 13% polyp detection for Shield [[deneb-blood-test-polyp-colorectal-cancer-detection]]. PANXEON shows the same ambition in the pancreas, flagging high-grade dysplasia in over 64% of high-risk cyst patients [[panxeon-blood-test-pancreatic-cancer-early-detection]]. The shared lesson is that a test which finds precancerous lesions functions as prevention, whereas one that only finds established cancer remains a diagnostic tool.

## Microbiome Signatures and Early-Onset Cancer
The Mayo Clinic cohort of 1,364 cancer patients carries the biomarker search into stool, associating 341 bacterial species with five cancer groups and finding distinct early-onset profiles: elevated lactate and more lactate-feeding *Veillonella parvula* in colorectal patients aged 50 and under, and 64 altered species with lower primary bile acids in younger breast cancer patients [[mayo-clinic-gut-microbiome-cancer-signatures-1300-patients]]. The same data hinted at which patients would develop diarrhea on 5-fluorouracil, though the researchers stress that tumors may themselves generate the lactate, so these are biomarker leads rather than explanations for rising early-onset rates. Stachelski's case gives that trend a human face, with months of bloating and dietary changes preceding the stool test that found occult blood and the colonoscopy that diagnosed her at 37 [[brca2-mutation-early-onset-colon-cancer-marisa-stachelski]]. Both colorectal blood-test notes underline the stakes by describing colorectal cancer as the leading cancer killer of Americans under 50 [[fda-approves-simplescreen-crc-blood-test-colorectal-cancer]] [[deneb-blood-test-polyp-colorectal-cancer-detection]].

## Hereditary and Personal Risk Stratification
Risk-based screening runs through the notes as the mechanism for moving high-risk people earlier in line: ACS guidance starts PSA testing at 50 for average-risk men, 45 for higher-risk men, and 40 for those with multiple affected relatives, while Black men face roughly 70% higher prostate cancer incidence [[prostate-cancer-cases-rising-early-detection]]. Stachelski's BRCA2 mutation surfaced only through genetic testing prompted by her early-onset diagnosis, despite no obvious family history, and family outreach then uncovered a cousin's recent colon cancer diagnosis [[brca2-mutation-early-onset-colon-cancer-marisa-stachelski]]; LaNasa's story similarly casts personal and family history as the inputs for tailoring mammography schedules [[katherine-lanasa-breast-cancer-early-detection-mammogram]]. PANXEON is aimed squarely at stratified groups such as people with genetic predisposition, pancreatic cysts, or chronic pancreatitis [[panxeon-blood-test-pancreatic-cancer-early-detection]], reinforcing that new detection tools are being deployed to defined high-risk populations rather than everyone.

## The Participation Gap
Better tests matter little if people skip screening, and the vault records that gap widening: women aged 21 to 29 who had never had cervical screening rose from 12.5% in 2005 to about 32% in 2023, alongside rising regional- and distant-stage cervical cancer in women 30 to 64 [[cervical-screening-decline-women-20s]]. Only about half of eligible women get annual mammograms, held back by cost, time, transportation, and fear [[katherine-lanasa-breast-cancer-early-detection-mammogram]], and around 60 million eligible Americans remain unscreened for colorectal cancer [[fda-approves-simplescreen-crc-blood-test-colorectal-cancer]]. Blood tests are positioned as a low-friction fallback for people who decline colonoscopy or stool testing, with possible real-world adherence near 95% [[fda-approves-simplescreen-crc-blood-test-colorectal-cancer]], and DENEB is likewise framed as a complement to colonoscopy rather than a substitute [[deneb-blood-test-polyp-colorectal-cancer-detection]]. The emerging picture is that removing barriers may save as many lives as improving raw test accuracy.

## Validation, False Alarms, and Correlation
Nearly every new signal in this cluster arrives with a caveat about proof. The blood-trend model still needs external validation, drew only on patients who already had test histories, and could trigger false alarms and unnecessary investigations [[blood-test-trends-predict-cancer-risk-before-symptoms]], while PANXEON remains investigational with false-positive rates near 3% in low-risk and 16% in high-risk individuals [[panxeon-blood-test-pancreatic-cancer-early-detection]]. The Mayo microbiome findings are associative and await independent, prospective cohorts [[mayo-clinic-gut-microbiome-cancer-signatures-1300-patients]], and SimpleScreen's lower accuracy than colonoscopy raises the worry that optimizing for completion rates could let more cancers slip through [[fda-approves-simplescreen-crc-blood-test-colorectal-cancer]]. The recurring tension is the sensitivity-specificity tradeoff: earlier, broader detection pays off only if it does not bury clinicians and patients in noise.
