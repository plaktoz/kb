# Treasury Yields and the Bond Market

This topic follows the 2026 bond selloff that carried US Treasury yields to their highest levels since 2002, the partial pause in the third week of October that brought the 10-year back toward 5.1% from its early-October peak, and the open question of whether that pause holds: what is pushing long-term borrowing costs up, how Washington has tried to lean against it, and how those costs ripple into stocks, housing, credit and alternative assets (Fed rate decisions themselves live in [[federal-reserve-monetary-policy]]).

## The Climb to Multi-Decade Highs
The long end moved first, with a mid-August 30-year auction clearing at the highest yield since 2001 [[stock-market-record-bofa-debt-yields-risk-aug2026]] and the 30-year touching 5.337% intraday days later, a level unseen since 2007 [[global-bond-selloff-chip-stocks-fomc-minutes-aug18-2026]]. The 10-year caught up through September, sitting near 4.8% early in the month [[10-year-treasury-yield-high-dividend-stocks-agnc-et-vz]] before breaking 5% on September 15 for the first time since October 2023 [[10-year-treasury-yield-tops-5-percent-sep2026]]. It then set new marks almost daily in the quarter's final week, from 5.24% on September 28 [[wall-street-dips-us-iran-standoff-sep2026]] to 5.304% on September 30 [[10-year-treasury-yield-24-year-high-sep2026]]. On October 1 it printed 5.34% intraday, a dot-com-bust level, and closed at 5.24%, the highest finish since 2007, while the 30-year reached 5.69% [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]].

## October: New Highs and a Rising Term Premium
The first full week of Q4 pushed the marks further. On October 5 the 10-year touched 5.349%, its highest since April 3, 2002, and the 30-year 5.703%, a late-May-2002 level. Infrastructure Capital's Jay Hatfield called the six-week selloff momentum-driven with room to run, since the 10-year usually trades about 100 bp over the terminal fed funds rate [[10-year-treasury-yield-fresh-2002-high-ism-services-oct5-2026]]. Two days later the 10-year reached 5.365% and the 30-year 5.732%, yet indirect bidders took 80.3% of a $39 billion 10-year auction against a 72.4% average [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. That solid sale brought some relief even as the New York Fed's estimate of the 10-year term premium hit its highest since 2014 [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. The week's peak reached 5.31% before the 10-year settled near 5.24% [[daily-market-snapshot-record-highs-10yr-yield-oct2026]], and a Reuters week-ahead preview put it near 5.23% [[wall-st-week-ahead-bank-earnings-cpi-sp500-record-oct9-2026]].

## Growth, Inflation and Oil as Drivers
This selloff is unusual because investors are dumping bonds on the view that the economy is running too hot, not fleeing to them for safety. AI spending is propping up growth, fuel prices are lifting inflation, and auction buyers are demanding more compensation [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]]. Oil has been the most visible accelerant. Brent above $109 triggered the break through 5% [[10-year-treasury-yield-tops-5-percent-sep2026]], and slowed Strait of Hormuz traffic kept crude above $100 while Fed officials Barr and Kashkari saw no clear route back to 2% inflation [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]]. In early October Brent above $101 and renewed Houthi attacks revived fears of sticky inflation [[stocks-fall-bond-yields-oil-fed-minutes-halt-record-rally-oct7-2026]], just as the ISM services price index rose to 74 and lifted its 12-month average to the highest since March 2023 [[10-year-treasury-yield-fresh-2002-high-ism-services-oct5-2026]]. Decompositions complicate the inflation story. ClearBridge attributed most of the rise from late February to mid-September to real rates (+50 bps) rather than inflation expectations (+15 bps) or term premium (+17 bps) [[stocks-resilient-despite-higher-bond-yields-sp500-2026]]. Reuters' Morning Bid likewise found long-run inflation expectations stable, crediting the October move mostly to a Fed rethink while tying the swelling term premium to debt levels, political risk and doubts about long-term government financing [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]].

## Soft Data, Hawkish Fed, Sticky Yields
Relief has repeatedly failed to stick. Core PCE came in at 3% against a 3.3% forecast, yet yields dipped only briefly before the long end resumed climbing [[10-year-treasury-yield-24-year-high-sep2026]]. The 29,000-job September payrolls miss knocked the 10-year to about 5.16% before it rebounded toward 5.30% even as October hike odds collapsed to 23% from 64% a week earlier [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]]. Oil's rebound was credited for the reversal [[us-stocks-near-record-jobs-report-tesla-nvidia-nike-oct9-2026]], and the jobs data delivered only a brief Treasury rally [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]]. The September FOMC minutes then showed a unanimous first hike since 2023 approved on split rationales, with October hike odds sliding to 17.2% [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. Most policymakers still expected further increases, and Governor Waller said more would likely be needed, so markets kept pricing three more hikes starting in December [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. That mix of resilient growth and energy- and AI-driven inflation is what keeps expectations anchored on higher for longer [[five-things-week-ahead-bond-yields-french-debt-fomc-minutes-oct2026]], consistent with the view that the forces behind high yields reach well beyond the next meeting [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]].

The September CPI showed the same pattern. Headline inflation rose to 3.7% against a 3.6% forecast on a 9.1% monthly jump in gasoline, core came in at 2.6% against 2.5%, and the 2-year yield (about 4.91%) and 10-year (about 5.29%) rose on the news [[september-cpi-3-7-percent-hot-inflation-fed-rate-cuts-at-risk-oct2026]]. The preview had stressed that the 2-year tracks near-term policy bets while the 10-year blends rate expectations, growth, inflation expectations and the term premium, so the composition of the print, not the headline, would steer yields [[september-cpi-preview-energy-headline-vs-core-fed-oct2026]]. A day later producer prices posted their biggest rise in four and a half years, yet Treasuries rallied on safe-haven flows tied to AI-debt worries and on remarks by Fed officials that they could wait, which lifted the odds of an October hold to 72%. Strategists argued that Fed hawkishness was already priced in, which limited the upside for yields and the dollar [[dollar-retreats-18-month-high-ppi-fed-hike-bets-ease-oct15-2026]].

## The Late-October Pause and Its Limits
The third week of October brought the first real relief. The 10-year dipped to an intraday 5.038% on a Thursday in that week before edging back to 5.12% on Friday [[us-treasury-yields-decades-high-oil-fed-debt-ai-oct2026]]. For the week ending October 16 the 10-year fell 15 bp to 5.09%, with the 2-year at 4.74% and the 5-year at 4.89%, as the curve flattened on a flight to safety during an equity sell-off [[strong-retail-sales-consumer-resilience-after-fed-rate-hike-oct2026]]. That flight to safety explains part of the rally, but it is not a verdict on inflation. On October 20 the 10-year sat near 5.12%, down from a 5.3% peak, while a WSJ poll found only 17 of 64 economists expecting it below 5% by the end of 2026 and most expecting it to stay above 5% into mid-2027 [[investopedia-markets-oct20-2026-dow-500-oil-pullback-treasury-5-percent]]. The same survey's central path put the 10-year at 5.1% by end-2026 and 4.78% by end-2027, which reads the dip as a pause rather than a turn [[us-treasury-yields-decades-high-oil-fed-debt-ai-oct2026]].

Pensford reads the episode as self-limiting. Higher yields restrain growth and do part of the Fed's work, and a selloff in leveraged AI plays after the hot CPI pulled the 10-year back to 5.08%. With futures pricing an 86% chance of a hold, the firm cited Bloomberg's rates team, which sees the peak as behind us, while the consensus still expected about 50 basis points more of hikes by March 2027 [[pensford-t10-headed-below-5-percent-core-cpi-oct2026]]. Raymond James adds the Treasury's November 4 refunding as a further variable. Trimming long-end issuance, possibly even eliminating the 20-year bond, could lower yields temporarily without reducing total borrowing [[raymond-james-key-catalysts-fed-earnings-refunding-midterms-oct2026]].

## Government Debt and Bond Supply
The structural case for higher-for-longer rests on supply, since persistent deficits require ever more issuance and US interest costs already exceed $1 trillion a year [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]]. Gross US debt crossed $40 trillion in August [[us-national-debt-40-trillion-fed-minutes]]. Bank of America's Hartnett, projecting $50 trillion by 2029, named debt and rising long yields as the two forces most able to end the bull market [[stock-market-record-bofa-debt-yields-risk-aug2026]], and high federal debt still appears among the drivers behind October's 2002-level yields [[daily-market-snapshot-record-highs-10yr-yield-oct2026]]. The buildup is global. The IIF tallied a record $365 trillion of debt in H1 2026 that, unlike past waves, had no discrete crisis as its trigger [[global-debt-record-365-trillion-iif-2026]]. The arithmetic still works for now because 6.3% nominal GDP growth outpaces a roughly 5.3% 10-year yield [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]], though every hike aimed at inflation also raises the government's own financing bill [[us-national-debt-40-trillion-fed-minutes]].

## AI Borrowing and Crowding Out
Treasuries now compete for savings with the AI buildout. Hyperscalers issued roughly $220–225 billion of AI-related debt in 2026 through August, about 974% more than a year earlier, and even Alphabet had to pay a 10–15 bp concession [[big-tech-ai-bond-splurge-crowding-out-weak-borrowers]]. Coverage from the August selloff through October's opening week kept listing this corporate borrowing alongside deficits and inflation as a core driver [[global-bond-selloff-chip-stocks-fomc-minutes-aug18-2026]] [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]]. A Financial Express survey of the drivers put numbers on the AI channel. ING credited AI with about one-fifth of the rise in long-dated yields, and Merrill Lynch counted nearly $280 billion of big-tech debt competing with Treasuries [[us-treasury-yields-decades-high-oil-fed-debt-ai-oct2026]]. Martin Pelletier frames rising term premiums and bond volatility as fixed-income markets asking who will fund both Washington's deficits and an AI build that Brookings estimates could exceed $10 trillion from 2025 to 2032. He warns the loop is reflexive, because higher borrowing costs make that infrastructure dearer to build [[hidden-warning-record-highs-breadth-bond-divergence]]. Financing structures are getting more creative too, with chipmakers such as Broadcom lending to customers like Anthropic and OpenAI in deals some read as vendor financing [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]] [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]]. The underlying logic is basic supply and demand: more government paper plus more corporate paper chasing the same capital means yields must rise to find buyers [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]].

## Treasury Intervention at the Long End
In August the Treasury doubled buybacks of 10- to 30-year bonds from $2 billion to $4 billion per operation, but the 30-year gave back its 20 bp drop within two days, and markets read the move as a symptom of fiscal strain [[gold-silver-debasement-trade-aug2026]]. JPMorgan's James Sullivan compared it to covering a mortgage with a credit card, since retiring long bonds while issuing more bills reshuffles maturities without reducing debt [[us-stock-selloff-treasury-yields-aug20-2026]]. A later report that Bessent might tap nearly $1 trillion in Treasury General Account funds bought only a short-lived dip to 4.625% on the 10-year [[stock-market-news-aug-26-2026-zacks]]. Standard Chartered cast buybacks plus a softer dollar as financial repression and forecast a 4.50–4.75% 10-year range [[standard-chartered-ai-capex-forecast-jackson-hole-aug2026]], a band that October's 5.365% has left far behind [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. Whether the Warsh Fed owns long-term borrowing costs stayed open [[warsh-jackson-hole-bond-market-pressure-clarify]], and by October Bessent was describing the bond moves as global rather than US-specific [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]].

## A Coordinated Global Selloff
The pressure spans developed markets. By October 1 the UK 30-year gilt had touched 6%, a first since 1998, and France's 10-year hit 4.95%, its highest since 2002 [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]]. British, French, German and Japanese borrowing costs all sit at multi-decade highs [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]], after Japan's 10-year crossed 3% for the first time since 1996 [[goldman-sachs-lower-returns-2026-headwinds]]. France has since become the flashpoint. Its spread over German Bunds passed 150 bp, the widest in about 15 years [[investors-shrug-off-bond-yield-surge-stocks-record-highs-oct5-2026]] and, in another account, the widest since 1990, raising fears of a new euro sovereign debt crisis [[five-things-week-ahead-bond-yields-french-debt-fomc-minutes-oct2026]]. Economists warned the contagion could push yields higher in other markets [[investors-shrug-off-bond-yield-surge-stocks-record-highs-oct5-2026]]. Navellier argues the slower-growing debtors are more exposed than the US because they lack its GDP growth to cushion higher rates [[stocks-grind-lower-interest-rate-pressure-fico-anthropic-sep2026]], and Pelletier notes the spillover reaches Canada, where a strong US dollar squeezes the loonie and constrains the Bank of Canada [[hidden-warning-record-highs-breadth-bond-divergence]].

## Equities: Absorbed Until They Are Not
Yields above 5% raise the hurdle for stocks twice over, offering a competitive low-risk return while squeezing corporate cash flows [[stocks-reality-check-expert-warnings-sep2026]]. On September 29 the move past 5.27% dragged the S&P 500 down 0.8% [[higher-bond-yields-pull-stocks-lower-sep2026]]. Yet October's record yields coincided with record stocks. Investors bought tech into a Nasdaq high on October 5 [[investors-shrug-off-bond-yield-surge-stocks-record-highs-oct5-2026]], and the S&P 500 posted its first record close since mid-August the next day, breaching the once-feared 5% line without major damage [[wall-st-week-ahead-bank-earnings-cpi-sp500-record-oct9-2026]]. Strong fundamentals and narrow credit spreads cushioned the move [[daily-market-snapshot-record-highs-10yr-yield-oct2026]]. Bulls explain this with growth. UBS argues yields bite mainly when growth falters, and Fundstrat sees valuations compressing only past about 5.5% [[market-every-reason-to-sell-off-resilience-yields-oct2026]]. Luke Lango says money flows to whatever can grow earnings faster than discount rates rise, which is overwhelmingly the AI complex [[investors-shrug-off-bond-yield-surge-stocks-record-highs-oct5-2026]]. The calm was less complete in mid-October, when the Nasdaq fell 4.5% and the S&P 500 1.9% over a week, their weakest since June, as borrowing costs and data-center returns weighed on AI stocks [[strong-retail-sales-consumer-resilience-after-fed-rate-hike-oct2026]]. Historical comparisons diverge. One Motley Fool column said the S&P 500 and Nasdaq had dropped an average of 10% and 12% within three months of the first hike in the last three cycles, while another cited the roughly 19% S&P 500 and 33% Nasdaq-100 declines of 2022 and Vanguard research suggesting short-run damage stays limited unless rates rise about a point more [[trump-economy-inflation-fed-hikes-correction-history-oct2026]] [[motley-fool-fed-minutes-another-hike-stocks-bonds-warsh-oct2026]]. Skeptics echo Matt Maley's tipping-point warning [[goldman-sachs-lower-returns-2026-headwinds]]. Pelletier notes that past bond-equity divergences in 1998–2000, 2007, 2018 and 2022 ended with stocks falling, though only after weeks to over a year [[hidden-warning-record-highs-breadth-bond-divergence]], and one strategist expects major indexes eventually to "cry uncle" [[wall-st-week-ahead-bank-earnings-cpi-sp500-record-oct9-2026]].

## Beneath the Index: Rate-Sensitive Sectors
The headline index understates the damage. The equal-weight S&P 500 headed for a seventh straight weekly loss, small caps posted their second-worst quarter against the S&P since 1999 with over a third of Russell 2000 members struggling to cover interest, banks fell more than 12% from their August peak, and utilities neared a bear market as short-term Treasury payouts eclipsed their dividends [[rising-yields-hidden-stock-market-pain-five-charts-oct2026]]. Pelletier counts roughly 60% of S&P 500 members more than 20% below their peaks, a breadth reading seen only in 1973–74 and 1999–2000 [[hidden-warning-record-highs-breadth-bond-divergence]]. On October 7 about 73% of index members fell, the Russell 2000 lost 1.3%, and rate-sensitive names such as CrowdStrike dropped 5% [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. These are the sectors markets flagged back in August as most exposed if rates stayed higher for longer, namely banks, real estate and smaller companies [[us-stocks-aug21-2026-higher-for-longer-rates]].

## Households, Weak Borrowers and Wall Street
Because Treasury yields benchmark lending across the economy, the selloff lands on households. It pushed the 30-year mortgage rate above 7% and raised auto-loan costs [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]], then to 7.28% in the biggest weekly rise since October 2022 [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]] and to 7.49% on MBA data by October 7, near a three-year high, as homebuilders fell 2.9% [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. Goldman Sachs and Oxford Economics expect housing to stay frozen through 2027, and surging rates have added $363 to the monthly payment on a typical new mortgage [[investopedia-markets-oct20-2026-dow-500-oil-pullback-treasury-5-percent]]. In corporate credit, a 30-year Treasury above 5.25% sets a high floor for every borrower. PIMCO warns CCC bonds maturing in 2027–2028 could see coupons roughly double, just as maturities for B-minus-and-below debt swell from $56.6 billion in 2026 to $215 billion in 2028 [[big-tech-ai-bond-splurge-crowding-out-weak-borrowers]]. Even Wall Street's record run is exposed. New York Comptroller DiNapoli warns a 5.35% 10-year could dampen dealmaking, trading returns and consumer spending behind a $45.9 billion first-half profit haul that underpins the city and state budgets [[wall-street-record-profits-h1-2026-rising-yields-risk]].

## Gold and the Fiscal-Credibility Trade
Gold's link to yields runs in two directions. On September 29 it fell 3.5% as higher yields made a non-yielding asset less appealing [[higher-bond-yields-pull-stocks-lower-sep2026]]. In August, however, the buyback news pushed it above $4,600 on a "dual-path" logic: gold gains if intervention works (lower opportunity cost, weaker dollar) and also if it fails (sovereign-credit and safe-haven demand) [[gold-breaks-4600-treasury-buyback-dual-path]]. The failure path is the debasement thesis, where chronic deficits, debt service above $1 trillion and a Treasury seen propping up its own bonds steer investors toward gold, silver and Bitcoin [[gold-silver-debasement-trade-aug2026]].

## Bonds as an Income and Hedge Asset
For bond buyers the selloff has rebuilt income. U.S. Bank calls 4–5% Treasuries and 5–7% corporates the best starting point in over a decade, and notes that starting yield is the strongest predictor of decade-long bond returns [[q3-2026-investment-outlook-us-bank]]. A 5.25% 30-year Treasury is pitched as a better downturn hedge than cash, because long bonds rally when recessions bring rate cuts [[stock-market-crash-long-term-bonds-hedge-tlt]]. The same yields raise the bar for income stocks, pointing investors toward payers such as AGNC, Energy Transfer and Verizon whose yields sit well above the benchmark [[10-year-treasury-yield-high-dividend-stocks-agnc-et-vz]]. Morningstar's Christine Benz cautions that stagflation could hurt stocks and bonds at once, favoring short-to-intermediate high-quality bonds and TIPS over high-yield and emerging-market debt [[stocks-reality-check-expert-warnings-sep2026]].

**T3. A higher discount rate shrinks the present value of distant cash flows** — Assets whose value lies furthest in the future lose the most when rates rise, and valuations hold only where earnings grow faster than the discount rate climbs. · *model:* [[Discounted Cash Flow]]
