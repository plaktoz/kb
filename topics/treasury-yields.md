# Treasury Yields and the Bond Market

This topic follows the 2026 bond selloff that carried US Treasury yields to their highest levels since 2002: what is pushing long-term borrowing costs up, how Washington has tried to lean against it, and how those costs ripple into stocks, housing, credit and alternative assets (Fed rate decisions themselves live in [[federal-reserve-monetary-policy]]).

## The Climb to Multi-Decade Highs
The long end moved first, with a mid-August 30-year auction clearing at the highest yield since 2001 [[stock-market-record-bofa-debt-yields-risk-aug2026]] and the 30-year touching 5.337% intraday days later, a level unseen since 2007 [[global-bond-selloff-chip-stocks-fomc-minutes-aug18-2026]]. The 10-year caught up through September, sitting near 4.8% early in the month [[10-year-treasury-yield-high-dividend-stocks-agnc-et-vz]] before breaking 5% on September 15 for the first time since October 2023 [[10-year-treasury-yield-tops-5-percent-sep2026]], then setting new marks almost daily in the quarter's final week, from 5.24% on September 28 [[wall-street-dips-us-iran-standoff-sep2026]] to 5.304% on September 30 [[10-year-treasury-yield-24-year-high-sep2026]]. On October 1 it printed 5.34% intraday, a dot-com-bust level, and closed at 5.24%, the highest finish since 2007, while the 30-year reached 5.69% [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]].

## October: New Highs and a Rising Term Premium
The first full week of Q4 pushed the marks further. On October 5 the 10-year touched 5.349%, its highest since April 3, 2002, and the 30-year 5.703%, a late-May-2002 level, extending a six-week selloff that Infrastructure Capital's Jay Hatfield called momentum-driven with room to run, since the 10-year usually trades about 100 bp over the terminal fed funds rate [[10-year-treasury-yield-fresh-2002-high-ism-services-oct5-2026]]. Two days later the 10-year reached 5.365% and the 30-year 5.732%, though indirect bidders took 80.3% of a $39 billion 10-year auction against a 72.4% average [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]], and that solid sale brought some relief even as the New York Fed's estimate of the 10-year term premium hit its highest since 2014 [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. Edward Jones tallied a weekly peak of 5.31% before a settle near 5.24% [[daily-market-snapshot-record-highs-10yr-yield-oct2026]], and the 10-year ended the week near 5.23% [[wall-st-week-ahead-bank-earnings-cpi-sp500-record-oct9-2026]].

## Growth, Inflation and Oil as Drivers
This selloff is unusual because investors are dumping bonds on the view that the economy is running too hot, not fleeing to them for safety, with AI spending propping up growth, fuel prices lifting inflation and auction buyers demanding more compensation [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]]. Oil has been the most visible accelerant, from Brent above $109 triggering the break through 5% [[10-year-treasury-yield-tops-5-percent-sep2026]] to slowed Strait of Hormuz traffic holding crude above $100 while Fed officials Barr and Kashkari saw no clear route back to 2% inflation [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]], and in October Brent above $101 plus Houthi attacks revived fears of sticky inflation [[stocks-fall-bond-yields-oil-fed-minutes-halt-record-rally-oct7-2026]] just as the ISM services price index rose to 74, lifting its 12-month average to the highest since March 2023 [[10-year-treasury-yield-fresh-2002-high-ism-services-oct5-2026]]. Decompositions complicate the inflation story: ClearBridge attributed most of the rise from late February to mid-September to real rates (+50 bps) rather than inflation expectations (+15 bps) or term premium (+17 bps) [[stocks-resilient-despite-higher-bond-yields-sp500-2026]], and Reuters' Morning Bid likewise found long-run inflation expectations stable, crediting the October move mostly to a Fed rethink while tying the swelling term premium to debt levels, political risk and doubts about long-term government financing [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]].

## Soft Data, Hawkish Fed, Sticky Yields
Relief has repeatedly failed to stick. Core PCE came in at 3% against a 3.3% forecast, yet yields dipped only briefly before the long end resumed climbing [[10-year-treasury-yield-24-year-high-sep2026]], and the 29,000-job September payrolls miss knocked the 10-year to about 5.16% before it rebounded toward 5.30% even as October hike odds collapsed to 23% from 64% a week earlier [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]], with oil's rebound credited for the reversal [[us-stocks-near-record-jobs-report-tesla-nvidia-nike-oct9-2026]] and the jobs data delivering only a brief Treasury rally [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]]. The September FOMC minutes then showed a unanimous first hike since 2023 approved on split rationales, with October hike odds sliding to 17.2% [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]], but most policymakers expected further increases, Governor Waller said more would likely be needed, and markets kept pricing three more hikes starting in December [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. That combination of resilient growth and energy- and AI-driven inflation is what keeps expectations anchored on higher for longer [[five-things-week-ahead-bond-yields-french-debt-fomc-minutes-oct2026]], consistent with the view that the forces behind high yields extend well beyond the next meeting [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]].

## Government Debt and Bond Supply
The structural case for higher-for-longer rests on supply, since persistent deficits require ever more issuance and US interest costs already exceed $1 trillion a year [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]]. Gross US debt crossed $40 trillion in August [[us-national-debt-40-trillion-fed-minutes]], Bank of America's Hartnett, projecting $50 trillion by 2029, named debt and rising long yields as the two forces most able to end the bull market [[stock-market-record-bofa-debt-yields-risk-aug2026]], and high federal debt still appears among the drivers behind October's 2002-level yields [[daily-market-snapshot-record-highs-10yr-yield-oct2026]]. The buildup is global, with the IIF tallying a record $365 trillion of debt in H1 2026 that, unlike past waves, had no discrete crisis as its trigger [[global-debt-record-365-trillion-iif-2026]]. The arithmetic still works for now because 6.3% nominal GDP growth outpaces a roughly 5.3% 10-year yield [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]], though every hike aimed at inflation also raises the government's own financing bill [[us-national-debt-40-trillion-fed-minutes]].

## AI Borrowing and Crowding Out
Treasuries now compete for savings with the AI buildout, as hyperscalers issued roughly $220–225 billion of AI-related debt in 2026 through August, about 974% more than a year earlier, and even Alphabet had to pay a 10–15 bp concession [[big-tech-ai-bond-splurge-crowding-out-weak-borrowers]]; coverage from the August selloff through October's opening week kept listing this corporate borrowing alongside deficits and inflation as a core driver [[global-bond-selloff-chip-stocks-fomc-minutes-aug18-2026]] [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]]. Martin Pelletier frames rising term premiums and bond volatility as fixed-income markets asking who will fund both Washington's deficits and an AI build that Brookings estimates could exceed $10 trillion from 2025 to 2032, warning the loop is reflexive because higher borrowing costs make that infrastructure dearer to build [[hidden-warning-record-highs-breadth-bond-divergence]]. Financing structures are getting more creative too, with chipmakers such as Broadcom lending to customers like Anthropic and OpenAI in deals some read as vendor financing [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]] [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]]. The underlying logic is basic supply and demand: more government paper plus more corporate paper chasing the same capital means yields must rise to find buyers [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]].

## Treasury Intervention at the Long End
In August the Treasury doubled buybacks of 10- to 30-year bonds from $2 billion to $4 billion per operation, but the 30-year gave back its 20 bp drop within two days and markets read the move as a symptom of fiscal strain [[gold-silver-debasement-trade-aug2026]]. JPMorgan's James Sullivan compared it to covering a mortgage with a credit card, since retiring long bonds while issuing more bills reshuffles maturities without reducing debt [[us-stock-selloff-treasury-yields-aug20-2026]], and a later report that Bessent might tap nearly $1 trillion in Treasury General Account funds bought only a short-lived dip to 4.625% on the 10-year [[stock-market-news-aug-26-2026-zacks]]. Standard Chartered cast buybacks plus a softer dollar as financial repression and forecast a 4.50–4.75% 10-year range [[standard-chartered-ai-capex-forecast-jackson-hole-aug2026]], a band October's 5.365% has left far behind [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]], while the question of whether the Warsh Fed owns long-term borrowing costs stayed open [[warsh-jackson-hole-bond-market-pressure-clarify]] and Bessent by October was describing the bond moves as global rather than US-specific [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]].

## A Coordinated Global Selloff
The pressure spans developed markets. By October 1 the UK 30-year gilt had touched 6%, a first since 1998, and France's 10-year hit 4.95%, its highest since 2002 [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]], leaving British, French, German and Japanese borrowing costs all at multi-decade highs [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]], after Japan's 10-year had crossed 3% for the first time since 1996 [[goldman-sachs-lower-returns-2026-headwinds]]. France has since become the flashpoint: its spread over German Bunds passed 150 bp, described as the widest in about 15 years [[investors-shrug-off-bond-yield-surge-stocks-record-highs-oct5-2026]] and elsewhere as the widest since 1990, raising fears of a new euro sovereign debt crisis [[five-things-week-ahead-bond-yields-french-debt-fomc-minutes-oct2026]], with economists warning the contagion could push yields higher elsewhere [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]]. Navellier argues the slower-growing debtors are more exposed than the US because they lack its GDP growth to cushion higher rates [[stocks-grind-lower-interest-rate-pressure-fico-anthropic-sep2026]], and Pelletier notes the spillover reaches Canada, where a strong US dollar squeezes the loonie and the Bank of Canada's options [[hidden-warning-record-highs-breadth-bond-divergence]].

## Equities: Absorbed Until They Are Not
Yields above 5% raise the hurdle for stocks twice over, offering a competitive low-risk return while squeezing corporate cash flows [[stocks-reality-check-expert-warnings-sep2026]], and on September 29 the move past 5.27% dragged the S&P 500 down 0.8% [[higher-bond-yields-pull-stocks-lower-sep2026]]. Yet October's record yields coincided with record stocks, as investors bought tech into a Nasdaq high on October 5 [[investors-shrug-off-bond-yield-surge-stocks-record-highs-oct5-2026]] and the S&P 500 posted its first record close since mid-August the next day, breaching the once-feared 5% line without major damage [[wall-st-week-ahead-bank-earnings-cpi-sp500-record-oct9-2026]], cushioned by strong fundamentals and narrow credit spreads [[daily-market-snapshot-record-highs-10yr-yield-oct2026]]. Bulls explain this with growth, as UBS argues yields bite mainly when growth falters and Fundstrat sees valuations compressing only past about 5.5% [[market-every-reason-to-sell-off-resilience-yields-oct2026]], while Luke Lango says money flows to whatever can grow earnings faster than discount rates rise, overwhelmingly the AI complex [[investors-shrug-off-bond-yield-surge-stocks-record-highs-oct5-2026]]. Skeptics echo Matt Maley's tipping-point warning [[goldman-sachs-lower-returns-2026-headwinds]]: Pelletier notes past bond-equity divergences in 1998–2000, 2007, 2018 and 2022 ended with stocks falling, albeit after weeks to over a year [[hidden-warning-record-highs-breadth-bond-divergence]], and one strategist expects major indexes eventually to "cry uncle" [[wall-st-week-ahead-bank-earnings-cpi-sp500-record-oct9-2026]].

## Beneath the Index: Rate-Sensitive Sectors
The headline index understates the damage, as the equal-weight S&P 500 headed for a seventh straight weekly loss, small caps posted their second-worst quarter against the S&P since 1999 with over a third of Russell 2000 members struggling to cover interest, banks fell more than 12% from their August peak, and utilities neared a bear market as short-term Treasury payouts eclipsed their dividends [[rising-yields-hidden-stock-market-pain-five-charts-oct2026]]. Pelletier counts roughly 60% of S&P 500 members more than 20% below their peaks, a breadth reading seen only in 1973–74 and 1999–2000 [[hidden-warning-record-highs-breadth-bond-divergence]], and on October 7 about 73% of index members fell, the Russell 2000 lost 1.3% and rate-sensitive names such as CrowdStrike dropped 5% [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. These are the sectors markets flagged back in August as most exposed if rates stayed higher for longer, namely banks, real estate and smaller companies [[us-stocks-aug21-2026-higher-for-longer-rates]].

## Households, Weak Borrowers and Wall Street
Because Treasury yields benchmark lending across the economy, the selloff lands on households, pushing the 30-year mortgage rate above 7% and raising auto-loan costs [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]], then to 7.28% in the biggest weekly rise since October 2022 [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]] and to 7.49% on MBA data by October 7, near a three-year high, as homebuilders fell 2.9% [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. In corporate credit, a 30-year Treasury above 5.25% sets a high floor for every borrower, and PIMCO warns CCC bonds maturing in 2027–2028 could see coupons roughly double just as maturities for B-minus-and-below debt swell from $56.6 billion in 2026 to $215 billion in 2028 [[big-tech-ai-bond-splurge-crowding-out-weak-borrowers]]. Even Wall Street's record run is exposed, as New York Comptroller DiNapoli warns a 5.35% 10-year could dampen dealmaking, trading returns and consumers behind a $45.9 billion first-half profit haul that underpins the city and state budgets [[wall-street-record-profits-h1-2026-rising-yields-risk]].

## Gold and the Fiscal-Credibility Trade
Gold's link to yields runs in two directions. On September 29 it fell 3.5% as higher yields made a non-yielding asset less appealing [[higher-bond-yields-pull-stocks-lower-sep2026]], yet in August the buyback news pushed it above $4,600 on a "dual-path" logic in which gold gains if intervention works (lower opportunity cost, weaker dollar) and also if it fails (sovereign-credit and safe-haven demand) [[gold-breaks-4600-treasury-buyback-dual-path]]. The failure path is the debasement thesis, where chronic deficits, debt service above $1 trillion and a Treasury seen propping up its own bonds steer investors toward gold, silver and Bitcoin [[gold-silver-debasement-trade-aug2026]].

## Bonds as an Income and Hedge Asset
For bond buyers the selloff has rebuilt income, with U.S. Bank calling 4–5% Treasuries and 5–7% corporates the best starting point in over a decade and noting that starting yield is the strongest predictor of decade-long bond returns [[q3-2026-investment-outlook-us-bank]], while a 5.25% 30-year Treasury is pitched as a better downturn hedge than cash because long bonds rally when recessions bring rate cuts [[stock-market-crash-long-term-bonds-hedge-tlt]]. The same yields lift the bar for income stocks, pointing investors toward payers such as AGNC, Energy Transfer and Verizon whose yields sit well above the benchmark [[10-year-treasury-yield-high-dividend-stocks-agnc-et-vz]]. Morningstar's Christine Benz cautions that stagflation could hurt stocks and bonds simultaneously, favoring short-to-intermediate high-quality bonds and TIPS over high-yield and emerging-market debt [[stocks-reality-check-expert-warnings-sep2026]].

**T2. The risk-free yield is the hurdle every other asset must clear** — As the guaranteed return on government debt rises, any riskier or non-yielding asset becomes relatively less attractive unless its expected return rises to match. · *model:* [[Opportunity Cost]]

**T3. A higher discount rate shrinks the present value of distant cash flows** — Assets whose value lies furthest in the future lose the most when rates rise, and valuations hold only where earnings grow faster than the discount rate climbs. · *model:* [[Discounted Cash Flow]]

**T4. Debt is sustainable while growth outpaces its interest rate** — A debt burden stays stable relative to output only as long as nominal growth exceeds the borrowing rate, so higher rates raise the debtor's own financing bill and hit slow-growing debtors hardest. · *model:* [[Debt Dynamics (r vs g)]]
