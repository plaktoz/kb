# Treasury Yields and the Bond Market

This topic follows the 2026 bond selloff that carried US Treasury yields to their highest levels since the early 2000s: what is pushing long-term borrowing costs up, how Washington has tried to lean against it, and how those costs ripple into stocks, housing, credit and alternative assets (Fed rate decisions themselves live in [[federal-reserve-monetary-policy]]).

## The Climb to Multi-Decade Highs
The long end moved first, with a mid-August 30-year auction clearing at the highest yield since 2001 [[stock-market-record-bofa-debt-yields-risk-aug2026]] and the 30-year touching 5.337% intraday days later, a level unseen since 2007 [[global-bond-selloff-chip-stocks-fomc-minutes-aug18-2026]]. The 10-year caught up through September, sitting near 4.8% early in the month [[10-year-treasury-yield-high-dividend-stocks-agnc-et-vz]] before breaking 5% on September 15 for the first time since October 2023 [[10-year-treasury-yield-tops-5-percent-sep2026]]. In the quarter's final week it set new marks almost daily, from 5.24% on September 28 [[wall-street-dips-us-iran-standoff-sep2026]] and 5.27% intraday a day later [[higher-bond-yields-pull-stocks-lower-sep2026]] to 5.304% on September 30 [[10-year-treasury-yield-24-year-high-sep2026]]. The peak so far came on October 1, when the 10-year printed 5.34% intraday, a level last seen in the dot-com bust, and closed at 5.24%, the highest finish since 2007, while the 30-year reached 5.69% [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]].

## Growth, Inflation and Oil as Drivers
This selloff is unusual because investors are dumping bonds on the view that the economy is running too hot, not fleeing to them for safety, with AI spending propping up growth, fuel prices lifting inflation and auction buyers demanding more compensation [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]]. Oil has been the most visible accelerant: Brent above $109 triggered the break through 5% [[10-year-treasury-yield-tops-5-percent-sep2026]], the unresolved US-Iran standoff kept crude and yields elevated together in late September [[wall-street-dips-us-iran-standoff-sep2026]], and slowed Strait of Hormuz traffic has held oil above $100 and diesel above $6 while Fed officials Barr and Kashkari see no clear route back to 2% inflation [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]]. A mid-September decomposition by ClearBridge complicates the inflation story, attributing most of the rise since late February to real rates (+50 bps) rather than inflation expectations (+15 bps) or term premium (+17 bps), which it read as a growth signal [[stocks-resilient-despite-higher-bond-yields-sp500-2026]].

## When Good News Stops Helping
By late September the bond market had begun ignoring relief. Core PCE came in at 3% against a 3.3% forecast, yet yields dipped only briefly before the long end resumed climbing [[10-year-treasury-yield-24-year-high-sep2026]], and a day on which WTI fell 3.7% still saw the 30-year rise for a sixth straight session, a divergence pointing to debt and inflation fears outweighing cheaper energy [[stocks-grind-lower-interest-rate-pressure-fico-anthropic-sep2026]]. The 29,000-job September payrolls miss did knock yields off their early-2000s highs [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]], but the 10-year recovered from 5.16% back toward 5.30% even as October hike odds collapsed to 23% from 64% a week earlier [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]], consistent with the argument that the forces behind high yields extend well beyond the next Fed meeting [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]].

## Government Debt and Bond Supply
The structural case for higher-for-longer rests on supply, since persistent deficits require ever more issuance and US interest costs already exceed $1 trillion a year [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]]. Gross US debt crossed $40 trillion in August [[us-national-debt-40-trillion-fed-minutes]], and Bank of America's Hartnett, projecting $50 trillion by 2029 after July's $432.3 billion deficit, named debt and rising long yields as the two forces most able to end the bull market [[stock-market-record-bofa-debt-yields-risk-aug2026]], while other strategists note the pile grows by roughly $2 trillion a year with no credible fiscal plan [[stocks-reality-check-expert-warnings-sep2026]]. The buildup is global, with the IIF tallying a record $365 trillion of debt across all sectors in H1 2026, a sixth straight quarterly rise led by China and the US that, unlike past waves, had no discrete crisis as its trigger [[global-debt-record-365-trillion-iif-2026]]. The arithmetic still works for now because 6.3% nominal GDP growth outpaces a roughly 5.3% 10-year yield, keeping debt stable against output [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]], though every hike aimed at inflation also raises the government's own financing bill [[us-national-debt-40-trillion-fed-minutes]].

## AI Borrowing and Crowding Out
Treasuries now compete for savings with the AI buildout, as hyperscalers issued roughly $220–225 billion of AI-related debt in 2026 through August, about 974% more than a year earlier, and even Alphabet had to pay a 10–15 bp concession on its August deal [[big-tech-ai-bond-splurge-crowding-out-weak-borrowers]]. Coverage of the August global selloff listed this corporate issuance alongside fiscal deficits and inflation as a core driver [[global-bond-selloff-chip-stocks-fomc-minutes-aug18-2026]], and Yardeni added an unwinding yen carry trade to AI debt as sources of selling pressure [[10-year-treasury-yield-tops-5-percent-sep2026]]. The logic is basic supply and demand: more government paper plus more corporate paper chasing the same capital means yields must rise to find buyers [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]].

## Treasury Intervention at the Long End
In August the Treasury doubled buybacks of 10- to 30-year bonds from $2 billion to $4 billion per operation, but the 30-year gave back its 20 bp drop within two days and markets read the move as a symptom of fiscal strain [[gold-silver-debasement-trade-aug2026]]. JPMorgan's James Sullivan compared it to covering a mortgage with a credit card, since retiring long bonds while issuing more bills reshuffles maturities without reducing debt, and stocks slid on August 20 once long yields climbed again [[us-stock-selloff-treasury-yields-aug20-2026]]; a later report that Bessent might tap nearly $1 trillion in the Treasury General Account for buybacks bought only a short-lived dip to 4.625% on the 10-year [[stock-market-news-aug-26-2026-zacks]]. The interventions left open whether the Warsh Fed would treat long-term borrowing costs as its responsibility or leave them to an increasingly active Treasury [[warsh-jackson-hole-bond-market-pressure-clarify]], and Standard Chartered cast buybacks plus a softer dollar as financial repression while forecasting a 4.50–4.75% range for the 10-year [[standard-chartered-ai-capex-forecast-jackson-hole-aug2026]], a band the October high of 5.34% has since left well behind [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]].

## A Coordinated Global Selloff
The pressure spans developed markets. On August 18 Japan's 10-year hit its highest since at least 2006 and German and French 30-year yields reached peaks last seen in 2011 and 2008 [[global-bond-selloff-chip-stocks-fomc-minutes-aug18-2026]], after which Japan's 10-year crossed 3% for the first time since 1996 [[goldman-sachs-lower-returns-2026-headwinds]]. By October 1 the UK 30-year gilt had touched 6%, a first since 1998, and France's 10-year hit 4.95%, its highest since 2002 [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]], leaving British, French, German and Japanese borrowing costs all at multi-decade highs [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]]. Navellier argues the slower-growing debtors among them are more exposed than the US because they lack its GDP growth to cushion higher rates [[stocks-grind-lower-interest-rate-pressure-fico-anthropic-sep2026]].

## Equities: Absorbed Until They Are Not
Yields above 5% raise the hurdle for stocks twice over, offering a competitive low-risk return while also squeezing corporate cash flows, the "double whammy" Pasquariello describes [[stocks-reality-check-expert-warnings-sep2026]]. Through the summer equities mostly absorbed it, with chip stocks sliding more than 5% on August 18 while broad indexes stayed calm [[global-bond-selloff-chip-stocks-fomc-minutes-aug18-2026]], and in mid-September the S&P 500 sat only about 2% below its record on 52% Q2 earnings growth, which ClearBridge's Schulze took as evidence yields were not yet a material threat [[stocks-resilient-despite-higher-bond-yields-sp500-2026]]. Miller Tabak's Matt Maley warned that such tolerance lasts only until a tipping point, and Goldman's Oppenheimer cut forward return expectations to mid-to-high single digits partly on yields [[goldman-sachs-lower-returns-2026-headwinds]]; by September 29 the 10-year's move past 5.27% was enough to drag the S&P 500 down 0.8% [[higher-bond-yields-pull-stocks-lower-sep2026]].

## Beneath the Index: Rate-Sensitive Sectors
The headline index understates the damage, because the AI trade has kept the S&P 500 within 2% of a record while the equal-weight index heads for a seventh straight weekly loss, a streak seen only after the 2002 dot-com bust and in the 2022 bear market [[rising-yields-hidden-stock-market-pain-five-charts-oct2026]]. The same analysis shows small caps posting their second-worst quarter against the S&P since 1999 with over a third of Russell 2000 members struggling to cover interest, banks down more than 12% from their August peak, utilities near a bear market as short-term Treasury payouts eclipse their dividends, and unprofitable tech, whose distant cash flows suffer most from higher discount rates, down 11% in Q3 [[rising-yields-hidden-stock-market-pain-five-charts-oct2026]]. These are the sectors markets already flagged in August as most exposed if rates stayed higher for longer, namely banks, real estate and smaller companies [[us-stocks-aug21-2026-higher-for-longer-rates]].

## Households and Weak Borrowers
Because Treasury yields benchmark lending across the economy, the selloff lands on households, pushing the 30-year mortgage rate above 7% for the first time since early 2025 and raising auto-loan costs [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]] while already weighing on home sales [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]]. The mortgage rate then jumped to 7.28%, its largest weekly rise since October 2022 [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]]. In corporate credit, a 30-year Treasury above 5.25% sets a high floor for every borrower, and PIMCO warns CCC bonds maturing in 2027–2028 could see coupons roughly double on refinancing just as maturities for B-minus-and-below debt swell from $56.6 billion in 2026 to $215 billion in 2028 [[big-tech-ai-bond-splurge-crowding-out-weak-borrowers]].

## Gold and the Fiscal-Credibility Trade
Gold's link to yields runs in two directions. On September 29 it fell 3.5% as higher yields made a non-yielding asset less appealing [[higher-bond-yields-pull-stocks-lower-sep2026]], yet in August the buyback news pushed it above $4,600 on a "dual-path" logic in which gold gains if intervention works (lower opportunity cost, weaker dollar) and also if it fails (sovereign-credit and safe-haven demand) [[gold-breaks-4600-treasury-buyback-dual-path]]. The failure path is the debasement thesis, where chronic deficits, debt service above $1 trillion and a Treasury seen propping up its own bonds steer investors toward gold, silver and Bitcoin as stores of value [[gold-silver-debasement-trade-aug2026]].

## Bonds as an Income and Hedge Asset
For bond buyers the selloff has rebuilt income, with U.S. Bank calling 4–5% Treasuries and 5–7% corporates the best starting point in over a decade and noting that starting yield is the strongest predictor of decade-long bond returns [[q3-2026-investment-outlook-us-bank]], while a 5.25% 30-year Treasury is pitched as a better downturn hedge than cash because long bonds rally when recessions bring rate cuts [[stock-market-crash-long-term-bonds-hedge-tlt]]. The same yields lift the bar for income stocks, so with the 10-year near 4.8% investors are pointed toward payers such as AGNC, Energy Transfer and Verizon whose yields sit well above the benchmark [[10-year-treasury-yield-high-dividend-stocks-agnc-et-vz]]. Morningstar's Christine Benz cautions that stagflation could hurt stocks and bonds simultaneously, favoring short-to-intermediate high-quality bonds and TIPS over high-yield and emerging-market debt [[stocks-reality-check-expert-warnings-sep2026]].
