# Treasury Yields and the Bond Market

This topic tracks the 2026 Treasury selloff, which carried the 10-year yield to its highest levels since 2002 in October, and asks whether the early-November dip below 5% holds, what keeps long-term borrowing costs elevated, and how they ripple into stocks, housing, credit and alternative assets (Fed rate decisions themselves live in [[federal-reserve-monetary-policy]]).

## The Climb to Multi-Decade Highs
The long end moved first, with a mid-August 30-year auction clearing at the highest yield since 2001 [[stock-market-record-bofa-debt-yields-risk-aug2026]] and the 30-year touching 5.337% intraday, a level unseen since 2007 [[global-bond-selloff-chip-stocks-fomc-minutes-aug18-2026]]. The 10-year broke 5% on September 15 for the first time since October 2023 [[10-year-treasury-yield-tops-5-percent-sep2026]] and kept setting marks into the quarter's end, reaching 5.304% on September 30 [[10-year-treasury-yield-24-year-high-sep2026]]. On October 1 it printed 5.34% intraday, a dot-com-bust level, and closed at 5.24%, the highest finish since 2007, while the 30-year reached 5.69% [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]].

## October Peak and the Term Premium
The first full week of October pushed the 10-year to 5.349%, its highest since April 2002, and Infrastructure Capital's Jay Hatfield called the move momentum-driven with room to run [[10-year-treasury-yield-fresh-2002-high-ism-services-oct5-2026]]. A $39 billion 10-year auction still drew indirect bidders for 80.3% of the offering against a 72.4% average, even as the New York Fed's term premium estimate reached its highest since 2014 [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. Reuters' Morning Bid found long-run inflation expectations stable and tied the swelling premium to debt levels, political risk and doubts about how long-term government financing will be funded [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]].

## Too Hot to Hold: Growth, Inflation and Oil
Investors are dumping bonds because the economy looks too strong rather than fleeing to them for safety, with AI spending propping up growth, fuel lifting inflation and auction buyers demanding more compensation [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]]. Oil has been the most visible accelerant, with Brent settling at $116.72 in the week before the Fed met, its highest since April [[five-things-moving-markets-oil-fed-big-tech-oct25-2026]]. Decompositions complicate the inflation story, since ClearBridge attributed most of the rise from late February to mid-September to real rates rather than to inflation expectations or term premium [[stocks-resilient-despite-higher-bond-yields-sp500-2026]].

## Hot Data, Hawkish Fed and Sticky Yields
Relief has repeatedly failed to stick. The 29,000-job September payrolls report pushed the 10-year to about 5.16% before it rebounded toward 5.30%, even as October hike odds collapsed to 23% from 64% a week earlier [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]] [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. The September minutes showed a unanimous first hike since 2023 approved on split rationales, and with Governor Waller saying more would likely be needed, markets kept pricing three more hikes starting in December [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. September CPI came in hot at 3.7% against a 3.6% forecast, with core at 2.6%, and lifted the 2-year to about 4.91% and the 10-year to about 5.29% [[september-cpi-3-7-percent-hot-inflation-fed-rate-cuts-at-risk-oct2026]].

## Reading the Composition of Data
The preview for the September CPI argued that the 2-year tracks near-term policy bets while the 10-year blends rate expectations, growth, inflation expectations and term premium, so the composition of the print would steer yields more than the headline [[september-cpi-preview-energy-headline-vs-core-fed-oct2026]]. Following hot producer prices, the biggest rise in four and a half years, Treasuries rallied on safe-haven flows tied to AI-debt worries and on Fed officials saying they could wait, lifting the odds of an October hold to 72% [[dollar-retreats-18-month-high-ppi-fed-hike-bets-ease-oct15-2026]]. Strategists judged the hawkishness already priced in, which limited the upside for both yields and the dollar [[dollar-retreats-18-month-high-ppi-fed-hike-bets-ease-oct15-2026]].

## The Late-October Pause and Its Limits
The third week of October brought the first real relief, with the 10-year dipping to an intraday 5.038% before edging back to 5.12% [[us-treasury-yields-decades-high-oil-fed-debt-ai-oct2026]]. A WSJ poll found only 17 of 64 economists expecting it below 5% by the end of 2026, and most expecting it to stay above 5% into mid-2027 [[investopedia-markets-oct20-2026-dow-500-oil-pullback-treasury-5-percent]]. Pensford read the dip as self-limiting, since a selloff in leveraged AI plays pulled the 10-year to 5.08% and Bloomberg's rates team saw the peak as behind us [[pensford-t10-headed-below-5-percent-core-cpi-oct2026]].

## Hold, Hawkish Messaging and a Growth Surprise
The Fed held at 3.75%–4.00% on October 28 by 10 votes to 2, with Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack dissenting in favor of a hike, and Chair Warsh gave no forward guidance in remarks that read as hawkish and pushed the 10-year back above 5% [[us-jobs-report-week-peso-real-fed-repricing-nov2026]] [[futures-drop-alphabet-9-8-percent-q3-miss-microsoft-beat-warsh-hold-oct2026]]. On October 29 the 10-year closed at 5.011% after third-quarter GDP grew 3.4% annualized against a 3.1% forecast and September PCE rose 3.6% year over year, with futures pricing an 83% chance of a December hike [[10-year-treasury-yield-above-5-gdp-pce-cnbc-oct29-2026]]. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 4.9%, the fastest pace since Q2 2021, while a surge in AI-equipment imports subtracted 3.32 points from headline growth [[us-q3-gdp-private-demand-ai-imports-debt-to-gdp-wolf-street]], and Thornburg's Ali Hassan read the Fed's message as a softer policy path rather than an inflation scare [[10-year-treasury-yield-above-5-gdp-pce-cnbc-oct29-2026]].

## Early November: Oil, Refunding and a Broken Correlation
On Monday, November 2, Brent fell 4.5% to $108.37 on renewed U.S.-Iran diplomacy, and the 10-year slipped 1 bp to 4.918%, with the UK 10-year gilt posting the biggest drop [[treasury-european-yields-fall-oil-drop-refunding-midterms-nov2-2026]]. BlackRock's Jeffrey Rosenberg says the roughly 80% oil-Treasury yield correlation that held through 2026 broke in October, when oil rose while yields fell, because markets now price AI risk above geopolitical risk [[oil-treasury-correlation-breaks-ai-risk-outranks-geopolitics-blackrock-rosenberg]]. Ahead lay Wednesday's quarterly refunding and an October payrolls report expected at 55,000, against 29,000 in September [[treasury-european-yields-fall-oil-drop-refunding-midterms-nov2-2026]].

## Election Week: A Rally Reversed and a Flight to Bills
Wednesday's rally after the Democratic sweep of Congress on November 3 favored AI platforms over infrastructure suppliers, but it was fully reversed on Thursday, when the S&P 500 fell 4.36%, the Nasdaq 5.52% and the SOX index about 12%, since platform capex is chipmaker revenue [[right-result-wrong-trade-election-ai-selloff-consolidated-wealth-nov6-2026]]. Traders bought front-end Treasuries on worries that the post-midterm political shift could stall the AI buildout: the 2-year fell 10.4 bp to 4.48%, while the 10-year fell only about 5 bp, which the note attributes to hesitation before Friday's jobs report [[treasury-flight-to-safety-tech-selloff-money-market-record-nov5-2026]]. Money-market assets reached a record $8.016 trillion and December hike odds fell to 66.1% from 82.7%, while UBS still expected hikes in December and March [[treasury-flight-to-safety-tech-selloff-money-market-record-nov5-2026]].

## Supply Feedback and Duration Choices
Osterweis's John Sheehan argues rising rates will not reverse soon, because new issuance rises whenever yields fall, capping any bond rally, and because rising interest expense forces more government borrowing [[bond-investor-sheehan-rising-rates-ai-trade-risk]]. He keeps duration near 1.75 years against a benchmark of about 3, collecting higher coupons while limiting losses if rates rise further, and he warns that continued issuance pressure could push some high-yield issuers into trouble [[bond-investor-sheehan-rising-rates-ai-trade-risk]].

## Government Debt, Growth and a Thin Margin
The structural case for higher-for-longer rests on supply, since persistent deficits require ever more issuance and US interest costs already exceed $1 trillion a year [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]]. Gross debt crossed $40 trillion in August, and every hike aimed at inflation also raises the government's own financing bill [[us-national-debt-40-trillion-fed-minutes]]. Nominal GDP growth of 6.3% still outpaces a roughly 5.3% 10-year yield, but Treasury debt grew 1.8% on the quarter against 1.37% for GDP, lifting debt-to-GDP to 121.7% [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]] [[us-q3-gdp-private-demand-ai-imports-debt-to-gdp-wolf-street]]. BofA's Michael Hartnett, projecting $50 trillion of debt by 2029, named debt and rising long yields as the two forces most able to end the bull market [[stock-market-record-bofa-debt-yields-risk-aug2026]].

## A Global Debt Wave
The IIF tallied a record $365 trillion of debt across government, household, financial and corporate sectors in the first half of 2026, the sixth straight quarterly increase, and unlike the 2008 and Covid episodes it had no discrete shock as its trigger [[global-debt-record-365-trillion-iif-2026]]. The bond selloff is synchronized, with UK, French, German and Japanese borrowing costs at multi-decade highs [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]] and France's spread over Bunds passing 150 bp, the widest in about 15 years [[investors-shrug-off-bond-yield-surge-stocks-record-highs-oct5-2026]]. Fitch cut France's outlook to negative on weaker growth and a wider deficit while affirming A+ [[treasury-european-yields-fall-oil-drop-refunding-midterms-nov2-2026]], and Navellier argues that weaker-growth debtors such as Japan, the UK and France face rate pressure without the US's growth to cushion it [[stocks-grind-lower-interest-rate-pressure-fico-anthropic-sep2026]].

## AI Borrowing and Crowding Out
Treasuries now compete for savings with the AI buildout. Hyperscalers issued roughly $220–225 billion of AI-related debt through August 2026, about 974% more than a year earlier, and Alphabet paid a 10–15 bp concession on its August deal [[big-tech-ai-bond-splurge-crowding-out-weak-borrowers]]. Merrill Lynch counted nearly $280 billion of big-tech debt competing with Treasuries, and ING credited AI with about one-fifth of the rise in long-dated yields [[us-treasury-yields-decades-high-oil-fed-debt-ai-oct2026]]. Rosenberg expects roughly $4 trillion of AI debt over four years and warns the market is not unlimited in capacity, while Pelletier notes that higher borrowing costs make the AI build-out, which Brookings estimates could exceed $10 trillion over 2025–2032, dearer to finish [[oil-treasury-correlation-breaks-ai-risk-outranks-geopolitics-blackrock-rosenberg]] [[hidden-warning-record-highs-breadth-bond-divergence]].

## Treasury Intervention at the Long End
In August the Treasury doubled buybacks of 10- to 30-year bonds from $2 billion to $4 billion per operation, but the 30-year gave back its 20 bp drop within two days, and markets read the move as a sign of fiscal strain [[gold-silver-debasement-trade-aug2026]]. JPMorgan's James Sullivan compared it to paying a mortgage with a credit card, since buying back long bonds while issuing bills reshapes maturities without reducing the debt [[us-stock-selloff-treasury-yields-aug20-2026]]. Raymond James flags the November 4 refunding as a possible trim to long-end issuance, perhaps eliminating the 20-year bond, which could lower yields temporarily without reducing total borrowing [[raymond-james-key-catalysts-fed-earnings-refunding-midterms-oct2026]]. Standard Chartered cast buybacks and a softer dollar as financial repression and forecast a 4.50–4.75% 10-year range [[standard-chartered-ai-capex-forecast-jackson-hole-aug2026]], a band that the October peak of 5.365% left far behind [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]].

## Gold and the Fiscal-Credibility Trade
On September 29 gold fell 3.5% as higher yields made a non-yielding asset less appealing [[higher-bond-yields-pull-stocks-lower-sep2026]]. In August the buyback news pushed gold above $4,600 on a dual-path logic that pays off whether intervention lowers yields or fails and yields rise on sovereign-credit fears [[gold-breaks-4600-treasury-buyback-dual-path]]. The failure path is the debasement thesis, which steers investors toward gold, silver and Bitcoin as deficits and debt service above $1 trillion erode trust in Treasuries [[gold-silver-debasement-trade-aug2026]].

## Bonds as an Income and Hedge Asset
For bond buyers the selloff has rebuilt income, since U.S. Bank calls 4–5% Treasuries and 5–7% corporates the best starting point in over a decade and notes that starting yield is the strongest predictor of decade-long bond returns [[q3-2026-investment-outlook-us-bank]]. A 5.25% 30-year Treasury is pitched as a downturn hedge because long bonds rally when recessions bring rate cuts [[stock-market-crash-long-term-bonds-hedge-tlt]], and Rosenberg adds that in a disinflationary AI wobble only the long end hedges, though he cautions this is not a return to bonds hedging stocks [[oil-treasury-correlation-breaks-ai-risk-outranks-geopolitics-blackrock-rosenberg]]. Morningstar's Christine Benz warns that stagflation could hurt stocks and bonds at once, favoring short-to-intermediate high-quality bonds and TIPS [[stocks-reality-check-expert-warnings-sep2026]].

## Equities: Absorbed Until They Are Not
Yields above 5% raise the hurdle for stocks twice over, offering a competitive low-risk return while squeezing corporate cash flows [[stocks-reality-check-expert-warnings-sep2026]], and on September 29 a 10-year yield above 5.27% helped drag the S&P 500 down 0.8% [[higher-bond-yields-pull-stocks-lower-sep2026]]. Yet October's record yields coincided with record stocks, as investors bought tech into a Nasdaq high on October 5, and Luke Lango argued that money flows to whatever can grow earnings faster than discount rates rise, which is overwhelmingly the AI complex [[investors-shrug-off-bond-yield-surge-stocks-record-highs-oct5-2026]]. Bulls argue growth carries the market, since UBS sees yields biting mainly when growth falters and Fundstrat sees valuations compress only past about 5.5% [[market-every-reason-to-sell-off-resilience-yields-oct2026]]. Skeptics point to Pelletier's observation that past bond-equity divergences in 1998–2000, 2007, 2018 and 2022 ended with stocks falling, though only after weeks to more than a year [[hidden-warning-record-highs-breadth-bond-divergence]].

## Beneath the Index: Rate-Sensitive Sectors
The headline index understates the damage, with the equal-weight S&P 500 headed for a seventh straight weekly loss, over a third of Russell 2000 members struggling to cover interest, and utilities close to a bear market as short-term Treasury payouts eclipsed their dividends [[rising-yields-hidden-stock-market-pain-five-charts-oct2026]]. Pelletier counts roughly 60% of S&P 500 members more than 20% below their peaks, a breadth reading seen only in 1973–74 and 1999–2000 [[hidden-warning-record-highs-breadth-bond-divergence]]. These are the groups flagged in August as most exposed if rates stayed higher for longer, namely banks, real estate and smaller companies [[us-stocks-aug21-2026-higher-for-longer-rates]].

## Households, Credit and Wall Street
Because Treasury yields benchmark lending across the economy, the selloff reaches households, with the 30-year mortgage rate at 7.49% on MBA data by October 7, near a three-year high [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. The transmission is not one-for-one with the Fed, since the 30-year fixed rate follows long Treasury yields and inflation expectations, while credit cards reprice within one to two billing cycles off the prime rate [[fed-holds-3-75-4-percent-consumer-borrowing-costs-cnbc-oct28-2026]]. In corporate credit, PIMCO warns that coupons on CCC bonds maturing in 2027–2028 could roughly double, as B-minus-and-below maturities swell from $56.6 billion in 2026 to $215 billion in 2028 [[big-tech-ai-bond-splurge-crowding-out-weak-borrowers]], and New York's comptroller warns that a 5.35% 10-year could dent the $45.9 billion first-half profit haul on Wall Street [[wall-street-record-profits-h1-2026-rising-yields-risk]].

## Emerging Markets and the Dollar
The same yield gap works through to emerging markets, where higher US yields raise the cost of holding peso and real assets and can narrow peso carry trades enough to force positions to unwind [[us-jobs-report-week-peso-real-fed-repricing-nov2026]]. The Brazilian real, by contrast, is driven by dollar liquidity, Treasury yields, commodities and domestic risk premiums, so strong US data can help exporters even as rate repricing weakens the currency [[us-jobs-report-week-peso-real-fed-repricing-nov2026]]. That note put the dollar about 11% higher against the peso since early September [[us-jobs-report-week-peso-real-fed-repricing-nov2026]].

## Technical Read: Ripples Within a Tide
Technical analysis of the same stretch reads the turbulence as ripples within a tide. Zaccardi's charts show the S&P 500 flat since late May with double-top resistance near 7,800–7,850, and his Dow-style framing holds that the primary cross-asset trends have not changed after the election [[blue-tsunami-midterms-technical-playbook-stockcharts-nov2026]]. On the 10-year, yields were testing their 38.2% Fibonacci retracement at 4.869%, with 4.75% as polarity support, while bond bears still controlled the trend [[blue-tsunami-midterms-technical-playbook-stockcharts-nov2026]].

## Rotations After the Midterms
Diyinvestor's post-election playbook argues the opportunity lies in spotting where market leadership shifts rather than in predicting politics, and notes that divided government leaves Congress unable to end the Iran conflict or reverse tariffs unilaterally, since Trump keeps veto and trade authority [[blue-wave-trade-five-rotations-investment-playbook-diyinvestor-nov2026]]. It treats the cause of a move as decisive, so falling yields with resilient growth help property while a growth scare hurts it, and lower oil helps airlines only when supply rather than weak demand is the source [[blue-wave-trade-five-rotations-investment-playbook-diyinvestor-nov2026]]. Its bottom line is rotation rather than retreat, favoring diversification and selectivity over crowded trades [[blue-wave-trade-five-rotations-investment-playbook-diyinvestor-nov2026]].

**T4. Rates feed back on the forces that set them** — Higher yields restrain growth and can cap their own rise, yet they also raise the government's interest bill, invite new issuance and make AI infrastructure dearer to build, so the loop can run in either direction. · *model:* [[Feedback Loops]]
