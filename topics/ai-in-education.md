# AI in Education

AI in education pairs the promise of patient, personalized tutoring at scale with mounting evidence that the same tools, deployed with answer-giving defaults, without human accountability, or against unredesigned assessments, produce the feeling of learning without its substance, which makes interaction design, the human layer around the tool, and equity the decisive variables rather than access alone.

## The Promise: Personalization at Scale

Large language models are turning historically rule-based Intelligent Tutoring Systems into conversational environments that generate explanations, exercises, and feedback on demand, which is why researchers see them dissolving the old tradeoff between costly one-to-one tutoring and generic mass instruction [[generative-ai-personalized-intelligent-tutoring-systems]]. In practice this mostly arrives as an interactive layer over existing material: a Fresno professor lets students ask for another explanation in the moment [[ai-in-classroom-fresno-teachers]], K-12 districts fighting record-low reading scores use tools like Reading Progress to flag read-aloud errors faster than a teacher with 150 students could [[k12-districts-boost-literacy-with-ai-technology]], and Harvard's CS50 Duck shortened office-hour waits that once stretched to an hour while making it socially cheaper to ask questions [[cs50-duck-ai-tutor-malan-interview]]. Maya Bialik frames the student-facing opportunity as a "learning assistant" that tutors on demand, distinct from a "doing assistant" that should only take over tasks not central to the learning goal, much as calculators freed students for harder work once basics were mastered [[irreplaceable-maya-bialik-ai-teaching-framework]]. Learners are receptive but not overwhelmingly so, since a Kaspersky survey of 7,200 people across 18 countries found Gen Z only slightly more likely than others to name personalised AI tutors the most promising education technology (18% versus 16%) [[gen-z-ai-tutors-virtual-classrooms-kaspersky-survey]].

## The Tutoring Evidence: Design Decides the Outcome

The research question has shifted from whether AI tutoring works to which kind does. The Bastani et al. PNAS experiment with about 1,000 Turkish high schoolers found an unguarded GPT-4 interface boosted assisted practice yet cut unassisted exam scores 17%, while a hint-based tutor largely removed that harm without beating control [[ai-us-schools-learning-evidence-student-privacy]] [[ai-learning-findings-august-2026]], and [[current-evidence-ai-tutoring-impact-learners]] sets that result against Harvard's custom physics tutor, which roughly doubled active-learning gains in a 194-student crossover trial with effect sizes of 0.73 to 1.3 SD. The 6,000-student Tennessee experiment found AI help lifted fractions scores about three points only when paired with a three-in-a-row mastery rule and mistake-specific walkthroughs, with gains confined to easy, practiced questions [[ai-mastery-learning-tennessee-fractions-study]], and a World Bank evaluation in Edo State, Nigeria, found a six-week, teacher-guided Copilot English program produced gains comparable to roughly 1.5 to 2 years of typical progress in similar settings [[world-bank-nigeria-ai-tutoring-six-week-program]]. Reviews converge on the same conditional verdict: a 35-study analysis cited by the University of Dayton found a net positive effect of ChatGPT that depended on instructional method [[university-of-dayton-ai-literacy-requirement-flyergpt]], a University of Minnesota consensus of 30 learning scientists placed AI's promise at the practice stage rather than initial acquisition or application [[ai-us-schools-learning-evidence-student-privacy]], and even purpose-built tutors fall short unevenly, as Khanmigo neither tailored tasks nor supported metacognition [[current-evidence-ai-tutoring-impact-learners]].

## Why Frictionless Help Fails

[[guidelines-for-learning-and-ai]] locates the failure in the See, Do, Feedback loop: AI can legitimately aid perception and review, but when it performs the doing, the productive struggle that builds skill vanishes, and [[five-rules-learning-age-of-ai-scott-young]] adds the autopilot warning that habitual automation erodes the very competence meant to serve as backup. A systematic review of 22 studies names the mechanism as cognitive offloading, finding that direct answers shift monitoring and evaluation onto the AI and induce "metacognitive laziness," and that ChatGPT can raise performance while students' self-evaluation fails to track it [[ai-impact-student-metacognition-systematic-review]]. The damage can appear fast, as Rundell cites findings that ten minutes of AI-assisted problem-solving left people performing worse and giving up sooner once access ended [[ai-harming-young-minds-happiness-guardian]], and it hides behind a sense of momentum, with 63% of 5,050 Preply respondents feeling busy without improving [[preply-progress-report-ai-learning-progress-gap]]. Learners often sense the cost without changing course: 39% of teens feel they miss out on learning when they lean on AI even as roughly seven in ten keep using it for schoolwork [[teens-ai-academic-support-common-sense-survey]], and 67% of students in RAND's panel fear AI harms critical thinking while homework use climbed from 48% to 62% [[ai-us-schools-learning-evidence-student-privacy]].

## Defaults and Engineered Friction

Because the same model can scaffold or supply, its configuration largely determines what students do with it. The metacognition review found every one of five Guided Interaction studies reported positive metacognitive outcomes, yet 15 of 20 classifiable studies used Direct Feedback, mostly through general-purpose tools [[ai-impact-student-metacognition-systematic-review]], and Maryland's Virtual Study Assistant shows the default effect in the wild, since few instructors switched from answer-giving mode to tutor mode and 73.8% of users sought information or solutions while only 0.7% asked for feedback on their own work [[umd-virtual-study-assistant-rct-low-uptake]]. CS50 shows how hard deliberate friction is to hold, layering Socratic prompts, response-rejecting code, and a Zelda-style "heart system" rate limit, yet the Duck still emits code blocks about a quarter of the time [[cs50-duck-ai-tutor-malan-interview]]. Policy is beginning to codify the principle, with the UK Department for Education requiring AI tutors to withhold direct answers by default under human oversight [[current-evidence-ai-tutoring-impact-learners]], StarSpark aiming for the smallest nudge that keeps a student at the edge of their ability [[ai-learning-findings-august-2026]], and Dayton drawing its line between "legitimate" use that amplifies a student's own work and "corrosive" use that replaces it [[university-of-dayton-ai-literacy-requirement-flyergpt]].

## Why Effects Resist Attribution

Strong results tend to come from bundles, which makes the AI's own contribution hard to isolate. The World Bank researchers credit the Nigerian gains to the whole program of curriculum alignment, structured prompts, teacher support, peer learning, and AI, explicitly declining to attribute them to the chatbot [[world-bank-nigeria-ai-tutoring-six-week-program]], while Maryland's 2,379-student trial ran into the inverse problem, as only about 15% of treated students used the tool, others had ChatGPT and Gemini anyway, and instructor-readable chat logs may have deterred honest use, leaving access slightly lowering grades and UMD pausing rollout [[umd-virtual-study-assistant-rct-low-uptake]]. Justin Reich's observation there that field trials are "a bundle of interventions" echoes Brookings' argument that RCTs assume a stable intervention that constantly updated models do not provide [[ai-learning-findings-august-2026]], and the Tennessee study's finding that AI helped only in combination with mastery repetition is another instance of the effect living in the pairing rather than the tool [[ai-mastery-learning-tennessee-fractions-study]].

## The Human Layer: Accountability Over Content

[[ai-tutoring-equity-gap-human-accountability]] argues that content was rarely what held struggling students back; stability, motivation, and an adult who notices when they disappear were, which is why affluent families keep buying human tutors in a private market projected to grow from about $92B to $155B by 2030 and why a well-designed product routes disengagement to a teacher rather than an automated nudge. University of Phoenix researchers reach a parallel conclusion for working-adult online learners, proposing that persistence is an institutional outcome in which instructor acknowledgement, belonging, and psychological safety sustain engagement while GenAI merely eases task initiation by taking "the panic of a blank page" away [[trauma-informed-ai-persistence-model-online-learners]]. The Nigerian program cast AI as an "assistant teacher" under human supervision, with teachers helping students spot wrong answers [[world-bank-nigeria-ai-tutoring-six-week-program]], adult learners rank human feedback (46%) far above AI reminders (18%) as a driver of consistency [[preply-progress-report-ai-learning-progress-gap]], and [[ai-era-higher-education-human-connection-ubuntu]] turns the point into a design test drawn from Ubuntu, judging AI good when it deepens participation, as when nursing students critique an AI diagnosis in teams, and harmful when it isolates.

## Educators as Designers, Not Just Users

A growing strand insists that teachers should shape AI rather than absorb it. Penn GSE's new Living Laboratory for Human Agency and AI is built as a network to co-design and test tools in real classrooms, on the premise that educators and students should be the designers of AI in education and build evidence of what their designs make possible [[penn-gse-living-lab-human-agency-ai]], while Bialik wants AI that elevates teacher ingenuity in planning, feedback, and coaching rather than adding "slop" [[irreplaceable-maya-bialik-ai-teaching-framework]]. Google frames its free educator series the same way, quoting a trust leader who sees AI assisting with resources and planning while teachers continue to interpret, inspire, and guide [[google-ai-educator-series-uk-ireland-650000-educators]]. Teacher-facing use is where adoption actually concentrates, with 63% of AP teachers using AI to prepare materials but only 15% for grading [[ai-spurs-college-board-redesign-ap-courses]] and weekly users in a Gallup survey estimating 5.9 hours saved per week, a self-reported outcome distinct from student learning [[ai-us-schools-learning-evidence-student-privacy]].

## Widespread Use, Lagging Preparation

Use has outrun guidance at every level. About 70% of teens use AI for academic help, with 25% taking answers unchanged, yet 70% say no teacher has discussed safe use [[teens-ai-academic-support-common-sense-survey]], while only 18% of U.S. K-12 teachers have formal AI training [[ai-in-classroom-fresno-teachers]], just 21% of faculty feel confident guiding classroom AI [[aix-weekly-ai-literacy-gate-higher-education]], and about a quarter of AP teachers work without any school AI policy [[ai-spurs-college-board-redesign-ap-courses]]. Maryland's trial showed what thin preparation costs, as faculty received only "light training" and the lead researcher concluded instructors need "much more extensive" support [[umd-virtual-study-assistant-rct-low-uptake]], while Penn's Living Lab starts from the same diagnosis that educators experiment daily without clear evidence of what works [[penn-gse-living-lab-human-agency-ai]]. Vendors are moving into the gap at scale, with Google opening 15-to-45-minute Gemini modules to some 650,000 UK and Ireland educators after an AI Works pilot of just 150 staff across 14 schools [[google-ai-educator-series-uk-ireland-650000-educators]], and Kyndryl and Skillsoft offering free coursework to up to 25,000 U.S. high schoolers and educators [[kyndryl-skillsoft-ai-education-high-school]].

## The Contested Meaning of "AI Literacy"

AI literacy is the standard prescription for that gap, but the term carries incompatible meanings. Kaspersky treats it as verification hygiene [[gen-z-ai-tutors-virtual-classrooms-kaspersky-survey]], Malan wants default skepticism because models simulate understanding rather than possess it [[cs50-duck-ai-tutor-malan-interview]], and Penn's algorithm-auditing project teaches high schoolers to investigate AI bias [[penn-gse-living-lab-human-agency-ai]], whereas Kyndryl and Skillsoft pitch prompt engineering and credentials as workforce readiness for every student [[kyndryl-skillsoft-ai-education-high-school]] and Dayton cites employers who say "awareness of AI is no longer enough" while also requiring every first-year to study AI alongside human dignity [[university-of-dayton-ai-literacy-requirement-flyergpt]]. [[ai-literacy-fallacies-kamperman-graff-higher-education]] targets the workforce framing directly, arguing campus programs presume use, treat ethics as an afterthought, and rest on myths that effective AI use is a simple skill that secures economic futures. In the workplace the term often measures nothing at all, since Pluralsight's finding that only 52% of organizations call most staff AI literate reflects an undefined self-perception [[pluralsight-2026-tech-skills-report-ai-literacy-gap-critique]], which is the verification gap Handshake targets with badges tied to published projects rather than self-reported skills that now appear nine times more often on graduate profiles [[handshake-ai-skills-studio-project-based-ai-badges]].

## Assessment and Integrity Under Pressure

AI agents have escalated cheating from pasted chatbot essays to impersonating a student across an entire course, a threat sharpest in asynchronous online programs that now reach over half of U.S. college students and lack oral or handwritten countermeasures [[ai-agents-cheating-online-courses]], and an estimated 9% of students in a 95,000-student study use AI to cheat [[aix-weekly-ai-literacy-gate-higher-education]]. UK universities are now abandoning the early "prohibition and detection" model for pedagogy-led integrity, combining AI literacy, closed assessments, open assessments that design AI in, validation through declarations and vivas, and a culture of openness, though students report that vague guidance turns declarations into a source of anxiety [[keeping-the-learning-real-academic-integrity-ai-coherence]]. Detection's failure is also driving College Board's 2027-28 split, in which AP Computer Science Principles adds an AI Create Project while AP Seminar blocks generative AI during scored writing because detectors produced false positives [[ai-spurs-college-board-redesign-ap-courses]], and Dayton's course-by-course honor code leaves faculty "stuck between a rock and a hard place" with no reliable way to discourage misuse [[university-of-dayton-ai-literacy-requirement-flyergpt]]. Kamperman and Graff draw the deeper consequence that once AI can plausibly produce the essay, educators must finally articulate why students write at all [[ai-literacy-fallacies-kamperman-graff-higher-education]].

## Governance: Infrastructure, Privacy, and Procurement

Shared infrastructure and contracts are becoming the governance layer beneath classroom tools. The CZI-backed Learning Commons gives more than 70 integrators a Knowledge Graph of all 50 states' standards and Evaluators that score AI content, though critics warn its curators effectively define valid pedagogy for every tool built on top [[czi-learning-commons-open-ai-platform-k12]], and districts report rollout works best when it starts from the learning standard rather than the technology [[k12-districts-boost-literacy-with-ai-technology]]. On privacy, NYC paused student-facing generative AI for about 600,000 students from 2-K through 8th grade and banned companion chatbots in all grades, while the AFT/UFT/Microsoft standard makes protections enforceable only through each district's contract and excludes general productivity, search, and cloud tools, leading the article to argue every purchase needs both learning evidence for the use and enforceable terms for the product [[ai-us-schools-learning-evidence-student-privacy]]. Build-versus-buy is unsettled too, as Maryland's experience prompted Stephen Aguilar to argue universities "can't compete against the market" on bespoke ed-tech [[umd-virtual-study-assistant-rct-low-uptake]] even as Dayton runs its own capped multi-model FlyerGPT platform [[university-of-dayton-ai-literacy-requirement-flyergpt]].

## Equity Fault Lines

The equity case for tutoring at scale [[generative-ai-personalized-intelligent-tutoring-systems]] is contested from several directions. Rundell forecasts wealthier schools retreating from classroom AI while poorer schools lean in to cut costs [[ai-harming-young-minds-happiness-guardian]], early data fits with 20% of teens in households under $30K doing most homework with AI versus 7% above $75K [[should-schools-use-ai-tools-student-learning]], and Kacharia notes the income achievement gap widened about 40% over the decades personalized content scaled, recasting the digital divide as "who gets a human" [[ai-tutoring-equity-gap-human-accountability]]. The counterevidence comes from deliberately targeted programs, including the UK's planned trial with 450,000 disadvantaged students and Third Space Learning's Skye, which helped struggling and anxious students most [[current-evidence-ai-tutoring-impact-learners]], and the Nigerian results, which the researchers say could narrow the private-tutoring gap only if access is deliberately extended and schools have electricity, internet, computers, and trained teachers [[world-bank-nigeria-ai-tutoring-six-week-program]]. Dayton justifies its universal requirement on the same logic, that leaving AI literacy to chance would widen a gap [[university-of-dayton-ai-literacy-requirement-flyergpt]], and [[ai-era-higher-education-human-connection-ubuntu]] adds that models trained mostly on non-African material can misread local practice, leaving local educators as indispensable interpreters.

## Measuring Learning, Not Adoption

The sources converge on a measurement problem: dashboards count seats and prompts but cannot show whether students reason or perform without the tool [[aix-weekly-ai-literacy-gate-higher-education]], and the core distinction is that a chatbot-produced answer is not evidence the student acquired the skill [[ai-us-schools-learning-evidence-student-privacy]]. Workplace learning shows the same failure, as Preply urges employers to stop treating time spent, completions, or streaks as progress [[preply-progress-report-ai-learning-progress-gap]] and Whitfield notes that 77% of tech executives have seen staff finish training yet struggle to apply it, proposing role-specific observable tasks across Kirkpatrick levels and staggered rollouts that leave an untrained team as a control [[pluralsight-2026-tech-skills-report-ai-literacy-gap-critique]]. Headline figures warrant the same scrutiny, since Google's claim that 74% of UK education roles could be enhanced by AI measures potential rather than realized gains [[google-ai-educator-series-uk-ireland-650000-educators]], and Nafukho proposes tracking access, learning, workload, well-being, integrity cases, and language performance, with whether students grow more connected as the real test [[ai-era-higher-education-human-connection-ubuntu]].
