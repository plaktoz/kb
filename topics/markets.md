# Markets Hub

This hub is the starting point for the vault's market, rates and holdings topics. It gives a short orientation per cluster and the principles that recur across them, and the detail lives in each linked topic file. It holds no sources of its own: every claim here comes from the child topics.

## The market and its rate backdrop

[[sp500]] tracks how the index is built and moved: who drives returns, how many stocks take part, and what mechanical flows shape the tape. ## Holdings and single-company topics

[[microsoft]], [[alphabet]] and [[ibm]] are the vault's company-level tests of how markets price AI spending and legacy transitions. [[microsoft]] and [[alphabet]] ask whether heavy capex and cloud backlog, much of it tied to a few AI customers, turns into revenue. [[ibm]] asks whether a shrinking legacy franchise is offset by hybrid cloud, governed AI and quantum. [[berkshire-hathaway]] is the vault's reference case for patient capital allocation, tracking how the post-Buffett era deploys a record cash pile.

## Principles that recur across the cluster

**Prices move on the gap between results and what is already priced in.** [[treasury-yields]] and [[federal-reserve-monetary-policy]] put it as surprises against expectations (T1 in each), [[sp500]] as outcomes against what prices assume (T2), and [[microsoft]] and [[alphabet]] as a buyer's return depending on what the price already assumes (T3 in each). A strong result can still fall if the price assumed more.

**A higher risk-free rate raises the hurdle for every other asset.** [[treasury-yields]], [[federal-reserve-monetary-policy]] and [[sp500]] each state it, and it hits distant cash flows and debt-funded borrowers first.

**Concentration passes one holder's shock to everyone exposed.** In [[sp500]] the index reports its largest members, and [[microsoft]] and [[alphabet]] inherit the risk of the counterparties behind their backlog.

**Capex reaches cash flow before it reaches earnings.** In [[microsoft]], spending that pays back later depresses free cash flow first. In [[alphabet]], capex that outruns operating cash flow must be funded from outside, and the terms of that funding feed into returns.

For the AI financing and valuation side of these same companies, see [[ai]], especially [[ai-bubble]] and [[ai-chips]].
