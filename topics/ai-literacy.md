# AI Literacy

AI literacy is how teachers, students, and workers learn to use AI well and how institutions train, require, measure, and credential that ability, and the vault's evidence shows the hard problem is closing the gap between fast-spreading use and demonstrated competence (tutoring outcomes live in [[ai-in-education]], organizational value in [[ai-workplace-adoption]]).

## What Counts as AI Literacy

Definitions in the vault centre on judgment rather than tool operation: UNESCO frames AI literacy as the ability to access, evaluate, and apply information in AI-integrated settings [[unesco-wysd-2026-ai-literacy-youth-skills]], and Kaspersky adds knowing what systems can and cannot do, checking output against reliable sources, and protecting personal data [[gen-z-ai-tutors-virtual-classrooms-kaspersky-survey]]. Classroom guidance turns this into teachable habits such as writing thoughtful prompts, spotting fabrication and bias, verifying sources, and judging when independent thinking beats AI help [[five-ways-ai-strengthens-teaching-2026-27]]. Anthropic's AI Fluency course aims for durability, defining fluency as effective, efficient, ethical, and safe collaboration organized around four competencies (Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence). It chose a framework because tactical prompting tips go out of date while a framework survives model generations [[anthropic-ai-fluency-framework-introduction]], [[ai-fluency-4d-framework]].

## The Critique: Tool Skill Is Not Literacy

Kamperman and Graff argue that university "AI literacy" offerings are largely marketing that assumes use, treats ethics perfunctorily, and rests on two myths: that effective generative-AI use is a simple foundational skill, and that it guarantees economic security. Their alternative, "AI and Literacy," puts reading and writing at the centre [[ai-literacy-fallacies-kamperman-graff-higher-education]]. Otherwise pro-AI sources reach a similar conclusion: UNESCO insists technical AI skills must be paired with creativity, critical thinking, and empathy [[unesco-wysd-2026-ai-literacy-youth-skills]], and MSU Denver faculty say the key skill is defining a problem and judging which tasks should stay human before directing AI at all [[ai-changing-rules-career-readiness]]. Even Anthropic's framework concedes the point, since its Discernment competency depends on domain expertise the user must already have [[ai-fluency-4d-framework]].

## Adoption Has Outrun Preparation

At every level of education, AI use arrived before training in how to use it: IBM found 76% of middle-school educators use AI weekly but only 20% of K-12 educators have had extensive training, with 42% naming lack of training as their top barrier [[ibm-k12-ai-readiness-gap-study-2026]]. In College Board research, 46% of AP teachers said they understood generative AI but still needed guidance, and about a quarter worked without a school AI policy [[ai-spurs-college-board-redesign-ap-courses]]. Higher education repeats the pattern, with only 21% of faculty confident guiding classroom AI use [[aix-weekly-ai-literacy-gate-higher-education]] and only 29% of students believing instructors can guide them, even though 64% of faculty say they have had AI-literacy training [[ai-higher-education-survey-2026-student-faculty-adoption-gap]]. The gap reaches students directly, as 70% of US teens say no teacher has ever discussed how to use AI safely [[teens-ai-academic-support-common-sense-survey]].

## Training Educators at Scale

The main response so far is short, free, vendor-backed training: Google is offering its AI Educator Series to about 650,000 UK and Ireland educators through 15–45 minute Gemini modules that award micro-credentials, though its headline claim that 74% of education roles could be enhanced by AI measures potential, not realized gains [[google-ai-educator-series-uk-ireland-650000-educators]]. UNESCO's Institute for Lifelong Learning has trained about 10,000 literacy educators through an 11-session course that stresses critical evaluation and "more conscious" AI use over tool adoption [[unesco-digital-skills-literacy-educators]], while IBM's K-12 fellowship and the College Board's expanded AP Summer Institute are smaller, targeted versions [[ibm-k12-ai-readiness-gap-study-2026]], [[ai-spurs-college-board-redesign-ap-courses]]. Penn GSE's Living Lab proposes a different role, in which educators and students design AI in learning and build evidence of what works instead of adopting tools after the fact [[penn-gse-living-lab-human-agency-ai]].

## Universities Make Literacy a Requirement

Higher education is moving from optional workshops to required literacy: UCCS will not provision a ChatGPT Edu account until a student completes an AI Literacy Foundations course [[aix-weekly-ai-literacy-gate-higher-education]], and the University of Dayton requires every first-year student to complete AI-fundamentals and human-dignity modules because leaving literacy to chance would widen existing gaps [[university-of-dayton-ai-literacy-requirement-flyergpt]]. Other institutions reject the standalone course, with Northeastern mapping literacy bottom-up through faculty in each college because "AI readiness is going to be inherently discipline-specific" [[universities-ai-integration-models]] and Washington and Syracuse embedding AI fluency in domain programmes such as bioinformatics and agriculture [[ai-learning-findings-august-2026]]. MSU Denver's no-code AI Fundamentals elective and the College Board's AI Create Project in AP Computer Science Principles carry the same content to non-specialists and secondary students [[ai-changing-rules-career-readiness]], [[ai-spurs-college-board-redesign-ap-courses]].

## Literacy as the Foundation of Academic Integrity

Integrity policy increasingly relies on literacy rather than detection: the Wonkhe and Studiosity review of UK universities finds institutions abandoning "prohibition and detection" and treating AI literacy, with both a baseline and discipline-specific competence, as one of five layered practices, while warning that vague guidance turns AI-use declarations into a source of anxiety [[keeping-the-learning-real-academic-integrity-ai-coherence]]. Dayton leaves permission to each instructor and separates "legitimate" use that amplifies a student's own work from "corrosive" use that replaces it [[university-of-dayton-ai-literacy-requirement-flyergpt]]. Where detection lost teachers' trust, the College Board chose secure AI-free writing for AP Seminar while still teaching AI content in Computer Science Principles [[ai-spurs-college-board-redesign-ap-courses]]. In each case literacy means judging when to use AI, not only how.

## Youth and Workforce Pipelines

Outside universities, AI literacy is sold as baseline workforce readiness. Kyndryl and Skillsoft offer free AI curriculum and credentials to up to 25,000 US high-school students and educators through May 2027, framing literacy as necessary "for every student" and not only future technologists [[kyndryl-skillsoft-ai-education-high-school]]. UNESCO's Skills for the Future Platform places AI skills inside a larger crisis in which 450 million young people lack adequate skills, 40% of current skillsets no longer match job needs, and only 22% of AI professionals are women [[unesco-skills-future-platform-2026-youth-ai-skills]]. Life Hub Education lists AI literacy alongside the 4Cs and social-emotional learning and argues that technology fluency needs ongoing adaptation rather than a one-time credential [[21st-century-learning-skills-framework-2026]].

## The Workplace Skills Gap

Employers raise the bar faster than they train for it: ZipRecruiter finds 74% treat AI skills as a strong advantage or requirement and 57% have raised productivity expectations, yet only 22% mandate AI training for all employees and 17% offer none [[more-jobs-higher-bar-2026-ai-employer-report]]. Pluralsight reports that 96% of organizations have invested in AI tools but only 52% say most staff are AI literate, and 28% spent more on tools than on literacy. Whitfield's critique of that report notes that "AI literate" is an undefined self-perception reported by a vendor that sells training [[pluralsight-2026-tech-skills-report-ai-literacy-gap-critique]]. Learners describe the same disconnect from their side, with 88% of professionals using AI to learn yet 63% feeling busy without improving, which Preply calls the progress gap [[preply-progress-report-ai-learning-progress-gap]].

## Measuring Competence, Not Completion

The vault's most consistent practical lesson is that completions, attendance, and self-reports do not show capability: 77% of tech executives have seen staff finish training yet struggle to apply it, so Whitfield proposes three observable tasks per role, scenario-based assessment, Kirkpatrick Level 2–3 evidence, and staggered rollouts that create an untrained control team [[pluralsight-2026-tech-skills-report-ai-literacy-gap-critique]]. The AIX newsletter asks the same of universities, urging them to evaluate literacy training's effect on judgment rather than count completions [[aix-weekly-ai-literacy-gate-higher-education]], and skills-based learning reframes corporate L&D around whether people "can perform the task successfully" [[skills-based-learning-rise-2026]]. Every's review of Anthropic's certification shows the limit of course-based credentials, which built shared vocabulary but lacked role-based tracks and were judged weaker than the public documentation [[anthropic-certification-15-hours-review]].

## Credentials and Hiring Signals

As self-reported AI skills multiply, credentials are shifting toward proof of finished work: Handshake reports AI skills appear nine times more often on Class of 2026 than 2022 profiles while applications per job are up 26%, so its AI Skills Studio awards badges only for published projects or paid AI work, and employers see them during sourcing [[handshake-ai-skills-studio-project-based-ai-badges]]. Jason Eckert, quoted in the Dayton report, puts the employer view bluntly, saying "awareness of AI is no longer enough" and asking for concrete examples of AI augmenting real work [[university-of-dayton-ai-literacy-requirement-flyergpt]]. Micro-credentials are gaining acceptance as signals alongside degrees [[21st-century-learning-skills-framework-2026]], but the ZipRecruiter note flags a Goodhart risk: once "AI fluency" becomes a job requirement, candidates may optimize for signaling it rather than building it [[more-jobs-higher-bar-2026-ai-employer-report]].
