# GLP-1 Drugs

This topic follows what the vault knows about GLP-1 receptor agonists, the drug class that includes semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy), tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound) and the investigational triple agonist retatrutide. The notes cover weight loss, heart and sleep outcomes, mechanisms, safety and access, and a few signals outside metabolism. Most of the evidence comes from observational records, early-phase trials or single studies, so the claims here are provisional.

## Weight Loss and the Dose Question

Semaglutide's effect on appetite is measurable over a full year. In a 60-week trial of 120 people, semaglutide users ate 24 to 30 percent fewer calories than placebo at every measurement point and lost 15.1 percent of their body weight against 3.4 percent on placebo [[glp1-semaglutide-sustained-calorie-reduction-one-year]]. Benefit rises with dose. Retatrutide, tested in 1,152 adults with obesity and type 2 diabetes in the Phase III TRIUMPH-2 trial, produced 11.9, 16.8 and 18.8 percent average weight loss at successively higher doses over 80 weeks, against 5.1 percent on placebo [[retatrutide-triumph-2-obesity-type-2-diabetes-phase-3]]. Patients who stayed on the lowest starting doses lost about 5.5 percent of body weight on tirzepatide and 2.2 percent on semaglutide after one year, compared with roughly 12 percent on higher-dose semaglutide, though that study followed routine prescribing rather than a planned microdosing protocol, so some patients may have simply tolerated low doses before escalating [[microdosing-glp1-wegovy-zepbound-weight-loss]].

## Beyond the Scale: Heart, Sleep and Health Outcomes

Weight is only part of what these drugs do, and several notes argue that it should not be the only yardstick. The Endocrine Society's scientific statement proposes judging obesity treatment by cardiovascular, metabolic, liver, sleep, mental and physical health rather than pounds lost [[obesity-treatment-health-outcomes-not-weight-loss-alone]]. In obstructive sleep apnea, tirzepatide reduced apnea events by 20 to 24 per hour in Phase 3 trials, with full remission in 42 to 50 percent of patients, and benefits appeared within four weeks, before peak weight loss. The same review still ranks CPAP as the superior treatment, because structural and neuromuscular causes of airway collapse are separate from fat and weight loss cannot fix them [[glp-1-tirzepatide-sleep-apnea-cpap]]. In type 2 diabetes, a BMJ study of nearly 53,000 people linked tirzepatide to a 33 percent lower heart-attack risk than sitagliptin, and the FDA approved Mounjaro on August 28, 2026 to reduce major adverse cardiovascular events in high-risk patients, a label backed by the randomized SURPASS-CVOT trial [[mounjaro-heart-health-type-2-diabetes]]. That trial enrolled a very high-risk population, so its results should not be carried over to lower-risk patients.

## How the Drugs Act on the Brain

A mouse study revised a long-held account of how semaglutide reduces appetite. The assumption was that the drug quiets AgRP "hunger" neurons, but Yale researchers found that semaglutide activates them, and mice that lacked AgRP neurons could not sustain weight loss on the drug, which suggests these neurons are needed for its long-term effect [[glp1-hunger-neurons-yale-mechanism]]. Human translation is untested. The one-year trial hints that the mechanism shifts over time, since hunger and food-preoccupation improvements stopped differing significantly from placebo by weeks 40 to 60 while calorie reduction persisted [[glp1-semaglutide-sustained-calorie-reduction-one-year]]. Beyond appetite, Yale researchers pooling 51 clinical studies found that semaglutide, metformin and anti-TNF therapies reduced biological age, while over-the-counter supplements did not, although lower epigenetic age has not been shown to mean longer life [[yale-epigenetic-clocks-anti-aging-interventions-nature-medicine]].

## Signals Beyond Metabolism

Some findings reach into other conditions, and each is weakly supported. In an eight-week phase 2 trial of 50 adults with moderate-to-severe alcohol use disorder, oral semaglutide significantly reduced heavy drinking days, but it missed its primary lab-based craving endpoint and the drinks-per-day secondary endpoint, and placebo participants guessed their assignment more often than semaglutide users, which complicates the blinded comparison [[semaglutide-alcohol-use-disorder-trial]]. A Texas analysis of 359,595 adults with type 2 diabetes found that GLP-1 starters had lower obesity-related cancer rates than insulin or sulfonylurea starters, but no advantage over metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors or DPP-4 inhibitors, and the gap against insulin probably reflects more advanced disease among insulin users rather than a drug effect [[glp1-cancer-risk-359k-observational-study]].

## Surgery, Access and Safety

Observational data on surgery offer reassurance without establishing cause. Across more than two million elective surgeries, patients on GLP-1 receptor agonists had 11 percent lower odds of major cardiovascular events and 8 percent lower odds of postoperative infection, but the design cannot show the drugs produced these differences, aspiration risk was not assessed, and the authors called the results hypothesis-generating [[glp1-surgery-outcomes-cardiovascular-infection-observational-study]]. Access raises the more pressing safety questions. In Nebraska's prescription-tracking data, compounded GLP-1s rose from zero in 2018 to 86 percent of compounded weight-loss prescriptions in 2025, and many contain additives such as vitamin B12 analogs and levocarnitine that have not been tested for safety alongside GLP-1s [[compounded-glp1-b12-additives-untested-jama-nebraska]]. Cash-pay purchases do not appear in claims data, so the scale of this market is hidden from the same systems that would reveal it. Use is spreading to younger patients as well. In a Pediatrics study of more than 3.5 million children, the share of children aged 8 to 11 without diabetes using a GLP-1 rose from 0.3 percent in 2019 to 9.3 percent by summer 2026, about 20,000 children in all [[glp1-use-children-8-11-pediatrics-study]]. The clinicians quoted there stress that medication should follow a thorough evaluation and intensive lifestyle treatment, that FDA approval for weight management starts at age 12, and that anxiety and depression should be screened before and after treatment.

## Durability and Discontinuation

Benefits depend on continued use. About half of patients stop GLP-1 medications within 12 months, and weight returns quickly after stopping, which has led researchers to explore lower maintenance doses and transitional medications [[obesity-treatment-health-outcomes-not-weight-loss-alone]]. Dose escalation is a related pressure, since many patients on low starting doses plateau and eventually need higher doses to reach their goals [[microdosing-glp1-wegovy-zepbound-weight-loss]]. Tolerability also drives dropouts at the top of the range, with 8 to 12 percent of retatrutide users quitting at higher doses against 5 percent on placebo [[retatrutide-triumph-2-obesity-type-2-diabetes-phase-3]]. The one-year semaglutide trial did not address weight maintenance after stopping, so that question remains open [[glp1-semaglutide-sustained-calorie-reduction-one-year]].

## Where the Evidence Stands

The strongest claims here come from randomized trials of hundreds to a few thousand people, but many others rest on observational records, a single 50-person trial, or mice. The surgery, cancer and pediatric findings are observational, the hunger mechanism is shown only in mice, and retatrutide has not been approved. Cost is a practical limit, since the sleep apnea review puts tirzepatide at about $1,000 a month, with insurance coverage varying by patient [[glp-1-tirzepatide-sleep-apnea-cpap]].
