# AI Chips

This topic tracks the AI semiconductor stack (GPUs for training, CPUs for agentic inference, memory for both, EUV lithography underneath, and the server OEMs that assemble it) and how earnings, capital allocation, and supply constraints across those layers shape one of the vault's most durable investment themes.

## Nvidia's Growth Trajectory and Valuation Ceiling

Nvidia's Q2 FY2027 revenue of $96.2 billion (+106% YoY) and Q3 guidance of $108 billion underpin CFO Colette Kress's ~70% FY2028 growth outlook, which she framed as limited by supply rather than demand [[nvidia-7-trillion-market-cap-broadcom-comparison-valuation]]. With the earnings path largely set, the swing variable is the multiple: at ~$5.45 trillion, reaching $7 trillion means adding roughly a whole Broadcom and re-rating from ~14x to ~18x FY2028 earnings, while a separate analysis days later, with the market cap near $5.6 trillion, judged ~24x forward earnings reasonable against the same 70% growth projection [[nvidia-record-buyback-authorization-235-billion-sep2026]]. Both readings place the real uncertainty in how long AI capital spending sustains the trajectory and what multiple the market is willing to pay for it.

## Nvidia's Capital Allocation: Cash, Buybacks, and Circularity

On September 28, Nvidia's board added $150 billion to its repurchase program, lifting remaining authorization to $235 billion (the largest increase on record and ~4.3% of market cap) to be deployed by FY2028, a pace of roughly $39 billion per quarter against a recent ~$20 billion quarter [[nvidia-record-buyback-authorization-235-billion-sep2026]]. The firepower comes from a hardware-and-full-stack monetization model in which GPUs and networking gear drive most revenue, supplemented by royalties and revenue-sharing with neoclouds and startups, leaving more than $50.5 billion in cash at Q2 to fund buybacks and a token dividend [[ai-monetization-nvidia-microsoft-alphabet-palantir]]. Jensen Huang pitched the buyback as proof that Nvidia can fund innovation and reward shareholders at once, a costly and hard-to-reverse signal of management confidence. The circular investments that one analysis counts as part of Nvidia's monetization engine are exactly what skeptics cite as the bear case, so the cash pile reads either as strength or as a feedback loop depending on whether end demand holds.

## Memory: The Bottleneck That Squeezes Margins

DRAM prices projected to quadruple by 2026, with supply tight through 2029–2030, make memory the next structural constraint on AI hardware [[micron-vs-sandisk-ai-memory-stock-comparison]], and the effect already reaches the top of the stack: Kress guided Nvidia's gross margin down from ~75% toward 71%–72% by fiscal Q4 2027, largely on rising memory costs [[nvidia-record-buyback-authorization-235-billion-sep2026]]. Suppliers are answering with capacity-ahead-of-demand bets. Samsung plans to more than double HBM4/HBM4E output in 2027, growing total HBM capacity ~40% to ~250,000 wafers/month and lifting the HBM4 family from ~40% to ~80% of shipments, after sending 12-layer HBM4E samples to Nvidia in May 2026 [[samsung-hbm4-output-doubling-2027]]. Among U.S. names, analysts prefer Micron's combined DRAM and NAND exposure (~53% projected upside) over Sandisk's NAND-only position (~24%) at similar valuations, which casts memory pricing power and Nvidia's margin compression as two sides of the same shortage.

## CPUs and the Agentic Inference Shift

Training is GPU-dominated, but agentic AI's planning and orchestration at inference time lean on CPUs, and Meta's Muse AI Agent topping U.S. iPhone downloads for three straight days sent Arm (+17%), Intel (+12%), and AMD (+10%) sharply higher on September 21, 2026 [[intel-arm-amd-chip-stocks-agentic-ai-cpu-rally-sep2026]]. The company forecasts behind the rally differ in size but agree on direction (Arm sees the server CPU market compounding 35% a year to $120 billion by 2030, AMD 50% a year to $220 billion), and together they correct the "AI = Nvidia/GPU" narrative of 2023–2025. Dell's results show the same agentic wave turning into hardware orders, as enterprises deploy autonomous agents on local servers instead of in the cloud [[dell-stock-surges-record-ai-server-orders-q2-2027]].

## AMD Moves Up the Stack Into World Models

AMD's $8.2 billion acquisition of Fei-Fei Li's World Labs, with Li becoming EVP and chief scientist, is explicitly a chip-design play: AMD wants the startup's physical-reality workloads to inform future silicon, extending an earlier partnership on inference and training optimization [[amd-acquires-world-labs-fei-fei-li]]. Coverage frames the deal as a response to Nvidia, which already offers open-weight world models such as Cosmos and whose full-stack lock-in is central to its monetization moat [[ai-monetization-nvidia-microsoft-alphabet-palantir]], so chip competition is increasingly fought over software ecosystems and workloads as well as silicon. Physical AI is becoming a shared frontier for chip designers. Arm is pushing into it through its AGI CPU and Neoverse chips [[arm-holdings-vs-asml-ai-stock-comparison]], and world models matter there because synthetic training data offsets the shortage of real-world data for humanoid robots and self-driving cars.

## AI Servers: Downstream Proof of Demand

Dell's fiscal Q2 2027 delivered $47 billion in revenue (+58% YoY), a record $60.9 billion in AI server orders, and a $95 billion backlog, with FY2027 AI server revenue guided to triple to $74 billion and its AI server market share rising from 5% to 17% in a year [[dell-stock-surges-record-ai-server-orders-q2-2027]]. Goldman Sachs raised its 2030 AI server market forecast to $1.24 trillion from $961 billion on higher volumes and ~15% higher average selling prices, so server OEMs now sit beside chip suppliers as picks-and-shovels beneficiaries of the buildout. Michael Dell's warning that a structural chip shortage could worsen in 2027 matches Nvidia's supply-constrained guidance [[nvidia-7-trillion-market-cap-broadcom-comparison-valuation]] and the multi-year DRAM tightness, which suggests supply rather than demand is the binding constraint at several layers of the stack.

## The Indispensable Layer: ASML, Arm, and the Growth-Value Split

ASML's monopoly on EUV lithography makes it the toolmaker that profits no matter which chip designer or foundry wins. It generated ~€32.7 billion in FY2025 revenue at a 29.4% net margin and €11.1 billion in free cash flow while trading at 27.9x forward earnings, with China export restrictions as its main risk [[arm-holdings-vs-asml-ai-stock-comparison]]. Arm, whose architecture is in ~350 billion chips, trades at 125x forward earnings on data center and physical-AI ambitions, yet ~69% of its operating cash flow goes to stock-based compensation and open-source RISC-V threatens its licensing model. The pair maps the growth-versus-value split across the stack, and Arm's 17% single-day jump in the CPU rally [[intel-arm-amd-chip-stocks-agentic-ai-cpu-rally-sep2026]] shows how sensitive high-multiple names are to narrative shifts.

## ETF Exposure and Portfolio Sizing

SMH beat the S&P 500 in 8 of 10 years from 2016–2025, turning $1,000 into ~$15,000 versus ~$4,000, but fell about twice as far as the index in its two losing years (~33% in 2022), and each worst year was immediately followed by its best [[vaneck-semiconductor-etf-smh-sp500-outperformance-history]]. Concentration adds to that amplification: Nvidia is ~23% of SMH's ~$72 billion in assets and the top five holdings about half, so the fund is heavily tied to the same company whose buyback and margin story dominates this topic. Investors who want AI chip exposure with less single-name risk can hold the S&P 500, where Nvidia is ~7.9% of the index [[nvidia-record-buyback-authorization-235-billion-sep2026]], and any SMH position should be sized to survive a ~33% drawdown so cyclical volatility does not become permanent loss.
