# AI Chips

This topic follows the AI semiconductor stack, from GPUs and the CPUs, memory and lithography around them to hyperscaler silicon, server makers, startups, inference software and export-controlled China demand, and asks how capital allocation, financing, supply limits, leadership and shifting market expectations shape it. The open questions heading into mid-November 2026 are whether Nvidia, after losing its founder-CEO and about 29% of its value in a month, can steady the market at its November 17 report, whether its balance sheet can carry its growing commitments, whether the CUDA moat survives challengers including Google's widening TPU line, and whether a market now demanding proof of payback will keep paying for chip growth.

## Nvidia's Growth Trajectory and Valuation Ceiling

Nvidia's Q2 FY2027 revenue of $96.2 billion, up 106% year over year, and Q3 guidance of $108 billion anchor a ~70% FY2028 growth outlook that CFO [[Colette Kress]] frames as limited by supply rather than demand ([[nvidia-7-trillion-market-cap-broadcom-comparison-valuation]]). With the earnings path largely set, the variable that moves the stock is the multiple. In September, reaching $7 trillion from roughly $5.45 trillion meant adding about one Broadcom and re-rating from roughly 14x to 18x FY2028 earnings, while a separate read with the market cap near $5.6 trillion called about 24x forward earnings reasonable against the same growth projection ([[nvidia-record-buyback-authorization-235-billion-sep2026]]). Both placed the real uncertainty in how long AI capex lasts and what multiple the market will pay.

That argument has since played out in reverse. By November 13 Nvidia traded near $162 with a market cap of about $3.91 trillion, down roughly 29% in a month and about $630 billion lighter, yet consensus estimates had barely moved, revenue growth had accelerated from 62.5% to 105.9% and the forward P/E stood near 17.8x ([[nvidia-falling-knife-or-buy-the-dip-nov13-2026]]). That note reads the move as a de-rating rather than an earnings downgrade, and the vault's notes quote the forward multiple on different bases that they do not reconcile, so the 14x and 17.8x figures should not be read as one path. Earlier, on October 30, Apple had overtaken Nvidia as the most valuable company, which one note reads as a hint of flows toward names less tied to data-center spending ([[nasdaq-100-intraday-correction-intel-chips-oct30-2026]]). The November 17 report is now a binary event, with consensus at EPS of $2.47 and revenue of $108.8 billion, just above the guidance given in September, and with technical readings still negative and the leadership question unresolved ([[nvidia-falling-knife-or-buy-the-dip-nov13-2026]], [[sp500-worst-week-since-april-2025-ai-chip-selloff-nvidia-cpi]]).

## A Leadership Shock at the Center of the Stack

The sell-off acquired a company-specific trigger when CNBC reported that U.S. intelligence had assessed China's Ministry of State Security as having recruited Megaspeed executive Alice Huang to gather information on [[Jensen Huang]], after he disclosed in a staff memo that he had been intimate with her; Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation, said it had no reason to believe secrets were compromised, and three House Republican chairs asked for a closed-door briefing on November 18, a day after earnings ([[nvidia-jensen-huang-china-spy-congress-scrutiny-megaspeed]]). Nvidia fell 4.3% on November 9 even as the broader market had its best day since April and AMD rebounded 13.3%, then 5.53% on November 10 after independent directors hired Joon H. Kim to run the probe ([[us-stocks-rally-recover-losses-oil-falls-nvidia-sinks-ap-nov9-2026]], [[nasdaq-falls-0-74-october-cpi-tops-forecasts-nvidia-drops-nov10-2026]]). On November 12 reports that the board was meeting on Huang's fate sent the stock down nearly 7% premarket, and he then resigned as CEO and director, with the shares down more than 14% at one point ([[wall-street-dips-nvidia-ceo-scandal-treasury-5-percent-fed-hike-bets-reuters-nov12-2026]], [[nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resigns-colette-kress-interim-ceo]]).

Kress, CFO since 2013, is interim CEO, Harvey Jones became the first board chair, and the candidates discussed range from insiders to outside names, with the Financial Times saying the board approached Arm CEO Rene Haas ([[nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resigns-colette-kress-interim-ceo]], [[us-stocks-mixed-nvidia-fallout-nasdaq-0-91-ppi-10yr-5-15-nov13-2026]]). The cost was steep: just under $600 billion of market value in the week, a sixth straight weekly loss that was the longest streak since October 2012, and at least seven Wall Street downgrades ([[stocks-fall-weekly-gain-nvidia-huang-resignation-ap-nov14-2026]], [[us-stocks-mixed-nvidia-fallout-nasdaq-0-91-ppi-10yr-5-15-nov13-2026]], [[weekly-highlights-nvidia-ceo-exit-sgx-volumes-treasury-yields-pension-index-nov13-2026]]). The notes stress that no established evidence shows Huang knowingly helped Beijing and that the board sees no compromise, so the shock is framed as governance risk rather than earnings risk, and one strategist cited in the Reuters report warns that strong results may not remove the governance discount ([[nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resigns-colette-kress-interim-ceo]], [[nvidia-falling-knife-or-buy-the-dip-nov13-2026]], [[wall-street-dips-nvidia-ceo-scandal-treasury-5-percent-fed-hike-bets-reuters-nov12-2026]]).

## Nvidia's Capital Allocation: Cash, Buybacks, and Circularity

On September 28 Nvidia's board added $150 billion to its repurchase program, bringing remaining authorization to $235 billion, the largest increase on record, roughly 4.3% of market value, to be deployed by fiscal 2028 at a pace of about $39 billion a quarter against a recent ~$20 billion quarter ([[nvidia-record-buyback-authorization-235-billion-sep2026]]). The firepower comes from a model in which GPUs and networking drive most revenue, supplemented by royalties and revenue-sharing with neoclouds and startups, leaving more than $50.5 billion in cash at Q2 ([[ai-monetization-nvidia-microsoft-alphabet-palantir]]). Jensen Huang presented the buyback as proof that Nvidia can fund innovation and reward shareholders at once, while skeptics point to circular financing with suppliers and customers as the bear case ([[nvidia-record-buyback-authorization-235-billion-sep2026]]).

That circularity has become a financing book. Nvidia acts as an AI financier, with $36 billion in neocloud backstops, a guarantee of up to $105 billion over 20 years of hardware for OpenAI's Ohio data center, and more than $500 billion of off-balance-sheet commitments, most maturing through fiscal 2029 ([[nvidia-ai-financier-risk-neocloud-selloff-oct2026]]). In October that book faced its first large test. CoreWeave, Nebius and IREN fell at least 30%, CoreWeave's credit default swap spread rose above 11%, and Nvidia had its third straight down week, though roughly $70 billion of first-half free cash flow and its credit rating give it a cushion that AMD lacks ([[nvidia-ai-financier-risk-neocloud-selloff-oct2026]]). A backstop costs nothing while borrowers perform and pays only on default, so the cash pile buffers a liability that grows quietly until partners stumble. Moody's counts such guarantees as exposure rather than debt, and Nvidia's search for insurers on GPU value risk is, in the note's words, not yet a complete fix ([[nvidia-ai-financier-risk-neocloud-selloff-oct2026]]). The financing role is still widening: Nvidia is reported to plan about $10 billion of investment in Anthropic's expected listing ([[nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resigns-colette-kress-interim-ceo]]).

## Expectations Tests: Revenue Scares and the Intel Shock

Chip valuations already embed years of growth, so the stocks trade on the gap between reported AI revenue and what prices assume. On October 8 a Financial Times report that OpenAI's annualized revenue was nearing $50 billion, about $20 billion below earlier estimates of $70 billion, sent Nvidia down more than 2%, Broadcom nearly 4% and Oracle more than 4%, even though the gap mostly reflected gross-versus-net revenue recognition and OpenAI was still growing over 70% ([[openai-50-billion-revenue-gap-ai-stocks-selloff-oct2026]]). The underlying worry, whether AI revenue can justify hundreds of billions in chip and data-center spending, is the same capex-durability question that kept sentiment guarded around the buyback ([[nvidia-record-buyback-authorization-235-billion-sep2026]]). Jim Cramer added a macro layer in the week of October 12, flagging long-bond yields pushed toward 6% by Treasury borrowing and data-center demand for money as the chief overhang ([[cramer-week-ahead-bank-chip-earnings-oct2026]]).

The same test returned harsher on October 30. Intel fell 28.6% after guiding fourth-quarter revenue to $15 to $16 billion and gross margin to 38%, against 44.1% in the quarter just reported, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 7.2%, its worst day since June 23 ([[nasdaq-100-intraday-correction-intel-chips-oct30-2026]]). The note reads the moment as a move from AI hopes to a show-me phase in which undershooting guidance is punished disproportionately. It also puts September's CPU enthusiasm in perspective, since Intel's 12% jump on the agentic-AI narrative was followed within weeks by a guidance-driven collapse ([[intel-arm-amd-chip-stocks-agentic-ai-cpu-rally-sep2026]]). The November 10 inflation report added a financing angle. Fed official Michelle Bowman warned that disappointing AI returns could trigger a sharp valuation correction, and Morgan Stanley expected leveraged finance for AI to stay subdued through year-end, in a year when AI-related leveraged issuance had reached $646 billion against $216 billion in all of 2025 ([[nasdaq-falls-0-74-october-cpi-tops-forecasts-nvidia-drops-nov10-2026]]).

## The November Drawdown Across Markets

The selloff did not wait for Nvidia's leadership crisis. After the midterm elections, Asian shares mostly rose on a U.S. rally, yet AI-linked chip names kept falling, with the Kospi briefly down more than 4%, Samsung Electronics off 5.7%, SK Hynix off 7.4%, and Tokyo Electron and Advantest down 7.7% and 9.4%, while the less AI-exposed Hang Seng and ASX 200 gained ([[asian-shares-advance-after-midterms-kospi-chip-selloff-nov5-2026]]). Commentary on that episode argues that profits and interest rates matter more to prices than which party holds Washington, though the loss of Republican sole control added to a worry that AI-boosted prices had run too high ([[asian-shares-advance-after-midterms-kospi-chip-selloff-nov5-2026]]). The U.S. move was sharper. The S&P 500 closed November 6 at 7,376.86 after a fourth straight weekly loss, its worst Friday-to-Friday week since the April 2025 tariff crash, while the semiconductor index fell 12.15% and the Dow less than 1%. Hot October jobs data lifted the odds of a December rate hike to 78% from 70%, which shows the same mechanism as the expectations tests above: distant earnings that chip valuations rest on lose value when rates rise ([[sp500-worst-week-since-april-2025-ai-chip-selloff-nvidia-cpi]]).

The following week showed how rates and the Nvidia story compounded each other. A November 9 rebound lifted AMD and CoreWeave, but October CPI came in hot on November 10, the 10-year Treasury yield returned to 5% on November 12 with the dollar at a 19-month high, and traders priced a 90% chance of a December hike, which strategists in the Reuters report called the deeper driver with Nvidia acting as a risk-off catalyst ([[us-stocks-rally-recover-losses-oil-falls-nvidia-sinks-ap-nov9-2026]], [[wall-street-dips-nvidia-ceo-scandal-treasury-5-percent-fed-hike-bets-reuters-nov12-2026]]). On November 13 a 6.5% year-on-year PPI reading pushed the 10-year to 5.15%, and the chip complex fell together, with AMD down 8.75%, Broadcom 4.8%, Dell 7.18%, Cisco 6.24% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index 3.4%, as CoreWeave, Nebius, IREN and Applied Digital also dropped ([[us-stocks-mixed-nvidia-fallout-nasdaq-0-91-ppi-10yr-5-15-nov13-2026]], [[stock-market-midday-nov13-2026-nasdaq-plunges-nvidia-turmoil-yields-5-percent]]). The week still ended as the S&P 500's first winning week since early October, up 2.5%, with more than 60% of its stocks rising on Friday even as the index fell 0.4%, a breadth mismatch that shows how much weight a few AI names carry ([[stocks-fall-weekly-gain-nvidia-huang-resignation-ap-nov14-2026]], [[us-stocks-fall-friday-nov13-2026-first-winning-week-since-october-ap]]).

## Memory: The Bottleneck That Squeezes Margins

DRAM prices were projected to roughly quadruple by 2026, with supply tight through 2029 to 2030, which makes memory the next structural constraint on AI hardware ([[micron-vs-sandisk-ai-memory-stock-comparison]]). The pressure already reaches the top of the stack: Kress guided Nvidia's gross margin down from about 75% toward 71% to 72% by fiscal Q4 2027, largely on memory costs ([[nvidia-record-buyback-authorization-235-billion-sep2026]]). Suppliers are answering with capacity ahead of orders. Samsung plans to more than double HBM4 and HBM4E output in 2027, growing total HBM capacity about 40% to roughly 250,000 wafers a month and lifting the HBM4 family from around 40% to 80% of shipments, after sending 12-layer HBM4E samples to Nvidia in May ([[samsung-hbm4-output-doubling-2027]]). Google's latest TPU shows the demand side of the same constraint, since the 8i Max doubles HBM bandwidth from 8.6 to 17.2 TB/s explicitly to attack the memory wall, and Samsung and SK hynix reportedly split Google's HBM3E orders about evenly, with SK hynix pursuing custom HBM for the TPUs ([[google-first-4-tpu-year-8tx-8i-max-hbm-memory-wall]]). Analysts favor Micron's combined DRAM and NAND exposure over NAND-only Sandisk at similar valuations, which reads memory pricing power and Nvidia's margin squeeze as two sides of one shortage ([[micron-vs-sandisk-ai-memory-stock-comparison]]). Startups are also aiming at memory itself, as XCENA's CXL controller moves preprocessing and KV-cache management inside memory ([[ai-chip-startup-series-b-rounds-2026-matx-positron-fractile-xcena]]).

## CPUs and the Agentic Inference Shift

Training remains GPU-dominated, but the planning and orchestration that agents do at inference time lean on CPUs. When Meta's Muse agent topped U.S. iPhone download charts for three straight days, Arm rose 17%, Intel 12% and AMD 10% on September 21 ([[intel-arm-amd-chip-stocks-agentic-ai-cpu-rally-sep2026]]). The company forecasts behind the rally differ in size but agree on direction: Arm sees the server CPU market compounding 35% a year to $120 billion by 2030, and AMD sees 50% a year to $220 billion by the decade's end ([[intel-arm-amd-chip-stocks-agentic-ai-cpu-rally-sep2026]]). Dell's results show the same wave turning into hardware orders as enterprises put autonomous agents on local servers instead of the cloud ([[dell-stock-surges-record-ai-server-orders-q2-2027]]). Google's TPU design points the same way at the host level, with its own Arm-based Axion CPUs replacing x86 hosts in TPU 8i servers and AMD processors paired with the 8tx ([[google-first-4-tpu-year-8tx-8i-max-hbm-memory-wall]]).

## Local AI Hardware: Grace Blackwell Moves to the Desk

Nvidia is moving Grace Blackwell silicon out of the data center. A 64GB DGX Spark goes on sale October 23 from $4,999 through six manufacturer partners, keeping the GB10 superchip and software stack of the 128GB model and running models up to 100 billion parameters on one unit, or up to 200 billion when two units pool memory over ConnectX-7 links ([[nvidia-dgx-spark-64gb-4999-cluster-128gb]]). Dell, working with Nvidia and Microsoft, takes the idea to Windows, with the RTX Spark superchip and up to 128GB of unified memory in an XPS 16 Creator Edition and a creator desktop, and a GB300 workstation claiming 20 petaflops of FP4 and 748GB of coherent memory ([[dell-rtx-spark-xps-16-creator-edition-pro-precision-gb300-windows]]). Both pitches rest on a shared CPU-GPU memory pool large enough for models a discrete GPU cannot hold, and on the argument that always-on agents need private local compute rather than hourly cloud rentals ([[nvidia-dgx-spark-64gb-4999-cluster-128gb]], [[dell-rtx-spark-xps-16-creator-edition-pro-precision-gb300-windows]]). The evidence remains thin. Nvidia's only clustering figure, a "1.7x" gain, comes from one in-house test with no stated metric, and Dell's desktop and GB300 Windows systems are still "coming soon" ([[nvidia-dgx-spark-64gb-4999-cluster-128gb]], [[dell-rtx-spark-xps-16-creator-edition-pro-precision-gb300-windows]]).

## Google's TPU Line: Hyperscaler Silicon Up a Notch

On November 9 Google unveiled the TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max, the first year in which it has introduced four TPU systems, after April's 8t and 8i delivered 2.7x training performance and 80% better inference price-performance ([[google-first-4-tpu-year-8tx-8i-max-hbm-memory-wall]]). The 8tx pairs with AMD processors to encrypt data in use at the hardware level, runs up to twice as fast as its predecessor with little performance penalty, and targets government and regulated industries such as banking and healthcare, while the 8i Max puts two chips in one package for more memory and compute, aimed at huge-context tools ([[alphabet-googl-tpu-8tx-8i-max-nvidia-rival-nov9-2026]]). Google has not officially confirmed the reported rise in 8i Max HBM capacity from 288 GB to 384 GB ([[google-first-4-tpu-year-8tx-8i-max-hbm-memory-wall]]).

The commercial logic is that Google increasingly rents TPUs to outside customers through Google Cloud, whose Q3 revenue rose 103% to $30.71 billion with TPU demand a key driver, and Alphabet shares climbed on the launch ([[alphabet-googl-tpu-8tx-8i-max-nvidia-rival-nov9-2026]]). It is capital-hungry: Alphabet spent $51.4 billion in Q3 and guides $195 to $205 billion for 2026, and Google's AI infrastructure chief described the new chips as reconfigurations responding to cloud customer requests ([[alphabet-googl-tpu-8tx-8i-max-nvidia-rival-nov9-2026]]). The supplier map shows how interlocked the stack is, since Broadcom is Google's TPU development partner, TSMC, Samsung and SK hynix are expected to benefit, and AMD appears here as a partner even as it competes with Nvidia elsewhere ([[google-first-4-tpu-year-8tx-8i-max-hbm-memory-wall]]).

## Startup Challengers and Amazon's Software Attack on the CUDA Moat

Venture money is backing niche challengers rather than GPU replacements. MatX ($500M), Positron ($230M), Fractile ($220M) and XCENA ($135M) closed the largest disclosed 2026 semiconductor Series B rounds, together about $1.085 billion between February and May, with strategies spanning chips for AI labs' own architectures, inference specialists, in-memory compute and CXL controllers ([[ai-chip-startup-series-b-rounds-2026-matx-positron-fractile-xcena]]). Only Positron, whose Intel-fabbed Atlas inference chip already ships, has silicon in customers' hands, none names a signed enterprise contract, and every performance claim is company-reported. Positron's later Series C at roughly $5 billion shows how quickly execution can widen the gap between funded claims and valuation ([[ai-chip-startup-series-b-rounds-2026-matx-positron-fractile-xcena]]). Outside the U.S., state-linked capital backs an inference specialist directly. Korea's FuriosaAI closed an 820 billion won Series D at about 3.8 trillion won, above its target, with the National Growth Fund investing 370 billion won, and it plans to commercialize its RNGD inference chip, pitched on power and cost, and to co-develop a third-generation accelerator with Broadcom ([[furiosaai-820-billion-won-series-d-rngd-challenges-nvidia]]). Incumbents hedge rather than cede ground: Nvidia, AMD and Samsung joined more than 60 startup financings of $100 million or more in 2026 ([[ai-monetization-nvidia-microsoft-alphabet-palantir]]).

The sharpest attack on the moat comes from software. Amazon's TorchNeuron lets existing PyTorch code run on Trainium with a configuration change rather than a rewrite, which goes straight at the switching costs CUDA creates beyond raw performance. It has more than 30 early customers, and Anthropic is a major Trainium user through Project Rainier ([[amazon-torchneuron-trainium-pytorch-cuda-moat]]). Amazon's aim is to make trying an alternative cheap rather than to displace CUDA at once, and Trainium still has to prove performance, reliability and economics on hard workloads ([[amazon-torchneuron-trainium-pytorch-cuda-moat]]). The warning that fabrication, yield and software lock-in have sunk better-funded challengers before points to the same full-stack defense AMD is trying to buy around ([[furiosaai-820-billion-won-series-d-rngd-challenges-nvidia]], [[amd-acquires-world-labs-fei-fei-li]]).

## Inference Efficiency: Fewer GPUs per Workload

Nvidia has also put money into software that can cut the number of GPUs a workload needs. It joined a $14 million Series B in Indian startup Simplismart, whose software tunes how models run on existing hardware, trading off response speed, throughput and compute use, and which integrates with Nvidia's Dynamo inference software ([[nvidia-backs-simplismart-ai-inference-fewer-gpus]]). The paradox is that efficiency software can shrink GPUs per workload, yet its CEO argues cheaper inference widens adoption and ultimately favors Nvidia hardware. The company's evidence is a customer that cut GPU serving costs 56% and a revenue run rate that grew fourteen-fold in six months, though the field is crowded with Nvidia, the hyperscalers and other startups ([[nvidia-backs-simplismart-ai-inference-fewer-gpus]]). Read alongside FuriosaAI's energy pitch ([[furiosaai-820-billion-won-series-d-rngd-challenges-nvidia]]) and Chinese buyers who want compliant Nvidia cards mainly for inference ([[nvidia-rtx-pro-5500-china-orders-35-billion-bytedance-alibaba-tencent]]), the shared theme is that the cost of running models, not only training them, is becoming a contested layer of the stack.

## AMD Moves Up the Stack Into World Models

AMD's $8.2 billion acquisition of Fei-Fei Li's World Labs, with Li becoming executive vice president and chief scientist, is explicitly a chip-design play. AMD wants the startup's physical-world workloads to inform future silicon, building on an existing partnership on inference and training optimization ([[amd-acquires-world-labs-fei-fei-li]]). Coverage frames it as a response to Nvidia, which already offers open-weight world models such as Cosmos and whose full-stack lock-in anchors its monetization ([[amd-acquires-world-labs-fei-fei-li]], [[ai-monetization-nvidia-microsoft-alphabet-palantir]]). Chip competition is therefore fought over software ecosystems and workloads as well as silicon. Arm is pushing into the same physical-AI frontier through its AGI CPU and Neoverse chips ([[arm-holdings-vs-asml-ai-stock-comparison]]), and world models matter there because synthetic training data can offset the shortage of real-world data for humanoid robots and self-driving cars ([[amd-acquires-world-labs-fei-fei-li]]).

## AI Servers: Downstream Proof of Demand

Dell's fiscal Q2 2027 delivered $47 billion in revenue (+58%), a record $60.9 billion in AI server orders and a $95 billion backlog, with FY2027 AI server revenue guided to triple to $74 billion and its share of the AI server market rising from 5% to 17% in a year ([[dell-stock-surges-record-ai-server-orders-q2-2027]]). Goldman Sachs raised its 2030 AI server market forecast to $1.24 trillion from $961 billion on higher volumes and higher selling prices, so server OEMs now sit beside chip suppliers as picks-and-shovels beneficiaries ([[dell-stock-surges-record-ai-server-orders-q2-2027]]). Michael Dell's warning that a structural chip shortage could worsen in 2027 matches Nvidia's supply-constrained guidance ([[nvidia-7-trillion-market-cap-broadcom-comparison-valuation]]) and the multi-year DRAM tightness, which suggests supply rather than demand is the binding limit at several layers of the stack ([[micron-vs-sandisk-ai-memory-stock-comparison]]). Demand evidence has not insulated the stocks from the November rout, though, since Dell fell 7.18% on November 13 alongside the chip names ([[us-stocks-mixed-nvidia-fallout-nasdaq-0-91-ppi-10yr-5-15-nov13-2026]]).

## China Demand: Export-Compliant Chips, Rationing and Political Risk

Export controls shape where demand lands. ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent ordered more than 3 million Nvidia RTX PRO 5500 workstation cards, a combined list price above $35 billion, for a chip that is less powerful than the H200 and B300 and fits within U.S. controls, since the Blackwell-based B300 is barred for Chinese buyers and H200 shipments need case-by-case licensing ([[nvidia-rtx-pro-5500-china-orders-35-billion-bytedance-alibaba-tencent]]). Nvidia warned allocations may be only 5% to 10% of requests, because 2027 output is capped at 3 million cards across all markets against Chinese orders above 6 million, and because TSMC slots shift toward the Rubin platform from 2027 ([[nvidia-rtx-pro-5500-china-orders-35-billion-bytedance-alibaba-tencent]]). Buyers want the cards mainly for inference, and Nvidia blames outdated U.S. controls and China's own import limits. Sentiment also turns quickly. The RTX 6000D drew little Chinese interest after its mid-2025 launch, and it is now discontinued and selling for twice its earlier China price ([[nvidia-rtx-pro-5500-china-orders-35-billion-bytedance-alibaba-tencent]]).

The Huang affair has put that demand under political scrutiny. Huang had campaigned for years against restrictions on China sales, the administration approved H200 exports in December 2025, and Chinese firms reportedly ordered over two million of those chips, potentially worth $54 billion ([[nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resigns-colette-kress-interim-ceo]]). Megaspeed, the Singapore firm tied to the case, is one the U.S. has suspected of helping China circumvent chip sales bans, and lawmakers also questioned the Commerce Department's clearance of New Matrix to assemble Nvidia chips in Beijing, where reporters found little evidence of operations ([[nvidia-jensen-huang-china-spy-congress-scrutiny-megaspeed]]). The other side of the trade moved too, with domestic Chinese GPU makers surging on expected share gains while Alibaba and Tencent fell on possible curbs on offshore datacentre access ([[wall-street-dips-nvidia-ceo-scandal-treasury-5-percent-fed-hike-bets-reuters-nov12-2026]]).

## The Indispensable Layer: ASML, Arm, and the Equipment Read-Through

ASML's monopoly on EUV lithography makes it the toolmaker that profits whoever wins the chip race. It generated about €32.7 billion of FY2025 revenue at a 29.4% net margin and €11.1 billion of free cash flow, trading near 27.9x forward earnings, with China export restrictions as its main risk ([[arm-holdings-vs-asml-ai-stock-comparison]]). Arm's architecture sits in roughly 350 billion chips, but it trades near 125x forward earnings, about 69% of its operating cash flow goes to stock-based compensation, and open-source RISC-V threatens its licensing model ([[arm-holdings-vs-asml-ai-stock-comparison]]). Its 17% jump in the CPU rally shows how sensitive such multiples are to narrative shifts ([[intel-arm-amd-chip-stocks-agentic-ai-cpu-rally-sep2026]]). Cramer treats ASML's guidance as a read-through for the equipment layer, a cue to consider Lam Research or Applied Materials, and he ranks TSMC's results as the week's biggest potential chip catalyst ([[cramer-week-ahead-bank-chip-earnings-oct2026]]).

## ETF Exposure and Portfolio Sizing

The VanEck Semiconductor ETF beat the S&P 500 in 8 of the 10 years from 2016 to 2025, turning $1,000 into about $15,000 against about $4,000, but it fell roughly twice as far as the index in its losing years, including about 33% in 2022, and each worst year was followed by a best one ([[vaneck-semiconductor-etf-smh-sp500-outperformance-history]]). Concentration amplifies that pattern. Nvidia is about 23% of the fund's roughly $72 billion in assets and the top five holdings about half, so the fund is tied to the same company whose buyback, financing, margin and now leadership story dominate this topic ([[vaneck-semiconductor-etf-smh-sp500-outperformance-history]]). A broad index is not a clean escape. Nvidia was about 7.9% of the S&P 500 by one reading ([[nvidia-record-buyback-authorization-235-billion-sep2026]]) and 7.58% of SPY by a March 2026 fact sheet, so the week that cut the index 2.68% also carried the chip drop into "diversified" holdings ([[sp500-worst-week-since-april-2025-ai-chip-selloff-nvidia-cpi]]). The AP's account of November 13 repeats the point from the other direction, since a few mega-cap technology stocks drove the index lower on a day when most of its members rose ([[stocks-fall-weekly-gain-nvidia-huang-resignation-ap-nov14-2026]]). Cap weighting causes this, and an equal-weight index that gives Nvidia the same weight as every other company is the alternative the sources point to ([[sp500-worst-week-since-april-2025-ai-chip-selloff-nvidia-cpi]]). The Nasdaq-100 showed the same transmission on October 30, when QQQ holders absorbed the semiconductor drop directly because chipmakers are top holdings ([[nasdaq-100-intraday-correction-intel-chips-oct30-2026]]). Any SMH position should be sized to survive a drawdown of about a third, so that cyclical volatility does not become permanent loss ([[vaneck-semiconductor-etf-smh-sp500-outperformance-history]]).
