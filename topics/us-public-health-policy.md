# US Public Health Policy: Vaccines, Outbreaks and HHS

This topic follows how US public health policy under HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. is changing (the breakdown of federal vaccine advisory machinery, disputes over vaccine science, the nation's largest current measles outbreak, and HHS initiatives outside vaccines) and how courts, states and medical societies are stepping into the gaps.

## Advisory Machinery Breaks Down: ACIP and the Guidance Vacuum
The root of this year's vaccine-policy turmoil is Kennedy's dismissal of all 17 members of the CDC's vaccine advisory committee and their replacement with vaccine-skeptic appointees, after which federal courts stayed the new panel's votes and temporarily blocked its cut of the recommended childhood schedule from 17 shots to 11 [[fda-withdraws-covid-shingles-vaccine-research-findings]]. Because a judge found the appointments unlawful, ACIP has not convened since December 2025 [[cdc-opens-covid-vaccine-orders-vaccines-for-children]], leaving the 2026–2027 season without federal COVID-19 or RSV recommendations. AAP, AAFP, ACOG and IDSA divided the population among themselves (children, adults, pregnant people and the immunocompromised) and issued their own schedules on September 2, backed by an AMA–Vaccine Integrity Project review in *JAMA* that found continued protection against severe illness and no new safety signals [[medical-groups-independent-flu-covid-rsv-vaccine-guidance]]. The sources describe the resulting gap as a failure of institutions rather than of science.

## Approval Is Not Access
The FDA approved four updated COVID-19 vaccines from Pfizer-BioNTech, Moderna and Novavax on August 27, all matched to the XFG "Stratus" variant and recommended mainly for adults 65 and older and people with underlying conditions [[fda-approves-updated-covid-19-vaccines-xfg-variant]]. The CDC opened ordering through the Vaccines for Children program almost a month later, the second year in a row that VFC access has lagged, and real availability depends on how fast each state rolls it out, with some clinics also getting flu doses late and in small batches [[cdc-opens-covid-vaccine-orders-vaccines-for-children]]. Young children bear the most risk from the lag, since infants aged 6–11 months are hospitalized for COVID-19 at rates similar to adults aged 65–74. Even where supply arrives, out-of-pocket costs lead low-income and uninsured families to skip shots, so federal approval does not solve the last-mile problem.

## mRNA: Approvals Continue While Federal Funding Retreats
The FDA approved Moderna's mFLUSIVA as the first mRNA seasonal flu vaccine for adults 50 and older after a phase 3 trial of more than 40,000 people showed about 27% higher relative efficacy than standard-dose shots in 50–64-year-olds, though the 65+ indication rests on accelerated approval and still needs confirmatory evidence [[moderna-mflusiva-mrna-flu-vaccine-fda-approval]]. The fall COVID-19 lineup also depends on how quickly mRNA can be reformulated, with Novavax as a protein-based option [[fda-approves-updated-covid-19-vaccines-xfg-variant]]. At the same time, Kennedy cancelled at least $500 million in federally funded mRNA vaccine development in August [[fda-withdraws-covid-shingles-vaccine-research-findings]], so the platform is winning regulatory approvals while its public research pipeline shrinks.

## Scientific Integrity: Withdrawn Studies and Mixed Messaging
The sharpest integrity dispute came when the FDA, at HHS's direction, pulled COVID-19 and shingles vaccine safety studies from publication, saying their conclusions went beyond the data. A former CDC official called it part of a pattern of blocking strong taxpayer-funded research that cuts against the department's anti-vaccine narrative, while the CDC itself still says the shingles vaccine is not linked to serious adverse events [[fda-withdraws-covid-shingles-vaccine-research-findings]]. Independent reviewers answered by grounding authority in the evidence rather than the agency, concluding that the science behind seasonal vaccination has not changed [[medical-groups-independent-flu-covid-rsv-vaccine-guidance]]. The public is confused too: an Annenberg survey found 31% of adults believe Kennedy recommends measles shots for children, 29% believe he does not, and 40% are unsure [[measles-outbreak-pennsylvania-900-cases-cdc-death-toll]].

## Measles in Pennsylvania: Outbreak Response and Disputed Counts
Pennsylvania's measles outbreak, now the nation's largest and fastest-growing, rose from 903 to 943 confirmed cases (about a quarter of the 2026 national total), with 176 hospitalizations at the 900-case mark and five deaths, the state's first measles deaths in 35 years [[measles-outbreak-pennsylvania-900-cases-cdc-death-toll]]. Fewer than 1% of patients were fully vaccinated and nearly a third are children, so the outbreak is concentrated among the unvaccinated. Federal and state figures also disagree: the CDC recognizes only two measles deaths nationally this year, which set off a public dispute between Pennsylvania officials and CDC/HHS leadership. STAT reporting adds that the true size is unknowable, because tallies count only confirmed cases and insular, low-vaccination Amish communities tend to seek care only once illness becomes severe.

## Fragile Outbreak Data
The cyclosporiasis outbreak spread to at least 47 states, with Michigan jumping from a typical ~50 annual cases to 14,277 in about seven weeks [[cyclosporiasis-outbreak-2026-us-produce]]. The FDA first linked it to Taylor Farms iceberg lettuce, which led to a voluntary recall, then retracted the lab result as a false positive because *Cyclospora* is hard to detect, leaving the source unidentified. Measles has a different problem, with confirmed-only counts that understate spread [[measles-outbreak-pennsylvania-900-cases-cdc-death-toll]], and together the two cases show that both the cause and the scale of an outbreak can stay uncertain well after officials act.

## HHS Initiatives Beyond Vaccines: Ticks and Clinical Trials
Through CDC and NIH, HHS awarded nearly $3 million to two Centers of Excellence in Vector-Borne Diseases to test acaricide-treated deer feed and rodent "super baits" that combine acaricide and antibiotics. The aim is to break the tick life cycle in wildlife hosts rather than rely only on personal prevention against more than 500,000 annual tickborne diagnoses, including Lyme disease and alpha-gal syndrome [[hhs-tick-control-funding-lyme-disease]]. Separately, ARPA-H's SURPASS program and its companion projects STACK, COMMONS and CINCH use digital-twin trial design, real-time adaptive statistics, AI-driven site activation and shared national data infrastructure to attack drug development that takes more than a decade, costs up to $2 billion and fails over 90% of the time [[hhs-arpa-h-surpass-clinical-trials]]. Kennedy pitches both efforts as fixing problems at the source, which shows the department's agenda reaching into disease ecology and research infrastructure, not just vaccine policy.

## Nutrition as a Policy Lever
HHS's Advancing Nutrition Education initiative asks medical schools to cover 71 nutrition topics, but only 73 of 211 eligible M.D. and D.O. programs have joined, and participants say they will adopt only evidence-backed content to avoid "wellness creep" from MAHA-aligned material [[medical-schools-nutrition-education-rfk-push]]. Organized medicine is following its own track: the AMA formally adopted food-is-medicine policy covering medically tailored meals, medically tailored groceries and produce prescriptions, urged HHS appropriations to fund them, and notes that physicians average only about 19 hours of nutrition training in medical school [[ama-food-is-medicine-policy-2026]]. As with vaccine guidance [[medical-groups-independent-flu-covid-rsv-vaccine-guidance]], professional bodies accept the underlying need but filter federal direction through their own evidence standards.
