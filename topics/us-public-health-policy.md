# US Public Health Policy: Vaccines, Outbreaks and HHS

This topic follows how US public health policy under HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. is changing (the breakdown of federal vaccine advisory machinery, disputes over vaccine science, the nation's largest current measles outbreak, and a widening HHS and NIH agenda that now includes a new women's health research structure) and how courts, states and medical societies are stepping into the gaps.

## Advisory Machinery Breaks Down: ACIP and the Guidance Vacuum
The root of this year's vaccine-policy turmoil is Kennedy's removal of all 17 members of the CDC's vaccine advisory committee and their replacement with vaccine-skeptic appointees, after which federal courts stayed the new panel's votes and temporarily blocked its cut of the recommended childhood schedule from 17 shots to 11 [[fda-withdraws-covid-shingles-vaccine-research-findings]]. Because a judge found the appointments unlawful, ACIP has not met since December 2025 [[cdc-opens-covid-vaccine-orders-vaccines-for-children]], and the 2026–2027 season arrived without federal COVID-19 or RSV recommendations. AAP, AAFP, ACOG and IDSA split the population among themselves (children, adults, pregnant people and the immunocompromised) and issued their own guidance on September 2, supported by an AMA–Vaccine Integrity Project review in *JAMA* that found continued protection against severe illness and no new safety signals [[medical-groups-independent-flu-covid-rsv-vaccine-guidance]]. That source frames the gap as a policy failure rather than a scientific one.

## Approval Is Not Access
The FDA approved four updated COVID-19 vaccines from Pfizer-BioNTech, Moderna and Novavax on August 27, all aimed at the XFG "Stratus" variant and recommended especially for adults 65 and older and people with underlying conditions [[fda-approves-updated-covid-19-vaccines-xfg-variant]]. The CDC opened Vaccines for Children ordering nearly a month later, the second straight year of delay, and actual availability turns on each state's rollout, with some clinics also receiving flu doses late and in small quantities [[cdc-opens-covid-vaccine-orders-vaccines-for-children]]. The lag matters most for infants, whose hospitalization rate at 6–11 months is similar to that of adults 65–74, and out-of-pocket costs can lead low-income and uninsured families to skip shots even when supply exists. A federal sign-off therefore does not settle the last-mile question of whether a vaccine reaches a clinic and a family.

## mRNA: Approvals Continue While Federal Funding Retreats
The FDA approved Moderna's mFLUSIVA as the first mRNA seasonal flu vaccine for adults 50 and older, on the strength of a phase 3 trial of more than 40,000 adults showing about 27% higher relative efficacy than standard-dose vaccines in ages 50–64, while the 65+ indication rests on accelerated approval and still needs confirmatory evidence [[moderna-mflusiva-mrna-flu-vaccine-fda-approval]]. The speed of mRNA reformulation also underpins the fall COVID-19 lineup, with Novavax offering a protein-based alternative [[fda-approves-updated-covid-19-vaccines-xfg-variant]]. Yet Kennedy cancelled at least $500 million in federally funded mRNA vaccine development in August [[fda-withdraws-covid-shingles-vaccine-research-findings]], so the platform is gaining regulatory approvals while its public research pipeline shrinks.

## Scientific Integrity: Withdrawn Studies and Mixed Messaging
The sharpest integrity dispute came when the FDA, at HHS's direction, withdrew COVID-19 and shingles vaccine safety studies, with HHS saying their conclusions went beyond the underlying data. A former CDC official described a pattern of blocking high-quality taxpayer-funded research that undercuts the department's anti-vaccine narrative, and the CDC continues to say the shingles vaccine is not associated with serious adverse events [[fda-withdraws-covid-shingles-vaccine-research-findings]]. Independent reviewers responded by anchoring authority in evidence rather than in the agency, concluding the science behind seasonal vaccination has not changed [[medical-groups-independent-flu-covid-rsv-vaccine-guidance]]. Public understanding is muddied as well: an Annenberg survey found 31% of adults think Kennedy recommends measles vaccination for children, 29% think he does not, and 40% are unsure [[measles-outbreak-pennsylvania-900-cases-cdc-death-toll]].

## Measles in Pennsylvania: Outbreak Response and Disputed Counts
Pennsylvania's measles outbreak, the nation's largest and fastest-growing, rose from 903 to 943 confirmed cases (about a quarter of the 2026 national total), with 176 hospitalizations at the 900-case mark and five deaths, the state's first measles deaths in 35 years [[measles-outbreak-pennsylvania-900-cases-cdc-death-toll]]. Fewer than 1% of patients were fully vaccinated and 31% of cases are children, tying the outbreak to low immunization coverage. Federal and state numbers diverge, since the CDC recognizes only two measles deaths nationally this year, which set off a public dispute between Pennsylvania officials and CDC/HHS leadership. STAT adds that the true size is not knowable, because tallies include only confirmed cases and the Amish communities in several counties tend to seek care only once illness is severe.

## Fragile Outbreak Data
The cyclosporiasis outbreak reached at least 47 states, with Michigan climbing from a typical ~50 annual cases to 14,277 in about seven weeks [[cyclosporiasis-outbreak-2026-us-produce]]. The FDA first tied it to Taylor Farms iceberg lettuce and a voluntary recall followed, but it then retracted the lab result as a false positive because *Cyclospora* is hard to detect, leaving the source unidentified. Measles shows a different weakness, with confirmed-only counts that understate spread [[measles-outbreak-pennsylvania-900-cases-cdc-death-toll]], and together the two outbreaks show that both the cause and the scale of an outbreak can remain uncertain long after officials act.

## HHS Initiatives Beyond Vaccines: Ticks and Clinical Trials
Through CDC and NIH, HHS awarded nearly $3 million to two Centers of Excellence in Vector-Borne Diseases to test acaricide-treated deer feed and rodent "super baits" combining acaricide and antibiotics, aiming to break the tick life cycle in wildlife hosts instead of relying only on personal prevention against more than 500,000 annual tickborne diagnoses, including Lyme disease and alpha-gal syndrome [[hhs-tick-control-funding-lyme-disease]]. Separately, ARPA-H's SURPASS program and its companion projects STACK, COMMONS and CINCH apply digital-twin trial design, real-time adaptive statistics, AI-driven site activation and shared national data infrastructure to a drug-development process that runs over a decade, costs up to $2 billion and fails more than 90% of the time [[hhs-arpa-h-surpass-clinical-trials]]. Kennedy frames both as fixing problems at the source, which shows the department's agenda extending into disease ecology and research infrastructure.

## Women's Health Research: A New NIH Division and White House Task Force
An executive order signed this week creates a White House women's health task force led by Susie Wiles and Kennedy, and a new NIH women's health division that must be established within 90 days and work alongside the Office of Research on Women's Health [[trump-executive-order-nih-womens-health-division-task-force]]. As reported, it prioritizes translating research into clinical care, asks grant recipients to consider sex as a biological variable, pushes women's inclusion in clinical trials, and replaces the Biden-era 2024 order and 2023 memorandum on women's health. The source notes that it arrives amid NIH cuts, a downsized federal health workforce and a mifepristone safety review that medical groups call politically motivated, which echoes the way professional bodies elsewhere in this topic test federal direction against their own evidence standards [[medical-groups-independent-flu-covid-rsv-vaccine-guidance]].

## Nutrition as a Policy Lever
HHS's Advancing Nutrition Education initiative asks medical schools to cover 71 nutrition topics, yet only 73 of 211 eligible M.D. and D.O. programs have signed on, and participants say they will adopt only evidence-backed content to avoid "wellness creep" from MAHA-aligned material [[medical-schools-nutrition-education-rfk-push]]. Organized medicine runs its own track: the AMA formally adopted food-is-medicine policy covering medically tailored meals, medically tailored groceries and produce prescriptions, urged HHS appropriations to fund them, and notes that physicians average only about 19 hours of nutrition training in medical school [[ama-food-is-medicine-policy-2026]]. As with vaccine guidance [[medical-groups-independent-flu-covid-rsv-vaccine-guidance]], professional bodies accept the underlying need but filter federal direction through their own evidence standards.
