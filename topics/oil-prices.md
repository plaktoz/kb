# Oil Prices

This topic follows the vault's picture of crude oil in 2026, covering how the US-Iran war and the Strait of Hormuz disruption set the price of Brent and WTI and how that price moved through inflation expectations, [[federal-reserve-monetary-policy]], [[treasury-yields]] and equity markets. Brent climbed to a four-year high in late October, then fell roughly 4.5% to 9% between November 2 and 4 on renewed US-Iran diplomacy and the midterm results, while AI-linked chip stocks sold off in Asia on November 5.

## The Hormuz Chokepoint and Physical Supply

The supply shock comes down to geography. By mid-August, tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz had fallen to about 11 ships a day from more than 100 before the war, and the IEA was forecasting a 1.8 million b/d global deficit for Q3 [[strait-of-hormuz-oil-market-analysis-aug2026]]. Houthi attacks on Saudi vessels added a second chokepoint by disrupting Bab el-Mandeb shipping, pushing Brent through $100 for the first time this cycle in late July [[4-big-tech-earnings-fed-meeting-100-oil-july-2026]], [[big-tech-earnings-fed-meeting-100-oil-busiest-week-q3-2026]]. The disruption intensified into late October, when Saudi Aramco evacuated its Dhahran headquarters [[goldman-sachs-refiners-supply-drop-houthi-oil-oct2026]], and Gulf petrostates increasingly expected Iran to keep effective control of the Strait for years, which would make the disruption a lasting regime rather than a spike [[strait-of-hormuz-oil-market-analysis-aug2026]]. Even in early November, Iran reported that two tankers had hit naval mines in the Strait while diplomacy was under way [[sp500-futures-rebound-polymarket-76-percent-apple-earnings-nov2-2026]].

## Thin Buffers and Supply Responses

The usual shock absorbers proved thin. Saudi Arabia could ship only about 200,000 b/d of a 1 million b/d output increase because of the Bab el-Mandeb disruption, leaving it storing record volumes at home, while the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell to its lowest level since 1983 [[strait-of-hormuz-oil-market-analysis-aug2026]]. Rystad Energy put the consequence plainly: fewer shipping routes, strained refineries and depleted buffers together create substantial rebound risk without a ceasefire [[big-tech-earnings-fed-meeting-100-oil-busiest-week-q3-2026]]. Coordinated releases helped where they arrived, since G7 releases of oil stocks and higher Middle East exports eased Brent to $100 on October 5 [[investors-shrug-off-bond-yield-surge-stocks-record-highs-oct5-2026]], but the IEA's agreement to speed up stockpile releases, with diesel as the priority, came when Brent was already settling above $100 [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]].

## Diplomacy and Headline-Driven Repricing

Crude has traded on the probability of escalation more than on barrels actually lost. A weekend pause in US-Iran hostilities cut Brent by about 9% to roughly $88 on July 27 [[dow-futures-rally-us-iran-pause-2026]], [[nasdaq-slips-chip-stocks-drop-oil-plunges-july-27]], and Iran's missile attack on US forces two days later lifted it about 8% to $91 [[markets-dow-closes-down-1150-points-2026-07-29]]. The pattern recurred into the fall: Brent rose above $106 after Trump reportedly rejected a conditional Iranian offer to reopen the Strait [[wall-street-dips-us-iran-standoff-sep2026]], and crude pulled back from near $110 on October 20 when reports said mediators were trying to bring Washington and Tehran together [[dow-up-523-points-russell-2000-oil-pivot-oct20-2026]], [[investopedia-markets-oct20-2026-dow-500-oil-pullback-treasury-5-percent]]. The kind of pressure mattered too, because crude fell on Bessent's sanctions campaign once traders judged economic pressure less disruptive to supply than military action [[nasdaq-sp500-close-lower-oil-drops-iran-sanctions]], [[equities-rise-tech-boost-yields-oil-fall-aug25-2026]]. Reports of Pentagon strike planning against Iranian energy targets reversed the drift, putting Brent back near $104 on October 8 [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]].

## Late October: Brent at a Four-Year High

Brent rose from under $97 at the start of October to $116.72 on October 23, its highest since June 2022, and nearly three in four S&P 500 stocks fell that day [[wall-street-oil-four-year-high-ai-economics-oct23-2026]]. Goldman Sachs' Daan Struyven attributed the climb to two kinds of buying: refiners were buying precautionarily while inventories were low, and investors bought oil as a hedge because bonds and equities had become unusually sensitive to crude [[goldman-sachs-refiners-supply-drop-houthi-oil-oct2026]]. Products tightened faster than crude, with Persian Gulf crude exports at about 110% of their 2025 average while diesel exports ran near 40%, partly because of refinery damage [[goldman-sachs-refiners-supply-drop-houthi-oil-oct2026]], and diesel above $6 was reported alongside gasoline up more than $1 [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]]. Goldman's base case still had Brent at $90 by the end of 2026 with risks skewed upward, and Brent stayed above $117 the following week [[new-york-stocks-weekly-close-ai-capex-spacex-telecom-oct24-2026]].

## Early November: Diplomacy, Midterms and a Sharp Pullback

Oil fell sharply in the first days of November on renewed diplomacy. Brent dropped about 4.5% to $108.37 on Monday, November 2 [[treasury-european-yields-fall-oil-drop-refunding-midterms-nov2-2026]], and Trump signaled the Iran conflict would end soon even as Iran reported tanker mine strikes in the Strait [[sp500-futures-rebound-polymarket-76-percent-apple-earnings-nov2-2026]]. Brent and WTI each fell about 6% that day, while a New York Times report of US-Israel strike plans after the midterms left room for volatility to return [[apple-earnings-midterms-oil-jobs-report-week-business-insider-nov2026]]. The midterm results arrived the same week: on Wednesday, November 4, the Dow jumped about 1,500 points, Brent fell 8.6% to $94.70, and defense, oil and AI names lagged, with Exxon and ConocoPhillips each down about 5% [[dow-soars-1500-points-midterms-brent-below-95-nov4-2026]]. Asian market reports on November 5 put Brent at $94.62 after an 8.7% fall, so across these sessions the declines ran from roughly 4.5% to 9% [[asian-shares-advance-after-midterms-kospi-chip-selloff-nov5-2026]].

## From Oil to Inflation, the Fed and Bond Yields

The vault traces the same chain from crude to inflation expectations, rate odds and yields. The August 31 oil surge revived inflation fears and pushed the odds of a September hike above 65% [[wall-street-aug31-close-oil-surge-monthly-gains]], and by mid-September Brent above $109 helped carry the 10-year yield over 5% for the first time since 2023 [[10-year-treasury-yield-tops-5-percent-sep2026]]. By October the 10-year had reached 5.34%, which one analysis attributed to oil-driven inflation alongside Fed hikes, a national debt above $40 trillion and AI-related borrowing [[us-treasury-yields-decades-high-oil-fed-debt-ai-oct2026]]. The pressure also showed up in prices, since a record diesel crack spread fed into a stubborn 3.7% PCE reading [[pce-holds-37-percent-real-spending-stalls-rate-hike-odds-rise]]. The chain ran in reverse in early November, when Treasury yields eased alongside oil and the 10-year fell to about 4.9% [[treasury-european-yields-fall-oil-drop-refunding-midterms-nov2-2026]], [[dow-soars-1500-points-midterms-brent-below-95-nov4-2026]].

## When Oil and Bonds Decouple

The link between oil and bonds was unusually strong for much of 2026. BlackRock's Jeffrey Rosenberg described an 80% oil-Treasury yield correlation that held through the year and broke in October, when oil rose while yields fell [[oil-treasury-correlation-breaks-ai-risk-outranks-geopolitics-blackrock-rosenberg]]. He reads the break as the market pricing AI risk above geopolitical risk, so weakness in the AI trade reads as weaker growth and disinflation, which leaves long-dated Treasuries as the only hedge, though he warns this is not a return to bonds hedging stocks [[oil-treasury-correlation-breaks-ai-risk-outranks-geopolitics-blackrock-rosenberg]]. The same mix appeared on November 2, when yields fell alongside oil and the drivers cited included AI concerns as well as lower oil [[treasury-european-yields-fall-oil-drop-refunding-midterms-nov2-2026]]. Goldman's account adds that investors bought oil itself as a hedge because bonds and equities had become unusually sensitive to crude [[goldman-sachs-refiners-supply-drop-houthi-oil-oct2026]].

## A Supply Shock Meets a Demand-Side Tool

The Fed's response is disputed because oil is a supply shock. One analysis argues that part of current inflation comes from the Persian Gulf conflict, which rate hikes cannot fix, so tightening may overshoot or underdeliver [[fed-rate-hike-bear-market-risk-historical-analysis]]. The September FOMC minutes show the split: the hike passed unanimously, but officials disagreed on whether it was meant to blunt energy shocks or to curb demand-driven inflation [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. Most officials still leaned toward another hike before year-end, citing energy costs among other pressures [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]], and in early November CME FedWatch showed a 78% chance of a 25 basis-point hike at the next meeting [[apple-earnings-midterms-oil-jobs-report-week-business-insider-nov2026]]. Persistent energy-cost pressure also undercuts Chair Warsh's thesis that AI productivity will do the disinflation work [[pce-holds-37-percent-real-spending-stalls-rate-hike-odds-rise]].

## Equity Transmission: Winners and Losers

Oil moves split the stock market instead of moving it as one block. When crude fell, energy names lagged while travel advanced [[stock-market-chip-stocks-fall-nasdaq-2026-07-27]], [[nasdaq-sp500-close-lower-oil-drops-iran-sanctions]], and the reverse showed on November 4, when Exxon and ConocoPhillips fell while First Solar gained 5.8% and HCA rose 9.6% [[dow-soars-1500-points-midterms-brent-below-95-nov4-2026]]. When crude rose, airlines fell on fuel costs and energy names outperformed [[higher-bond-yields-pull-stocks-lower-sep2026]], [[wall-street-aug31-close-oil-surge-monthly-gains]]. The October 23 session showed the split at its sharpest, as Chevron rose 2.9% and ConocoPhillips 3.6% while Micron, Intel, Microsoft and AMD cushioned an index in which most stocks fell [[wall-street-oil-four-year-high-ai-economics-oct23-2026]].

## Compounding Shocks and Longer-Horizon Views

Oil shocks hurt most when they coincide with other stresses. On July 29 the Iran attack landed alongside a hawkish Fed hold and a chip selloff, and the Dow lost about 1,150 points [[markets-dow-closes-down-1150-points-2026-07-29]], [[dow-plunges-fed-hold-iran-attack-july-29-2026]]. Goldman counts oil above $90 as one of three converging headwinds that cap forward returns [[goldman-sachs-lower-returns-2026-headwinds]], a view that sits uneasily with longer-horizon commentary treating blockade-driven drawdowns like July's 0.8% S&P 500 dip as noise [[trump-strait-of-hormuz-sp500-down]], [[trump-strait-of-hormuz-sp500-geopolitical-volatility]]. Strategists in the early-November coverage argued that earnings and interest rates, not election results, ultimately drive stock prices [[dow-soars-1500-points-midterms-brent-below-95-nov4-2026]], [[asian-shares-advance-after-midterms-kospi-chip-selloff-nov5-2026]].
