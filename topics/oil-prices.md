# Oil Prices

This topic follows the vault's picture of crude oil in 2026: how the US-Iran war and the Strait of Hormuz disruption set the price of Brent and WTI, and how that price spread into inflation, Federal Reserve policy, Treasury yields and equity markets.

## The Hormuz Chokepoint and Physical Supply

The supply shock comes down to geography. By mid-August, tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz had fallen to about 11 ships a day from more than 100 before the war, under the combined pressure of Iranian attacks and a US blockade of Iranian vessels, and the IEA was forecasting a 1.8 million b/d global deficit for Q3 [[strait-of-hormuz-oil-market-analysis-aug2026]]. A second chokepoint compounded the first: Houthi attacks on Saudi vessels disrupted Bab el-Mandeb shipping and helped push Brent through $100 for the first time this cycle in late July [[4-big-tech-earnings-fed-meeting-100-oil-july-2026]], [[big-tech-earnings-fed-meeting-100-oil-busiest-week-q3-2026]]. Renewed Houthi attacks on Saudi airports, Yemen's offensive against Houthi-held areas, and a burning tanker in the Gulf of Oman were still feeding supply fears in October [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]], [[stocks-fall-bond-yields-oil-fed-minutes-halt-record-rally-oct7-2026]], [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]]. Gulf petrostates increasingly expect Iran to keep effective control of the Strait for years, which would make the disruption a lasting regime rather than a temporary spike [[strait-of-hormuz-oil-market-analysis-aug2026]].

## Thin Buffers and Supply Responses

The usual shock absorbers proved weak. Saudi Arabia could ship only about 200,000 b/d of a 1 million b/d output increase because of the Bab el-Mandeb disruption, which left it storing record volumes at home, and the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell to its lowest level since 1983 [[strait-of-hormuz-oil-market-analysis-aug2026]]. Rystad Energy summarized the fragility: fewer shipping routes, strained refineries and depleted buffers together create substantial rebound risk without a ceasefire [[big-tech-earnings-fed-meeting-100-oil-busiest-week-q3-2026]]. Coordinated releases did move prices when they arrived. Brent slipped to $100 on October 5 after G7 stock releases and higher Middle East exports [[investors-shrug-off-bond-yield-surge-stocks-record-highs-oct5-2026]], and the IEA agreed to speed up stockpile releases with diesel as the priority [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]].

## Diplomacy and Headline-Driven Repricing

Crude has traded on the probability of escalation more than on barrels actually lost. A weekend pause in US-Iran hostilities cut Brent by about 9% to roughly $88 on July 27 [[dow-futures-rally-us-iran-pause-2026]], [[nasdaq-slips-chip-stocks-drop-dow-rises-oil-plunge]], [[nasdaq-slips-chip-stocks-drop-oil-plunges-july-27]], [[stock-market-chip-stocks-fall-nasdaq-2026-07-27]], [[stock-market-today-chip-stocks-fall-nasdaq-july-27]]. Two days later, Iran's missile attack on US forces sent Brent up about 8% to $91 [[markets-dow-closes-down-1150-points-2026-07-29]], [[dow-plunges-fed-hold-iran-attack-july-29-2026]]. The same pattern recurred through the fall. Bessent's hint that a Hormuz deal could come "today or tomorrow" pushed WTI down 5.8% [[dow-record-close-iran-talks-wall-street-rally-aug2026]], a stalled standoff kept Brent near $89 ahead of July CPI [[us-iran-impasse-alphabet-stock-sinks-aug2026]], Iran's week-long de-escalation proposal took WTI down 2.3% [[sp500-dow-nasdaq-week-higher-chipmaker-oil-sep26-2026]], and Trump's rejection of a conditional offer to reopen the Strait lifted Brent above $106 [[wall-street-dips-us-iran-standoff-sep2026]]. The kind of pressure also matters: crude fell on Bessent's "Operation Economic Outcast" sanctions campaign because traders judged economic pressure less disruptive to supply than military action [[stocks-slip-bessent-iran-sanctions-campaign]], [[nasdaq-sp500-close-lower-oil-drops-iran-sanctions]], [[equities-rise-tech-boost-yields-oil-fall-aug25-2026]]. Reports of Pentagon strike planning against Iranian energy targets, by contrast, put Brent back near $104 on October 8 [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]].

## From Oil to Inflation, the Fed and Bond Yields

The vault keeps tracing the same chain: crude, then inflation expectations, then rate-hike odds, then yields. The August 31 oil surge revived inflation fears and pushed September hike odds above 65% [[wall-street-aug31-close-oil-surge-monthly-gains]]. On September 15, Brent above $109 helped carry the 10-year yield over 5% for the first time since 2023, and Goldman moved to forecast a hike [[10-year-treasury-yield-tops-5-percent-sep2026]]. By October, oil above $100, diesel above $6 and gasoline up more than $1 were listed among the structural forces keeping global yields high [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]]. Brent above $101 then joined 24-year-high yields in ending a record equity rally [[stocks-fall-bond-yields-oil-fed-minutes-halt-record-rally-oct7-2026]]. The pressure shows up in prices as well as forecasts. A record diesel crack spread fed into a stubborn 3.7% PCE reading [[pce-holds-37-percent-real-spending-stalls-rate-hike-odds-rise]], consumer confidence fell on gas prices [[dow-selloff-fed-hold-iran-attack-2026-07-29]], and Singapore's central bank tightened preemptively against oil-driven inflation [[dow-futures-rally-us-iran-pause-2026]], [[market-rally-us-iran-pause-earnings-week-2026]].

## A Supply Shock Meets a Demand-Side Tool

The Fed's response is disputed because oil is a supply shock. One analysis argues that part of current inflation comes from the Persian Gulf conflict, which rate hikes cannot fix, so tightening may overshoot or underdeliver [[fed-rate-hike-bear-market-risk-historical-analysis]]. The September FOMC minutes show the same split: officials approved the hike unanimously but disagreed on whether it was meant to blunt energy shocks or curb demand-driven inflation [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. Most still leaned toward another hike, citing energy costs among other pressures [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]]. Persistent energy-cost pressure is also undercutting Chair Warsh's thesis that AI productivity will do the disinflation work [[pce-holds-37-percent-real-spending-stalls-rate-hike-odds-rise]].

## Equity Transmission: Winners, Losers and Compounding Shocks

Oil moves split the stock market instead of moving it as one block. When crude fell, energy stocks lagged or led declines while travel names advanced [[stock-market-chip-stocks-fall-nasdaq-2026-07-27]], [[nasdaq-sp500-close-lower-oil-drops-iran-sanctions]], [[dow-record-close-iran-talks-wall-street-rally-aug2026]]. When it rose, airlines fell on fuel costs and energy services outperformed [[higher-bond-yields-pull-stocks-lower-sep2026]], [[wall-street-aug31-close-oil-surge-monthly-gains]]. Oil shocks hurt most when they coincide with other stresses. On July 29 the Iran attack landed alongside a hawkish Fed hold and an AI-chip selloff, and the Dow lost about 1,150 points [[markets-dow-closes-down-1150-points-2026-07-29]], [[dow-jones-oil-iran-tensions-earnings-july-2026]]. Goldman counts oil above $90 as one of three converging headwinds that cap forward returns [[goldman-sachs-lower-returns-2026-headwinds]]. Long-horizon commentary, though, treats blockade-driven drawdowns like July's 0.8% S&P dip as noise against historical recoveries [[trump-strait-of-hormuz-sp500-down]], [[trump-strait-of-hormuz-sp500-geopolitical-volatility]].

**T2. A concentrated chokepoint with thin buffers turns local disruption into a global supply shock** — When most flows pass through one or two routes and alternative routes, inventories and reserves are already constrained, a disruption cannot be routed around, so prices stay high until buffers are rebuilt or released. · *model:* [[Single Point of Failure]]

**T3. Oil shocks reach asset prices through inflation expectations, rate odds and yields** — A crude spike raises expected inflation, which raises the odds of policy tightening and lifts bond yields, which in turn compresses equity valuations, so oil acts on stocks mainly through the rate channel. · *model:* [[Second-Order Effects]]

**T4. Demand-side tools only partly address supply-side inflation** — Rate hikes curb demand but cannot restore lost barrels, so when part of inflation comes from an energy supply shock, the tightening's purpose and effectiveness stay contested. · *model:* [[Root Cause Analysis]]

**T5. An oil price move transfers value between producers and consumers of fuel** — Higher crude lifts energy producers and services while hurting fuel-intensive sectors such as airlines and travel, and lower crude reverses the transfer, so oil shocks shift sector leadership more than they move the whole market.
