# Oil Prices

This topic follows the vault's picture of crude oil in 2026, from the US-Iran war and the Strait of Hormuz disruption through to how the price has moved inflation expectations, [[federal-reserve-monetary-policy]], [[treasury-yields]] and equity markets. The latest entries show Brent settling at $92.57 on November 9, its lowest settlement since August 31, after a late-October climb to a four-year high [[uso-exxon-chevron-oil-lowest-since-august-iran-nov9-2026]], [[wall-street-oil-four-year-high-ai-economics-oct23-2026]].

## The Hormuz Chokepoint and Physical Supply

By mid-August, tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz had fallen to about 11 ships a day from more than 100 before the war, and the IEA was forecasting a 1.8 million b/d global deficit for Q3 [[strait-of-hormuz-oil-market-analysis-aug2026]]. Houthi attacks on Saudi vessels added a second chokepoint by disrupting Bab el-Mandeb shipping, which pushed Brent through $100 for the first time this cycle in late July [[4-big-tech-earnings-fed-meeting-100-oil-july-2026]], [[big-tech-earnings-fed-meeting-100-oil-busiest-week-q3-2026]]. The disruption reached into late October, when Saudi Aramco evacuated its Dhahran headquarters, and Gulf petrostates increasingly expected Iran to keep effective control of the Strait for years [[goldman-sachs-refiners-supply-drop-houthi-oil-oct2026]], [[strait-of-hormuz-oil-market-analysis-aug2026]]. Even in early November, Iran reported that two tankers had hit naval mines in the Strait while talks were under way [[sp500-futures-rebound-polymarket-76-percent-apple-earnings-nov2-2026]].

## Thin Buffers and Supply Responses

The usual shock absorbers proved thin. Saudi Arabia could move only about 200,000 b/d of a 1 million b/d output increase past the Bab el-Mandeb disruption, and the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve had fallen to its lowest level since 1983 by mid-August [[strait-of-hormuz-oil-market-analysis-aug2026]]. Rystad Energy warned that fewer shipping routes, strained refineries and depleted buffers together made a substantial rebound in prices likely without a ceasefire [[big-tech-earnings-fed-meeting-100-oil-busiest-week-q3-2026]]. Coordinated releases helped where they arrived, since G7 stock releases and higher Middle East exports eased Brent to $100 on October 5 [[investors-shrug-off-bond-yield-surge-stocks-record-highs-oct5-2026]], but the IEA's agreement to speed up stockpile releases, with diesel first, came when Brent was already settling above $100 [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]].

## Diplomacy and Headline-Driven Repricing

Crude has traded on the odds of escalation more than on barrels actually lost. A weekend pause in US-Iran hostilities cut Brent about 9% to roughly $88 on July 27 [[dow-futures-rally-us-iran-pause-2026]], [[nasdaq-slips-chip-stocks-drop-oil-plunges-july-27]], and Iran's missile attack on US forces two days later lifted it about 8% to $91 [[markets-dow-closes-down-1150-points-2026-07-29]]. The pattern recurred through the fall, as Brent rose above $106 after Trump reportedly rejected a conditional Iranian offer [[wall-street-dips-us-iran-standoff-sep2026]], and crude pulled back from near $110 on October 20 when reports said mediators were trying to bring Washington and Tehran together [[dow-up-523-points-russell-2000-oil-pivot-oct20-2026]], [[investopedia-markets-oct20-2026-dow-500-oil-pullback-treasury-5-percent]]. Sanctions followed a similar logic, with crude falling roughly 2% to 3% in late August once traders judged economic pressure less disruptive to supply than military action [[nasdaq-sp500-close-lower-oil-drops-iran-sanctions]], [[equities-rise-tech-boost-yields-oil-fall-aug25-2026]].

## Late October: Brent at a Four-Year High

After Pentagon strike reports pushed Brent back near $104 on October 8 [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]], it climbed from under $97 at the start of October to $116.72 on October 23, its highest since June 2022, on a day when nearly three in four S&P 500 stocks fell [[wall-street-oil-four-year-high-ai-economics-oct23-2026]]. Goldman Sachs' Daan Struyven attributed the rise to two kinds of buying: refiners were buying precautionarily while inventories were low, and investors bought oil as a hedge because bonds and equities had become unusually sensitive to crude [[goldman-sachs-refiners-supply-drop-houthi-oil-oct2026]]. Products tightened faster than crude, with Persian Gulf crude exports near 110% of their 2025 average while diesel exports ran near 40%, and diesel above $6 was reported alongside gasoline up more than $1 [[goldman-sachs-refiners-supply-drop-houthi-oil-oct2026]], [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]]. Goldman's base case still had Brent at $90 by the end of 2026, with risks skewed upward [[goldman-sachs-refiners-supply-drop-houthi-oil-oct2026]], while a weekly-close report kept Brent above $117 on Middle East tensions [[new-york-stocks-weekly-close-ai-capex-spacex-telecom-oct24-2026]].

## Early November: Diplomacy, Midterms and a Sharp Pullback

Oil fell sharply in the first days of November on renewed diplomacy, as Brent dropped about 4.5% to $108.37 on Monday, November 2, while Trump signaled the Iran conflict would end soon even as Iran reported tanker mine strikes in the Strait [[treasury-european-yields-fall-oil-drop-refunding-midterms-nov2-2026]], [[sp500-futures-rebound-polymarket-76-percent-apple-earnings-nov2-2026]]. A New York Times report of US-Israel strike plans after the midterms left room for volatility to return, and by one account Brent and WTI each fell about 6% that session [[apple-earnings-midterms-oil-jobs-report-week-business-insider-nov2026]]. The midterm results arrived the same week: on November 4 the Dow jumped about 1,500 points while Brent fell 8.6% to $94.70, Exxon and ConocoPhillips dropped about 5%, and the 10-year yield eased to 4.89% [[dow-soars-1500-points-midterms-brent-below-95-nov4-2026]]. Asian market reports on November 5 quoted Brent at $94.62 after an 8.7% fall [[asian-shares-advance-after-midterms-kospi-chip-selloff-nov5-2026]].

## Physical Balance and the November 9 Pullback

The November 9 drop came while physical indicators pointed to a surplus: JPMorgan estimated a 1.4 million bpd October surplus as high prices cut demand, OPEC output rose 1.38 million bpd to 22.87 million, and Dated Brent had slipped to a discount to nearby futures [[oil-falls-trump-restraint-iran-brent-93-nov9-2026]], [[uso-exxon-chevron-oil-lowest-since-august-iran-nov9-2026]]. Brent settled about 5.8% lower at $92.57 that day, even though Trump had said new strikes were unlikely while talks progressed and ships were still being hit near the Strait [[oil-falls-trump-restraint-iran-brent-93-nov9-2026]], [[us-stocks-rally-recover-losses-oil-falls-nvidia-sinks-ap-nov9-2026]]. The S&P 500 rose 2.3% in the same session, its best day since April, recovering most of the prior week's loss as oil eased [[us-stocks-rally-recover-losses-oil-falls-nvidia-sinks-ap-nov9-2026]]. One analysis read the move as supply-led rather than diplomacy-led, noting that Trump's "effectively over" remark came after the settlement and that no ceasefire had been signed [[uso-exxon-chevron-oil-lowest-since-august-iran-nov9-2026]].

## From Oil to Inflation, the Fed and Bond Yields

The August 31 oil surge reignited inflation fears, and hike odds stood above 65% after Chair Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole address [[wall-street-aug31-close-oil-surge-monthly-gains]]. By mid-September Brent above $109 helped carry the 10-year yield above 5% for the first time since October 2023 [[10-year-treasury-yield-tops-5-percent-sep2026]]. By October the 10-year had reached 5.34%, which one analysis attributed to oil-driven inflation alongside Fed hikes, a national debt above $40 trillion and AI-related borrowing [[us-treasury-yields-decades-high-oil-fed-debt-ai-oct2026]]. Energy costs also reached consumer prices, with PCE at 3.7% in July and the diesel crack spread near a record $102 a barrel in August [[pce-holds-37-percent-real-spending-stalls-rate-hike-odds-rise]].

## When Oil and Bonds Decouple

BlackRock's Jeffrey Rosenberg described an 80% oil-Treasury yield correlation that held through 2026 until October, when oil rose while yields fell [[oil-treasury-correlation-breaks-ai-risk-outranks-geopolitics-blackrock-rosenberg]]. He reads the break as the market pricing AI risk above geopolitical risk, so weakness in the AI trade reads as weaker growth and disinflation, which leaves long-dated Treasuries as the only hedge [[oil-treasury-correlation-breaks-ai-risk-outranks-geopolitics-blackrock-rosenberg]]. He warns this is not a return to bonds hedging stocks, since persistent inflation and oil keep that relationship from normalising [[oil-treasury-correlation-breaks-ai-risk-outranks-geopolitics-blackrock-rosenberg]]. On November 2 yields fell alongside oil, with AI concerns among the drivers [[treasury-european-yields-fall-oil-drop-refunding-midterms-nov2-2026]], and Goldman's account adds that investors bought oil itself as a hedge [[goldman-sachs-refiners-supply-drop-houthi-oil-oct2026]].

## A Supply Shock Meets a Demand-Side Tool

The Fed's response is disputed because oil is a supply shock, and one analysis argues rate hikes cannot fix the part of inflation coming from the Persian Gulf conflict, so tightening may overshoot or underdeliver [[fed-rate-hike-bear-market-risk-historical-analysis]]. The September FOMC minutes show the split: the hike passed unanimously, but officials disagreed on whether it was meant to blunt energy shocks or to curb demand-driven inflation [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. Most officials still leaned toward another hike before year-end, citing energy costs among other pressures [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]]. In early November, CME FedWatch showed a 78% chance of a 25 basis-point hike at the next meeting [[apple-earnings-midterms-oil-jobs-report-week-business-insider-nov2026]].

## Equity Transmission: Winners and Losers

When crude fell, energy names lagged while travel stocks advanced [[stock-market-chip-stocks-fall-nasdaq-2026-07-27]], [[nasdaq-sp500-close-lower-oil-drops-iran-sanctions]]. The reverse showed on November 4, when Exxon and ConocoPhillips fell while First Solar gained 5.8% and HCA rose 9.6% [[dow-soars-1500-points-midterms-brent-below-95-nov4-2026]]. When crude rose, airlines fell on fuel-cost worries and energy names outperformed [[higher-bond-yields-pull-stocks-lower-sep2026]], [[wall-street-aug31-close-oil-surge-monthly-gains]]. The October 23 session showed the split at its sharpest, with Chevron up 2.9% and ConocoPhillips up 3.6% while Micron, Intel, Microsoft and AMD cushioned an index in which most stocks fell [[wall-street-oil-four-year-high-ai-economics-oct23-2026]].

## Compounding Shocks and Longer-Horizon Views

Oil moves hit hardest when they coincide with other stress. On July 29 the Iran attack landed alongside a hawkish Fed hold and a chip selloff, and the Dow lost about 1,150 points [[markets-dow-closes-down-1150-points-2026-07-29]], [[dow-plunges-fed-hold-iran-attack-july-29-2026]]. Goldman counts oil above $90 as one of three converging headwinds that cap forward returns [[goldman-sachs-lower-returns-2026-headwinds]], a view that sits uneasily with commentary treating the roughly 0.8% S&P 500 dip from the July blockade as background noise [[trump-strait-of-hormuz-sp500-down]], [[trump-strait-of-hormuz-sp500-geopolitical-volatility]]. Strategists in the early-November coverage argued that earnings and interest rates, not election results, ultimately drive stock prices [[dow-soars-1500-points-midterms-brent-below-95-nov4-2026]], [[asian-shares-advance-after-midterms-kospi-chip-selloff-nov5-2026]].
