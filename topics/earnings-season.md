# Earnings Season

This topic tracks how quarterly reporting seasons work as market events in 2026: who reports first and why it matters, how expectations are set and revised, why stocks often react to guidance rather than results, and how aggregate earnings growth competes with rising bond yields to move the index. General S&P 500 structure and breadth live in [[sp500]].

## How a Season Opens
Each season starts with a short warm-up of early reporters before the big banks begin the main run. Q3 2026 opened the week of October 5 with Delta, PepsiCo and Levi Strauss ([[week-ahead-q3-earnings-season-delta-pepsi-fed-minutes-oct2026]]), and Investing.com read the Pepsi and Levi reports mainly as a check on the U.S. consumer, given Pepsi's back-and-forth on chip pricing and Levi's bet on premium shoppers ([[five-things-week-ahead-bond-yields-french-debt-fomc-minutes-oct2026]]). JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and Wells Fargo then report on October 13 and Bank of America, Morgan Stanley and BlackRock a day later ([[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]]), in the same week as CPI, PPI, retail sales and the ASML and TSMC chip reports, so one week tests both corporate profits and the rate outlook ([[wall-st-week-ahead-bank-earnings-cpi-sp500-record-oct9-2026]]). Cramer's framing is that earnings season ends the guesswork of trading on scattered data points and makes the market price actual results ([[cramer-week-ahead-bank-chip-earnings-oct2026]]).

## Banks as the First Read
Bank results are treated as a reading on the whole economy (consumer spending, credit quality and capital-markets activity) and not just on the banks themselves. Going into Q2, analysts expected profit growth at all six big banks on the back of card spending up 6.3% year over year, benign credit, AI deal flow and a roughly $500M SpaceX IPO windfall, yet they warned the quarter would not reset expectations higher, and Jamie Dimon himself said JPMorgan was "overearning" ([[big-bank-earnings-q2-2026-consumer-spending]]). By the Q3 preview the swing factor had narrowed to capital markets: trading is the upside risk and investment-banking fees the downside, Oppenheimer cut its IB revenue view 9.5% and expected only JPMorgan to beat, and Goldman, as the purest capital-markets name, is most exposed to an advisory miss ([[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]]). Cramer took the other side on Goldman while calling JPMorgan priced for near-perfect execution, which shows that the same consensus numbers can support opposite stock calls ([[cramer-week-ahead-bank-chip-earnings-oct2026]]).

## Setting the Bar: Consensus and Revisions
In 2026 the bar sat unusually high. During Q2, FactSet and Bloomberg put aggregate S&P 500 EPS growth at 22.5% to 25%, the strongest since 2021 ([[economies-investors-earnings-fed-signals-week-ahead-aug2026]]), and Wolfe Research found 64% of companies that gave guidance putting it above consensus, the highest share since COVID ([[wolfe-research-sp500-earnings-momentum-aug2026]]), a pace of growth seen outside a recession recovery only in 1994 and 1999 ([[sp500-earnings-growth-pace-1994-1999-comparison]]). For Q3, analysts raised estimates 1.3% during the quarter instead of the usual cuts and now expect 29.1% growth, a third straight quarter above 25%, which Wolfe says also raises the bar the results have to clear ([[week-ahead-q3-earnings-season-delta-pepsi-fed-minutes-oct2026]]).

## Beats That Sell Off
When expectations are already this high, beating consensus is not enough. In Q2 about 90% of early reporters beat, but tech stocks' post-earnings moves lagged the historical average by 2.6 points, and Alphabet and Tesla fell despite strong results ([[stock-market-july-28-2026-dow-sp500-nasdaq-earnings]]). AMD and SpaceX dropped 8–10% on beats because investors had traded on higher unofficial "whisper numbers" ([[stock-market-record-highs-spacex-amd-weakness-aug2026]]), and Barclays found that both beats and misses produced negative average price reactions for the first time since Q4 2024, reading this as investors demanding a "clean beat plus stronger guidance" ([[sp500-rally-fundamentals-diverge-aug2026]]). The same pattern appeared outside tech: Walmart fell 9% after beating Q2 because its Q3 guidance came in below estimates ([[markets-aug20-2026-walmart-bitcoin-oil-earnings]]), and Samsung's shares slipped after a 700%-plus profit jump on signs that growth was cooling ([[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]).

## AI Spending as the Real Test
In 2026 earnings seasons have mostly served as a vote on the AI trade. The July mega-cap week asked Meta, Microsoft and Amazon to justify AI capex while rising rates made those long-dated returns harder to defend ([[week-ahead-watchlist-big-tech-earnings-fed-pce-july-2026]]), and Schwab's August outlook asked whether strong Microsoft and Amazon results had really calmed concerns about AI economics ([[us-stock-market-outlook-august-2026]]). IBD treated Sandisk, Western Digital and AMD as a separate read on AI hardware demand, apart from the hyperscaler-capex story ([[dow-futures-trump-calls-off-iran-attacks-aug2026]]). By October the stakes were higher still: debt-financed "circular" chip deals such as Broadcom–OpenAI and SpaceX–Nvidia make earnings updates the main evidence on whether AI demand will last ([[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]), and Cramer called the TSMC report the week's biggest potential catalyst for chip stocks ([[cramer-week-ahead-bank-chip-earnings-oct2026]]).

## Earnings Versus Yields at the Index Level
Over 2026, earnings growth has been the main force holding the index up against rising rates. In August the S&P 500 set records while its forward P/E fell to 19.6, below the 19.9 five-year average, because earnings were growing faster than prices ([[stock-market-record-highs-spacex-amd-weakness-aug2026]]), and in October it climbed a [[Wall of Worry]] to new highs as the 10-year yield hit 5.31%, the highest since 2002, which Edward Jones credited to robust earnings and narrow credit spreads ([[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]], [[daily-market-snapshot-record-highs-10yr-yield-oct2026]]). The cushion has limits: the October 6 record, driven partly by strong earnings estimates, gave way the next day as yields above 5.35% made Treasuries a tougher alternative to stocks ([[stocks-fall-bond-yields-oil-fed-minutes-halt-record-rally-oct7-2026]]), and Reuters quoted a warning that indices will eventually "cry uncle" on higher rates ([[wall-st-week-ahead-bank-earnings-cpi-sp500-record-oct9-2026]]). The 1994 precedent, when EPS rose 39.8% while Fed hikes left the index slightly down, shows that strong earnings alone do not guarantee gains ([[sp500-earnings-growth-pace-1994-1999-comparison]]).

## Earnings Quality: Concentration and One-Offs
The headline growth rates overstate how broad the earnings strength is. Wolfe trims 2026 EPS growth from the 31% consensus to about 27% once large one-time gains at mega-cap tech companies are removed ([[wolfe-research-sp500-earnings-momentum-aug2026]]), and Amazon's and Alphabet's GAAP earnings were lifted by gains on their Anthropic and SpaceX stakes, while Nvidia and Micron posted outlier growth of 95% and 1,000%-plus ([[sp500-earnings-growth-pace-1994-1999-comparison]]). Sector estimates spread even wider, from energy up 121% to healthcare down 9% ([[economies-investors-earnings-fed-signals-week-ahead-aug2026]]), and bank profits leaned on a one-time SpaceX IPO windfall alongside Dimon's own "overearning" admission ([[big-bank-earnings-q2-2026-consumer-spending]]). Capital Economics warned that if AI-linked earnings and capex assumptions are revised down, the result could be a broader correction ([[economies-investors-earnings-fed-signals-week-ahead-aug2026]]).

**T3. Above-trend profits built on cyclical peaks and one-time gains tend to revert** — Earnings inflated by windfalls, stake gains or peak conditions overstate durable earning power, so headline growth should be discounted toward what is repeatable. · *model:* [[Mean Reversion]]

**T4. An aggregate is dominated by its largest and most extreme components** — Index-level earnings growth is pulled by a few mega-caps and outlier sectors, so the headline figure can say little about the typical company. *(implied)*

**T5. Companies upstream of many others carry information about them, so their reports are read as signals for the wider economy** — Banks, consumer staples and chip suppliers sit on flows (credit, spending, capex) that many other businesses depend on, so early or upstream reporters set the tone for the rest of the season.
