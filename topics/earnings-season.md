# Earnings Season

This topic tracks how quarterly reporting seasons work as market events in 2026: who reports first and why it matters, how expectations are set and revised, why stocks so often react to guidance, cash flow and capex rather than to the headline beat, and how aggregate earnings growth competes with rising bond yields to move the index. The Q3 2026 season reached its heaviest stretch in the last week of October, when Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon and Intel reported. Alphabet's result produced the largest one-day loss in its public history, and the selling then spread through the Nasdaq-100 and the semiconductor group. General S&P 500 structure and breadth live in [[sp500]].

## How a Season Opens
Each season starts with a short warm-up of early reporters before the big banks begin the main run. Q3 2026 opened the week of October 5 with Delta, PepsiCo and Levi Strauss ([[week-ahead-q3-earnings-season-delta-pepsi-fed-minutes-oct2026]]), and Investing.com read the Pepsi and Levi reports mainly as a check on the U.S. consumer, given Pepsi's back-and-forth on chip pricing and Levi's bet on premium shoppers ([[five-things-week-ahead-bond-yields-french-debt-fomc-minutes-oct2026]]). JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and Wells Fargo then reported on October 13 and Bank of America, Morgan Stanley and BlackRock a day later ([[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]]), in the same week as CPI, PPI, retail sales and the ASML and TSMC chip reports, so one week tested both corporate profits and the rate outlook ([[wall-st-week-ahead-bank-earnings-cpi-sp500-record-oct9-2026]]). Cramer's framing is that earnings season ends the guesswork of trading on scattered data points and makes the market price actual results ([[cramer-week-ahead-bank-chip-earnings-oct2026]]). The October 13 slate, led by Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and JPMorgan, also included Johnson & Johnson, UnitedHealth and Kinder Morgan, with CPI on Wednesday and PPI on Thursday ([[dow-sp500-nasdaq-outlook-earnings-cpi-middle-east-oct2026]], [[dow-futures-bank-earnings-houthi-russian-diesel-spacex-oct12-2026]]), and Capital.com's Kyle Rodda framed the banks as the first test of corporate fundamentals before the Magnificent Seven report ([[dow-futures-bank-earnings-houthi-russian-diesel-spacex-oct12-2026]]). The week of October 18 accelerated the season and opened with the consumer-facing and tech names: SpaceX on Tuesday, Tesla and Meta on Wednesday, then Netflix, IBM, GE Aerospace and the airlines, with Meta billed as the opening act for the hyperscalers ([[earnings-week-oct-2026-spcx-tsla-meta-nflx-ibm-preview]]). Raymond James counted 172 companies, 37% of market cap, reporting in the week after that ([[raymond-james-key-catalysts-fed-earnings-refunding-midterms-oct2026]]).

## Banks as the First Read
Bank results are treated as a reading on the whole economy (consumer spending, credit quality and capital-markets activity) and not just on the banks themselves. Going into Q2, analysts expected profit growth at all six big banks on the back of card spending up 6.3% year over year, benign credit, AI deal flow and a roughly $500M SpaceX IPO windfall, yet they warned the quarter would not reset expectations higher, and Jamie Dimon himself said JPMorgan was "overearning" ([[big-bank-earnings-q2-2026-consumer-spending]]). By the Q3 preview the swing factor had narrowed to capital markets: trading is the upside risk and investment-banking fees the downside, Oppenheimer cut its IB revenue view 9.5% and expected only JPMorgan to beat, and Goldman, as the purest capital-markets name, is most exposed to an advisory miss ([[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]]). Cramer took the other side on Goldman while calling JPMorgan priced for near-perfect execution, which shows that the same consensus numbers can support opposite stock calls ([[cramer-week-ahead-bank-chip-earnings-oct2026]]).

## Setting the Bar: Consensus and Revisions
In 2026 the bar sat unusually high. During Q2, FactSet and Bloomberg put aggregate S&P 500 EPS growth at 22.5% to 25%, the strongest since 2021 ([[economies-investors-earnings-fed-signals-week-ahead-aug2026]]), and Wolfe Research found 64% of companies that gave guidance putting it above consensus, the highest share since COVID ([[wolfe-research-sp500-earnings-momentum-aug2026]]), a pace of growth seen outside a recession recovery only in 1994 and 1999 ([[sp500-earnings-growth-pace-1994-1999-comparison]]). For Q3, analysts raised estimates 1.3% during the quarter instead of the usual cuts and expected 29.1% growth, a third straight quarter above 25%, which Wolfe says also raises the bar the results have to clear ([[week-ahead-q3-earnings-season-delta-pepsi-fed-minutes-oct2026]]). By the week the banks reported, FactSet's estimate stood at 29.5%, and because actual growth has historically beaten estimates by roughly 7 points, the same data was read as leaving room for 40% or more ([[dow-sp500-nasdaq-outlook-earnings-cpi-middle-east-oct2026]]), while other coverage cited analysts at about 30% growth that remained highly concentrated ([[dow-futures-bank-earnings-houthi-russian-diesel-spacex-oct12-2026]]). Reuters, via Investing.com, put the Q3 expectation above 30% per LSEG ([[ai-rally-wobbles-earnings-meta-hyperscaler-capex-oct2026]]). Results so far have cleared the bar on paper: CNBC reported that 85% of reporting S&P 500 companies had beaten expectations by October 19 ([[cnbc-morning-squawk-ai-dip-buying-oct19-2026]]), and Raymond James put the beat rate at 82% against a ten-year average of 77%, with EPS up about 29% year over year and a fifth straight quarter of double-digit growth, the longest streak since 2018 ([[raymond-james-key-catalysts-fed-earnings-refunding-midterms-oct2026]]). The forward calendar is where analysts are less sure: LSEG data show Q2 2027 EPS growth of only 3.6%, the weakest since Q2 2023, with Anthropic mark-ups at Alphabet and Amazon distorting the year-over-year comparisons for the first half of 2027 ([[sp500-eps-growth-q2-2027-dip-fomc-pce-hyperscalers-oct2026]]).

By the final week of October the scale of the reporting was clear. About 179 S&P 500 companies, roughly 36% of the index, were due to report, and Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon alone are worth close to $11 trillion, so their results were expected to set the market's direction ([[sp500-third-of-companies-report-earnings-week-confidence-cracks-oct2026]]). The median company was expected to grow only about 10% against 29.5% for the weighted index, which led Jonathan Golub to call the rest of the market "pretty average" once tech and energy were set aside ([[sp500-third-of-companies-report-earnings-week-confidence-cracks-oct2026]]). The revenue beat rate was also narrowing, to 1.7% from 3.8% in Q2, even as earnings beat by 6.5% and the forward profit margin sat at a record 16.8% ([[wall-street-looks-past-fed-pause-tech-earnings-ai-rebound-cnbc-oct28-2026]]). With much of that strength already priced in, Golub argued that a narrow beat could be read as a miss ([[sp500-third-of-companies-report-earnings-week-confidence-cracks-oct2026]]). Amazon had guided third-quarter sales to $197B–$202B, up 9% to 12%, and operating income to $22.5B–$26.5B ([[week-ahead-oct26-30-fomc-alphabet-microsoft-amazon-earnings-tradingkey]]). TheStreet called the oil earnings potentially astonishing, with Exxon Mobil expected to report EPS of $4.03, up 114%, on revenue of $106B, and Chevron's EPS expected to rise from $1.85 to $4.86, with crude up nearly 80% this year ([[thestreet-fed-tech-oil-earnings-exciting-week-oct26-2026]]).

## Beats That Sell Off
When expectations are already this high, beating consensus is not enough. In Q2 about 90% of early reporters beat, but tech stocks' post-earnings moves lagged the historical average by 2.6 points, and Alphabet and Tesla fell despite strong results ([[stock-market-july-28-2026-dow-sp500-nasdaq-earnings]]). AMD and SpaceX dropped 8–10% on beats because investors had traded on higher unofficial "whisper numbers" ([[stock-market-record-highs-spacex-amd-weakness-aug2026]]), and Barclays found that both beats and misses produced negative average price reactions for the first time since Q4 2024, reading this as investors demanding a "clean beat plus stronger guidance" ([[sp500-rally-fundamentals-diverge-aug2026]]). The same pattern appeared outside tech: Walmart fell 9% after beating Q2 because its Q3 guidance came in below estimates ([[markets-aug20-2026-walmart-bitcoin-oil-earnings]]), and Samsung's shares slipped after a 700%-plus profit jump on signs that growth was cooling ([[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]). The Q3 run repeated the pattern in the week of October 19, but with a clear split between guidance-driven winners and losers. 3M rose about 6% on a beat-and-raise, with options volume triple the average ([[dow-up-523-points-russell-2000-oil-pivot-oct20-2026]]), whereas General Motors fell roughly 7% despite topping estimates and announcing a $20B buyback, on worries about pricing and costs, and Steel Dynamics fell 5% on a profit miss ([[investopedia-markets-oct20-2026-dow-500-oil-pullback-treasury-5-percent]]).

The late-October mega-cap reports followed the same logic. Alphabet's revenue of about $127.6B, up 25%, fell short of expectations, most visibly in advertising, and one account put its EPS at $2.12 against roughly $3.01 expected ([[google-stock-falls-again-after-worst-day-q3-miss-cloud-103-percent-yahoo-oct30-2026]], [[alphabet-500bn-loss-ai-mode-hits-ad-sales-amazon-microsoft-capex-bbc]]). The stock lost roughly 12–13% on October 29, its worst day since the 2004 listing, and slipped another 1% the next session ([[alphabet-711-billion-wipeout-worst-day-record-ai-search-anxiety-oct2026]], [[google-stock-falls-again-after-worst-day-q3-miss-cloud-103-percent-yahoo-oct30-2026]]). Strong Cloud growth did not offset the miss, because the market was weighing the advertising shortfall and the cash burn together ([[alphabet-googl-analyst-consensus-strong-buy-45-percent-upside-oct31-2026]]).

IBM is the cleanest single-stock case. Going in, the stock was stalled near $240 and down 19% for the year after a record 25.2% one-day drop on July 14, options priced a 4.3% move against a 6.3% average, and it had closed higher the next session in only five of its last twelve reports ([[ibm-stock-struggling-at-240-ahead-of-q3-earnings-options-pricing-oct2026]]); a Motley Fool preview argued the decision hinged on whether software growth could recover from 5% in Q2, all of it acquired ([[ibm-q3-preview-software-growth-29-percent-below-high]]). The October 21 report was solid, with revenue up 5% to $17.1B and adjusted EPS of $2.95 against $2.87 expected, and the stock still did not rally. Coverage disagrees on the tape, with one report saying shares slid because full-year revenue guidance was unchanged and CEO Arvind Krishna warned that clients see some AI spending "completely out of control with no real return" ([[ibm-ceo-ai-spending-roi-warning-stock-falls-oct2026]]) and another recording a 0.3% gain after a 17.7% three-month run, read as investors catching their breath ([[ibm-q3-2026-margins-improve-stock-muted-simply-wall-st]]). The details explain the lack of enthusiasm: reported software growth of 9% falls to about 4% organic without the Confluent acquisition, guidance implies a Q4 slowdown, and the $11.4B of acquisitions cut cash to $11.1B and lifted debt to $63.8B ([[ibm-q3-2026-strong-results-muted-stock-reaction-organic-growth]]). The segment breakdown showed software up 8.2%, infrastructure up 4.2% with IBM Z down 19.6%, and a full-year free-cash-flow growth outlook raised to more than $1B, while Krishna's "Super Intelligence" deployment claims went unquantified ([[ibm-q3-2026-segment-detail-fcf-outlook-raised-dataphoenix]]). Before the report, the $5.5B generative AI book was the metric to watch, with the main risk being clients delaying discretionary IT and AI projects ([[ibm-q3-2026-earnings-outlook-gen-ai-book-simply-wall-st-oct26]]).

## AI Spending as the Real Test
In 2026 earnings seasons have mostly served as a vote on the AI trade. The July mega-cap week asked Meta, Microsoft and Amazon to justify AI capex while rising rates made those long-dated returns harder to defend ([[week-ahead-watchlist-big-tech-earnings-fed-pce-july-2026]]), and Schwab's August outlook asked whether strong Microsoft and Amazon results had really calmed concerns about AI economics ([[us-stock-market-outlook-august-2026]]). IBD treated Sandisk, Western Digital and AMD as a separate read on AI hardware demand, apart from the hyperscaler-capex story ([[dow-futures-trump-calls-off-iran-attacks-aug2026]]). By October the stakes were higher still: debt-financed "circular" chip deals such as Broadcom–OpenAI and SpaceX–Nvidia make earnings updates the main evidence on whether AI demand will last ([[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]), and Cramer called the TSMC report the week's biggest potential catalyst for chip stocks ([[cramer-week-ahead-bank-chip-earnings-oct2026]]).

The week of October 12 turned that worry into price action. The S&P 500 had its biggest weekly drop since June, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index was down about 12% for the month, CoreWeave's roughly $50B of debt raised financing fears, and Goldman estimated that five hyperscalers will spend about $1.2T next year, which left investors looking to Meta for proof that the spending pays ([[ai-rally-wobbles-earnings-meta-hyperscaler-capex-oct2026]]). CNBC captured the split afterward, with the Nasdaq down 4.5% on the week, the VIX at its highest since June, Dan Ives calling the drawdown a buying opportunity and BTIG urging clients to use any semiconductor rebound to cut exposure ([[cnbc-morning-squawk-ai-dip-buying-oct19-2026]]). Meta then delivered the test. It reported negative free cash flow of about $2.12B and lifted its 2026 capex range to $135–$145B, and on October 22 the Nasdaq 100 had its worst day in four months at -2.9% and the semiconductor index fell 7%, with Brent's jump to $113 compounding the move ([[nasdaq-100-worst-day-four-months-meta-capex-oct2026]]). By the Friday close, Tesla and Meta had reignited spending concerns while SpaceX and Super Micro had cushioned them ([[new-york-stocks-weekly-close-ai-capex-spacex-telecom-oct24-2026]]), and nearly three in four S&P 500 stocks fell as investors questioned upfront costs, debt-funded spending and the timing of returns ([[wall-street-oil-four-year-high-ai-economics-oct23-2026]]).

The next week was built around the same question for the remaining hyperscalers. Alphabet and Microsoft reported on October 28 and Amazon the next day, alongside the FOMC decision and September PCE ([[sp500-eps-growth-q2-2027-dip-fomc-pce-hyperscalers-oct2026]]), and Raymond James said they would be judged on cloud revenue, capex guidance and free cash flow ([[raymond-james-key-catalysts-fed-earnings-refunding-midterms-oct2026]]). Patel framed that week as a test of whether AI investment turns into profit, with guidance and the Fed's tone mattering more than the headline decision ([[five-things-moving-markets-oil-fed-big-tech-oct25-2026]]). The Alphabet previews show how the bar was set. Consensus was about $127.4B–$127.8B of revenue and EPS of $2.94 to $3.04, but the Q2 print combined 82% Cloud growth with negative free cash flow of $5.9B, so one analyst argues capex and cash flow matter more than EPS, and the stock fell 3.15% to $324.22 into the report ([[alphabet-googl-triangle-breakdown-q3-earnings-capex-test-oct2026]]). A preview built on Cloud's accelerating growth, a $514B backlog and a 35.6% Cloud margin says a tougher Search comparison and heavy spending could still pressure margins and cash flow ([[googl-q3-2026-earnings-preview-cloud-vs-capex]]), while Adam Levy predicts Cloud revenue will more than double, which would need over $30.3B ([[alphabet-google-cloud-q3-2026-100-percent-growth-prediction]]). Before the report the company's $80B equity raise and a capex guide raised to $195–$205B had left the stock about 20% below its May record, so the question was whether management could show payback on AI infrastructure; Thomas Kurian claimed AI server payback under two years ([[alphabet-stock-26-percent-up-20-percent-below-record-capex-equity-raise-oct2026]]). A Motley Fool preview made the same point, arguing that the $514B backlog is a promise rather than recognized revenue, that Q2 free cash flow was negative $5.9B, and that cloud margins, whether capex turns into revenue and Search growth mattered more than the backlog figure, with third-party capacity possibly pressuring Cloud margins slightly in the third quarter ([[alphabet-q3-2026-earnings-watch-margins-capex-search]]). See [[alphabet]] for the company-level picture.

The results split the group. Alphabet's Cloud revenue rose 103% to $30.7B, above the roughly $29.1B expected, with a margin of about 36%, yet advertising missed and free cash flow stayed negative at about $9.1B for a second quarter ([[google-stock-falls-again-after-worst-day-q3-miss-cloud-103-percent-yahoo-oct30-2026]], [[alphabet-711-billion-wipeout-worst-day-record-ai-search-anxiety-oct2026]]). Net income fell about a quarter to $26.2B, and the spending was judged against the cash it consumed ([[alphabet-googl-analyst-consensus-strong-buy-45-percent-upside-oct31-2026]]). Microsoft reported revenue of $92.3B, up 19%, with Azure up 48% against a 44–45% forecast, and cut its calendar-2026 capex forecast to $170B from $175B because third-party GPU capacity slipped into the second half of its fiscal year ([[microsoft-trims-capex-forecast-170b-gpu-capacity-delays-oct2026]]). Free cash flow still fell about 27% to $18.6B as capex reached $45.2B, and the Microsoft Cloud gross margin slipped to 66% as lower-margin Azure grew as a share of cloud sales ([[microsoft-fy27-q1-report-card-azure-a-fcf-c-plus-motley-fool]]). Microsoft's positive cash generation is why it could absorb the spending without much market damage ([[alphabet-711-billion-wipeout-worst-day-record-ai-search-anxiety-oct2026]]). Its shares rose after hours but reversed early gains on October 29 ([[markets-live-oct-29-2026-alphabet-sell-off-gdp-pce-ai-spending]]). Around the print Microsoft also offered voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees, roughly 27% of its workforce ([[microsoft-msft-voluntary-buyouts-60000-employees-earnings-eve-oct2026]]), and it priced a $25B bond sale the following day, which shows the spending being funded partly with debt even at the company with the strongest cash flow ([[microsoft-25-billion-bonds-azure-beat-tikr-2031-outlook]]). Amazon's sales rose 11% to $201B, but its cash flow was negative $42B and its capex reached $77.3B against about $58.7B expected, and the shares fell about 6% after hours ([[alphabet-500bn-loss-ai-mode-hits-ad-sales-amazon-microsoft-capex-bbc]], [[alphabet-711-billion-wipeout-worst-day-record-ai-search-anxiety-oct2026]]).

## Alphabet and the Cannibalization Question
Alphabet's report raised a different concern from capex alone: whether its own AI product was eating into its core business. Management said AI Mode queries earn less than comparable web searches, with fewer and cheaper ads, and query growth slowed to 12% from 17% in Q2 and 19% in Q1, leaving search revenue flat ([[google-ai-mode-lower-revenue-per-query-q3-2026-stock-drop]]). Kim Scott framed the trade-off as a deliberate long-term bet, Google choosing to eat its own lunch rather than let rivals do it, and the day's loss took more than $500B off the company's value and pushed the shares to a six-month low ([[alphabet-500bn-loss-ai-mode-hits-ad-sales-amazon-microsoft-capex-bbc]]). Investors read the episode as the search business being cannibalized by a product that serves the same need at lower monetization, and the open question became where AI value will ultimately be captured ([[alphabet-711-billion-wipeout-worst-day-record-ai-search-anxiety-oct2026]]). The same mechanism reached other companies: Reddit fell 29% on weak daily users, with the company citing Google's AI-driven search for lower referral traffic ([[us-tech-stocks-fall-intel-alphabet-ai-spending-1tn-oct30-2026]]). Sundar Pichai argued that agents expand search rather than cannibalize it, while Meta's Muse agent, used by about 10 million people mostly for shopping, is a possible long-term search threat ([[google-stock-falls-again-after-worst-day-q3-miss-cloud-103-percent-yahoo-oct30-2026]]). VanEck argued that it was premature to treat the weak quarter as proof the core is threatened ([[alphabet-711-billion-wipeout-worst-day-record-ai-search-anxiety-oct2026]]).

## The Late-October Sell-Off
The tech sell-off deepened on October 30. Intel fell about 28.6% after guiding fourth-quarter revenue to $15–16B and gross margin to 38% against 44.1%, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 7.2%, its worst day since June 23 and down 15.4% for October ([[nasdaq-100-intraday-correction-intel-chips-oct30-2026]]). The Nasdaq-100 fell 3.46% and touched 10% below its October 6 record intraday, but it closed 9.1% below that record, so it never finished a session in correction ([[nasdaq-100-intraday-correction-intel-chips-oct30-2026]]). The Nasdaq Composite posted its worst monthly slide since March 2025 yet was still up about 7.8% over twelve months ([[us-tech-stocks-fall-intel-alphabet-ai-spending-1tn-oct30-2026]]). Analysts described investors demanding payback on AI spending, with Steve Sosnick saying anything less than perfect bites richly valued shares and Dan Ives expecting 2027 to be the year AI revenue begins to justify the outlay ([[tech-rout-deepens-intel-30-percent-amazon-alphabet-ai-spending-doubts-oct31]]). Underneath the index moves, breadth was better than the headlines. On October 29 the Nasdaq fell 1.4% and the S&P 500 and Dow each 0.3%, yet most S&P 500 stocks and most sectors finished higher, and the Russell 2000 and S&P 400 gained ([[barrons-oct-29-2026-alphabet-worst-day-nasdaq-breadth-holds]], [[markets-live-oct-29-2026-alphabet-sell-off-gdp-pce-ai-spending]]). Apple overtook Nvidia as the most valuable company at $5.03 trillion, which the closing-price correction measure captured as a shift toward names less exposed to data-center spending ([[nasdaq-100-intraday-correction-intel-chips-oct30-2026]]).

## Earnings Versus Yields at the Index Level
Over 2026, earnings growth has been the main force holding the index up against rising rates. In August the S&P 500 set records while its forward P/E fell to 19.6, below the 19.9 five-year average, because earnings were growing faster than prices ([[stock-market-record-highs-spacex-amd-weakness-aug2026]]), and in October it climbed a [[Wall of Worry]] to new highs as the 10-year yield hit 5.31%, the highest since 2002, which Edward Jones credited to robust earnings and narrow credit spreads ([[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]], [[daily-market-snapshot-record-highs-10yr-yield-oct2026]]). The cushion has limits: the October 6 record, driven partly by strong earnings estimates, gave way the next day as yields above 5.35% made Treasuries a tougher alternative to stocks ([[stocks-fall-bond-yields-oil-fed-minutes-halt-record-rally-oct7-2026]]), and Reuters quoted a warning that indices will eventually "cry uncle" on higher rates ([[wall-st-week-ahead-bank-earnings-cpi-sp500-record-oct9-2026]]). The 1994 precedent, when EPS rose 39.8% while Fed hikes left the index slightly down, shows that strong earnings alone do not guarantee gains ([[sp500-earnings-growth-pace-1994-1999-comparison]]). The October 12 week showed how outside shocks cross-cut the earnings test: the S&P 500's forward P/E of 19.1 sat below its five- and ten-year averages, yet Houthi strikes on Riyadh's airport and Iranian attacks near Hormuz threatened an oil-to-inflation-to-Fed chain that could undercut that discount ([[dow-sp500-nasdaq-outlook-earnings-cpi-middle-east-oct2026]], [[dow-futures-bank-earnings-houthi-russian-diesel-spacex-oct12-2026]]). The strong earnings season did not stop a second straight weekly loss in the index, with Brent at $116.72, its highest since June 2022, the 10-year yield near 4.99% and a Fed that has already hiked, so a December hike remains heavily favored ([[wall-street-oil-four-year-high-ai-economics-oct23-2026]]). Strategists cited by Reuters still saw the Q3 growth rate as a cushion against higher rates ([[ai-rally-wobbles-earnings-meta-hyperscaler-capex-oct2026]]), but the cost of capital also raises the return AI capex has to earn, and Horizon's Chuck Carlson noted that it makes the spending boom costlier ([[ai-rally-wobbles-earnings-meta-hyperscaler-capex-oct2026]]). On October 20 a one-day drop in oil from near $110 and upbeat earnings lifted the Dow over 500 points even as the 10-year sat near 5.12%, and a WSJ poll found only 17 of 64 economists expecting it below 5% by year-end ([[investopedia-markets-oct20-2026-dow-500-oil-pullback-treasury-5-percent]]).

The last week of October added a rate shock to the earnings story. The Fed held at 3.75% to 4% on October 28, with two dissents favoring a hike, and Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish-sounding remarks pushed the 10-year yield back above 5%, lifting the odds of a December hike to about 83.5% ([[futures-drop-alphabet-9-8-percent-q3-miss-microsoft-beat-warsh-hold-oct2026]]). Markets barely reacted to the decision itself, since it had been widely telegraphed and Warsh again gave no forward guidance, so attention moved to the earnings calendar ([[wall-street-looks-past-fed-pause-tech-earnings-ai-rebound-cnbc-oct28-2026]]). Before the meeting, the CME FedWatch Tool put the chance of an unchanged rate at about 90.6%, after a September hike to 3.75%–4.00% that was the first since July 2023, and because the October meeting had no dot plot, the statement and press conference were expected to set December expectations ([[week-ahead-oct26-30-fomc-alphabet-microsoft-amazon-earnings-tradingkey]]). The schedule put the rate decision and Warsh's press conference on Wednesday, with Thursday's Q3 GDP and Core PCE data arriving hours after the rate call, so the rate and growth readings landed together with the Alphabet and Microsoft reports ([[big-tech-earnings-fed-warsh-gdp-pce-week-ahead-oct26-2026]]). Third-quarter GDP growth of 3.4% annualized beat the 3.2% forecast, helped by AI spending, while September headline PCE rose 3.6% year over year and core PCE 3.1% ([[markets-live-oct-29-2026-alphabet-sell-off-gdp-pce-ai-spending]]). Hot inflation and a firm Fed kept the discount-rate argument alive, which is why the earnings cushion was thinner than it had been in the summer.

## Earnings Quality: Concentration and One-Offs
The headline growth rates overstate how broad the earnings strength is. Wolfe trims 2026 EPS growth from the 31% consensus to about 27% once large one-time gains at mega-cap tech companies are removed ([[wolfe-research-sp500-earnings-momentum-aug2026]]), and Amazon's and Alphabet's GAAP earnings were lifted by gains on their Anthropic and SpaceX stakes, while Nvidia and Micron posted outlier growth of 95% and 1,000%-plus ([[sp500-earnings-growth-pace-1994-1999-comparison]]). Alphabet's Q2 diluted EPS of $9.11 included a $99.0B mostly unrealized securities gain, which is why the Alphabet preview treats operating income as the cleaner gauge ([[alphabet-googl-triangle-breakdown-q3-earnings-capex-test-oct2026]], [[alphabet-stock-26-percent-up-20-percent-below-record-capex-equity-raise-oct2026]]). The same mark-ups now cut the other way in the forward calendar, since Q1 2027 growth estimates dropped to 12.1% after the Anthropic mark-ups before recovering to 17.1% on upward revisions ([[sp500-eps-growth-q2-2027-dip-fomc-pce-hyperscalers-oct2026]]). Analysts also noted that one-time AI-investment gains had inflated profit growth at the mega-caps, so the headline percentage can say little about the typical company ([[sp500-third-of-companies-report-earnings-week-confidence-cracks-oct2026]]). IBM shows a company-level version, with an acquisition-boosted growth rate masking a weaker organic one ([[ibm-q3-2026-strong-results-muted-stock-reaction-organic-growth]]). Sector estimates spread even wider, from energy up 121% to healthcare down 9% ([[economies-investors-earnings-fed-signals-week-ahead-aug2026]]), and bank profits leaned on a one-time SpaceX IPO windfall alongside Dimon's own "overearning" admission ([[big-bank-earnings-q2-2026-consumer-spending]]). Capital Economics warned that if AI-linked earnings and capex assumptions are revised down, the result could be a broader correction ([[economies-investors-earnings-fed-signals-week-ahead-aug2026]]).
