# Earnings Season

This topic covers how quarterly reporting seasons move markets in 2026, from how the bar is set and revised to why stocks react more to guidance, cash flow and capex than to the headline beat. The Q3 2026 season peaked in the last week of October, when Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon and Intel reported, and its tail ran into November with Apple, the midterms and the jobs report. General S&P 500 structure and breadth live in [[sp500]].

## How a Season Opens
Q3 2026 opened the week of October 5 with Delta, PepsiCo and Levi Strauss, and Investing.com read the Pepsi and Levi results mainly as a read on the U.S. consumer, given Pepsi's back-and-forth on chip pricing and Levi's premium bet ([[week-ahead-q3-earnings-season-delta-pepsi-fed-minutes-oct2026]], [[five-things-week-ahead-bond-yields-french-debt-fomc-minutes-oct2026]]). JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and Wells Fargo reported on October 13 alongside Johnson & Johnson, UnitedHealth and Kinder Morgan, in the same week as CPI and PPI, so one week tested both corporate profits and the rate outlook ([[dow-sp500-nasdaq-outlook-earnings-cpi-middle-east-oct2026]], [[wall-st-week-ahead-bank-earnings-cpi-sp500-record-oct9-2026]]). Jim Cramer framed the season as the point where markets stop guessing from scattered data and start pricing actual results ([[cramer-week-ahead-bank-chip-earnings-oct2026]]). The week of October 18 brought SpaceX, Tesla and Meta, with Meta billed as the opening act for the hyperscalers ([[earnings-week-oct-2026-spcx-tsla-meta-nflx-ibm-preview]]).

## Banks as the First Read
Bank results are read as a signal on the wider economy, and in Q2 that meant card spending up 6.3% year over year in June, benign credit and AI deal flow ([[big-bank-earnings-q2-2026-consumer-spending]]). Analysts still warned the quarter would not reset expectations higher, and a roughly $500M windfall from the SpaceX IPO flattered the results, while Jamie Dimon said JPMorgan was overearning ([[big-bank-earnings-q2-2026-consumer-spending]]). For Q3 the swing factor was capital markets, with trading the upside risk and investment-banking fees the downside, and Oppenheimer cut its IB revenue view 9.5% and expected only JPMorgan to beat ([[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]]). Cramer was bullish on Goldman Sachs and Wells Fargo but cautious on JPMorgan, which he said was priced for near-perfect execution, so the same consensus numbers supported opposite calls ([[cramer-week-ahead-bank-chip-earnings-oct2026]]).

## Setting the Bar: Consensus and Revisions
The bar sat unusually high in 2026, with Q2 aggregate EPS growth estimated at 22.5% to 25%, the strongest since 2021, and Wolfe Research finding 64% of companies giving guidance above consensus, the highest share since COVID ([[economies-investors-earnings-fed-signals-week-ahead-aug2026]], [[wolfe-research-sp500-earnings-momentum-aug2026]]). For Q3 analysts raised estimates 1.3% during the quarter instead of cutting them and expected 29.1% growth, a third straight quarter above 25% ([[week-ahead-q3-earnings-season-delta-pepsi-fed-minutes-oct2026]]). With FactSet at 29.5%, one preview argued that a typical beat of about 7 points could push growth above 40% ([[dow-sp500-nasdaq-outlook-earnings-cpi-middle-east-oct2026]]). Results cleared that bar, with 85% of reporters beating by October 19 and an 82% beat rate against a 77% ten-year average ([[cnbc-morning-squawk-ai-dip-buying-oct19-2026]], [[raymond-james-key-catalysts-fed-earnings-refunding-midterms-oct2026]]), while the forward calendar looked weaker, with LSEG data showing Q2 2027 EPS growth of only 3.6%, the lowest since Q2 2023 ([[sp500-eps-growth-q2-2027-dip-fomc-pce-hyperscalers-oct2026]]).

## The Final Week of October
By then about 179 S&P 500 companies, roughly 36% of the index, were due to report, and Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon alone were worth close to $11 trillion, so their results were expected to set the direction ([[sp500-third-of-companies-report-earnings-week-confidence-cracks-oct2026]]). Revenue beats had narrowed to 1.7% from 3.8% in Q2, even as earnings beat by 6.5% and the forward profit margin sat at a record 16.8% ([[wall-street-looks-past-fed-pause-tech-earnings-ai-rebound-cnbc-oct28-2026]]). Oil producers were expected to post outsized gains, with Exxon Mobil seen earning $4.03 a share, up 114%, and Chevron's EPS rising from $1.85 to $4.86 ([[thestreet-fed-tech-oil-earnings-exciting-week-oct26-2026]]). With the median company expected to grow about 10% against 29.5% for the index, Jonathan Golub warned that a narrow beat could read as a miss once so much was already priced in ([[sp500-third-of-companies-report-earnings-week-confidence-cracks-oct2026]]).

## Beats That Sell Off
When expectations are high, beating consensus is not enough. About 90% of early Q2 reporters beat, yet tech stocks' post-earnings moves lagged the historical average by 2.6 points, and Alphabet and Tesla fell despite strong results ([[stock-market-july-28-2026-dow-sp500-nasdaq-earnings]]). AMD and SpaceX dropped 8–10% on beats because investors had traded on higher unofficial whisper numbers ([[stock-market-record-highs-spacex-amd-weakness-aug2026]]), and Barclays found both beats and misses producing negative average reactions for the first time since Q4 2024, which it read as demand for a beat plus stronger guidance ([[sp500-rally-fundamentals-diverge-aug2026]]). Walmart fell 9% after beating Q2 because its Q3 guidance landed below estimates ([[markets-aug20-2026-walmart-bitcoin-oil-earnings]]), and in the week of October 19 the split followed guidance, with 3M up about 6% on a beat-and-raise while General Motors fell about 7% despite a beat and a $20B buyback ([[dow-up-523-points-russell-2000-oil-pivot-oct20-2026]], [[investopedia-markets-oct20-2026-dow-500-oil-pullback-treasury-5-percent]]).

## IBM Before the Report
IBM was stalled near $240 and down 19% for the year before its October 21 report, after a record 25.2% one-day drop in July, and options priced a 4.3% move against a 6.3% average, with the stock higher the next session in only five of its last twelve reports ([[ibm-stock-struggling-at-240-ahead-of-q3-earnings-options-pricing-oct2026]]). A Motley Fool preview said the call hinged on whether software growth, which had slowed to 5% in Q2 with all of it acquired, could recover toward high single digits ([[ibm-q3-preview-software-growth-29-percent-below-high]]). The $5.5B generative AI book was the metric to watch, with clients delaying discretionary IT and AI projects the main risk ([[ibm-q3-2026-earnings-outlook-gen-ai-book-simply-wall-st-oct26]]).

## IBM After the Report
IBM beat, with revenue up 5% to $17.1B and adjusted EPS of $2.95 against $2.87 expected, and it raised its full-year free-cash-flow growth outlook to more than $1B ([[ibm-ceo-ai-spending-roi-warning-stock-falls-oct2026]], [[ibm-q3-2026-segment-detail-fcf-outlook-raised-dataphoenix]]). The stock still lagged because reported software growth of 9% falls to about 4% organically without Confluent, guidance implies a slower fourth quarter, and $11.4B of acquisitions cut cash to $11.1B and lifted debt to $63.8B ([[ibm-q3-2026-strong-results-muted-stock-reaction-organic-growth]]). Coverage disagreed on the tape, with one report blaming a slide on unchanged full-year guidance and the CEO's warning that some clients saw AI spending as out of control with no real return, and another recording a 0.3% gain after a 17.7% three-month run ([[ibm-ceo-ai-spending-roi-warning-stock-falls-oct2026]], [[ibm-q3-2026-margins-improve-stock-muted-simply-wall-st]]). The segments showed software up 8.2%, infrastructure up 4.2% with IBM Z down 19.6%, and Krishna's "Super Intelligence" deployment claims went unquantified ([[ibm-q3-2026-segment-detail-fcf-outlook-raised-dataphoenix]]).

## AI Spending as the Real Test
Through 2026 earnings seasons have increasingly served as a vote on AI, from July's mega-cap week asking Meta, Microsoft and Amazon to justify capex to August's test of whether strong Microsoft and Amazon results had calmed concerns about AI economics ([[week-ahead-watchlist-big-tech-earnings-fed-pce-july-2026]], [[us-stock-market-outlook-august-2026]]). IBD read Sandisk, Western Digital and AMD as a separate gauge of AI hardware demand ([[dow-futures-trump-calls-off-iran-attacks-aug2026]]), and by October debt-financed "circular" chip deals such as Broadcom–OpenAI and SpaceX–Nvidia made earnings updates the main evidence on whether AI demand would last ([[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]). The week of October 12 turned that worry into price action, with the S&P 500's biggest weekly drop since June, CoreWeave's roughly $50B of debt raising financing fears, and Goldman estimating five hyperscalers would spend about $1.2T next year ([[ai-rally-wobbles-earnings-meta-hyperscaler-capex-oct2026]]). CNBC put the Nasdaq down 4.5% that week, with Dan Ives calling the drawdown a buying opportunity and BTIG urging clients to use any semiconductor rebound to cut exposure ([[cnbc-morning-squawk-ai-dip-buying-oct19-2026]]).

## Meta and the October 22 Drop
Meta reported negative free cash flow of about $2.12B and lifted its 2026 capex range to $135–$145B, and on October 22 the Nasdaq 100 fell 2.9%, its worst day in four months, as the semiconductor index dropped 7% ([[nasdaq-100-worst-day-four-months-meta-capex-oct2026]]). Brent's 4% jump to $113 compounded the selloff, and by the Friday close Tesla and Meta had reignited spending concerns while SpaceX and Super Micro had cushioned them ([[nasdaq-100-worst-day-four-months-meta-capex-oct2026]], [[new-york-stocks-weekly-close-ai-capex-spacex-telecom-oct24-2026]]). Nearly three in four S&P 500 stocks fell that week as investors questioned upfront AI costs, debt-funded spending and the timing of returns ([[wall-street-oil-four-year-high-ai-economics-oct23-2026]]). Blackstone's Jon Gray argued AI capacity was being built to meet demand rather than in bubble-style speculation, which suggested the selloff was sentiment-driven ([[nasdaq-100-worst-day-four-months-meta-capex-oct2026]]).

## Alphabet Before the Print
Ahead of its October 28 report, Alphabet's Cloud revenue had grown 82% in Q2 to $24.8B and backlog stood at $514B, yet free cash flow was negative $5.9B, and an $80B equity raise and a capex guide of $195–$205B had left the stock about 20% below its May record ([[googl-q3-2026-earnings-preview-cloud-vs-capex]], [[alphabet-stock-26-percent-up-20-percent-below-record-capex-equity-raise-oct2026]]). Previews split on what mattered, with one arguing the backlog was a promise rather than revenue, so cloud margins, capex conversion and Search growth counted for more ([[alphabet-q3-2026-earnings-watch-margins-capex-search]]), and another predicting Cloud revenue would more than double, which would require over $30.3B ([[alphabet-google-cloud-q3-2026-100-percent-growth-prediction]]). Thomas Kurian claimed AI server payback under two years, and about half that on Alphabet's own silicon, which framed the question as whether management could show payback ([[alphabet-stock-26-percent-up-20-percent-below-record-capex-equity-raise-oct2026]]). See [[alphabet]] for the company-level picture.

## The Alphabet Print
Revenue rose 25% to about $127.6B but fell short of expectations, with advertising at $82.01B against $83.5B expected and EPS of $2.12 against about $3.01 ([[google-stock-falls-again-after-worst-day-q3-miss-cloud-103-percent-yahoo-oct30-2026]], [[alphabet-500bn-loss-ai-mode-hits-ad-sales-amazon-microsoft-capex-bbc]]). Google Cloud revenue rose 103% to $30.7B, above the roughly $29.1B expected, yet free cash flow stayed negative at about $9.1B for a second straight quarter ([[alphabet-711-billion-wipeout-worst-day-record-ai-search-anxiety-oct2026]]). The shares fell roughly 12–13% on October 29, their worst day since the 2004 listing, and slipped another 1% the next session, as Cloud strength failed to offset the advertising miss and the cash burn ([[alphabet-711-billion-wipeout-worst-day-record-ai-search-anxiety-oct2026]], [[alphabet-googl-analyst-consensus-strong-buy-45-percent-upside-oct31-2026]]).

## Cannibalization: When AI Search Sells Less Per Query
Management said AI Mode queries earn less than comparable web searches, with fewer and cheaper ads, and query growth slowed to 12% from 17% in Q2 and 19% in Q1, leaving search revenue flat ([[google-ai-mode-lower-revenue-per-query-q3-2026-stock-drop]]). Kim Scott framed the trade-off as a deliberate long-term bet, Google choosing to eat its own lunch rather than let rivals do it, and the day's loss took more than $500B off the company's value, from $3.97T to $3.46T ([[alphabet-500bn-loss-ai-mode-hits-ad-sales-amazon-microsoft-capex-bbc]]). The same mechanism reached other companies, since Reddit fell 29% on weak daily users and cited Google's AI-driven search for lower referral traffic ([[us-tech-stocks-fall-intel-alphabet-ai-spending-1tn-oct30-2026]]). Sundar Pichai argued that agents expand search rather than cannibalize it, while Meta's Muse agent, used by about 10 million people mostly for shopping, was flagged as a possible long-term search threat ([[google-stock-falls-again-after-worst-day-q3-miss-cloud-103-percent-yahoo-oct30-2026]]).

## Microsoft's Quarter
Microsoft beat on both lines, with revenue of $92.3B up 19% and Azure up 48% against a 44–45% forecast, and it cut its calendar-2026 capex forecast to $170B from $175B because third-party GPU capacity slipped into the second half of its fiscal year ([[microsoft-trims-capex-forecast-170b-gpu-capacity-delays-oct2026]], [[microsoft-fy27-q1-report-card-azure-a-fcf-c-plus-motley-fool]]). Capex of $45.2B pulled free cash flow down about 27% to $18.6B, and Microsoft Cloud gross margin slipped to 66% as lower-margin Azure grew as a share of cloud sales ([[microsoft-fy27-q1-report-card-azure-a-fcf-c-plus-motley-fool]]). Microsoft still priced a $25B bond sale after the print and had offered voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees, roughly 27% of its workforce ([[microsoft-25-billion-bonds-azure-beat-tikr-2031-outlook]], [[microsoft-msft-voluntary-buyouts-60000-employees-earnings-eve-oct2026]]). Its shares rose after hours but reversed early gains on October 29 and finished down by under 1% ([[markets-live-oct-29-2026-alphabet-sell-off-gdp-pce-ai-spending]], [[alphabet-500bn-loss-ai-mode-hits-ad-sales-amazon-microsoft-capex-bbc]]).

## Pricing the Aftermath
Motley Fool called Alphabet's dip unjustified, citing a $561B cloud backlog up 262%, about 55% of it due within 24 months, and a forward P/E of 18x against 17x for communication services ([[three-reasons-buy-alphabet-cloud-ai-18x-forward-earnings-motley-fool]]). The Street still leaned bullish, with 45 of 53 analysts rating the stock Strong Buy and a mean target of $406.04, 45.1% above the price, though slightly less bullish than three months earlier ([[alphabet-googl-analyst-consensus-strong-buy-45-percent-upside-oct31-2026]]). Barchart saw Microsoft's forward P/E of 27.03x below a 29.85x sector median, but a PEG of 1.67x against 1.19x erases the discount once growth is counted, leaving about 12% upside to a $594.15 average target ([[microsoft-q1-fy2027-beat-how-to-think-about-msft-stock]]). Motley Fool wanted a Microsoft price near $465 because free cash flow was falling, and TIKR noted that commercial bookings fell 12% and that the Azure guide of 49–50% depends on efficiency gains that CFO Amy Hood said still had to be found ([[microsoft-fy27-q1-report-card-azure-a-fcf-c-plus-motley-fool]], [[microsoft-25-billion-bonds-azure-beat-tikr-2031-outlook]]).

## Amazon's Cash Burn
Amazon reported sales up 11% to $201B, but its cash flow was negative about $42B and capex reached $77.3B against roughly $58.7B expected, and the shares fell about 6% after hours ([[alphabet-500bn-loss-ai-mode-hits-ad-sales-amazon-microsoft-capex-bbc]], [[alphabet-711-billion-wipeout-worst-day-record-ai-search-anxiety-oct2026]]). Investors disliked spending that outran cash generation, with Amazon's AI spending nearly doubling and implying more debt and about $220B of capital spending committed this year ([[tech-rout-deepens-intel-30-percent-amazon-alphabet-ai-spending-doubts-oct31]], [[us-tech-stocks-fall-intel-alphabet-ai-spending-1tn-oct30-2026]]).

## The Late-October Sell-Off
Intel fell 28.6% on October 30 after guiding fourth-quarter revenue to $15–16B and gross margin to 38% against 44.1%, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 7.2%, its worst day since June 23 ([[nasdaq-100-intraday-correction-intel-chips-oct30-2026]]). The Nasdaq-100 touched 10% below its October 6 record intraday but closed 9.1% below, so it never finished a session in correction, while the Nasdaq Composite posted its worst monthly slide since March 2025 yet was still up about 7.8% over twelve months ([[nasdaq-100-intraday-correction-intel-chips-oct30-2026]], [[us-tech-stocks-fall-intel-alphabet-ai-spending-1tn-oct30-2026]]). Breadth held up on October 29, when most S&P 500 stocks and sectors finished higher even as the Nasdaq fell 1.4% ([[barrons-oct-29-2026-alphabet-worst-day-nasdaq-breadth-holds]]). The damage was concentrated, with Meta and Alphabet more than 20% below their 52-week highs, Tesla down 19.2%, Amazon 15.1% and Nvidia 12.2%, and Steve Sosnick warned that richly valued shares punish anything short of perfect ([[magnificent-7-stunning-declines-meta-alphabet-bear-market-oct2026]], [[tech-rout-deepens-intel-30-percent-amazon-alphabet-ai-spending-doubts-oct31]]).

## Earnings Versus Yields at the Index Level
Earnings growth has held the index up against rising rates, with the S&P 500 setting records in August at a forward P/E of 19.6, below its 19.9 five-year average, while the 10-year yield hit 5.31% in October, the highest since 2002 ([[stock-market-record-highs-spacex-amd-weakness-aug2026]], [[daily-market-snapshot-record-highs-10yr-yield-oct2026]]). The cushion has limits, since yields above 5.35% made Treasuries a tougher alternative on October 7, and the 1994 precedent, when EPS rose 39.8% while Fed hikes left the index slightly lower, shows that strong earnings alone do not guarantee gains ([[stocks-fall-bond-yields-oil-fed-minutes-halt-record-rally-oct7-2026]], [[sp500-earnings-growth-pace-1994-1999-comparison]]). Oil added to the squeeze, with Houthi strikes and Hormuz attacks threatening an oil-to-inflation-to-Fed chain and Brent reaching $116.72 by October 23, its highest since June 2022 ([[dow-sp500-nasdaq-outlook-earnings-cpi-middle-east-oct2026]], [[wall-street-oil-four-year-high-ai-economics-oct23-2026]]). On October 20 falling oil lifted the Dow more than 500 points even as the 10-year sat near 5.12%, and a WSJ poll found only 17 of 64 economists expecting it below 5% by year-end ([[investopedia-markets-oct20-2026-dow-500-oil-pullback-treasury-5-percent]]).

## The Late-October Rate Shock
The Fed held rates in a 3.75% to 4% range on October 28 with two dissents favoring a hike, and Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish-sounding remarks pushed the 10-year yield back above 5% and lifted December hike odds to about 83.5% ([[futures-drop-alphabet-9-8-percent-q3-miss-microsoft-beat-warsh-hold-oct2026]], [[markets-live-oct-29-2026-alphabet-sell-off-gdp-pce-ai-spending]]). Markets barely reacted to a widely telegraphed decision, and Warsh again gave no forward guidance, so attention moved to earnings ([[wall-street-looks-past-fed-pause-tech-earnings-ai-rebound-cnbc-oct28-2026]]). Patel argued that the direction of rate expectations, not the headline hold, would drive markets ([[five-things-moving-markets-oil-fed-big-tech-oct25-2026]]). Third-quarter GDP grew 3.4% annualized, beating the 3.2% forecast, while September headline PCE rose 3.6% year over year and core PCE 3.1%, data that arrived hours after the rate call alongside the Alphabet and Microsoft reports ([[markets-live-oct-29-2026-alphabet-sell-off-gdp-pce-ai-spending]], [[big-tech-earnings-fed-warsh-gdp-pce-week-ahead-oct26-2026]]).

## Earnings Quality: Concentration and One-Offs
