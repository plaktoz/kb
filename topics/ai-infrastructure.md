# AI Infrastructure

AI infrastructure covers the data centers, power, servers, memory, networking, and financing structures that AI runs on, and across the vault it appears in three roles: the physical bottleneck on AI progress, the most dependable profit pool of the boom, and the place where political, geopolitical, and balance-sheet risk is quietly building up.

## The Capacity Arms Race

U.S. large-scale data center capacity could nearly triple by 2030 as Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, and Meta build ahead of AI revenue [[ai-data-center-footprint-triple-2030]], and Microsoft by itself plans to go from about 12 GW to more than 38 GW by 2032, roughly a third of it AI-dedicated, after $145B of capex in its last fiscal year [[microsoft-data-center-38gw-2032-expansion]]. Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta have together set aside around $2.4 trillion for compute, which turns this into a winner-takes-most race where a capacity gap shows up as lost enterprise deals rather than slower growth. Jensen Huang's estimate that a 1 GW AI campus costs $50B–$60B to build puts physical numbers on the stakes, valuing Nebius's 5 GW of contracted power at up to $300B in replacement terms [[jensen-huang-gigawatt-data-center-valuation-neocloud]]. Demand forecasts keep rising with the spending: Goldman Sachs lifted its 2030 AI server market estimate to $1.24 trillion from $961B, and Dell's COO expects AI to make up 75% of data center demand by then [[dell-stock-surges-record-ai-server-orders-q2-2027]].

## The Cost of Getting Capacity Wrong

Microsoft's 2025 expansion pause is the vault's clearest case of a shared capacity shortfall spilling into unrelated businesses: Temu moved to Oracle, GitHub went down for eight hours, and Xbox had to cap cloud game streaming [[microsoft-data-center-38gw-2032-expansion]]. That lesson pushes hyperscalers to build ahead of demand, which carries the opposite risk, since Michael Burry warns that softer-than-expected AI demand could force large writedowns on the commitments made to avoid shortfalls, hurting both tech firms and their lenders [[microsoft-burry-3-trillion-hidden-ai-risk]]. Capacity planning therefore has two failure modes, with under-building punished right away and over-building punished later.

## Power and Chips as Twin Constraints

The recurring argument in the vault is that electricity, grid connections, and cooling, all with multi-year lead times, are overtaking chip supply as the limit on AI expansion, so securing energy may decide the winners more than algorithmic edge does [[ai-data-center-footprint-triple-2030]]. Politics tightens the physical limit, with governors in Texas and New York halting new data center construction over energy and land use [[microsoft-data-center-38gw-2032-expansion]]. Michael Dell's warning that a structural chip shortage could worsen in 2027 complicates the neat story of chips handing the bottleneck over to power [[dell-stock-surges-record-ai-server-orders-q2-2027]], and the vault's evidence now points to both constraints binding at the same time.

## Nuclear and Fusion: Capital Ahead of Power

Nuclear is drawing money well before it produces electricity. Kairos Power has Samsung C&T contributing up to $100M toward a 50 MW fluoride salt-cooled reactor for Google, due by 2030 and counting toward Google's goal of about 500 MW of nuclear by 2035 [[kairos-power-samsung-google-nuclear-reactor]]. The SMR path is slower than its pitch: Oklo still lacks U.S. construction approval, NuScale has not commercialized its design, only two SMRs have ever been built, and the 80+ projects in development may be a decade or more from meaningful output, while Alphabet's large conventional plants and SpaceX's natural gas buildout compete for the same demand [[oklo-nuscale-smr-data-center-backlash]]. Fusion faces the same timing question, with Helion's 2028 power-delivery commitment to Microsoft now under scrutiny [[geekwire-200-fall-2026-temporal-no1]].

## Energy Sourcing: Optionality Over Lock-in

Operators are choosing flexibility over scale-optimized power deals. Crusoe cancelled a $1.25B order for 29 of Boom Supersonic's 42 MW gas turbines so it could pick energy sources site by site, which left Boom, which had raised $300M partly to fund the turbine line as a subsidy for its Overture jet, without its anchor customer [[crusoe-abandons-boom-turbine-deal]]. Oracle's force majeure notice on the 2.45 GW Project Jupiter Stargate campus works as a hedge rather than an exit: an Energy Transfer gas pipeline slipped about six months to February 2027 and a Bloom Energy fuel cell permit is still pending, yet Oracle and Blue Owl both say the project stays funded [[oracle-force-majeure-stargate-new-mexico]]. Exit rights run through compute procurement as well, since Anthropic's Akamai commitment lets either party walk away if conditions are not met [[anthropic-akamai-11-6-billion-cloud-deal]].

## Social License and Community Opposition

Local opposition is now broad and measurable. Gallup puts opposition to a nearby data center at about 70% of Americans [[oklo-nuscale-smr-data-center-backlash]], Pew finds 60% would be uncomfortable with one (54% of Republicans and 68% of Democrats), and UMass Amherst finds 65% opposed to a local AI data center [[mike-johnson-ai-data-center-chinese-psyop]]. In Philadelphia, activists are fighting proposed sites in Grays Ferry, a neighborhood already harmed by a refinery, pointing to diesel backup generators that run more often during grid stress and a BloombergNEF projection that U.S. data centers could burn more gas than Germany and Japan combined by 2035, while moratoriums have been adopted or proposed in Denver, Indianapolis, Asheville, Charlotte, Reno, and New York [[ai-data-center-community-opposition-philadelphia]]. Resident and environmental opposition heading into the midterms also hangs over Oracle's New Mexico campus, so a project can be technically permittable and still run on an unpredictable schedule [[oracle-force-majeure-stargate-new-mexico]].

## The Politics of Who Pays

Political responses are splitting between accommodation and dismissal. Governor Hochul has made consent-based siting explicit and halted new large data center permits in New York [[ai-data-center-community-opposition-philadelphia]], Maryland's Wes Moore has gone from easing data center rules in 2024 to pushing repeal of their 2020 tax exemption [[ciena-200-million-ai-startup-venture-fund]], and House Republicans are drafting a Ratepayer Protection Act that would make data centers carry part of the cost of new generation. Speaker Mike Johnson instead calls the opposition a "Chinese psyop," citing roughly 200 accounts inside a suspected 200,000-account bot farm, an argument that targets the alleged source of the opposition while bipartisan domestic polling shows the concern stands on its own [[mike-johnson-ai-data-center-chinese-psyop]]. Across both parties, the argument is shifting from whether data centers get built to who pays for their power and taxes.

## Geopolitical Exposure in the Gulf

The same hunt for cheap energy, open land, and sovereign capital that drew Amazon, Google, Microsoft, and OpenAI into the Middle East has exposed them to war. Iranian strikes damaged AWS facilities in Bahrain and the UAE, believed to be the first U.S. data centers disrupted by military conflict, along with an Oracle-owned site in Dubai, and six months on one AWS UAE zone still has not fully recovered [[big-tech-ai-data-centers-middle-east-iran-war]]. Developers are reworking Stargate UAE from a single 5 GW campus into scattered, hardened sites that may go underground or into mountains, a concentration-risk response that parallels Crusoe's preference for site-by-site flexibility [[crusoe-abandons-boom-turbine-deal]]. The buildout is pausing rather than reversing: the region holds about 3% of global capacity and is still expected to quadruple it by 2030, and Microsoft has reaffirmed more than $15B of UAE AI spending through 2029 [[big-tech-ai-data-centers-middle-east-iran-war]].

## Hidden Leverage and Circular Financing

Off-balance-sheet financing is the newest risk thread in this topic. Michael Burry estimates that about $3 trillion of AI-linked exposure at Microsoft and its peers sits in infrastructure commitments, long-term energy deals, and capacity contracts that never appear as conventional debt [[microsoft-burry-3-trillion-hidden-ai-risk]], and a WSJ tally of roughly $3 trillion in off-balance-sheet commitments across nine large U.S. tech firms as of August supports the scale [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]]. The same market session surfaced two concrete mechanisms, with Amazon reportedly weighing a move of about $8B of installed Nvidia Blackwell chips into a special purpose vehicle that leases them back, and Broadcom reportedly raising up to $60B to lend to AI customers such as Anthropic, a supplier financing its own buyers. Akamai's warrant to Anthropic runs the other way by handing the customer equity that vests with its spending [[anthropic-akamai-11-6-billion-cloud-deal]], and corporate venture money such as Ciena's new $200M fund and Nvidia's $5B bet on a single AI startup blurs the line between procurement and investment even further [[ciena-200-million-ai-startup-venture-fund]].

## Neocloud Economics and Capital Markets

Neoclouds have found a financing route that leans on customers rather than lenders. Per-megawatt contract values climbed from $12M at the start of 2026 to more than $20M in Q2 and above $40M for short-term Q3 deals, customer prepayments now fund 50–60% of Nebius's capex and helped build an $8B cash position, and Iren's unencumbered data center portfolio gives it collateral beyond GPU-backed debt [[jensen-huang-gigawatt-data-center-valuation-neocloud]]. Public markets are the next step, with Nscale seeking up to $3.5B ahead of a planned fall 2026 IPO and adding Fidji Simo to a board that already includes Sheryl Sandberg, Nick Clegg, and Susan Decker to signal governance maturity [[nscale-fidji-simo-board-ipo]]. That customer-funded model contrasts with the hyperscaler pattern above, where obligations are moved into structures that stay off the balance sheet [[microsoft-burry-3-trillion-hidden-ai-risk]].

## Picks and Shovels: Power, Servers, Networking

The suppliers between the grid and the server rack are where the buildout already shows up as revenue: GE Vernova's data center orders topped $5B through Q2 2026, more than double its full-year 2025 total, Quanta Services' backlog reached $53.4B, and Eaton's Q2 sales rose 21% to $8.5B [[anthropic-ipo-ai-infrastructure-beneficiaries-gev-pwr-etn]]. Dell posted $47B of quarterly revenue (up 58%), booked $60.9B of AI server orders in a single quarter, ended with a $95B backlog, and grew its AI server share from 5% to 17% in a year, with HPE rallying alongside it [[dell-stock-surges-record-ai-server-orders-q2-2027]]. In networking, Ciena's fiscal Q3 revenue rose 37% to $1.67B even as its stock slipped from a May high, and the company is now putting money back into the ecosystem through Ciena Ventures [[ciena-200-million-ai-startup-venture-fund]]. Valuations already reflect much of this, since the GE Vernova, Quanta, and Eaton analysis found none of the three cheap, although Dell's 21x forward earnings still sits below the Nasdaq-100's 24x.

## Agent Workloads Reshape Procurement

AI agents are moving part of the demand from GPUs toward CPUs and enterprise-owned hardware. Anthropic's $11.6B, seven-year commitment to Akamai is built on CPU capacity for agent tasks such as orchestration, inference routing, and memory, could grow to about $20B, and will not produce revenue until 2H 2027 while Akamai spends about $5.5B on buildout [[anthropic-akamai-11-6-billion-cloud-deal]], and Microsoft's new East US 3 cluster near Atlanta likewise runs on Intel CPUs rather than GPUs [[microsoft-data-center-38gw-2032-expansion]]. Dell credits enterprises running agentic AI on their own servers for its order surge and sees a refresh pipeline in 1.2 million customer servers still on 14th-generation hardware or older [[dell-stock-surges-record-ai-server-orders-q2-2027]]. Agent infrastructure has a software layer too, as Temporal, whose durable-execution platform keeps agents running reliably for customers including OpenAI, Nvidia, and Netflix, raised $550M at a $12.55B valuation with annualized revenue above $250M [[geekwire-200-fall-2026-temporal-no1]].

## Compute Hardware Competition

Nvidia's integrated stack is being contested one layer at a time. Cornelis, an Intel spin-out that raised $205M, sells an Active Compute Fabric that keeps GPUs from idling by letting them compute and move data at once, and it pairs with any accelerator instead of tying buyers to CUDA [[cornelis-ai-infrastructure-205m-funding-nvidia-alternative]], while vendors such as IBM build their own accelerators, like the 75-watt Spyre chip developed largely at its India lab [[ibm-quantum-ai-developments-india]]. Memory is a parallel bottleneck that Samsung is attacking by more than doubling HBM4/HBM4E output in 2027, growing HBM capacity from roughly 180K to 250K wafers a month and lifting the HBM4 family's share of shipments from about 40% to 80%, after sending 12-layer HBM4E samples to Nvidia in May 2026 [[samsung-hbm4-output-doubling-2027]]. Nvidia is responding with capital as well as chips, having put $5B into one AI startup and paid $12.9B for Hugging Face [[ciena-200-million-ai-startup-venture-fund]].

## Quantum as Adjacent Infrastructure

IBM is building quantum as long-horizon infrastructure through ecosystems as much as hardware. Its ETH Zurich hub pairs the Nighthawk processor with a 10-year academic partnership, Lockheed Martin work on quantum sensing and alloy additive manufacturing, and a program of courses and hackathons [[ibm-quantum-innovation-hub-eth-zurich]], while in India a free Qiskit course with IIT Madras drew 208,785 enrollments in 2026 and a Quantum System Two installation is planned for Amaravati [[ibm-quantum-ai-developments-india]]. The hardware roadmap runs from today's 200–300-qubit Heron through Nighthawk toward 4,000 qubits, backed by Anderon, a dedicated quantum-wafer foundry in Albany built on standard 300-mm wafers. Quantum also creates infrastructure demand before it is useful, because adversaries harvesting encrypted data today make post-quantum cryptography urgent, and without hardware acceleration that cryptography could cut authentication throughput to a tenth [[ibm-quantum-ai-developments-india]].

## Space-Based Compute

Moving compute to the energy source is the far end of the power-constraint argument. Google's Project Suncatcher is betting on low Earth orbit's up-to-8x solar advantage, has shown its Trillium TPUs survive more than a five-year orbital radiation dose, is flying a prototype on SpaceX's Transporter-18 with Planet Labs, and plans a two-satellite laser-link test in 2027, though passive cooling in vacuum is still being engineered [[google-project-suncatcher-tpu-in-orbit]]. Investors are backing the idea beyond Google, with Starcloud raising $250M at a $2.3B valuation with Nvidia among its investors [[geekwire-200-fall-2026-temporal-no1]]. Orbital compute sits alongside nuclear deals and Gulf siting as another wager that energy access will set the ceiling on AI.
