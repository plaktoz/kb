# Berkshire Hathaway

Berkshire Hathaway is the vault's reference case for patient capital allocation, and this topic tracks how the post-Buffett era under Greg Abel is deploying a record cash pile into buybacks, public equities and acquisitions.

## Abel's Capital-Allocation Style

Early coverage treated Greg Abel's first quarters as a test of whether he would follow Warren Buffett's playbook, with the [[berkshire-hathaway-q2-2026-earnings-preview]] pointing to the $6.8 billion Taylor Morrison deal and continued buybacks as the first signals. The second-quarter results suggest he follows the same philosophy more aggressively: [[berkshire-q2-2026-alphabet-delta-record-buybacks-abel]] reports additions to Alphabet and Delta Air Lines and about $4.5 billion of repurchases, up from $235 million in the first quarter. Even after that spending, the cash and short-term investment balance stood near $359 billion, so the deployment is large but leaves substantial dry powder.

## Alphabet as the Conviction Position

Berkshire's Alphabet stake grew to roughly 79 million shares, about 9.4% of the portfolio, after Abel added 36 million shares in the first quarter and 24.5 million in the second, according to [[berkshire-hathaway-alphabet-new-top-holding-greg-abel]]. That note frames the buying as a bet that Alphabet's first negative free-cash-flow quarter, caused by AI capital spending, is a trough rather than a structural problem, much as Amazon's heavy-spending phase later paid off. Apple remains by far the largest holding, and [[apple-buffett-dividend-stock-buyback-growth]] ties that position to ecosystem lock-in and buybacks that have cut the share count by 31.5% over a decade.

## Buybacks as a Valuation Signal

Berkshire's buybacks carry information because they are rule-bound. The filing language described in [[berkshire-q2-2026-alphabet-delta-record-buybacks-abel]] allows repurchases only when the CEO judges the price below conservatively estimated intrinsic value, so the average Class B prices of about $476 in May and $488 in June show where management saw value. The earlier preview note records that UBS estimated roughly $8.5 billion of buybacks across mid-April to mid-July and quotes Abel saying he will keep buying while intrinsic value exceeds market value.

## Backing the Buyer of Cheap Software

Berkshire has also moved into a newer kind of exposure. According to [[bending-spoons-berkshire-software-consolidator-saaspocalypse]], it added a $330 million stake in Bending Spoons, taking its total above $900 million, in a company that buys software businesses sold off by the AI boom and holds them for profit rather than resale. The note presents this as a Buffett-style model applied to technology, with the unresolved risk that growth has come almost entirely from acquisitions while AI squeezes software margins.

## Temperament and Discipline

The behavioural lessons drawn from Buffett's career remain the frame for reading these moves. [[buffett-30-year-lessons-temperament-enough-business-owner]] argues that emotional control and an owner's perspective, not intellect, separate good investors, while [[buffett-stock-market-church-casino-gambling-mentality]] records his warning of a speculative "casino" mood alongside the advice to keep a sustainable buy-and-hold approach rather than time the market.
