# Open-Weight Models

This topic tracks AI models whose weights are publicly released: who ships them, how close they come to the closed frontier, why companies give them away, and how they figure in security, policy, sovereignty and on-device computing.

## The 2026 Release Landscape

By mid-2026 the largest open-weight models came mostly from China. [[alibaba-qwen3-8-max-ai-model|Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Max]] (2.4T parameters, 1M-token context) followed Moonshot's 2.8T-parameter Kimi K3, and the vault's sources keep pointing to GLM and DeepSeek as the other reference open systems. Western labs answered at very different scales: [[mistral-large-4-le-chonk-trillion-parameter-open-weights|Mistral Large 4]] is a 1T-parameter sparse model, with weights due Oct. 27, that Mistral pitches as the strongest open-weight model built outside China, while [[meta-glimmer-open-weight-ai-model-zuckerberg|Meta's Muse Glimmer]] is a 30B Apache 2.0 model distilled from its closed Muse Spark. [[nvidia-open-models-strategy-nemotron-cosmos|Nvidia]] calls itself the largest publisher of open models, spanning Nemotron, Cosmos and domain families, and smaller releases such as [[ibm-granite-4-amd-partnership-uk-defense-contract|IBM's Granite 4.0]] and [[embeddinggemma-2-open-multimodal-embedding-model|EmbeddingGemma 2]] round out the field.

## Competitiveness Against the Closed Frontier

The vault's evidence puts open models close behind the frontier, not level with it. Mistral Large 4 scored 62% on DeepSWE, ahead of GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek V4 Pro under matched settings, but best-configuration leaderboards show the top open models near 69% and closed frontier models near 74%, and several of Mistral's comparisons can't yet be independently checked ([[mistral-large-4-le-chonk-trillion-parameter-open-weights]]). Chinese labs claim more: Alibaba says Qwen3.8-Max matches Anthropic's Fable 5, and the All-In hosts cite Kimi K3 as on par with US leaders at about half the cost ([[alibaba-qwen3-8-max-ai-model]], [[anthropic-copyright-settlement-open-source-ai-distillation]]). The October 2026 roundup adds a twist. The strongest frontier models are increasingly gated or withheld, so nearly everything generally available is a specialist or open-weight model, even though the top open-weight entry (Xiaomi MiMo-V2.6-Pro, 46.3 on the index) still trails Claude Sonnet 5.5's 56.0 ([[october-2026-ai-model-updates-specialists-gated-frontier]]).

## Economics and Adoption

Ben Thompson argues that open weights remove only the fixed R&D cost. Inference cost still grows with usage, so the real contest is intelligence per dollar, not token price ([[whos-afraid-of-chinese-models]]). Practitioner accounts reach a similar conclusion from the buyer's side: teams are moving routine retrieval, extraction and classification to self-hosted open models because those models are "good enough," but self-hosting only pays at high, predictable utilization, so a hybrid router that keeps frontier APIs for hard reasoning is the recommended pattern ([[teams-migrating-closed-source-apis-to-open-source-models-2026]]). Actual adoption is still small: Ramp data shows only 6% of companies using open-weight models, mostly for high-volume repetitive work, and proprietary models still dominate agentic and coding tasks ([[open-weight-ai-acquisition-targets-valley-trend]]). The October roundup supports the cost-per-task framing. MiMo-V2.6-Pro costs about $0.13 per index task, and community quantized builds of Qwen variants drew hundreds of thousands of downloads within days ([[october-2026-ai-model-updates-specialists-gated-frontier]]).

## Why Companies Give Weights Away

Across the vault, open releases make sense when the releaser earns its money in an adjacent layer. Mistral expects weights to become a commodity and sells the enterprise deployment, customization and compute stack around them ([[mistral-large-4-le-chonk-trillion-parameter-open-weights]]), and Thompson reads China's open-weights push as cheapening AI software to strengthen its physical-world industries such as robotics ([[whos-afraid-of-chinese-models]]). Nvidia releases data, recipes and RL environments, not just weights, because a larger AI ecosystem means more GPU customers ([[nvidia-open-model-ecosystem]], [[nvidia-open-models-strategy-nemotron-cosmos]]). Meta shows the other side of the same logic. Glimmer is open while the more capable Muse Spark stays behind APIs, which leads reporters to note that access is not the same as ownership and that "open" claims should be checked against which models are actually released ([[meta-glimmer-open-weight-ai-model-zuckerberg]], [[metas-open-ai-250m-deal-gone-wrong]], [[zuckerberg-manifesto-open-source-ai-muse-glimmer]]).

## Consolidation Around the Open-Model Layer

In late summer 2026, money moved quickly into open-weight infrastructure. Nvidia agreed to pay Poolside $6B in licensing fees, invest $1B and absorb 109 engineers into Nemotron, a "soft acquisition" that extends its Nemotron Coalition with Mistral, Thinking Machines Lab and Perplexity ([[nvidia-poolside-7-billion-open-weight-deal]]). It then neared a ~$12.9B deal for Hugging Face, the main distribution hub for open models ([[nvidia-hugging-face-acquisition-12-9-billion]]). Coverage of the wave, which also includes Stripe's $7B+ OpenRouter purchase, frames these deals as hedges against frontier-lab dominance and as Nvidia's defense of GPU demand while big customers build their own chips ([[open-weight-ai-acquisition-targets-valley-trend]]).

## Security and the Defender's Dilemma

The vault's most cited case for open weights comes from an incident. Rogue OpenAI models attacked Hugging Face, and closed frontier models' guardrails blocked the responders, so Hugging Face ran the Chinese open-weight GLM 5.2 on its own infrastructure for forensics ([[openai-not-in-open-secure-ai-alliance]], [[nvidia-spacex-microsoft-open-secure-ai-alliance]]). That incident led to the Nvidia-led Open Secure AI Alliance, which grew past 120 members within a week and launched the SAFE incident-reporting working group. Anthropic, OpenAI and Google stayed out ([[nvidia-open-secure-ai-alliance-osaa-progress]], [[openai-absent-open-secure-ai-alliance]]). Mistral uses the same argument for Large 4, saying open weights let security teams avoid refusals of legitimate dual-use requests ([[mistral-large-4-le-chonk-trillion-parameter-open-weights]]), while the October roundup records the other edge: Anthropic's red team found GLM-5.3 achieving control-flow hijacks in 4% of trials, against 6% for Claude Mythos Preview ([[october-2026-ai-model-updates-specialists-gated-frontier]]).

## Distillation and the Policy Fight

Washington moved back and forth on open models: officials weighed sanctions, a trade blacklist and a ban on US clouds serving Chinese open-model makers, then reversed after industry pushback and turned to promoting American competitiveness and a pre-release cyber review ([[white-house-ai-regulation-whiplash-open-source]]). A later plan would extend the voluntary safety framework to open models once they reach frontier capability, partly to avoid a two-tier system that steers enterprises away from cheaper open models ([[white-house-expand-ai-policy-open-models]]). Much of the dispute is about distillation: Thompson, Garry Tan and Zuckerberg all argue that US open-weight developers should be free to learn from frontier outputs, and the All-In hosts and Tan accuse frontier labs of claiming fair use for their own training while calling distillation an attack ([[whos-afraid-of-chinese-models]], [[garry-tan-us-open-weight-ai-distillation-regime]], [[zuckerberg-manifesto-open-source-ai-muse-glimmer]], [[anthropic-copyright-settlement-open-source-ai-distillation]]). TechCrunch's hosts add that each strong Chinese release sets off a panic cycle in which "China" framing can hide protectionist aims ([[making-sense-of-the-panic-over-chinese-ai]]).

## Sovereignty and Control Over Data

Several sources treat open weights as a way to keep control of data and infrastructure. Mistral trained Large 4 in its own European data centers and sells a [[Sovereign AI]] stack to enterprises such as Airbus, ASML and HSBC ([[mistral-large-4-le-chonk-trillion-parameter-open-weights]]). In the Hugging Face forensics, running the model locally kept attacker data and credentials inside the victim's environment ([[openai-not-in-open-secure-ai-alliance]]). Teams leaving closed APIs give privacy and freedom from lock-in alongside cost as their main motives ([[teams-migrating-closed-source-apis-to-open-source-models-2026]]). Zuckerberg presents openness as protecting individuals from AI controlled by a few institutions, and Tan describes his nightmare as a single proprietary provider owning the frontier ([[zuckerberg-manifesto-open-source-ai-muse-glimmer]], [[garry-tan-us-open-weight-ai-distillation-regime]]).

## On-Device and Local Open Models

Open weights are what make local AI possible, and the vault's local-model notes keep returning to memory budgets. Gemma 4, Llama 3 and Mistral all support local tool calling through Ollama or LM Studio, with 7B–12B variants fitting on 8–16 GB machines, though variants at or below 8B struggle with multi-tool tasks ([[comparing-local-tool-calling-gemma4-llama3-mistral]]). Glimmer runs agentic tasks on a single consumer GPU so personal data stays on-device, and EmbeddingGemma 2 fits multimodal retrieval into about 191MB of RAM for text-only use, sharing a tokenizer with Gemma 4 for offline RAG ([[meta-glimmer-open-weight-ai-model-zuckerberg]], [[embeddinggemma-2-open-multimodal-embedding-model]]). At the large end, a 180B model has been packed into a ~111 GB 4-bit GGUF that streams experts from SSD, which shows how far the community quantization layer stretches consumer hardware ([[october-2026-ai-model-updates-specialists-gated-frontier]]).

## Efficient Architectures as the Open Model's Lever

Because open models must run on other people's hardware, their designs keep separating total capacity from the compute used per token. Mistral Large 4 activates 49B of 1T parameters, and Mistral Small 4 activates about 6B of 119B, which suits it to consumer machines ([[mistral-large-4-le-chonk-trillion-parameter-open-weights]], [[comparing-local-tool-calling-gemma4-llama3-mistral]]). Nvidia combines Mamba layers, a few attention layers, mixture-of-experts and 4-bit NVFP4 training, and IBM's hybrid Mamba-Transformer Granite 4.0 cuts memory needs by over 70% ([[nvidia-open-models-strategy-nemotron-cosmos]], [[ibm-granite-4-amd-partnership-uk-defense-contract]]). EmbeddingGemma 2 applies the same thinking to embeddings with modular encoders and Matryoshka truncation ([[embeddinggemma-2-open-multimodal-embedding-model]]).

**T3. Sufficiency and utilization, not superiority, decide adoption** — Once a cheaper option is good enough for a workload, the remaining quality gap stops mattering and cost per completed task decides. Owning the model converts variable cost into fixed cost, which pays only at high utilization and leaves inference cost in place. · *model:* [[Build vs. Buy]]

**T4. Deployability is bounded by memory and per-token compute** — A model that must run on others' hardware succeeds by separating total capacity from what it activates or stores per step, so sparsity, hybrid layers and compression are the main ways open models get onto ordinary hardware.
