# Alphabet

This topic follows Alphabet (GOOGL and GOOG) as a holding, covering its stock behavior, Google Cloud and the Anthropic TPU backlog, the capex and funding required to build AI capacity, Gemini as a business driver, the advertising base that still pays for most of it, and the legal and valuation questions that frame the position going into its Q3 report on October 28.

## Google Cloud as the Growth Engine
Cloud is where Alphabet's AI investment shows up in reported revenue. Cloud growth accelerated from 63% in Q1 to 82% in Q2, when revenue reached $24.8B ([[googl-q3-2026-earnings-preview-cloud-vs-capex]], [[alphabet-googl-vs-msft-amzn-ai-cloud-26-percent-upside]]), and the company has now posted 12 straight quarters of double-digit total revenue growth ([[googl-q3-2026-earnings-preview-cloud-vs-capex]]). Cloud margin rose to 35.6% from 20.7% over the same stretch, which the preview note treats as the clearest sign that scale is paying off ([[googl-q3-2026-earnings-preview-cloud-vs-capex]]). Enterprise uptake is broad: nearly 90% of the Fortune 100 uses Gemini Enterprise ([[alphabet-q2-2026-earnings-cloud-surge]]), and BMO estimates about 80% of Cloud customers now use Google AI ([[analyst-ai-moves-alphabet-gemini-agent-marvell-oct-2026]]). Alphabet also gains distribution as a host, since OpenAI's models and Codex are coming to Google Cloud, a move announced on October 21 ([[openai-models-google-cloud-enterprise-channel-partners]]). Whether Q3 keeps the pace is the open question. A 100% Cloud growth outcome would require Cloud revenue above $30.3B, and the prediction note argues that half of the backlog converts within 24 months, which makes the pace visible but not guaranteed ([[alphabet-google-cloud-q3-2026-100-percent-growth-prediction]]).

## Capex, Free Cash Flow and Funding
The investment is large enough to strain the cash flow statement. Guidance for 2026 capex is $195–205B in most notes ([[alphabet-amazon-meta-nvidia-ai-capex-500-billion-2026]], [[alphabet-googl-triangle-breakdown-q3-earnings-capex-test-oct2026]]), although the 247wallst scenario note cites $175–185B, so the range should be read as a source-level disagreement rather than settled guidance ([[alphabet-10000-double-2031-scenarios-cloud-backlog]]). In Q2, capex of $44.9B ran ahead of operating cash flow of $39.1B, leaving free cash flow at -$5.9B, which the triangle note calls the first negative quarter ([[alphabet-googl-triangle-breakdown-q3-earnings-capex-test-oct2026]]); the Berkshire note puts the figure at -$5.8B ([[berkshire-hathaway-alphabet-new-top-holding-greg-abel]]). Trailing twelve-month free cash flow was $53B against $132B of capex, and the broader hyperscaler spend of more than $700B is the context for that gap ([[alphabet-ad-antitrust-ruling-googl-buy-2026]], [[alphabet-amazon-meta-nvidia-ai-capex-500-billion-2026]]). The funding has come from outside the business. Alphabet raised $49.6B through June stock sales and $20.3B in notes ([[alphabet-supply-constrained-anthropic-5gw-tpu-deal]]). Reports of the equity total vary: about $80B including $10B from Berkshire ([[alphabet-googl-triangle-breakdown-q3-earnings-capex-test-oct2026]]), and $85B in a Thompson analysis of AI financing ([[nvidias-risky-business-ai-infrastructure-financing]]). Long-term debt is cited at $98.2B after rising from $46.5B in six months ([[alphabet-googl-triangle-breakdown-q3-earnings-capex-test-oct2026]]), while the 247wallst note describes a move from $16B to about $100B ([[alphabet-10000-double-2031-scenarios-cloud-backlog]]). The bond market has noticed: Alphabet's August bond needed a 10–15 basis point concession ([[big-tech-ai-bond-splurge-crowding-out-weak-borrowers]]), and hyperscalers raised $194B of debt in 2026 ([[nvidias-risky-business-ai-infrastructure-financing]]). Thompson draws a parallel to the 1870s railroad-bond financing that ended in the Panic of 1873, arguing that hyperscalers are moving to progressively riskier capital as they shift from cash flow to debt to equity ([[nvidias-risky-business-ai-infrastructure-financing]]). Alphabet's cash position still supports the balance sheet, with net cash of $144.3B ([[alphabet-googl-vs-msft-amzn-ai-cloud-26-percent-upside]]).

## Backlog and the Anthropic TPU Concentration
The Cloud backlog grew from $462B to $514B in the latest quarter, and the largest single source is Anthropic ([[alphabet-supply-constrained-anthropic-5gw-tpu-deal]]). The company's relationship with Anthropic has two stages. The October 2025 deal covered 1 million TPUs and more than 1 GW, and the April 2026 expansion covers about 5 GW from 2027 through Google and Broadcom ([[alphabet-supply-constrained-anthropic-5gw-tpu-deal]]). Google has invested up to $40B in Anthropic, and Anthropic's annualized revenue has passed $30B ([[alphabet-supply-constrained-anthropic-5gw-tpu-deal]]). Thompson estimates that more than 20% of Google TPU shipments from 3Q26 through 4Q27 will go to Anthropic ([[nvidias-risky-business-ai-infrastructure-financing]]). Pichai describes supply as the constraint, saying Google is "supply constrained" ([[alphabet-supply-constrained-anthropic-5gw-tpu-deal]]). That concentration cuts both ways: Broadcom's filing flags its own dependence on Anthropic's success ([[alphabet-supply-constrained-anthropic-5gw-tpu-deal]]). The same backlog is also a pressure point. The prediction note says neocloud capacity leased to meet demand weighs on margins, and TPU inventory has grown to $10B from $2.4B ([[alphabet-google-cloud-q3-2026-100-percent-growth-prediction]]). A separate report describes a reported SpaceX/xAI compute lease at about $920M a month through 2029, roughly $11B a year for about 110,000 GPUs, and Pichai called DeepMind's compute "non-negotiable" when the stock fell 3.15% on October 22 ([[googl-falls-3-percent-324-tech-selloff-oct22-2026]]).

## Gemini and AI Products as Business Drivers
Gemini's usage is now large, but monetization is the test. The Gemini app passed 1 billion monthly users on August 11, and AI Mode has also passed 1 billion ([[google-gemini-app-one-billion-users]]). From October 9 the free tier was reduced to Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite only, with paid tiers at $4.99 (AI Plus), $19.99 (Pro) and $99.99 (Ultra), a change that reads as a move from user acquisition to monetization ([[google-gemini-free-tier-flash-lite-only-oct-2026]]). The broader lesson is that AI tools alone do not yet produce clear revenue at Google or Meta, and investors want proof that they do ([[big-tech-ai-earnings-three-lessons-chatbots-demand]]). BMO named Alphabet a Top Pick on Gemini Agent and its full-stack AI position, citing a 98% drop in cost per token ([[analyst-ai-moves-alphabet-gemini-agent-marvell-oct-2026]]), and Wedbush listed Alphabet among its Best Ideas, noting Reddit data licensing worth about $60M a year ([[analyst-ai-top-picks-q2-earnings-2026]]). Waymo is the other bet: about 500,000 paid rides a week across 15 cities with roughly 4,000 vehicles ([[waymo-scaling-fleet-data-2026]]), and the antitrust note says Waymo revenue could start next year ([[alphabet-ad-antitrust-ruling-googl-buy-2026]]). Leadership is a risk in its own right. In August Demis Hassabis became Chair and Chief Scientist, Koray Kavukcuoglu became SVP of DeepMind, and Jeff Dean and Sanjay Ghemawat left to found Discovery Loop, a change the note flags as key-person risk ([[google-deepmind-leadership-shakeup-discovery-loop]]).

## Advertising Dependence, Antitrust and Legal Risk
Advertising still funds most of the company. The Q2 figures put ads at 68% of revenue, down from 74% a year earlier and 89% a decade ago, with Cloud at 21% ([[alphabet-ad-antitrust-ruling-googl-buy-2026]]), while the other note in the set says advertising is above 70% of revenue ([[alphabet-googl-vs-msft-amzn-ai-cloud-26-percent-upside]]). Search holds more than 90% share, which is the source of the ad-data flywheel that supports Alphabet's AI monetization ([[alphabet-cheapest-magnificent-seven-ai-winner]], [[ai-monetization-nvidia-microsoft-alphabet-palantir]]). That dominance is also the legal exposure. A judge rejected the DOJ's request to divest the ad exchange business ([[alphabet-ad-antitrust-ruling-googl-buy-2026]]), but the matter is not closed. A securities class action, Stewart v. Alphabet (N.D. Cal., No. 26-cv-11290), concerns the July 16 drop of 4.4%, from $370.92 to $354.46, after Bloomberg reported that Gemini 3.5 Pro was months behind, and the lead plaintiff deadline is December 1 ([[alphabet-securities-fraud-class-action-gemini-3-5-pro-delay]]). Reddit's own shares fell 23–24% on choppy Google referral traffic while Alphabet rose 6%, a reminder that Alphabet's search reach also drives traffic to others ([[alphabet-reddit-social-media-stock-divergence]]).

## Valuation, Analyst Views and Berkshire
The valuation estimates diverge widely because they use different earnings and cash flow bases. Forward P/E figures in the notes run from about 15x ([[alphabet-cheapest-magnificent-seven-ai-winner]]) and 16x ([[berkshire-hathaway-alphabet-new-top-holding-greg-abel]]) to 17x ([[googl-q3-2026-earnings-preview-cloud-vs-capex]]), 22.83x against Microsoft's 25.13x and Amazon's 23.64x ([[alphabet-googl-vs-msft-amzn-ai-cloud-26-percent-upside]]), about 24x ([[alphabet-googl-triangle-breakdown-q3-earnings-capex-test-oct2026]]) and 26x ([[alphabet-google-cloud-q3-2026-100-percent-growth-prediction]]). On trailing free cash flow, the comparison is less generous: Alphabet trades at about 80x against Microsoft's 59x, and a $98B unrealized gain flatters reported earnings ([[microsoft-vs-alphabet-fcf-valuation-ai-cloud]]). Q2 EPS of $9.11 included a $99.0B unrealized gain ([[alphabet-googl-triangle-breakdown-q3-earnings-capex-test-oct2026]]), and the sp500 comparison note says GAAP EPS rose about 90% on the Anthropic and SpaceX gains ([[sp500-earnings-growth-pace-1994-1999-comparison]]). Berkshire is the most visible institutional buyer. It holds about 79M shares worth about $28.2B, or 9.41% of its portfolio, and Greg Abel added 36M shares in Q1 and 24.5M in Q2 ([[berkshire-hathaway-alphabet-new-top-holding-greg-abel]]). Berkshire's CFO Ashkenazi nevertheless expects free cash flow to stay under pressure ([[berkshire-hathaway-alphabet-new-top-holding-greg-abel]]). Analyst targets are mixed: the average target is $430.51 ([[alphabet-googl-triangle-breakdown-q3-earnings-capex-test-oct2026]]), KeyBanc cut its target to $410 from $435, JPMorgan raised to $425 from $420, and UBS stays Neutral at $380 ([[alphabet-up-1-7-gemini-4-carbon-analyst-targets-oct-2026]]). Fund ownership rose to 275 long funds from 265 in the same window ([[alphabet-googl-vs-msft-amzn-ai-cloud-26-percent-upside]]).

## Stock Behavior and the October 28 Setup
The stock has moved with the AI trade rather than apart from it. It fell in August when investors questioned AI-infrastructure fundraising, dropping about 3.6% on August 11 ([[us-iran-impasse-alphabet-stock-sinks-aug2026]]). In October it closed at $324.22 on October 22, about 21% below its 52-week high of $408.61 ([[alphabet-googl-triangle-breakdown-q3-earnings-capex-test-oct2026]], [[googl-falls-3-percent-324-tech-selloff-oct22-2026]]). On October 22 it fell 3.15% against a 2.60% Nasdaq decline, in a session where Meta fell 6.98%, Amazon 4.57% and Microsoft 2.23% ([[googl-falls-3-percent-324-tech-selloff-oct22-2026]]). The technical levels the triangle note cites are support at $315.29 and $297.45 and resistance at $335.72 ([[alphabet-googl-triangle-breakdown-q3-earnings-capex-test-oct2026]]). A later session lifted the stock 1.7% ([[alphabet-up-1-7-gemini-4-carbon-analyst-targets-oct-2026]]). Consensus for Q3 is revenue of about $127.4–127.8B and EPS of $2.94–3.04 ([[alphabet-googl-triangle-breakdown-q3-earnings-capex-test-oct2026]]). Alphabet and Microsoft report on October 28, and the report will show whether Cloud growth and backlog justify the capex and debt that now frame the stock ([[sp500-eps-growth-q2-2027-dip-fomc-pce-hyperscalers-oct2026]]).

**T1. Capital spent ahead of revenue must be financed from outside the business, and the financing becomes part of the return** — When the build-out runs ahead of the cash it produces, the company borrows or sells equity, and bond spreads, dilution and debt service then determine what the capacity is worth. · *model:* [[Capital Cycle]]

**T2. Concentrated demand creates concentrated risk** — When a large share of revenue or backlog sits with one customer, one product, or one source of traffic, that customer's success or failure drives the whole outcome, so backlog gains come with a dependence that can turn quickly. · *model:* [[Concentration Risk]]

**T3. Scale can raise margins while capacity bought ahead of demand compresses them** *(implied)* — Fixed-cost infrastructure lifts margin once revenue outruns it, but capacity that is leased or bought before demand arrives adds cost first, so the same spending can both expand and erode margins depending on utilization. · *model:* [[Operating Leverage]]

**T4. Backlog and capex lead revenue, so the leading signals carry more weight than the lagging ones** — Capex and contracted backlog show what revenue will look like in later quarters, but they say little about timing or pricing, so they are useful for the trajectory and need to be checked against the revenue that follows. · *model:* [[Leading vs. Lagging Indicators]]

**T5. Scarce compute is allocated by what it is worth to each buyer, so every TPU or GPU sold elsewhere is a cost to someone** — When capacity is supply-constrained, dedicating it to one customer, or leasing it to another, means giving up what it could earn internally, so the allocation choices shape both growth and credibility. · *model:* [[Opportunity Cost]]
