# Mental Health

This topic tracks what the vault knows about depression, anxiety and PTSD: the exposures that raise risk, including early-life adversity and rising food costs, the hidden forms distress can take, and the treatments being tested, from exercise, heat and nature to personalized brain stimulation, psychedelic drugs and AI chatbots. The evidence mixes large observational cohorts, small trials and company-reported results, so most claims here are provisional. Diet gets a deeper treatment in [[diet-mental-health]], and youth-specific findings in [[child-adolescent-health]].

## Early-Life Adversity Leaves Lasting Marks

Several large studies point to childhood as the period when mental-health risk is most strongly shaped, though not in one uniform way. The UCLA-led ENIGMA mega-analysis of 3,711 people tied more severe abuse and neglect to structural brain differences that varied by maltreatment type, age and sex and were strongest in young women [[childhood-abuse-brain-structure-enigma-mega-analysis]]. Adversity is wider than trauma: in about 30,000 children, household unpredictability such as irregular routines and parental mood swings predicted depression better than a traditional adversity screen [[childhood-unpredictability-screening-mental-health]]. Early nutrition appears to matter too, since UK adults with less sugar exposure in their first 1,000 days had 11% lower depression and 20% lower anxiety risk, and because the 46% lower Alzheimer's figure rests on few cases, the psychiatric findings look more robust [[sugar-restriction-infancy-dementia-alzheimers-risk]].

## Separating Cause from Correlation

Several studies here are observational, and their conclusions shift once shared causes are held constant. Among more than 21,000 Swedish twins, genes and upbringing explained much of the link between heavy screen use and depression or anxiety, although adolescent interactive screen use still predicted later problems [[twin-study-screen-time-mental-health-family-factors]]. Detection and direction complicate the picture further: women with type 2 diabetes averaged 92 primary-care visits to men's 32, so recorded rates may partly reflect contact with services [[type-2-diabetes-mental-health-sex-differences]], while disrupted sleep may be a symptom of depression rather than its cause [[rem-sleep-lower-risk-83-diseases]]. The insomnia umbrella review makes the same point at population level, since its pooled risks describe groups and do not show that insomnia causes any individual's outcome [[insomnia-health-risks-review-sleep-habits-cbt]].

## Comorbidity in Chronic Illness

Depression and anxiety often arrive alongside physical illness. About one in three stroke survivors develops depression and up to a quarter develop anxiety, both of which impede rehabilitation, and the 2026 American Stroke Association guidelines now treat mental-health screening as standard care [[stroke-rehab-guidelines-2026-whole-person-recovery]]. Caregivers carry a similar burden, since a 2025 systematic review found nearly 44% of them showed depressive symptoms [[stroke-rehab-guidelines-2026-whole-person-recovery]]. In diabetes, women aged 16 to 39 had the highest probability of developing mental-health conditions, and experts called for routine psychological screening in follow-up care [[type-2-diabetes-mental-health-sex-differences]].

## Occupational and Perinatal Strain

Some groups face cumulative exposure that conventional services are not built for. First responders and military personnel meet occupational trauma that accumulates over routine calls and deployments rather than arriving as a single incident, and because their training teaches them to suppress distress, systems that rely on self-reporting fail them structurally rather than individually [[mental-health-first-responders-military-indiana-2026]]. In perinatal care, the IMPACT formative evaluation found that detection is not the bottleneck: over 90% of 72 surveyed home visiting programs already screen, yet fear of judgment and scheduling logistics deter engagement [[impact-trial-scaling-perinatal-mental-health-home-visiting-iowa-indiana]]. Indiana and Iowa needed different support priorities to scale a cognitive-behavioral program, which is why the team built its implementation package around local conditions [[impact-trial-scaling-perinatal-mental-health-home-visiting-iowa-indiana]].

## Discrimination as an Exposure

Discrimination carries measurable psychological costs. Among 17,942 LGBTQ+ college students, those who experienced discrimination reported 84% higher depression, 87% higher anxiety and 78% higher suicidal thoughts, and denial of health care carried the greatest risk [[lgbtq-college-students-discrimination-mental-health-risks]]. Transgender and nonbinary students reported higher anxiety and suicidality and were more likely to avoid care, so campus counseling and clinician training are the places where this distress can be identified and reduced [[lgbtq-college-students-discrimination-mental-health-risks]].

## Economic Strain and Food Insecurity

Everyday costs form another exposure. A Penn State analysis of Household Pulse Survey data linked rising grocery prices, which climbed more than 20% between 2020 and 2024, to higher food spending, greater risk of food insufficiency and worse anxiety across all household types [[grocery-price-hikes-food-insufficiency-mental-health-penn-state]]. Households sorted into four coping profiles, from minimal responders at 44% to resource-strained sacrificers at 8%, and the most drastic tactics, such as taking extra work, borrowing on credit and cutting utilities, appeared in the most strained group, which suggests that intensive coping signals existing vulnerability rather than protection [[grocery-price-hikes-food-insufficiency-mental-health-penn-state]]. Price-sensitive substituters may spend less yet report more anxiety, and the study suggests that bundled support across food assistance, rent relief and mental health resources may fit this overlapping hardship best [[grocery-price-hikes-food-insufficiency-mental-health-penn-state]].

## Hidden Depression and Anxiety as Habit

Distress does not always look like withdrawal. "Overfunctioning" describes depression hidden behind full calendars, where busyness stops giving stability and becomes a way to avoid internal experience, and a warning sign is that finishing a task brings little relief because another replaces it [[productivity-hides-depression-overfunctioning]]. Jud Brewer's account of anxiety as a learned habit follows the same loop: worry feels productive under uncertainty and the brain reinforces it, while under stress willpower is unreliable and curiosity about one's own experience is what breaks the cycle [[anxiety-as-learned-habit-brewer-browns-staff-day]]. Both accounts suggest the behavior survives because it relieves discomfort in the short term rather than because it helps, and stigma keeps it hidden, since 42% of surveyed employees fear that discussing mental health would hurt their careers [[productivity-hides-depression-overfunctioning]].

## Movement as a Transdiagnostic Treatment

Exercise evidence is moving from "it helps" toward explaining how and for whom it helps. In a secondary analysis of the 400-person ImPuls trial, reductions in perceived stress and repetitive negative thinking fully accounted for the program's benefit across depression, anxiety, PTSD and insomnia, though sleep quality did not significantly improve [[exercise-mental-health-stress-negative-thinking]]. Activity also interacts with inherited risk: in All of Us data, genetic risk was less strongly linked to depression severity among more active adults, which makes activity a possible buffer rather than a proven cure [[physical-activity-genetic-depression-risk-all-of-us]]. A network meta-analysis of 24 randomized trials in college students shows that the best option depends on the outcome, with Baduanjin and mindfulness-based exercise superior for depression, while no mind-body approach significantly improved well-being or sleep, a null result the authors call inconclusive [[mind-body-exercise-college-student-mental-health-network-meta-analysis]].

## Social and Traditional Movement Programs

Group and traditional formats show benefits too. An eight-week salsa program reduced PHQ-9 scores by a clinically meaningful 2.45 points versus a waitlist among 121 young adults, with the combination of movement, music and social contact offered as one reason for its appeal [[salsa-dancing-depression-young-adults]]. A meta-analysis of 18 trials linked the low-intensity Baduanjin to lower depressive symptoms and better sleep in older adults, though high heterogeneity across the trials calls for caution [[baduanjin-exercise-sleep-depression-older-adults-meta-analysis]].

## Sleep as Mediator and Risk Marker

Sleep keeps appearing as a link between mental-health symptoms and how people function. In early adolescence, sleep quality explained 36.3% of the association between anxiety and worse academic and social outcomes and 18.5% for depression, well ahead of screen time's 5 to 6.3% [[adolescent-sleep-academic-social-outcomes]]. In adults, irregular sleep timing went with anxiety and major depression and deep sleep with lower depression risk, although reverse causation means disease may also be disrupting sleep [[rem-sleep-lower-risk-83-diseases]]. The insomnia umbrella review describes the two-way loop, in which anxiety and depression disrupt sleep while poor sleep raises vulnerability to both [[insomnia-health-risks-review-sleep-habits-cbt]].

## Sleep as a Treatment Target

Sleep is also becoming a direct treatment target. The NEUSLeeP ultrasound patch, tested in a 28-person trial, brought REM onset 43 minutes earlier and raised heart-rate variability, and its developers plan trials in PTSD, depression and insomnia because REM is framed as central to emotional reset and stress adaptation [[wearable-ultrasound-patch-rem-sleep]]. For insomnia itself, cognitive behavioral therapy is described as the gold-standard treatment, alongside consistent schedules and fewer screens before bed, while trying harder to sleep tends to backfire [[insomnia-health-risks-review-sleep-habits-cbt]].

## Emerging Physiological Therapies

Heat therapy rests on the observation that depressed people run hot: in wearable data from about 20,000 people, worse depression went with higher skin temperature and less nightly cooling [[heat-therapy-sauna-mental-health-depression-research]]. In a small landmark trial, about two hours in a whole-body infrared machine that raised body temperature to 38.5°C halved symptoms within a week for most of the 30 patients, and the antidepressant effect lasted through six weeks [[heat-therapy-sauna-mental-health-depression-research]]. Pilots pairing eight weeks of CBT with four infrared sessions left most participants in remission, but they were not powered to prove efficacy, and sauna-goers' gains may reflect nature, digital detox or socializing rather than heat [[heat-therapy-sauna-mental-health-depression-research]].

## Brain Stimulation Moves Toward Personalization

Brain stimulation is shifting from standard protocols toward individualized targeting. Temporal interference stimulation, delivered through weak scalp currents, reached the ventral striatum, the reward region tied to anhedonia, and a single 20-minute session paired with mindful breathing increased reward-related activity and reduced anxiety for about a week, though the technique remains investigational [[temporal-interference-stimulation-reward-anxiety-emory]]. Wave Neuroscience's MeRT pairs EEG measurement with transcranial magnetic stimulation and an AI algorithm to tailor each protocol, and it has FDA clearance for PTSD, with company-reported results of 52% symptom reduction and 68% remission [[meert-personalized-brain-stimulation-ptsd-fda-clearance]]. Those figures are hard to compare with medication, because the 10.9% maximum remission rate for medication comes from a 2020 review of different studies, full trial data are not yet public, and insurance coverage and affordability are unresolved [[meert-personalized-brain-stimulation-ptsd-fda-clearance]].

## Psychedelic Drugs and the Trial Problem

Pharmaceutical interest in psychedelics has grown since the FDA rejected Lykos Therapeutics' MDMA therapy for PTSD in 2024, with Eli Lilly's plan to acquire AtaiBeckley for up to $3.8 billion targeting treatment-resistant depression and AbbVie's $1.2 billion 2025 deal for the short-acting psychedelic bretisilocin [[pharma-billion-dollar-bets-psychedelic-drugs-after-mdma-setback]]. Compass Pathways reported positive results from two late-stage psilocybin trials, and Definium's LSD pill significantly reduced generalized anxiety symptoms in late-stage studies [[pharma-billion-dollar-bets-psychedelic-drugs-after-mdma-setback]]. The MDMA rejection shows why these results are hard to interpret: the FDA flagged functional unblinding, where participants and researchers guess who received the active drug, and the difficulty of separating MDMA's effect from the psychotherapy delivered with it [[pharma-billion-dollar-bets-psychedelic-drugs-after-mdma-setback]]. Developers now aim for shorter-acting drugs with fewer hallucinations, though the extent of adverse effects and the duration of benefit remain unknown [[pharma-billion-dollar-bets-psychedelic-drugs-after-mdma-setback]].

## Nature Exposure and Restoration

Time in nature shows measurable benefits, and the amount of exposure matters. A University of Hong Kong meta-analysis of research from 1980 to 2025 found a nonlinear dose-response, with benefits rising steeply at low weekly exposure and tapering as visits grow more frequent or longer, so one hour or one visit a week reaches about half the modeled maximum benefit, a share of benefit rather than a change in symptoms [[weekly-dose-of-nature-benchmarks-mental-health-hku-meta-analysis]]. A University of New Hampshire study of College Woods visitors found that visits improved mood and raised self-reported restoration by nearly 30%, and perceived biodiversity predicted well-being better than measured bird and mammal counts did [[perceived-biodiversity-nature-walk-mental-wellbeing-unh-study]]. Educational signs modestly raised perceived biodiversity and positive mood without a clear effect on restoration [[perceived-biodiversity-nature-walk-mental-wellbeing-unh-study]], and the heat research cautions that mood gains attributed to sauna use may come from nature exposure rather than from heat [[heat-therapy-sauna-mental-health-depression-research]].

## Diet as Risk and Treatment

Diet is moving from a correlate toward an intervention for depression. The SMILES trial found 32% remission with Mediterranean-style dietary counseling versus 8% with social support, with inflammation and gut-derived serotonin proposed as mechanisms [[nutrition-and-mental-health-link]]. In a 20-person UCSF crossover pilot, cutting ultra-processed food intake by more than 85% produced moderate-to-large improvements on three depression measures, yet the design cannot show whether removing poor food or adding good food drove the effect, since there was no healthy-diet comparison arm, participants knew their diet, and the gut-inflammation mechanism was never measured [[cutting-ultra-processed-food-depression-pilot-caveats-mindgrow]]. Restriction can backfire, since calorie-restricted dieters in a cross-sectional study of 28,525 US adults reported more depressive symptoms, possibly because of B vitamin, iron and omega-3 deficits [[low-calorie-diets-may-increase-depression]].

## Gut Barrier and Brain Nutrients

The mechanisms behind diet links run through the gut and the supply of micronutrients. Tim Spector describes gut microbes whose outputs can drive inflammation, and argues that chronic low-grade inflammation can push the brain into a recovery state that lowers motivation for social contact and activity [[tim-spector-nutrition-mental-health-gut-brain]]. A UK Biobank study of 181,990 people linked dietary nutrients to brain structure, and low-fibre eaters had less tissue in 11 brain regions, consistent with fibre-starved microbes degrading the gut's mucus layer so that inflammation can reach the brain [[three-diet-changes-boost-brain-mental-health-fibre-micronutrients]]. Fibre-derived short-chain fatty acids support both the gut barrier and the blood-brain barrier [[three-diet-changes-boost-brain-mental-health-fibre-micronutrients]], a leaky-gut path the UCSF pilot proposed for ultra-processed food but never measured [[cutting-ultra-processed-food-depression-pilot-caveats-mindgrow]]. Spector's estimate that better diet could cut dementia incidence by about 50% comes from a popular account rather than a trial, and should be read that way [[tim-spector-nutrition-mental-health-gut-brain]].

## Fruit and Sugary Drinks

Beverages add a sharper signal to the diet picture. In a three-year cohort of 6,537 Australian adolescents, each extra daily serving of fruit was linked to 13% lower depression and 7% lower anxiety risk, while sugar-sweetened beverages raised risk and vegetables showed no significant association [[fruit-sugary-drinks-adolescent-anxiety-depression-australia-study]]. Those links rest on observational design and self-reported intake, so the study cannot show that changing diet prevents disorders [[fruit-sugary-drinks-adolescent-anxiety-depression-australia-study]]. In adults, a Newcastle trial of 42 people found that adding a daily glass of 100% fruit juice or smoothie to a 5-a-day intervention lowered PHQ-9 scores by 2.52 points over four weeks without adverse metabolic effects, though the trial was small, short and funded by the Fruit Juice Science Centre [[fruit-juice-lower-depression-scores]].

## AI Chatbots and the Treatment Gap

AI tools are being pitched at a large unmet need, since about 25% of people worldwide experience a mental-health condition and about 85% receive no adequate care [[ai-mental-health-chatbots-empathetic-safety-lags]]. A scoping review of 21 studies found that LLM chatbots, mostly built on CBT, score well on satisfaction and perceived empathy, yet engagement declines over multi-week use, empathy does not reliably produce better clinical outcomes, and crisis handling remains a critical failure mode [[ai-mental-health-chatbots-safety-evidence-review]]. Georgia Tech researchers see promise in AI that flags behavioral change before a crisis, but warn that heavier AI interaction may deepen isolation [[ai-healthcare-revolution-diagnosis-wellness]]. The 2026 state bans on standalone AI therapy chatbots, which keep complex trauma care with licensed professionals, reflect this evidence gap [[mental-health-first-responders-military-indiana-2026]] [[ai-mental-health-chatbots-safety-evidence-review]].

## A Field of Promising but Preliminary Evidence

Many of the most striking findings come from very small or early studies, so the vault holds them as hypotheses rather than settled results. The heat-therapy RCT had 30 participants, the UCSF diet pilot had 20, and the fruit-juice trial had 42 and was funded by the Fruit Juice Science Centre [[heat-therapy-sauna-mental-health-depression-research]] [[ultra-processed-food-reduction-depression-ucsf-pilot]] [[fruit-juice-lower-depression-scores]]. The nature dose curve is built from population averages in observational studies, the MeRT figures are company-reported, temporal interference stimulation is still investigational, and the college mind-body meta-analysis was read only through its abstract, so its null results may reflect limited evidence rather than no effect [[weekly-dose-of-nature-benchmarks-mental-health-hku-meta-analysis]] [[meert-personalized-brain-stimulation-ptsd-fda-clearance]] [[temporal-interference-stimulation-reward-anxiety-emory]] [[mind-body-exercise-college-student-mental-health-network-meta-analysis]]. The better-established findings rest on larger designs, such as the 400-person ImPuls trial and a 2026 meta-analysis of 105 behavioral-activation trials with benefits lasting 12 months [[exercise-mental-health-stress-negative-thinking]] [[productivity-hides-depression-overfunctioning]].
