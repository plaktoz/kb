# Mental Health

This topic tracks what the vault knows about depression, anxiety and PTSD: the exposures that raise risk, the hidden forms distress can take, and the treatments being tested, from exercise, heat and nature to personalized brain stimulation, psychedelic drugs and AI chatbots. The evidence mixes large observational cohorts, small trials and company-reported results, so most claims here are provisional. Diet gets a deeper treatment in [[diet-mental-health]], and youth-specific findings in [[child-adolescent-health]].

## Early-Life Adversity Leaves Lasting Marks

Several large studies point to childhood as the period when mental-health risk is most strongly shaped, though not in one uniform way. The UCLA-led ENIGMA mega-analysis of 3,711 people linked more severe abuse and neglect to a "neurobiological scar" of structural deviations, with some regions thinning and others thickening, varying by maltreatment type, age and sex and strongest in young women [[childhood-abuse-brain-structure-enigma-mega-analysis]]. Adversity is wider than trauma: in about 30,000 children, household unpredictability such as irregular routines and parental mood swings predicted depression better than a traditional adversity screen [[childhood-unpredictability-screening-mental-health]]. Early nutrition appears to count as well, since UK adults with less sugar in their first 1,000 days showed lower depression and anxiety risk, and a later analysis of more than 46,000 people linked longer rationing exposure from the womb to age two with lower anxiety [[sugar-restriction-infancy-dementia-alzheimers-risk]].

## Separating Cause from Correlation

Several of the studies here are observational, and their results shift once shared causes are controlled. In a Karolinska study of more than 21,000 Swedish twins, genes and upbringing explained much of the link between heavy screen use and depression or anxiety, although interactive use in adolescence still predicted later problems [[twin-study-screen-time-mental-health-family-factors]]. Detection is a confounder too: women with type 2 diabetes developed more anxiety and depression than men but averaged 92 primary-care visits to men's 32, so recorded rates may partly reflect contact with services [[type-2-diabetes-mental-health-sex-differences]]. Direction matters as well, since the UK Biobank sleep study warns that disrupted sleep may be a symptom of depression rather than its cause [[rem-sleep-lower-risk-83-diseases]]. Digital detox is similarly unresolved rather than disproven, because a scoping review of 45 studies found little long-term data and only one sample averaging under 16 years old [[social-media-detox-research-youth-mental-health-unresolved-review]].

## Comorbidity in Chronic Illness

Depression and anxiety often arrive alongside physical illness. About one in three stroke survivors develops depression and up to a quarter develop anxiety, both of which slow rehabilitation, and nearly 44% of stroke caregivers show depressive symptoms, which is why the 2026 guidelines now treat mental-health screening as standard [[stroke-rehab-guidelines-2026-whole-person-recovery]]. In diabetes, women aged 16 to 39 had the highest probability of developing mental-health conditions, and experts called for routine psychological screening in follow-up care [[type-2-diabetes-mental-health-sex-differences]].

## Occupational and Perinatal Strain

Some groups face cumulative exposure that conventional services are not built for. First responders and military personnel meet occupational trauma that differs from a single-incident PTSD, and because their training teaches them to suppress distress, systems that rely on self-reporting fail them structurally rather than individually [[mental-health-first-responders-military-indiana-2026]]. In perinatal care, the IMPACT formative evaluation found that detection is not the bottleneck, since over 90% of 72 surveyed home visiting programs already screen, yet fear of judgment and scheduling logistics deter engagement, and Indiana and Iowa needed different support priorities to scale a cognitive-behavioral program [[impact-trial-scaling-perinatal-mental-health-home-visiting-iowa-indiana]].

## Discrimination as an Exposure

Discrimination carries measurable psychological costs. Among 17,942 LGBTQ+ college students, those who experienced discrimination reported 84% higher depression, 87% higher anxiety and 78% higher suicidal thoughts, and denial of health care carried the greatest risk [[lgbtq-college-students-discrimination-mental-health-risks]]. Transgender and nonbinary students reported higher anxiety and suicidality and were more likely to avoid care, and the study points to campus counseling and clinician training as the places where this distress can be identified and addressed [[lgbtq-college-students-discrimination-mental-health-risks]].

## Hidden Depression and Anxiety as Habit

Distress does not always look like withdrawal. "Overfunctioning" describes depression hidden behind full calendars, where busyness stops giving stability and becomes a way to avoid internal experience, and a warning sign is that finishing a task brings little relief because another replaces it [[productivity-hides-depression-overfunctioning]]. Jud Brewer's account of anxiety as a learned habit follows the same loop, since worry feels productive under uncertainty and the brain reinforces it, while under stress willpower is unreliable and curiosity-driven awareness is what breaks the cycle [[anxiety-as-learned-habit-brewer-browns-staff-day]]. Both accounts suggest the behavior survives because it relieves discomfort in the short term rather than because it helps, and stigma keeps it hidden, since 42% of surveyed employees fear that discussing mental health would hurt their careers [[productivity-hides-depression-overfunctioning]].

## Movement as a Transdiagnostic Treatment

Exercise evidence is moving from "it helps" toward explaining how and for whom it helps. In a secondary analysis of the 400-person ImPuls trial, reductions in perceived stress and repetitive negative thinking fully accounted for the programme's benefit across depression, anxiety, PTSD and insomnia, although sleep quality did not improve significantly [[exercise-mental-health-stress-negative-thinking]]. A network meta-analysis of 24 randomized trials in college students adds nuance: Baduanjin and mindfulness-based exercise were superior for depression, mindfulness-based exercise beat yoga for stress, and the combined programme gave the largest anxiety effect, yet no mind-body option significantly improved well-being or sleep, which the authors call inconclusive [[mind-body-exercise-college-student-mental-health-network-meta-analysis]]. Activity also interacts with inherited risk. In All of Us data, genetic risk was less strongly linked to depression severity among adults who took more steps and logged more moderate-to-vigorous activity, which makes activity a possible buffer rather than a proven cure [[physical-activity-genetic-depression-risk-all-of-us]]. Social formats show results as well, since an eight-week salsa programme reduced PHQ-9 scores by a clinically meaningful 2.45 points versus waitlist among 121 young adults [[salsa-dancing-depression-young-adults]], and a meta-analysis of 18 trials linked the low-intensity Baduanjin to lower depressive symptoms and better sleep in older adults, though high heterogeneity calls for caution [[baduanjin-exercise-sleep-depression-older-adults-meta-analysis]].

## Sleep as Mediator and Risk Marker

Sleep keeps appearing as a link between mental-health symptoms and how people function. In early adolescence, sleep quality explained 36.3% of the association between anxiety and worse academic and social outcomes and 18.5% for depression, well ahead of screen time's 5 to 6.3% [[adolescent-sleep-academic-social-outcomes]]. In adults, irregular sleep went with anxiety and major depression and deep sleep with lower depression risk, although reverse causation means disease may also be disrupting sleep [[rem-sleep-lower-risk-83-diseases]]. The insomnia umbrella review describes the two-way loop, in which anxiety and depression disrupt sleep while poor sleep raises vulnerability to both [[insomnia-health-risks-review-sleep-habits-cbt]].

## Sleep as a Treatment Target

Sleep is also becoming a direct treatment target. The NEUSLeeP ultrasound patch, tested in a 28-person trial, brought REM onset 43 minutes earlier and raised heart-rate variability, and its developers plan trials in PTSD, depression and insomnia because REM is framed as the brain's emotional reset [[wearable-ultrasound-patch-rem-sleep]]. For insomnia itself, cognitive behavioral therapy is described as the gold-standard treatment, alongside consistent schedules and fewer screens before bed, while trying harder to sleep tends to backfire [[insomnia-health-risks-review-sleep-habits-cbt]].

## Emerging Physiological Therapies

Heat therapy rests on the observation that depressed people "run hot": in wearable data from about 20,000 people, worse depression went with higher skin temperature and less nightly cooling [[heat-therapy-sauna-mental-health-depression-research]]. In a small landmark trial, about two hours in a whole-body infrared machine raising body temperature to 38.5°C halved symptoms within a week for most of the 30 patients, and the antidepressant effect lasted through six weeks [[heat-therapy-sauna-mental-health-depression-research]]. Pilots pairing eight weeks of CBT with four infrared sessions left most participants in remission, but those pilots were not powered to prove efficacy, and sauna-goers' gains may reflect nature, digital detox or socializing rather than heat, so sham-controlled trials are still needed [[heat-therapy-sauna-mental-health-depression-research]].

## Brain Stimulation Moves Toward Personalization

Brain stimulation is shifting from standard protocols toward individualized targeting. Temporal interference stimulation, delivered through scalp electrodes, reached the ventral striatum, the reward region tied to anhedonia, and a single 20-minute session paired with mindful breathing increased reward-related activity and reduced anxiety for about a week, though the technique remains investigational [[temporal-interference-stimulation-reward-anxiety-emory]]. Wave Neuroscience's MeRT combines EEG measurement with transcranial magnetic stimulation and an AI algorithm to tailor each protocol, and it received FDA clearance for PTSD, which the source describes as the first FDA-cleared nonmedication treatment for the condition, with company-reported early results of 52% symptom reduction and 68% remission [[meert-personalized-brain-stimulation-ptsd-fda-clearance]]. The comparison is limited, because the 10.9% maximum medication remission figure comes from a 2020 review of different studies, full trial data are not yet public, and insurance coverage and affordability still need to be worked out [[meert-personalized-brain-stimulation-ptsd-fda-clearance]].

## Psychedelic Drugs and the Trial Problem

Pharmaceutical interest in psychedelics has grown since the FDA rejected Lykos Therapeutics' MDMA therapy for PTSD in 2024. Eli Lilly's plan to acquire AtaiBeckley for up to $3.8 billion targets BPL-003 for treatment-resistant depression, and AbbVie paid $1.2 billion in 2025 for bretisilocin, a short-acting psychedelic [[pharma-billion-dollar-bets-psychedelic-drugs-after-mdma-setback]]. Compass Pathways reported positive results from two late-stage psilocybin trials in treatment-resistant depression, and Definium's LSD pill significantly reduced generalized anxiety symptoms in late-stage studies [[pharma-billion-dollar-bets-psychedelic-drugs-after-mdma-setback]]. The MDMA rejection shows why these results are hard to interpret. The FDA flagged functional unblinding, where participants and researchers guess who received the active drug, and the difficulty of separating MDMA's effect from the psychotherapy delivered with it [[pharma-billion-dollar-bets-psychedelic-drugs-after-mdma-setback]]. The field is now pursuing shorter-acting drugs with fewer hallucinations, but the extent of adverse effects and the duration of benefit remain unknown [[pharma-billion-dollar-bets-psychedelic-drugs-after-mdma-setback]].

## Nature Exposure and Restoration

Time in nature also shows measurable benefits. A University of New Hampshire study of visitors to College Woods found that visits improved mood and raised self-reported restoration by nearly 30%, and what visitors perceived predicted well-being better than measured bird and mammal counts did, with pleasant sounds such as birdsong and flowing water as another strong predictor [[perceived-biodiversity-nature-walk-mental-wellbeing-unh-study]]. Awareness can be built as well, since educational signs modestly raised perceived biodiversity and positive mood without a clear effect on restoration or negative emotions [[perceived-biodiversity-nature-walk-mental-wellbeing-unh-study]]. The heat research makes the same caution from the other side, since mood gains attributed to sauna use may come from nature exposure, mindfulness or socializing rather than from heat [[heat-therapy-sauna-mental-health-depression-research]].

## Diet as Risk and Treatment

Diet is moving from a correlate toward an intervention for depression. The SMILES trial found 32% remission with Mediterranean-style dietary counseling versus 8% with social support, with inflammation and gut-derived serotonin proposed as mechanisms [[nutrition-and-mental-health-link]]. In a 20-person UCSF crossover pilot, cutting ultra-processed food intake by more than 85% produced moderate-to-large improvements on three depression measures [[ultra-processed-food-reduction-depression-ucsf-pilot]]. The write-up of that pilot is explicit about what the design cannot show: depression scores fell 11 points on the low-processed diet against 4 on the usual diet, but there was no healthy-diet comparison arm, participants knew their diet, everyone had metabolic disease, the four-week period was short, and the gut-inflammation mechanism was never measured [[cutting-ultra-processed-food-depression-pilot-caveats-mindgrow]]. Restriction can backfire, since calorie-restricted dieters in a cross-sectional study of 28,525 US adults reported more depressive symptoms, possibly because of B vitamin, iron and omega-3 deficits [[low-calorie-diets-may-increase-depression]].

## Gut Barrier and Brain Nutrients

The mechanisms behind diet links run through the gut and the supply of micronutrients. Tim Spector describes gut microbes whose outputs can drive inflammation, and argues that chronic low-grade inflammation can signal the brain into a recovery state that lowers motivation for social contact and activity, while recommending foods such as mixed nuts, Greek yogurt, frozen berries and canned beans [[tim-spector-nutrition-mental-health-gut-brain]]. Spector's estimate that better diet could cut dementia incidence by about 50% comes from a popular account and is not a trial result [[tim-spector-nutrition-mental-health-gut-brain]]. A UK Biobank study of 181,990 people linked dietary nutrients to brain structure, and low-fibre eaters had less tissue in 11 brain regions, since fibre-starved microbes degrade the gut's mucus layer and let inflammation reach the brain [[three-diet-changes-boost-brain-mental-health-fibre-micronutrients]]. Fibre-derived short-chain fatty acids support both the gut barrier and the blood-brain barrier [[three-diet-changes-boost-brain-mental-health-fibre-micronutrients]], which fits the leaky-gut account the UCSF pilot offers for ultra-processed food [[ultra-processed-food-reduction-depression-ucsf-pilot]].

## Fruit and Sugary Drinks

Beverages add a sharper signal to the diet picture. In a three-year cohort of 6,537 Australian adolescents, each extra daily serving of fruit was linked to 13% lower depression and 7% lower anxiety risk, while sugar-sweetened beverages raised risk and vegetables showed no significant association [[fruit-sugary-drinks-adolescent-anxiety-depression-australia-study]]. Those links rest on observational design and self-reported intake, so the study cannot show that changing diet prevents disorders [[fruit-sugary-drinks-adolescent-anxiety-depression-australia-study]]. In adults, a Newcastle RCT of 42 people found that adding a daily glass of 100% fruit juice or smoothie to a 5-a-day intervention lowered PHQ-9 scores by 2.52 points over four weeks without adverse metabolic effects, though the trial was small, short and funded by the Fruit Juice Science Centre [[fruit-juice-lower-depression-scores]].

## AI Chatbots and the Treatment Gap

AI tools are being pitched at a large unmet need, since about 25% of people worldwide experience a mental-health condition and about 85% receive no adequate care [[ai-mental-health-chatbots-empathetic-safety-lags]]. A scoping review of 21 studies found that LLM chatbots, mostly built on CBT and aimed at depression and anxiety, score well on satisfaction and perceived empathy, yet engagement declines over multi-week use, empathy does not reliably produce better clinical outcomes, and crisis handling remains a critical failure mode [[ai-mental-health-chatbots-safety-evidence-review]]. Georgia Tech researchers see promise in AI that flags behavioral change before a crisis, but warn that more AI interaction may deepen isolation: "The use of AI is encouraged, but reliance on AI is not" [[ai-healthcare-revolution-diagnosis-wellness]]. The 2026 state bans on standalone AI therapy chatbots, which keep complex trauma care with licensed professionals, reflect this evidence gap [[mental-health-first-responders-military-indiana-2026]] [[ai-mental-health-chatbots-safety-evidence-review]].

## A Field of Promising but Preliminary Evidence

Many of the most striking findings come from very small or early studies, so the vault holds them as hypotheses rather than settled results. The heat-therapy RCT had 30 participants, the UCSF diet pilot had 20, and the fruit-juice trial had 42 and was funded by the Fruit Juice Science Centre [[heat-therapy-sauna-mental-health-depression-research]] [[ultra-processed-food-reduction-depression-ucsf-pilot]] [[fruit-juice-lower-depression-scores]]. The college mind-body meta-analysis was read only through its abstract, and its null results for well-being and sleep may reflect limited evidence rather than no effect [[mind-body-exercise-college-student-mental-health-network-meta-analysis]]. The MeRT figures are company-reported, temporal interference stimulation is still investigational, the psychedelic drugs are still in late-stage testing with open questions on adverse effects, and most chatbot studies test early prototypes rather than validated clinical tools [[meert-personalized-brain-stimulation-ptsd-fda-clearance]] [[temporal-interference-stimulation-reward-anxiety-emory]] [[pharma-billion-dollar-bets-psychedelic-drugs-after-mdma-setback]] [[ai-mental-health-chatbots-safety-evidence-review]]. The better-established findings rest on larger designs, such as the 400-person ImPuls trial and a 2026 behavioral-activation meta-analysis of 105 trials with benefits lasting 12 months [[exercise-mental-health-stress-negative-thinking]] [[productivity-hides-depression-overfunctioning]].

**T2. Inputs during developmental windows have outsized, lasting effects on mental-health risk** — Because networks are still forming in childhood, adversity, unpredictability and early nutrition shape brain structure and later risk for depression and anxiety, in ways that vary from person to person. · *model:* [[Critical Period Hypothesis]]

**T3. Interventions on shared upstream mechanisms can improve several conditions at once** — Stress reactivity, repetitive negative thinking, sleep, inflammation and thermoregulation sit beneath several diagnoses, so acting on one of them can help depression, anxiety, PTSD and insomnia together, though effects also differ by outcome and intervention.

**T4. Easy-to-measure proxies diverge from the outcomes they stand in for** — When satisfaction or perceived empathy becomes the target, systems optimize for them while clinical benefit, sustained engagement and safety go unmeasured. · *model:* [[Goodhart's Law]]

**T5. Average effects hide variation between people and subgroups** — When an exposure or treatment harms some people, helps others and leaves the rest unaffected, population averages look small or inconsistent, and one-size-fits-all approaches fit poorly. · *model:* [[Flaw of Averages]]
