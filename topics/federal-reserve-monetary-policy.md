# Federal Reserve Monetary Policy

This topic follows the Federal Reserve's 2026 return to rate hikes, from the September 16 increase and Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish guidance, through a committee split on urgency, sticky services inflation and a long-end bond rout, to the October 27–28 meeting. That meeting held the federal funds rate at 3.75%–4.00% on a 10–2 vote with two dissents in favor of a hike. By the start of November, markets priced a December hike with high probability while Warsh still declined to give forward guidance, so the timing and size of the next move remained contested.

## The September 2026 Hike and Its Setup

On September 16, 2026, the FOMC voted 12-0 to raise the federal funds rate a quarter point to 3.75%–4.00%, its first increase since 2023, with Chair Kevin Warsh pointing to inflation that had shown no meaningful improvement over the summer [[fed-12-0-rate-hike-3-75-percent-housing-stocks-sep2026]]. A Cook speech note puts trailing-year inflation at the time of the vote at about 3.8% headline and 3.4% core [[fed-governor-cook-ai-economy-speech-sep2026]], and previews of the October 5–9 week stressed that inflation had run above the 2% target for more than half a decade [[five-things-week-ahead-bond-yields-french-debt-fomc-minutes-oct2026]]. The move was largely priced in. Brent above $109 had briefly pushed the 10-year yield past 5% the day before, and Goldman's David Mericle argued that with hike odds near 90% the committee would want to avoid the market reaction to standing pat, while Ed Yardeni suggested a hike might even ease long-term yields by restoring credibility [[10-year-treasury-yield-tops-5-percent-sep2026]]. The vote was unanimous although several key officials had earlier signaled reluctance [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]] [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]].

## Warsh's Guidance and the Dot Plot

On decision day the guidance moved markets more than the hike did. Warsh called for a "timelier return" to 2%, said he was "hard-pressed" to call financial conditions restrictive, and the Dow fell 631 points [[warsh-timelier-return-fomc-dot-plot-ai-sep2026]]. He also described the move as removing "a dose of accommodation," which Yardeni read as an admission that the funds rate still sits below neutral. That reading is reinforced by the median longer-run neutral estimate rising to 3.25%, a post-pandemic high [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]] [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]]. The sources differ on the dot plot's emphasis. The decision-day note described 50 more basis points before year-end, while the minutes coverage and Yardeni put the median at one more hike in 2026, with a 4.1% funds rate at end-2026 and end-2027. Investopedia counted four of 19 participants expecting two more hikes [[warsh-timelier-return-fomc-dot-plot-ai-sep2026]] [[week-ahead-q3-earnings-season-delta-pepsi-fed-minutes-oct2026]]. On the broader shape, the sources agree that 16 of 18 forecasters expected at least one more 2026 move, that no cuts were projected for 2027, and that most officials saw no easing until 2028 or later [[fed-12-0-rate-hike-3-75-percent-housing-stocks-sep2026]] [[pce-inflation-preview-fed-rate-hike-outlook-sep2026]].

## Fed Speakers Split on Urgency, Not Direction

Within two weeks of the hike, NY Fed President John Williams said there was "no need for urgency" while still leaving a hike "late this year" on the table. That single sentence cut CME FedWatch odds of an October hike from 70% to below 50%, because his permanent vote and the New York Fed's market-operations role give his words more weight than other officials' [[ny-fed-williams-rate-increase-can-wait-sep2026]] [[october-rate-hike-odds-below-50-williams-barr-cook-sep2026]]. Governors Barr and Cook leaned the other way the same week. Barr noted core PCE had hit target in only 2 of the last 20 months and blamed tariffs and the prolonged Iran war for knocking disinflation off course, while Cook flagged pressure from the AI build-out and oil prices [[pce-inflation-preview-fed-rate-hike-outlook-sep2026]]. After the minutes, Governor Christopher Waller said more hikes would likely be needed to reach 2%, but with "flexibility" on pace. The committee's dividing line therefore sits on timing rather than on whether to tighten [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]].

## Inflation Data: Softer Prints, Stickier Services

The August PCE report came in better than the preview's 3.7% headline and 3.3% core expectation, at 3.4% headline and 3% core. Part of that relief reflected BEA methodology changes that the preview had flagged as trimming the backward-looking level without changing the outlook [[pce-inflation-preview-fed-rate-hike-outlook-sep2026]] [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]]. Underneath, services kept running hot. Yardeni noted supercore PCE had been rising since October 2025, and the September ISM services price index rose to 74, lifting its 12-month average to the highest since March 2023 even as the headline services index cooled to 54.9 [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]] [[10-year-treasury-yield-fresh-2002-high-ism-services-oct5-2026]]. Expectations sent mixed signals. The New York Fed's consumer survey showed one-year inflation fears at their highest since May 2023, while Reuters' Morning Bid judged long-run inflation expectations stable [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. Energy remains the main upside risk. Brent traded between roughly $101 and $104 in early October on Houthi attacks, Yemen's offensive and reports of Pentagon strike preparations against Iran, and the minutes cited energy costs, tariffs and AI spending as reasons to lean toward another hike [[stocks-fall-bond-yields-oil-fed-minutes-halt-record-rally-oct7-2026]] [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]] [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]]. By late October Brent had reached $116.72, its highest since April, which Alpesh Patel read as a shock that feeds transport and distribution costs, squeezes margins and leaves policymakers less room to cut [[five-things-moving-markets-oil-fed-big-tech-oct25-2026]].

## The October Inflation Data and the Hike Debate

The September CPI was previewed as a test of composition: a gasoline-driven headline would matter little for policy unless core also strengthened [[september-cpi-preview-energy-headline-vs-core-fed-oct2026]]. It beat on both counts, with headline at 3.7% against 3.6% expected, core at 2.6% against 2.5%, and gasoline up 9.1% on the month. The report was widely read as making rate cuts harder to justify [[september-cpi-3-7-percent-hot-inflation-fed-rate-cuts-at-risk-oct2026]]. Producer prices then rose 1.3% against a 0.3% estimate, and CME FedWatch October hike odds moved up to nearly 34% from about 29%, as Navy Federal's Heather Long said business inflation was hot even excluding energy [[hot-september-cpi-ppi-revive-fed-hike-fears-stock-futures-fall-gotrade-oct2026]]. The hike fears faded within a day or two. Governor Michelle Bowman and San Francisco's Mary Daly said the Fed could wait for clarity, and FedWatch hold odds for October 27–28 rose to 72% from 53% a month earlier [[dollar-retreats-18-month-high-ppi-fed-hike-bets-ease-oct15-2026]]. Retail sales told a similar story of strong headlines over weak underlying demand. The September gain of 0.8% owed much to gasoline stations, which added 0.55 percentage points, while sales excluding gasoline rose only 0.2% and the control group just 0.1% after 1.2% in August [[strong-retail-sales-consumer-resilience-after-fed-rate-hike-oct2026]]. Pensford argued the core CPI surprise was less alarming underneath, since owners' equivalent rent supplied more than half of the monthly core increase and the share of CPI components rising above 3% had slipped to 48%, from 51% a month earlier and 58% before that [[pensford-t10-headed-below-5-percent-core-cpi-oct2026]]. In mid-October futures put the odds of an October hike at only 15%. CNBC's Matt Peterson argued that this could be complacent, because Chair Warsh rejects forward guidance, disowned the September projections as "not my forecasts," and calls the 2% PCE goal a firm target [[warsh-october-fed-hike-surprise-risk-market-complacency]]. Deutsche Bank's Matt Luzzetti added a structural point. The October 28 meeting falls a day before the September PCE release, and Warsh's floated six-meeting calendar would give officials data roughly 30% timelier at the cost of two extra weeks between meetings [[fed-six-meetings-a-year-timelier-data-luzzetti-oct2026]].

## The October 27–28 Hold: Telegraphed, Then Re-Read

Markets had priced the hold for weeks. Fed funds futures showed about 90% odds of no change in the days before the meeting [[stock-futures-edge-up-oil-falls-ai-fed-focus-oct26-2026]], and a Reuters economist poll found 94 of 100 economists expecting the hold [[stonex-fomc-preview-warsh-hawkish-hold-oct2026]]. The meeting carried no Summary of Economic Projections or dot plot, so the statement and the chair's press conference were the only new signals [[week-ahead-oct26-30-fomc-alphabet-microsoft-amazon-earnings-tradingkey]]. Pre-meeting coverage described the consensus as a hold followed by a December hike, with the 10-year yield at 5% on October 23, above 5% in 21 of 22 sessions [[thestreet-fed-tech-oil-earnings-exciting-week-oct26-2026]]. The timing was also awkward. Q3 GDP and core PCE were scheduled for Thursday morning, the day after the decision, so a hawkish tone could weigh on growth stocks that depend on lower borrowing costs, while strong AI guidance from the Big Tech reports could steady chip and software names [[big-tech-earnings-fed-warsh-gdp-pce-week-ahead-oct26-2026]]. Pre-meeting analysis treated the vote count as the most actionable clue. StoneX read no dissents as neutral, one as mildly hawkish, and two or more as a true hawkish hold [[stonex-fomc-preview-warsh-hawkish-hold-oct2026]]. The result was a 10–2 vote, with Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack favoring a hike [[us-jobs-report-week-peso-real-fed-repricing-nov2026]] [[fed-musalem-logan-more-rate-hikes-needed-oct30-2026]]. Warsh gave no forward guidance and repeated that he did not believe in it "absent a crisis," so the reaction was muted and attention moved to earnings [[wall-street-looks-past-fed-pause-tech-earnings-ai-rebound-cnbc-oct28-2026]]. The press conference was still read as hawkish. Warsh cited resilient spending and stable employment, the 10-year yield moved back above 5%, and December hike odds rose to 83.5% from 72.8% [[futures-drop-alphabet-9-8-percent-q3-miss-microsoft-beat-warsh-hold-oct2026]]. Before the meeting, Claudio Irigoyen of Bank of America had framed the decision as a test of Warsh's resolve to bring a "sea change" to forward guidance, noting that a hike risked overtightening worries while a hold risked doubts about credibility [[stock-futures-edge-up-oil-falls-ai-fed-focus-oct26-2026]]. Economists disagreed on whether further hikes were warranted at all. Krishna Guha of Evercore ISI warned of "material execution risk" given Warsh's reluctance to guide and expected about one hike per quarter, Brett Ryan of Deutsche Bank expected Warsh to skip October while signaling further tightening over time, and Mohamed El-Erian argued tightening was the wrong tool and favored "fiscal containment" [[fed-hold-warsh-more-hikes-execution-risk-cnbc-oct27-2026]]. Citigroup, according to the same coverage, saw cuts next year [[fed-hold-warsh-more-hikes-execution-risk-cnbc-oct27-2026]].

## Hawks Push for More After the Hold

Within days, Fed presidents argued for further tightening. Alberto Musalem said more firming was needed "soon," and Lorie Logan called for at least two more quarter-point hikes followed by a pause. Futures then priced roughly 76% odds of a December increase [[fed-musalem-logan-more-rate-hikes-needed-oct30-2026]]. Logan argued that skipping a meeting does not change the destination [[fed-musalem-logan-more-rate-hikes-needed-oct30-2026]]. The governance question is also open. Warsh's plan for strategy-focused meetings in 2027 and 2028 drew criticism, with former official Jeffrey Lacker seeing too little urgency and James Bullard opposing skipped meetings [[fed-musalem-logan-more-rate-hikes-needed-oct30-2026]]. Taken together, the post-hold speeches shift the debate from whether to tighten toward how fast the committee should move, and how much the chair's communication style can be relied on to convey that pace [[fed-meeting-five-things-to-watch-warsh-oct2026]].

## The September Jobs Report Pushes the Next Hike to December

The October 2 jobs report settled the October question. Payrolls rose just 29,000 against an 84,000 forecast, July and August were revised down by a combined 60,000, annual wage growth slowed to 3%, and unemployment rose to 4.2% mostly because 485,000 people joined the labor force [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. Jefferies' Thomas Simons called it "the nail in the coffin for an October hike," and October hike odds slid from roughly 64% a week earlier to under 23%. Yardeni put them at 22% for October against 67% by December, and only 3% for two hikes [[us-stocks-near-record-jobs-report-tesla-nvidia-nike-oct9-2026]] [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]]. The rest of the labor data argued against reading this as a downturn. Initial claims sat near 197,000, close to a 57-year low, ADP private payrolls rose 90,000, and the Atlanta Fed tracked third-quarter GDP at 3.7%, leaving a low-hire, low-fire market that justifies patience rather than easing [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]] [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. AMP's Shane Oliver described the print as "not too hot, not too cold," data that supports the view that the Fed will not rush into another hike [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]]. The October jobs report, due November 6, is the next labor test. Polymarket priced another 2026 hike at 70.5% and zero cuts at 96.9%, and analysts stressed revisions and the surprise relative to consensus over the headline level [[us-jobs-report-week-peso-real-fed-repricing-nov2026]].

## The September Minutes: Another Hike, No Date

Released October 7, the minutes showed "most participants" judging another increase "likely appropriate by year end," and "many" calling a higher path a prudent insurance policy against inflation that stays high because of strong demand or new supply shocks. They gave no hint whether the move would come on October 28 or December 9 [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]]. Markets took the confirmation without accelerating the timeline. FedWatch October hike odds fell to 17.2% from 37.6% a week earlier, held near 21.6% the next morning, and pricing still pointed to three more hikes over the next year starting in December [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]] [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. Futures ran more hawkish than the dots, implying 3.3 hikes over 12 months with the 2-year yield at 4.78%, well above the 4% top of the target range. Views on that gap diverge. Yardeni argues the Fed should keep going until it has reversed all three of last year's "insurance" cuts, of which September undid only one, while BlackRock calls the market's tightening expectations "too much" [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]] [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]].

## The Long-End Rout and the Term Premium

Long yields kept climbing even as near-term hike odds fell. The 10-year hit 5.349% intraday on October 5 and 5.365% on October 7, its highest since April 2002, with the 30-year reaching about 5.73%. [[10-year-treasury-yield-fresh-2002-high-ism-services-oct5-2026]] [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. Fed staff attributed the rise to expected hikes, the AI build-out, solid growth and uncertainty around the Treasury's buyback program. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's ramped-up buybacks of long-dated debt barely moved yields, and he cast the move as global rather than American [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. The global framing has support. The French-German 10-year spread was at its widest since 1990, and Deutsche Bank described bond markets as under "high stress and high alert." The 10-year term premium reached its highest since 2014, which suggests investors are charging for debt levels, political risk and long-run financing uncertainty on top of the expected Fed path [[five-things-week-ahead-bond-yields-french-debt-fomc-minutes-oct2026]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. Infrastructure Capital's Jay Hatfield called it a momentum sell-off with room to run, noting the 10-year usually trades about 100 basis points over the terminal funds rate, which ties the long end's level to how high markets think the Fed will ultimately go [[10-year-treasury-yield-fresh-2002-high-ism-services-oct5-2026]]. The rout then paused. After its October 7 peak the 10-year dipped to an intraday 5.038% before edging back to about 5.12%, the 30-year stood at 5.46% on October 16, and a WSJ survey expected the 10-year to end 2026 at 5.1% [[us-treasury-yields-decades-high-oil-fed-debt-ai-oct2026]]. Pensford argued that higher rates self-limit by restricting growth, and that a selloff in leveraged AI stocks after the hot CPI pulled the 10-year back to 5.08% [[pensford-t10-headed-below-5-percent-core-cpi-oct2026]]. The yield moved again after the hold. On October 29, with GDP growth of 3.4% annualized beating a 3.1% forecast and September PCE at 3.6% year over year, the 10-year was at 5.011% and the 2-year at 4.693%. Thornburg's Ali Hassan read the Fed's update as a softer policy path rather than an inflation scare [[10-year-treasury-yield-above-5-gdp-pce-cnbc-oct29-2026]]. Raymond James added the Treasury's November 4 refunding as a further variable. The refunding could trim long-end issuance and possibly eliminate the 20-year bond [[raymond-james-key-catalysts-fed-earnings-refunding-midterms-oct2026]].

## AI as a New Monetary Policy Variable

Governor Cook's September 28 speech made the clearest case for treating AI as a macroeconomic variable. She framed it as a general-purpose technology whose investment boom is adding to inflation now, through chips and more broadly through construction labor and energy, while any productivity gains would bring only modest and delayed disinflation [[fed-governor-cook-ai-economy-speech-sep2026]]. Williams also named AI-related goods demand as an inflation driver. By October the minutes listed the AI build-out among the forces behind both inflation risk and 2002-high Treasury yields, and corporate borrowing for AI was cited alongside government spending as a reason the bond rout persisted [[pce-inflation-preview-fed-rate-hike-outlook-sep2026]] [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]] [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]]. A Financial Express survey put numbers on the AI channel. ING credited AI with about one-fifth of the rise in long-dated yields, and Merrill Lynch counted nearly $280 billion of big-tech debt competing with Treasuries [[us-treasury-yields-decades-high-oil-fed-debt-ai-oct2026]]. On jobs, Cook pointed to early labor effects in coding, translation and entry-level roles, and the September report's 10,000 information-sector job losses landed amid the same worry [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. The risk for policymakers is that AI delivers price pressure and labor disruption at the same time.

## Sectoral Transmission: Housing, Consumer Credit and AI Infrastructure

Tightening reaches debt-dependent sectors first, and the benchmark a borrower faces determines how quickly rates move. Credit card rates track prime, which sits about three points above the Fed's benchmark and reprices within one or two billing cycles. Card rates have been rising, and used-car loan rates exceeded 10% [[fed-holds-3-75-4-percent-consumer-borrowing-costs-cnbc-oct28-2026]]. Mark Zandi noted that high-net-worth consumers were spending freely on AI-stock gains while lower- and middle-income consumers were more cautious [[fed-holds-3-75-4-percent-consumer-borrowing-costs-cnbc-oct28-2026]]. Housing took the earlier hikes first. Mortgage applications were down 19% year over year in September, and by early October the 30-year fixed mortgage rate had reached 7.49%, its highest in nearly three years, while homebuilder stocks fell 2.9% [[fed-12-0-rate-hike-3-75-percent-housing-stocks-sep2026]] [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. A TheStreet preview put the 30-year mortgage rate near 7.3% in late October, below the 7.61% peak reached on October 5 [[thestreet-fed-tech-oil-earnings-exciting-week-oct26-2026]]. Oxford Economics estimated that high rates put homes out of reach for 5 million more households [[fed-meeting-five-things-to-watch-warsh-oct2026]]. AI infrastructure is the second exposure in a cycle Goldman called "particularly capital intensive," and the financing is getting more leveraged. SpaceX has completed a $25 billion bond offering, and October coverage described up to $40 billion in chip finance involving SpaceX and Nvidia, along with debt-financed deals between Broadcom and OpenAI that some read as vendor financing [[goldman-sachs-fed-tightening-sp500-historical-returns]] [[fed-rate-hike-sep2026-ai-stocks-self-funding-edge]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. Morgan Stanley's argument that AI is shifting from growth stories to profitability points to the resulting edge, which is financial rather than technological. Firms that fund their own capital spending are insulated from higher rates [[fed-rate-hike-sep2026-ai-stocks-self-funding-edge]]. The hold coverage makes the same point from the other side, noting that tech is insulated while housing, car loans and consumer credit absorb the hit [[fed-hold-warsh-more-hikes-execution-risk-cnbc-oct27-2026]].

## Equity Markets Through the Cycle

Goldman's history sets the baseline. The S&P 500 has averaged a 2% decline in the three months after a tightening cycle begins but a 9% gain over twelve months, driven mainly by earnings. So far earnings are carrying the load. S&P 500 earnings were expected to grow about 29% year over year in the third quarter, and strong earnings estimates helped the index close above 7,800 for the first time on October 6 [[week-ahead-q3-earnings-season-delta-pepsi-fed-minutes-oct2026]] [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. The next day showed the rate sensitivity underneath, as 24-year-high yields, oil above $101 and minutes nerves pulled the indexes back while about 73% of S&P 500 stocks fell [[stocks-fall-bond-yields-oil-fed-minutes-halt-record-rally-oct7-2026]] [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. In this regime soft data reads as good news. The weak jobs report lifted Wall Street and Asian futures, and lower yields boosted AI valuations led by Nvidia. BlackRock stayed overweight US equities on AI-driven earnings [[us-stocks-near-record-jobs-report-tesla-nvidia-nike-oct9-2026]] [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]] [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]]. By mid-October the rate worry had become a drawdown.
