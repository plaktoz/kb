# Federal Reserve Monetary Policy

This topic follows the Federal Reserve's 2026 return to rate hikes: the September decision and Warsh's hawkish guidance, the split among Fed speakers, sticky inflation, the weak September jobs report that pushed the next hike toward December, the September minutes that confirmed another hike without dating it, and a long-end bond rout that kept tightening financial conditions even as near-term hike odds fell.

## The September 2026 Hike and Its Setup

On September 16, 2026, the FOMC voted 12-0 to raise the federal funds rate a quarter point to 3.75%–4.00%, its first increase since 2023, with Chair Kevin Warsh pointing to inflation that had not meaningfully improved over the summer [[fed-12-0-rate-hike-3-75-percent-housing-stocks-sep2026]]. Governor Cook later put trailing-year inflation at the time of the vote at about 3.8% headline and 3.4% core, and preview coverage of the October 5–9 week stressed that inflation had now run above the 2% target for more than half a decade [[fed-governor-cook-ai-economy-speech-sep2026]] [[five-things-week-ahead-bond-yields-french-debt-fomc-minutes-oct2026]]. Markets had seen the move coming: Brent above $109 had briefly pushed the 10-year yield past 5% the day before, and Goldman's David Mericle argued that with hike odds near 90% the committee would want to avoid the market reaction to standing pat, while Ed Yardeni suggested a hike might even ease long-term yields by restoring credibility [[10-year-treasury-yield-tops-5-percent-sep2026]]. The minutes later showed the vote was unanimous although several key officials had earlier signaled reluctance, and that members agreed on the action more than on its rationale, with some framing it as blunting energy shocks and others as curbing demand-driven inflation [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]] [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]].

## Warsh's Guidance and the Dot Plot

On decision day the guidance moved markets more than the hike: Warsh called for a "timelier return" to 2%, said he was "hard-pressed" to call financial conditions restrictive, and the Dow fell 631 points [[warsh-timelier-return-fomc-dot-plot-ai-sep2026]]. He also described the move as removing "a dose of accommodation," which Yardeni read as an admission that the funds rate still sits below neutral, a reading reinforced by the median longer-run neutral estimate rising to 3.25%, a post-pandemic high [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]] [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]]. The vault's accounts of the dot plot differ in emphasis: the decision-day note described 50 more basis points before year-end, while the minutes coverage and Yardeni put the median at one more hike in 2026 (a 4.1% funds rate at end-2026 and end-2027) and Investopedia counted four of 19 members expecting two more [[warsh-timelier-return-fomc-dot-plot-ai-sep2026]] [[week-ahead-q3-earnings-season-delta-pepsi-fed-minutes-oct2026]]. On the broader shape the sources agree that 16 of 18 forecasters expected at least one more 2026 move, no cuts were projected for 2027, and most officials saw no easing until 2028 or later [[fed-12-0-rate-hike-3-75-percent-housing-stocks-sep2026]] [[pce-inflation-preview-fed-rate-hike-outlook-sep2026]].

## Fed Speakers Split on Urgency, Not Direction

Within two weeks of the hike, NY Fed President John Williams said there was "no need for urgency" while still leaving a hike "late this year" on the table, and that single sentence cut CME FedWatch odds of an October hike from 70% to below 50%, because his permanent vote and the New York Fed's market-operations role give his words more weight than other officials' [[ny-fed-williams-rate-increase-can-wait-sep2026]] [[october-rate-hike-odds-below-50-williams-barr-cook-sep2026]]. Governors Barr and Cook leaned the other way the same week: Barr noted core PCE had hit target in only 2 of the last 20 months and blamed tariffs and the prolonged Iran war for knocking disinflation off course, while Cook flagged pressure from the AI build-out and oil pass-through [[pce-inflation-preview-fed-rate-hike-outlook-sep2026]]. After the minutes, Governor Christopher Waller added that more hikes would likely be needed to reach 2%, but with "flexibility" on pace, which keeps the committee's dividing line on timing rather than on whether to tighten [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]].

## Inflation Data: Softer Prints, Stickier Services

The August PCE report came in better than the preview's 3.7% headline and 3.3% core expectation, at 3.4% headline and 3% core, though the minutes coverage attributed part of that relief to the BEA methodology changes the preview had flagged as likely to trim the backward-looking level without changing the outlook [[pce-inflation-preview-fed-rate-hike-outlook-sep2026]] [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]]. Underneath, services kept running hot: Yardeni noted supercore PCE had been rising since October 2025, and the September ISM services price index rose to 74, lifting its 12-month average to the highest since March 2023 even as the headline services index cooled to 54.9 [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]] [[10-year-treasury-yield-fresh-2002-high-ism-services-oct5-2026]]. Expectations sent mixed signals, with the New York Fed's consumer survey showing one-year inflation fears at their highest since May 2023 while Reuters' Morning Bid judged long-run inflation expectations stable [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. Energy remains the live upside risk: Brent traded between roughly $101 and $104 during the week on Houthi attacks, Yemen's offensive and reports of Pentagon strike preparations against Iran, and the minutes themselves cited energy costs, tariffs and AI spending as reasons to lean toward another hike [[stocks-fall-bond-yields-oil-fed-minutes-halt-record-rally-oct7-2026]] [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]] [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]].

## The September Jobs Report Pushes the Next Hike to December

The October 2 jobs report settled the October question: payrolls rose just 29,000 against an 84,000 forecast, July and August were revised down by a combined 60,000, annual wage growth slowed to 3%, and unemployment rose to 4.2% mostly because 485,000 people joined the labor force [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. Jefferies' Thomas Simons called it "the nail in the coffin for an October hike," and October hike odds slid from roughly 64% a week earlier to under 23%, with Yardeni putting them at 22% for October against 67% by December and only 3% for two hikes [[us-stocks-near-record-jobs-report-tesla-nvidia-nike-oct9-2026]] [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]]. The rest of the labor data argued against reading this as a downturn, since initial claims sat near 197,000 (close to a 57-year low), ADP private payrolls rose 90,000 and the Atlanta Fed tracked third-quarter GDP at 3.7%, leaving a low-hire, low-fire market that justifies patience rather than easing [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]] [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. AMP's Shane Oliver described the print as "not too hot, not too cold," data that supports the view that the Fed will not rush into another hike [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]].

## The September Minutes: Another Hike, No Date

Released October 7, the minutes showed "most participants" judging another increase "likely appropriate by year end," and "many" calling a higher path a prudent insurance policy against inflation stuck high from strong demand or new supply shocks, but they gave no hint whether the move would come on October 28 or December 9 [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]]. Markets took the confirmation without accelerating the timeline: FedWatch October hike odds fell to 17.2% from 37.6% a week earlier, held near 21.6% the next morning, and pricing still pointed to three more hikes over the next year starting in December [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]] [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. That leaves futures more hawkish than the dots, implying 3.3 hikes over 12 months with the 2-year yield at 4.78% well above the 4% top of the target range, and views on the gap diverge: Yardeni argues the Fed should keep going until it has reversed all three of last year's "insurance" cuts, of which September undid only one, while BlackRock calls market tightening expectations "too much" [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]] [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]].

## The Long-End Rout and the Term Premium

Long yields kept climbing even as near-term hike odds fell: the 10-year hit 5.349% intraday on October 5 and 5.365% on October 7, its highest since April 2002, with the 30-year reaching about 5.73%, and the weak jobs report produced only a brief rally inside a six-week sell-off [[10-year-treasury-yield-fresh-2002-high-ism-services-oct5-2026]] [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]] [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]]. Fed staff attributed the rise to expected hikes, the AI build-out, solid growth and uncertainty around the Treasury's buyback program, while Treasury Secretary Bessent's ramped-up buybacks of long-dated debt barely moved yields and he cast the move as global rather than American [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. The global framing has support, with the French-German 10-year spread at its widest since 1990 and Deutsche Bank describing bond markets as on "high stress and high alert," and the 10-year term premium at its highest since 2014 suggests investors are charging for debt levels, political risk and long-run financing uncertainty on top of the expected Fed path [[five-things-week-ahead-bond-yields-french-debt-fomc-minutes-oct2026]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. Infrastructure Capital's Jay Hatfield called it a momentum sell-off with room to run, noting the 10-year usually trades about 100 basis points over the terminal funds rate, which ties the long end's level to how high markets think the Fed will ultimately go [[10-year-treasury-yield-fresh-2002-high-ism-services-oct5-2026]].

## AI as a New Monetary Policy Variable

Governor Cook's September 28 speech made the clearest case for treating AI as a macroeconomic variable: she framed it as a general-purpose technology whose investment boom is adding to inflation now through chips and, more broadly, through construction labor and energy, while any productivity gains would bring only modest and delayed disinflation [[fed-governor-cook-ai-economy-speech-sep2026]]. Williams also named AI-related goods demand as an inflation driver, and by October the minutes listed the AI build-out among the forces behind both inflation risk and 2002-high Treasury yields, with corporate borrowing for AI cited alongside government spending as a reason the bond rout persisted [[pce-inflation-preview-fed-rate-hike-outlook-sep2026]] [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]] [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]]. On jobs, Cook saw early weakness in coding, translation and entry-level roles and warned that the Fed's tools are too blunt to offset AI-driven skill-mismatch unemployment without fueling inflation, and the September report's 10,000 information-sector losses landed amid the same worry [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. The risk for policymakers is that AI delivers price pressure and labor disruption at the same time.

## Sectoral Transmission: Housing and AI Infrastructure

Tightening hits debt-dependent sectors first: mortgage applications were down 19% year over year in September as earlier hikes still worked through housing, and by October 7 the 30-year fixed mortgage rate had reached 7.49%, its highest in nearly three years, as homebuilder stocks fell 2.9% [[fed-12-0-rate-hike-3-75-percent-housing-stocks-sep2026]] [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. AI infrastructure is the second exposure in a cycle Goldman called "particularly capital intensive," and the financing is getting more leveraged: after SpaceX's $25 billion bond offering, October brought reports of SpaceX arranging up to $40 billion to buy Nvidia chips and debt-financed chip deals between Broadcom and OpenAI that some read as vendor financing [[goldman-sachs-fed-tightening-sp500-historical-returns]] [[fed-rate-hike-sep2026-ai-stocks-self-funding-edge]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. Morgan Stanley's argument that AI is shifting from growth stories to profitability points to the resulting edge, which is financial rather than technological: firms that fund their own capital spending are insulated from higher rates, and that advantage widens the longer the cycle lasts [[fed-rate-hike-sep2026-ai-stocks-self-funding-edge]].

## Equity Markets Through the Cycle

Goldman's history sets the baseline: the S&P 500 has averaged a 2% decline in the three months after a tightening cycle begins but a 9% gain over twelve months, driven mainly by earnings, though hiking cycles have tended to mark the peaks of "high-valuation, high-concentration" bull markets [[goldman-sachs-fed-tightening-sp500-historical-returns]]. So far earnings are carrying the load, with analysts raising third-quarter S&P 500 estimates during the quarter and expecting roughly 29%–31% earnings growth, which helped the index close above 7,800 for the first time on October 6 [[week-ahead-q3-earnings-season-delta-pepsi-fed-minutes-oct2026]] [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. The next day showed the rate sensitivity underneath, as 24-year-high yields, oil above $101 and minutes nerves pulled the indexes back while about 73% of S&P 500 stocks fell and NYSE new lows outnumbered new highs more than four to one [[stocks-fall-bond-yields-oil-fed-minutes-halt-record-rally-oct7-2026]] [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. In this regime soft data reads as good news, since the weak jobs report lifted Wall Street and Asian futures and lower yields boosted AI valuations led by Nvidia, and BlackRock stayed overweight US equities on AI-driven earnings while preferring short- to medium-term Treasuries over long duration [[us-stocks-near-record-jobs-report-tesla-nvidia-nike-oct9-2026]] [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]] [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]].

**T2. When policy is the binding constraint, weak data lifts risk assets** — If tighter policy is what holds prices down, softer economic news lowers the expected rate path and can raise equities even though it signals a weaker economy. · *model:* [[Second-Order Thinking]]

**T3. Higher rates hurt most where value depends on distant cash flows or borrowed capital** — A higher risk-free rate raises the hurdle every risky asset must clear and discounts far-off earnings more heavily, so long-duration growth stocks and debt-financed sectors bear tightening first, while self-funded firms gain relative ground. · *model:* [[Discounted Cash Flow]]

**T4. Long-term yields price risks beyond the policy path** — Long yields compensate holders for debt supply, fiscal and political uncertainty, and global spillovers as well as expected short rates, so they can keep rising while near-term hike odds fall, and official tools aimed at them can fail.

**T5. After a prolonged overshoot, policymakers weigh under-tightening as the costlier error** — The longer inflation stays above target, the more officials treat extra tightening as insurance, because entrenched inflation is judged more damaging than a rate path that proves slightly too high. · *model:* [[Asymmetric Risk]]
