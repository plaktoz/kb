# Federal Reserve Monetary Policy

This topic follows the Federal Reserve's 2026 return to rate hikes, from the September 16 increase under Chair Kevin Warsh, through a committee split on urgency, sticky services inflation and a long-end bond rout, to the October 27–28 hold at 3.75%–4.00% on a 10–2 vote with Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack dissenting in favor of a hike. Early November showed how quickly December pricing can swing: a tech-led selloff on November 5 cut the odds of a December hike to 66.1% from 82.7%, and a stronger October jobs report on November 6 pushed them back to roughly 77% to 78% ahead of the December 8–9 meeting.

## The September Hike and the Guidance That Moved Markets

On September 16, 2026, the FOMC voted 12-0 to raise the federal funds rate a quarter point to 3.75%–4.00%, and Warsh tied the move to inflation that had shown no meaningful improvement over the summer [[fed-12-0-rate-hike-3-75-percent-housing-stocks-sep2026]]. The wording mattered more than the move: his call for a "timelier return" to 2% and his view that financial conditions were hard to describe as restrictive pushed the Dow down 631 points [[warsh-timelier-return-fomc-dot-plot-ai-sep2026]]. Yardeni read his remark that the hike removed "a dose of accommodation" as a sign the funds rate still sat below neutral [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]]. The dot plot was read two ways, with one decision-day note seeing about 50 basis points of hikes before year-end while the minutes coverage put the median at one more 2026 hike [[warsh-timelier-return-fomc-dot-plot-ai-sep2026]] [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]].

## Officials Split on Timing, Not Direction

Within two weeks of the hike, New York Fed President John Williams said there was "no need for urgency" while leaving a late-year increase on the table, and because his vote and market-operations role carry weight, October hike odds fell from 70% to below 50% on that one sentence [[ny-fed-williams-rate-increase-can-wait-sep2026]] [[october-rate-hike-odds-below-50-williams-barr-cook-sep2026]]. Governors Michael Barr and Lisa Cook took a harder line that same week, with Barr noting core PCE had reached target in only 2 of the last 20 months and Cook flagging pressure from the AI build-out and oil [[pce-inflation-preview-fed-rate-hike-outlook-sep2026]] [[october-rate-hike-odds-below-50-williams-barr-cook-sep2026]]. Christopher Waller later said more hikes would likely be needed but with "flexibility" on pace, while Austan Goolsbee, Philip Jefferson and Williams wanted more data before another move [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]] [[fed-six-meetings-a-year-timelier-data-luzzetti-oct2026]]. The committee's dividing line sits on timing and pace rather than on whether to tighten [[pce-inflation-preview-fed-rate-hike-outlook-sep2026]].

## Inflation: Softer Headlines, Sticky Services

August PCE came in at 3.4% headline and 3% core, below the preview's 3.7% and 3.3%, though part of that relief reflected a BEA revision that trimmed the backward-looking level without changing the outlook [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]] [[pce-inflation-preview-fed-rate-hike-outlook-sep2026]]. Services stayed firm, since supercore PCE has risen since October 2025 [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]] and the September ISM services price index climbed to 74, lifting its 12-month average to the highest since March 2023 [[10-year-treasury-yield-fresh-2002-high-ism-services-oct5-2026]]. Expectations sent mixed signals, with the New York Fed's consumer survey showing one-year inflation fears at their highest since May 2023 while Reuters' Morning Bid judged longer-run expectations stable [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. Energy kept the upside risk alive, as Brent traded roughly between $98 and $104 in early October and reached $116.72 by late October, a level Alpesh Patel said squeezes margins and leaves policymakers less room to cut [[us-stocks-near-record-jobs-report-tesla-nvidia-nike-oct9-2026]] [[five-things-moving-markets-oil-fed-big-tech-oct25-2026]].

## The September CPI and PPI Revive Hike Fears

September CPI came in hot, with headline at 3.7% against 3.6% expected and core at 2.6% against 2.5%, as gasoline jumped 9.1% in the month [[september-cpi-3-7-percent-hot-inflation-fed-rate-cuts-at-risk-oct2026]] [[hot-september-cpi-ppi-revive-fed-hike-fears-stock-futures-fall-gotrade-oct2026]]. The preview had framed the test as composition, asking whether core strengthened as well as the headline [[september-cpi-preview-energy-headline-vs-core-fed-oct2026]], and Pensford noted that owners' equivalent rent supplied more than half of the monthly core increase even as the share of components rising above 3% fell to 48% [[pensford-t10-headed-below-5-percent-core-cpi-oct2026]]. A PPI jump of 1.3% against a 0.3% estimate lifted October hike odds to about 34%, but that fear faded within days after Michelle Bowman and Mary Daly said the Fed could wait for clarity, and hold odds for October rose to 72% from 53% a month earlier [[hot-september-cpi-ppi-revive-fed-hike-fears-stock-futures-fall-gotrade-oct2026]] [[dollar-retreats-18-month-high-ppi-fed-hike-bets-ease-oct15-2026]]. Retail sales told a similar story, with the 0.8% headline driven by gasoline stations while the control group rose only 0.1% [[strong-retail-sales-consumer-resilience-after-fed-rate-hike-oct2026]].

## Complacency Ahead of a Telegraphed Hold

In the weeks before the meeting, futures priced only about a 15% chance of an October hike, and CNBC's Matt Peterson argued that this could be complacent, since Warsh rejects forward guidance, disowned the September projections as "not my forecasts," and calls the 2% PCE goal a firm target [[warsh-october-fed-hike-surprise-risk-market-complacency]]. By the meeting, fed funds futures showed about 90% odds of no change, and 94 of 100 economists in a Reuters poll expected a hold [[stock-futures-edge-up-oil-falls-ai-fed-focus-oct26-2026]] [[stonex-fomc-preview-warsh-hawkish-hold-oct2026]]. The October 28 meeting carried no Summary of Economic Projections or dot plot, which left the statement and the chair's press conference as the only new signals [[week-ahead-oct26-30-fomc-alphabet-microsoft-amazon-earnings-tradingkey]].

## The Hold and the Hawkish Read

Warsh gave no forward guidance and repeated that he did not believe in it "absent a crisis," so markets barely reacted to the decision and turned to tech earnings [[wall-street-looks-past-fed-pause-tech-earnings-ai-rebound-cnbc-oct28-2026]]. The press conference still read as hawkish, since Warsh cited resilient spending and stable employment, the 10-year yield moved back above 5%, and December hike odds rose to 83.5% from 72.8% [[futures-drop-alphabet-9-8-percent-q3-miss-microsoft-beat-warsh-hold-oct2026]]. The vote was 10-2, with Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack favoring a hike, and StoneX had argued before the meeting that two or more dissents would make it a true hawkish hold [[us-jobs-report-week-peso-real-fed-repricing-nov2026]] [[stonex-fomc-preview-warsh-hawkish-hold-oct2026]].

## Execution Risk and the Hawks After the Hold

Economists disagreed on whether further hikes were warranted at all. Krishna Guha of Evercore ISI warned of "material execution risk" from Warsh's reluctance to guide and expected about one hike per quarter, Brett Ryan of Deutsche Bank expected a skipped October with tightening signaled over time, and Mohamed El-Erian argued tightening was the wrong tool [[fed-hold-warsh-more-hikes-execution-risk-cnbc-oct27-2026]]. Hawks were more direct within days, as Alberto Musalem wanted "additional firming... soon" and Lorie Logan called for at least two more quarter-point hikes followed by a pause, with futures near 76% odds of a December increase [[fed-musalem-logan-more-rate-hikes-needed-oct30-2026]]. Logan argued that skipping a meeting does not change the destination, a point that bears on Warsh's plan for strategy-focused meetings in 2027 and 2028, which Jeffrey Lacker and James Bullard criticized [[fed-musalem-logan-more-rate-hikes-needed-oct30-2026]]. Luzzetti of Deutsche Bank proposed six meetings a year to make data roughly 30% timelier at the cost of two extra weeks between meetings [[fed-six-meetings-a-year-timelier-data-luzzetti-oct2026]].

## The September Jobs Report Settles the October Question

Payrolls rose only 29,000 against an 84,000 forecast, July and August were revised down by a combined 60,000, and wage growth slowed to 3% year over year [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. Unemployment rose to 4.2% mainly because 485,000 people joined the labor force, a detail that kept the household survey stronger than the payroll count [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. Jefferies' Thomas Simons called the print "the nail in the coffin for an October hike," and market-implied odds for October fell from roughly 64% a week earlier to under 23% [[us-stocks-near-record-jobs-report-tesla-nvidia-nike-oct9-2026]]. The rest of the labor data argued against a downturn, with initial claims near 197,000, close to a 57-year low, ADP private payrolls up 90,000, and the Atlanta Fed tracking third-quarter GDP at 3.7%, a low-hire, low-fire market that favors patience [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]] [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]].

## The September Minutes: Another Hike, No Date

The October 7 minutes said "most participants" judged another increase "likely appropriate by year end," and "many" called a higher path prudent insurance against stubborn inflation, yet they gave no sign whether the move would come on October 28 or December 9 [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]]. Markets took the message without accelerating the timeline, as October hike odds fell to 17.2% from 37.6% a week earlier while pricing still pointed to hikes starting in December [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. Yardeni argued the Fed should keep hiking until it reverses all three of last year's 25-basis-point insurance cuts, of which September undid only one, while BlackRock called the market's tightening expectations "too much" [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]] [[stock-futures-decline-pentagon-iran-strikes-brent-104-oct8-2026]].

## The Long End and the Term Premium

The [[treasury-yields]] at the long end kept climbing even as near-term hike odds fell, with the 10-year touching 5.349% on October 5 and about 5.365% on October 7, its highest since April 2002 [[10-year-treasury-yield-fresh-2002-high-ism-services-oct5-2026]] [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. Officials attributed the rise to expected hikes, the AI build-out, solid growth and uncertainty about the Treasury's buyback program, yet Scott Bessent's ramped-up buybacks of long-dated debt barely moved yields [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]]. The global framing gained support as the French-German 10-year spread reached its widest since 1990 and the 10-year term premium hit its highest since 2014, which suggests investors were charging for debt levels, political risk and long-run financing uncertainty on top of the expected Fed path [[five-things-week-ahead-bond-yields-french-debt-fomc-minutes-oct2026]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. Raymond James noted that the November 4 refunding could trim long-end issuance, though such a supply shift would lower yields only temporarily [[raymond-james-key-catalysts-fed-earnings-refunding-midterms-oct2026]].

## AI as a Policy Variable

Governor Lisa Cook's September 28 speech framed AI as a general-purpose technology whose investment boom is adding to inflation now through chips, construction labor and energy, while productivity gains would bring only modest and delayed disinflation [[fed-governor-cook-ai-economy-speech-sep2026]]. Williams named AI-related goods demand as an inflation driver, and the minutes listed the AI build-out among the forces behind high Treasury yields [[pce-inflation-preview-fed-rate-hike-outlook-sep2026]] [[fomc-september-minutes-another-hike-by-year-end-oct2026]]. The bond channel is measurable, since ING credited AI with about one-fifth of the rise in long-dated yields and Merrill Lynch counted nearly $280 billion of big-tech debt competing with Treasuries [[us-treasury-yields-decades-high-oil-fed-debt-ai-oct2026]]. On jobs, Cook pointed to early labor effects in coding, translation and entry-level roles, a worry that sat alongside September's 10,000 information-sector job losses [[fed-governor-cook-ai-economy-speech-sep2026]] [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]].

## Housing and Consumer Credit Feel the Hikes First

Tightening reaches debt-dependent households first, and credit card rates track prime, about three points above the funds rate, repricing within one or two billing cycles while used-car loans exceed 10% [[fed-holds-3-75-4-percent-consumer-borrowing-costs-cnbc-oct28-2026]]. Mortgage applications fell 19% year over year in September, and the 30-year fixed rate reached 7.49% in early October, its highest in nearly three years, while homebuilder stocks fell 2.9% [[fed-12-0-rate-hike-3-75-percent-housing-stocks-sep2026]] [[stocks-retreat-records-treasury-yields-24-year-high-fed-minutes-oct7-2026]]. Oxford Economics estimated that high rates put homes out of reach for 5 million more households [[fed-meeting-five-things-to-watch-warsh-oct2026]]. Mark Zandi noted that high-net-worth consumers were still spending freely on AI-stock gains while lower- and middle-income households were more cautious [[fed-holds-3-75-4-percent-consumer-borrowing-costs-cnbc-oct28-2026]].

## AI Financing and the Self-Funding Edge

Goldman Sachs called the current cycle "particularly capital intensive," and AI infrastructure is increasingly debt-financed, with SpaceX completing a $25 billion bond offering and October coverage describing up to $40 billion in chip finance tied to SpaceX and Nvidia [[goldman-sachs-fed-tightening-sp500-historical-returns]] [[fed-rate-hike-sep2026-ai-stocks-self-funding-edge]] [[morning-bid-bond-squeeze-term-premium-ai-vendor-financing-oct8-2026]]. Morgan Stanley's argument that AI is shifting from growth stories to profitability implies that the edge is financial rather than technological, because firms that fund their own capital spending are insulated from higher rates [[fed-rate-hike-sep2026-ai-stocks-self-funding-edge]]. The hold coverage made the same point from the other side, noting that tech is insulated while housing, car loans and consumer credit absorb the hit [[fed-hold-warsh-more-hikes-execution-risk-cnbc-oct27-2026]].

## Equity Markets Through the Cycle

Goldman's history sets a baseline: the S&P 500 has averaged a 2% decline in the three months after a tightening cycle begins but a 9% gain over twelve months, with earnings as the main driver [[goldman-sachs-fed-tightening-sp500-historical-returns]]. Earnings have carried the index so far, with third-quarter S&P 500 earnings expected to grow about 29% and Yardeni's figures showing a record 16.8% forward profit margin [[week-ahead-q3-earnings-season-delta-pepsi-fed-minutes-oct2026]] [[wall-street-looks-past-fed-pause-tech-earnings-ai-rebound-cnbc-oct28-2026]]. Historical comparisons diverge, since one column noted that the S&P 500 and Nasdaq had dropped an average of 10% and 12% within three months of the first hike in past cycles, while a Motley Fool piece citing Vanguard argued short-run damage stays limited unless rates rise about a point more [[trump-economy-inflation-fed-hikes-correction-history-oct2026]] [[motley-fool-fed-minutes-another-hike-stocks-bonds-warsh-oct2026]]. The rate sensitivity showed up in mid-October, when the Nasdaq fell 4.5% and the S&P 500 1.9% in one week, their weakest since June [[strong-retail-sales-consumer-resilience-after-fed-rate-hike-oct2026]].

## The November Selloff and the Jobs Rebound

On November 5 a tech-led selloff pushed the Nasdaq down more than 5% and the S&P 500 down more than 4% on fears that the post-midterm political shift could stall the AI buildout, and traders bought short-dated Treasurys, cutting the 2-year yield 10.4 basis points to 4.48% [[treasury-flight-to-safety-tech-selloff-money-market-record-nov5-2026]]. The same day, December hike odds fell to 66.1% from 82.7%, and money-market funds swelled to a record $8.016 trillion, a sign of how much cash was waiting on the sidelines [[treasury-flight-to-safety-tech-selloff-money-market-record-nov5-2026]]. The October jobs report on November 6 then reversed much of the rate move, with payrolls rising 165,000 against forecasts of roughly 60,000 to 70,000, unemployment falling to 4.1%, and August and September revised up by a combined 84,000 [[yahoo-october-jobs-165000-fed-december-hike-wages-inflation]] [[october-jobs-165000-beat-december-fed-hike-odds-77-percent-mortgage-rates]]. That lifted December hike odds back to roughly 77% to 78%, and the S&P 500 closed at 7,376.86 on November 6, its worst week since April 2025, as the semiconductor index fell 12.15% [[sp500-worst-week-since-april-2025-ai-chip-selloff-nvidia-cpi]].

## Mixed Reads on the October Jobs Report

Wages rose 3.1% from a year earlier, still trailing inflation, which keeps the Fed focused on the price-stability side of its mandate [[yahoo-october-jobs-165000-fed-december-hike-wages-inflation]]. Warsh had called employment conditions quite positive while saying inflation had been too high for close to six years, and the Fed hopes to cool prices with smaller hikes than in 2022 [[yahoo-october-jobs-165000-fed-december-hike-wages-inflation]]. Economists split on the report, with Greg Daco expecting a December hike and Chris Hodge seeing stability rather than heat, while the broader U-6 underemployment rate rose to 7.9% from 7.6% [[yahoo-october-jobs-165000-fed-december-hike-wages-inflation]] [[october-jobs-165000-beat-december-fed-hike-odds-77-percent-mortgage-rates]]. The Mortgage Bankers Association forecasts a December hike and another in the first quarter of 2027, and October CPI on November 10 and Nvidia's earnings on November 17 are the next catalysts for the rate path [[october-jobs-165000-beat-december-fed-hike-odds-77-percent-mortgage-rates]] [[sp500-worst-week-since-april-2025-ai-chip-selloff-nvidia-cpi]].

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Prices move on surprises against expectations, and those expectations are built from policy signals** — Asset prices respond to the gap between what markets already expect and what the Fed or the data delivers, so a decision that matches consensus moves little, while a shift in guidance, a credible speaker's remark or an unannounced path can move prices a lot. · *model:* [[Expectations Investing]]

**T2. With policy as the binding constraint, weak data can lift risk assets and strong data can weigh on them** — When tighter policy is what restrains valuations, a soft report lowers the expected rate path and can support equities even though it signals a weaker economy, while a strong report raises hike odds and pressures them. · *model:* [[Second-Order Thinking]]

**T3. Higher rates lower the value of distant cash flows and hit debt-funded borrowers first** — A higher risk-free rate raises the discount applied to future earnings and the cost of borrowed money, so long-duration growth stocks, capital-intensive businesses that rely on debt and borrowers on short-reset loans bear tightening first, while firms that fund their own spending gain relative ground. · *model:* [[Discounted Cash Flow]]

**T4. Long yields price more than the expected policy path** — Long-dated yields compensate holders for debt supply, fiscal and political uncertainty, and global spillovers as well as expected short rates, so they can keep rising while near-term hike odds fall, and changes in supply or buyback programs can move them only briefly. · *model:* [[Term Premium]]
