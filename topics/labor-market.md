# US Labor Market

This topic tracks 2026 US labor data (monthly payroll reports, unemployment, participation, jobless claims and wages) and how each print moved rate-hike odds and asset prices. The Fed's own decisions are covered in [[federal-reserve-monetary-policy]].

## A Summer and Fall of Payroll Whiplash

Monthly payrolls swung hard through 2026. July's report showed a 23,000 decline against forecasts of roughly +83,000 to +85,000, the first monthly drop in more than two years, and May and June were revised down by a combined 103,000 [[gold-rally-fed-jobs-data-aug2026]] [[sp500-record-high-7800-aug2026]]. August then surprised the other way at 162,000 against 53,000 expected [[september-market-weakness-setup-has-teeth]], and September came in at just 29,000 against an 84,000 consensus, with August cut to 133,000 and July flipped to −10,000 [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. Over a longer window, [[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]] counts 667,000 jobs added in the last 18 months against 1.96 million in the 18 months before, which fits J.P. Morgan's description of payroll growth as anemic at 50,000–100,000 a month [[jpmorgan-economic-outlook-what-lies-beneath-aug2026]].

## Labor Supply and the Shrinking Jobs Breakeven

The unemployment rate has repeatedly moved against payrolls because the labor force itself keeps changing. In July unemployment ticked down to 4.1% even as payrolls fell, because participation shrank to 61.4%, near a 50-year low outside the pandemic that one note partly blames on an immigration crackdown and that J.P. Morgan expected to pull unemployment toward 4.0% despite weak hiring [[gold-rally-fed-jobs-data-aug2026]] [[dow-record-close-iran-talks-wall-street-rally-aug2026]] [[us-stocks-rate-hike-bets-fade-palantir-surge]] [[jpmorgan-economic-outlook-what-lies-beneath-aug2026]]. September reversed the pattern: 485,000 people joined the labor force, participation rose to 61.8%, and unemployment climbed to 4.2% even though household-survey employment rose 406,000 [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]] [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]]. Contrarian Outlook concludes that earlier retirement and lower immigration have cut the monthly job gains needed to hold unemployment steady, so a 29,000 print is less alarming than it looks [[btx-blackrock-tech-private-equity-cef-discount]].

## Low Hiring, Low Firing

Beneath the weak payroll counts, layoffs have stayed remarkably low: CNBC describes a low-hire, low-fire economy with layoffs at a four-year-low rate [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]], and Yardeni notes that in the same weeks payrolls disappointed, initial jobless claims sat near a 57-year low at 197,000 and continuing claims fell to 1.701 million, the lowest since March 2023 [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]]. Earlier, Challenger reported July layoffs at a two-year low on the eve of July's payroll decline [[dow-record-close-iran-talks-wall-street-rally-aug2026]], and Governor Cook said unemployment and layoffs stayed low through August despite rising AI adoption [[fed-governor-cook-ai-economy-speech-sep2026]]. Unemployment has now held below 5% for 61 straight months, the longest run since a 64-month stretch that ended in 1970 [[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]].

## Wages Losing Ground to Inflation

Pay growth is where the softness shows most clearly. September average hourly earnings rose only 0.1% on the month and 3% on the year, the slowest pace since May 2021, and Heather Long said inflation was wiping out the gain entirely [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]] [[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]]. J.P. Morgan had already flagged wages running below CPI over the summer [[jpmorgan-economic-outlook-what-lies-beneath-aug2026]]. The bullish counter-reading uses Bank of America data: after-tax wages for lower-income workers rose 4.7% in August against 3.4% inflation, which that author thinks is enough to keep supporting corporate profits [[btx-blackrock-tech-private-equity-cef-discount]].

## Where the Jobs Are Going, and AI

In September, health care, construction and manufacturing added jobs, while government, temp help, information and finance cut them, and the information-services losses came amid worries about AI's effect on jobs [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]] [[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]]. LPL's Jeffrey Roach reads this as a split between goods-producing sectors that feed the AI boom and services sectors hurt by technological change [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]]. Cook sees early signs of AI reducing demand for coding, translation and new-graduate roles, and she warns that unemployment caused by AI-driven skill mismatch would leave the Fed with blunt tools, because cutting rates to help would risk more inflation [[fed-governor-cook-ai-economy-speech-sep2026]].

## From Payroll Print to Hike Odds

Each report has fed almost mechanically into rate-hike pricing. July's miss raised the odds of a September hold to 55% from 33% a week earlier [[corning-tariff-polysilicon-sp500-record-aug2026]], August's strong print pushed September hike odds to about 52% [[september-market-weakness-setup-has-teeth]], and September's miss cut October hike odds to about 22–23% from 64% a week before, lifting hold odds above 80% [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]] [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]] [[us-stocks-near-record-jobs-report-tesla-nvidia-nike-oct9-2026]]. Analysts read the weakness as a delay, not a change of course: Jefferies' Thomas Simons called it the nail in the coffin for an October hike, yet Elfenbein still expects a December hike and markets priced 67% odds of one by then, because core inflation near 3% remains the bigger worry [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]] [[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]] [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]]. Labor data also competes with inflation data for attention, and in mid-August a cooler CPI did more to lift stocks than the July payroll drop did to hurt them [[sp500-record-high-7800-aug2026]].

## Bad News Is Good News, Up to a Point

Because traders price the Fed's reaction rather than the economy itself, weak jobs data has repeatedly lifted risk assets. After the July decline, the S&P 500 closed at a record near 7,758 to cap a 3.6% week, Nvidia added about $562 billion in market value over the week, and spot gold jumped 2.4% to a seven-week high [[sp500-record-close-weak-jobs-report-aug2026]] [[us-stocks-rate-hike-bets-fade-palantir-surge]] [[gold-rally-fed-jobs-data-aug2026]]. September's miss sent the Nasdaq to a record intraday high and lifted Asian futures the following Monday, while under Chair Warsh's inflation-first Fed the logic also ran in reverse when August's hot print hurt stocks [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]] [[us-stocks-near-record-jobs-report-tesla-nvidia-nike-oct9-2026]] [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]] [[september-market-weakness-setup-has-teeth]]. The relief has limits, though: after the September report the 10-year yield dipped to about 5.16%, then climbed back toward 5.30% within the session, because the wider bond selloff, driven by inflation, government spending and AI borrowing, continued [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]] [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]].

## Reading a Noisy, Divergent Dataset

The notes keep warning against taking one print at face value. Revisions have been large and consistently downward (103,000 off May and June, then 60,000 off July and August), and Simons argues August's surge was only a rebound from weak June and July hiring, not new momentum [[gold-rally-fed-jobs-data-aug2026]] [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. The surveys also disagree with each other and with the wider economy: Yardeni lists claims near multi-decade lows, ADP private payrolls up 90,000 and a ninth month of manufacturing expansion alongside the 29,000 payroll print [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]], while the Atlanta Fed tracks third-quarter GDP at 3.7% [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. J.P. Morgan explains this gap as an AI investment boom masking real weakness in jobs and wages [[jpmorgan-economic-outlook-what-lies-beneath-aug2026]].
