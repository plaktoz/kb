# US Labor Market

This topic tracks US labor data, including monthly payrolls, unemployment, participation, layoffs and wages, and how each release moved Fed rate-hike odds, Treasury yields and equities through 2026. It also covers AI's effect on where employers hire, which the notes tie to slower entry-level hiring alongside a rising premium on demonstrated AI skill.

## A Summer and Fall of Payroll Whiplash

July fell by 23,000 against forecasts of roughly +83,000 to +85,000, the first monthly decline in about two years, and May and June were revised down by a combined 103,000 [[gold-rally-fed-jobs-data-aug2026]] [[sp500-record-high-7800-aug2026]]. August then beat expectations at 162,000 against 53,000 [[september-market-weakness-setup-has-teeth]], before September's 29,000 against an 84,000 consensus trimmed August to 133,000 and turned July into a 10,000 decline [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. October reversed the picture with 165,000 jobs against forecasts from about 59,000 to 70,000, lifting the three-month average to 137,000 against 48,000 over the past year [[october-jobs-165000-beat-december-fed-hike-odds-77-percent-mortgage-rates]] [[yahoo-october-jobs-165000-fed-december-hike-wages-inflation]]. Over a longer stretch, the economy added 667,000 jobs in the last 18 months against 1.96 million in the 18 months before, a pace J.P. Morgan calls anemic at 50,000 to 100,000 a month [[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]] [[jpmorgan-economic-outlook-what-lies-beneath-aug2026]].

## Labor Supply and the Shrinking Jobs Breakeven

Unemployment has repeatedly moved against payrolls because the labor force keeps changing. In July the jobless rate fell to 4.1% even as payrolls dropped, since participation slid to 61.4%, near a 50-year low outside the pandemic, a drop partly blamed on an immigration crackdown [[gold-rally-fed-jobs-data-aug2026]] [[jpmorgan-economic-outlook-what-lies-beneath-aug2026]]. September reversed the pattern, with 485,000 people entering the labor force and participation rising to 61.8%, which lifted unemployment to 4.2% even though household employment grew 406,000 [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. Contrarian Outlook argues that earlier retirement and lower immigration have cut the monthly job gains needed to hold unemployment steady, which makes a 29,000 print less worrying [[btx-blackrock-tech-private-equity-cef-discount]].

## Low Hiring, Low Firing

Beneath weak payroll counts, layoffs have stayed unusually low, and CNBC describes a low-hire, low-fire economy with layoffs at a four-year-low rate [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. Yardeni notes initial jobless claims near a 57-year low at 197,000 and continuing claims at 1.701 million, the lowest since March 2023 [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]], while Governor Cook said unemployment and layoffs stayed low through August despite rising AI adoption [[fed-governor-cook-ai-economy-speech-sep2026]]. Unemployment has held below 5% for 61 straight months, the longest such run since a 64-month stretch that ended in 1970 [[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]]. Hiring is the weak side of that picture, and unemployment spells were lengthening even as the October headline was strong [[october-jobs-165000-beat-december-fed-hike-odds-77-percent-mortgage-rates]].

## Wages Losing Ground to Inflation

Pay growth shows the softness most clearly. September wages rose 0.1% on the month and 3% on the year, the slowest pace since May 2021, and Heather Long argued that inflation was erasing that gain [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]] [[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]]. October's figures were firmer, at 0.4% monthly and 3.1% annually against 2.9% expected, but they still trailed inflation, which Diane Swonk calls a regressive tax on lower- and middle-income households [[october-jobs-165000-beat-december-fed-hike-odds-77-percent-mortgage-rates]] [[yahoo-october-jobs-165000-fed-december-hike-wages-inflation]]. Bank of America data showed after-tax wages for lower-income workers up 4.7% in August against 3.4% inflation, a reading Contrarian Outlook takes as support for corporate profits [[btx-blackrock-tech-private-equity-cef-discount]].

## Where the Jobs Are Going

In September health care, construction and manufacturing added jobs while government, temp help, information and finance shed them, and the information losses came amid worries about AI's effect on jobs [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]] [[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]]. LPL's Jeffrey Roach reads the pattern as a split between goods-producing sectors that feed the AI boom and services sectors hurt by technological change [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]]. October's gains came mainly from business services, healthcare and transportation, with manufacturing adding 19,000, yet residential construction shed more than 15,000 jobs as weak purchase demand hit housing [[yahoo-october-jobs-165000-fed-december-hike-wages-inflation]] [[october-jobs-165000-beat-december-fed-hike-odds-77-percent-mortgage-rates]].

## AI Is Reshaping Where Employers Hire

Several 2026 readings converge on slower hiring in AI-exposed work, especially at entry level. An IMF analysis of about 40 million online postings across 19 Asia-Pacific economies found demand in more AI-exposed occupations down nearly 10%, an effect that appears to have stabilized, while Beige Book reports from Dallas, Cleveland and Minneapolis describe delayed entry-level hiring tied to AI even as overall employment rose modestly [[ai-careers-weekly-six-reports-hiring-oct-2026]]. Cook sees early signs of the same shift in coding, translation and new-graduate roles, even though unemployment and layoffs have so far stayed low [[fed-governor-cook-ai-economy-speech-sep2026]]. Executives still plan to grow, with 89% of CEOs in KPMG's survey expecting higher headcount within three years, though 75% say AI has changed entry-level skill requirements [[ai-careers-weekly-six-reports-hiring-oct-2026]].

## A Rising Premium on AI Skills

Demand for demonstrated AI skill is rising in both pay and scarcity. Oyster finds explicit AI requirements in job postings tripled from about 5% to 15% between 2022 and 2026, a quarter of surveyed financial firms tie raises, promotions and bonuses to AI proficiency, and ManpowerGroup ranks AI skills above IT skills as the hardest to find [[ai-careers-weekly-six-reports-hiring-oct-2026]]. Firms are building that capability internally, with 53% relying on training against 14% on hiring, and 95% of HR leaders say staff already do AI work outside their job descriptions [[ai-careers-weekly-six-reports-hiring-oct-2026]].

## From Payroll Print to Hike Odds

Hike odds have swung with each print: July's miss lifted September hold odds to 55% from 33% [[corning-tariff-polysilicon-sp500-record-aug2026]], and August's strong print pushed September hike odds to about 52% [[september-market-weakness-setup-has-teeth]]. September's 29,000 cut October hike odds to roughly 22 to 23%, from 64% a week earlier, and Jefferies' Thomas Simons called it the nail in the coffin for an October hike [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]] [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. Analysts read that weakness as a delay rather than a change of course, since Elfenbein still expected a likely December hike, markets priced 67% odds of one by then, and core inflation near 3% remained the bigger worry [[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]] [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]]. October's 165,000 gain then lifted CME FedWatch odds of a December hike to roughly 77%, and the Mortgage Bankers Association forecasts a December hike and another in the first quarter of 2027 [[october-jobs-165000-beat-december-fed-hike-odds-77-percent-mortgage-rates]].

## Hike Odds Firm Heading Into December

Early November data moved the debate further toward a December move. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.884% on November 9 after the October jobs report priced out near-term rate cuts, ahead of a CPI release that consensus put at 3.9% headline and 2.8% core [[treasury-yields-rise-cpi-preview-jobs-mortgage-rates-nov9-2026]]. October CPI then printed 0.4% against 0.3% expected, with core at 0.3% against 0.2%, lifting the odds of at least a quarter-point December hike from 75% to 100% on CME FedWatch while the Dollar Index extended its climb above 102.50 [[december-fed-hike-odds-jump-dollar-extends-gains-tastyfx-nov12-2026]]. The AP's account of the same week framed a dilemma in which strong hiring could cushion a slowdown in data center construction but could also push inflation higher [[ai-stocks-halt-worldwide-selloff-midterms-data-center-ap-nov8-2026]].

## Bad News Is Good News, Up to a Point

Because traders price the Fed's reaction rather than the economy itself, weak jobs data has repeatedly lifted risk assets, from the record S&P 500 close after the July decline to the Nasdaq's record intraday high after September's miss [[sp500-record-close-weak-jobs-report-aug2026]] [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]] [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]]. Strong data reverses the effect, since August's hot payrolls hurt stocks [[september-market-weakness-setup-has-teeth]] and the October jobs and CPI data together pushed hike odds up [[december-fed-hike-odds-jump-dollar-extends-gains-tastyfx-nov12-2026]]. The counter-risk is an AI-led selloff, which the AP reported left the S&P 500 down 2.7% for the week, its worst since April 2025 [[ai-stocks-halt-worldwide-selloff-midterms-data-center-ap-nov8-2026]]. TastyFX warns that a sustained tech and AI selloff could itself make the Fed hold, so the reaction to labor data depends on what else is moving markets [[december-fed-hike-odds-jump-dollar-extends-gains-tastyfx-nov12-2026]].

## Reading a Noisy, Divergent Dataset

Revisions have swung in both directions, from a combined 103,000 taken off May and June earlier in the year to an October report that revised August up 53,000 to 186,000 and September up 31,000 to 60,000 [[gold-rally-fed-jobs-data-aug2026]] [[october-jobs-165000-beat-december-fed-hike-odds-77-percent-mortgage-rates]]. Simons argues August's surge was a rebound from weak June and July hiring rather than new momentum [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]], and Yahoo's economists split on whether one strong report signals real labor-market heat or simply noisy data [[yahoo-october-jobs-165000-fed-december-hike-wages-inflation]]. The headline also hides weakness, since unemployment fell to 4.1% while the broader U-6 measure rose to 7.9% from 7.6% [[october-jobs-165000-beat-december-fed-hike-odds-77-percent-mortgage-rates]]. The surveys disagree with each other and with the wider economy: Yardeni cites claims near multi-decade lows and ADP private payrolls up 90,000 [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]], the Atlanta Fed tracks third-quarter GDP at 3.7% [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]], and J.P. Morgan reads the gap as an AI investment boom masking real weakness in jobs and wages [[jpmorgan-economic-outlook-what-lies-beneath-aug2026]].
