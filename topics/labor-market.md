# US Labor Market

This topic tracks 2026 US labor data (monthly payroll reports, unemployment, participation, jobless claims and wages) and how each print moved rate-hike odds and asset prices, and as of late October it also covers AI reshaping where employers hire: slower hiring in exposed roles alongside a rising premium on demonstrated AI proficiency. The Fed's own decisions are covered in [[federal-reserve-monetary-policy]].

## A Summer and Fall of Payroll Whiplash

Monthly payrolls swung hard through 2026. July fell by 23,000 against forecasts of roughly +83,000 to +85,000, the first monthly decline in about two years, and May and June were revised down by a combined 103,000 [[gold-rally-fed-jobs-data-aug2026]] [[sp500-record-high-7800-aug2026]]. August then beat expectations at 162,000 against 53,000 [[september-market-weakness-setup-has-teeth]], before September's 29,000 against an 84,000 consensus trimmed August to 133,000 and turned July into a 10,000 decline [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. Over longer stretches, [[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]] counts 667,000 jobs added in the last 18 months against 1.96 million in the 18 months before, a pace J.P. Morgan describes as anemic at 50,000 to 100,000 a month [[jpmorgan-economic-outlook-what-lies-beneath-aug2026]].

## Labor Supply and the Shrinking Jobs Breakeven

Unemployment has repeatedly moved against payrolls because the labor force keeps changing. In July the jobless rate ticked down to 4.1% even as payrolls fell, since participation slid to 61.4%, near a 50-year low outside the pandemic, a drop partly blamed on an immigration crackdown [[gold-rally-fed-jobs-data-aug2026]] [[jpmorgan-economic-outlook-what-lies-beneath-aug2026]]. September reversed the pattern: 485,000 people entered the labor force and participation rose to 61.8%, lifting unemployment to 4.2% even though household employment grew 406,000 [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. Contrarian Outlook argues that earlier retirement and lower immigration have cut the monthly job gains needed to hold unemployment steady, which makes a 29,000 print less worrying [[btx-blackrock-tech-private-equity-cef-discount]].
## Low Hiring, Low Firing

Beneath the weak payroll counts, layoffs have stayed unusually low. CNBC describes a low-hire, low-fire economy with layoffs at a four-year-low rate [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]], and Yardeni notes that initial jobless claims sat near a 57-year low at 197,000 while continuing claims fell to 1.701 million, the lowest since March 2023 [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]]. Challenger's July layoff count was the lowest in two years [[dow-record-close-iran-talks-wall-street-rally-aug2026]], and Governor Cook said unemployment and layoffs stayed low through August despite rising AI adoption [[fed-governor-cook-ai-economy-speech-sep2026]]. Unemployment has now held below 5% for 61 straight months, the longest such run since a 64-month stretch that ended in 1970 [[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]].

## Wages Losing Ground to Inflation

Pay growth shows the softness most clearly. September average hourly earnings rose only 0.1% on the month and 3% on the year, the slowest pace since May 2021, and Heather Long argued that inflation was erasing that gain [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]] [[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]]. J.P. Morgan had already flagged wages running below CPI over the summer [[jpmorgan-economic-outlook-what-lies-beneath-aug2026]]. The bullish counter-reading uses Bank of America data: after-tax wages for lower-income workers rose 4.7% in August against 3.4% inflation, which Contrarian Outlook takes as enough to keep supporting corporate profits [[btx-blackrock-tech-private-equity-cef-discount]].

## Where the Jobs Are Going

In September health care, construction and manufacturing added jobs, while government, temp help, information and finance shed them [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. The information-services losses came amid worries about AI's effect on jobs [[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]]. LPL's Jeffrey Roach reads the pattern as a split between goods-producing sectors that feed the AI boom and services sectors hurt by technological change [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]].

## AI Is Reshaping Where Employers Hire

Several 2026 readings converge on slower hiring in AI-exposed work, especially at entry level. An IMF analysis of about 40 million online postings across 19 Asia-Pacific economies found demand in more AI-exposed occupations down nearly 10%, an effect that appears to have stabilized, while Federal Reserve Beige Book reports from Dallas, Cleveland and Minneapolis describe delayed entry-level hiring tied to AI even though overall employment still rose modestly [[ai-careers-weekly-six-reports-hiring-oct-2026]]. Cook sees early signs of the same shift in coding, translation and new-graduate roles, while unemployment and layoffs have so far stayed low [[fed-governor-cook-ai-economy-speech-sep2026]]. Executives still plan to grow: 89% of CEOs in KPMG's survey expect higher headcount within three years, though 75% say AI has changed entry-level skill requirements [[ai-careers-weekly-six-reports-hiring-oct-2026]].

## A Rising Premium on AI Skills

Demand for demonstrated AI skill is climbing in both pay and scarcity. Explicit AI requirements in job postings roughly tripled between 2022 and 2026, from about 5% to 15% of postings according to Oyster, a quarter of surveyed financial firms now tie raises, promotions and bonuses to AI proficiency per Selby Jennings, and ManpowerGroup ranks AI skills above IT skills as the hardest to find [[ai-careers-weekly-six-reports-hiring-oct-2026]]. Firms are building that capability internally, with 53% relying on training against 14% on hiring, and 95% of HR leaders say staff already do AI work outside their job descriptions [[ai-careers-weekly-six-reports-hiring-oct-2026]].

## From Payroll Print to Hike Odds

July's miss lifted September hold odds to 55% from 33% [[corning-tariff-polysilicon-sp500-record-aug2026]], and August's strong print pushed September hike odds to about 52% [[september-market-weakness-setup-has-teeth]]. September's miss cut October hike odds to roughly 22 to 23%, from 64% a week before, and pushed hold odds above 80% [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]] [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]] [[us-stocks-near-record-jobs-report-tesla-nvidia-nike-oct9-2026]]. Analysts read the weakness as a delay rather than a change of course: Jefferies' Thomas Simons called it the nail in the coffin for an October hike, yet Elfenbein still expects a likely December hike and markets priced 67% odds of one by then, because core inflation near 3% remains the bigger worry [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]] [[cws-market-review-big-bank-q3-2026-earnings-preview-sp500-record-oct6-2026]] [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]]. Inflation data can matter more than labor data for the market: in mid-August cooler CPI and PPI, not payrolls, pushed the S&P 500 above 7,800 for the first time [[sp500-record-high-7800-aug2026]].

## Bad News Is Good News, Up to a Point

Because traders price the Fed's reaction rather than the economy itself, weak jobs data has repeatedly lifted risk assets. After the July decline the S&P 500 closed at a record near 7,758 to cap a 3.6% week, Nvidia added about $562 billion in market value over that week, and spot gold jumped 2.4% to a seven-week high [[sp500-record-close-weak-jobs-report-aug2026]] [[gold-rally-fed-jobs-data-aug2026]]. September's miss sent the Nasdaq to a record intraday high and lifted Asian futures the following Monday [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]] [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]]. The relief has limits: under the same inflation-first logic strong payrolls hurt stocks in August [[september-market-weakness-setup-has-teeth]], and after the September report the 10-year yield dipped to 5.16% before climbing back toward 5.30% as the broader bond selloff, driven by inflation, government spending and AI-related borrowing, continued [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]] [[asian-stocks-rise-hike-bets-ease-brent-103-oct5-2026]].

## Reading a Noisy, Divergent Dataset

Revisions have been large and consistently downward: 103,000 off May and June, then 60,000 off July and August. Simons argues August's surge was a rebound from weak June and July hiring rather than new momentum [[gold-rally-fed-jobs-data-aug2026]] [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. The surveys also disagree with each other and with the wider economy. Yardeni cites claims near multi-decade lows and ADP private payrolls up 90,000 alongside the 29,000 payroll print [[yardeni-economic-week-ahead-fomc-minutes-oct5-9-2026]], while the Atlanta Fed tracks third-quarter GDP at 3.7% [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]]. J.P. Morgan reads that gap as an AI investment boom masking real weakness in jobs and wages [[jpmorgan-economic-outlook-what-lies-beneath-aug2026]].

**T4.
