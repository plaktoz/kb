# Anthropic

Anthropic, the company behind Claude, has become a bellwether for the AI capital cycle: its ~$2 trillion IPO ambition, its compute commitments and the web of supplier-investors around it tie the fortunes of Nvidia, Google, Microsoft and a long tail of neoclouds to one young lab, while its products move software stocks and its push to slow the frontier keeps it in conflict with Washington, and in negotiation with it.

## Valuation and the Investor-Supplier Web

Anthropic's private valuation has compounded at extraordinary speed. Forbes' 2026 AI 50 put it at $380 billion on $4.5 billion of prior-year revenue [[forbes-ai-50-2026-list]], and within months it had filed confidentially to go public at roughly $2 trillion, having raised $143 billion of its ~$161 billion lifetime capital (debt included) in just the twelve months to mid-August; together with OpenAI and Databricks it accounts for 92% of everything the IA40 cohort has ever raised [[madrona-ia40-summit-who-owns-customer-ai-agents]]. Much of that money comes from companies that also sell Anthropic compute. Microsoft's $5 billion stake from November 2025 came paired with a $30 billion Azure purchase commitment and booked a $3.2 billion gain in a single quarter that nearly matched Microsoft's full-year OpenAI gain [[microsoft-anthropic-openai-investment-returns-q4-2026]], while Google has committed up to $40 billion alongside its TPU deals [[alphabet-supply-constrained-anthropic-5gw-tpu-deal]], so Anthropic's private marks already show up in its suppliers' reported earnings (see [[microsoft]]).

## Revenue Growth and the Profitability Question

The growth behind the valuation is steep, but the numbers mix filed financials with reported run rates and projections: the IPO prospectus showed 2025 revenue up about 12x to roughly $4.6 billion against an $8 billion operating loss, a $42 billion net loss and $20.3 billion of year-end cash [[stocks-grind-lower-interest-rate-pressure-fico-anthropic-sep2026]]. The annualized run rate then passed $30 billion by April 2026, up from about $9 billion at the end of 2025 [[alphabet-supply-constrained-anthropic-5gw-tpu-deal]], and by August it was cited near $65 billion and growing faster than OpenAI's [[openai-private-safety-processing-vs-anthropic-retention]]. All-In's hosts projected $100–120 billion of annualized revenue by year-end at gross margins above 80%, and Gavin Baker called the token economics profitable for everyone in the chain [[anthropic-2t-ipo-zuck-ai-manifesto-nvidia-500b-grok]], [[all-in-chip-stocks-frontier-labs-ai-pacing-letter]]; the profit claim is narrower than it sounds, though. Anthropic reported only a small adjusted Q2 profit on more than $11.5 billion of sales [[anthropic-ipo-3-reasons-to-avoid]], and Ben Thompson notes that its adjusted profitability excludes training costs, while the bigger capital risk sits with the hyperscalers and neoclouds building capacity for it [[frontier-overhangs-anthropic-pacing-strategy]].

## The IPO: Timing, Scale, and the Bear Case

The listing is expected to rival SpaceX's record June offering, with an anticipated raise of about $100 billion and Nvidia in talks to anchor up to $10 billion of it [[nvidia-anthropic-ipo-10-billion-circular-demand]]. Early reporting pointed to an October debut, with a public filing as soon as late August, which would put Anthropic on the market ahead of OpenAI [[anthropic-2t-ipo-zuck-ai-manifesto-nvidia-500b-grok]], but by late September the IPO was not expected until after the November midterms [[stocks-grind-lower-interest-rate-pressure-fico-anthropic-sep2026]]. The bear case rests on base rates rather than business quality: Truist found that the last 30 major tech IPOs suffered an average 55% maximum drawdown in their first year, and a $2 trillion debut would sit far above the 30x price-to-sales level that has historically reverted [[anthropic-ipo-3-reasons-to-avoid]]. Bulls concede the headline number is partly a banker construct, too: Baker suspects the $2 trillion figure is sandbagged, and the leaked target itself acts as a reflexive anchor for investor expectations [[anthropic-2t-ipo-zuck-ai-manifesto-nvidia-500b-grok]].

## Index Fast-Track and Mechanical Demand

SpaceX's IPO pushed index providers to adopt fast-track rules, so a ~$2 trillion Anthropic could enter major benchmarks within days instead of waiting months for a scheduled reconstitution [[anthropic-ipo-etf-inclusion-qqq-iwf-fpx]]. Under FTSE Russell's rule it could join the Russell growth indexes tracked by IWF after its fifth trading day, provided it ranks in the Russell Top 500 and is classified as growth. Nasdaq's rule would add it to the Nasdaq-100 (QQQ) after 15 trading days if it ranks among the 40 largest components, and FPX would hold it as a newly public company for up to 1,000 trading days. Index funds must buy to track their benchmarks, so these rules create near-term demand regardless of fundamentals, and holders of broad growth funds would get Anthropic exposure almost at once, which the author frames as a more measured alternative to chasing IPO-day shares.

## Locking Up Compute Years in Advance

Anthropic's planned spending dwarfs its revenue: the prospectus outlines $518 billion of AI infrastructure buildout against ~$4.6 billion of 2025 revenue [[stocks-grind-lower-interest-rate-pressure-fico-anthropic-sep2026]]. The commitments run through nearly every kind of supplier, starting with Google's 1 million TPUs and 1+ GW under an October 2025 deal, followed by about 5 GW of next-generation capacity through Google and Broadcom from 2027 [[alphabet-supply-constrained-anthropic-5gw-tpu-deal]], plus up to 5 GW from Amazon [[nvidia-anthropic-ipo-10-billion-circular-demand]]. Newer deals reach further down the market: British startup Nscale is renting Anthropic $45 billion of Nvidia Vera Rubin capacity over six years from late 2027, capping a run that also included AMD, SpaceX and Volta [[anthropic-45-billion-compute-deal-nscale]]; the $10 billion Volta deal covers a 133 MW Norwegian site built with crypto miner Bitdeer [[anthropic-signs-10-billion-deal-volta]]; and a 20-year, ~$19 billion lease sent former bitcoin miner TeraWulf's stock up nearly 20% [[terawulf-anthropic-lease-stock-surge]]. Anthropic is buying from whoever can deliver, including repurposed mining sites, in a market where even Alphabet calls itself supply constrained despite up to $205 billion of 2026 capex; the wider capacity and power race is tracked in [[ai-infrastructure]].

## Diversifying the Hardware Stack

To avoid depending on any single chip vendor, Anthropic is building a custom silicon team to co-design inference chips with its models, following OpenAI, Google and Meta, with Samsung reported as a possible fabrication partner [[anthropic-custom-ai-chip-team]]. The economic case is that co-designed inference silicon can cut total cost of ownership by up to 65% compared with Nvidia GPUs, although training is still expected to need Nvidia hardware. The $11.6 billion, seven-year Akamai deal diversifies along another axis: it buys CPUs rather than GPUs, matching the orchestration-heavy compute that AI agents consume [[anthropic-akamai-11-6-billion-cloud-deal]]. Spreading demand across TPUs, Amazon, Nvidia-based neoclouds, AMD, CPUs and in-house chips also limits how much of any IPO proceeds would flow back to Nvidia [[nvidia-anthropic-ipo-10-billion-circular-demand]].

## Circular Financing and Counterparty Concentration

Anthropic sits at the center of the AI industry's circular deal structures: Nvidia's $10 billion commitment in November 2025 was tied to Anthropic buying Nvidia-powered compute, and an IPO anchor stake on top would extend the loop into public equity, leaving Nvidia exposed to correlated hits on GPU orders and investment marks if AI spending slowed [[nvidia-anthropic-ipo-10-billion-circular-demand]]. Broadcom is likewise reported to be assembling up to $60 billion to lend to AI customers including Anthropic [[indexes-rise-weak-jobs-report-nasdaq-high-stock-market-today-oct2-2026]], while the Akamai deal inverts the usual pattern by giving the customer a warrant convertible into roughly 5% of the supplier's shares, vesting as its spending milestones are hit [[anthropic-akamai-11-6-billion-cloud-deal]]. For suppliers, the flip side is concentration risk: a Broadcom filing warns that Anthropic's use of the expanded TPU capacity depends on its continued commercial success, which leaves part of Google Cloud's $514 billion backlog riding on one company [[alphabet-supply-constrained-anthropic-5gw-tpu-deal]], and TeraWulf's analysts flag the same tenant-concentration exposure at a much smaller scale [[terawulf-anthropic-lease-stock-surge]].

## Claude as the Business Driver

Claude product launches have become market-moving events: the launch of Claude Cowork reportedly wiped $285 billion off SaaS, IT and legal-services stocks, and Claude passed ChatGPT as the App Store's most-downloaded app [[forbes-ai-50-2026-list]]. Analysts cite Claude Code's automation of COBOL modernization as a direct threat to IBM's consulting revenue, one reason IBM is down 21% year-to-date while its industry has more than doubled [[ibm-slumps-21-ytd-claude-code-disruption-risk]]. Anthropic is also consolidating its product surface, folding Chat, Cowork, Artifacts and Claude Design into one auto-routed window whose new Presentations and Docs features reach paying Pro and Max subscribers first [[anthropic-claude-chat-cowork-unified-interface]], and it is reaching into science through a Bay Area wet lab that grew out of its April acquisition of Coefficient Bio; the lab avoids drug discovery so that it doesn't compete with pharma partners like Novo Nordisk [[anthropic-wet-biology-lab-life-sciences]]. Inside the company, Anthropic says it writes about 200x more code than 18 months ago while customers manage 2–3x at most, a gap IA40 speakers blamed on organizations rather than the technology [[madrona-ia40-summit-who-owns-customer-ai-agents]]; the agent mechanics behind that are covered in [[ai-agents]].

## Pricing, Tiers, and the Enterprise Race

Anthropic overtook OpenAI in Ramp's business-adoption data in spring 2026 and still led 43.5% to 39.7% in July, though OpenAI was growing faster after its GPT-5.6 launch and many companies pay for both, so the race is not zero-sum. The premium tier shows the costs of Anthropic's positioning: Fable 5 drew 11.4% of Anthropic dollars on just 6% of tokens, with adoption held back by price and by a 30-day data-retention rule for safety monitoring [[openai-narrows-anthropic-business-lead-ramp-aug2026]], a rule OpenAI quickly attacked with a zero-retention alternative [[openai-private-safety-processing-vs-anthropic-retention]]. Anthropic has answered by pushing down the cost curve, pricing Claude Opus 5 at about half of Fable 5 as US labs cut prices against cheaper Chinese models [[ai-model-price-war-openai-anthropic-deepseek-aug2026]] and then shipping Opus 5.5 with near-Fable 5.1 performance at 40% less than Opus 5 [[claude-opus-5-5-launch]]. Partners are hedging too: Microsoft, itself an Anthropic investor, pitches swappable models and claims its MAI Cyber One Flash beats Anthropic's Mythos at half the cost [[microsoft-openai-anthropic-model-competition-nadella]], while Anthropic CTO Rahul Patil counters that hedging across providers forces "least common denominator" builds [[madrona-ia40-summit-who-owns-customer-ai-agents]], the same modularization fight Thompson sees eroding the pricing power of frontier models [[frontier-overhangs-anthropic-pacing-strategy]].

## Copyright and IP Exposure

Training-data litigation is Anthropic's most concrete legal liability: a ruling in the Bartz case held that acquiring training data through piracy is not protected by fair use, even if training on copyrighted works may be [[sony-warner-sue-anthropic-music-copyright]], and the case settled for $1.5 billion, described as the largest in US history, over roughly 7 million pirated books [[anthropic-copyright-settlement-open-source-ai-distillation]]. Follow-on suits have arrived, with Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell alleging that Anthropic torrented books containing lyrics and sheet music and naming Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann personally, on top of separate Concord and UMG actions that are still pending; Anthropic says it will defend itself. All-In's hosts point to an awkward symmetry: Anthropic relies on fair use for its own training while branding rivals' mass querying of Claude as "industrial-scale distillation attacks" [[anthropic-copyright-settlement-open-source-ai-distillation]], and Opus 5.5 now ships with built-in anti-distillation measures [[claude-opus-5-5-launch]].

## The Pentagon Dispute and the White House

Anthropic's sharpest clash with Washington began when it refused to loosen safeguards against fully autonomous weapons and mass surveillance, and the administration responded by labeling it a "supply chain risk," which barred federal agencies from working with the company [[anthropic-pentagon-supply-chain-risk-court-win]], [[forbes-ai-50-2026-list]]. In August, Judge Rita Lin ruled the designation unlawful First Amendment retaliation and a violation of due process, noting that the Defense Department was pursuing contracts and Defense Production Act status with Anthropic at the same time; a separate suit continues in Washington, D.C. Relations have since thawed in places: Amodei signed Trump's voluntary self-policing accord alongside other AI chiefs [[trump-tech-ceos-ai-self-policing-accord-sep2026]] and was then invited to his first one-on-one White House dinner, even though Trump dismisses safety concerns as politically motivated and the fight over the Pentagon label is still unresolved [[dario-amodei-trump-white-house-dinner]].

## Pacing Advocacy as Policy and Strategy

Anthropic's distinctive political bet is that government should help slow the frontier: Amodei's "We Must Pace the Frontier" essay lays out a path from unilateral commitments, to coordination among labs in democratic countries under antitrust waivers, to eventual arms-control-style treaties [[dario-amodei-we-must-pace-the-frontier]], and the company backs the pro-regulation group Public First Action against Leading the Future, the super PAC funded by a16z and Greg Brockman [[a16z-brockman-ltf-data-center-ads-midterms]]. Skeptics read the stance as business strategy, with Thompson arguing that pacing conveniently solves several of Anthropic's commercial "overhangs" and surfaced just as OpenAI retook the frontier lead [[frontier-overhangs-anthropic-pacing-strategy]], and David Sacks calling the related lab-employee letter "monopoly masking" by a duopoly pulling away from the field [[all-in-chip-stocks-frontier-labs-ai-pacing-letter]]. The stance also sits awkwardly with the IPO: TechCrunch's Equity hosts argued that a lab close to listing can less afford to argue for slowing down than OpenAI can [[sam-altman-ai-decel-debate]], yet Amodei kept urging caution as the listing slipped past the midterms [[stocks-grind-lower-interest-rate-pressure-fico-anthropic-sep2026]]. The safety substance of the debate is covered in [[ai-safety]] and [[llm-security]].
