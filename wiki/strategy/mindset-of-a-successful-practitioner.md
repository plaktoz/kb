## Summary

Lori Kennedy outlines 5 mindset elements she considers essential for health practitioners building a successful business. The framework centers on inner conviction, purpose-driven work, and sustained effort over the long arc of building a business. Kennedy argues that mindset — not tactics — is the primary differentiator between practitioners who succeed and those who quit.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Self-Belief]]**: Success requires inner conviction that survives obstacles; external validation is a substitute that fails when things get hard.
- **[[Specificity of Purpose]]**: A deep, visceral sense of purpose — not just a "cool idea" — sustains action through inevitable setbacks and identity shifts.
- **[[Growth Mindset]]**: Embracing new approaches rather than defending existing habits; resistance to change is "fighting for your own limitations."
- **[[Work Ethic in Entrepreneurship]]**: Startup phases demand intensive effort — the "10 hours a week" narrative typically follows years of hard, unrecognized work.
- **[[Resilience]]**: Entrepreneurship is a rollercoaster that reshapes identity; tolerating and even embracing the difficulty is itself a competitive advantage.

## Key Takeaways

- **Self-Belief**: Seeking reassurance signals a lack of inner conviction that won't survive real obstacles.
- **Purpose**: Kennedy's own purpose evolved — candida → weight loss → coaching — but always followed an internal drive.
- **Work Hard**: Startup success rarely comes from short hours; years of sacrifice typically precede the "easy" phase.
- **Growth Mindset**: Saying "that's not how it's done" is fighting for your limitations, not protecting good judgment.
- **Enjoy the Ride**: The transformation that emerges from entrepreneurial struggle justifies the difficulty.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Growth Mindset]]**: Kennedy's 4th element maps directly onto Dweck's framework — treating challenges as stepping stones rather than threats is the operational definition of a growth mindset in a business context.
- **[[Intrinsic Motivation]]**: Purpose described as a "pit-of-your-stomach feeling" is intrinsic motivation at its most visceral — Kennedy implicitly argues that extrinsic goals (money, status) fail as fuel when obstacles mount, while intrinsic drive persists.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does Kennedy identify as the most foundational mindset element for practitioner success?
**A**: Self-belief over external validation — only inner conviction sustains you through obstacles, since others' reassurance disappears when things get hard.

**Q2**: How does Kennedy describe the nature of purpose, and why does it matter more than a "cool idea"?
**A**: Purpose is a "pit-of-your-stomach feeling" — visceral and driving — that sustains action through inevitable setbacks in a way that intellectual interest alone cannot.

**Q3**: How should a practitioner apply the "enjoy the ride" principle when facing entrepreneurial difficulty?
**A**: Rather than treating difficulties as signs of failure, recognize that the struggle itself reshapes your identity and sense of possibility — the transformation on the other side is the payoff.
