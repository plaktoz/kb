## Summary

[[BCG]] proposes a "Growth Compass" for CEOs: six growth vectors combined into a portfolio of mutually reinforcing bets, calibrated to what growth contributes to shareholder returns. AI cuts across all six vectors and changes the economics of growth. CEOs must keep recalibrating and distinguish execution problems from strategic weaknesses.

## Core Concepts

- [[Growth Compass]]: framework for choosing and combining growth vectors.
- Six vectors: accelerate the commercial engine, sharpen value and revenue management, new demand spaces, new geographies, new businesses, acquisitions and partnerships.
- [[Total Shareholder Return]]: the common yardstick; revenue growth drives 49% of 10-year TSR for top-quartile S&P 500 firms.
- [[Right to Win]] and "welcome to win": competitive advantage plus value created for local stakeholders.
- [[Revenue Operations]]: integrating sales, marketing and customer success to execute growth.
- Examples: [[Trane Technologies]], [[Aurobindo Pharma]], [[Starbucks]], [[SATS]].

## Key Takeaways

- **Multiple Paths**: Mentions of "multiple growth paths" rose 46.5% quarter over quarter across about 7,000 earnings calls.
- **Growth Value**: Revenue growth is 49% of 10-year TSR for top-quartile S&P 500 firms.
- **Not All Growth Pays**: Only 60% of companies achieved value-creating growth in 2015-2025.
- **Reinforcing Combinations**: Combine vectors that create more value together than apart.
- **Risk as Enabler**: Pair growth and risk leaders; build buffers into timelines and supply chains.
- **AI Leaders**: Among the top 6% in AI adoption, most reinvested productivity gains into growth.
- **Diagnose Before Reacting**: Temporary sales dips warrant fixes; sustained share loss signals strategy reset.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Combined bets beat isolated bets** — Growth vectors that share capabilities, customers or technology create more value together than separately. → explains: Reinforcing Combinations, Multiple Paths

**T2. Separate execution failure from strategic failure** — Underperformance demands a different response depending on whether the opportunity's economics still hold. → explains: Diagnose Before Reacting

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of the Growth Compass?
**A**: CEOs should build a company-specific portfolio of mutually reinforcing growth vectors, anchored to growth's contribution to shareholder returns, and recalibrate it as conditions change.

**Q2**: What data supports the importance of growth?
**A**: BCG finds revenue growth accounts for 49% of 10-year TSR among top-quartile S&P 500 performers, yet only 60% of companies achieved value-creating growth in 2015-2025.

**Q3**: How do you know when to fix execution versus rethink strategy?
**A**: A temporary sales drop may warrant an operational response; sustained share loss to an emerging competitor or changed economics signals a reset. Watch customer, competitor, regulatory, economic and talent shifts.
