## Summary

McKinsey's Global 2,000 analysis for 2016–2025 finds that programmatic M&A, a steady cadence of small and midsize deals tied to strategic themes, delivers the highest median excess TSR of any approach. An Australian extension covering 365 listed companies shows the same pattern, even though few Australian firms use the approach. The authors argue that deal scarcity in Australia reflects board and management choices and capability gaps, and that dealmaking should be built as a repeatable capability.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Programmatic M&A]]**: More than two small or midsize deals a year (ten or more over the decade in the Australian extension), each tied to a strategic theme.
- **[[Total Shareholder Return]] (excess TSR)**: The performance measure used to compare M&A approaches against benchmarks.
- **Four M&A approaches**: Programmatic, selective, large-deal and organic.
- **[[Programmatic Divestitures]]**: Continuous portfolio reshaping on the sell side, not accumulation alone.
- **[[Large-Deal M&A]]**: A coin flip on its own, but better when pursued by programmatic acquirers.
- **[[ResMed]]**: Australian example with 18 acquisitions that built an out-of-hospital software portfolio.
- **Global examples**: [[Constellation Software]], [[Danaher]], [[ASSA ABLOY]] and [[Hitachi]].
- **[[Integration Playbooks]] and postmortems**: Capabilities that programmatic acquirers are more likely to have.

## Key Takeaways

- **Global Edge**: Programmatic acquirers earned about 3.5% median excess TSR a year; organic growers trailed by over 2%.
- **Spread Widening**: The gap between programmatic and organic approaches is about 5.7 percentage points.
- **Australian Scarcity**: Under 5% of Australian-listed companies took a programmatic approach versus roughly 20% in the US and Canada.
- **Australian Results**: 32 programmatic acquirers earned 1.7 points median excess TSR; organic earned -0.7.
- **Small Deals Dominate**: About 95% of acquisitions by number targeted under 5% of acquirer market cap.
- **Large Deals With Experience**: Programmatic large-deal acquirers earned about 4% globally and 2.8% in Australia.
- **Sell Side Matches**: Programmatic divestors earned two points a year; single large disposals trailed by 8.5.
- **Capability Gap**: Programmatic acquirers are twice as likely to have dedicated integration teams and 2.5 times likelier to run postmortems.
- **Choice Not Constraint**: Authors attribute Australian deal scarcity to board and management choices, not market structure.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Capability compounds through repetition** — Skill at a complex activity improves with each repetition, so frequent practice raises the odds that later, larger bets succeed. · *model:* [[Deliberate Practice]]
→ explains: Large Deals With Experience, Capability Gap, Global Edge

**T2. Many small bets limit exposure to any single failure** — Spreading value across many modest transactions keeps any one misstep from seriously harming the whole. · *model:* [[Diversification]]
→ explains: Small Deals Dominate, Large Deals With Experience

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about M&A strategy?
**A**: Programmatic M&A, a steady cadence of small and midsize deals tied to strategic themes, is the approach most likely to create value with the least risk. This holds globally and in Australia, where few companies have tried it.

**Q2**: What data shows programmatic acquirers outperform in Australia?
**A**: Among 365 Australian-listed companies, the 32 programmatic acquirers had a median excess TSR of 1.7 points a year, versus -0.5 for selective, 0.5 for large-deal and -0.7 for organic companies.

**Q3**: What should a company do to build a programmatic M&A engine?
**A**: Treat dealmaking as a capability: link deals to a few strategic themes, keep deals small, and build standing pipelines, integration playbooks, integration teams and postmortem habits, matched by disciplined divestitures.
