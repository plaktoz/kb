## Summary

[[Felix Oberholzer-Gee]] argues that strategy is simply a plan to create value — not profit — for customers, employees, and suppliers. The [[Value Stick]] framework visualizes total value as the gap between willingness to pay (WTP) and willingness to sell (WTS), with profit being a middle wedge split from that gap. Strategy execution reduces to two moves: raise WTP or lower WTS.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Value Stick]]**: A diagram spanning from [[Willingness to Pay]] (top) to [[Willingness to Sell]] (bottom); total value created is the spread between them.
- **[[Willingness to Pay]]**: The maximum price a customer would pay before walking away; raising it grows the pie.
- **[[Willingness to Sell]]**: The minimum compensation an employee accepts to work; lowering it grows the pie.
- **Customer Delight**: Value captured by customers = WTP minus actual price charged.
- **Employee Value**: Value captured by employees = compensation minus WTS.
- **Profit**: Middle wedge = price minus compensation (captured by the firm).
- **[[Complements]]**: Products or services that raise another product's WTP (e.g., razor and razorblade, espresso machine and capsules).
- **[[Network Effects]]**: Broader adoption increases individual WTP (e.g., Instagram).
- **[[Best Buy]] Turnaround**: Real-world case where raising WTP (faster shipping via store-as-warehouse) and lowering WTS (vendor store-in-store model, focused employee roles) reversed a $1B quarterly loss to 20%+ ROIC.
- **[[Felix Oberholzer-Gee]]**: Harvard Business School professor, author of *Better, Simpler Strategy*.

## Key Takeaways

- **Strategy defined**: A plan to create value — not maximize profit.
- **Value Stick**: Total value = WTP minus WTS, split among customers, employees, and firm.
- **Two levers only**: All strategic moves either raise WTP or lower WTS.
- **Profit is a result**: Financials are an endpoint, not a starting point.
- **Raise WTP via**: Product quality, complements, or network effects.
- **Lower WTS via**: Better job conditions (creates value) or higher pay (only redistributes value).
- **Best Buy**: Went from −$1B/quarter to 20%+ ROIC by applying both levers simultaneously.
- **Pay vs. job quality**: Higher pay shifts value to employees; better job conditions actually creates new value.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Value Creation vs. Value Capture]]**: Strategy starts with expanding the total pie (creation) before deciding how to split it (capture) — Oberholzer-Gee's core reframe against profit-first thinking.
- **[[Jobs to Be Done]]**: Lowering WTS by making the job intrinsically better maps directly to understanding what employees actually want from work, not just what they are paid.
