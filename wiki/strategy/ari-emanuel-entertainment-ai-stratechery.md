## Summary

Ben Thompson and Hollywood super-agent Ari Emanuel discuss how [[AI Agents]] will disintermediate search-based advertising, why [[Live Events]] become more valuable in a world of infinite digital content, and why the ability to "generate human desire" is the key competitive moat in a post-AI economy. Emanuel argues that live, unscalable experiences are the one thing AI cannot replicate.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Ari Emanuel]]** — Executive Chairman of WME Group, CEO of TKO (UFC/WWE), founder of MARI live-events company
- **[[Ben Thompson]] / [[Stratechery]]** — Tech analyst and interviewer
- **Agent-driven disintermediation** — Once AI agents handle purchasing decisions, search-based ad models (Google, Amazon) lose their moat; human is no longer in the loop
- **On-device personalized advertising** — Future ads will predict desires inside a private VM; credibly private because the computation is local, enabling truly personalized ads without data sharing
- **[[Live Events]] as post-AI moat** — Experiences that cannot be scaled or digitized (live concerts, sports, in-person events) become more scarce and valuable as digital content becomes infinite and cheap
- **[[MARI]]** — Emanuel's live-events company, premised on AI creating demand for the irreducible real
- **[[SaaS]] vs advertising** — OpenAI's subscription model vs. Muse's (Microsoft) ad-supported model; Emanuel agrees ad-supported may ultimately win

## Key Takeaways

- **Agent threat to search**: When AI handles buying decisions, Google's and Amazon's search-ad moats evaporate.
- **On-device ads**: Personalized predictive ads inside a private VM can be credibly private while still highly targeted.
- **Live is anti-fragile to AI**: What can't be digitized or replicated by AI becomes more valuable, not less.
- **Human desire as the scarce good**: The post-AI economy's core product is generating demand for real experiences.
- **Subscription vs. ads**: Muse/Microsoft may have an advantage not charging users while earning via AI-enhanced advertising.
- **Phones as the great equalizer**: Regular phone calls maintain relationships and reduce polarization — a skill the AI era makes more important.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Disintermediation cascades when the human leaves the loop.** Every current ad model is built on intercepting human search intent; remove the human and the entire revenue model collapses. · *model:* [[Disruption Theory]]
→ explains: Agent threat to search, Subscription vs. ads

**T2. Scarcity preserves value where abundance destroys it.** AI makes infinite digital content trivially cheap; this inversion makes scarce, in-person, unreplicable experiences more valuable. · *model:* [[Scarcity Principle]]
→ explains: Live is anti-fragile to AI, Human desire as the scarce good

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does Ben Thompson argue that AI agents pose a greater threat to Google than ChatGPT did?
**A**: ChatGPT kept humans in the loop, so Google Search remained useful; AI agents act autonomously and remove the human entirely, eliminating the search-skim step that underpins Google's ad revenue.

**Q2**: What is the "on-device advertising" model and why does it matter?
**A**: Ads computed locally inside a private VM can predict a user's desires without sharing data externally, enabling truly personalized advertising that is also credibly private — a step-change from today's two-towers matching approach.

**Q3**: How does Ari Emanuel's MARI business strategy reflect the post-AI economy thesis?
**A**: MARI bets on live events and experiences that AI cannot replicate or disintermediate, premised on the idea that AI's abundance of digital content will increase demand for the scarce, real, and in-person.
