## Summary
Former Best Buy CEO Hubert Joly argues that leaders can't build a credible long-term strategy without first making explicit their worldview across three components: the personal (leadership philosophy), the micro (company and customers), and the macro (the wider world).

## Core Concepts
- Joly's three-part [[Worldview Framework]]: personal, micro, macro.
- The link between an explicit [[Worldview]] and the ability to shape events rather than merely react to them.

## Key Takeaways
- **Personal**: A leader's own distinct leadership philosophy.
- **Micro**: Deep understanding of the company and its customers.
- **Macro**: A clear analysis of broader world dynamics.
- **Core Claim**: Skipping this step leaves leaders reactive rather than strategic.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Joly's core claim?
**A**: Leaders need an explicit, three-part worldview (personal, micro, macro) before they can build a credible long-term strategy.

**Q2**: What are the three components of the framework?
**A**: The personal (leadership philosophy), the micro (company/customer understanding), and the macro (analysis of the wider world).

**Q3**: What happens if leaders skip this step?
**A**: They tend to react to events as they happen rather than shape a coherent strategic direction through them.
