## Summary
The CEO Project identifies 7 mental models that help executives make faster, higher-quality decisions — anchored by McKinsey data showing top-performing companies make decisions 2.5x faster and are 70% more likely to be in the top financial performance quartile — and argues that "mental model stacking" (applying multiple frameworks to the same problem simultaneously) produces deeper understanding than any single model alone.

## Core Concepts
- **[[First Principles Thinking]]**: Break problems down to primitive building blocks and reason from fundamentals rather than analogy or convention. Classic CEO example: Elon Musk rethinking rocket manufacturing cost models rather than benchmarking against existing suppliers.
- **[[Inversion Mental Model]]**: Solve by considering the opposite of your goal. "How do we increase customer dissatisfaction?" surfaces risks invisible to forward-thinking analysis. Particularly valuable for pre-mortem failure analysis.
- **[[OODA Loop]]**: Observe–Orient–Decide–Act; military framework from John Boyd. Encourages agility in changing environments. Best in crisis scenarios where speed of decision cycle beats decision accuracy.
- **[[Second-Order Thinking]]**: Before implementing a decision, contemplate downstream ripple effects 6–12 months out. A cost-cutting measure that improves quarterly margins but reduces morale and productivity is a classic second-order failure.
- **[[80/20 Principle (Pareto)]]**: 80% of results come from 20% of efforts. For CEOs: identify and concentrate resources on the 20% of products, customers, or initiatives driving 80% of value.
- **[[Eisenhower Matrix]]**: Urgency × importance quadrant for task prioritization; helps CEOs offload non-critical tasks and protect strategic thinking time. Maps directly to delegation decisions.
- **[[Mental Model Stacking]]**: Applying multiple models simultaneously to the same problem — combining First Principles, Second-Order Thinking, and 80/20 gives richer analysis than any model alone. The meta-skill of decision-making.

## Key Takeaways
- **McKinsey**: companies with agile decision-making are 70% more likely to be in the top financial performance quartile.
- **McKinsey**: executives in top-performing companies make decisions 2.5x faster than competitors.
- **7 models**: First Principles, Inversion, Eisenhower Matrix, OODA Loop, Second-Order Thinking, 80/20, Mental Model Stacking.
- **Model stacking**: the most powerful application — multiple frameworks simultaneously.
- **Decision journal**: record decisions made, models used, and outcomes — enables systematic learning from experience.
- **Team training**: CEOs should train leadership team on core models to prevent decision bottlenecks.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Cognitive Leverage]]**: Mental models are leverage tools for the mind — they compress complex decision problems into simpler structures that can be solved faster with less cognitive bandwidth. The CEO's comparative advantage is quality and speed of judgment, not information processing.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does McKinsey data say about decision speed in top-performing companies?
**A**: Companies with agile decision-making processes are 70% more likely to be in the top financial performance quartile; executives at top-performing companies make decisions 2.5x faster than their competitors.

**Q2**: What is "mental model stacking" and why is it the most powerful application of mental models?
**A**: Using multiple models simultaneously to analyze the same problem — combining First Principles (what are the fundamentals?), Second-Order Thinking (what are the downstream effects?), and 80/20 (where is the highest leverage?) gives a richer multi-dimensional understanding than any single framework can provide.

**Q3**: How does Inversion differ from standard forward problem-solving and when is it most useful?
**A**: Instead of asking "how do we improve X?" you ask "how could we make X worse?" — this surfaces risks and failure modes that forward-thinking analysis tends to miss. Most useful for pre-mortem analysis before major strategic decisions and for identifying blind spots in conventional strategy.
