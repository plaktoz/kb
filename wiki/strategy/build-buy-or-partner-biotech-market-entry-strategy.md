## Summary
A BIO-Europe 2026 panel recap argues that biotech market entry strategy should start at company formation, be governed explicitly with scenarios and pivot points, and be revisited as data and markets change. It also covers geographic sequencing under MFN pricing and how to structure partnerships so partners bring capability and commitment, not just capital.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Market Entry Strategy]]**: Answers where the company will play and how it will execute, layered across corporate, market entry, and commercial strategy.
- **[[Build vs. Buy vs. Partner]]**: Licensing to a regional player, building a commercial organization, or a hybrid.
- **Asset vs. Company Building**: Maximizing a program for exit, versus using it as a base for a broader organization.
- **[[Scenario Planning]]**: Base, blue-sky, and downside cases with defined pivot points.
- **[[Most-Favored-Nation Pricing]]**: Disrupts the US-first, then rest-of-world launch sequence.
- **[[Regulatory Harmonization]]**: Reconciling FDA and EMA requirements and planning country-level reimbursement.
- **[[Horizon Therapeutics]] and Tepezza**: Case of a focused company out-executing larger players before an Amgen acquisition.
- **[[Partnership Deal Structure]]**: Opt-in rights, split indications, co-promotion governance, and "commercially reasonable efforts" clauses.

## Key Takeaways
- **Day Zero**: Start market entry thinking at formation to inform trial design and evidence.
- **Therapy Area**: Rare disease favors integration; large immunology usually needs a pharma partner.
- **Partner Input**: Ask potential partners early what they need to see; it costs nothing.
- **Alignment**: Internal stakeholders are harder to align than external ones; set governance and KPIs.
- **Pivot Triggers**: Revisit strategy against new data; keep a Plan B and C.
- **Hybrid Launch**: An insomnia launch grew to seven or eight partnerships as demand emerged.
- **Paying Twice**: Building in small uncertain markets then exiting wastes capital.
- **Partner Test**: A partner should bring infrastructure and expertise; money alone can be raised directly.
- **Tepezza**: Roughly $1.7bn first full year; Amgen agreed to acquire Horizon for $27.8bn.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the panel's central message about market entry strategy?
**A**: It is not a late-stage commercial exercise. It should start at company formation, be governed explicitly, be revisited as data and markets change, and be grounded in a realistic view of the therapy area and what a partner would add.

**Q2**: How does the Tepezza case illustrate the value of conviction?
**A**: Horizon declined to partner without a strategically sound deal and focused on the launch and patient journey. Tepezza earned about $1.7bn in its first full year, and Amgen agreed to acquire Horizon for $27.8bn.

**Q3**: How should leadership teams choose and structure partners?
**A**: Pick partners who bring infrastructure, expertise, and resources, not just capital, and who are fully committed to the vision. Tightly define opt-in rights and set governance, roles, and KPIs in co-development and distribution deals.
