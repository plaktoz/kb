## Summary
Every past advantage (scale, digital transformation, now AI-driven speed) became table stakes, so the scarce edge is market adaptation: how fast the whole organization senses a change and coordinates a response. Most firms split this into four separate functions, and the time lost between them is the real cost. The author argues adaptability should be treated as a measurable cycle time rather than a cultural trait.

## Core Concepts
- [[Market Adaptation]]: the capacity to sense a change and coordinate a response across every market and function.
- [[Competitive Advantage]]: scale, digital transformation, and AI each expired into a cost of entry.
- [[Sensing, Prioritizing, Aligning, Executing]]: the four functions usually owned by separate groups.
- [[Cycle Time]]: the time from signal to full response, proposed as the measure of adaptability.
- [[Stanley Black & Decker]]: cut translation costs by up to 70% while shortening turnaround across markets.
- [[Smartcat]]: the AI localization platform vendor publishing the piece.

## Key Takeaways
- **Expiring Advantages**: Scale, then digital transformation, then AI each became table stakes within a decade.
- **Speed vs Coordination**: Speed without coordination arrives at the wrong destination sooner.
- **Handoff Loss**: Each function runs well alone; time is lost between them.
- **Hidden Cost**: The gap shows up as late launches and stale content, not a line item.
- **Measurable Capability**: Adaptability is signal-to-full-response cycle time, not a cultural trait.
- **Leadership Question**: It spans functions, so no single owner can fix it.
- **Result**: Stanley Black & Decker cut translation costs up to 70%.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Every advantage that can be copied becomes table stakes** — Once rivals can all adopt a capability, it stops differentiating and becomes a cost of entry; durable edge lies in what stays scarce afterward. · *model:* [[Commoditization]]
→ explains: Expiring Advantages, Speed vs Coordination

**T2. System speed is set by handoffs, not by the speed of any one part** — A chain of competent functions still responds slowly if work waits between them; the whole loop's cycle time is what counts. · *model:* [[Coordination Problem]]
→ explains: Handoff Loss, Hidden Cost, Leadership Question

**T3. What is not measured cannot be managed** — Treating adaptability as a trait leaves it unmanageable; defining it as a cycle time makes it improvable.
→ explains: Measurable Capability

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does the article argue is the new competitive advantage of the AI era?
**A**: Market adaptation: how quickly the whole organization can sense a change and coordinate a response across every market. Speed alone will become table stakes like scale and digital transformation did.

**Q2**: Why do organizations lose time even when each function performs well?
**A**: Sensing, prioritizing, aligning, and executing are owned by separate groups, and the loss happens between them. A signal sensed in week one can take until week eleven to reach twelve markets.

**Q3**: How should a leader act on this idea?
**A**: Measure adaptability as the cycle time from signal to full response and ask how quickly the organization can adapt when the plan changes. Collapse the four functions into one continuous loop.
