## Summary
A reflection on returning to a marketing fundamentals classroom reveals that first principles — understand the customer, craft a clear value proposition, communicate it honestly — outlast every platform and tool shift, including AI. Practitioners focused on tactics are vulnerable to disruption; those who reason from first principles are resilient across cycles.

## Core Concepts

- [[First Principles Marketing]] — identifying what the customer actually needs, what the product uniquely delivers, and the simplest way to communicate that
- [[Platform Disruption]] — SEO, paid social, email automation, AI — each generation disrupts the previous tactic layer, but not the underlying principles
- [[Value Proposition]] — the one thing the product does better than any alternative; the first-principles question that never becomes obsolete
- [[AI and Marketing]] — AI tools change tactics and channels; they cannot substitute for strategic first-principles thinking

## Key Takeaways

- **First-principles marketing questions** that never go out of date:
  1. Who exactly is the customer and what are their core motivations?
  2. What is the one thing this product does better than any alternative?
  3. What is the simplest, most honest way to communicate that?
- **Tactics are rented; principles are owned** — relying on platform-specific tactics creates dependency; understanding first principles creates durability
- **AI amplifies tactics but doesn't replace strategy** — an AI-powered marketer without first-principles thinking is just running bad strategy at scale
- **Classroom context surfaces timeless truths**: the fundamentals taught to beginners often contain the deepest truths, stripped of fashionable complexity

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Inversion]]**: Instead of asking "what AI marketing tools should I use?", first-principles marketers ask "what would still be true if all tools disappeared?" — the answer reveals what's actually durable.
- **[[Lindy Effect]]**: The marketing principles that have survived 100 years of tool disruption (understand your customer, communicate value clearly) are precisely the ones most likely to survive the next 100 years.
