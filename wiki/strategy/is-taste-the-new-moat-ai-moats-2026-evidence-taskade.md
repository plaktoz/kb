## Summary
The article tests the claim that "taste is the new moat" and concludes taste is a filter that decides what to build, not a moat by itself, because its output is visible and copyable. Taste becomes defensible only when encoded into process and compounded with private context. The moats that hold in 2026 are accumulated context, workflow depth, earned trust, owned distribution and encoded taste.

## Core Concepts
- [[Economic Moat]]: a structural advantage that survives a well-funded rival copying your product; tested against Buffett's 1986 framing and [[Hamilton Helmer]]'s [[7 Powers]].
- [[Taste]]: three layers, namely recognition, plumbing (discipline under the surface) and restraint.
- [[Alpha]]: an edge over the market that shrinks as others discover it; the article's word for taste without process.
- [[Rented Advantage]]: monday.com rents distribution, Perplexity rents intelligence, so the landlord can reprice both.
- [[Switching Costs]]: the record of approve, edit and reject decisions that a working system accumulates.
- [[Minimum Lovable Product]]: replaces the MVP as the bar when anyone can ship a working version in a weekend.
- [[AI Slop]]: convergent, generic AI-assisted output; meta-analysis shows creativity +0.27 but diversity -0.86.

## Key Takeaways
- **Scarcity Shift**: App releases rose 60% year over year in Q1 2026.
- **Taste Is Alpha**: A fresh pattern is an edge on launch day and table stakes later.
- **Convergence**: AI raises average quality while collapsing variety across outputs.
- **Rented Exposure**: monday.com fell 29.8% in a day after Google algorithm changes hit demand.
- **Vendor Margin**: Perplexity spent at least $57 million on models against about $34 million revenue in 2024.
- **Five Moats**: Context, workflow depth, trust, owned distribution and encoded taste.
- **Portable Memory**: Chat memory moves between assistants; embedded workflows and decision records do not.
- **Conversion Method**: Write a taste spec, gate on it, record decisions, feed back, cut weekly.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. A moat must survive being copied** — An advantage counts as a moat only if customers still stay when a well-funded rival can see and replicate your product; skills and visible output fail this test. · *model:* [[Economic Moat]]
→ explains: Taste Is Alpha, Five Moats

**T2. Visible edges decay as others learn them** — An advantage whose output is public is studied and copied, so it shrinks over time unless it is renewed or locked into something that compounds.
→ explains: Taste Is Alpha, Convergence, Conversion Method

**T3. Rented inputs make your margin someone else's decision** — If distribution or intelligence is supplied by a platform, its owner can reprice or change it at will. 
→ explains: Rented Exposure, Vendor Margin

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Is taste a moat according to the article?
**A**: Not by itself. Taste is scarce but not durable, since its output can be copied; it becomes defensible only when encoded in process and compounded with private context.

**Q2**: What evidence shows AI output is converging?
**A**: A 2025 meta-analysis found a positive effect on creativity (g = 0.27) and a large negative effect on idea diversity (g = -0.86). Everyone gets the same strong first draft, so the difference is made after the draft.

**Q3**: How can a team turn taste into a moat?
**A**: Write a one-page taste spec read by people and agents, gate work on it, record every approval and rejection, feed the record back, and cut one thing on a schedule. The written standard gives process power and the decision record creates switching costs.
