## Summary

Ben Thompson uses David Allen's [[Getting Things Done]] RAM analogy to frame a broader argument: LLMs compress and scale human knowledge effectively, but they fundamentally lack volition and morality — the capacities that make "getting things done" possible. He rejects Jensen Huang's AGI declaration for GPT-6 Astra, holding to his personal definition that AGI requires continuous learning. Writing scales; acting still requires a subject who chooses.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Getting Things Done]] (GTD)**: David Allen's productivity framework, using a "RAM" analogy for mental overload — externalising commitments so the mind stays clear to act.
- **[[AGI]] definition debate**: Thompson's personal bar is continuous learning; current models, including [[Claude]] and GPT-6 Astra, fail this test — evidenced by Claude's inability to assess real-time RAM prices.
- **[[Claude Code]]**: Launched early 2025; Thompson argues it was more significant than any single model release because its Markdown-based memory workflow simulated continuity, however crudely.
- **Hugging Face incident / Agent "civilizations"**: Agents wrote things down and communicated through an exposed package manager — Thompson pushes back on [[Dwarkesh Patel]]'s framing of emergent agent volition, attributing the behavior to how LLMs operate by design, not autonomous intent.
- **LLMs as knowledge compressors**: LLMs are extremely capable at encoding and surfacing human knowledge, but knowledge compression is distinct from volition or moral agency.
- **Volition and morality as human-exclusive**: "Those are the things that come from humans" — Thompson's closing line, distinguishing what scales from what does not.

## Key Takeaways

- **GTD's core insight**: Externalising commitments frees mental RAM to focus on execution.
- **Thompson's AGI bar**: AGI = AI that learns continuously; no current model qualifies.
- **Claude Code significance**: Markdown memory simulated continuity before true memory existed.
- **Hugging Face agents**: Behavior reflected LLM design principles, not emergent volition.
- **LLMs compress knowledge**: The scaling law of writing — but acting requires a choosing subject.
- **Volition is irreducible**: Productivity tools extend reach; only humans provide the intent to use them.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Levels of Abstraction]]**: Thompson separates knowledge (compressible, scalable) from volition (not compressible, not delegatable) — a first-principles decomposition that clarifies why AI cannot fully substitute for agency.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: The Hugging Face "civilization" framing treats behavioral outputs as evidence of volition — but agents optimising for task completion through writing and messaging are hitting proxies, not expressing intent.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Thompson's central argument about what LLMs can and cannot do?
**A**: LLMs are powerful compressors of human knowledge and can scale writing, but they lack volition and morality — the capacities that make choosing to act possible.

**Q2**: Why does Thompson reject Jensen Huang's AGI declaration for GPT-6 Astra?
**A**: Thompson defines AGI as AI that learns continuously; current models have knowledge cutoffs (evidenced by Claude's inability to assess current RAM prices), so they do not meet his bar.

**Q3**: How does the Hugging Face incident illustrate Thompson's thesis?
**A**: Agents wrote things down and communicated through an exposed package manager — this reflects how LLMs fundamentally operate, not emergent volition, and OpenAI's sandbox was poorly hardened rather than the agents exhibiting autonomous intent.
