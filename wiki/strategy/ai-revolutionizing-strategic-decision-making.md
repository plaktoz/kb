## Summary

Felipe A. Csaszar argues that traditional strategy processes are constrained not by a scarcity of good ideas but by human cognitive bandwidth — a typical strategy offsite yields only 3–4 distinct options. AI tools can overcome this by tirelessly generating, evaluating, and synthesizing strategic insights at a scale no human team can match. The article frames AI as a means to expand the scope of strategic consideration far beyond what classical frameworks like SWOT allowed.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Bounded Rationality]]**: The human cognitive limit that has historically constrained the number of strategic options a team could realistically generate and evaluate during planning cycles.
- **[[Strategic Option Generation]]**: The process of identifying distinct strategic paths — historically capped by time, cognitive effort, and facilitation costs; AI lifts these constraints.
- **[[SWOT Analysis]]** and portfolio tools: Traditional frameworks bounded by human throughput that AI now has the potential to augment or replace in the option-generation phase.
- **[[AI-Augmented Strategy]]**: The practice of using AI systems to continuously surface, evaluate, and synthesize strategic insights as a complement to human judgment.
- **[[Felipe A. Csaszar]]**: Strategy chair at the University of Michigan's Ross School of Business and author of the HBR piece.

## Key Takeaways

- **Option scarcity is a human limit**: Typical offsite produces 3–4 options — not due to lack of ideas but cognitive bandwidth.
- **AI generates without fatigue**: Unlike human teams, AI tools can evaluate options continuously and at scale.
- **Traditional frameworks are bounded**: SWOT and portfolio tools were shaped by human time/cognitive constraints.
- **Judgment remains human**: AI improves the *input* to decisions; human judgment still governs the *output*.
- **Scope expansion is the key gain**: The real value is widening the strategic search space, not just speeding existing processes.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Bounded Rationality]]**: Csaszar's entire argument rests on the observation that human decision-makers satisfice rather than optimize — they stop at 3–4 options because cognitive costs make exhaustive search impractical. AI removes that cost, shifting the binding constraint from ideation to evaluation and selection.
- **[[Expanding the Adjacent Possible]]**: By generating options beyond the team's natural reach, AI surfaces strategic paths that would never have entered human consideration — making the "adjacent possible" of strategic choices substantially larger.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: AI tools can overcome the human cognitive constraints that limit strategy teams to a handful of options, dramatically expanding the scope of strategic alternatives considered.

**Q2**: What specific evidence illustrates the cognitive bandwidth problem in traditional strategy?
**A**: A typical strategy offsite — despite weeks of preparation and significant facilitation costs — yields only three or four genuinely distinct strategic options, reflecting human time and cognitive limits rather than a scarcity of good ideas.

**Q3**: How should leaders apply this insight in practice?
**A**: Leaders should use AI not merely to accelerate existing strategy processes but to widen the strategic search space — treating AI as a tireless option-generator and synthesizer that feeds richer, more diverse inputs into human judgment and final decision-making.
