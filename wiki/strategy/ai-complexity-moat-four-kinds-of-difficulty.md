## Summary
Businesses that charged a premium for making complex things simple are being repriced by AI. Of four kinds of difficulty, explanatory and assembly difficulty are collapsing, consequential difficulty holds, and structural difficulty appreciates because AI lifts the human working-memory cap on useful model complexity. The strategic move is to stop compressing complexity and start building complexity worth holding.

## Core Concepts
- [[Economic Moat]]: a premium built on standing between a customer and something difficult.
- Four kinds of difficulty: explanatory, assembly, consequential, structural.
- [[Theory of Constraints]]: human working memory was the binding limit on how complex a useful model could be.
- [[Cognitive Load Theory]]: frameworks were sized to the reader's head, not to reality.
- [[Commoditization]]: explanation is now free to anyone with a prompt.
- [[Key Person Risk]]: structure held only in a few senior people's heads is a retention risk.
- [[Private Equity]]: a constellation of specialised, sometimes conflicting mental models.

## Key Takeaways
- **Explanatory Difficulty**: Paying to have things made comprehensible is now free with a prompt.
- **Assembly Difficulty**: Gathering, cross-referencing, and drafting premiums are collapsing fast.
- **Consequential Difficulty**: Defensible because machines do not absorb liability for being wrong.
- **Structural Difficulty**: Irreducible systems you encode appreciate as the working-memory cap lifts.
- **Compressor to Architect**: Stop flattening reality; build and operate richer models with machine help.
- **Encode the Structure**: Written, versioned structure is an asset underwritable at exit; people-only is risk.
- **Useful vs Useless Complexity**: Merely complicated structures collapse under the first serious interrogation.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. A moat lasts only as long as the constraint behind it** — When a premium rests on a scarce capacity (here, human working memory), removing the constraint reprices the premium, and value shifts to what the lifted constraint newly permits. · *model:* [[Theory of Constraints]]
→ explains: Explanatory Difficulty, Assembly Difficulty, Structural Difficulty, Compressor to Architect

**T2. Defensible value sits where risk or irreducibility cannot be transferred to a cheaper substitute** — Liability and genuinely irreducible structure resist commoditization, while translation and assembly do not. · *model:* [[Economic Moat]]
→ explains: Consequential Difficulty, Structural Difficulty, Encode the Structure

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about complexity and pricing?
**A**: AI has repriced the premium once paid for simplifying complexity. Value now lies in building and operating complexity worth holding, not in compressing it.

**Q2**: Which of the four kinds of difficulty remain defensible, and why?
**A**: Consequential difficulty holds because machines do not absorb liability. Structural difficulty appreciates because the human working-memory limit on model complexity has been lifted.

**Q3**: If your pricing rested on difficulty, what should you do this quarter?
**A**: Identify which kind of difficulty each revenue line sells. If it is structural, encode the structure so it is not held only in a few people's heads.
