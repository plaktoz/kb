## Summary

Technology leaders from Asia-Pacific argue that in volatile markets, competitive advantage comes from responsiveness rather than efficiency, with boards treating technology as strategy and holding business owners accountable for outcomes. They urge starting with the business problem before AI, solving root causes, treating resilience as a growth capability, and staying clear on priorities.

## Core Concepts

- [[Responsiveness vs. Efficiency]]: efficiency keeps you running, responsiveness helps you win ([[Romit Raut]]).
- [[Board Technology Governance]]: technology moving from a risk and spend item to the strategy agenda ([[Kim Stewart-Smith]]).
- [[Outcome Ownership]]: business functions, not only the CIO, own transformation results ([[Rahul Shinde]]).
- [[AI Investment Discipline]]: define the problem and decision before the technology.
- [[Cyber Resilience]]: framing recovery time and risk appetite in financial terms ([[Keith Sng]], Veeam).
- [[Portfolio Coherence]]: aligning IT investments to capabilities and discontinuing misaligned ones.

## Key Takeaways

- **Speed of Adaptation**: Volatile markets reward faster decisions, not just efficient operations.
- **Strategy Agenda**: Boards now ask what technology makes possible and who gets disrupted.
- **Shared Accountability**: Finance, commercial, and product owners share transformation outcomes with the CIO.
- **Problem First**: Many presentations lead with AI; discuss problem, decision, then technology.
- **Root Cause**: Firefighting grows the "fire brigade" yearly; fix root causes instead.
- **Resilience as Advantage**: Firms that recover fast take market share from slower competitors.
- **Decision Rights**: Teams leaving mid-transformation signals missing authority to drive change.
- **Priority Clarity**: In uncertainty, clear priorities prevent chasing multiple directions.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Accountability requires that decision-makers bear the consequences** — If only the CIO loses the bonus when a transformation fails, the business that owns the outcome has no stake in it. · *model:* [[Skin in the Game]]
→ explains: Shared Accountability, Decision Rights

**T2. Fixing symptoms repeatedly raises cost without removing the problem** — Recurring interventions expand teams and costs while underlying problems persist; leaders should make tomorrow's problem easier to solve. · *model:* [[Root Cause Analysis]]
→ explains: Root Cause

**T3. Sequencing decisions as problem, then decision, then technology avoids solution-first investment** — Starting from technology lets enthusiasm set priorities before the business problem is established.
→ explains: Problem First, Priority Clarity

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why do the panelists say strategy matters more than efficiency in volatile markets?
**A**: Efficiency keeps an organization running, but volatile markets reward how quickly it can adapt decisions. Decisions that once took months may need to happen within weeks.

**Q2**: What question does Rahul Shinde pose about accountability for technology investments?
**A**: He asks who loses the bonus if the outcome is not achieved 18 months later. He argues that treating transformation as a technology-only initiative, with the CIO bearing the risk, will fail.

**Q3**: How should boards sequence AI and resilience investments?
**A**: Discuss the problem first, then the decision, and technology last, and test whether the data and resilience foundation can support both AI and cyber resilience. Resilience is framed as a growth capability that lets firms keep operating while competitors cannot.
