## Summary

McKinsey senior partners argue that geopolitical volatility has shifted from an occasional disruption to the default operating environment for global businesses. In response, they developed a structured formula to help companies quantify enterprise value at risk from geopolitical exposure — moving beyond vague qualitative awareness toward a financial assessment. The framework aims to give leaders a rigorous, comparable basis for risk-adjusted decision-making across markets and operations.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Geopolitical Risk Quantification]]**: Translating qualitative geopolitical threats into measurable enterprise value impact
- **[[Enterprise Value at Risk]]**: Financial metric expressing how much company value is exposed to geopolitical scenarios
- **[[McKinsey Geopolitical Exposure Framework]]**: A formula-based methodology for assessing business exposure across geographies, supply chains, and revenue streams
- **[[Risk Management]]**: Moving from awareness to structured, quantified response
- **[[Globalization]]**: The baseline operating assumption that creates geopolitical interdependence for multinationals
- **[[Strategic Decision-Making]]**: Using exposure assessments to prioritize where to hedge, diversify, or exit

## Key Takeaways

- Geopolitical volatility is now the default operating environment, not an exception.
- McKinsey developed a formula to convert geopolitical risk into enterprise value at stake.
- Two companies facing the same geopolitical moment can have radically different outcomes.
- The framework targets structured financial assessment over qualitative risk awareness.
- Key topics covered: risk management, globalization, trade, and corporate strategy.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Second-Order Thinking]]**: The formula forces leaders to trace geopolitical events through supply chains, revenues, and valuation — not just react to headlines — exactly the discipline second-order thinking demands.
- **[[Expected Value]]**: By attaching a financial figure to geopolitical scenarios, the framework operationalizes expected-value reasoning for decisions that once relied on gut instinct about "political risk."

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core argument of the McKinsey geopolitical exposure framework?
**A**: Geopolitical volatility is now a persistent operating condition, and companies need a formula to quantify the enterprise value at risk — not just acknowledge risk qualitatively.

**Q2**: What financial metric does the framework produce, and why does it matter?
**A**: It produces an "enterprise value at risk" figure, allowing leaders to compare and prioritize geopolitical exposures in financial terms rather than abstract threat levels.

**Q3**: How should a company use this framework in practice?
**A**: By calculating their geopolitical exposure score, leaders can make risk-adjusted decisions about where to hedge, diversify supply chains, or exit markets before a crisis crystallizes.
