## Summary
Harvard Business School's Henrique Castro-Pires argues that [[Game Theory]] lets leaders systematically anticipate how competitors, suppliers, and buyers will respond to their moves, replacing unsubstantiated assumptions with analysis of incentives. Tools like payoff matrices and game trees map interdependent choices and likely outcomes. As AI agents increasingly make strategic calls, shorter decision windows make this modeling more valuable.

## Core Concepts
- [[Game Theory]]: A mathematical framework modeling strategic interactions among players, actions, and payoffs.
- [[Henrique Castro-Pires]]: HBS Assistant Professor teaching "Game On: Strategic Thinking, Competition, and Cooperation."
- [[Payoff Matrix]]: A map of interdependencies showing each player's best response to the other's moves.
- [[Nash Equilibrium]]: An outcome where neither player would unilaterally change their action given the other's choice.
- [[Game Trees]]: Diagrams of sequential moves and outcomes, used when one player moves first.
- [[Best Response]]: The optimal action for each possible competitor action.
- [[John Nash]]: Mathematician whose work, popularized by *A Beautiful Mind*, underpins the field.
- [[AI Agents]]: Automated competitors whose goals and instructions must be modeled.

## Key Takeaways
- **Systematic Thinking**: Game theory forces analysis of incentives rather than unsubstantiated assumptions about behavior.
- **Hidden Retaliation**: Price cuts can trigger competitive responses leaving everyone worse off.
- **Cooperation Value**: Squeezing suppliers may undermine quality investment; cooperative agreements can create more long-term value.
- **Framing**: Define players, available actions, and payoffs such as profit or reputation.
- **Prediction Rule**: Most likely outcome is where each competitor best responds, a Nash equilibrium.
- **Five Questions**: Competitor's-shoes view, best responses, likely outcome, favorable changes, inducing behaviors.
- **Playing Against Agents**: Ask what goal and instruction the AI agent was given.
- **Steps**: Analyze each player, look forward then reason backward, expect interactions to change the game.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Your best move depends on others' moves** — When outcomes are jointly determined, the optimal action is a best response to what others will do, and predicting that requires modeling their incentives. · *model:* [[Game Theory]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Castro-Pires' core argument about game theory in business?
**A**: It helps managers systematically anticipate how others will respond by working through incentives, instead of making unsubstantiated assumptions about behavior.

**Q2**: What are the building blocks for analyzing a strategic situation, and what is a Nash equilibrium?
**A**: Players, their available actions, and the payoffs at stake, mapped with a payoff matrix or game tree. A Nash equilibrium is an outcome where neither player would unilaterally change their action given the other's choice.

**Q3**: How should a leader apply this when launching a product or dealing with a supplier?
**A**: Analyze each player's objectives and payoffs, consider your best response to each competitor action (and theirs to yours), and weigh whether cooperation creates more long-term value than retaliation or price pressure.
