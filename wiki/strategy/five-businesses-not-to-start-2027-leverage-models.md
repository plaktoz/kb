## Summary
Katie Melissa argues that the business model matters almost as much as the entrepreneur, and that heading into 2027 the best models offer leverage, recurring revenue and systems. She advises against five commoditized or founder-dependent businesses and proposes a more specialized, system-dependent alternative for each. Her master filter is: can this eventually work without me?

## Core Concepts
- [[Leverage]]: AI, software, recurring revenue, management teams, capital and systems each let output grow faster than personal time.
- [[Commoditization]]: generic agencies, broad courses and generic stores compete on sameness against freelancers, offshore teams and [[Artificial Intelligence (AI)]].
- [[Specialization]]: solve one expensive problem for one type of customer and sell the outcome, not the technology.
- [[Recurring Revenue]]: repeat customers, email lists and brands compound, unlike ads that stop when switched off.
- [[Customer Lifetime Value]]: weigh the cost of acquiring a customer against what they are worth over time.
- [[Founder Dependence]]: a business that collapses when the founder stops posting is income, not an independent business.
- [[AI Automation Services]]: niche automation, such as lead follow-up for dental practices.
- [[Passive Ownership of Ecommerce]]: owning a professionally managed storefront, like a property owner using a property manager.

## Key Takeaways
- **Model Over Idea**: A great operator can fight a bad model, but why choose one?
- **Generic Agencies**: Now compete with freelancers, offshore teams, in-house hires and AI tools.
- **Sell Outcomes**: "Recovering lost leads" beats "social media management" as an offer.
- **Customer Economics**: Is a $40 acquired customer worth $200 over two years?
- **Information Glut**: Information is no longer scarce; real results are.
- **Digital Product Ecosystem**: Many narrow tools are harder to replace with one AI prompt.
- **Founder Test**: Plan a path from founder-dependent to system-dependent.
- **Managed Ownership**: Not risk-free; vet who operates the store and how profits are calculated.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Leverage decouples output from personal time** — The valuable asset is whatever lets results grow without proportional founder effort: technology, recurring revenue, people, capital and systems. · *model:* [[Leverage]]
→ explains: Model Over Idea, Founder Test, Managed Ownership

**T2. Value migrates to what is scarce once a thing becomes abundant** — When information, services or products are widely replicable, price pressure rises and differentiation shifts to outcomes, niches and bundles. · *model:* [[Commoditization]]
→ explains: Generic Agencies, Sell Outcomes, Information Glut, Digital Product Ecosystem

**T3. Single points of dependency are fragile** — A business whose revenue depends entirely on one person's continued effort is only as durable as that person's willingness and energy. · *model:* [[Key Person Risk]]
→ explains: Founder Test

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about choosing a business?
**A**: The business model matters almost as much as the entrepreneur, so choose models with leverage, recurring revenue and systems. The filter is whether the business can eventually work without you.

**Q2**: Why does the author reject generic dropshipping, and what does she suggest instead?
**A**: Selling products hundreds of sites also sell gives little advantage and leaves you dependent on paid ads. She recommends a niche brand with repeat-purchase potential, asking whether a $40 customer can be worth $200 over two years.

**Q3**: How should someone with little capital but plenty of time apply this?
**A**: She suggests a specialized AI automation service that solves one expensive problem for one type of business, selling the outcome rather than the technology. Those with capital but little time might consider a professionally managed business.
