## Summary

An [[EY-Parthenon]] survey of 100 UK CEOs finds AI is the leading driver of productivity gains, yet only 14% have comprehensive real-time visibility of AI costs and 32% report significant gaps in assessing return on investment. Legacy technology and operating models are the top barrier to converting gains into business results, and CEOs are also piloting AI in deal evaluation. EY argues productivity alone won't determine returns; operating models, processes, and skills must change with it.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI ROI Gap]]**: Productivity gains from AI that CEOs cannot yet tie to measurable financial return.
- **[[EY CEO Outlook Survey]]**: UK subset of a global survey of 1,200 large-company CEOs across 21 countries and five industries.
- **[[Operating Model Transformation]]**: [[Silvia Rindone]] says businesses need the right operating models, processes, and skills to create sustainable value.
- **[[Legacy Technology]]**: Named by 34% of UK CEOs as the biggest obstacle to turning productivity into business results.
- **[[AI in Transaction Evaluation]]**: [[Matthew Ringelheim]] describes AI accelerating due diligence and opportunity assessment in M&A, divestments, and alliances.
- **[[Skills Shortage]]**: 74% expect skills gaps to constrain growth more than access to capital within three years.
- **[[Workforce Reskilling]]**: Cited as a productivity driver by 38%, yet 47% say skills development lags AI innovation.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI Leads Productivity**: 50% rank AI top-three, ahead of process redesign (40%) and reskilling (38%).
- **Cost Visibility Gap**: Only 14% have comprehensive, real-time visibility of AI costs.
- **ROI Blind Spots**: 32% report significant gaps in assessing AI return on investment.
- **Legacy Barrier**: 34% cite legacy technology and operating models as the biggest obstacle.
- **Reinvestment**: 44% reinvest productivity gains in growth, innovation, or transformation.
- **Top Benefit**: 34% name greater output without added headcount or capital.
- **Deals Adoption**: 54% pilot AI in transaction evaluation; 9% embed it across most activities.
- **Roles Over Headcount**: 71% expect AI to change roles and skills more than workforce size.
- **Skills Lag**: 47% say workforce skills aren't developing fast enough for AI.
- **Related**: Parallels [[ey-ceo-outlook-ai-productivity-gains-not-profits]], the global version of this survey.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Efficiency gains become value only through complementary change** — Productivity improvements don't automatically yield returns; they convert only when operating models, processes, and skills are rebuilt around the technology. · *model:* [[Productivity Paradox]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the EY-Parthenon UK CEO survey about AI?
**A**: AI is the leading source of productivity gains for UK CEOs, but many struggle to measure the financial return. EY says the conversation has shifted from adoption to impact.

**Q2**: What data shows the gap between productivity gains and understanding of value?
**A**: Only 14% have comprehensive real-time visibility of AI costs, 32% report significant gaps in assessing ROI, and 34% name legacy technology and operating models as the biggest obstacle.

**Q3**: What should businesses do to turn AI productivity into sustainable value?
**A**: Align capital, technology, and talent, with the right operating models, processes, and skills. In deals, AI should be paired with rigorous analysis, clear strategic choices, and disciplined execution.
