## Summary

[[Anne-Laure Le Cunff]] identifies a paradox she calls the "Omnipotence Dilemma": when AI makes nearly anything technically achievable, decision-making and prioritization collapse rather than improve. Three reinforcing dynamics — synthetic plausibility, cheap starts, and endless iteration — create a feedback loop that produces the feeling of progress without meaningful advancement. The remedy is an experimental mindset that frames work as bounded, time-limited commitments rather than open-ended pursuits.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Omnipotence Dilemma]]**: The paradox where near-unlimited technical capability undermines the ability to commit, prioritize, and make identity-shaping choices — abundance becomes a liability.
- **[[Synthetic Plausibility]]**: AI can make almost every idea sound viable and well-reasoned, eroding the discernment needed to say no and making prioritization much harder.
- **[[Cheap Starts]]**: The low effort required to begin projects with AI assistance means starting resembles consumption rather than commitment, leading to proliferating abandoned initiatives.
- **[[Endless Iteration]]**: AI-assisted constant refinement creates a feeling of productive motion without constituting meaningful advancement toward a defined goal.
- **[[Bounded Experiments]]**: A framework for committing to work using the format "I will [action] for [duration]" — creating a defined sandbox that generates real learning regardless of outcome.
- **[[Attention as Scarcity]]**: As AI removes execution constraints, the truly scarce resources become attention, conviction, taste, trust, time, and responsibility.

## Key Takeaways

- **The core paradox**: More capability → harder prioritization, not easier.
- **Three feedback loop drivers**: Synthetic plausibility, cheap starts, endless iteration.
- **Old scarcity**: Execution used to force identity-shaping choices about direction.
- **New scarcity**: Attention, conviction, taste, trust, time, and responsibility.
- **Bounded experiment format**: "I will [action] for [duration]" creates real commitment.
- **Metacognitive loop**: Pair curiosity with ambition; reflect afterward to replace the AI-slop loop.
- **Core principle**: Meaningful progress requires committing to a direction, not pursuing every direction simultaneously.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Paradox of Choice]]**: When options proliferate without cost, choosing becomes harder — Le Cunff's Omnipotence Dilemma is exactly this dynamic applied to AI-era execution, where the removal of technical barriers creates decision paralysis rather than liberation.
- **[[Commitment Devices]]**: The bounded experiment format ("I will [action] for [duration]") works because it voluntarily reintroduces the scarcity that AI has removed — a deliberate constraint that forces the prioritization that unlimited possibility makes impossible.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Endless iteration optimizes for the feeling of progress (the proxy) rather than actual advancement toward meaningful goals — the core mechanism of the AI-slop loop Le Cunff describes.
