## Summary
As foundation models become widely accessible, simply having AI is unlikely to be a durable advantage; proprietary data is the likelier differentiator. A real data moat depends on exclusivity, relevance, depth, rights, AI readiness, and learning effects rather than volume. Because internally developed datasets are generally expensed, their economic value often never reaches the balance sheet, creating problems for accountants in valuation, M&A, and controls.

## Core Concepts
- [[Dwarkesh Patel]] and [[Jerry Han]]: estimated a 12x compute-efficiency gain from data improvements versus 3.7x from model improvements (2019–2025, at tested compute scale).
- [[Peter Diamandis]] (Moonshots): "The next 10x may be sitting in the data" already inside an organization.
- [[Data Moat]]: proprietary data that enables AI outcomes competitors cannot readily reproduce.
- [[Privileged Data]]: [[McKinsey]]'s term for such data; the advantage compounds as product usage generates more of it.
- [[Foundation Models]]: licensable by competitors, so access alone does not differentiate.
- [[Economic Moat]]: assessed across six dimensions: exclusivity, relevance, history and depth, rights and control, AI readiness, learning effects.
- [[Internally Developed Intangible Assets]]: generally expensed under [[US GAAP]], so not recognized separately on the balance sheet.
- [[ASC 805]] (acquisition accounting): databases can be recognized separately from goodwill at acquisition-date fair value.
- [[FASB]]: has an active research project on accounting and disclosure of intangibles.
- [[Data Governance]]: completeness, accuracy, lineage, and legal usability matter when AI outputs feed financial reporting.

## Key Takeaways
- **Data Over Models**: Data improvements may have driven more recent AI gains than model improvements.
- **No Durable Edge From AI Alone**: Widely licensable foundation models make "having AI" insufficient.
- **Six-Factor Test**: Exclusivity, relevance, depth, rights, AI readiness, and learning effects define a data moat.
- **Volume Is Not Value**: Petabytes of data can carry little competitive significance.
- **Value Channels**: Data matters when it lifts growth, margins, retention, pricing power, or cash-flow durability.
- **Accounting Gap**: Internally built data is expensed, yet acquired databases can be recognized at fair value.
- **Controls Risk**: Data quality and lineage become financial-reporting issues when AI feeds estimates.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Commoditized inputs stop differentiating** — When a capability becomes available to all competitors on similar terms, advantage shifts to what cannot be copied or bought. · *model:* [[Commoditization]]
→ explains: No Durable Edge From AI Alone, Data Over Models

**T2. Moats rest on scarcity, not size** — A resource defends a position only if rivals cannot obtain or recreate it, and it matters to commercially important outcomes. · *model:* [[Economic Moat]]
→ explains: Six-Factor Test, Volume Is Not Value, Value Channels

**T3. Usage-generated data compounds** — When product use produces new proprietary information that improves the product, the advantage grows over time. · *model:* [[Flywheel Effect]]
→ explains: Six-Factor Test, Data Over Models

**T4. Accounting value can diverge from economic value** — Costs expensed as incurred leave self-built assets off the balance sheet even when they generate large future benefits.
→ explains: Accounting Gap, Controls Risk

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about AI competitive advantage?
**A**: As foundation models become widely accessible, the model itself is less differentiating and proprietary data becomes the more important moat. Simply having AI is unlikely to be a durable advantage.

**Q2**: What evidence and criteria does the article give for a real data moat?
**A**: Patel and Han estimated a 12x compute-efficiency gain from data versus 3.7x from model improvements. A data moat is judged on exclusivity, relevance, history and depth, rights and control, AI readiness, and learning effects.

**Q3**: Why does proprietary data create a problem for accountants?
**A**: Internally developed datasets are generally expensed and absent from the balance sheet, yet databases can be recognized at fair value in acquisitions under ASC 805. Accountants must therefore weigh data in purchase price allocations, diligence, impairment, forecasts, and controls.
