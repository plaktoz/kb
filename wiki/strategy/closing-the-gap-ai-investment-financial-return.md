## Summary

Despite heavy corporate investment in AI pilots, workforce training, data infrastructure, and model adoption, many companies fail to demonstrate concrete financial returns. The core problem is strategic misalignment: firms pursue low-value AI opportunities — adding features customers don't truly value or giving employees time-saving tools that never translate into measurable business outcomes — leaving CFOs unable to connect AI spending to bottom-line impact. The authors argue companies are "abuzz with AI" but "returns have been difficult to quantify."

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI ROI Gap]]**: The widening disconnect between corporate AI investment and demonstrable financial return — a systemic failure to translate AI spend into bottom-line impact.
- **[[Low-Value AI Opportunities]]**: Initiatives that add features customers don't value or employee productivity tools whose time savings never flow through to measurable outcomes; a primary cause of poor AI ROI.
- **[[Digital Transformation]]**: The broader strategic context; [[David L. Rogers]] brings advisory experience helping major corporations navigate digital transformation and connects AI investment failure to longstanding transformation pitfalls.
- **[[AI Transformation Leadership]]**: [[Krishnan Sankaranarayanan]] draws on experience leading AI transformation at Eastman and 24 years at ExxonMobil, SABIC, and Eastman — grounding the argument in practitioner insight.
- **[[CFO Accountability for AI]]**: The organizational bottleneck — finance leaders cannot justify continued AI budgets without a clear line of sight from spending to financial outcomes.

## Key Takeaways

- **Investment ≠ return**: Heavy AI spending has not produced proportional financial results at many firms.
- **Wrong opportunities chosen**: Companies pursue AI that adds unvalued features or non-translating time savings.
- **CFO disconnect**: Finance leaders lack visibility to connect AI spend to bottom-line impact.
- **Quantification problem is real**: Authors state returns have been "difficult to quantify" across the industry.
- **Strategic framing matters**: Choosing which problems AI solves is more decisive than the AI capability itself.
- **Practitioner-backed**: Sankaranarayanan's 24-year industry career and Eastman transformation role anchor the argument empirically.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When "AI adoption" becomes the corporate KPI, organizations optimize for pilots and tool deployments rather than actual business outcomes — investment accelerates while returns stagnate, precisely because the metric being hit is not the one that matters.
- **[[Opportunity Cost]]**: Pursuing low-value AI applications crowds out capital and organizational attention from high-value ones; the gap between AI investment and return is partly a resource-allocation failure, not merely a measurement problem.
