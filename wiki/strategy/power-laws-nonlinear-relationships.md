## Summary

A power law describes a nonlinear relationship between two quantities, expressed as **Y = MX^B**, where the exponent B determines how dramatically outputs scale with inputs. Unlike linear thinking — which dominates everyday intuition — power laws mean that small changes in input can produce massively disproportionate changes in output, creating leverage points that reward concentrated effort over evenly spread effort.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Power Law]]**: A mathematical relationship Y = MX^B where the exponent B governs scaling behavior — B > 1 means accelerating returns, B < 0 means diminishing returns with distance.
- **[[Compounding]]**: The only power law with a variable exponent, capable of limitless growth; small early investments vastly outperform larger later ones due to exponential accumulation over time.
- **[[Zipf's Law]]**: A power-law distribution of word frequency in language — roughly 135 words comprise half of everyday speech, making early vocabulary the highest-leverage learning target.
- **[[Pareto Principle]]**: The 80/20 rule — 20% of inputs produce 80% of outputs — appears across wealth, productivity, website traffic, and earthquake magnitudes.
- **[[Kleiber's Law]]**: Metabolic rate scales at three-fourths the power of body mass; larger animals are proportionally more energy-efficient and live longer because they metabolize more slowly.
- **[[Diminishing Returns]]**: Past a threshold, positive exponents can turn negative — excess training causes injury, income above ~$75,000/year shows negligible happiness gains.
- **[[Peter Thiel]]** on venture capital: the single best investment in a fund equals or outperforms the entire rest of the fund combined.
- **[[Linear Thinking]]**: Default human intuition that consistently underestimates exponential and power-law dynamics.

## Key Takeaways

- **Formula**: Y = MX^B; exponent B determines whether returns accelerate, decelerate, or invert.
- **Compounding**: Earlier investment beats larger later investment — time is the high-leverage variable.
- **Zipf's Law**: 135 words cover ~50% of everyday speech; early vocab learning has outsized returns.
- **Pareto Principle**: Severe inequality in outputs is the norm across natural and social systems.
- **VC skew**: One breakout investment routinely beats an entire portfolio combined.
- **Business focus**: One revenue source typically dominates; concentrate there, not evenly across all.
- **Kleiber's Law**: Metabolic scaling explains why larger animals live longer.
- **Diminishing returns**: Every positive power law has a saturation threshold — more input eventually hurts.
- **Intuition gap**: Everyday linear experience makes exponential dynamics consistently surprising.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Leverage]]**: Power laws reveal where input effort multiplies disproportionately into output — identifying those exponent-rich levers (compounding, skill accumulation, network effects) is the core strategic task.
- **[[Pareto Principle]]**: The 80/20 distribution is not an anomaly but a structural feature of power-law systems; trying to equalize effort across activities actively destroys value by ignoring where B is highest.
- **[[Non-Linearity]]**: Human intuition calibrated to linear everyday experience systematically underestimates both the upside and downside of exponential relationships, creating persistent blind spots in forecasting and resource allocation.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central claim of this article about power laws?
**A**: Power laws describe nonlinear relationships (Y = MX^B) where small input changes can produce dramatically large output changes, and recognizing where these operate allows far better allocation of time and resources toward high-leverage activities.

**Q2**: How does Zipf's Law illustrate a power-law dynamic in language learning?
**A**: Word frequency in language follows a power-law distribution — roughly 135 words comprise about half of everyday speech, meaning early vocabulary acquisition yields exponentially higher returns than later vocabulary expansion.

**Q3**: How should understanding power laws change investment or business strategy?
**A**: In venture capital, Peter Thiel's observation that one investment can outperform all others combined means portfolio strategy should concentrate on finding outliers; in business, identifying the single dominant revenue driver and concentrating resources there beats spreading effort evenly.
