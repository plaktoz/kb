## Summary

Confirmation bias is the tendency to seek information that validates existing beliefs while ignoring or discounting contradictory evidence, and it is most pronounced in emotionally or ideologically charged views. Quine and Ullian distinguished wanting to be right (truth-seeking) from wanting to have been right (ego protection), with the latter being the core obstacle to genuine learning. The antidote is actively seeking disconfirming evidence and auditing which parts of any argument you skimmed or uncritically accepted.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Confirmation Bias]]**: The tendency to filter new information through a pre-existing belief lens, reinforcing rather than updating beliefs.
- **[[Disconfirming Evidence]]**: Evidence that contradicts a held belief — the most valuable and most avoided type of information for belief revision.
- **[[Cognitive Dissonance]]**: The psychological discomfort when new facts conflict with existing beliefs, which confirmation bias helps us avoid.
- **[[Leon Festinger]]**: Researcher who documented that people confronted with undeniable disproof often emerge more convinced than before.
- **[[Warren Buffett]]** on motivated reasoning: humans excel at interpreting new information so prior conclusions stay intact.
- **[[Motivated Reasoning]]**: The cognitive process of constructing justifications for a conclusion already reached — confirmation bias in action.
- **[[Critical Thinking]]**: Lerner and Tetlock found critical thinking sharpens mainly when people must justify their views to others.

## Key Takeaways

- **Core Mechanism**: Bias filters new data through existing beliefs, not the reverse.
- **Ego vs. Truth**: Wanting to *have been* right blocks genuine learning.
- **Festinger's Finding**: Disproof often *strengthens* belief rather than correcting it.
- **Cognitive Efficiency**: Mental shortcuts conserve energy; scrutiny is costly without external accountability.
- **Accountability Sharpens Thinking**: Critical thinking improves when we must justify views to others.
- **The Antidote**: Ask what you skimmed, ignored, or accepted without scrutiny in any argument.
- **Examples**: Creationism, doomsday prophecies, homeopathy, scientific peer review all show bias at scale.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Incentive-Caused Bias]]**: Emotional investment in a belief creates an incentive to protect it — confirmation bias is the mechanism through which ego-stakes corrupt information processing.
- **[[Bayesian Updating]]**: The rational norm confirmation bias violates — new evidence should update prior beliefs proportionally; instead, disconfirming evidence is discounted or rationalized away.
- **[[Accountability Effect]]**: Lerner and Tetlock's finding is a first-principles reminder that social scrutiny — not internal motivation alone — reliably activates epistemic vigilance.
