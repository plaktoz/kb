## Summary

Despite U.S. GDP growing ~3% annually from 2014–2023, medium-to-large American corporations paradoxically reduced their median annual investment rate by 16% over the same period. A 2025 OECD study found real business investment across 17 advanced economies sits roughly 23% below pre-financial-crisis levels. Drawing on a decade of performance data from 2,900 U.S. public companies, authors Paul Blase and Paul Leinwand argue that most firms systematically misallocate capital and offer a framework for getting it right.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Capital Allocation]]**: The strategic decision of how much to invest in growth versus cost reduction or returns to shareholders — identified as a core area where most firms underperform.
- **[[Corporate Underinvestment]]**: A widespread trend where firms reduced investment rates even as the macro economy grew, reflecting a systematic bias toward short-term returns over long-run growth.
- **[[OECD Business Investment Data]]**: A 2025 cross-national study showing real business investment ~23% below pre-financial-crisis levels across 17 advanced economies.
- **[[Growth Investment Framework]]**: A data-driven model built from 2,900 U.S. public companies' performance over a decade, designed to benchmark and guide investment decisions.
- **[[Paul Blase]]** and **[[Paul Leinwand]]**: PwC U.S. principals and authors; Leinwand also leads the PwC Leadership Center and teaches at Northwestern's [[Kellogg School of Management]].

## Key Takeaways

- **Investment Paradox**: U.S. firms cut investment 16% even as GDP grew 3%/year (2014–2023).
- **Global Underinvestment**: OECD data shows business investment 23% below pre-crisis levels across 17 countries.
- **Data Foundation**: Framework is grounded in 10 years of data across 2,900 public U.S. companies.
- **Core Problem**: Most corporations get [[Capital Allocation]] wrong — the article builds a framework to fix that.
- **Strategic Bias**: Firms systematically prefer near-term certainty over long-term growth investment.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Hyperbolic Discounting]]**: Firms systematically undervalue future growth payoffs relative to near-term costs, explaining the persistent underinvestment even in expanding macro environments.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When quarterly earnings or margin targets become the primary metric, capital allocation optimizes for those proxies rather than durable competitive position — the mechanism behind the 16% investment decline.

**A**: Despite U.S. GDP growing roughly 3% annually from 2014–2023, medium-to-large American corporations actually reduced their median annual investment rate by 16% over the same period.

**Q2**: What data source does the framework rely on, and what scope does it cover?
**A**: The framework draws on a decade of performance data from 2,900 U.S. public companies, supplemented by a 2025 OECD study covering 17 advanced economies.

**Q3**: How can leaders apply this framework practically?
**A**: By benchmarking their firm's investment rate against the 2,900-company dataset, leaders can identify whether they are over- or under-investing relative to peers and adjust capital allocation toward durable growth rather than short-term returns.
