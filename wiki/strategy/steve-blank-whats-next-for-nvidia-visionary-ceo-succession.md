## Summary
Steve Blank argues that after Jensen Huang's resignation, Nvidia's board faces the classic risk of replacing a visionary CEO with an execution CEO, as happened at Microsoft under Ballmer and Apple under Cook. Execution CEOs trim risky bets and drive out creative talent, which can leave a company coasting on momentum. The board must decide whether it is hiring someone to run today's business or to reinvent it.

## Core Concepts
- [[Jensen Huang]]: reinvented [[Nvidia]] three times (CUDA in 2006, deep learning in 2012, Mellanox and rack-scale systems in 2020).
- [[Visionary CEO vs. Execution CEO]]: innovators who shape markets versus operators who value stability and process.
- [[Steve Ballmer]] and [[Satya Nadella]]: Microsoft's execution era and its cloud-led recovery.
- [[Tim Cook]] and [[John Ternus]]: Apple's incremental growth under Cook and the September 2026 handoff.
- [[CEO Succession Planning]]: Huang named no successor, had about 60 direct reports, and left the board too.
- [[Innovator's Dilemma]]: holding tightly to current products invites disruption.
- [[Customers Becoming Competitors]]: Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, OpenAI, and Tesla build their own AI chips.
- [[Board of Directors Governance]] under crisis.

## Key Takeaways
- **Three Reinventions**: CUDA, deep learning, and integrated racks were business model shifts, not product tweaks.
- **Microsoft Precedent**: Ballmer grew revenue to $78 billion but missed search, mobile, cloud, and media.
- **Apple Precedent**: Cook grew revenue from $108 billion to $416 billion, but Vision Pro and AI stumbled.
- **Creative Death Spiral**: Execution CEOs add process; creative people leave; more process people are hired.
- **Nvidia Structure**: About 60 direct reports and no succession plan force new management layers.
- **Bets at Risk**: Annual chip cadence, CUDA moat, and roughly $50 billion in lab investments may be trimmed.
- **Board Dilemma**: Crisis boards reach for stability, which yields an execution CEO by another road.
- **Counterexample**: Andy Jassy had built AWS before succeeding Bezos.
- **Closing Maxim**: Execution pays your salary, innovation pays your pension.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Holding tightly to the current business invites disruption** — In fast-changing markets, optimizing the existing product and business model without reinventing it leaves a company exposed to more agile competitors. · *model:* [[Innovator's Dilemma]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Blank's core claim about Nvidia after Huang's departure?
**A**: Replacing a visionary CEO with an execution CEO often leads to a company coasting on momentum and being disrupted. Nvidia's board must decide whether it wants an innovator or an operator.

**Q2**: What mechanism does Blank describe by which execution CEOs erode innovation?
**A**: They remove chaos and favor stability and process, so creative people leave and executors fill senior roles. They then hire more process people, which pushes out the remaining creative talent.

**Q3**: What should Nvidia's board ask of its candidates?
**A**: Blank says the board should ask what candidates have built under uncertainty. A crisis board's pull toward stability tends to produce another execution CEO.
