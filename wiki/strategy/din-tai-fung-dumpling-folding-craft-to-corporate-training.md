## Summary
Din Tai Fung's North America CEO says dumpling-folding talent used to be the chain's growth bottleneck, but a structured training program has largely overcome it. The piece argues that Chinese restaurants in America that scaled did so by replacing family labor with systems and process. Din Tai Fung's remaining constraints are its own quality standards and scarce large real estate.

## Core Concepts
- [[Din Tai Fung]]: highest per-store sales of any US restaurant chain, averaging $27.4 million in 2024 per Technomic data.
- [[Albert Yang]]: CEO of Din Tai Fung North America, source of the "bottleneck" quote.
- [[Training Programs]]: a 12-week program plus a dumpling training manager in every US restaurant.
- [[Family Labor Model]]: the old Chinese-American restaurant relied on cheap immigrant and family labor with no succession plan.
- [[Panda Express]]: scaled through process; [[Peggy Cherng]] built a point-of-sale system in the 1980s, and the chain runs Panda University.
- [[Theory of Constraints]]: the binding constraint moved from folding talent to self-imposed quality standards and real estate.
- [[Tacit Knowledge]]: folding craft (18 folds, 21 grams per dumpling) turned into a trainable job track.
- [[Chinese Chains Entering the US]]: HeyTea, Chagee, Luckin Coffee, Wallace, and Haidilao arrive with systems already built.

## Key Takeaways
- **Old Bottleneck**: "The bottleneck used to be dumpling-folding talent," says Yang.
- **Family Labor Ran Out**: Immigrant waves faded and college-educated kids left; Chinese restaurants fell from 7.3% to 6.5% of restaurants.
- **Craft As Job Track**: Training takes 12 weeks, though full speed needs six months to a year.
- **Scale Output**: Each restaurant makes about 10,000 dumplings a day with roughly 30 chefs.
- **New Constraints**: Quality standards and 10,000–20,000 square foot sites now limit growth.
- **Expansion**: Three US restaurants opened this year; Downtown Brooklyn is slated for 2027.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Scaling requires converting tacit craft into teachable systems** — Growth that depends on individuals' hard-to-transfer skill is capped by the supply of those individuals; codifying the skill into training and process lifts the cap. · *model:* [[Tacit Knowledge]]
→ explains: Old Bottleneck, Craft As Job Track, Family Labor Ran Out

**T2. Removing one constraint exposes the next** — Once the talent bottleneck was solved, the limiting factors became quality standards and real estate. · *model:* [[Theory of Constraints]]
→ explains: New Constraints, Expansion

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does Din Tai Fung's CEO say changed about its growth bottleneck?
**A**: Dumpling-folding talent used to limit growth, but continuous improvement of training programs has largely overcome it. The constraint is now a self-imposed one driven by quality standards, plus scarce large real estate.

**Q2**: What specific evidence shows craft being turned into a system?
**A**: Folding is a corporate job track with a 12-week training program and a dumpling training manager in every US restaurant. Each dumpling is held to 18 folds and 21 grams.

**Q3**: What does the article imply about how Chinese food businesses can scale in America?
**A**: The chains that scaled replaced family labor with process early, as Panda Express and Din Tai Fung did. New Chinese entrants are arriving with systems already built.
