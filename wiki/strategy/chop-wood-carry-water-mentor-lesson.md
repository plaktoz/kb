## Summary

Sahil Bloom recounts meeting [[Tim Cook]] at a 4:45am gym session in 2014, observing that Cook showed zero pretension despite his enormous stature. The central lesson is drawn from the Zen principle "chop wood, carry water" — that success does not exempt anyone from the fundamentals, and true discipline means the routine never changes regardless of outcome or recognition.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Chop Wood Carry Water]]**: Zen principle stating that the right action before and after enlightenment is identical — fundamentals persist regardless of status.
- **[[Tim Cook]]**: Apple CEO whose consistent early-morning routine and willingness to mentor without expectation of return embodied the principle in practice.
- **[[Sahil Bloom]]**: Author and investor who shares the lesson as a formative mentor relationship.
- **[[Discipline and Consistency]]**: The idea that unglamorous, recurring habits are the substrate of lasting success.
- **[[Mentorship]]**: Cook's willingness to give time and guidance to someone with "little tangible value to offer in return" as a model of unconditional investment in others.

## Key Takeaways

- **Fundamentals persist**: Titles and outcomes shift, but core discipline stays the same.
- **Zero pretension**: Cook's demeanor at the gym illustrated humility at scale.
- **Unglamorous work matters**: Showing up consistently beats occasional brilliance.
- **Mentorship without transactional logic**: Cook continued mentoring despite no apparent return.
- **Action over words**: Cook embodied the principle through daily behavior, not speeches.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Chop Wood Carry Water]]**: The pre/post-enlightenment symmetry is a first-principles argument that discipline is not a means to an outcome but the permanent structure of a good life — success changes the scale of the work, not the nature of it.
- **[[Identity-Based Habits]]**: Cook's behavior illustrates that durable routines are anchored to identity ("I am someone who shows up at 4:45am"), not to external motivation — which is why status and recognition couldn't erode them.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central lesson Bloom draws from his relationship with Tim Cook?
**A**: That success does not exempt anyone from fundamentals — the discipline of showing up, doing unglamorous work, and staying grounded in core values must remain constant regardless of titles or outcomes.

**Q2**: What Zen principle anchors the piece, and what does it mean?
**A**: "Chop wood, carry water" — meaning the right action before enlightenment and after enlightenment is identical, illustrating that the routine itself is the point, not a stepping stone to something else.

**Q3**: How might someone apply this lesson practically?
**A**: By identifying their own "chop wood, carry water" habits — the foundational routines that matter — and committing to them regardless of whether they've already achieved success or recognition.
