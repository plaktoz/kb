## Summary
McKinsey argues that a CEO's tacit judgment can be codified by an "executive AI" and made available across the organization, amplifying rather than replacing human judgment. The system has five parts: steering attention, codifying tacit knowledge, augmenting decisions in real time, sparring, and closing the learning loop. The authors warn of four pitfalls (refill, convergence, inheritance, governance) and recommend proving value before scaling.

## Core Concepts
- [[Executive AI]]: an ambient layer with context, memory, and a persistent record, built on personal and organizational [[Knowledge Graph]]s.
- [[Tacit Knowledge]]: the unspoken mental models, inference patterns, and standards that top leaders use.
- [[Elicitation Techniques]]: shadowing and reaction interviews, preferred over self-reporting.
- [[CEO Excellence]] and *A CEO for All Seasons*: McKinsey research on executive judgment and four CEO phases.
- [[Red Team-Blue Team]] sparring and synthetic personas to pressure-test decisions.
- [[Flywheel Effect]]: organizational learning and system quality reinforce each other.
- [[Michael Porter]] and [[Nitin Nohria]]: HBS study on CEO time use.

## Key Takeaways
- **Five Parts**: Attention, codification, real-time decisions, sparring, and learning loop.
- **Opacity Problem**: Successors spend months reconstructing the predecessor's unstated standard of judgment.
- **Behavior Over Self-Report**: Shadowing reveals how leaders operate better than interviews.
- **CEO Time**: HBS study found under half of 62.5 weekly hours advance strategy.
- **Case Result**: 30% more decisions ready on first pass; about 100 reusable knowledge assets.
- **Case Baseline**: CEO logged 1,800 meetings; nearly 500 produced no decisions.
- **Four Pitfalls**: Refill, convergence, inheritance, and governance.
- **Dissent Instrumentation**: Record what was rejected and why; require outside input on major decisions.
- **Measures**: Flow measures and personal measures must move together.
- **Start Small**: Proof before scale, using three-to-six months of real decision records.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Tacit expertise is revealed by observed behavior, not self-description** — Experts cannot fully articulate their judgment, so it must be elicited from what they actually do. Once made explicit, it can be transmitted beyond the person who holds it. · *model:* [[Tacit Knowledge]]
→ explains: Opacity Problem, Behavior Over Self-Report, Five Parts

**T2. Learning systems compound when the organization and the tool improve each other** — As standards become legible, decisions arrive ready and the AI learns from wider use, which accelerates calibration across the organization. · *model:* [[Flywheel Effect]]
→ explains: Case Result, Measures

**T3. A shared calibration point filters out variance unless dissent is designed in** — When everyone calibrates against one standard, anomalous ideas get filtered and a tuned system can hand a successor dogma. Preserving rejected options, reasoning conditions, and outside input counters this.
→ explains: Four Pitfalls, Dissent Instrumentation

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim about executive AI?
**A**: AI can codify the tacit judgment of CEOs and top leaders and make it available across the organization. It amplifies human judgment rather than replacing it.

**Q2**: How does the system capture a leader's judgment, and what results did the case study report?
**A**: It uses shadowing and reaction interviews rather than self-reporting, built on a knowledge graph of decisions and signals. In the case, 30% more decisions arrived ready on the first pass, escalations dropped, and about 100 reusable knowledge assets were created.

**Q3**: What pitfalls should leaders guard against when deploying it?
**A**: Reclaimed hours refilling with marginal work, convergence that filters out dissent, successors inheriting dogma, and unresolved governance questions. Countermeasures include protecting reclaimed time, instrumenting for dissent, recording the reasoning behind standards, and setting capture and retention rules.
