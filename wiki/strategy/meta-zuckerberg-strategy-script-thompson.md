## Summary

Ben Thompson writes a fictional earnings call speech for [[Mark Zuckerberg]], framing what he wishes Meta's CEO would say publicly. The piece diagnoses past strategic mistakes — platform obsession, Reality Labs overinvestment, underestimating entertainment — and makes the affirmative case for why [[Meta]]'s consumer focus gives it a durable AI advantage. Thompson argues Meta's moat is celebrating and connecting humans, not building productivity tools or competing with [[OpenAI]].

## Core Concepts

- **[[Meta]]'s pivot to entertainment** — the feed is fundamentally an entertainment product; [[TikTok]] recognised this before Meta did
- **[[Reality Labs]] miscalculation** — hardware platform ambitions drove massive losses with insufficient strategic payoff
- **[[Targeted Advertising]]** — Thompson argues Zuckerberg never fully championed Meta's ad business as a genuinely valuable connection between entrepreneurs and consumers
- **AI as defensive investment** — all digital businesses face existential AI risk; Meta's AI spend is partly about not being disrupted
- **Compute spot-market discipline** — renting spare compute on the spot market creates a market-based hurdle rate to discipline internal AI spending
- **[[Ben Thompson]]** / [[Stratechery]] — the author's framing device exposes the gap between Meta's actions and its public narrative

## Key Takeaways

- **Platform Mistake**: Prioritizing third-party developers over Meta's own content hurt early mobile performance.
- **Entertainment Blind Spot**: TikTok revealed the feed was always an entertainment product, not a social graph tool.
- **Ad Business Neglect**: Zuckerberg never made the affirmative case for targeted advertising's societal value.
- **AI Inventory Expansion**: "AI makes every pixel monetizable" — biggest inventory expansion in Meta's history.
- **Consumer Advantage**: Meta's consumer focus (connection, entertainment, commerce) beats enterprise AI competitors.
- **Compute Hurdle Rate**: Renting spare compute on spot markets forces internal AI projects to justify their costs.
- **Core Moat**: Meta's enduring purpose is celebrating and connecting humans — not chatbots or productivity tools.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Opportunity Cost]]**: Thompson's compute spot-market proposal is a direct application of opportunity cost — Meta should only use its own compute when returns exceed what it could earn renting it out, making the hurdle rate explicit rather than implicit.
- **[[Jobs To Be Done]]**: Meta's strategic confusion stems from misidentifying its job — the feed's job is entertainment and connection, not platform infrastructure; clarity on the job unlocks the right competitive strategy.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Thompson's central argument about Meta's competitive advantage in the AI era?
**A**: Meta's moat is its consumer focus — celebrating, connecting, entertaining, and enabling commerce among humans — which is a structural advantage over enterprise AI competitors like OpenAI.

**Q2**: How does Thompson propose Meta discipline its internal AI compute spending?
**A**: By renting spare compute capacity on the spot market, creating a market-based hurdle rate that forces internal AI projects to outperform the rental revenue they forgo.

**Q3**: What practical implication does the "AI makes every pixel monetizable" claim have for Meta's business?
**A**: AI-improved ad targeting and content recommendation will unlock the largest inventory expansion in Meta's history, directly strengthening the core advertising revenue engine.
