## Summary

Scott Berinato of HBR argues that data storytelling is simpler than people assume: every story follows a universal three-part structure of setup, conflict, and resolution. He demonstrates by deconstructing a Global Real Home Price Index chart into focused, narrative-driven states — each highlighting only the data elements that serve the story. The result is that audiences do not merely understand the data; they feel it.

## Key Takeaways

- **Universal story arc**: Every story — setup, conflict, resolution — applies to data.
- **Change is conflict**: Without change in the data, there is no story to tell.
- **Titles carry the story**: Use chart titles that name the narrative, not just the dataset.
- **Strip distractions**: Remove all data not relevant to the story you are telling.
- **Multi-state charts**: Splitting one chart into states helps the audience track the narrative.
- **Emotional connection**: Narrative makes audiences feel data, not just process it.
- **Re-anchor baselines**: Understand what index points represent before assigning conflict.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Setup-Conflict-Resolution Framework]]**: The fundamental story arc — situation, disrupting change, new reality — is itself a mental model; Berinato shows it maps one-to-one onto chart structure, making it a practical design heuristic rather than just a literary concept.
- **[[Signal vs. Noise]]**: Berinato's explicit instruction to exclude data that does not serve the story is a direct application of ruthless signal-noise separation — the audience's attention is finite, so every element must earn its place.
