## Summary
BCG argues that CEO-CFO tension is natural, since the CFO shapes how the board sees the CEO, and can improve decisions if grounded in mutual trust. It recommends three moves: set clear expectations, welcome dissent, and invest in the relationship, plus a succession plan for the CFO question.

## Core Concepts
- **[[CEO-CFO Relationship]]**: The CFO has a direct line to the board and supplies the financial evidence used to judge the CEO.
- **[[BCG CEO Insomnia Index]]**: 2026 survey in which 26% of CEOs call the CFO the biggest threat to their job security.
- **[[Constructive Dissent]]**: Challenge that tests assumptions and prepares the CEO for scrutiny by the board and investors.
- **[[Decision Rights]]**: Ambiguity over roles and decision rights strains the relationship during major strategic decisions.
- **[[Mutual Trust]]**: The condition that turns friction into better decisions rather than strain.
- **[[Succession Planning]]**: CEO and board jointly clarify whether the CFO is a potential successor.
- **[[Julia Dhar]]**, **[[Michael Demyttenaere]]**, **[[James Tucker]]**: BCG partners quoted on expectations, dissent and investing in the relationship.

## Key Takeaways
- **Replacement Risk**: CFOs rank first among senior executives CEOs plan to replace.
- **Job Threat**: 26% of CEOs see the CFO as the biggest threat.
- **Leadership Churn**: 57% of CEOs plan leadership team changes within six months.
- **Clear Expectations**: Ask "How are you seeing this situation?" to surface hidden assumptions early.
- **Welcome Dissent**: Treat challenge as a tool for testing assumptions, not a threat.
- **Invest, Don't Manage**: Working around a distrusted CFO usually creates more problems later.
- **Succession Clarity**: Board and CEO should plan openly for the CFO's potential successor status.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Trust determines whether friction helps or harms** — Disagreement between interdependent leaders improves decisions only when they trust they share the same goal; without trust it erodes alignment and decision quality.
→ explains: Welcome Dissent, Invest, Don't Manage, Job Threat

**T2. Challenge tests assumptions before outsiders do** — Internal dissent surfaces weak assumptions and prepares a decision for scrutiny by the board, investors and other stakeholders.
→ explains: Welcome Dissent, Clear Expectations

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is BCG's core claim about CEO-CFO tension?
**A**: The tension is inevitable and not necessarily unproductive. With mutual trust, CEOs and CFOs can turn disagreement into better decisions instead of relationship strain.

**Q2**: What data shows how fraught the relationship is?
**A**: In the BCG CEO Insomnia Index, 26% of CEOs name the CFO as the biggest threat to their job security, and CFOs top the list of executives CEOs plan to replace. 57% plan leadership changes within six months.

**Q3**: What should a CEO do to build a productive relationship with the CFO?
**A**: Set clear expectations on roles and success, welcome constructive dissent, and invest in the relationship rather than working around the CFO. Also agree a succession plan with the board.
