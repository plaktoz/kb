## Summary
A new framework in the Global Strategy Journal models two types of geopolitical-economic order (state-based vs market-based) and shows that optimal firm strategy differs fundamentally between them. State-based orders reward bargaining power and national alignment; market-based orders reward global efficiency exploitation. Firms that misread which order governs their target market risk strategic misalignment.

## Core Concepts

- [[Geopolitical-Economic Order]] — the state-market relationship that firms encounter, determined by geopolitical dynamics
- [[State-Based Order]] — firms succeed through bargaining power, corporate nationality, and alignment with host country national goals
- [[Market-Based Order]] — firms succeed by exploiting global efficiencies; competitive advantage from operations, not geopolitics
- [[International Anarchy]] — the first principle: no world government means states prioritize self-interest, creating cooperative and competitive incentives that shape the state-market relationship
- [[MNE Strategy]] — multinational enterprise strategy must adapt to the type of order in each market it operates in

## Key Takeaways

- **State-based order strategy**: highlight relevance to host country's national goals; mitigate security concerns; large firms or firms from powerful home countries have an edge
- **Market-based order strategy**: focus on global efficiencies, cost optimization, and operational excellence
- **First principles starting point**: anarchy is what makes international politics different — work from this to derive the correct strategic posture
- **Wrong order → wrong strategy**: a firm optimizing for efficiency in a state-based market will fail to build the political relationships it actually needs to operate
- **Research citation**: Blake et al., "Geopolitics and global strategy: Making money under anarchy," Global Strategy Journal (2026)

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[First Principles Thinking]]**: The researchers explicitly returned to the first principles of international relations (anarchy → national self-interest → state-market relationship) rather than applying an existing framework — demonstrating that correct strategic models are derived from axioms, not borrowed from analogues.
- **[[Context Dependency]]**: Competitive advantage is not intrinsic to a firm's capabilities — it is contingent on the order type governing the market. The same capabilities can be a strength or a liability depending on context.
