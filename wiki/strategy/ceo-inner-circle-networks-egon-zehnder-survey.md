## Summary
An October [[Egon Zehnder]] survey of 843 CEOs finds leaders leaning on business alliances and informal peer networks to navigate geopolitical instability, AI disruption, and economic uncertainty. Peer relationships ranked above public-private partnerships as key external ties. CEOs also report that leading through uncertainty demands more endurance than expected.

## Core Concepts
- [[Egon Zehnder]]: Executive search firm that surveyed 843 CEOs globally.
- [[Business Alliances]]: Ranked the most important external relationship, cited by 87% of CEOs.
- [[Informal CEO Networks]]: Cited by 47%; give access to peers facing similar problems across industries and geographies.
- [[Public-Private Partnerships]]: Cited by 32% as important external relationships.
- [[Geopolitical Risk]]: 62% of CEOs expect greater disruption in the next 12 months.
- [[AI Adoption]]: Tops the list of challenges facing CEO organizations.
- [[Leadership Resilience]]: 82% say the environment requires more endurance than imagined.

## Key Takeaways
- **Alliance Priority**: 87% of CEOs rank business alliances as most important.
- **Peer Networks**: 47% value informal CEO networks for trusted, candid exchange.
- **Rising Importance**: Nearly half of alliance-prioritizers say they grew more important this year.
- **New Partnerships**: 30% of CEOs are forming new strategic alliances.
- **Assumption Testing**: Peers who faced similar decisions help executives evaluate risks faster.
- **Endurance Demand**: 82% say leadership now requires greater endurance.
- **Resilience Need**: 85% say success needs personal resilience plus traditional capabilities.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Trusted relationships speed up decision-relevant information** — Peers who have faced comparable decisions can surface practical information quickly, helping leaders test assumptions and assess risk under uncertainty.
→ explains: Peer Networks, Assumption Testing, Alliance Priority

**T2. Uncertainty raises the value of shared experience** — As disruption grows, leaders seek outside perspective and support that even seasoned executives may not find inside their own organizations.
→ explains: Rising Importance, New Partnerships, Endurance Demand

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about CEO networks?
**A**: A CEO's personal network is becoming an increasingly valuable business asset, as leaders turn to trusted peers amid geopolitical, technological, and economic uncertainty.

**Q2**: What data supports this shift?
**A**: In the Egon Zehnder survey of 843 CEOs, 87% cited business alliances and 47% informal CEO networks as important external relationships. 62% expect greater geopolitical disruption and 30% are forming new alliances.

**Q3**: How can a CEO use a peer network in practice?
**A**: A CEO weighing an AI investment, supply chain restructuring, or a politically sensitive expansion can draw on peers who faced similar decisions. This surfaces practical information faster and helps evaluate risks.
