## Summary

[[Darrin Donnelly]]'s *Think Like a Warrior* distills the success philosophies of five legendary sports coaches — [[John Wooden]], [[Buck O'Neil]], [[Herb Brooks]], [[Bear Bryant]], and [[Vince Lombardi]] — into a unified warrior mindset applicable to entrepreneurship and professional life. Each coach addresses a distinct crisis point: loss of control, bitterness, small thinking, quitting, and fear at the moment of success. Together, the five philosophies form a repeatable operating system for moving from struggle to unstoppable momentum.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Locus of Control]] (John Wooden)**: The only two things in your control are effort and attitude in the present moment — results are downstream consequences, not primary targets.
- **[[Duty to Be Joyful]] (Buck O'Neil)**: Gratitude and enthusiasm are moral obligations, not optional moods; O'Neil maintained joy despite systemic injustice and extreme hardship in the [[Negro Leagues]].
- **[[Unrealistic Goals]] (Herb Brooks)**: The real failure is setting safe, realistic goals that leave potential unrealized — Brooks coached the 1980 US Olympic hockey team to its famous [[Miracle on Ice]] upset.
- **[[Mental Toughness]] (Bear Bryant)**: Toughness is a daily decision built by refusing excuses and remembering that tough times end; the quitting habit is self-reinforcing and must be resisted early.
- **[[Faith Over Fear]] (Vince Lombardi)**: Confidence must be actively rebuilt even — especially — at the moment of success, by collecting evidence of past wins and visualizing the next.

## Key Takeaways

- **Two daily questions (Wooden)**: Did I give full attention today? Did I bring my best attitude?
- **Entitlement kills progress**: Believing you should already be further along is a destructive attitude.
- **Joy is a duty**: Model O'Neil — if he could stay joyful through historic injustice, most modern struggles are manageable.
- **Ignore naysayers' fears**: Others' risk aversion should not override your own informed assessment.
- **First quit is the hardest**: The second is easier, the third is automatic — protect your streak of not quitting.
- **Success creates vulnerability**: Fear re-enters exactly when things are going well; proactively guard against it.
- **Surround yourself with believers**: Your environment shapes your belief in what's possible.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Locus of Control]]**: Wooden's philosophy is the textbook application — directing energy only toward controllable inputs (effort, attitude) and detaching from uncontrollable outputs (results) reduces anxiety and sustains motivation across setbacks.
- **[[Habit Loop]]**: Bryant's warning about the quitting habit maps directly onto how repeated behaviors become automatic — the first quit cues a reward (relief from discomfort), making the loop easier to trigger next time.
