## Summary

[[Occam's Razor]], also known as the law of parsimony, is a problem-solving principle stating that among competing hypotheses, the one with the fewest assumptions should be selected. It is best used for forming initial conclusions before complete information is available, since simpler explanations are easier to test, verify, and execute. The principle has limits — complexity is sometimes warranted, and confirmation bias can distort our perception of which explanation is actually simpler.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Occam's Razor]]** (Law of Parsimony): prefer the hypothesis requiring the fewest assumptions
- **[[William of Ockham]]**: 14th-century friar and philosopher credited with formalizing the principle
- **[[Aristotle]]** and **[[Robert Grosseteste]]**: ancient antecedents who argued for fewer postulates producing knowledge more efficiently
- **[[Second Law of Thermodynamics]]**: physical support for parsimony — nature minimizes energy use (e.g., ball rolling downhill)
- **[[Confirmation Bias]]**: a key distorter of Occam's Razor; we may perceive the explanation that fits our priors as "simpler"
- **[[Hanlon's Razor]]**: a complementary mental model that interlocks with Occam's Razor
- **[[Availability Heuristic]]** and **[[Hindsight Bias]]**: other models that work alongside Occam's Razor

## Key Takeaways

- **Core rule**: choose the explanation with the fewest unnecessary assumptions.
- **Einstein's version**: "Everything should be made as simple as possible, but not simpler."
- **Medical application**: "When you hear hoofbeats, think horses, not zebras" — favor the most likely diagnosis.
- **Punishment theory**: Jeremy Bentham applied parsimony to justice — penalties shouldn't exceed the pain they prevent.
- **Key caveat**: simplicity alone isn't sufficient; empirical backing is still required.
- **Lisa Randall warning**: the world is sometimes more complicated than expected; simpler isn't always correct.
- **Common misreading**: the principle is not "the simplest answer is correct" — it's "don't stack unnecessary complexity."
- **Brain science**: habits form as neural pathways that require less energy, mirroring parsimony in biology.
- **Mental models interlock**: Occam's Razor works best alongside confirmation bias awareness and Hanlon's Razor.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Occam's Razor]]**: The principle itself is the subject — unnecessary assumptions increase the chance of error, so the fewest-assumption hypothesis minimizes compounded uncertainty before evidence is in.
- **[[Confirmation Bias]]**: Our judgment of which explanation is "simpler" is corrupted by prior beliefs, meaning Occam's Razor can be weaponized to rationalize preferred conclusions rather than eliminate them.
- **[[Second Law of Thermodynamics]]**: Nature minimizes energy expenditure by default, suggesting parsimony is not merely a heuristic but a structural feature of how systems evolve — making the razor a reflection of physical reality, not just logic.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of Occam's Razor?
**A**: Among competing hypotheses, the one requiring the fewest assumptions should be selected — simplicity reduces compounded uncertainty and makes theories easier to test and execute.

**Q2**: What is a key limitation of applying Occam's Razor in practice?
**A**: Confirmation bias can distort which explanation seems simpler, causing people to mistake their preferred explanation for the most parsimonious one; additionally, the world is sometimes genuinely complex and a simpler model may simply be wrong.

**Q3**: How is Occam's Razor applied in medicine, and what benefit does this provide?
**A**: Doctors seek the fewest causes for a patient's symptoms, summarized as "think horses, not zebras" — this minimizes over-treatment and reduces dangerous interactions between treatments.
