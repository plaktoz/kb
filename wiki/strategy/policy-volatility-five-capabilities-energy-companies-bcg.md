## Summary

BCG argues that government [[Industrial Policy]] interventions in advanced economies have nearly tripled in a decade, creating policy whiplash that most energy companies monitor but fail to integrate into capital allocation. Leaders differ by building five capabilities: assessment, quantification, scenario simulation, tracking, and enterprise-wide integration. Because volatility cuts both ways, these capabilities are a durable source of competitive advantage.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Industrial Policy]] Interventions**: Focused government actions that reshape competition, such as carbon pricing, tariffs, sanctions, subsidies, and tax credits.
- **Policy Whiplash**: Rapid reversals, e.g. the US introduced and largely unwound [[Inflation Reduction Act]] subsidies in 35 months.
- **[[Scenario Planning]]**: Granular 10+ year scenarios tested against edge cases, with built-in on- and off-ramps.
- **[[Optionality]]**: Flexibility to delay, switch suppliers, hedge, or reverse course, which carries a cost but is usually worth it.
- **[[Stranded Assets]]**: The same policy shift that strands one company's investments can create a windfall for another.
- **[[Second-Order Effects]]**: Leaders trace how policy ripples through supply chains and stakeholders.
- **[[Boston Consulting Group]] Center for Energy Impact (CEI)**: Source of the 150-executive survey and the $7.5 trillion capex forecast.
- **AI-Enabled Signposts**: Tools that map the regulatory landscape and score live signals tied to scenario playbooks.

## Key Takeaways

- **Frequency**: Industrial policy interventions rose from 2,273 in 2015 to 6,651 in 2025.
- **Energy Exposure**: Energy and energy-intensive sectors account for roughly 65% of interventions.
- **Stakes**: CEI forecasts $7.5 trillion capex from 275 energy companies in 2026-2030.
- **Embedding Gap**: Only 19% of executives say policy is fully embedded in investment decisions.
- **Siloed Analysis**: About 70% of government affairs teams monitor policy; only 12% analyze commercial impact.
- **Capability Gaps**: 66% lack early warning signals; 52% lack scenario-planning capabilities.
- **Horizon Mismatch**: Firms plan on 3-5 year cycles against assets lasting 40+ years.
- **Five Dimensions**: Assess, quantify, simulate, track, and integrate across the enterprise.
- **Proof Points**: Flexible offshore wind developers and hedged automakers adapted quickly in 2025.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Flexibility has a price, but rigidity costs more under uncertainty** — When outcomes are unpredictable, keeping options open costs something up front yet is usually cheaper than having none when conditions shift. · *model:* [[Optionality]]
→ explains: Proof Points, Five Dimensions

**T2. Information creates value only when it reaches the decision** — Monitoring a risk does not reduce it; insight must be quantified and wired into the choices that allocate capital, or the risk goes unaddressed.
→ explains: Embedding Gap, Siloed Analysis, Capability Gaps

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What distinguishes companies that handle policy volatility well?
**A**: They treat policy as a capability, not a monitoring task. They build five linked abilities (assessment, quantification, scenario simulation, tracking, enterprise integration) so policy shapes capital decisions.

**Q2**: What evidence shows most companies fail to integrate policy into decisions?
**A**: Only 19% of surveyed executives say policy is fully embedded in investment decisions, and only 12% say government affairs analyzes commercial impact. Also, 66% lack early warning signals and 52% lack scenario-planning capabilities.

**Q3**: How should an executive start building these capabilities?
**A**: Unify siloed policy teams into one company-wide view, run more frequent and granular scenario tests with AI-built signposts, and build optionality into the path from project funnel to final investment decision.
