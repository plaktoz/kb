## Summary

Responding to Felipe Csaszar's HBR argument that AI widens strategic search, keeps market models updated, and makes challenge routine, Schwarz argues that generating thousands of scenarios misses the point because scenarios only work when they change managers' mental models. AI becomes useful for foresight in organisations that already have an appetite for scenario thinking, by improving search, representation, and challenge. It amplifies the existing mindset and cannot do the reperceiving for a leadership team.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Felipe Csaszar]]**: Michigan Ross strategy chair arguing AI changes strategic decision-making through search, representation, and challenge.
- **[[Scenario Planning]]**: Foresight practice limited, like strategy offsites, by what a workshop can hold in its head (the two-by-two gives four scenarios).
- **[[Pierre Wack]]**: Shell scenario pioneer whose 1985 HBR articles held that scenarios fail unless they change managers' mental models.
- **[[Strategic Foresight]]**: Research of 400 senior managers found foresight yields conversations that rarely shift decisions.
- **[[Business Wargaming]]**: Structured challenge, close to Csaszar's AI debate, that links scenarios to decisions.
- **[[Prospective Sensemaking]]**: Making sense of what has not happened yet; the skill leaders lack.
- **[[Robust Leadership]]**: Framework of three capacities: imagining, sensing, challenging.

## Key Takeaways

- **Option Limits**: Teams consider only as many options as time and attention allow.
- **Quantity Trap**: A thousand scenarios is a document nobody inhabits.
- **Missing Friction**: Foresight conversations stay polite and lack cognitive friction, not input.
- **AI Convergence**: Teams using AI converged faster and disagreed less in scenario work.
- **Search**: Pre-workshop AI maps obvious futures so teams focus on what is unexpected.
- **Representation**: Continuously updated market views can tie signposts to scenarios and track drift.
- **Challenge**: Standing AI-run tests of decisions against each scenario link foresight to choices.
- **Mindset Condition**: Certainty-seeking organisations will use a thousand scenarios to confirm the current plan.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Scenarios work by changing perception, not by volume** — A scenario set is valuable only to the extent it changes how decision-makers see their situation; more output that confirms existing beliefs adds nothing. · *model:* [[Confirmation Bias]]
→ explains: Quantity Trap, Missing Friction, Mindset Condition

**T2. Group options are capped by attention and time** — Teams consider only as many alternatives as their time and attention allow, so familiar tools are simple by necessity. · *model:* [[Bounded Rationality]]
→ explains: Option Limits, Search

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does Schwarz doubt that AI-generated thousands of scenarios improve foresight?
**A**: Scenarios only matter if they change leaders' mental models, as Pierre Wack argued; a thousand scenarios that confirm existing beliefs are worth less than four that make a team see differently.

**Q2**: What evidence does Schwarz cite that more input does not fix foresight problems?
**A**: A study of 400 senior managers found foresight produces conversations that rarely shift decisions, and a Bavarian Foresight-Institute experiment found AI-using teams converged faster and disagreed less.

**Q3**: How should organisations with an appetite for scenarios apply AI?
**A**: Use it for pre-workshop search, continuously updated signposts tied to scenarios, and standing AI-driven challenge of decisions against each scenario, while humans still do the reperceiving.
