## Summary
The DECIDE framework is a six-step, deliberately heavyweight decision-making model (Define, Evaluate/Establish criteria, Create options, Identify solution, Decide and commit, Engage and execute) meant for irreversible "one-way door" decisions. It pairs with the lighter [[RAPID Framework]] for role assignment and contrasts with faster processes better suited to reversible "two-way door" decisions.

## Core Concepts
- The [[DECIDE Framework]] itself: a structured, sequential, documented decision process.
- [[One-Way Door Decisions]] vs [[Two-Way Door Decisions]] (irreversible vs reversible) — DECIDE is only worth the overhead for the former.
- The [[RAPID Framework]] (Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input, Decide) used within DECIDE's "Define" step to assign a single accountable decider.
- [[High Velocity Decision Making]] as the recommended lighter-weight alternative for common, reversible decisions.

## Key Takeaways
- **Six Steps**: Define, Establish Criteria, Create Options, Identify Solution, Decide/Commit, Engage/Execute.
- **Single Owner**: A named decider (not group consensus) improves both quality and speed.
- **Criteria First**: Set fundamental, major, and minor criteria before generating options to preserve objectivity.
- **Genuine Alternatives**: Options must include status quo/inaction, brainstormed without early criticism.
- **Written Record**: Documenting the decision and reasoning creates an audit trail and reduces ambiguity.
- **Explicit Commitment**: Dissenters must "disagree and commit" — silent non-buy-in shows up as under-prioritized execution.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models
- **[[Reversibility]]**: DECIDE's core gate is whether a decision is reversible — irreversible ("one-way door") choices justify heavyweight process, reversible ones don't, mirroring Bezos's classic decision-speed heuristic.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is DECIDE's core claim about when to use a heavyweight decision process?
**A**: It should only be used for irreversible, "one-way door" decisions — most everyday (reversible) decisions are better served by faster, lighter methods.

**Q2**: How does DECIDE ensure decision quality and speed simultaneously?
**A**: It assigns a single named decider via the RAPID framework, so real ownership avoids the need for unanimous buy-in while still gathering broad input.

**Q3**: How would a team apply DECIDE in practice?
**A**: Before generating options, define the problem and success measures, set tiered criteria, then require the decider to write down the final decision and reasoning and get explicit "disagree and commit" buy-in from stakeholders.
