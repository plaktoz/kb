## Summary

Reviewing [[Simone Stolzoff]]'s book *How to Not Know*, the piece argues the defining 21st-century skill is comfort with irreducible uncertainty rather than more certainty, since more information often just changes uncertainty's form rather than eliminating it. A hypothetical "Munger cut" layers on [[Charlie Munger]]'s discipline: he'd endorse humility and probabilistic thinking but push back on romanticizing uncertainty, insisting people should keep converting "I don't know" into investigation, expected-value reasoning, and resilient system design.

## Core Concepts

- [[Simone Stolzoff]] — author of *How to Not Know*, arguing uncertainty is a permanent condition to navigate, not a problem to solve.
- [[Charlie Munger]] — provides the "cut" or counter-lens applied to the book's ideas throughout.
- [[Uncertainty Intolerance]] — the psychological discomfort that drives conspiracy theories, dogmatism, and false confidence.
- [[Probabilistic Thinking]] — reasoning in odds ("I know roughly 60%") rather than binary certainty.
- [[Circle of Competence]] — mapping what you know, probably know, and definitely don't know as a competitive advantage.
- [[Optionality]] — structuring decisions so mistakes remain survivable under irreducible uncertainty.
- [[Inversion]] — Munger's habit of converting "I don't know" into "how could I know?"

## Key Takeaways

- **Core Thesis**: uncertainty is the permanent condition of life, not a solvable problem.
- **Information ≠ Knowledge**: more data often produces analysis paralysis rather than resolution.
- **Three Horsemen of Delusion**: comfort, hubris, and control distort our relationship with uncertainty.
- **Good Bets, Not Perfect Decisions**: judgment matters more than prediction under incomplete information.
- **Munger's Endorsement**: mapping what you know, probably know, and don't know is a real competitive advantage.
- **Munger's Pushback**: "I don't know" is only step one — always ask "how could I know?" next.
- **Optionality as Insurance**: avoiding leverage and keeping liquidity keeps mistakes survivable.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Probabilistic Thinking]]**: Munger's answer to "I don't know" is "I know roughly" — reasoning in odds (60%, 80%) rather than treating uncertainty as binary.
- **[[Circle of Competence]]**: Mapping what you know, probably know, and definitely don't know is framed as the real source of durable advantage over raw intelligence.
- **[[Inversion]]**: Munger's habit of converting ignorance into investigation — asking how a problem could be bounded, estimated, or inverted rather than simply accepted as unknowable.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the book's central argument according to the article?
**A**: The defining skill of the 21st century is becoming comfortable with irreducible uncertainty rather than seeking more certainty, since more information often just changes the form of uncertainty rather than resolving it.

**Q2**: How would Charlie Munger's "cut" on the book differ from its original argument?
**A**: Munger would endorse humility and probabilistic thinking but reject romanticizing uncertainty, insisting "I don't know" is only step one and that uncertainty should be reduced through investigation wherever possible.

**Q3**: What practical technique does the article highlight for making decisions under irreducible uncertainty?
**A**: Optionality — structuring decisions (avoiding leverage, keeping liquidity, preserving reputation) so that mistakes remain survivable even when outcomes can't be predicted.
