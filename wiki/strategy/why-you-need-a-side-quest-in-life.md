## Summary

[[Sahil Bloom]] argues that "life gravity" — both external (sunk costs, identity, salary) and internal (inability to perceive alternatives) — traps people in default life paths. He uses the metaphor of a gorilla hidden on a CT scan to illustrate [[Inattentional Blindness]]: prolonged focus on one thing literally blinds us to everything else. Side quests — low-stakes, goal-free pursuits outside career and family — break this blindness and restore the awareness that being trapped is optional.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Life Gravity]]**: The compounding forces that lock people into chosen paths; exists in two forms.
  - *External gravity*: visible sunk costs — salary, reputation, identity — that make leaving feel costly.
  - *Internal gravity*: the subtler force; you stop perceiving alternatives as real options altogether.
- **[[Inattentional Blindness]]**: A psychological phenomenon where sustained focus on a primary task causes you to miss obvious stimuli. Bloom cites a 2013 Harvard/Brigham study where 83% of radiologists missed a gorilla on a CT scan.
- **[[Side Quests]]**: Pursuits outside the two main life tracks (career and personal/family). Described as *atelic* — pursued for their own sake, not to reach a goal. Examples: hobbies, physical challenges, art, learning.
- **[[Atelic Activity]]**: Activity without an endpoint or external goal; valued for the process rather than outcome.

## Key Takeaways

- **Two forms of life gravity**: external (sunk costs) and internal (perceptual narrowing).
- **Inattentional blindness study**: 83% of radiologists missed a visible gorilla on CT scans.
- **Side quests are atelic**: pursued without a goal, making them low-pressure and sustainable.
- **Side quests as perception tool**: they restore awareness that alternatives exist, not just as escape routes.
- **Bloom's example**: pandemic writing became a newsletter career — not planned, just started.
- **Core argument**: side quests matter because they prove being trapped is *optional*.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Opportunity Cost Blindness]]**: Internal life gravity is a form of opportunity cost blindness — when you stop seeing alternatives, you can't even weigh them. Side quests force the counterfactual into view.
- **[[Inattentional Blindness]]**: The gorilla-on-CT-scan study is a first-principles illustration of why optionality atrophies: narrow focus is cognitively efficient but perceptually costly — you trade breadth of awareness for depth of performance.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Bloom's central argument for why people should pursue side quests?
**A**: Side quests counteract "life gravity" — the forces that trap people in default paths — by restoring the awareness that being trapped is optional, not inevitable.

**Q2**: What does the 2013 Harvard/Brigham CT scan study demonstrate, and how does Bloom apply it?
**A**: 83% of radiologists missed a gorilla embedded in a CT scan due to inattentional blindness; Bloom uses this to argue that prolonged focus on one life path causes people to stop perceiving other options entirely.

**Q3**: How should someone approach a side quest, and what outcome should they expect?
**A**: Side quests should be pursued atelically — without a goal — because the value is in restoring perceptual breadth, not in achieving a specific outcome (though life-changing results are possible, as with Bloom's newsletter).
