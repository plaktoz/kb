## Summary

When AI collapses the processing advantage, analysts who feed the same vendor data into similar models produce consensus outputs rather than differentiated insight. Secondary data is a depreciating asset whose value now converges to consensus within hours, pushing data vendors toward true content exclusivity or a price race to the bottom. The author argues organisations must ask where their unique informational advantage comes from, and previews "primary intelligence" as the alternative in a follow-up article.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Secondary Data]]**: information already collected, processed, and distributed by someone else; essential for baseline but shared.
- **[[Factor Crowding]]**: in quantitative finance, a signal degrades when too many funds trade on it; the analytical equivalent now applies to AI analysis.
- **[[Competitive Intelligence]]**: market research and competitor analysis, increasingly built on shared sources.
- **[[Data Vendors]]**: Bloomberg, Refinitiv, Crunchbase, PitchBook, Statista, and S&P Capital IQ, whose edge came from speed and format.
- **[[Primary Intelligence]]**: source-level, proprietary information previewed as the alternative.
- **[[Commoditization]]**: differentiated insight turning into a commodity output.

## Key Takeaways

- **Processing Edge Gone**: AI ingests and synthesises data in seconds, removing the vendor-era advantage.
- **Consensus Output**: Same inputs and similar models yield near-identical reports.
- **Compressed Cycle**: Consensus lag fell from days to roughly 30-minute steps within a morning.
- **Depreciating Asset**: Secondary data's value decays faster as AI agents share signals.
- **Vendor Pressure**: Only vendors with content exclusivity can sustain premium pricing.
- **Strategic Question**: Where does your unique informational advantage come from?

## 🧠 Core Principles

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about AI and competitive intelligence?
**A**: When analysts feed the same secondary data into similar AI models, the output becomes consensus rather than a proprietary edge. Clients pay for differentiated insight and receive a commodity.

**Q2**: What mechanism makes secondary data lose value so quickly?
**A**: It has already passed through multiple hands, and AI agents monitoring the same feeds distribute the same signals. The gap between data availability and consensus approaches zero, like factor crowding in quantitative finance.

**Q3**: What should organisations and data vendors do in response?
**A**: Organisations should ask where their unique informational advantage originates rather than relying on shared platforms. Vendors thrive only by claiming content exclusivity, such as proprietary or source-level data.
