## Summary

In a Wharton@Work Q&A, [[Kartik Hosanagar]] argues that AI pilots stall because companies treat the model as the change, when workflows, incentives, decision rights, and accountability must be redesigned around it. Executives should evaluate AI by starting from the decision and its baseline, ask which business-model constraint AI relaxes and which bottleneck appears next, and keep the analytical foundations needed to question fluent but invalid outputs. Oversight must be operationally meaningful and matched to risk.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Transformation]] stages**: individual productivity, [[Workflow Redesign]], [[AI Agents]] with decision rights, and AI as part of the operating model (allocation of work, information, authority, accountability).
- **Local vs. system productivity**: faster individual tasks do not make the process faster when output waits on review or effort shifts from production to verification.
- **[[Business Model Change]]**: ask what AI makes abundant and what stays scarce; value may move to judgment, proprietary context, customer trust, and ownership of decisions. Example: [[Management Consulting]] shifting from delivering answers to implementation and outcome-based fees.
- **Agents as customers**: [[AI Agents]] may research, filter, and transact for buyers, so marketers must make brands legible to machines as well as people.
- **Analytical foundations**: [[Correlation vs Causation]], experiment design, and measurement literacy let leaders question fluent analyses.
- **[[Responsible AI]] governance**: guardrails, systematic testing, exception routing, human override, and oversight scaled to risk (see [[AI Bill of Rights]], [[Accountable AI Lab]]).
- **Decision-first evaluation**: define the decision, baseline, and [[Counterfactual Thinking]] before judging a model.
- **Executive education**: separating [[Executive Education]] credentials from capability; online suits foundations, in-person suits peer learning and current curriculum.

## Key Takeaways

- **Model Is Not the Change**: A capable model is one component of a working business system.
- **Productivity Gap**: "You can make every employee 30 percent more productive without making the company 30 percent more productive."
- **Four Stages**: Individual tool, workflow redesign, agents with decision rights, operating-model change.
- **Verification Shift**: AI can create more output than teams absorb, moving effort to review.
- **Beyond Labor Savings**: Labor-savings cases are easy to estimate but strategically narrow.
- **Next Bottleneck**: Ask "What bottleneck appears next?" not just "What can we generate?"
- **Hypothesis Mindset**: Treat new offerings and operating models as hypotheses to test.
- **Agents as Audience**: Brands may need to be credible to systems acting for buyers.
- **Analytics Matters More**: Fluent answers to poorly framed measurement questions are still wrong.
- **Capability Over Credential**: Test whether learning changes how leaders frame problems and question outputs.
- **Risk-Matched Oversight**: Reviewing every output does not scale; scrutiny should match stakes.
- **Start With Decision**: Establish baseline, failure modes, and ownership before declaring success.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Local gains are not system gains** — Speeding up one task raises organizational output only if the surrounding process, approvals, and review capacity change too; otherwise work queues elsewhere. · *model:* [[Local Optima vs. Global Optima]]
→ explains: Productivity Gap, Model Is Not the Change, Verification Shift

**T2. Relaxing one constraint exposes the next** — When AI makes one input abundant, the limiting factor moves to another (judgment, testing, distribution, buy-in), so strategy must target the new bottleneck. · *model:* [[Theory of Constraints]]
→ explains: Next Bottleneck, Beyond Labor Savings, Hypothesis Mindset

**T3. Judge against a baseline** — A system's value is its improvement over the present alternative or doing nothing, with failures detectable and containable; accuracy alone says little. · *model:* [[Counterfactual Thinking]]
→ explains: Start With Decision, Analytics Matters More

**T4. Verification requires a mental model** — Without foundational understanding of a domain, people cannot reliably check a polished AI answer for omissions, spurious causation, or infeasibility.
→ explains: Capability Over Credential, Analytics Matters More

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why do AI pilots often fail to become durable business results, according to Hosanagar?
**A**: Companies treat the model or tool as the change, while workflows, incentives, decision rights, and accountability stay the same. A capable model is only one component of a working business system.

**Q2**: What is the difference between local and system productivity, and why does it matter?
**A**: Local productivity is an employee finishing a task faster; system productivity is the organization moving faster. AI can create more output than teams can review, so work waits longer or effort shifts to verification.

**Q3**: How should an executive evaluate whether an AI tool solves the right business problem?
**A**: Start with the decision: establish how it is made today, its cost, performance, and the do-nothing baseline, then examine failure modes, integration costs, and ownership of the outcome. Only a system that improves a consequential decision with detectable, containable failures solves the problem.
