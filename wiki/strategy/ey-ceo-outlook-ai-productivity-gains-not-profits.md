## Summary

[[EY]]'s latest [[EY CEO Outlook Survey]] finds that AI is now the top-cited driver of material productivity gains, yet nearly a quarter of CEOs struggle to turn those gains into measurable financial outcomes. EY argues productivity only becomes value when management makes a "second decision" about how to redeploy freed capacity: raising output, shortening cycle time, improving quality, strengthening resilience, or moving people to higher-value work. Only 16% of CEOs have clear, real-time visibility into AI return on investment, so many leaders cannot see whether that redeployment is happening.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI ROI Gap]]**: The disconnect between AI-driven productivity gains and measurable growth or profitability; EY puts the share of CEOs who struggle with it at nearly a quarter.
- **[[EY CEO Outlook Survey]]**: EY's survey of 1,200 CEOs across 21 countries, conducted in August and September 2026.
- **Productivity-to-Value "Second Decision"**: EY's framing that efficiency gains need an explicit management choice about where the extra capacity goes before they become value.
- **[[Operating Model Transformation]]**: [[Andrea Guerzoni]] (EY-Parthenon global vice chair) argues the winners will redesign how people and technology work together and allocate capital carefully.
- **Capacity Absorption**: Gains get used up by operational complexity, regulation, risk management, and extra work demands instead of reaching the bottom line.
- **[[Business Process Redesign]]**: Ranked as the second-biggest productivity driver (46%), just behind AI (50%).
- **[[Capital Allocation]]**: Positioned as a core lever for turning productivity into long-term sustainable growth.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI Leads Productivity**: 50% of CEOs name AI as the biggest productivity driver.
- **Process Redesign Close Behind**: 46% cite business-process redesign as the top driver.
- **Conversion Struggle**: Nearly a quarter can't convert gains into measurable financial outcomes.
- **Output Per Employee Up**: Almost all CEOs report more output per employee year over year.
- **Redeployment Split**: 48% redirect capacity to growth, innovation, and transformation.
- **Absorbed Gains**: 23% say complexity, regulation, and risk work absorb productivity improvements.
- **Visibility Deficit**: Only 16% have clear, real-time visibility into AI ROI.
- **Core Quote**: "Productivity alone is not a strategy," per EY-Parthenon's Guerzoni.
- **Related Framing**: Echoes [[closing-the-gap-ai-investment-financial-return]] and [[why-ai-is-booming-but-productivity-isnt]].

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Parkinson's Law]]**: Work expands to fill the time available, which describes the 23% of CEOs who see freed capacity absorbed by complexity and new demands rather than converted to output.
- **[[Productivity Paradox]]**: As in earlier technology waves, measurable efficiency gains don't show up in financial results until organizations restructure around the technology, which is why EY stresses operating-model redesign.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of EY's 2026 CEO Outlook Survey about AI?
**A**: AI is delivering real productivity gains (half of CEOs call it the top driver), but many companies, nearly a quarter, struggle to turn those gains into measurable financial outcomes like growth and profitability.

**Q2**: What happens to the freed-up capacity from AI productivity gains, according to the survey?
**A**: 48% of CEOs say they put additional capacity toward growth, innovation, and transformation, while 23% say the gains are absorbed by operational complexity, regulatory requirements, risk management, and additional work demands.

**Q3**: What should a leader do to make AI productivity show up in financial results?
**A**: Make the explicit "second decision" about where capacity goes (more output, shorter cycle times, better quality, more resilience, or higher-value work), and build real-time AI ROI visibility, which only 16% of CEOs currently have.
