## Summary

Centralize, founded by ex-Meta and Slack engineers Rachit Kataria and William Wang, has raised a $15M Series A led by NEA to build a "deal GPS" for enterprise revenue teams. The platform addresses a structural gap in modern sales: the absence of a live relationship layer that tracks all stakeholders across a buying committee. AI agents continuously map org charts, flag stakeholder changes, and guide reps through multi-threading, treating the people involved in a deal as a navigable map.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Multi-Threading]]** — the practice of identifying, engaging, and organizing all necessary stakeholders high and wide within a target company, including procurement, legal, and the C-suite, to prevent single-point-of-failure account risk.
- **[[Deal GPS]]** — Centralize's central metaphor: a visual, multiplayer account map where "people are the map" and AI provides turn-by-turn navigation through the buying committee.
- **[[Relationship Layer]]** — the missing abstraction in most CRM/sales platforms; existing tools capture activity but not the live graph of who holds influence, who has left, and who is newly relevant.
- **[[AI Sales Agent]]** — "Centra," Centralize's AI assistant, answers relationship questions in real time (e.g., "Who owns the budget?") and proactively surfaces champion departures or unengaged decision-makers.
- **[[Rachit Kataria]]** — co-founder and CEO; previously founding engineer on Facebook Shops at [[Meta]], scaled it from zero to 250M MAU in one year.
- **[[William Wang]]** — co-founder and CTO; created [[Slack]] Huddles alongside [[Stewart Butterfield]] and Cal Henderson.
- **[[New Enterprise Associates]]** (NEA) — led the Series A; noted that buying committees have nearly doubled in size over the last decade, widening the structural gap Centralize targets.
- **[[Salesforce Ventures]]** — participated in both the seed and Series A rounds.
- **[[Y Combinator]]** — backed Centralize through its Winter 2024 batch.

## Key Takeaways

- Centralize raised $15M Series A (NEA-led); $19M total since 2023 founding.
- Core insight: enterprise deals fail because the relationship graph shifts faster than reps track it.
- AI agents ingest calls, emails, calendar events, and web data to build live org charts.
- Platform flags champion departures, unengaged decision-makers, and missing stakeholders proactively.
- Pricing model: "accounts under management" with unlimited seats — encourages cross-functional adoption.
- Revenue grew ~8x in the past year; clients include CoreWeave, Cognition, Brex, LangChain, Webflow.
- Free single-player tier launched alongside funding to drive bottoms-up adoption.
- 2026 venture funding into sales/marketing/CRM tech on pace to beat 2025, which was the strongest year since 2022.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Single Point of Failure]]**: Relying on one champion in a multi-stakeholder sale creates catastrophic brittleness — when that person leaves, the entire deal context evaporates. Multi-threading is the structural fix: redundancy at the relationship layer, not just the technical layer.
- **[[Map vs. Territory]]**: Most CRMs capture a frozen snapshot (the territory as it was) while deals unfold in a living social graph (the actual territory). Centralize's "deal GPS" reframes sales tooling as continuous cartography — the map must update as the territory moves.
