## Summary

The article distinguishes instinct (evolutionary, biology-driven, amygdala-based) from intuition (experience-driven rapid pattern recognition), two "gut feelings" that are commonly conflated. Drawing on Gary Klein's research with firefighters, Anne-Laure Le Cunff proposes a two-axis Gut Decision Matrix to determine when each type of gut response should be trusted. The core reframe is to ask not *whether* to trust your gut, but *what kind* of gut feeling it actually is.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Instinct]] vs [[Intuition]]**: Instinct is biological and survival-oriented; intuition is learned expertise compressed into rapid pattern-matching.
- **[[Gut Decision Matrix]]**: A 2×2 framework with axes of (instinct vs intuition) and (immediate danger vs complex situation).
- **[[Gary Klein]]**: Psychologist whose research on naturalistic decision-making showed expert firefighters acting on tacit pattern recognition, not conscious reasoning.
- **[[Pattern Recognition]]**: The mechanism underlying expert intuition — the brain matches current cues to a library of prior experiences before conscious explanation.
- **[[Amygdala]]**: The brain structure central to instinctual threat responses.

## Key Takeaways

- **Instinct + immediate danger**: act quickly — survival circuits are well-calibrated for this.
- **Instinct + complex situation**: slow down; biological wiring may misfire in non-survival contexts.
- **Intuition + fast-moving situation with domain expertise**: reasonable to trust it.
- **Intuition + complex/slower situation**: treat as a hypothesis; test before committing.
- **Klein's firefighters**: experts sensed danger before articulating why — pattern, not mystery.
- **Core shift**: diagnostic question changes from "trust it?" to "what kind is it?"

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[System 1 vs System 2 Thinking]]**: Instinct and intuition are both System 1 outputs, but the matrix distinguishes *calibrated* System 1 (expert intuition) from *uncalibrated* System 1 (raw biological instinct) — an important split Kahneman's original framing collapses.
- **[[Probabilistic Thinking]]**: Treating expert intuition as a prior hypothesis to be tested rather than a verdict applies probabilistic discipline to gut signals, preserving their speed while reducing overconfidence.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central claim of the Gut Decision Matrix?
**A**: Rather than asking whether to trust your gut, you should first identify *what kind* of gut feeling it is — instinct (biological/survival) or intuition (experience-based pattern recognition) — because each deserves a different response.

**Q2**: What evidence does Gary Klein's research provide for the model?
**A**: Klein found that seasoned firefighters could sense danger before consciously explaining why, demonstrating that expert intuition is rapid pattern-matching against learned domain experience, not a mysterious sixth sense.

**Q3**: How should you apply the matrix to a complex business decision where you have a "bad feeling"?
**A**: First classify the feeling: if it is instinct with no relevant domain expertise, slow down and question it; if it is intuition grounded in real experience, treat it as a testable hypothesis rather than an immediate verdict before committing.
