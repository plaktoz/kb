## Summary
The first quarterly AI Strategy Review analyzes how the 150 largest US and European companies (S&P 100 and STOXX Europe 50) set out, own, and measure AI. The widest gap is between why they want AI (61% cite efficiency) and what they can show for it (only 9% report a company-wide money figure), and tools often arrive before training.

## Core Concepts
- **[[AI Strategy Index]]**: Tracks [[Enterprise AI Strategy]] across 150 companies as of November 24, 2026.
- **[[AI Ownership]]**: CEO, dedicated AI leader, [[Chief Information Officer]], or data and AI chief.
- **[[AI Fluency Matrix]]**: A "fluency floor" is a company-wide baseline; all 21 companies sit at Level 1, Chatting.
- **[[AI Agents]]**: 61% use or plan them in operations.
- **[[AI Value Measurement]]**: Company-wide money figures, e.g. [[IBM]], [[Bank of America]], [[Deutsche Telekom]], [[Banco Santander]].

## Key Takeaways
- **Strategy Visibility**: Only 15% publish a standalone AI strategy; 32% are inferred only.
- **Efficiency First**: 61% cite efficiency; 48% growth; 25% a new operating model.
- **Focus Areas**: 58% target operations; 57% staff tools; 44% build AI into products.
- **Tools Before Training**: 29% give AI tools to all staff; only 12% require training.
- **Value Gap**: Only 9% report a company-wide money figure for AI value.
- **Owner Effect**: 24% report a figure when CEO or data/AI chief owns AI, vs 3%.
- **Tech Paradox**: 52% of technology companies have no stated AI strategy of their own.
- **Jobs Link**: 29% tie job cuts, smaller teams or slower hiring to AI.
- **US vs Europe**: US builds (software, products); Europe trains; both rarely report value.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. What isn't baselined and owned can't be shown to work** — Claimed value needs a measure, a baseline or target, and an accountable owner who reports against it; without them results stay unadded activity. The report notes these are associations, not proof of cause. · *model:* [[Principal-Agent Problem]]
→ explains: Value Gap, Owner Effect

**T2. Access to a capability is not the same as the ability to use it** — Distributing tools without required training leaves a low capability floor.
→ explains: Tools Before Training

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the widest gap in how large companies approach AI?
**A**: The gap between why they want AI and what they can show for it: 61% cite efficiency, but only 9% report a company-wide money figure for AI's value.

**Q2**: What pattern links AI ownership to reporting value?
**A**: 24% of companies where the CEO or a data and AI chief owns AI report a money figure, against 3% of the rest; none of the 28 with a dedicated AI leader do.

**Q3**: What should a company's AI strategy include, according to the report's checklist?
**A**: A written, readable strategy with one named owner, a value measure with a baseline, and a fluency floor for every employee with a level, a date, and required training.
