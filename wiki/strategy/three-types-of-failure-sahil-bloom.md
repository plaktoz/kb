## Summary

[[Sahil Bloom]] shares a personal DNF at mile 21 of a marathon — caused by dehydration on an unexpectedly hot, humid day — and extracts three durable lessons about failure. He proposes a taxonomy of failure that separates uncontrollable bad luck from fixable execution errors, freeing the mind to respond appropriately to each. The overarching message is that identity must be rooted in the effort and person formed during the journey, not in reaching the finish line.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Three Types of Failure]]**: A framework for classifying setbacks by their origin
  - *Failures of Nature* — caused by uncontrollable external forces; no productive response beyond acceptance
  - *Failures of Effort* — caused by not trying hard enough; the only category Bloom considers truly intolerable
  - *Failures of Execution* — caused by a fixable mistake; a gift in disguise because they are learnable
- **[[Survive and Advance]]**: The mantra that prioritizes staying in the game over short-term toughness; quitting an injury today preserves compounding participation tomorrow
- **[[Identity vs. Achievement]]**: Decoupling self-worth from arbitrary external outcomes; the person forged in training retains value regardless of race result
- **[[Compounding]]**: Life and growth compound only for those who remain participants — DNF-ing an injured race is not defeat, it is protecting the compounding trajectory
- **[[Get in the Arena]]**: Bloom invokes a Rooseveltian frame — the willingness to attempt and fail publicly is itself the defining act

## Key Takeaways

- **Stay in the Game**: Pushing through injury for bravado risks long-term sideline time.
- **Failures of Nature**: Uncontrollable; spend zero emotional energy here.
- **Failures of Effort**: The one truly intolerable failure — never acceptable.
- **Failures of Execution**: Fixable; diagnose and correct next time.
- **Marathon DNF cause**: Known forecast ignored, hydration not adjusted, water bottle missed twice.
- **Identity anchor**: Tie identity to the process and person formed, not outcomes.
- **Cool Runnings quote**: "If you're not enough without it, you'll never be enough with it."

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Locus of Control]]**: By sorting failures into controllable vs. uncontrollable buckets, Bloom operationalises this model — directing energy only toward the fixable tier (Execution) and releasing the uncontrollable tier (Nature) without guilt.
- **[[Compounding]]**: The "Survive and Advance" principle rests on the compounding insight: early exit from a bad day preserves the long run of participation; reckless persistence can permanently foreclose future compounding.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of Bloom's three-types-of-failure framework?
**A**: Not all failures deserve the same emotional response — categorising by origin (Nature, Effort, Execution) lets you direct energy only where it can make a difference.

**Q2**: What specifically caused Bloom's marathon DNF, and why does he classify it as a Failure of Execution?
**A**: He knew the forecast (70°F+, 90% humidity) but didn't adjust his hydration plan and missed his water bottle twice — all fixable decisions, making it an Execution failure rather than bad luck.

**Q3**: How should the identity/achievement distinction change how someone approaches ambitious goals?
**A**: Root identity in the person formed during training, not in reaching any specific outcome; this lets you attempt hard things repeatedly without existential risk from any single failure.
