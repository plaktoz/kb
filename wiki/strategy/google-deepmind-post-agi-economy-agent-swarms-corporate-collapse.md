## Summary

Sylvain Saurel argues that a [[Google DeepMind]] paper on the post-AGI economy shows the real disruption comes not from a single model outsmarting humans but from AI agents coordinating into swarms that outcompete whole organizations. Human companies are bounded by internal coordination costs, so an organization that does not suffer that "gravity" could erase the limits keeping any one firm from dominating. The excerpt covers the framing and the first of four scenarios (Minimal AGI) and ends there.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Google DeepMind]] post-AGI framework**: Splits scenarios along two axes, individual capability (solo task skill) and cooperative capability (agents coordinating at scale), yielding four futures.
- **[[Agent Swarms]]**: Coordinated digital agents that could compete as organizations rather than as individual workers.
- **[[Ronald Coase]] and [[Transaction Cost Economics]]**: Firms exist because internal coordination is cheaper than market transactions.
- **Internal [[Coordination Costs]]**: Grow with headcount and cap firm size, shielding startups from giants.
- **[[Brooks's Law]]**: Adding people to a late software project makes it later; named by [[Fred Brooks]].
- **[[Dunbar's Number]]**: [[Robin Dunbar]]'s roughly 150-person ceiling on stable social trust; past it, bureaucracy replaces trust.
- **[[Minimal AGI]] scenario**: AI at average human level; employment stays stable but wages for routine knowledge work collapse.
- **[[AI Benchmarks]]**: Called a "single-player" measure that misses real organizational work.

## Key Takeaways

- **Wrong Threat**: Focus on solo model intelligence misses coordinated agent swarms.
- **Organizations Compete**: In the real economy, organizations compete against organizations, not individuals.
- **Basketball Analogy**: Five brilliant solo robots would still lose to a coordinated championship team.
- **Productivity Puzzle**: Faster individuals gain little when output waits two weeks for approvals.
- **Bureaucracy as Moat**: Coordination costs cap firm size, leaving room for small startups.
- **Four Futures**: Individual and cooperative capability axes define four post-AGI worlds.
- **Minimal AGI**: Jobs persist, but average cognitive labor's wages and bargaining power erode.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about where the biggest AI disruption comes from?
**A**: It comes when AI agents learn to coordinate as swarms that outcompete human institutions, not when a single model becomes smarter than a person.

**Q2**: Why does the article say human corporations are limited in size?
**A**: Coase's transaction costs explain why firms exist, but internal coordination costs (meetings, sign-off chains, politics, the trust limit around 150 people) rise with scale and slow big companies. That slowness leaves room for startups.

**Q3**: What does the article imply about AI tools that only speed up individual workers?
**A**: They yield little macro productivity gain while approval and alignment bottlenecks remain. The humans who act as organizational glue are only safe as long as AI cannot coordinate itself.
