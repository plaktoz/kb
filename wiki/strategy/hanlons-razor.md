## Summary

Hanlon's Razor is the principle "never attribute to malice that which can be adequately explained by neglect," asserting that incompetence and inattention account for far more harmful outcomes than deliberate bad acts. The idea predates its naming, appearing in writings by Goethe and Napoleon. The recommended default is to assume good faith broadly, while reserving ruthlessness for confirmed cases of malice.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Hanlon's Razor]]**: The core heuristic — assume [[Negligence]] before [[Malice]] when interpreting others' behavior.
- **[[Confirmation Bias]]**: Expecting malice primes you to find it everywhere, distorting interpretation of neutral events.
- **[[Availability Heuristic]]**: Vivid past incidents of wrongdoing color how new, unrelated events are perceived.
- **[[Manufactured Outrage]]**: Media outlets exploit the tendency to attribute malice; the razor helps resist this framing.
- **[[Charitable Interpretation]]**: Default assumption of good faith preserves relationships and reduces unnecessary conflict.

## Key Takeaways

- **Core Rule**: Never attribute to malice what neglect can explain — incompetence dominates.
- **Historical Depth**: Goethe (1774) and Napoleon each independently articulated the same insight.
- **Relationships**: Most perceived slights are carelessness, not cruelty.
- **Media Literacy**: Outrage-driven media exploits malice attribution; the razor is a filter.
- **Confirmation Bias Risk**: Assuming bad intent creates a self-fulfilling interpretive loop.
- **Important Limit**: If malice is confirmed, the razor no longer applies — respond firmly.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Hanlon's Razor]]**: Steers interpretation away from the most emotionally salient explanation (malice) toward the statistically dominant one (error), reducing unnecessary conflict and preserving cooperative relationships.
- **[[Availability Heuristic]]**: Memorable betrayals inflate the perceived frequency of malice, making the razor a necessary corrective — it forces a return to base rates rather than vivid anecdotes.
- **[[Confirmation Bias]]**: Once malice is assumed, all subsequent evidence gets filtered through that lens; applying the razor breaks the loop before it starts.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central claim of Hanlon's Razor?
**A**: Never attribute to malice that which can be adequately explained by neglect — incompetence and inattention far outnumber deliberate bad acts in everyday life.

**Q2**: What cognitive biases does Hanlon's Razor help counteract, and how?
**A**: It counters confirmation bias (expecting malice makes you find it everywhere) and the availability heuristic (vivid past incidents distort interpretation of new events) by defaulting to a charitable, base-rate explanation.

**Q3**: When does Hanlon's Razor no longer apply, and what should you do then?
**A**: When malice is confirmed rather than suspected, the razor's charitable default is set aside and the recommended response is to be ruthless.
