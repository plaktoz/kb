## Summary
The article argues that war gaming, where participants play competitors and stakeholders to stress-test a strategy before it becomes a commitment, is strengthened by AI when AI is used to broaden scenarios, simulate stakeholder agents, and extend analysis after live sessions. It insists AI should amplify human judgment rather than replace it, and that war gaming should be run as an ongoing capability rather than a one-off exercise.

## Core Concepts
- **[[War Gaming]]**: An immersive exercise in which participants take on competitor and stakeholder roles, make decisions under pressure, and reveal the incentives, biases, and blind spots that shape behavior.
- **[[Strategic Foresight]]**: Preparing for what lies ahead by envisioning possible worlds, defining what winning looks like, identifying decision points and tradeoffs, and monitoring signals for if-then decisions.
- **[[Scenario Planning]]**: Building multiple future worlds and exploring the moves, countermoves, and decision paths within each; AI can generate dozens of additional scenarios before a live session.
- **[[Multiagent Systems]]**: Separate AI agents represent distinct stakeholders (company, competitors, channel partners, regulators, customers) instead of one model simulating every viewpoint.
- **[[Agent-Based Modeling]]**: The method that made the article's large-scale post-workshop simulations possible, with hundreds of agent stakeholder versions.
- **[[Red-Teaming]]**: The article's "often the greatest value comes from trying to break a strategy" framing of war gaming.
- **[[Human-in-the-Loop]]**: AI supports the process in real time but remains an assistive tool; the core dynamic stays with human teams.

## Key Takeaways
- **Stress-testing**: War games expose blind spots before strategy becomes commitment.
- **Pregame compression**: AI analyzes dozens of scenarios, so live time focuses on decisive moves.
- **Multiagent stakeholders**: Separate agents for competitors, regulators, and customers capture real friction.
- **Human judgment**: Participants supply the "why" (incentives, biases, blind spots) that AI cannot fully replicate.
- **Post-game testing**: AI tests live-session moves across wider scenarios and bundled combinations.
- **Always-on capability**: Revisit scenarios, update assumptions, and monitor signals as conditions change.
- **Hold on to the keys**: Organizations should not let AI control or own the exercise.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about AI and war gaming?
**A**: AI amplifies the human core of war gaming and acts as a force multiplier for leaders, not merely an efficiency engine for running exercises.

**Q2**: What scale did the global health care example reach, and what did it replace?
**A**: The company modeled more than 50 scenario permutations, ran more than 350 simulations, and tested more than 11,000 actions, using 400+ agent stakeholder versions versus four stakeholder groups in an earlier pre-AI workshop.

**Q3**: How should an organization apply this?
**A**: Treat war gaming as an ongoing capability: regularly revisit scenarios, update assumptions, and monitor signals such as competitor moves, regulatory developments, patent filings, and shifts in customer behavior.
