## Summary

[[Matt Mullenweg]], co-creator of [[WordPress]] and CEO of [[Automattic]], posted to company Slack claiming he had retaken control just days after the board voted to place him on paid leave and named CFO Mark Davies interim CEO. The chaotic reversal unfolded publicly through cryptic social media posts, a deactivated Slack account for Davies, and no official board confirmation at publication time.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Matt Mullenweg]]**: Automattic CEO and WordPress co-creator at the center of the leadership dispute
- **[[Automattic]]**: The company behind WordPress.com, WooCommerce, and other web publishing products
- **[[Corporate Governance]]**: Board authority vs. founder control — the board placed Mullenweg on leave; he claims to have reversed it
- **[[Silver Lake]]**: Private equity firm majority-owning [[WP Engine]], currently in active litigation with Automattic; Mullenweg alleged Silver Lake orchestrated a plan to "destroy" his life
- **[[WP Engine]]**: Competing managed WordPress host, in ongoing legal conflict with Automattic
- **Mark Davies**: CFO briefly named interim CEO during the board-imposed leave

## Key Takeaways

- **Board ouster**: Automattic board voted to put Mullenweg on paid leave mid-week.
- **Rapid reversal**: Two days later, Mullenweg posted to Slack claiming reinstatement.
- **Erratic signals**: He removed all Slack admins and made an unusual profanity-laced "pirate" post before the announcement.
- **No official confirmation**: Automattic had not confirmed the reversal at publication.
- **Davies deactivated**: CFO/interim CEO Mark Davies' Slack account was reportedly deactivated.
- **PE involvement alleged**: Mullenweg blamed Silver Lake for conspiring against him.
- **External support**: WordPress.org Executive Director Mary Hubbard expressed relief at his reported return.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Principal-Agent Problem]]**: Mullenweg's alleged end-run around the board — removing admins and announcing reinstatement unilaterally — illustrates the tension between a founder-principal and a governance board acting as the agent for broader shareholders.
- **[[Information Asymmetry]]**: Mullenweg's cryptic posts ("Things are different than they appear") and the absence of official board statements left employees, press, and investors without reliable information, amplifying uncertainty and speculation.

**Q3**: What does this episode reveal about founder-vs-board power dynamics at Automattic?
**A**: Mullenweg's apparent ability to reverse a board-mandated leave within days — whether through negotiation or unilateral action — suggests founders with operational control over company infrastructure can contest board authority even after a formal governance decision.
