## Summary

Large enterprises are scrutinizing AI ROI and shifting from expensive frontier models to cheaper open-weight models, driven by a lack of measurable returns at scale. IBM CEO Arvind Krishna highlighted that enterprises can run open-weight models at 10–25% of frontier model token costs using just two or three racks of on-premises infrastructure. AI token costs have fallen sharply overall, yet hyperscaler capex continues to rise, creating a divergence between infrastructure build-out and enterprise willingness to pay.

## Core Concepts

- [[Open-Weight Models]]: AI models with public core components; far cheaper per token than frontier models.
- [[Frontier Models]]: top-tier proprietary models like [[OpenAI]] GPT-6 and [[Anthropic]] Claude; premium-priced.
- [[AI ROI]]: return on investment for AI spend; enterprises now demanding measurable revenue, margin, or cost impact.
- [[Token Cost]]: price per token of compute; open-weight models run at 10–25% of frontier model costs.
- [[On-Premises AI Infrastructure]]: IBM's value proposition; enables IP protection and cost efficiency.
- [[Hyperscaler Capex]]: continued investment in AI data centers despite enterprise demand cooling for frontier models.
- [[Apollo Global]]: chief economist Torsten Sløk argues the key metric is price customers will pay for compute.
- [[Claude Haiku 5.5]]: Anthropic's lower-cost model targeting enterprise cost sensitivity.

## Key Takeaways

- **ROI scrutiny**: Large enterprises are auditing AI spend for real revenue, margin, or cost impact.
- **Cost gap**: Open-weight models cost 10–25% of frontier model token prices.
- **Infrastructure shift**: 2–3 racks of on-prem hardware sufficient for enterprise open-weight deployment.
- **Token deflation**: AI token costs have fallen sharply, pressuring frontier model pricing.
- **Hyperscaler disconnect**: Infrastructure capex keeps rising even as enterprise demand for premium compute softens.
- **Frontier response**: OpenAI launched mid-range GPT-6 variants; Anthropic launched Claude Haiku 5.5.
- **Willingness to pay**: The limiting factor is whether AI actually boosts enterprise revenue or margins.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Infrastructure builds ahead of demonstrated demand risk over-investment** — Hyperscalers are expanding compute capacity before enterprises have proven AI-driven ROI, creating a potential demand gap when willingness to pay has not been established. · *model:* [[Jevons Paradox]]
→ explains: Hyperscaler disconnect, ROI scrutiny

**T2. Cheaper substitutes commoditize the high-end offering** — As open-weight models reach acceptable quality thresholds, they commoditize frontier models for the majority of enterprise use cases, compressing frontier model pricing power. · *model:* [[Commoditization]]
→ explains: Cost gap, Token deflation, Frontier response

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central shift IBM CEO Arvind Krishna describes in enterprise AI?
**A**: Large enterprises are becoming more cognizant of AI ROI and increasingly worried that some AI spending is "completely out of control with no real return."

**Q2**: How cost-effective are open-weight models compared to frontier models for enterprises?
**A**: Open-weight models run at 10–25% of the token cost of frontier models, deployable on just two or three racks of on-premises infrastructure.

**Q3**: What implication does this shift have for frontier AI developers like OpenAI and Anthropic?
**A**: They are being forced to launch lower-cost mid-range models (GPT-6 mid-range, Claude Haiku 5.5) to retain enterprise customers unwilling to pay frontier prices without proven ROI.
