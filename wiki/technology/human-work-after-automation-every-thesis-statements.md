## Summary

[[Dan Shipper]] asks what human work will look like once intelligence becomes abundant and execution is automated, building on Every's prior arguments that AI enables individuals to do team-scale work and turns knowledge workers into "managers of models." To generate durable, revisitable answers, Every launched **Thesis Statements** — a project assembling specific, contestable predictions from 100 builders and thinkers with daily hands-on AI experience. The initiative pairs the predictions with a companion conference aimed at building "a world after automation that is not only more productive but more human."

## Core Concepts

- **[[Thesis Statements Initiative]]**: Every's project collecting specific, falsifiable predictions from 100 AI practitioners about the future of human work — designed to be publicly revisited to track which predictions hold, fail, or evolve over time.
- **[[Thesis 2027 Conference]]**: One-day companion conference on November 5, 2026, at [[Pioneer Works]] in Red Hook, Brooklyn. Application-based in-person attendance; free livestream available. Format includes talks, demos, working sessions, office hours, and small-group conversations. Speakers include Ivan Zhao ([[Notion]]), Andrew Ambrosino ([[OpenAI]] Codex), Cat de Jong ([[Anthropic]]), Nick Thompson (The Atlantic), Josh Miller ([[The Browser Company]]), Cristobal Valenzuela ([[Runway]]), and Sahil Lavingia ([[Gumroad]]).
- **[[Managers of Models]]**: Every's framing that [[Knowledge Workers]] in an AI era shift from direct execution to orchestrating and directing AI models — a role analogous to managing people but at machine scale.
- **[[Intelligence Abundance]]**: The condition where AI makes cognitive output cheap and plentiful, shifting scarcity from answers and execution to questions, taste, and judgment.
- **[[Computational Thinking]]**: Contributor Yash Tekriwal (Clay) predicts this skill will reshape jobs as workers must reason about systems, loops, and data pipelines rather than rote tasks.
- **[[Design as AI Bottleneck]]**: Contributor Karri Saarinen ([[Linear]] CEO) predicts design — taste, intent, aesthetic judgment — becomes AI's hardest constraint as execution becomes trivial.
- **[[Boring Infrastructure]]**: Contributor Tina He (Pace Capital) predicts reliable, unglamorous infrastructure will win as flashy AI applications commoditize.
- **[[Question Scarcity]]**: Contributor Anne-Laure Le Cunff ([[Ness Labs]]) predicts questions become the scarce resource while answers grow cheap — inverting the traditional knowledge economy.

## Key Takeaways

- **Execution Automation**: Once AI handles execution, human value shifts to questions, taste, and oversight.
- **Falsifiable Predictions**: Thesis Statements collects *specific, contestable* claims — not vague optimism.
- **Design Bottleneck**: Karri Saarinen: design judgment becomes AI's biggest constraint.
- **Self-Resolving Problems**: Chris Pedregal: some hard problems will simply disappear as AI changes the landscape.
- **Question > Answer**: Anne-Laure Le Cunff: formulating the right question matters more than finding answers.
- **Computational Thinking**: Yash Tekriwal: systems/pipeline reasoning will become a core job skill.
- **Track Record**: The project creates a public record — predictions will be revisited, not forgotten.
- **Thesis 2027 Conference**: One-day event Nov 5, 2026, Pioneer Works Brooklyn; application-based; free livestream.
- **Speaker Lineup**: Ivan Zhao (Notion), Andrew Ambrosino (OpenAI Codex), Cat de Jong (Anthropic), Cristobal Valenzuela (Runway), Sahil Lavingia (Gumroad), Josh Miller (The Browser Company).
- **Target Audience**: Builders and operators already using AI for ambitious work — living the future, not speculating about it.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Inversion]]**: Rather than asking "what will AI replace?" Shipper inverts to "what will humans do that AI cannot?" — reframing the problem from threat to design challenge.
- **[[Prediction Markets Logic]]**: By collecting specific, falsifiable predictions with named contributors, Thesis Statements creates accountability — the same mechanism that makes prediction markets informative rather than vacuous.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central question Dan Shipper poses in this piece?
**A**: When intelligence becomes abundant and execution gets automated, how will humans spend their working hours — what remains distinctively human about work?

**Q2**: What makes the Thesis Statements initiative different from typical AI predictions or opinion pieces?
**A**: It collects specific, contestable predictions from practitioners with daily AI experience and creates a public, revisitable record to track which predictions hold, fail, or evolve — building in accountability rather than letting predictions quietly disappear.

**Q3**: What implication does "question scarcity" have for how knowledge workers should develop their skills?
**A**: If answers grow cheap while questions grow scarce, knowledge workers should invest in developing taste, curiosity, and the ability to frame problems well — rather than deepening expertise in execution or information retrieval.
