## Summary

Oracle issued a force majeure notice to Blue Owl Capital, the developer of Project Jupiter — a 2.45-gigawatt [[Stargate]] AI data center campus in New Mexico — allowing Oracle to delay payments if the facility misses its 2028 launch target. The campus faces energy supply delays, including a six-month pipeline setback and a pending air-quality permit for Bloom Energy fuel cells. Despite the notice, both Oracle and Blue Owl maintain their financial commitments to the project.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Stargate Initiative]]**: A flagship [[AI]] infrastructure program announced by [[Oracle]], [[OpenAI]], and [[SoftBank]] alongside President Trump, targeting massive data center capacity.
- **Project Jupiter**: Oracle's primary Stargate campus in New Mexico, rated at 2.45 gigawatts — one of the largest AI data center builds underway.
- **[[Force Majeure]]**: A contractual clause invoked here to protect Oracle from payment penalties should external obstacles (energy permitting delays) push the 2028 launch deadline.
- **[[Blue Owl Capital]]**: The real-estate and infrastructure financier developing Project Jupiter as primary landlord to Oracle.
- **[[Energy Transfer]] Pipeline**: A gas pipeline critical to the campus's power supply, delayed roughly six months to February 2027 after repeated permit denials and route changes.
- **[[Bloom Energy]] Fuel Cells**: An alternative on-site power source whose air-quality permit faces a November 23 state deadline in New Mexico.

## Key Takeaways

- Oracle invoked force majeure to defer payments if 2028 launch target is missed.
- Both Oracle and Blue Owl insist the project remains on schedule and fully funded.
- Energy Transfer gas pipeline delayed ~6 months (target now Feb. 1, 2027) due to permit denials.
- Pipeline route was rerouted after regulatory rejections, adding further complexity.
- Bloom Energy fuel cell air-quality permit deadline is November 23, 2026.
- Project Jupiter faces opposition from residents and environmental groups near midterm elections.
- Oracle publicly stated: "Project Jupiter remains on our planned schedule."

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Regulatory Risk]]**: Large-scale infrastructure projects depend on permit chains where a single denial cascades into months-long delays — Project Jupiter illustrates how energy permitting bottlenecks can threaten AI buildout timelines even when capital commitments are solid.
- **[[Optionality]]**: Oracle's force majeure notice is a contractual hedge — preserving financial flexibility without exiting the deal — demonstrating the value of embedding optionality clauses in long-duration infrastructure contracts.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did Oracle issue a force majeure notice for Project Jupiter?
**A**: Oracle invoked force majeure to give itself the right to delay payments to Blue Owl Capital if the 2.45-gigawatt New Mexico campus misses its 2028 launch target due to energy-related setbacks, without exiting the project.

**Q2**: What specific energy supply obstacles is Project Jupiter facing?
**A**: The Energy Transfer gas pipeline has been delayed roughly six months to February 2027 after repeated permit denials and a route rerouting, and a pending air-quality permit for Bloom Energy fuel cells faces a November 23 state deadline.

**Q3**: What does Oracle's use of force majeure here signal for large-scale AI infrastructure investment?
**A**: It signals that even well-capitalized AI megaprojects backed by major players like Oracle, OpenAI, and SoftBank face significant regulatory and permitting risk that can delay timelines — and that building contractual hedges into long-duration infrastructure deals is prudent risk management.
