## Summary

Travis Kalanick has returned to the mobility sector via [[Atoms]], a holding company that raised $1.7 billion led by [[Andreessen Horowitz]] to expand industrial AI and physical automation — with Uber itself investing $100 million despite the turbulent history. Meanwhile, [[Tesla]]'s robotaxi program disclosed a troubling 36% quarter-over-quarter decline in paid miles and quietly walked back volume production promises for the Cybercab. The broader AV industry saw Aurora launch second-generation driverless trucks and [[Waymo]] report a 68% lower crash rate than human drivers, while internally discussing an exit from its Uber partnership.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Travis Kalanick]]** — Uber co-founder and former CEO returning via [[Atoms]], a rebranded holding company built atop his ghost kitchen project
- **[[Atoms]]** — Kalanick's new venture; raised $1.7B from [[Andreessen Horowitz]], Bain Capital, Fifth Wall, and [[Uber]]; acquiring [[Anthony Levandowski]]'s industrial automation startup [[Pronto]]
- **[[Pronto]]** — Industrial automation startup focused on OEM-agnostic autonomy for mining and transport; core strategic priority within Atoms
- **[[Tesla Robotaxi]]** — Fleet of Model Y SUVs operating as paid robotaxis; paid miles dropped ~36% Q1 to Q2 2026 despite cumulative charts appearing to show growth
- **[[Cybercab]]** — Tesla's purpose-built robotaxi; volume production postponed; requires dedicated mileage accumulation using retrofitted steering-wheel versions to calibrate chassis
- **[[Aurora]]** — Launched second-generation driverless trucks with million-mile service life on Dallas-to-Houston route; targeting 200 trucks by year end
- **[[Waymo]]** — Reports 68% lower crash rate than human drivers; internally discussing ending [[Uber]] partnership ahead of May 2028 contract expiration
- **[[Mobileye]]** — Founder [[Amnon Shashua]] stepping down after nearly three decades as company pivots into robotaxis and humanoid robots
- **[[Ford]]** — Partnering with [[Apple]] (MapKit for Automotive) for next-gen EV navigation, debuting in a $30,000 midsize truck in 2027

## Key Takeaways

- **Kalanick's Return**: Atoms raised $1.7B; focus on industrial AI and autonomous transport
- **Uber's Bet**: Uber invested $100M in Atoms — backing its own former CEO
- **Tesla Robotaxi Decline**: Paid miles fell 36% Q1→Q2 despite misleading cumulative charts
- **Cybercab Delay**: Requires chassis-specific data collection; volume production postponed
- **Tesla Financials**: CapEx doubled, back to negative free cash flow, net income down 5% YoY
- **Aurora Milestone**: Driverless trucks with no human observer running commercially
- **Waymo Safety**: 68% fewer crashes than human drivers in police-reportable incidents
- **Waymo-Uber Exit**: Internal discussions on ending partnership before 2028 expiration
- **Einride Acquires Flipturn**: All-stock EV charging acquisition worth $38M
- **IBM + HRL Labs**: IBM acquiring Boeing/GM joint quantum computing lab

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Tesla's cumulative chart for robotaxi miles obscures the underlying quarterly decline — when a cumulative metric becomes the public signal, it stops tracking actual momentum and can actively mislead.
- **[[Narrative Fallacy]]**: Kalanick's comeback narrative (disgraced founder redeemed by capital) may overshadow sober analysis of whether Atoms has a defensible edge over established industrial automation players.
