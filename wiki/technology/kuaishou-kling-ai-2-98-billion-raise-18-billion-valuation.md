## Summary

[[Kuaishou]] announced on October 30, 2026 that its AI video unit, Beijing Kling, closed a roughly $2.98 billion round at about $15 billion pre-money and $18 billion post-money. Kuaishou keeps two-thirds of the equity and 52.45% of the voting rights through Lucky Labs. The article reads the raise as a sign that generative video is capital-intensive and that platforms are spinning out AI units to attract specialised capital.

## Core Concepts

- [[Kuaishou]]: the listed short-video platform parent that retains control of the unit.
- [[Kling AI]]: Kuaishou's AI video generation unit, incorporated as Beijing Kling.
- [[Lucky Labs]]: the holding vehicle through which Kuaishou holds the equity and votes.
- [[Pre-Money and Post-Money Valuation]]: $15 billion before the raise and $18 billion after.
- [[Generative Video]]: a GPU-, data-, and safety-intensive segment, more compute-hungry than text models.
- [[Corporate Spin-Out Financing]]: carving out an AI division to raise outside capital while keeping control.
- [[OpenAI]] and [[Google]]: named as the rivals Kling must keep pace with.

## Key Takeaways

- **Round Size**: About 19.8 billion yuan, or $2.98 billion, announced October 30, 2026.
- **Valuation**: Roughly $15 billion pre-money and $18 billion post-money.
- **Control Split**: Kuaishou keeps two-thirds of equity but only 52.45% of votes.
- **Undisclosed**: Investor identities and use of proceeds were not confirmed in the reporting.
- **Capital Intensity**: Video models need heavy spending on GPUs, data licensing, and safety.
- **Isolated Spending**: A separate entity shields parent cash flow and reported financials from AI costs.
- **Competitive Pressure**: Independent AI video startups now face a well-funded, platform-backed rival.
- **Watch Next**: Investor identities, fund allocation, and any preferred rights or board seats.
