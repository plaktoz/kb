## Summary

Sony Music Publishing, Warner Chappell, and other major music publishers filed suit against [[Anthropic]] and co-founders [[Dario Amodei]] and [[Benjamin Mann]] in the Northern District of California, alleging a "brazen campaign" of illegally torrenting, scraping, and downloading copyrighted works to train [[Claude]]. The lawsuit follows a 2026 ruling in the *Bartz v. Anthropic* case that acquiring training data via piracy — even if using copyrighted works for training itself may be permissible — resulted in a $1.5 billion settlement. Anthropic stated it "disagrees with the publishers' claims" and intends to defend itself in court.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Anthropic]]** — frontier AI lab; defendant in multiple copyright actions over Claude's training data
- **[[AI Training Data Copyright]]** — legal question of whether acquiring copyrighted content via torrenting for AI training constitutes infringement
- **[[Bartz v. Anthropic]]** — 2026 case in which a judge ruled that piracy-based acquisition of training data is unlawful; settled for $1.5B
- **[[Sony Music Publishing]] / [[Warner Chappell]]** — major music publishers bringing the new suit; allege lyrics and sheet music were included in illegally obtained datasets
- **[[Fair Use Defense]]** — Anthropic's anticipated defense that training on third-party content is transformative and thus permissible
- **[[Concord Music Group]] / [[Universal Music Group]]** — additional plaintiffs in separate ongoing litigation against Anthropic

## Key Takeaways

- **New plaintiffs**: Sony Music Publishing, Warner Chappell, and other music publishers — not book authors.
- **Specific allegations**: Illegal torrenting of millions of books that contained lyrics and sheet music.
- **Personal liability**: Suit names co-founders Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann individually.
- **Prior precedent**: *Bartz v. Anthropic* (2026) established that piracy-based acquisition is not shielded by fair-use; settled for $1.5B.
- **Broader pattern**: Concord Music Group and UMG have separate suits still pending.
- **Anthropic's stance**: Denies claims and will "defend robustly" — no settlement signaled yet.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Precedent Effect]]**: The *Bartz* ruling — distinguishing between *using* copyrighted content (potentially fair use) and *acquiring* it via piracy (not protected) — creates a lower bar for future plaintiffs; each new lawsuit benefits from the established legal framework.
- **[[Liability Aggregation]]**: Individual suits over different content types (books, lyrics, sheet music) may be strategically coordinated so that cumulative settlements or judgments force behavioral change in how frontier labs source training data.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core legal claim Sony Music and Warner Chappell make against Anthropic?
**A**: That Anthropic engaged in a "brazen campaign" of illegally torrenting, scraping, and downloading copyrighted works — including lyrics and sheet music — to train Claude, constituting copyright infringement.

**Q2**: What legal precedent from earlier in 2026 strengthens the music publishers' case?
**A**: In *Bartz v. Anthropic*, a judge ruled that acquiring training data via piracy is not protected even if using copyrighted works for training might be; Anthropic settled that case for $1.5 billion.

**Q3**: What broader implication does this lawsuit have for frontier AI labs' data-acquisition practices?
**A**: It signals that the "fair use for training" defense does not extend to the method of acquisition — labs that torrented or scraped data without licensing face mounting legal exposure across multiple content industries.
