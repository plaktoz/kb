## Summary
Starting August 2, 2026, transparency obligations under Article 50 of the [[EU AI Act]] took effect, requiring AI systems to disclose when users are interacting with AI and mandating machine-readable labels on AI-generated content. The rules apply globally to any provider serving EU users, with fines of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover for noncompliance.

## Core Concepts
- **[[EU AI Act]]**: The EU's comprehensive AI regulatory framework; Article 50 specifically governs transparency obligations for chatbots, deepfakes, and AI-generated content.
- **AI disclosure requirements**: Chatbots and interactive AI systems must identify themselves as AI, not human.
- **Deepfake labeling**: AI-edited or AI-generated images, video, and audio must be labelled, with exceptions for content under human editorial control or clearly artistic/satirical work.
- **[[AI Governance]]**: The AI Office's voluntary Code of Practice on Transparency offers providers a recognized compliance path.

## Key Takeaways
- **Global reach**: Rules apply to any company serving EU users, regardless of where it's based.
- **Steep penalties**: Fines reach €15M or 3% of global turnover, whichever is higher.
- **Editorial carve-out**: Human-reviewed AI-assisted content with clear editorial responsibility is exempt from disclosure.
- **Compliance path exists**: A voluntary Code of Practice lets providers demonstrate good-faith compliance.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core requirement of the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency rules?
**A**: AI systems must disclose when users are interacting with AI rather than a human, and AI-generated content must carry machine-readable labels.

**Q2**: What is the penalty for noncompliance?
**A**: Fines of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

**Q3**: How might these rules affect companies outside the EU?
**A**: Any company whose AI systems serve EU users must comply, effectively making the EU AI Act a global compliance baseline similar to GDPR.
