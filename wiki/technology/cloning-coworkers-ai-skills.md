## Summary

Every CEO Dan Shipper is building AI "clones" of team members by packaging their expertise into self-improving skills that others can use without consuming the expert's calendar. The flagship example, KateBench, is a copyediting agent trained on 30,000+ historical edits from editor Kate Lee; tracked changes feed back into the system via Codex to continuously improve the skill. The article also touches on broader trends: open-weight models surging from 11% to 29% of Vercel AI Gateway tokens, and Walleye Capital mandating AI use across all 400 employees.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Skill Cloning]]**: Packaging an individual's domain expertise into an AI agent that teammates can invoke, reducing reliance on the expert's time.
- **[[KateBench]]**: A copyediting agent trained on 30,000+ edits from editor Kate Lee; runs before human review and self-improves via [[Codex]] by converting tracked changes into training data.
- **[[DanLens]]**: A planned AI clone of Dan Shipper's marketing instincts, enabling colleagues to apply his judgment without scheduling time with him.
- **[[Self-Improving Skills]]**: When an AI agent errs, the failure is documented, a self-improve routine is run, and the fix suggestion is applied — turning mistakes into training data in a tight feedback loop.
- **[[Open-Weight Models]]**: Frontier model [[Fable]] captures only ~6% of Anthropic business tokens one month post-launch; open-weight models jumped from 11% to 29% of Vercel AI Gateway tokens (April–June) at under 4% of spend.
- **[[Walleye Capital]]**: $10B hedge fund mandating AI use for all 400 employees; CEO stance: results matter, not effort alone.

## Key Takeaways

- **Cloning expertise**: Wrap domain knowledge into an AI agent so colleagues self-serve.
- **KateBench**: 30,000+ edits trained; tracked changes auto-feed back into the model.
- **Self-improvement loop**: Document AI errors → run self-improve skill → targeted fix.
- **Open-weight surge**: 11% → 29% of Vercel gateway tokens in two months, at <4% cost.
- **Fable underperforms**: Only 6% Anthropic business token share one month post-launch.
- **Walleye Capital**: All 400 employees mandated to use AI; output > effort as the metric.
- **AI authorship debate**: Druckenmiller used AI on a WSJ op-ed; WSJ allows it, FT bans it.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Leverage]]**: Encoding an expert's judgment into a reusable AI skill multiplies their impact without multiplying their hours — the same input produces more output across the team.
- **[[Flywheel Effect]]**: Each KateBench edit creates training data that improves the next edit, compounding quality improvements over time with no extra human effort per cycle.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central idea behind "cloning your coworkers"?
**A**: Packaging an individual's domain expertise into a self-improving AI skill so teammates can invoke it on demand, reducing the burden on the expert's calendar.

**Q2**: How does KateBench improve itself over time?
**A**: Tracked changes from editor Kate Lee's human reviews are fed back into the system via Codex, which rewrites the skill to incorporate those corrections as ongoing training data.

**Q3**: What does the open-weight model trend imply for AI strategy?
**A**: Open-weight models nearly tripled their share of Vercel AI Gateway traffic in two months at under 4% of spend, suggesting teams can achieve most knowledge-work gains at a fraction of frontier model costs.
