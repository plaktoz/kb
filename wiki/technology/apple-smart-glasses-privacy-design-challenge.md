## Summary

Apple is developing its first smart glasses, with launch delayed to WWDC 2027, partly to address the privacy controversies that dogged Meta's competing product. Apple's design reportedly prioritizes on-device processing, no facial recognition, and no use of customer recordings for AI training — a deliberate contrast to Meta's approach. The delay reflects how seriously Apple is treating the privacy constraint as a design requirement, not an afterthought.

## Core Concepts

- [[Apple Smart Glasses]] vs. [[Meta Smart Glasses]] as competing privacy design philosophies
- [[On-Device Processing]] as the key privacy-preserving architecture for wearable AI
- [[Non-Consensual Recording]] as the central failure mode Apple is designing against
- [[Privacy as Brand]] — Apple's marketing identity creates both constraints and competitive advantage

## Key Takeaways

- **Launch timeline**: WWDC June 2027, availability by end of 2027
- **Privacy pillars**: On-device processing, no facial recognition, no training on customer footage
- **Lessons from Meta**: Controversy over non-consensual recordings directly shaped Apple's design
- **Delayed for privacy**: The delay is attributed partly to working through privacy architecture, not just engineering
