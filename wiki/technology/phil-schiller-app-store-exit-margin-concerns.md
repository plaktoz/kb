## Summary

[[Phil Schiller]] is stepping down from leading the [[App Store]], with Bloomberg's [[Mark Gurman]] reporting that his departure was partly driven by wariness about the direction new [[Apple]] CEO [[John Ternus]] and services chief [[Eddy Cue]] intend to take the platform. Schiller had reservations about plans to boost App Store margins and generate more recurring revenue, fearing these moves would escalate tensions with developers and regulators. He will remain at Apple as a Fellow, working on unspecified projects.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Phil Schiller]]** — longtime Apple executive and App Store steward stepping down from that role
- **[[App Store]]** — Apple's primary platform for iOS app distribution, a major revenue source under the [[Services]] business
- **[[John Ternus]]** — Apple's new CEO, reportedly aligned with plans to increase App Store profitability
- **[[Eddy Cue]]** — Apple's services chief co-driving the margin-expansion strategy
- **[[Developer-Regulator Tension]]** — ongoing friction between Apple and developers / antitrust regulators over App Store fees and policies
- **[[Apple Fellow]]** — honorary role Schiller transitions into, allowing continued involvement without operational responsibility

## Key Takeaways

- Schiller's exit was driven partly by disagreement over future App Store profit strategy.
- New leadership ([[John Ternus]] + [[Eddy Cue]]) plans to boost margins and recurring revenue.
- Schiller feared profit push would worsen relationships with developers and regulators.
- No major internal conflict; Schiller chose to step back rather than oppose leadership.
- He remains at Apple as a Fellow on unspecified projects.
- Bloomberg's [[Mark Gurman]] broke the story behind the departure rationale.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Stakeholder Tradeoff]]**: Schiller's concern illustrates the classic tension between maximizing short-term margins and maintaining the ecosystem trust that sustains long-term platform health — optimizing for one degrades the other.
- **[[Principal-Agent Problem]]**: As Apple's services strategy shifts toward shareholder-friendly margin expansion, executives whose incentives are tied to ecosystem goodwill (developers, regulators) will inevitably misalign with new leadership priorities.
