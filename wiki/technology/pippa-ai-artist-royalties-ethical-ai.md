## Summary

AI video startup Pippa is testing whether paying artists directly — $0.005 per image, $0.003 per second of video, plus a 5% royalty pool — can convince illustrators to embrace generative AI instead of fighting it. But with only 800 subscribers and four signed artists, and models still trained partly on scraped internet art, Pippa's "ethical AI" positioning remains more aspiration than reality.

## Core Concepts

[[Generative AI]] video startups like [[Pippa]] represent an emerging category of "[[AI Ethics|ethical AI]]" companies attempting to resolve the [[Creator Economy|creator compensation]] problem that has fueled [[Artist Lawsuits|lawsuits against AI firms]] like Google, Meta, and Anthropic. Pippa's royalty model, explicitly compared to [[Spotify]]'s payout structure, highlights the tension between wanting to pay artists fairly and the economics of subscription-funded AI platforms. The company still relies on [[ByteDance]]'s Seedance 2.5 model, illustrating how even "ethical" AI companies remain dependent on the same underlying [[Foundation Models|foundation models]] built from broadly scraped training data.

## Key Takeaways

- **Direct compensation model**: Every Pippa-generated image or video derived from a signed artist's style pays that artist directly, funded by subscription revenue.
- **Small scale so far**: Only ~800 paying subscribers and 4 signed artists (with 4 more in talks) since a May 2026 launch.
- **Unresolved theft**: Pippa's underlying models still have "initial training on the broader set of content out there," meaning uncredited scraped art remains part of its foundation.
- **Anonymity option**: Artists can submit work under pseudonyms to avoid being seen as "crossing the picket line" by their creative communities.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Incentive Alignment]]**: Pippa's cofounders are betting that aligning artist and platform incentives around revenue-sharing — rather than one-time licensing or pure extraction — will produce a more sustainable and defensible AI business, echoing how the music industry's 99-cent song model displaced Napster-era piracy.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Pippa is testing whether directly paying artists a share of subscription revenue is enough to convince the creative community to embrace generative AI, but the approach faces real limits.

**Q2**: How does Pippa's payment structure work?
**A**: Artists receive $0.005 per AI-generated image and $0.003 per second of AI-generated video derived from their style, plus access to a 5% royalty pool funded by overall subscription revenue.

**Q3**: What is the central tension in Pippa's "ethical AI" positioning?
**A**: Despite paying artists directly, Pippa's underlying models are still built in part on art scraped from the internet without consent, meaning it hasn't fully escaped the practice it's trying to distance itself from.
