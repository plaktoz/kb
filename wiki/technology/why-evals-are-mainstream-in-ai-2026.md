## Summary

AI evaluations ("evals") have moved from niche engineering practice to a core product management skill, with nearly half of surveyed PM job listings now requiring eval experience. The shift reflects a maturing AI market where multiple models handle common tasks at comparable quality, making speed, cost, and task-specific fit the new differentiators. This issue also covers Grok 4.7's mixed launch, a short video production workflow using Claude, and emerging predictions about intelligence living "in your pocket."

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Evals]]**: Structured test suites that measure how well a model handles specific tasks — now a required skill in ~50% of PM job listings flagged by [[Lenny Rachitsky]].
- **[[Grok 4.7]]**: xAI's latest model, which launched to mixed reviews — early internal benchmarks showed a drop from 84% to 42% on one test, though performance improved before public release.
- **[[Model Commoditization]]**: As multiple models reach parity on common tasks, evals become the primary tool for differentiating on speed, cost, and personal fit rather than raw intelligence.
- **[[Short Video AI Workflow]]**: Every's social media lead uses [[Claude]] + [[Hyperframes]] + [[Fable]] to convert long-form articles into animated short videos in four steps: visualize → storyboard → animate → refine.
- **[[Conversational Platform Agents]]**: Willie Williams' thesis that Slack-native agents will follow the same adoption arc as websites — from optional to essential — making the @mention the new "front door" for businesses.
- **[[Daniela Rus]]**: MIT researcher predicting that intelligence will live "in your pocket, not a data center," pointing toward on-device AI as the long-term architecture.

## Key Takeaways

- **Evals now required in PMs**: ~50% of PM listings surveyed by Lenny Rachitsky explicitly require eval experience.
- **Volume exploding**: One company ran ~1,800 eval checks in a month, up from near zero in May 2026.
- **Grok 4.7 inconsistent**: Benchmark regression (84% → 42%) before release; improved post-launch but weak on multi-agent coordination.
- **Writing quality criticized**: Grok 4.7's writing described as having "big Elon Musk energy" — assertive, low on reader empathy.
- **Video workflow in 4 steps**: Visualize → storyboard → animate → refine using Claude + Hyperframes + Fable.
- **Detail as investment**: Geoffrey Litt argues that detailed work is "an investment" in compounding future ideas.
- **Agents as front doors**: Business @mentions in Slack agents predicted to become as essential as company websites.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Commoditization Trap]]**: When products reach feature parity, competition shifts to auxiliary factors — the eval surge is precisely this dynamic applied to LLMs, where raw capability no longer differentiates and measurement of fit becomes the edge.
- **[[Adoption S-Curve]]**: Willie Williams' prediction that conversational agents will follow the website adoption arc mirrors the classic S-curve — early optional use → mainstream standard → essential infrastructure.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why are AI evals becoming a required PM skill in 2026?
**A**: Multiple models can now handle common tasks at comparable quality, so evals are the primary tool for measuring speed, cost, and task-specific fit — the new competitive differentiators.

**Q2**: What was the key criticism of Grok 4.7's writing quality and its benchmark performance at launch?
**A**: Early internal tests showed a benchmark drop from 84% to 42%, and staff described its writing as having "big Elon Musk energy" — assertive with no empathy for readers.

**Q3**: How could a team use the short video AI workflow described in the article?
**A**: Convert existing long-form articles into animated short videos by passing them through Claude (visualize) → Hyperframes (storyboard) → Fable (animate) → manual refinement — a four-step pipeline requiring no video production expertise.
