## Summary
Aventide, a two-person bootstrapped startup in the Columbia River Gorge, opened to the public on Oct. 29 with AI software that replaces the separate booking, proposal, contract, portal and invoice apps used by consultants and coaches. Its founders say the real barrier to adoption is the time it takes to switch systems, not price or features, and that AI lets a team of two build what once took 100-200 people. Nothing the AI drafts reaches a client until the owner approves it.

## Core Concepts
- [[Aventide]] is an AI-native client management platform for people paid by the session, project or event, built by CEO [[Christian Quiver]] and chief design officer [[Eric Baxter]].
- [[Tool Fragmentation]] in small client businesses leaves the owner as "the human glue" holding separate apps together.
- [[AI-Native Software]] wires AI into bookings, contracts, portals and payments, rather than adding a chat box to legacy [[CRM]] software.
- [[Human-in-the-Loop]] review: Aventide drafts proposals, invoices and follow-ups, but the owner approves before anything is sent.
- [[Switching Costs]] drive adoption: customers lack time to change systems, so Aventide onboards each one personally.
- [[Bootstrapping]] with a two-person team: 81 users so far, a 14-day free trial, then $39 or $99 a month.

## Key Takeaways
- **Product Scope**: One login replaces booking, proposal, contract, portal and invoice apps across several businesses.
- **Adoption Barrier**: Customers lack time to switch systems; price and features are not the obstacle.
- **Onboarding**: Aventide onboards anyone who wants it one-on-one.
- **Build Speed**: The founders claim AI cuts idea-to-execution time by at least 50x.
- **Focus Decision**: A vague AI-era mission was narrowed to one product for client-services owners.
- **Traction**: 81 users, $39 or $99 monthly plans, and a 50,000-business goal.
- **Transparency**: The app publishes electricity and water estimates per task, possibly off by 2x.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Adoption friction outweighs product quality** — A product's biggest barrier can be the effort of changing what people already use, not its price or features. Reducing that effort, here through hands-on onboarding, matters as much as the product itself. · *model:* [[Switching Costs]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Aventide and who is it for?
**A**: Aventide is AI-native client management software for consultants, coaches and others who sell expertise one client at a time. It replaces separate booking, proposal, contract, portal and invoice apps with one login.

**Q2**: What did the founders learn was the real barrier to adoption?
**A**: The barrier was neither price nor features: people don't have time to switch systems, and many aren't comfortable with their own software stack. So Aventide onboards each customer one-on-one.

**Q3**: What does the founders' advice imply about how software should be designed now?
**A**: Quiver argues narrow single-purpose tools are "old hat" because nobody wants another tab to manage. He says the future belongs to software that serves as one tool for a whole category of a person's life, with AI built in rather than bolted on.
