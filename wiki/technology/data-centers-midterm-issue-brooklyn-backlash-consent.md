## Summary

At a Brooklyn midterm event hosted by Newsreel, AI and data centers dominated nearly every conversation, and a crowd asked who was pro-data center responded with boos. The author argues a growing chasm exists between the tech world and everyone else, with data centers becoming the physical embodiment of anger toward the tech class and a ballot-box issue that could sway some November 3 races. Panelists tied the backlash to lack of consent, secret local deals, cost-of-living strain, and temporary jobs.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Data Center]] backlash**: [[Data Center Watch]] found almost $200 billion in projects delayed, stalled or blocked in H1 2026 due to political and community opposition.
- **[[State Data Center Regulation]]**: Pennsylvania, Georgia, Texas, New York and Virginia are weighing more guardrails and transparency.
- **[[Community Consent]]**: Residents feel they lost control; secret deals with local officials and unclear zoning hide what is being built until too late to protest.
- **[[Labor Unions]] and jobs**: Some unions support data centers for jobs, but construction jobs are not permanent.
- **[[Chi Ossé]]**: Wants tech executives held more accountable and trillionaires like [[Elon Musk]] taxed more.
- **[[Jack Brewster]]**: Says backlash in rural areas is about feeling replaced; in Brooklyn it is about who gets rich.
- **AI art as cheapness signal**: Luxury brands such as Prada and Hermès are spotlighting handmade work.

## Key Takeaways

- **Blocked projects**: Almost $200 billion in data center projects stalled in H1 2026.
- **Election issue**: AI was the second most common topic in event RSVP questions.
- **Lack of consent**: Secret deals and unclear zoning fuel resentment, even among Republicans in affected places.
- **Temporary benefits**: Panelists say a "temporary check" does not outweigh health and environmental risks.
- **Two backlashes**: Rural fear of replacement versus urban anger over who profits.
- **Grassroots spread**: Yard signs showing dragons choking a data center now appear in multiple languages.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Concentrated local costs without consent breed opposition** — When the costs of a project fall on a local community that had no say, and the benefits are temporary or accrue to others, resistance grows regardless of aggregate gains. · *model:* [[Externalities]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why are data centers becoming a midterm election issue?
**A**: They have become the physical embodiment of anger toward the tech class and rapid technological change. Residents feel they lost control over their communities and worry about environmental and health risks.

**Q2**: What evidence shows how much opposition is costing the industry?
**A**: A Data Center Watch report found almost $200 billion in projects delayed, stalled or blocked in the first half of 2026 due to political and community opposition.

**Q3**: How do the rural and Brooklyn backlashes differ, per Jack Brewster?
**A**: In rural communities the backlash is about feeling replaced, while in a place like Brooklyn it is more about who gets rich from AI.
