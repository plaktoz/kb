## Summary
[[Nvidia]] is in early talks to acquire [[Reflection AI]], acquihire its team, or significantly raise its stake, per the [[Financial Times]] as summarized by [[Reuters]], signaling a push to own AI models and not just the chips they run on. Reflection, founded by ex-[[Google DeepMind]] researchers and recently launched its first open-weight model Beam, is positioned as an American answer to low-cost Chinese models like [[DeepSeek]] and [[Kimi]]. Nvidia can easily afford the deal, but its dominance in AI chips while investing in chip-buying customers makes market power the sensitive issue, and an [[Acquihire]] structure does not shield it from [[Antitrust]] scrutiny.

## Core Concepts
- **Strategic shift**: Nvidia moving from supplying chips to owning models, expanding a model business that already includes its open [[Nemotron]] models.
- **Three options**: Full acquisition, acquihire (hire staff and license technology without buying the company), or a larger investment.
- **Groq precedent**: In December 2025 Nvidia brought on the team of chip start-up [[Groq]] and licensed its technology, a deal [[CNBC]] valued at about $20 billion.
- **Existing stake**: Nvidia is among Reflection's biggest backers, having committed $800 million; Reflection has raised close to $2.6 billion from investors including [[Sequoia Capital]] and [[Lightspeed Venture Partners]].
- **Valuation**: CEO [[Misha Laskin]] said in April that Reflection was raising at a $25 billion pre-money valuation.
- **Founders**: Misha Laskin and [[Ioannis Antonoglou]] founded Reflection in 2024.
- **Beam**: Reflection's first [[Open-Weight Models|open-weight model]], built for coding and [[AI Agents]].
- **Open-weight competition**: [[Mistral AI]] unveiled Large 4, a one-trillion-parameter model with weights set for release, and raised $3.5 billion in Q3, the largest European venture round ever.
- **Financial capacity**: Nvidia generated about $127 billion in free cash flow over the past four quarters.
- **Market power concern**: Nvidia dominates AI chips while investing in a growing number of companies that buy those chips; per [[Yahoo Finance]], an acquihire may avoid classic merger review but not antitrust scrutiny.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What strategic shift does the potential Reflection AI deal signal for Nvidia?
**A**: Nvidia no longer wants only to build the chips AI models run on; it wants to own the models too, expanding a model business that already includes its open Nemotron models.

**Q2**: How would an acquihire work, and what precedent exists?
**A**: Nvidia would hire Reflection's employees and license its technology without buying the company, potentially avoiding lengthy regulatory review, as it did with Groq in December 2025 in a deal CNBC valued at about $20 billion.

**Q3**: Why is market power, not cost, the sensitive issue for this deal?
**A**: Nvidia can easily afford it with about $127 billion in free cash flow, but it dominates AI chips while investing in chip-buying companies, and an acquihire does not shield the deal from antitrust scrutiny.
