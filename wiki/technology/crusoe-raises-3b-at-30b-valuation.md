## Summary

Crusoe, an AI infrastructure and hyperscale data center company, has reportedly raised $3 billion at a $30 billion valuation in a round co-led by Atreides Management and Valor Equity Partners. The raise — a 3x valuation jump from its $10 billion Series E just ten months prior — was catalyzed by a five-year, $13 billion GPU cloud contract with quantitative trading firm Jane Street. Founded in 2018 as a flared natural gas crypto miner, Crusoe has pivoted to become a major AI compute provider serving Meta, Microsoft, and OpenAI.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Crusoe Energy]]** — AI infrastructure company providing GPU and hyperscale data center services; evolved from crypto mining origins
- **[[AI Infrastructure]]** — physical compute layer (GPUs, data centers, cloud) underpinning large-scale AI model training and inference
- **[[Hyperscale Data Centers]]** — large-scale facilities built for cloud and AI workloads, central to Crusoe's current business
- **[[Jane Street]]** — quantitative trading firm; anchor customer with a $13B, five-year cloud contract for GPU and AI infrastructure
- **[[Mubadala Capital]]** — Abu Dhabi sovereign wealth fund subsidiary participating in the round; signals sovereign capital flowing into AI infrastructure
- **[[Venture Capital]]** — Atreides Management and Valor Equity Partners co-led the $3B round
- **[[IPO]]** — Crusoe has reportedly held discussions with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley about a potential public offering

## Key Takeaways

- **$3B raise**: Round closed at $30B valuation, up 3x from Series E ten months prior.
- **$13B anchor contract**: Five-year GPU cloud deal with Jane Street was a key catalyst.
- **Key clients**: Meta, Microsoft, and OpenAI are existing customers.
- **Origin pivot**: Founded 2018 as flared gas crypto miner → now AI data center provider.
- **Sovereign participation**: Abu Dhabi's Mubadala Capital joined the round.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Pivot as Strategy]]**: The company's evolution from flared gas crypto mining to AI infrastructure illustrates how sunk-cost assets (energy infrastructure, operations) can be redeployed into higher-value markets when demand shifts.
