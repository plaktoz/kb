## Summary
Two LA-area startups, [[Hadrian]] and [[Valar Atomics]], raised a combined $2.37B this week to build the physical infrastructure AI needs — automated defense/aerospace manufacturing and factory-built nuclear reactors, respectively. Both rounds signal that venture capital is shifting from funding the software layer toward funding the underlying physical capacity — factories, energy, and supply chains — that AI cannot create on its own. The scale of the checks suggests "reindustrialization" has moved from a buzzword to a mainstream billion-dollar investment thesis.

## Core Concepts
- [[Hadrian]]: Torrance-based automated factory company for defense/aerospace, raised $1.37B Series D at a $7.87B valuation; customers include Lockheed Martin, RTX, and Anduril.
- [[Valar Atomics]]: El Segundo nuclear startup building standardized, factory-built reactors; closed a $1B Series B led by Sequoia Capital plus a $200M credit facility at a roughly $6B valuation.
- [[Reindustrialization]]: Investment thesis that the next tech wave requires physical manufacturing and energy capacity, not just software.
- [[Nuclear Energy]]: Valar's Ward 250 reactor achieved a self-sustaining reaction and powered an Nvidia Blackwell system in June.

## Key Takeaways
- **Combined Raise**: $2.37B in equity across two LA startups in a single week.
- **Hadrian**: $1.37B Series D, $7.87B valuation, up from a $260M round just over a year ago.
- **Valar Atomics**: $1B Series B (Sequoia-led) plus a $200M credit facility, ~$6B valuation.
- **Reactor Milestone**: Valar's Ward 250 reactor went self-sustaining in June and powered an Nvidia Blackwell system a week later.
- **Underlying Thesis**: AI can design and automate but "cannot manufacture a missile component or generate a megawatt" on its own.
- **Capital Shift**: The biggest venture bets are moving from the software layer to the physical infrastructure underneath it.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core thesis connecting these two large funding rounds?
**A**: AI's growth is bottlenecked by physical constraints — manufacturing capacity and electricity — so venture capital is now flowing into companies that build the industrial infrastructure underneath the software layer.

**Q2**: What specific technical milestone did Valar Atomics reach before its raise?
**A**: In June, Valar's Ward 250 reactor achieved a self-sustaining nuclear reaction, and a week later it demonstrated generating electricity to power an Nvidia Blackwell system.

**Q3**: What does this funding pattern imply for where the next wave of billion-dollar startups will come from?
**A**: Some of the largest future venture bets will target physical infrastructure — factories, energy systems, and supply chains — rather than purely software products, as "reindustrialization" becomes a mainstream investment thesis.
