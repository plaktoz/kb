## Summary
Some news outlets are now treating AI agents, not human readers, as the intended audience for their content. Axios is launching structured feeds for agents, while Every embeds prompts and queryable databases so subscribers' agents can tailor stories. Whether publishers can monetize this remains unproven and uneven.

## Core Concepts
- [[Axios Direct]]: RSS-like, structured-data feeds for AI agents, starting with corporate clients (M&A content for Axios Pro Deals subscribers, in-house LLM training) and a consumer feed planned for next year.
- [[Every]] and [[Dan Shipper]]: articles with copy-paste prompts, "Read With Claude" buttons, and "extendable" articles backed by a reporting database that agents can query to build custom articles, podcasts, or videos.
- [[Generative Engine Optimization]] (GEO) and [[LLM.txt]]: strategies and files that help AI discover and surface publisher content.
- Publisher stances: licensing deals (Condé Nast) versus litigation ([[The New York Times]] v. [[OpenAI]] and [[Microsoft]]).
- [[Rasmus Kleis Nielsen]]: calls the market for publishing to agents "emerging," immature, and "extremely uneven."
- [[Jacquelyn Cameron]] and [[Allison Murphy]] of [[Axios]]: frame AI as a tool for reaching the audience, not the audience itself.

## Key Takeaways
- **Agent Audience**: Outlets design feeds and articles for AI agents rather than human eyes.
- **Axios Strategy**: Short, structured content already favored by crawlers; Direct feeds cut narrative.
- **Every Strategy**: Prompts, agent buttons, and databases let agents tailor each reader's version.
- **Business-First Rollout**: Corporate budgets tolerate unproven experiments better than individual subscribers do.
- **Pricing Signal**: Axios Pro Deals costs $2,499 a year; an Every subscription costs $288.
- **Monetization Doubt**: Chatbots answer on a silver platter; only unique information earns payment.
- **Brand Visibility**: Agent crawls may help the brand even without homepage visits.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Content adapts to the form its consumer can use best** — When a new intermediary becomes the main reader, producers restructure content for that intermediary's needs. Axios's "be where the audience is" and "time is money for crawlers" reasoning is this principle at work.
→ explains: Agent Audience, Axios Strategy, Every Strategy

**T2. Willingness to pay tracks scarcity of the information** — People pay only for information that competing publications and LLMs do not already provide.
→ explains: Monetization Doubt, Pricing Signal

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What shift in media does the article describe?
**A**: Some outlets are producing content for AI agents as the intended audience, through feeds optimized for AI or pieces structured so agents can build personalized articles.

**Q2**: How do Axios and Every differ in serving agents?
**A**: Axios trims already-short content into structured, RSS-like Direct feeds for agents. Every adds prompts, agent-launch buttons, and a reporting database so subscribers' agents can generate tailored articles, podcasts, or videos.

**Q3**: Why is it unclear whether serving agents will make money, and what does the article suggest publishers sell?
**A**: Chatbots can supply answers for free, so people only pay for information others lack, such as Every's in-the-weeds AI guidance or Axios Pro Deals' market-moving speed. Brand visibility inside AI answers may also have value, as with social platforms.
