## Summary

[[Uber]] invested $100 million into [[Travis Kalanick]]'s holding company [[Atoms]], which is pushing into industrial AI and physical automation via its [[Pronto]] acquisition, nearly a decade after Kalanick's scandal-driven resignation as Uber CEO. Separately, [[Tesla]]'s shareholder letter revealed its paid [[Robotaxi]] fleet covered 36% fewer miles quarter-over-quarter, and the company disclosed it must accumulate new driving data specific to the [[Cybercab]] before wide deployment. Other notable deals: [[IBM]] agreed to buy quantum lab [[HRL Laboratories]], and the [[Insurance Institute for Highway Safety]] found [[Waymo]]'s robotaxis crash 68% less often than human drivers.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Atoms]]** — Travis Kalanick's holding company, raised $1.7B led by [[Andreessen Horowitz]], with Uber participating as an investor
- **[[Pronto]]** — industrial automation startup (acquired from Anthony Levandowski) now core to Atoms' strategy
- **[[Tesla Robotaxi]]** — paid miles fell from ~1.1M in Q1 to ~700K in Q2 2026, despite cumulative charts suggesting steady growth
- **[[Cybercab]]** — requires retrofitted data-collection vehicles before scaling; existing customer-fleet data doesn't directly transfer
- **[[Waymo]]** — IIHS study found a 68% lower crash rate than human drivers when including police-reportable crashes; reportedly weighing ending its Uber partnership

## Key Takeaways

- **Kalanick's return**: Uber invested $100M in its former CEO's new venture, six months before public disclosure.
- **Robotaxi mile decline**: Tesla's paid robotaxi miles dropped 36% quarter-over-quarter, obscured by cumulative charting.
- **Cybercab data gap**: Tesla's 10M-car fleet data doesn't calibrate directly to the new Cybercab chassis.
- **IBM quantum move**: IBM agreed to buy HRL Laboratories, jointly owned by Boeing and GM.
- **Waymo safety edge**: 68% lower crash rate than human drivers in IIHS study, though data collection methods need improvement.
