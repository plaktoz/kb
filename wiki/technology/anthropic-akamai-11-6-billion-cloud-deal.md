## Summary

[[Anthropic]] has committed $11.6 billion over seven years to [[Akamai]] for cloud infrastructure — over six times a previously reported $1.8 billion arrangement — with either party able to exit if conditions aren't met. The deal, Akamai's largest ever, centers on CPUs rather than GPUs, reflecting growing demand from AI agents. Akamai projects roughly $1.7 billion in annual revenue from the deal by end of 2028, funded by ~$5.5 billion in planned capacity buildout.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Anthropic]]**: The AI safety company behind Claude, aggressively locking in multi-year compute deals across multiple cloud providers to secure infrastructure for its AI workloads.
- **[[Akamai]]**: A cloud and CDN provider; this deal is its largest ever and reflects a pivot toward AI infrastructure, focused on CPU-based compute suited to AI agent workloads.
- **[[CPU vs GPU Compute]]**: The deal's emphasis on CPUs rather than GPUs signals a shift — AI agents increasingly rely on CPU-intensive tasks (orchestration, inference routing, memory) rather than pure GPU training.
- **[[Conditional Capacity Deal]]**: The $11.6B commitment is conditional on Akamai meeting delivery and service requirements, and can scale to ~$20B if Anthropic increases spend. Revenue recognition begins only in 2H 2027.
- **[[Customer Equity Warrant]]**: Akamai issued Anthropic a warrant for nonvoting preferred stock convertible to ~7.7 million common shares (~5% of outstanding) at $111.33/share, vesting proportionally with Anthropic's spending milestones.

## Key Takeaways

- **Deal scale**: $11.6B over 7 years; could grow to ~$20B with additional Anthropic spending.
- **Revenue ramp**: $0 in 2026; $150–300M in 2H 2027; ~$1.7B annually by end of 2028.
- **Capacity investment**: Akamai spending ~$5.5B on buildout plus $1.7B in accelerated component purchases.
- **CPU-centric**: Unusual focus on CPU capacity, not GPUs — shaped by AI agent compute patterns.
- **Inverted equity deal**: Customer (Anthropic) receives equity in the supplier (Akamai) — a reversal of the typical circular AI deal structure.
- **AMD–OpenAI precedent**: Structure mirrors a 2025 arrangement between [[AMD]] and [[OpenAI]].
- **Market reaction**: Akamai shares rose as much as 17% after-hours on the announcement.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Supplier Lock-in via Equity]]**: By granting Anthropic an equity stake that grows with spending, Akamai aligns incentives and makes it economically painful for Anthropic to walk away — turning a procurement relationship into a semi-permanent partnership with shared upside.
- **[[Optionality]]**: The conditional, milestone-based structure preserves exit rights for both parties while anchoring Anthropic's infrastructure roadmap; both sides get optionality without losing the commitment benefit of a long-term deal.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core structure of Anthropic's deal with Akamai?
**A**: Anthropic committed $11.6 billion over seven years for cloud infrastructure, conditional on Akamai meeting service requirements, with the deal potentially growing to ~$20 billion based on Anthropic's spending.

**Q2**: Why does the deal focus on CPUs rather than GPUs, and what does this signal?
**A**: The CPU focus reflects the compute patterns of AI agents, which rely heavily on orchestration and inference routing rather than the GPU-intensive training workloads that have dominated earlier AI infrastructure buildouts.

**Q3**: How does the equity warrant structure invert the typical "circular AI deal," and what precedent does it follow?
**A**: Rather than the supplier investing in the AI lab, the supplier (Akamai) grants its customer (Anthropic) a growing equity stake tied to spending milestones — mirroring a similar structure AMD used with OpenAI in 2025.
