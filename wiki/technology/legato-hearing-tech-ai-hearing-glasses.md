## Summary

Hearing tech startup [[Legato]] emerged from stealth with $12 million in funding and its first AI-powered hearing glasses, "Legato Frames," which embed hearing-assistance technology into eyewear arms. The product targets the roughly 50 million US adults with hearing loss, most of whom never seek treatment, using AI to isolate voices from background noise.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Legato Frames]]**: AI hearing glasses combining a proprietary acoustic system, signal-processing stack, and dual-speaker design that reduces leaked sound by 99%.
- **[[AI Noise Differentiation]]**: Unlike traditional directional-microphone hearing aids that struggle in noisy settings, Legato uses AI to distinguish background noise from voices and amplify only speech.
- **[[Founder Background]]**: Co-founders [[Mehul Trivedi]] (former Bose Frames lead, later at [[EssilorLuxottica]] on Meta Ray-Bans) and [[Steve Romine]] (former Bose hearing aids division head, later COO of [[Audicus]]) bring combined wearables and hearing-care expertise.
- **[[Treatment Gap]]**: Only ~20% of US adults with diagnosed hearing loss seek treatment, the underlying market gap Legato targets.

## Key Takeaways

- Legato raised $12M and unveiled AI hearing glasses ahead of a fall launch.
- Product targets mild-to-moderate hearing loss, the largest affected segment.
- AI isolates voices from noise instead of relying on directional mics alone.
- Open-ear dual-speaker design cuts leaked sound by 99% near the ear.
- Glasses will cost less than traditional hearing aids and may qualify for vision insurance.
