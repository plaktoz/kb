## Summary

HBR field research comparing Perplexity's conversational assistant to its autonomous agent finds that AI agents don't just answer questions faster — they change what knowledge work actually is, by delivering finished outputs (spreadsheets, charts, memos) instead of raw information a human must still assemble.

## Core Concepts

The study frames a distinction between AI as an information-retrieval aid versus AI as a [[Agentic AI]] collaborator that completes [[Knowledge Work]] end-to-end. This maps onto broader industry discussion of the shift from chat-based LLM interaction to [[AI Agents]] that autonomously execute multi-step tasks.

## Key Takeaways

- **Chatbot vs. agent gap**: A chatbot returns links and summaries; an agent returns completed deliverables.
- **Time tradeoff**: The agent took roughly 20+ minutes versus seconds for the chatbot, but skipped the manual assembly work.
- **Nature, not just speed**: The research frames this as agents changing the nature of knowledge work, not merely accelerating it.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this research?
**A**: AI agents change the nature of knowledge work by delivering completed outputs rather than just information to act on.

**Q2**: What comparison illustrates this finding?
**A**: Asking the same competitive-landscape question to a chatbot versus an agent — the chatbot returns a summary and links in seconds, while the agent returns a built spreadsheet, chart, and draft memo after about 20 minutes.

**Q3**: What's the practical implication for knowledge workers?
**A**: Workers may increasingly shift from manually assembling AI-provided information to reviewing and refining AI-completed deliverables.
