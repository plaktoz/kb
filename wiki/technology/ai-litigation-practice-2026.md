## Summary
Massachusetts litigators are adopting AI for document review, brief drafting, legal research, and deposition summaries — but are treating tools as starting points, not finished products. The shift is raising the bar for incoming lawyers, who must now arrive with interpersonal and analytical skills that AI cannot replicate. Fabricated AI evidence is already appearing in court, and professional responsibility obligations now require attorneys to understand AI limitations.

## Core Concepts

- [[Legal AI Tools]] — Lexis Protégé, CoCounsel, Harvey, EvenUp; trained on legal material, designed for data privacy
- [[Document Review AI]] — automated document classification and relevance scoring; cuts review time from hours to seconds
- [[AI-Fabricated Evidence]] — AI-generated documents, photos, or video that may be submitted as authentic; authentication is now a new litigation risk
- [[Billable Hour]] — traditional legal billing model under pressure as AI compresses time for routine work
- [[Professional Responsibility (Legal)]] — duty of competence now includes understanding AI limitations and verifying AI output

## Key Takeaways

- **Where AI excels**: Document review, legal research, first-draft pleadings, deposition summaries
- **Where AI fails**: Grasping case precedent in context; will "tell you what it thinks you want to hear"
- **New risk**: AI-fabricated evidence is appearing — lawyers must authenticate both their own and opposing exhibits
- **Billing disruption**: Clients refuse to pay for work AI does in seconds; flat fees replacing hourly billing in some practices
- **New hire priorities**: Interpersonal skills (client management, settlement judgment) and analytical skills (spotting argument weaknesses) — must arrive on day one, not developed over time
- **Cost range**: General-purpose AI assistant to $1,200/seat for enterprise legal AI systems

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Comparative Advantage]]**: AI handles the time-intensive but structurally routine work (doc review, research), leaving lawyers to specialize in judgment, relationship, and advocacy work — the tasks with highest marginal value that AI cannot replicate.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Clients optimizing for AI-generated briefs (the measurable output) will get clean prose but may lose the irreplaceable strategic judgment — optimizing for the artifact, not the outcome.
