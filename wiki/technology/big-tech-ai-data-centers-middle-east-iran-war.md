## Summary

Big Tech's rapid expansion of AI data centers into the Middle East — drawn by cheap energy, open land, and sovereign wealth funding — has collided with the Iran war, which has damaged AWS facilities in Bahrain and the UAE and an Oracle-owned data center in Dubai. Six months after the strikes, AWS still cannot fully restore one affected UAE service zone, and the industry is now redesigning flagship projects like Stargate UAE toward decentralized, hardened sites instead of single mega-campuses. Despite the disruption, companies including Microsoft are reaffirming multibillion-dollar regional investment plans, treating the war as a "pause, not cancel" on the region's AI buildout.

## Core Concepts

- [[Middle East AI Data Center Buildout]] — regional expansion by [[Amazon]], [[Google]], [[Microsoft]], and [[OpenAI]] chasing energy access and sovereign wealth capital
- [[Stargate UAE]] — originally planned as one 5-gigawatt, 10-square-mile Abu Dhabi campus, now being redesigned as distributed, hardened sites
- [[AWS]] — Amazon's cloud unit; its Bahrain and UAE data centers were struck by Iranian drones or nearby strikes, causing prolonged outages
- [[Geopolitical Risk]] — armed conflict exposure now facing physical tech infrastructure that was previously mostly a cyber-threat concern
- [[Uptime Guarantees]] — contractual reliability commitments (99.9%+) that make physical outages financially costly for cloud providers
- [[Iran War]] — conflict whose strikes on Gulf infrastructure forced a rethink of AI data center siting and hardening

## Key Takeaways

- **First known case**: AWS Bahrain/UAE strikes are believed to be the first US data centers disrupted by military conflict.
- **Ongoing outage**: Six months later, one AWS UAE service zone remains unable to restore full access.
- **Small but fast-growing footprint**: Middle East holds ~3% of global data center capacity but is set to quadruple by 2030.
- **Design pivot**: Stargate UAE is shifting from one central campus to decentralized, potentially underground or mountain-built sites.
- **Investment persists**: Microsoft reaffirmed $15B+ UAE AI spending plans through 2029 despite the war.
- **Risk was underpriced**: Industry prioritized expansion speed over physical protection, per SCSP's Chip Usher.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Single Point of Failure]]**: The redesign of Stargate UAE from one massive campus into scattered, hardened sites is a direct application of eliminating concentration risk — a single large target becomes many smaller, harder-to-disable ones.
