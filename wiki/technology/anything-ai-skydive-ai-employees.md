## Summary

San Francisco startup Anything AI, maker of a $100M-valued vibe-coding platform, launched Skydive, a product that lets companies build custom AI "teammates" — always-on agents with their own Slack, email, and phone presence — by simply describing the role needed. The company already runs its own internal operations partly through named AI agents handling engineering, brand, and scheduling work.

## Core Concepts

Skydive extends the logic of [[AI Agents]] from task automation to full role replacement, treating hiring as a natural-language specification problem rather than a recruiting process. This reflects a broader shift toward [[Agentic AI]] as organizational infrastructure — agents integrated into the same communication channels (Slack, email, phone) used by human [[Coworkers]], rather than siloed tools.

## Key Takeaways

- **Role-as-prompt**: Users describe a job in natural language to spin up a dedicated AI teammate.
- **Full channel integration**: Skydive agents get Slack, email, web, and phone/iMessage presence.
- **Always-on labor**: Agents work continuously, including while human employees sleep.
- **Real internal usage**: Anything AI already runs bug triage, ops forecasting, and brand work via named agents.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Anything AI's new Skydive product lets companies "hire" AI agents as full teammates rather than using AI as a background tool.

**Q2**: What specific mechanism makes Skydive different from a typical AI tool?
**A**: Agents are integrated directly into normal communication channels (Slack, email, phone, iMessage) and can be described into existence via natural language or templates, functioning like always-on employees.

**Q3**: What's the broader implication for how companies staff up?
**A**: It signals a shift where scaling a team may increasingly mean deploying more agents rather than hiring more people, especially for well-defined operational roles.
