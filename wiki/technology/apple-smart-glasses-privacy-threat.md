## Summary

Apple is developing its first smart glasses, with a launch pushed to WWDC June 2027 and availability by year's end. The delay is partly informed by controversy around [[Meta]] smart glasses being used for non-consensual video recordings. Apple's approach reportedly prioritizes on-device processing and strict data practices to uphold its privacy brand.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Apple Smart Glasses]]**: Apple's first wearable glasses product, targeting a 2027 reveal and year-end availability.
- **[[Privacy-by-Design]]**: Apple's planned architecture avoids facial recognition, on-device processing, and prohibits using customer recordings for AI training.
- **[[Meta Ray-Ban Smart Glasses]]**: Competitor product that drew backlash for enabling non-consensual surveillance, serving as a cautionary example for Apple.
- **[[On-Device Processing]]**: Processing data locally on the device rather than sending it to the cloud — a key privacy safeguard in Apple's approach.
- **[[Mark Gurman]] (Bloomberg)**: The journalist who reported Apple's smart glasses plans and privacy strategy details.

## Key Takeaways

- **Launch Timeline**: Apple smart glasses reveal at WWDC June 2027; availability by year-end.
- **Meta Lessons**: Meta's glasses sparked controversy over non-consensual video recording use cases.
- **No Facial Recognition**: Apple reportedly ruling out facial recognition entirely.
- **No Cloud Training**: Customer footage will not be used to train AI models.
- **No Contractor Review**: Distancing from Meta's practice of using contractors to review footage.
- **Privacy Brand Risk**: Apple's privacy marketing makes any misstep especially costly.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Competitive Differentiation via Constraints]]**: By treating privacy as a hard constraint rather than a feature toggle, Apple turns a competitor's liability into a brand moat — the same first-principles logic that drove end-to-end encryption in iMessage.
- **[[Reputational Asymmetry]]**: A company that builds its brand on privacy faces disproportionately higher damage from privacy failures than a competitor without that positioning — Apple must clear a higher bar than Meta precisely because it set one.
