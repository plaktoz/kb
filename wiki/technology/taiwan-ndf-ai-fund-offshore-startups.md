## Summary

Taiwan's [[National Development Fund]] (NDF) is changing its rules so its AI fund, which the headline puts at $314M, can invest in offshore AI companies whose main business is still in Taiwan. Officials found that many Taiwanese startups were raising money overseas and setting up offshore entities, which made them ineligible for a scheme that originally backed only Taiwanese companies. The change shows Taiwan using state capital to keep homegrown AI talent close to home while its startups chase global markets.

## Core Concepts

- **[[National Development Fund]] (NDF)**: In 2024 it earmarked US$289.5M for the [[Ministry of Digital Affairs]] to invest in AI startups and the digital economy over 10 years.
- **Fund structure**: The program launched last year with a seven-year window for investing and a three-year window for divesting.
- **Offshore eligibility test**: Offshore entities qualify only if their main business stays in Taiwan, measured by R&D, headcount and revenue thresholds.
- **[[Co-Investment]] model**: The NDF co-funds startups alongside venture capital firms, accelerators and corporate investors to help them scale abroad.
- **[[Lin Jiunn-shiow]]**: Director-General of the Administration for Digital Industries, who called the change "a demonstration of Taiwan's power in AI-related industries."
- **[[Turing Drive]]**: A portfolio company running [[Autonomous Vehicles]] demos on Japan's Miyakojima and Uwajima islands, with [[Tier IV]] as an investor.
- **[[Linker Vision]]**: A portfolio company that joined [[AT&T]], [[Cisco]] and [[Nvidia]] in building AI grid infrastructure.

## Key Takeaways

- **Rule change**: NDF can now invest in offshore AI firms headquartered operationally in Taiwan.
- **Eligibility thresholds**: Taiwan R&D HQ, >50% R&D budget, >50% employees, 50% revenue.
- **Deployment so far**: 10 AI startups funded by August, with US$7.38M from NDF.
- **Private leverage**: The private sector added US$16.5M alongside NDF capital.
- **2026 pace**: About US$5.62M went into seven startups from January to August.
- **Portfolio expansion**: Turing Drive plans to expand into Nagoya and Singapore with Tier IV.
- **Strategic bet**: Funding offshore structures still counts as investing in Taiwan.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What change is Taiwan's National Development Fund making to its AI investment program, and why?
**A**: It is changing its rules so it can invest in offshore AI companies whose main business is still in Taiwan. Officials found that many Taiwanese startups were raising money overseas and setting up offshore entities, which the original Taiwan-only scheme could not back.

**Q2**: What conditions must an offshore entity meet to qualify for NDF investment?
**A**: It must keep its main business in Taiwan. That means an R&D headquarters there, more than 50% of its R&D budget and employees there, and 50% of revenue tied to Taiwan-based offices.

**Q3**: What does this policy suggest about how governments can keep startup talent at home when startups move capital abroad?
**A**: Taiwan is tying eligibility to where operations, R&D and staff actually sit, not where the company is legally incorporated. That lets state capital follow startups into global financing structures while keeping the economic activity in Taiwan.
