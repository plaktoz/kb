## Summary

Newly unsealed court filings in the NYT v. OpenAI/Microsoft copyright lawsuit reveal that executives at both companies privately acknowledged serious ethical and legal problems with their AI training data practices. A top Microsoft executive internally described AI scraping as "the largest theft of labor in human history," while internal data showed [[Microsoft Copilot]] caused NYT click-through rates to drop as much as 93% versus traditional Bing search. The filings also expose coordinated data-sharing initiatives — Projects Taxi and Mango — that allegedly contained hundreds of thousands of copies of publisher works.

## Core Concepts

- **[[NYT v. OpenAI]] lawsuit** — ongoing copyright litigation by The New York Times against [[OpenAI]] and [[Microsoft]] over unauthorized use of publisher content for AI training
- **[[AI Training Data]] ethics** — the practice of scraping web content (including paywalled material) to train large language models, and internal acknowledgment of its legal exposure
- **[[Microsoft Copilot]] substitution effect** — internal data showed Copilot reduced NYT click-through rates by up to 93%, creating what employees called a "doom loop" for publishers
- **Project Taxi / Project Mango** — joint Microsoft–OpenAI data-sharing initiatives; Project Mango allegedly contained copies of at least 160,903 unique publisher works
- **[[Paywall]] bypassing** — employees allegedly devised methods to circumvent publisher paywalls undetected and stripped copyright notices before model ingestion
- **[[Satya Nadella]]** — Microsoft CEO who testified that paywalled content "should be licensed" and said he would have required retraining had he known about paywall scraping
- **[[Brent Hecht]]** — Microsoft Director of Applied Science who described AI scraping as "the largest theft of labor in human history" in a January 2023 internal memo
- **[[Nick Turley]]** — OpenAI head of ChatGPT who wrote internally that publishers face an "existential threat" from products that are "largely substitutive"

## Key Takeaways

- **Internal admission**: Brent Hecht's memo called AI scraping the largest labor theft in history.
- **Traffic collapse**: Copilot caused up to 93% drop in NYT click-throughs vs. Bing search.
- **Scale of copying**: Training datasets contained 91,692+ copies of NYT, Daily News, and CIR works.
- **Common Crawl exposure**: A single dataset had 2 million+ nytimes.com documents.
- **Paywall bypass**: Employees created methods to scrape paywalled content undetected.
- **Copyright stripping**: Copyright notices were removed from training data before ingestion.
- **Nadella testimony**: CEO said paywalled content "should be licensed" and he would have acted differently.
- **Existential framing**: OpenAI's Turley internally called AI products "largely substitutive" for publishers.
- **Sealed context**: Underlying exhibits remain sealed; quotes appear without full original context.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Externalities]]**: Microsoft and OpenAI captured the economic value of publisher content while offloading the costs (lost traffic, lost revenue) onto the content creators — a textbook negative externality that executives privately understood but did not correct.
- **[[Principal-Agent Problem]]**: Executives knew scraping practices were ethically and legally problematic yet continued — internal knowledge diverged sharply from public positioning, exposing a gap between what leadership knew and what was disclosed.
