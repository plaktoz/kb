## Summary

Samsung Electronics plans to more than double its [[HBM4]] and HBM4E high-bandwidth memory production in 2027, with glass carrier outsourcing volumes rising 2.5-fold to 50,000 sheets/month. Overall HBM capacity is projected to grow ~40% to roughly 250,000 wafers/month, with the HBM4 family's shipment share climbing from ~40% to ~80%. This expansion centers on high-value, advanced-stack products, reflecting Samsung's strategic focus on premium memory amid intensifying AI-driven demand.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Samsung Electronics]]** — leading the HBM4/HBM4E production ramp as a key supplier for AI chip customers
- **[[HBM4]] (High Bandwidth Memory 4)** — next-generation memory using 10nm-class 1c [[DRAM]] with a 4nm base die; mass production began February 2026
- **[[HBM4E]]** — enhanced variant featuring 12-layer stacks; sample units provided to [[Nvidia]] in May 2026
- **Glass carriers** — temporary structural supports attached to wafer undersides during thinning and drilling, preventing warping; become increasingly critical as stack counts rise
- **Wafer capacity scaling** — total HBM capacity expanding from ~180,000 to ~250,000 wafers/month (~40% increase)
- **Outsourcing growth** — glass carrier outsourcing to third parties growing 2.5× (20,000 → 50,000 sheets/month), indicating supply chain expansion beyond Samsung's internal capacity

## Key Takeaways

- **Production ramp**: HBM4/HBM4E output to more than double in 2027.
- **Capacity target**: ~250,000 wafers/month, up ~40% from current ~180,000.
- **Glass carrier outsourcing**: rising 2.5× to 50,000 sheets/month in 2027.
- **Shipment share shift**: HBM4 family rising from ~40% to ~80% of HBM shipments.
- **Mass production**: HBM4 in production since February 2026; 12-layer HBM4E samples shipped to Nvidia in May 2026.
- **Stack complexity**: 12-layer and higher configurations drive glass carrier demand.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Capacity Ahead of Demand]]**: Samsung is building production well ahead of confirmed orders, betting that AI infrastructure spending will absorb the supply — a high-stakes capital allocation pattern common in memory cycles.
- **[[Product Mix Shift]]**: Moving from ~40% to ~80% HBM4 share within the same wafer base is a margin and positioning play — same fab, higher value output — illustrating how product mix can reshape economics without expanding total capacity proportionally.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Samsung's HBM production plan for 2027 and what does it signal strategically?
**A**: Samsung plans to more than double HBM4/HBM4E output, growing total HBM capacity ~40% to ~250,000 wafers/month, placing HBM4 — a high-value product — at the center of its memory strategy.

**Q2**: What role do glass carriers play in HBM manufacturing, and how does the outsourcing plan reflect production scale?
**A**: Glass carriers are temporary wafer supports that prevent warping during thinning and drilling; their outsourcing volume rising 2.5× (to 50,000 sheets/month) signals that Samsung's internal capacity cannot absorb the ramp alone.

**Q3**: What are the implications of HBM4's shipment share rising from ~40% to ~80%?
**A**: It means the majority of Samsung's HBM output will shift to its most advanced, highest-margin product line, likely improving revenue per wafer and positioning Samsung more competitively for AI accelerator supply chains (e.g., Nvidia).
