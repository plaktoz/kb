## Summary

Per Reuters, [[ByteDance]], [[Alibaba]] and [[Tencent]] ordered over 3 million [[Nvidia]] RTX PRO 5500 cards, with a combined list price above $35 billion. The export-compliant chip is in such demand that Nvidia warned allocations may be only 5% to 10% of requests. Chinese buyers want the cards mainly for AI inference.

## Core Concepts

- **RTX PRO 5500**: A workstation card at roughly $11,400 each, less powerful than the H200 and B300 and similar to the RTX 6000D.
- **[[Export Controls]]**: The Blackwell-based B300 is barred for Chinese buyers; Hopper-based H200 shipments need case-by-case approval and licensing.
- **Supply shortfall**: 2027 output capped at 3 million cards across all markets versus Chinese orders above 6 million.
- **[[AI Inference]]**: The stage where a finished model answers queries; the primary intended use.
- **[[TSMC]] capacity**: Nvidia expected to redirect many production slots to its upcoming [[Rubin]] data-center platform from 2027.
- **Timeline**: First samples in December; commercial shipments in Q1 2027.
- **RTX 6000D**: Launched mid-2025, drew little Chinese interest, now discontinued and selling for twice its earlier China price.
- **Tansun Technology**: Contracted 20,000 cards in an Oct. 13 filing and warned of delivery delays.

## Key Takeaways

- **Order Size**: Over 3 million cards, above $35 billion at list price.
- **Allocation Risk**: Customers may receive just 5% to 10% of requested volumes.
- **Capacity Gap**: 2027 output tops out at 3 million versus 6 million-plus demanded.
- **Compliance**: Chip is a less powerful design that fits U.S. export controls.
- **Use Case**: Chinese firms intend it chiefly for inference, not large training clusters.
- **Price Signal**: The discontinued RTX 6000D now sells for twice its previous China price.
- **Nvidia Stance**: Spokesperson blames outdated U.S. controls and China's own import limits.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Demand above capacity forces rationing** — When orders exceed what can be produced, suppliers cut allocations and secondary prices rise. · *model:* [[Supply and Demand]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent order from Nvidia?
**A**: More than 3 million RTX PRO 5500 cards with a combined list price above $35 billion, according to Reuters.

**Q2**: Why can Nvidia not fill the orders?
**A**: Chinese demand exceeds planned production: 2027 output is capped at 3 million cards across all markets while Chinese orders top 6 million, and TSMC slots are shifting to Rubin.

**Q3**: What does the episode imply about export-controlled chip markets?
**A**: Even a deliberately less powerful, compliant chip can draw massive demand, with allocations possibly 5% to 10% of requests. Past weak demand for the RTX 6000D shows how quickly sentiment can change.
