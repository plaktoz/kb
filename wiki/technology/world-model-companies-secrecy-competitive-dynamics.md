## Summary

At the All In conference, the two dominant world model startups — [[Yann LeCun]]'s [[AMI Labs]] and [[Fei-Fei Li]]'s [[World Labs]] — remained deliberately tight-lipped about commercialization plans, with AMI describing itself as still in a "research and building phase." The author frames this silence as a rational strategic choice: in a crowded AI landscape, announcing a clear direction invites competition from rivals, newer entrants, and incumbents like OpenAI and Anthropic. He calls this a "dark forest scenario" where silence is the primary form of competitive protection.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AMI Labs]]** — world model startup co-founded by [[Yann LeCun]] and [[Michael Rabbat]]; still in research phase with no disclosed commercialization plans
- **[[World Labs]]** — world model startup founded by [[Fei-Fei Li]]; most visible offering is [[Marble]], a capability demo spanning media creation, game environments, and CGI
- **[[Marble (World Labs)]]** — World Labs' most developed product; positioned as a capability showcase rather than a shipping product
- **[[Physicl]]** — data provider supplying training data to world model companies; CEO Alex de Vigan noted even suppliers are kept in the dark about end use
- **[[Dark Forest Scenario]]** — competitive metaphor applied here: in an uncertain landscape, broadcasting your direction attracts predators (rivals), so silence becomes rational strategy
- **[[World Models]]** — AI systems designed to build internal representations of the physical world, enabling simulation, planning, and generative media at a spatial level

## Key Takeaways

- **Strategic Secrecy**: Both AMI Labs and World Labs refused to detail commercialization timelines.
- **AMI Labs Status**: Still in "research and building phase" per co-founder Michael Rabbat.
- **Marble as Showcase**: World Labs' Marble demos media, gaming, and CGI — not yet a product.
- **Supplier Opacity**: Even data suppliers like Physicl don't know what clients are building.
- **Dark Forest Logic**: Announcing direction attracts competition from OpenAI, Anthropic, and newcomers.
- **Funding Without Disclosure**: Both companies raised substantial capital while withholding product roadmaps.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Dark Forest Theory]]**: LeCun's silence mirrors the game-theoretic logic of the dark forest — in a competitive ecosystem where capabilities are unclear, revealing your position (strategy) invites attack before you're ready to defend it.
- **[[Information Asymmetry]]**: World model companies exploit information asymmetry as a moat: the less rivals know about your architecture and target market, the harder it is to preemptively outcompete you — a classic first-mover protection tactic when lead time is short.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central argument the author makes about world model company secrecy?
**A**: Companies like AMI Labs and World Labs stay deliberately vague about plans because announcing a clear direction in a crowded AI market would attract competition from rivals, startups, and giants like OpenAI and Anthropic — silence is strategic protection.

**Q2**: What specific evidence from the panel illustrates how deep the secrecy goes?
**A**: Even data supplier Physicl's CEO admitted he doesn't know what his clients are actually building, saying "I wish they would tell us more. We could build more useful data if we knew what they were working on."

**Q3**: How might the "dark forest scenario" framing change how investors or partners approach world model companies?
**A**: It suggests secrecy is a feature, not a bug — rational behavior in a competitive race — so partners and investors should expect opacity and evaluate these companies on funding raised, team credentials, and benchmark demos rather than disclosed product roadmaps.
