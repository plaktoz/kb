## Summary

Mike Taylor argues that [[Microsoft Copilot Studio]] is a surprisingly capable no-code AI agent builder for enterprise use, but its potential is buried under a deeply frustrating onboarding experience and a confusing product namespace with over 80 products named "Copilot." Despite [[Microsoft]] losing ground in AI coding tools — [[GitHub Copilot]] ceded its lead to [[Claude Code]], which now commands 63% of survey respondents — Copilot Studio quietly serves enterprise clients well. The core tension is that the product's depth is real, but the new-user experience is a significant barrier outside of established enterprise IT environments.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Microsoft Copilot Studio]]** — Microsoft's no-code AI agent builder, positioned for enterprise automation; praised by IT leaders for depth despite poor discoverability.
- **Copilot namespace fragmentation** — Microsoft has launched 80+ products named "Copilot," creating confusion and obscuring Copilot Studio's utility behind layered branding.
- **[[GitHub Copilot]]** — Microsoft's AI coding assistant, which lost market leadership to [[Claude Code]] (63% survey share), illustrating Microsoft's uneven AI portfolio performance.
- **Enterprise AI agents** — Business use case driving Copilot Studio adoption; IT leaders cited it as "the best place" to build internal AI agents.
- **No-code agent building** — Copilot Studio's core capability, enabling non-developers to build AI agents without writing code.
- **Onboarding friction** — Getting a colleague access required ~15 steps across multiple portals, mandatory bundled subscriptions (~$282/year), and still failed to surface the right product.

## Key Takeaways

- **63% survey share**: [[Claude Code]] now leads AI coding tools; [[GitHub Copilot]] lost its top position.
- **80+ Copilots**: Microsoft's naming sprawl makes Copilot Studio nearly undiscoverable organically.
- **15-step onboarding**: Accessing Copilot Studio required navigating multiple portals and ~$282/year in bundled subscriptions.
- **Enterprise word-of-mouth**: Taylor only discovered it via an IT leader tip, not Microsoft's own marketing.
- **Real product depth**: Despite UX problems, the underlying agent-building capability is genuinely strong per enterprise practitioners.
- **"January 2025" UX**: A colleague's reaction on first access — highlighting how dated the interface feels relative to modern AI tools.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Distribution vs. Product]]**: Copilot Studio illustrates that a strong product can still fail to reach its audience if distribution and discoverability are broken — the best tool wins nothing if users cannot find it.
- **[[Namespace Pollution]]**: Launching 80+ products under one brand name degrades the signal value of the brand itself, making it harder for any individual product to stand out — a self-inflicted discoverability penalty.
