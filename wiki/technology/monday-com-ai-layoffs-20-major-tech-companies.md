## Summary

Monday.com announced layoffs of ~600 employees (~20% of its workforce) citing an "AI-driven growth strategy," joining at least 20 other major tech companies in 2026 that have attributed headcount reductions to AI adoption. U.S. tech firms have eliminated nearly 140,000 jobs in 2026, with Amazon, Oracle, Meta, and Microsoft alone accounting for ~50,000. Notably, companies that cited AI in layoff announcements have underperformed the Nasdaq by ~10% in the 30 days following those announcements.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI-Driven Layoffs]]**: A 2026 trend where major tech companies explicitly attribute workforce reductions to AI adoption, automation, or AI-enabled productivity gains.
- **[[Workforce Automation]]**: AI agents and tools reducing demand for support, compliance, HR, and middle-management roles across enterprises.
- **[[Tech Industry Restructuring]]**: Companies like [[Meta]], [[Microsoft]], [[Salesforce]], and [[Oracle]] reorganizing headcount toward AI-focused roles or leaner team structures.
- **[[AI Productivity Claims]]**: Executives citing AI as enabling smaller teams to deliver the same or greater output — e.g., [[Coinbase]] CEO claiming engineers now "ship in days what used to take a team weeks."
- **[[Nasdaq Underperformance]]**: Post-layoff market signal suggesting investors are skeptical of AI-as-cost-cutter narratives.

## Key Takeaways

- **Monday.com**: ~600 cuts (~20% workforce); co-founder denied cost-cutting motive.
- **Amazon**: 16,000 corporate cuts; Jassy predicted AI would reduce headcount.
- **Salesforce**: ~5,000 support roles; AI agents reduced case volume.
- **Block**: ~4,000 jobs (~50% workforce); Dorsey cited AI-enabled smaller teams.
- **Oracle**: 21,000 total cuts over 12 months; cited AI adoption directly.
- **IBM**: 15,000+ cumulative cuts; ~200 HR roles replaced by AI agents.
- **Meta**: 8,000 laid off; 7,000 simultaneously moved into AI roles.
- **Cloudflare**: 1,100 cuts despite record revenue; CEO targeted "measurers" — middle management.
- **Google**: Est. 1,500–3,000+ engineers cut via rolling reviews and buyouts.
- **Scale**: ~140,000 U.S. tech jobs eliminated in 2026; top 4 firms account for ~50,000.
- **Market signal**: AI-layoff companies underperformed [[Nasdaq]] by ~10% post-announcement.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When AI productivity becomes the stated rationale for cuts, companies risk optimizing for the narrative rather than genuine efficiency — the underperformance vs. Nasdaq suggests markets see through the framing.
- **[[Automation Paradox]]**: Firms simultaneously laying off workers while racing to hire AI talent reveal that AI is not uniformly reducing labor needs — it is shifting skill demand, not eliminating it wholesale, as Meta's 7,000 redeployments illustrate.
