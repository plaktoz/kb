## Summary

NYT Dealbook examines the widening rift in Big Tech over open vs. closed AI models, spotlighted by Nvidia's Jensen Huang launching the Open Secure AI Alliance and reports that Nvidia may guarantee $250 billion in OpenAI financing for an Ohio data center. The piece frames chipmaker-backed vendor financing as history repeating itself, echoing 19th-century railroad financing and the 2001 telecom vendor-debt bust.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nvidia]]** — reportedly brokering a $250B financing guarantee for [[OpenAI]]'s Ohio data center project
- **[[Jensen Huang]]** — Nvidia CEO pushing a "big-tent" open-and-closed-model coexistence stance
- **[[Open Secure AI Alliance]]** — new coalition to bolster AI-era cyberdefenses using open models
- **[[Vendor Financing]]** — chipmakers co-signing buyers' credit, echoing past infrastructure booms and busts
- **[[Circular Financing]]** — investor concern over AI companies financing each other's growth

## Key Takeaways

- **Nvidia in talks** to guarantee $250B for OpenAI's Ohio data center.
- **Open Secure AI Alliance launched**, championing open-weight models industry-wide.
- **Vendor financing risk**: echoes 19th-century railroads and the 2001 telecom bust.
- **Huang's open-model stance** could reshape the AI economy's power balance.
