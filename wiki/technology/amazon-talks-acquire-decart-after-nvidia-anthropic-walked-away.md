## Summary

[[Amazon]] is reportedly in talks to acquire [[Decart]], a Tel Aviv real-time video and world-model AI startup, at around a $7 billion valuation, after both [[Nvidia]] and [[Anthropic]] pursued and then abandoned deals for the company. Anthropic outbid Nvidia and negotiated a mostly-stock deal at roughly $7 billion, but walked away in early September after due diligence. The author argues that Decart's scarce, working real-time generative video infrastructure keeps drawing bidders, and that the biggest balance sheets tend to win this cycle's AI acquisition spree.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Decart]]**: Founded in 2023 in Tel Aviv by [[Dean Leitersdorf]] and [[Moshe Shalev]]; valued at nearly $4 billion in a [[Radical Ventures]]-led round months earlier.
- **DOS (Decart Optimization Stack)**: Inference and training platform claiming 1,600+ tokens per second for agentic workloads versus an industry average near 200; DOS 2.0 also processes up to 100 HD video frames per second for world models.
- **Lucy and Oasis**: Lucy is a real-time [[World Models|world model]] for virtual try-on and live video transformation (Lucy 2.5 runs at 30 fps); Oasis is a world model for [[Physical AI]] and robotics.
- **[[Real-Time Generative Video]]**: Decart's pitch is speed; its software reacts during interaction rather than rendering clips minutes later, making it usable in game engines, robotics stacks or live streaming.
- **Failed suitors**: Nvidia (an existing investor) held exclusive talks that collapsed when a bigger offer appeared; Anthropic then won over Nvidia's higher bid but walked away after [[Due Diligence]].
- **Other interest**: [[SpaceX]] and [[Nebius]] were linked to Decart; [[Elon Musk]] called the SpaceX reports "fake news." Amazon hasn't confirmed talks.
- **Strategic fit for Amazon**: [[AWS]] is adding generative AI across products like Alexa+ and the Rufus shopping assistant, and a claimed 8x agentic-inference speed advantage would feed that effort.
- **IPO timing**: Anthropic is reportedly targeting a public listing before Thanksgiving, which may explain why it preferred to walk away cleanly rather than carry an unresolved acquisition into an IPO.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three Suitors in Four Months**: Nvidia, Anthropic and now Amazon have pursued Decart.
- **Valuation Jump**: Potential ~$7B deal versus a ~$4B valuation months earlier.
- **Anthropic Walkaway**: Abandoned a ~$7B mostly-stock deal after due diligence in September.
- **Founders Chose Anthropic**: Picked Anthropic over Nvidia's higher bid.
- **Speed Is the Pitch**: DOS claims 1,600+ tokens/sec versus a ~200 industry average.
- **Real-Time World Models**: Lucy 2.5 generates live video at 30 frames per second.
- **Inference Buyers**: Interested parties all need faster AI inference, not flashier video.
- **Diligence Red Flag**: Walking away after opening the books usually signals pitch-reality mismatch.
- **Scarce Asset**: Working real-time generative video infrastructure keeps attracting bidders despite setbacks.
- **IPO Constraint**: Anthropic's pending IPO may have made the deal less attractive.
- **Balance Sheets Win**: The biggest balance sheets tend to win this cycle's AI acquisitions.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Withdrawal after full information access is a signal** — A buyer who walks away only after seeing a target's private information reveals that what was found likely diverged from the seller's story, which other parties can read as a warning. · *model:* [[Information Asymmetry]]
→ explains: Anthropic Walkaway, Diligence Red Flag

**T2. Scarcity of a capability that many buyers need sustains its price** — When a working capability is rare and every major player needs it, competing demand keeps bids rising even after individual buyers drop out.
→ explains: Three Suitors in Four Months, Valuation Jump, Scarce Asset, Inference Buyers, Speed Is the Pitch

**T3. A buyer's constraints shape which deals it can absorb** — Acquirers facing scrutiny from new shareholders are less willing to carry unresolved, risky acquisitions, so the least constrained, best-capitalized buyers end up winning contested assets.
→ explains: IPO Constraint, Balance Sheets Win
