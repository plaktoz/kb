## Summary

This ByteByteGo digest distinguishes [[Latency]] (single-packet delay), [[Throughput]] (actual data delivery rate), and [[Bandwidth]] (maximum link capacity), explains Google's 8th-generation [[TPU]] split into training-focused TPU 8t and inference-focused TPU 8i, catalogs [[Claude Code]]'s 7 permission modes, and previews five 2026 AI trends spanning efficient reasoning, persistent agents, repo-scale coding, open-weight models, and world models/physical AI.

## Core Concepts

- [[Latency]] — delay for a single packet to travel from sender to receiver
- [[Throughput]] — actual data transfer rate achieved, always less than bandwidth
- [[Bandwidth]] — maximum capacity of a link under ideal conditions
- [[TPU]] — Google's custom AI chip, purpose-built for deep learning matrix multiplication
- [[GPU]] — general-purpose chip originally built for graphics, contrasted with TPU
- [[Claude Code]] — has 7 permission modes controlling how much autonomy the agent is given
- [[Mixture of Experts]] — cited via Qwen3.5's sparse architecture as an efficient-reasoning trend
- [[World Models]] — emerging foundation for physical AI, referenced via Google Genie 3

## Key Takeaways

- Latency = delay; throughput = actual rate; bandwidth = maximum capacity
- Throughput is always less than bandwidth due to congestion and overhead
- Google's 8th-gen TPU splits into TPU 8t (training) and TPU 8i (inference)
- Claude Code has 7 permission modes, from plan to bypassPermissions
- Only 5 of the 7 permission modes are user-selectable
- 2026 AI trends: efficient reasoning, persistent agents, repo-scale coding
- Open-weight models (GLM5, Kimi K2.5) are closing the gap with closed models
- World models and physical AI are emerging from multimodal generation gains
