## Summary

[[Applied Compute]], an AI training and inference startup founded by former [[OpenAI]] researchers, raised $350 million at a $3.25 billion valuation in a round led by [[Elad Gil]]. Annualized recurring revenue has passed $100 million less than 18 months after launch. Its platform helps enterprises customize and deploy open-weight models on their own data.

## Core Concepts

- [[Applied Compute]]: cloud platform for training and running large language models, founded in 2025 in San Francisco by Yash Patil (CEO), Rhythm Garg and Linden Li.
- [[Elad Gil]]: angel investor leading the round; [[Nvidia]], Shopify Ventures and HubSpot Ventures also joined.
- [[Venture Funding]]: valuation rose from $1.3B post-money (April, $80M round) to $3.25B.
- [[Reinforcement Learning]]: supports asynchronous RL (data generation and weight updates run at the same time) and an online RL feature that periodically fine-tunes a model on the prompts it receives.
- [[Self-Distillation]]: removes the need for a separate teacher model.
- [[Open-Weight Models]]: customers customize and deploy open-source and open-weight models on their own data.
- [[Model Routing]]: Shift-1 router sends each request to the most cost-efficient LLM and can split complex tasks across several models.
- [[AI Inference]]: autoscaling inference engine on the serving side.

## Key Takeaways

- **Funding**: $350M raised at a $3.25B valuation, led by Elad Gil.
- **Investors**: Nvidia, Shopify Ventures, HubSpot Ventures and over a dozen others joined.
- **Revenue Growth**: ARR above $100M, roughly double the ~$50M reported in August.
- **Valuation Climb**: April round was $80M at $1.3B post-money, $160M total funding then.
- **Platform**: Training, inference and routing, plus staffed researchers helping customers build evaluations.
- **Customers**: Microsoft and DoorDash, the latter for specialized agent workflows.
- **Demand Driver**: Enterprises seeking lower inference costs and less reliance on one model vendor.
- **Vision**: Founders call these "the first pieces of a much larger stack" to let companies own their intelligence.
