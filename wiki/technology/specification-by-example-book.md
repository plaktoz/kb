## Summary

*Specification by Example* by [[Gojko Adzic]] draws on case studies from over 50 projects to show how Lean and Agile teams can deliver software without defects through collaborative, example-driven requirements. The book examines how organizations bridge communication gaps between business stakeholders and implementation teams using [[Specification by Example]], [[Behaviour-Driven Development]] (BDD), and agile acceptance testing. It spans teams from small startups to globally distributed enterprises using XP, Scrum, Kanban, and similar methodologies.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Specification by Example]]** — a requirements technique where teams express expected system behavior through concrete, executable examples rather than abstract text.
- **[[Behaviour-Driven Development]] (BDD)** — an evolution of TDD that frames tests in business-readable language, aligning developers, testers, and stakeholders around shared examples.
- **[[Agile Acceptance Testing]]** — automated tests derived from agreed examples that serve as both executable specification and regression safety net.
- **[[Living Documentation]]** — specifications that remain synchronized with the system and serve as a single source of truth across the full delivery lifecycle.
- **Stakeholder–team collaboration** — the book emphasizes closing the communication gap between business and technical roles as the primary driver of quality.
- **Short iterative cycles** — teams delivering in lean/agile sprints use example-driven specs to reduce rework and defects at the source.

## Key Takeaways

- **Shared understanding**: Examples align business and dev before a line of code is written.
- **Reduced rework**: Defects caught at specification time cost far less than post-deployment fixes.
- **Executable specs**: Acceptance tests double as living documentation that stays current.
- **Cross-methodology**: Patterns apply across XP, Scrum, Kanban, and distributed teams.
- **Wide applicability**: Case studies cover high-traffic sites and internal back-office systems alike.
- **Award recognition**: Won the 2012 Jolt Award for Best Book; cited as a standard BDD reference.
- **Audience**: Testers, BAs, developers, and PMs on Agile/Lean projects seeking quality improvements.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Shift Left Testing]]**: Moving quality checks earlier — into the specification phase — reduces the compounding cost of defects, exactly the mechanism Adzic documents across 50+ projects.
- **[[Ubiquitous Language]]**: Framing requirements as concrete examples forces a shared vocabulary between business and technical stakeholders, eliminating the ambiguity that causes most rework.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central claim of *Specification by Example*?
**A**: Successful Agile/Lean teams reduce defects and rework by collaboratively defining requirements as concrete, executable examples that bridge the gap between business stakeholders and implementation teams.

**Q2**: How does Specification by Example relate to Behaviour-Driven Development?
**A**: BDD is described as a closely related practice — Dan North called the book "the closest I've seen to a BDD book" — sharing the core pattern of expressing behavior through readable examples that serve as both specification and automated tests.

**Q3**: Who is the intended audience and how can they apply this book?
**A**: Testers, business analysts, developers, and project managers on Agile/Lean projects can use the patterns and case studies to improve collaboration with business stakeholders, reduce rework, and deliver software with fewer defects in short iterative cycles.
