## Summary

[[YouTube Music]] is rolling out labels that identify tracks made with [[Generative AI]], including AI's role in composition and vocals, with the labels extending to the main [[YouTube]] app. The move comes as AI-generated music floods streaming platforms, with rivals such as [[Deezer]] and [[TIDAL]] taking stricter stances by excluding AI tracks from recommendations or royalties. YouTube instead favors disclosure over restriction: AI involvement alone does not make a track ineligible for monetization.

## Core Concepts

- [[AI Content Labeling]] — a label on the player page that, when clicked, explains the specific role AI played in a track's creation.
- Three-tier disclosure — partners declare "Fully Gen AI" (entirely AI-generated), "Partly Gen AI" (AI components plus live elements) or "No Gen AI" (including pitch-corrected tracks, e.g. [[Auto-Tune]]).
- [[AI Detection Tools]] — YouTube combines partner declarations with its own detection to identify AI-generated music.
- Platform approaches:
  - [[Apple Music]] — planning "Made With AI" labels as part of its AI Transparency Tags later this year.
  - [[Spotify]] — AI labels plus a music spam filter.
  - [[Deezer]] — labels AI tracks, excludes them from algorithmic recommendations and editorial playlists, withholds royalties from fraudulent streams, and is building a user filter.
  - [[TIDAL]] — labels fully AI-generated recordings, lets listeners disable them, and excludes them from royalty attribution.
- [[Dream Track]] — YouTube's own AI music experiment letting select creators generate soundtracks using AI versions of participating artists' voices.
- Related: [[sony-warner-sue-anthropic-music-copyright]].

## Key Takeaways

- **Player-Page Labels**: Clickable labels explain AI's role in each track.
- **Three Designations**: Fully Gen AI, Partly Gen AI, or No Gen AI.
- **Auto-Tune Exempt**: Pitch-corrected tracks count as "No Gen AI."
- **Hybrid Detection**: Partner declarations combined with YouTube's own detection tools.
- **AI Flood**: Deezer received nearly 90,000 fully AI-generated tracks daily (July).
- **Disclosure Not Restriction**: AI involvement doesn't block monetization on YouTube.
- **Stricter Rivals**: Deezer and TIDAL cut AI tracks from recommendations or royalties.
- **Industry Trend**: Apple Music and Spotify also adopting AI labels.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is YouTube Music's new policy on AI-generated music?
**A**: It labels tracks made with generative AI, explaining AI's role in composition and vocals, across YouTube Music and the main YouTube app.

**Q2**: How does YouTube classify AI involvement in a track?
**A**: Partners declare one of three designations: Fully Gen AI, Partly Gen AI (AI components plus live elements) or No Gen AI, which still covers pitch-corrected tracks like Auto-Tune.

**Q3**: How does YouTube's approach differ from Deezer's and TIDAL's, and why does it matter?
**A**: YouTube relies on disclosure and still allows AI tracks to be monetized, while Deezer and TIDAL restrict AI tracks from recommendations or royalty attribution, so the economics for AI music creators differ by platform.
