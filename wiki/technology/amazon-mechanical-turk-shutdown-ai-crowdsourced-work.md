## Summary

Amazon is shutting down [[Mechanical Turk]] on September 30, 2026, ending a 21-year experiment in crowdsourced digital labor after AI models became capable of handling many of the microtasks — image labeling, transcription, text analysis — the platform was built to farm out to humans. A new generation of data-labeling startups has absorbed the demand MTurk once served.

## Core Concepts

- [[Mechanical Turk]] — launched in 2005, a marketplace for paying humans to complete "Human Intelligence Tasks" that computers of the era couldn't handle; peaked at over 500,000 workers.
- [[Artificial Artificial Intelligence]] — Jeff Bezos's term for MTurk's model of putting real people behind tasks machines struggled with.
- [[Data Labeling Startups]] — [[Scale AI]], [[Mercor]], and [[Prolific]] have built businesses supplying human expertise and training data to AI developers, extending beyond basic labeling to programmers and scientists evaluating model outputs.
- [[Turkopticon]] — worker advocacy group whose organizer Krista Pawloski said Amazon invested fewer resources in MTurk as competing platforms gained ground.

## Key Takeaways

- **Shutdown date**: MTurk closes September 30, 2026, after Amazon "regularly evaluate[s] our programs, tools and services."
- **Peak scale**: The platform counted more than 500,000 workers at its height, paying pennies per micro-task.
- **AI cannibalized the use case**: Image classification, transcription and text analysis — MTurk's bread and butter — are now standard AI capabilities.
- **Data quality problem**: A 2023 Swiss study found up to 46% of MTurk workers in one experiment used AI models to complete tasks meant to supply human-only answers.
- **Worker impact**: Some workers relied on MTurk as a full-time income source; advocacy groups say the closure leaves them scrambling for alternatives.
