## Summary

[[Amazon]] is in talks to acquire AI startup [[Decart]] at a valuation of around $7 billion, according to people familiar with the matter cited by [[The Wall Street Journal]]. This coverage frames Decart as a developer of software that makes it easier to switch AI workloads between different chips, giving Amazon technology that reduces dependence on any single chip platform. The talks are ongoing, with no guarantee of a final agreement and no other terms disclosed.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Decart]]**: Described here as a San Francisco-based startup building software that lets developers move between different [[AI Chips]]. (Other coverage frames Decart as a Tel Aviv real-time world-model company; see [[amazon-talks-acquire-decart-after-nvidia-anthropic-walked-away]].)
- **[[Hardware Portability]]**: Software that lets AI workloads run across a mix of hardware architectures rather than on a single supplier's chips.
- **[[AWS]] fit**: Chip-switching software would complement AWS cloud infrastructure and its [[Custom Silicon]] initiatives, giving customers more deployment options by performance, availability, and cost.
- **Amazon's AI stack strategy**: Heavy investment across cloud infrastructure, custom chips, model development, and partnerships with AI companies.
- **[[AI Infrastructure]] bottlenecks**: Rising demand for [[GPUs]] and specialized accelerators makes multi-chip software valuable for flexibility and capacity utilization.

## Key Takeaways

- **Deal Size**: Talks could value Decart at roughly $7 billion, per the WSJ.
- **Core Product**: Software that eases switching AI workloads between different chips.
- **Less Lock-In**: Acquisition would make Amazon's AI workloads less dependent on one chip platform.
- **Capacity Use**: Multi-chip software can improve flexibility and use of scarce accelerators.
- **AWS Complement**: Fits alongside AWS cloud infrastructure and custom silicon efforts.
- **Customer Choice**: AWS customers could deploy across hardware by performance, availability, cost.
- **Not Final**: Discussions ongoing; no guarantee of an agreement.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Portability across suppliers reduces dependence and raises usable capacity** — When workloads can move freely between hardware platforms, a buyer is no longer bound to one supplier's availability or pricing and can draw on whatever capacity exists. · *model:* [[Platform Lock-In]]
