## Summary

Stoke Space Technologies has closed the initial tranche of a $1B Series E, bringing total funding to $2.3 billion, led by Point72 Ventures and Spark Capital. The company's Nova Pathfinder rocket aims to achieve full reusability — both booster and second stage return to Earth — a milestone never previously accomplished, using a novel liquid-hydrogen thermal protection system. First flight is targeted for early 2027, with a larger follow-on vehicle, Nova Block 2, planned for 2029 to coincide with the anticipated retirement of [[SpaceX]]'s Falcon 9.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Stoke Space Technologies]]**: Seattle-based launch startup pursuing full rocket reusability as its core differentiator against [[SpaceX]] and other launch providers.
- **[[Nova Pathfinder]]**: Stoke's first rocket; 3 metric ton to LEO capacity; distinguished by full reusability of both booster and second stage.
- **[[Nova Block 2]]**: Planned second-generation vehicle; 15 metric ton to LEO; targeted for 2029.
- **[[Full Rocket Reusability]]**: The goal of recovering and reflying every stage of a rocket — not yet achieved by any operator. Stoke's liquid-hydrogen heat shield is its proposed path.
- **[[Liquid Hydrogen Thermal Protection]]**: Stoke's novel approach of flowing super-cooled liquid hydrogen through the second-stage heat shield instead of passive ablative materials.
- **Point72 Ventures & Spark Capital**: Lead investors in the Series E round, alongside Y Combinator, General Innovation, and Glade Brook Capital.

## Key Takeaways

- **$1B Series E**: Initial close brings total Stoke funding to $2.3 billion.
- **Full reusability**: Both booster and second stage designed to return — unprecedented in industry.
- **Novel heat shield**: Liquid hydrogen flows through the shield, replacing passive thermal materials.
- **First flight**: Nova Pathfinder targets early 2027 launch.
- **Nova Block 2**: 15 metric ton vehicle planned for 2029, timed to Falcon 9 retirement.
- **Pure launch provider**: Stoke will not operate its own space assets; sells launch capacity only.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[First-Mover Advantage vs. Fast Follower]]**: Stoke explicitly times Nova Block 2 to fill the gap left by Falcon 9's retirement, exemplifying a fast-follower strategy that targets a known future market vacancy rather than competing on day one.
- **[[Technical Moats via Novel Manufacturing]]**: The liquid-hydrogen heat shield, if it works at scale, creates a defensible technical moat — competitors cannot simply copy a reusability approach that requires an entirely different propellant-management system.
