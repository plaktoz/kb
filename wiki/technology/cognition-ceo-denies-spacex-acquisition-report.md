## Summary

Bloomberg reported that [[SpaceX]] attempted to acquire AI coding startup [[Cognition]], but Cognition CEO Scott Wu publicly denied the report, saying the company "is not for sale." The rumor surfaced days after SpaceX closed its $60 billion acquisition of rival coding startup [[Cursor]], and Bloomberg says deal talks with Cognition are no longer active though the companies may still explore a compute-supply relationship.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Cognition]]** — AI coding startup behind the agent [[Devin]], with enterprise customers including Mercedes-Benz, Citi, and Goldman Sachs; recently raised $1B at a $25B valuation and is reportedly in talks for a new round at $40B.
- **[[SpaceX]]** — Elon Musk's company, now also parent of [[xAI]], which went public in June 2026 at a ~$2.3T peak market cap and is selling AI ambitions to investors, including building data centers in space.
- **[[Cursor]]** — AI coding startup SpaceX acquired for $60B, whose deal closed the week before this report; Cursor and SpaceX jointly released Grok 4.6.
- **Vertical AI infrastructure consolidation** — infrastructure-rich players like SpaceX acquiring application-layer AI coding companies to deepen enterprise reach.

## Key Takeaways

- **Cognition CEO Scott Wu denied** the acquisition report on X, calling it inaccurate.
- **Report followed** SpaceX's $60B Cursor acquisition closing the prior week.
- **Deal talks are reportedly inactive**, but a compute-supply relationship may still be discussed.
- **Cognition remains independent**, one of the largest AI coding startups not yet acquired by a major model maker.
- **SpaceX already rents compute** to other AI labs, including Anthropic, until it needs capacity itself.
