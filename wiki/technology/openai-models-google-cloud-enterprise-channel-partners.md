## Summary
OpenAI is bringing its leading models and Codex tools to Google Cloud, announced Oct. 21, letting enterprises buy them through existing Google Cloud accounts despite the two firms competing in AI. The deal strengthens hyperscaler marketplaces as an AI purchasing route and opens service opportunities for channel partners. Pricing, partner margins, and some security protections remain undisclosed or pending.

## Core Concepts
- [[OpenAI]]: supplies GPT-6 Astra (most capable), GPT-6.1 Sol (lower-cost coding/automation), and GPT-6 Luna (speed and low cost), plus Codex workflows.
- [[Google Cloud]]: positions its enterprise AI platform as a multi-provider catalog of 200+ first-party, third-party, and open models, alongside [[Gemini]].
- [[Cloud Marketplace]] purchasing: spend counts toward existing cloud commitments and Flexible Savings Plans, with a consolidated invoice.
- [[Channel Partners]]: systems integrators can offer model benchmarking, integration, agent development, security assessments, and cost optimization.
- [[Microsoft]]: remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner; Google is an added distribution channel.
- [[Enterprise AI Governance]]: data stays in designated boundaries and is not used for training; Zero Data Retention and Confidential Compute are still planned.

## Key Takeaways
- **Rivals Cooperate**: [[OpenAI]] and Google compete in AI yet partner on distribution.
- **Models Offered**: Three GPT-6 family models in private preview; broader availability in weeks.
- **Purchasing Route**: Marketplace buys count toward existing Google Cloud spend commitments.
- **Workload-Level Choice**: Model selection becomes a per-workload decision, not a single-provider commitment.
- **Preview Limits**: Preview covers development and internal testing only, not production.
- **Open Questions**: Pricing, partner margins, reseller eligibility, and revenue sharing are undisclosed.
- **Security Gaps**: Zero Data Retention and Confidential Compute remain planned, not available.
- **Partner Hires**: OpenAI hired ex-Google Cloud executive Philip Larson to lead its Partner Network.
