## Summary

Trump administration officials debated aggressive interventions against Chinese open-source AI models — including sanctions, a trade blacklist, or banning US cloud companies from serving them — before backing off after Silicon Valley pushback, pivoting instead toward promoting American AI models' competitiveness and a pre-release cybersecurity review framework.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Open-Source AI Regulation]]**: A White House group including chief of staff [[Susie Wiles]], Treasury Secretary [[Scott Bessent]], and Commerce Secretary [[Howard Lutnick]] debated whether to restrict freely downloadable AI models, particularly those from Chinese developers.
- **Policy Reversal**: Senior officials considered sanctions, a trade blacklist, and banning US cloud companies from doing business with open-source AI makers, but reportedly changed course after industry outcry.
- **Pre-Release Review Framework**: The administration invited US tech companies to discuss a proposal that would let the government review new AI models for cybersecurity issues before public release.

## Key Takeaways

- **Considered actions**: sanctions, trade blacklist against Chinese open-source AI firms, and a ban on US cloud providers serving them.
- **Reversal driver**: outcry from Silicon Valley pushed officials toward promoting competitiveness instead of restriction.
- **New framework**: a potential pre-release government review of AI models for cybersecurity risk.
- Senator Jon Husted (R-OH), after meeting Nvidia and OpenAI's CEOs: "This is all happening so fast... I don't know that I have any definite conclusions about any of it."
- This whiplash follows the same week's news that 200+ companies (including OpenAI and Google) signed an open letter urging the administration not to restrict open-source AI — see [[Nvidia Open Secure AI Alliance OSAA Progress]].
