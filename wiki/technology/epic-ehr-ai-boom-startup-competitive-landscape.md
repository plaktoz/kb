## Summary

A Redesign Health survey of 112 senior health system leaders finds the AI boom is strengthening [[Epic Systems]]'s dominant position in the EHR market — 71% of leaders describe their organization as "Epic-first" — even as executives remain willing to buy from startups in specific functional areas where Epic is less equipped to compete.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Epic Systems]]**: Commands 43.7% of the acute-care EHR market, installed in 3,700+ hospitals covering 325M+ patients, and is weaving generative AI directly into its EHR via AI Charting, an AI scribe (Art), a revenue-cycle copilot (Penny), a patient assistant (Emmie), and a medical foundation model (Curiosity).
- **[[Redesign Health]]**: Venture capital and technology firm that surveyed 112 health system executives on Epic vs. external-vendor purchasing behavior.
- **Market consolidation**: Health systems allocate 57% of clinical/administrative IS budgets to Epic versus 43% to external vendors on average, with non-profit systems skewing more Epic-heavy than for-profit systems.
- **Startup opportunity zones**: enterprise imaging and AI add-ons, quality reporting and registries, discharge and care transitions, and care access tools (scheduling, intake, navigation) — areas where health systems say they're most open to non-Epic vendors.
- **[[Agentic AI]] in healthcare**: As AI evolves into agents handling prior authorization, coding, claims appeals, care navigation and scheduling across systems, the competitive landscape could shift, though Epic is already positioning to combat this with expanded ERP and application-builder capabilities.

## Key Takeaways

- **71% of health system leaders** describe their organization as "Epic-first," and 80% of those expect the bias to intensify over the next three to five years.
- **91% of executives** have "complete" or "significant" confidence Epic will execute its AI roadmap to match best external solutions — yet 64% are still likely or very likely to buy from a net-new startup.
- **Ease of integration and interoperability** is the top purchasing factor, cited by 74% of respondents.
- **48% of executives** said Epic having merely "good enough" capabilities is itself sufficient to sway them toward Epic over alternatives — a high bar for challengers.
- Epic faces a leadership shakeup ahead of its August 17 Users Group Meeting: President Sumit Rana stepped down after 28 years, and several senior AI leaders (including AI lead Seth Hain) have also departed, though this is expected to have limited impact on health systems' Epic-first confidence.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Switching Costs]]**: The survey's core finding — that "good enough" Epic capabilities alone sway 48% of buyers — is a direct illustration of how integration and interoperability switching costs let an incumbent win on convenience even when a challenger's product may be objectively better in isolation.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: The AI boom is reinforcing Epic's dominant EHR market position because of how deeply integrated and trusted the platform already is, even though real (narrowing) opportunities remain for startups in specific functional niches.

**Q2**: What data point best shows the paradox facing healthcare AI startups?
**A**: 91% of health system executives trust Epic to execute its AI roadmap, yet 64% are still open to buying from a net-new startup — meaning trust in the incumbent doesn't fully close the door on challengers, but does raise the bar they must clear.

**Q3**: Where should a healthcare AI startup focus to have the best chance of winning against Epic?
**A**: Areas requiring outside-organization data interaction (discharge, transitions of care, quality reporting), deep clinical expertise (imaging, smart intake), or new data types like computer vision — combined with easy integration and fast time-to-value.
