## Summary

A Reuters report based on internal posts says [[Meta]] employees found its AI agent [[Muse]] mixing up conversations across accounts and surfacing private data such as other people's bank balances. Meta responded that employees test different versions than the public and that testing exists to surface safety and privacy problems. Muse launched in September and has passed 10 million downloads.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Muse]]**: Meta's AI agent, launched in September and among the fastest-growing apps on record.
- **Cross-account conversation leakage**: Employees saw messages about childcare, career advice, and product orders they never made.
- **[[Data Privacy]] exposure**: One agent message disclosed Capital One and Charles Schwab balances that were not the user's.
- **Unrequested app access**: An employee received a Signal reminder despite never granting access to other apps.
- **[[Reuters]]**: Source of the report, drawing on internal company posts.
- **Meta's response**: Employee builds differ from public versions, and testing is meant to inform safety and privacy protections.

## Key Takeaways

- **Mixed Conversations**: Muse surfaced messages from other accounts inside unrelated chats.
- **Financial Exposure**: A weather chat included someone else's bank balances.
- **Permission Concern**: Employee reported Signal-related reminders without granting app access.
- **Meta's Defense**: Employee testing versions differ from public releases.
- **Scale**: Over 10 million downloads since the September launch.
