## Summary

The BDD Books: Formulation teaches the Formulation practice of [[Behaviour Driven Development]] (BDD), guiding readers to translate concrete examples into business-readable [[Gherkin]] scenarios using Given/When/Then syntax. Written by the creator of [[SpecFlow]] and the author of The Cucumber for Java Book, it follows a team building living documentation for a product under development. The book was the second volume in the BDD Books series but has since been withdrawn and its content folded into a new Manning publication, *Effective Behavior Driven Development*.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Behaviour Driven Development]] (BDD)**: A collaborative software development methodology that bridges the gap between technical and non-technical stakeholders by expressing requirements as concrete examples.
- **Formulation**: The specific BDD practice of converting discovered examples into structured, business-readable scenarios — the step between discovery workshops and automated tests.
- **[[Gherkin]] Syntax**: A structured plain-English format using Given/When/Then steps to describe system behaviour in a way all stakeholders can read and validate.
- **[[Living Documentation]]**: Documentation that is generated from and kept in sync with executable specifications, ensuring it never goes stale.
- **[[SpecFlow]]**: A .NET implementation of the Cucumber BDD framework; one of the book's authors (Gáspár Nagy) is its creator.
- **Stakeholder Inclusivity**: The book targets all roles — product owners, analysts, developers, and testers — emphasising shared ownership of specifications.

## Key Takeaways

- **BDD Formulation**: Translates raw examples into Given/When/Then [[Gherkin]] scenarios.
- **Audience**: Written for the whole team, not just developers or testers.
- **Authors**: [[Seb Rose]] and [[Gáspár Nagy]], leading figures in the BDD community.
- **Living Docs**: Scenarios double as executable tests and up-to-date documentation.
- **Status**: Withdrawn; content merged into *Effective Behavior Driven Development* (Manning).
- **Translations**: Japanese and Brazilian Portuguese versions remain available on Leanpub.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Shared Language]]**: BDD's Given/When/Then format enforces a ubiquitous language between business and engineering, directly applying the [[Domain-Driven Design]] principle that alignment on vocabulary reduces rework and misunderstandings.
