## Summary

Kairos Power has selected [[Samsung C&T]] as its engineering and equity partner, receiving up to $100M to build a 50-megawatt demonstration reactor for [[Google]] by 2030. Two reactors — Hermes 1 and Hermes 2 — are under construction in Oak Ridge, Tennessee, both with NRC approval received in late 2024. This deal advances Google's broader goal of procuring roughly half a gigawatt of nuclear capacity by 2035, originally contracted in fall 2024.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Kairos Power]]**: Startup building a fluoride salt-cooled high-temperature reactor never before deployed at commercial scale.
- **[[Samsung C&T]]**: Engineering conglomerate with experience building ~12 nuclear reactors globally; contributing $70M equity + in-kind services totaling up to $100M.
- **[[Google]]**: Tech company funding nuclear buildout, likely to power AI data center energy demands.
- **[[Hermes Reactors]]**: Two units under construction — Hermes 1 (low-power demonstrator) and Hermes 2 (first commercial-scale unit, 50 MW toward Google agreement).
- **[[TRISO Fuel]]**: Uranium encapsulated in ceramic and carbon layers within billiard ball-sized spheres; designed to prevent meltdowns.
- **[[Fluoride Salt-Cooled Reactor]]**: Novel design operating at low pressure, reducing blowout risk compared to conventional pressurized water reactors.
- **[[NRC Construction Approval]]**: Both Hermes reactors received Nuclear Regulatory Commission construction permits in late 2024.

## Key Takeaways

- Samsung C&T invests $70M equity + in-kind services, totaling up to $100M.
- Hermes 2 is the first commercial-scale unit; its 50 MW output counts toward the Google deal.
- Google's original contract (fall 2024) targets ~500 MW of nuclear by 2035.
- Fluoride salt-cooled design runs at low pressure, lowering blowout risk.
- TRISO fuel encases uranium in ceramic/carbon layers to prevent meltdown.
- Both reactors received NRC construction approval in late 2024.
- Completion target for the demonstration reactor: 2030.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Vertical Integration]]**: Google is effectively co-investing in its own energy supply chain — partnering with Kairos, who brings in Samsung — rather than relying on grid power, which mirrors the logic of owning infrastructure critical to your core product (AI compute).
