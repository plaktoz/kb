## Summary

The Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence ([[AGMAI]]) is a newly formed independent body hosted at the [[Institute for Advanced Study]] in Princeton, created to advise AI companies on responsible engagement with the mathematical research community. The group emerged after [[OpenAI]] approached some members about forming an external advisory board; the researchers chose independence instead, operating without payment from any AI company and publishing recommendations publicly. Their immediate task is advising OpenAI on releasing what the company claims are "a large number of significant results in mathematics" produced by an internal AI model.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AGMAI]] (Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence)**: Independent advisory body hosted at the [[Institute for Advanced Study]], Princeton; bridges AI industry and academic mathematics.
- **[[AI Governance]]**: Structured oversight mechanism — AGMAI represents a community-led model where researchers retain independence rather than becoming paid insiders.
- **[[AI-Generated Mathematical Results]]**: [[OpenAI]]'s internal model reportedly produced significant mathematical findings; AGMAI is advising on how to release and validate these responsibly.
- **[[Research Community Independence]]**: The group's founding decision to reject a paid advisory board in favor of an independent entity reflects a principle of preserving academic integrity.
- **Notable members**: [[François Charles]], [[Camillo De Lellis]], [[Timothy Gowers]], [[Martin Hairer]], [[Nikhil Srivastava]], [[Ulrike Tillmann]], [[Ravi Vakil]], [[Edward Witten]], [[Melanie Matchett Wood]].

## Key Takeaways

- **Independent formation**: AGMAI rejected a paid OpenAI advisory role to stay fully independent.
- **Public recommendations**: All AGMAI recommendations will be published openly.
- **Immediate focus**: Advising OpenAI on releasing AI-produced mathematical results responsibly.
- **Community input**: AGMAI solicits feedback via agmai.org; responses kept private unless approved.
- **Host institution**: Hosted at the [[Institute for Advanced Study]], Princeton — a signal of academic credibility.
- **No compensation**: Members receive no payment from any AI company, preserving impartiality.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Principal-Agent Problem]]**: By refusing payment from AI companies, AGMAI eliminates the misaligned incentives that typically compromise advisory boards — the group's interests align with the broader research community rather than with any funder.
- **[[Credibility Through Independence]]**: The decision to self-organize rather than accept a company-sponsored seat reflects the principle that perceived legitimacy requires structural independence, not just stated neutrality.
