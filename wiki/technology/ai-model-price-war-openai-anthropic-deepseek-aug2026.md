## Summary

OpenAI and Anthropic are cutting prices on some frontier models to compete with lower-cost Chinese labs, even as DeepSeek raises API prices by up to 1,100% on its new V4 Pro flagship — a sign that AI competition is shifting from a pure benchmark race toward a race on cost-per-completed-task, while Big Tech's AI infrastructure purchase commitments approach $1.5 trillion.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Pricing War]]**: OpenAI cut pricing substantially for GPT-5.6 Luna; Anthropic priced Claude Opus 5 at roughly half of Claude Fable 5, as both compete against cheaper Chinese alternatives like DeepSeek and Moonshot AI.
- **[[DeepSeek V4 Pro]]**: DeepSeek's new flagship model raises some API pricing by up to 1,100% (up to $1.32/million cache-miss input tokens, $3.96/million output tokens at peak), signaling a shift from disruption-through-cheap-inference toward monetizing premium performance.
- **[[AI Infrastructure Purchase Commitments]]**: Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Nvidia, Oracle, and Meta have accumulated close to $1.5 trillion in purchase commitments for compute, chips, and data centers — separate from another ~$1.5 trillion in lease commitments identified by Goldman Sachs.
- **[[Unit Economics of Inference]]**: Enterprise buyers are increasingly asking how much useful work each dollar of inference buys, rather than which model tops the leaderboard — favoring efficient models and opening space for Chinese developers.

## Key Takeaways

- **Price divergence**: US labs (OpenAI, Anthropic) are cutting prices while a leading Chinese lab (DeepSeek) is raising them on its highest-performance tier.
- **Scale of commitments**: Big Tech's AI purchase commitments (~$1.5T) plus lease commitments (~$1.5T) represent future cash obligations largely absent from standard capex figures.
- **Unicorn boom**: 40 companies reached $1B+ valuations in July 2026 — the highest monthly total in over four years — spreading beyond foundation models into robotics, chips, and energy.
- **Applied Materials**: Reported $9.12B quarterly revenue (+25% YoY) on AI-driven chip equipment demand, though shares fell on already-high investor expectations.
- **Latency as competition**: OpenAI's new Cerebras-powered "Ultrafast" GPT-5.6 Sol tier (14x faster) shows speed is becoming as competitive a dimension as price or intelligence.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Commoditization]]**: As frontier model intelligence becomes increasingly interchangeable for routine tasks, competition shifts to price and unit economics — exactly the dynamic pushing OpenAI and Anthropic to cut prices while chasing Chinese competitors on cost.
