## Summary

[[Donald Trump]] announced the formation of a federal [[Super Intelligence Force]], chaired by national intelligence director [[Jay Clayton]], tasked with coordinating government efforts to keep America leading the world in "super intelligence." The task force follows Trump's September promise of an "AI Force" and AI czar, his executive order rebranding AI as super intelligence, and a non-binding White House safety pledge signed by tech executives. Its charter emphasizes responding to SI-enabled threats while explicitly avoiding overregulation and [[Regulatory Capture]].

## Core Concepts

- **[[Super Intelligence Force]]**: Federal task force announced via a Sunday [[Truth Social]] post, named to match the administration's [[Super Intelligence Rebrand]].
- **[[Jay Clayton]]**: Chairs the force, effectively becoming the new AI/SI czar; argues one of the biggest risks is "not being first" while countries like [[China]] advance.
- **Vice chairs**: [[Federal Trade Commission]] Chair [[Andrew Ferguson]], Undersecretary of War for Research and Engineering [[Emil Michael]], and Office of Personnel Management Director [[Scott Kupor]].
- **120-day mandate**: The force reportedly must produce a report on the risks and opportunities presented by AI.
- **Charter language**: Develop plans for "SI-enabled threats to our society, while preventing overregulation and regulatory capture that would stifle innovation and competition."
- **Backdrop**: Trump has largely dismissed the [[AI Safety]] debate as a hoax, yet promised an AI Force in September; tech executives then signed the non-binding [[White House Accord on Super Intelligence]].

## Key Takeaways

- **Clayton is SI czar**: National intelligence director chairs the new task force.
- **Three vice chairs**: FTC's Ferguson, War Department's Michael, OPM's Kupor.
- **120-day clock**: Report on AI risks and opportunities due within four months.
- **Race framing dominates**: Clayton calls "not being first" one of the biggest risks.
- **Anti-overregulation charter**: Threat response paired with explicit avoidance of regulatory capture.
- **Branding continuity**: Naming follows the executive order replacing "AI" with "super intelligence."

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Arms Race Dynamics]]**: Framing the top risk as "not being first" to China makes speed the overriding goal, which explains why the charter treats overregulation as a threat on par with SI-enabled harms.
