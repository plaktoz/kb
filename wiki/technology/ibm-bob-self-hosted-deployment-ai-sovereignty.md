## Summary

[[IBM]] announced self-hosted deployment for [[IBM Bob]], its agentic software development platform, so organizations can run AI-assisted software delivery and modernization on-premises or in private-cloud, sovereign-cloud, and air-gapped environments they control. The move targets regulated enterprises whose sensitive source code, regulated data, and mission-critical infrastructure keep them off public AI platforms. IBM frames this as "bringing AI to the data instead of moving data for the AI," with [[AI Sovereignty]] and governance as the deciding factors in enterprise AI adoption.

## Core Concepts

- **[[IBM Bob]]**: IBM's [[Agentic AI]] software development platform, built to go beyond code generation and apply AI to software delivery and [[Application Modernization]].
- **Self-Hosted Deployment**: Runs on customer-managed infrastructure (on-prem, private cloud, sovereign cloud, or air-gapped) using supported models the customer has licensed.
- **Hybrid Configurations**: Bob can also connect to supported external model services, mixing local control with outside models.
- **[[AI Sovereignty]]**: Control over where AI runs, what data it can access, and how it is governed. IBM presents this as a prerequisite for regulated industries.
- **[[Data Sovereignty]]**: According to the [[IBM Institute for Business Value]] report "The Calculus of AI Sovereignty," 68% of executives find cross-geography residency and sovereignty requirements challenging.
- **[[Hybrid Cloud]] Shift**: [[Futurum Research]] projects hybrid and edge at 44% of the AI infrastructure market by 2030, with public cloud falling to 46%.
- **[[Neel Sundaresan]]**: IBM GM of AI and Automation, who says enterprise AI's future depends on "security, governance and sovereignty."

## Key Takeaways

- **Deployment Flexibility**: Bob now runs on-prem, in private or sovereign clouds, and air-gapped.
- **Core Pitch**: Bring AI to the data rather than moving the data to the AI.
- **Target Buyers**: Regulated industries with sensitive code and mission-critical infrastructure.
- **Differentiator**: Many rival AI coding tools require sending data to external services.
- **Market Forecast**: Hybrid and edge reach 44% of AI infrastructure by 2030; public cloud falls to 46%.
- **Executive Pain**: 68% of executives struggle with data residency and sovereignty requirements.
- **Modernization Reality**: Enterprise apps are spread across on-prem and private environments, not just cloud-native ones.
- **Related**: Builds on IBM's sovereign push in [[ibm-yotta-sovereign-agentic-ai-platform-india]].

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Data Gravity]]**: Large, sensitive, regulated datasets and codebases are costly or forbidden to move, so the compute and AI tooling has to go to where the data already lives, which is the logic behind self-hosting Bob.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did IBM announce for IBM Bob on October 1, 2026?
**A**: A self-hosted deployment option that lets organizations run Bob, IBM's agentic software development platform, on-premises and in private-cloud, sovereign-cloud, and air-gapped environments they control.

**Q2**: What evidence does IBM cite for demand for sovereign, self-hosted AI?
**A**: Futurum Research projects hybrid and edge deployments will capture 44% of the AI infrastructure market by 2030 (public cloud falling to 46%). IBM's Institute for Business Value found 68% of executives find data residency and sovereignty requirements challenging.

**Q3**: Why does self-hosting matter for a regulated enterprise choosing an AI coding tool?
**A**: Many AI coding tools require moving code and data to external services, which strict security and compliance rules may forbid. Self-hosting lets the enterprise use agentic AI where its code, application context, and data already reside while keeping governance and operational control.
