## Summary

Seattle Tech Week 2026 drew record attendance and Bay Area investor interest, with AI as the dominant throughline across 250+ events. A recurring tension emerged: are companies built on top of frontier models genuinely "AI companies," or just application layers renting someone else's intelligence — a question sharpened by venture data that ranks Seattle behind Philadelphia, Austin, and New York despite substantial hidden engineering activity in the region.

## Core Concepts

- [[Foundation Models Go Vertical]] — panel discussion on enterprises building atop frontier models trained on public web data rather than their own proprietary data
- [[Application Layer vs Intelligence Layer]] — the debate over whether a company built on someone else's foundation model is really an "AI company"
- [[Outcome-Based Pricing]] — a shift from charging per-seat or per-unit software to charging based on the outcome delivered, enabled by AI capability
- [[Seattle Startup Ecosystem]] — undercounted in venture rankings because engineering talent and co-founders based there often get attributed to Bay Area companies
- [[AI Cost Volatility]] — one startup CEO described his Anthropic bill tripling in three months, prompting a shift to AWS Bedrock and local models for low-level tasks

## Key Takeaways

- **"Are you an AI company or an application company on top of somebody else's intelligence?"** — the sharpest framing to emerge from the week, from Oumi CEO Manos Koukoumidis
- **Enterprises use models trained on the 5% of data on the public web**, not the 95% of proprietary data sitting inside their own organizations
- **AI isn't reducing headcount needs even for AI companies** — OpenAI's deputy general counsel noted the legal team needs more people despite productivity gains
- **Venture rankings undercount Seattle's real economic activity** since talent and co-founders based there are often attributed elsewhere
- **"We're gonna laugh when we thought that AI was gonna save us time"** — Mistral AI's CMO on the gap between AI's time-saving promise and reality

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Build vs Rent]]**: Koukoumidis's framing of "renting intelligence" versus owning it is a first-principles lens for evaluating any AI startup's defensibility — a company differentiated only by prompting someone else's model has little moat once that model improves or a competitor accesses the same API.
