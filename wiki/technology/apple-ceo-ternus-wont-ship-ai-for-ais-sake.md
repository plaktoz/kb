## Summary

In a Wired interview with Steven Levy, Apple CEO John Ternus defended Apple's AI position despite criticism that it is behind, saying Apple will leverage AI but won't "ship AI for AI's sake." He argued users shouldn't have to care whether AI or traditional software delivered a feature; what matters is building something they love. Asked whether agents could make apps unnecessary, he said product creation is a "deeply human and personal thing."

## Core Concepts

- [[John Ternus]]: Apple CEO since the beginning of September; longtime Apple executive.
- [[Apple]]: described as behind on AI after delaying a more powerful [[Siri]], which launched this summer.
- [[AI Strategy]]: AI applied only where it serves a product outcome, not as a headline feature.
- [[Siri]]: the delayed assistant whose release fed the "behind on AI" narrative.
- [[AI Agents]]: could make apps unnecessary, a possibility Ternus acknowledged but downplayed.
- [[Steven Levy]]: Wired interviewer who pressed Ternus on agents and the app ecosystem.

## Key Takeaways

- **Position**: Ternus says "I really like where we're at" on AI despite critics.
- **Commitment**: Apple is "absolutely going to leverage AI" in new products.
- **Invisible AI**: Users shouldn't need to know whether AI or traditional software did it.
- **Success metric**: "Did we build something that they really love?"
- **Agents question**: Agents could simplify life, but product creation stays "deeply human."
- **Context**: Siri's more powerful version was delayed, then launched this summer.
