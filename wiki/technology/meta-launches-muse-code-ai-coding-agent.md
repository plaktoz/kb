## Summary

Meta launched Muse Code, its first AI coding agent, alongside the updated Muse Spark 1.2 coding model, entering the competitive AI coding-agent market against OpenAI and Anthropic with a pricing-led strategy and a lower-cost data-sharing "contributor tier."

## Core Concepts

- **[[Muse Code]]**: Meta's first AI coding agent, built on a coding harness that manages models for complete software engineering workflows — planning, writing, and validating code — rather than just generating snippets.
- **[[Meta Superintelligence Labs]]**: The AI division led by Chief AI Officer [[Alexandr Wang]], created after Meta's Llama models underperformed rivals, now driving the Muse model family (Muse Code, Muse Image, Muse Video).
- **[[AI Coding Agent Market]]**: A highly competitive segment where [[OpenAI]] and [[Anthropic]] have already demonstrated AI can automate much of software development — Meta is now a third major entrant.
- **[[Contributor Tier Pricing]]**: A lower-cost usage tier where developers agree to share coding data to help train future models, alongside a zero-data-retention option for enterprises.

## Key Takeaways

- **Muse Code and Muse Spark 1.2** launched together in preview, handling planning, writing, and validation across entire codebases.
- **Pricing over raw capability**: standard tier costs $1.25/million input tokens and $4.25/million output tokens, undercutting several competitors.
- **Meta's AI capex guidance rose** to $134–145 billion for the year, reflecting the scale of investment behind the Muse model family.
- Meta compares Muse Spark 1.2's performance against Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and xAI models, though it doesn't claim frontier-leading capability.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Meta has entered the AI coding-agent market with Muse Code, competing against OpenAI and Anthropic primarily on price rather than claiming the most capable model.

**Q2**: How does Muse Code's pricing structure work?
**A**: A standard pay-as-you-go tier at $1.25/$4.25 per million input/output tokens, plus a lower-cost "contributor tier" where developers share usage data to help train Meta's models, and a zero-data-retention option for enterprises.

**Q3**: What does this launch signal about Meta's broader AI strategy?
**A**: It's part of a deliberate reset of Meta's AI business under Meta Superintelligence Labs after Llama's underwhelming developer reception, backed by a capex increase to as much as $145 billion.
