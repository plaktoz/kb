## Summary

[[Sam Altman]] confirmed in a Fortune interview that [[OpenAI]] will not go public in 2026, calling such a move "ill-advised" given ongoing [[AI Safety]] concerns. Altman said OpenAI will list "when we're ready," tying readiness to both business health and broader societal context around AI. As of late September 2026, OpenAI is reportedly in private funding discussions that would value the company above $1.2 trillion, with existing major stakeholders — including [[Microsoft]], [[Amazon]], [[Nvidia]], [[Peter Thiel]], and [[Andreessen Horowitz]] — potentially being approached again.

## Core Concepts

- [[OpenAI]] — AI research company that filed confidentially for an IPO but is now deferring the listing in favor of private funding
- [[Sam Altman]] — OpenAI CEO; characterized the current moment as ill-suited for going public; cited ~10% catastrophic AI risk probability
- [[Initial Public Offering (IPO)]] — the mechanism OpenAI has been preparing for via confidential filing and banker/lawyer engagement, now delayed
- [[AI Safety]] — cited by Altman as a reason public market scrutiny is premature; Altman stressed treating any low-probability catastrophic risk seriously
- [[Tech Stock Volatility]] — external market factor contributing to the IPO delay alongside internal financial challenges
- [[Private Funding Round]] — alternative path; OpenAI in talks for a $1.2T+ valuation round from institutional investors less sensitive to near-term regulatory pressures

## Key Takeaways

- Altman explicitly confirmed: "I would say not 2026, yeah."
- Called a 2026 IPO "ill-advised" due to AI safety context, not just market timing.
- OpenAI had hired bankers and lawyers targeting a Q3/Q4 2026 listing.
- NYT reported OpenAI was already leaning toward 2027 due to stock volatility and internal financial challenges.
- Readiness threshold is dual: business performance *and* societal AI context.
- New private funding round discussions value OpenAI above $1.2 trillion.
- Key investors being approached: [[Microsoft]], [[Amazon]], [[Nvidia]], [[Peter Thiel]], [[Andreessen Horowitz]].
- Private institutional investors seen as less sensitive to regulatory pressures than public markets.
- Double-edged delay risk: higher IPO entry valuation for retail investors, but underwhelming AI infrastructure returns could hurt eventual offering.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Optionality]]**: By staying private longer, OpenAI preserves flexibility to time the IPO when both internal readiness and external conditions align — going public locks in scrutiny and short-term reporting pressure that could constrain high-risk research decisions.
- **[[Risk-Adjusted Timing]]**: The $1.2T private round signals that delaying the IPO is not purely defensive — OpenAI can capture capital at a higher valuation while avoiding regulatory and market scrutiny that public status would impose.
