## Summary

[[Sam Altman]] confirmed in a Fortune interview that [[OpenAI]] will not go public in 2026, calling such a move "ill-advised" given ongoing [[AI Safety]] concerns. Altman said OpenAI will list "when we're ready," tying readiness to both business health and broader societal context around AI. This aligns with prior reporting that OpenAI was leaning toward a 2027 IPO despite having hired bankers and lawyers for a potential Q3/Q4 2026 listing.

## Core Concepts

- [[OpenAI]] — AI research company that filed confidentially for an IPO but is now deferring the listing
- [[Sam Altman]] — OpenAI CEO; characterized the current moment as ill-suited for going public
- [[Initial Public Offering (IPO)]] — the mechanism OpenAI has been preparing for via confidential filing and banker/lawyer engagement
- [[AI Safety]] — cited by Altman as a reason public market scrutiny is premature right now
- [[Tech Stock Volatility]] — external market factor contributing to the IPO delay alongside internal financial challenges

## Key Takeaways

- Altman explicitly confirmed: "I would say not 2026, yeah."
- Called a 2026 IPO "ill-advised" due to AI safety context, not just market timing.
- OpenAI had hired bankers and lawyers targeting a Q3/Q4 2026 listing.
- NYT reported OpenAI was already leaning toward 2027 due to stock volatility and internal financial challenges.
- Readiness threshold is dual: business performance *and* societal AI context.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Optionality]]**: By staying private longer, OpenAI preserves flexibility to time the IPO when both internal readiness and external conditions align — going public locks in scrutiny and short-term reporting pressure that could constrain high-risk research decisions.
