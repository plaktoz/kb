## Summary

Microdrama startup Shortical is shifting to wholly AI-generated series and is soliciting people to license their faces for AI avatars, debuting its first such deal with actor Aki Avni in "Inevitable." Cheaper production and higher profit potential drive the shift, but compensation terms are vague, early face-licensing offers in China paid only $15 to $700, and industry voices question whether audience demand exists. Concerns about job loss, consent, and gig-ification of the industry accompany the push.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Shortical]]**: Founded last January; subscription microdrama platform where the first 7-10 episodes are free, then a monthly fee unlocks the rest.
- **[[Microdramas]]**: Two-minute, cliffhanger-driven, risqué romance episodes watched on phones; viewership skews female and, for Shortical, mostly US-based.
- **[[AI Face Licensing]]**: Actors or creators license their likeness to generate digital avatars; Aki Avni is Shortical's first such deal, done directly rather than via the platform.
- **[[Generative AI]] in production**: Lower overhead lets small teams stage "epic fantasies" with kingdoms, dragons, and werewolves.
- **[[Gig Economy]] dynamics**: Partnership with Payouts.com for creator disbursements; royalty terms are "case by case."
- **[[Venture Capital]] incentives**: Jennifer Cooper (Vertical Drama Love) says funding requires mentioning AI.

## Key Takeaways

- **Cost Logic**: AI content has far lower production overhead and higher profit potential than live-action.
- **Face Licensing**: Shortical's site invites people to "LICENSE YOUR FACE"; no platform contracts signed yet.
- **Thin Pay**: Chinese microdrama firms reportedly paid $15 to $700 per facial license.
- **Opaque Terms**: Shortical won't disclose fees; royalties like TV residuals are undecided.
- **Labor Impact**: Microdramas, once an entry-level path for young actors, are being downsized.
- **Demand Doubt**: Some AI series get millions of views, but Cooper says AI is pushed on audiences.
- **Consent Issues**: Creators have claimed their likenesses were stolen; licensing aims to address this.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Shortical betting on, and what is the central tension?
**A**: Shortical is betting that AI-generated microdramas starring licensed human faces can cut costs and raise profits. The tension is that this threatens actors' work and may be driven by investor appetite for "AI" rather than audience demand.

**Q2**: What evidence is given about how much face licensing pays?
**A**: A Rest of World report found microdrama firms in China paying $15 to $700 for facial likeness licenses. Shortical declines to share figures, and says Avni's pay matched industry standards for a comparable non-AI acting role.

**Q3**: How could face licensing change the creator economy?
**A**: It could gig-ify the industry, with creators paying distributors for exposure and then receiving payouts based on audience engagement, as with Shortical's Payouts.com partnership. Royalty-style compensation remains undecided.
