## Summary
Anthropic launched reporting tools that show enterprise managers what a team produced, what it cost and where workflows broke down, in an effort to prove and improve the return on token spend. Jellyfish data suggests enterprise stickiness comes from standardized workflows and switching costs, which these tools reinforce as a lighter form of forward-deployed engineering.

## Core Concepts
- [[Anthropic]] and [[Claude Code]]: reported an operating profit in Q2 and 900% net revenue retention (Q3 2025 to Q3 2026 spend from the same customers).
- [[AI Spend Reporting Tools]]: internal analytics and employee surveys showing team output, cost and workflow breakdowns, plus improvement recommendations and pooled group budgets.
- [[AI ROI]]: a major concern for large customers; most of the Fortune 500 are not seeing major gains.
- [[Jellyfish]]: its report covers agentic coding tool use by 276,000 customers.
- [[OpenAI]] Astra and Codex: a perceived threat that has not slowed Anthropic's adoption.
- [[Forward-Deployed Engineering]]: embedding AI consultants inside customers; the new tools are described as "FDE-lite".
- [[Switching Costs]]: effort spent standardizing workflows and agent configuration makes changing vendors harder.

## Key Takeaways
- **Retention**: 900% net revenue retention, with an operating profit in Q2.
- **Reporting Tools**: Managers see team output, cost and workflow breakdowns, plus recommendations.
- **Spend Growth**: Agentic coding tool spend grew 10x this year, about 2% of engineering cost.
- **Market Share**: Anthropic-based tools produce nearly a third of proposed code changes in Jellyfish data.
- **Competition**: Astra has not slowed Anthropic; enterprise motions and standardized workflows keep customers.
- **Fortune 500 Gap**: Most large firms are not yet seeing major gains from AI deployment.
- **Optimization Phase**: Per Jellyfish, spending is no longer enough; customers must optimize it.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Effort invested in a vendor raises the cost of leaving it** — Once an organization has standardized workflows and configuration around one product, switching is no longer free even if alternatives are nearly equivalent. · *model:* [[Switching Costs]]
→ explains: Competition, Retention

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Anthropic trying to accomplish with its new reporting tools?
**A**: It wants to show enterprise customers the return on their token spend and how to improve it, addressing a major ROI concern among large customers. The tools report what a team produced, its cost and where workflows broke down.

**Q2**: What evidence does the article give for why Anthropic keeps enterprise customers despite competition?
**A**: Jellyfish's data shows Anthropic-based tools lead usage, and its AI product lead says Anthropic had strong enterprise motions and customers invested effort standardizing workflows around it. Anthropic also reported 900% net revenue retention.

**Q3**: How do the new tools relate to switching costs?
**A**: They act as a lighter version of forward-deployed engineering, deepening customer investment in the platform. As Jellyfish's Albarran notes, once customers are in, switching gets harder because the cost to switch is not zero.
