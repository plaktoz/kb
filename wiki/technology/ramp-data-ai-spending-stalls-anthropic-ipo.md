## Summary
Ramp data shows AI token spend grew just 1.5% across August and September (versus 42% in July) even as usage volumes rose, driven by price cuts, discounts and customers getting thriftier. Because spend is concentrated in the top 1% of AI-heavy firms, a small slowdown there stalls revenue for OpenAI and Anthropic. This arrives as Anthropic's potential IPO valuation reportedly fell from $2 trillion to $1.2-1.3 trillion.

## Core Concepts
- [[Ramp]] and economist [[Ara Kharazian]]: source of the spend data and the "Cracks in the AI Thesis" blog series.
- [[Anthropic]] and [[OpenAI]]: model companies cutting API prices (GPT-6 Sol/Luna, Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5) amid a [[AI Price War]].
- [[Concentration Risk]]: nearly all AI spend comes from venture-backed, high-growth tech firms.
- [[AI Cost Optimization]]: defaults, budgets and routers that shift work to cheaper models, plus [[Jev]] for simple Yes/No tasks.
- [[Personal Agents]]: the speculative next growth driver after [[AI Coding Agents]].

## Key Takeaways
- **Flat Spend**: Token spend grew 1.5% in Aug-Sep versus 42% in July.
- **Volume vs Revenue**: Token volumes rose while spending stayed flat.
- **Top 1% Stutter**: The most AI-heavy firms dipped just below $10,000 per employee.
- **Spend Distribution**: Median firms spend $12-$15 monthly per employee; top 10% spend $600-$1,000.
- **Price Cuts**: Anthropic said its price cuts slashed revenue growth by about $600 million.
- **Buyer's Market**: Competition prevents Uber-style subsidize-then-raise-prices lock-in.
- **Thrift Effect**: Advanced users impose budgets, defaults and routers to [[Cost Control]] spend.
- **Valuation Hit**: Anthropic's potential valuation reportedly fell to $1.2-1.3 trillion; OpenAI's to $1.25 trillion.
- **October Rebound**: Token spend rose 8.9% from September 23 to October 23.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Concentrated demand makes aggregate revenue fragile** — When a few heavy buyers supply most spend, a small slowdown among them stalls the total even as broad usage grows. · *model:* [[Concentration Risk]]
→ explains: Top 1% Stutter, Spend Distribution, Flat Spend

**T2. Competition shifts power to buyers and lets usage rise without revenue** — With many substitutes, price cuts and thrift mean customers use more AI for less money, and lock-in subsidies fail. · *model:* [[Price Elasticity of Demand]]
→ explains: Price Cuts, Buyer's Market, Thrift Effect, Volume vs Revenue

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Ramp's central finding about AI spending?
**A**: Token spend grew only 1.5% across August and September, versus 42% in July, even though token volumes kept rising. Usage is increasing but revenue for model companies is not.

**Q2**: Why does a slowdown among the top 1% of firms matter so much?
**A**: Almost all AI spend comes from venture-backed, high-growth tech companies, and the top 1% dipped just below $10,000 per employee. Gains among median and top-10% firms aren't enough to offset that, so revenue stalls.

**Q3**: What does this imply for AI model companies' growth outlook?
**A**: Price competition and customer thrift make it a buyer's market, so assumptions about hypergrowth are breaking down. Investors are hoping new products such as personal agents will drive growth, but Kharazian is skeptical.
