## Summary
Enterprises have adopted AI faster than they have built the data, governance and change-management foundation needed to make it pay off; Gartner finds only 39% of tech leaders are confident AI will improve financial performance. The article argues the differentiator is not the model but trusted, governed proprietary data, cost visibility per workload, and matching models to jobs. Governance is framed as infrastructure that speeds AI approval rather than a constraint.

## Core Concepts
- [[Enterprise AI ROI Gap]]: adoption outpacing value; [[Gartner]] reports successful organizations invest up to 4x more in data, governance and change management.
- [[AI Cost Visibility]]: tying every AI action to a workload, owner, dataset, model and cost ([[Ben Schein]], [[Progress Software]]).
- [[Data Silos]] and [[Data Provenance]]: fragmented data and per-person AI assistants inflate cost and undermine governance ([[Jason Longhurst]]).
- [[AI Model Selection]] and portfolio management: small models in batch for repetitive work, frontier models for decisions that need them; pricing varies up to 30-fold.
- [[AI Governance]]: [[BCG]] finds centralized requests and automated review make companies 30% more likely to approve the average use case within four weeks.
- [[Proprietary Context]] as the enterprise moat ([[Ed Keisling]]); [[Prosper Insights & Analytics]] survey data on adoption and trust.

## Key Takeaways
- **Confidence Gap**: Only 39% of tech leaders expect AI to improve financial performance.
- **Foundation Spend**: Successful AI organizations invest up to 4x more in data, governance, change management.
- **Visibility Problem**: Without per-action workload, owner and cost records, spend compounds silently.
- **Model Commoditization**: Leading models are interchangeable; prices differ up to 30-fold.
- **Portfolio Matching**: Small batch models for repetitive work; frontier models only where required.
- **Governance Speeds Approval**: Centralized, automated review makes approval within four weeks 30% more likely.
- **Trust Deficit**: 54% of executives use generative AI versus 36% of employees; nearly 4 in 10 Americans want human oversight.
- **Owned Context**: Customer history, taxonomies and operational data are what vendors cannot ship.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Durable advantage lives in what rivals cannot copy or rent** — When the core capability (the model) becomes interchangeable, advantage shifts to the scarce, owned complement: governed proprietary data and context. · *model:* [[Economic Moat]]
→ explains: Model Commoditization, Owned Context, Foundation Spend

**T2. Unmeasured activity is not value** — Usage that cannot be tied to a workload, owner and outcome produces cost and noise rather than return, and the gap compounds silently until it is measured. 
→ explains: Visibility Problem, Confidence Gap, Trust Deficit

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does most enterprise AI fail to pay off, according to the article?
**A**: Companies adopted AI faster than they built the data, governance and change-management foundation to support it. The differentiator is trusted data and visibility, not the model.

**Q2**: What evidence shows the importance of that foundation?
**A**: Gartner found only 39% of tech leaders are confident AI will improve financial performance, and successful organizations invest up to four times more in data, governance and change management. BCG found centralized, streamlined governance makes approving a use case within four weeks 30% more likely.

**Q3**: How should an enterprise apply this to model choice and spend?
**A**: Match models to workloads, running high-volume repetitive work on small batch models and reserving frontier models for decisions that need them. Also log who triggered each AI action, its workload, datasets, model and cost.
