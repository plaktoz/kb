## Summary
Google will block K-12 students from Search starting in January unless districts opt into its AI-integrated version, prompting accusations of bullying from parent advocates and calls to reconsider reliance on Google. Critics note that districts face high switching costs from long contracts, training, and staffing limits, while Common Sense Media found Google's AI Mode completed homework every time in testing.

## Core Concepts
- [[Google]]: requires districts to opt into AI-integrated Search or have it turned off, with no AI-free search option.
- [[Guided Learning]]: the AI product that becomes the default in Search for schools.
- [[AI Mode]] and [[AI Overview]]: stay integrated in Search even if Gemini and Gemini Notebook are turned off.
- [[Common Sense Media]]: tested AI Mode 180 times and found it completed assignments every time.
- [[Schools Beyond Screens]]: parent group led by [[Lila Byock]], which calls the move an "ultimatum".
- [[Electronic Frontier Foundation]]: [[Rory Mir]] calls for a discussion of Google's role as a monopolist.
- [[Los Angeles Unified]]: the largest California district, which already restricts generative AI and is reviewing the announcement.
- [[Degoogling]]: divesting from Google software, which is harder for districts than for individuals.

## Key Takeaways
- **The Ultimatum**: From January, K-12 Search is blocked unless districts opt into AI-integrated Search.
- **No AI-Free Option**: AI Mode and AI Overview remain in Search even if Gemini is off.
- **Usage**: About three-quarters of US teens and tweens interact with AI summaries in search.
- **Test Results**: AI Mode completed homework in all 180 tests, invented answers, and cited Reddit and Facebook.
- **Switching Friction**: Long contracts, educator training, and monetary and labor costs slow leaving Google.
- **Capacity Gap**: Districts with stronger tech departments find work-arounds more easily.
- **Mid-Year Disruption**: Districts must choose or find an alternative during the school year.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Google's new policy for K-12 students, and why is it controversial?
**A**: Starting in January, Google will block student Search unless districts opt into its AI-integrated version, with no AI-free option. Critics call it an ultimatum that undermines school control over AI policies.

**Q2**: What evidence do critics cite about the risks of Google's AI in schools?
**A**: Common Sense Media ran 180 tests and found AI Mode completed students' homework every time, invented fake answers, gave inconsistent answers, and cited unreliable sources. It has not reviewed the Guided Learning mode.

**Q3**: Why can't districts simply leave Google?
**A**: Mir of the EFF says schools sign long contracts and need training, so switching carries real monetary and labor costs and disruption. Districts with weaker staffing and funding have fewer work-arounds.
