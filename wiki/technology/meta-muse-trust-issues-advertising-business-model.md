## Summary

TechCrunch's Equity podcast hosts discuss whether [[Muse]], Meta's AI agent, can overcome the trust deficit created by Meta's advertising-based business model. Panelist Sean O'Kane found the app useful in a limited way but noted it gradually nudges users toward sharing more Meta app data over time, prompting comparisons to [[Apple]]'s [[Siri]] as a more trustworthy alternative. The hosts also noted Meta's consumer-first AI strategy contrasts with [[OpenAI]] and [[Anthropic]]'s pivot toward enterprise, possibly driven by pre-IPO revenue pressure.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Muse]]**: Meta's consumer AI agent, showcased prominently at Meta's Connect event, drawing more attention than recent model releases from OpenAI and Anthropic.
- **[[Advertising Business Model Trust Gap]]**: Skepticism that a company monetized through ads will resist using personal data gathered by an AI agent to improve ad targeting.
- **[[Progressive Data Sharing]]**: Muse initially operated without pulling in the user's existing Meta app data, then gradually encouraged deeper data-sharing over time.
- **[[Siri]]**: Referenced as a trust benchmark — Apple's non-ad-dependent business model makes users more comfortable sharing sensitive data with it than with Meta's Muse.
- **[[Consumer AI vs Enterprise AI Strategy]]**: Meta is doubling down on consumer applications (including a Tamagotchi-style AI wearable for adults) while OpenAI and Anthropic pivot toward enterprise clients, reportedly to generate revenue ahead of potential public offerings.

## Key Takeaways

- Muse's Connect reveal overshadowed recent OpenAI and Anthropic model releases in media attention.
- O'Kane found Muse useful for discovering unclaimed funds, but called it a "party-trick" rather than daily-use tool.
- Muse began without accessing existing Meta app data, increasing initial user comfort.
- Muse gradually encourages deeper data-sharing over time, unsettling that same trust.
- O'Kane trusts Apple's Siri more with sensitive data since Apple's business isn't ad-driven.
- Korosec argues Meta's deep consumer embedding (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp) favors its consumer-first AI bet.
- OpenAI and Anthropic's enterprise pivot may be driven by pre-IPO revenue pressure, per Ha.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Incentive Alignment]]**: Trust in an AI agent tracks the alignment between its maker's revenue model and the user's interests — an ad-funded company gathering more personal data creates a structural conflict of interest that a subscription- or hardware-funded company (like Apple) does not face.
