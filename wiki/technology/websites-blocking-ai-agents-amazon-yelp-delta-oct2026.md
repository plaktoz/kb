## Summary

Major websites are blocking consumer [[AI Agents]] from [[OpenAI]], [[Meta]], xAI and others, including Amazon, Yelp, Delta, JPMorgan Chase and Charles Schwab. Reasons range from protecting ad revenue and data-licensing income to security concerns, and generic anti-bot systems catch agents even where no ban exists. The pushback undercuts the pitch that agents can do anything a person can do online.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Amazon]] vs. agents**: Amazon sued [[Perplexity]] last year over its shopping agent; an appeals court overturned the injunction in August and Amazon filed an amended complaint. It now says Meta's [[Muse]] violates site policies.
- **Paid access**: [[Yelp]] allows only agents that pay for its data licensing program.
- **Security blocks**: JPMorgan Chase and Charles Schwab block agents from accessing accounts; Delta prevents agent bookings.
- **Quiet blocking**: Users report Cars.com and Amtrak no longer work with their agents, suggesting unannounced blocks.
- **Partnership route**: Meta partnered with Walmart, Gap, Sephora and Best Buy so Muse can shop there, and [[Shopify]] opened its storefronts to agents.
- **Anti-bot collateral**: Systems built to stop scams and scrapers also trigger on agents; [[Cloudflare]] began blocking crawlers that scrape for both search and AI training.

## Key Takeaways

- **Access Limits**: Popular services increasingly refuse third-party AI agents.
- **Ad Economics**: Amazon's resistance protects the human attention its ads depend on.
- **Pay or Partner**: Agent makers either license data or sign retailer deals.
- **Hidden Friction**: Anti-bot defenses block agents even without explicit bans.
- **Value Erosion**: Fewer reachable sites means fewer useful things agents can do.
- **Human Verification Creep**: Experts expect more "prove you're human" hurdles, even on grocery sites.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Platforms with a gatekeeper position resist intermediaries that erode the revenue the gate protects** — Amazon's ad business depends on direct human browsing, so agents that bypass it are treated as threats until they pay or partner.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central claim of the article?
**A**: Popular websites are blocking AI agents, which limits what agents from OpenAI, Meta, Google and others can actually do for users.

**Q2**: What mechanisms keep agents out even where sites have not banned them?
**A**: Ubiquitous anti-bot systems built to stop scams and hacking trigger on agents too, and Cloudflare blocks crawlers that scrape for search and AI training together.

**Q3**: What are the two ways agent makers are responding?
**A**: They pay for data access, as with Yelp, or sign partnerships with retailers such as Walmart, Gap, Sephora and Best Buy, and Shopify has opened its network to agents.
