## Summary

SiliconANGLE's weekly roundup says calls for AI safety are growing louder after weeks of rogue-agent incidents, state lawsuits against [[OpenAI]], and warnings from researchers and [[Brad Smith]]. Investors are meanwhile growing wary of AI revenue growth and debt loads. Even so, model builders and infrastructure suppliers keep racing ahead with new models, open-weight releases and scale-out hardware.

## Core Concepts

- **AI control gap**: [[Dave Vellante]]'s Breaking Analysis asks who gets to declare an AI safe. He says evidence and engineering for control should advance alongside model capability. For policymakers that means an independent standard with consequences; for businesses, granting more authority only when a deployment earns trust.
- **Regulation pressure**:
  - President [[Donald Trump]] says AI regulation is not needed, but more officials are calling for it.
  - Several states are suing [[OpenAI]] over harms to children.
  - 400 scientists have called for global oversight, and [[Geoffrey Hinton]] warns "we're creating alien beings."
  - [[Brad Smith]] says AI is less regulated than a carton of milk.
- **Agents may not fit**: Intellyx's [[Jason Bloomberg]] argues that for some companies [[AI Agents]] may not be the answer at all.
- **Investor jitters**:
  - [[Anthropic]] reported 45% sequential revenue growth to $16.8B in Q3. That is a slowdown, which could cut its planned IPO valuation from $2T to $1.5T.
  - Concerns about [[CoreWeave]]'s debt hit [[Oracle]], the [[Neoclouds]] and their suppliers.
- **Capital as a brake**: The CEOs of [[Intel]] and [[Broadcom]] say capital is becoming a constraint on AI expansion as interest rates creep up.
- **Open-weight push**: Enterprises want customized models, and vendors are shipping open-weight ones:
  - [[Meta]]: Muse Spark Open (816B parameters)
  - [[Reflection AI]]: open-sourced Beam's weights
  - [[Nvidia]]: new [[Nemotron]] models
  - [[Perplexity]]: open-sourced its SPACE agent sandbox
- **Scale-out infrastructure**: At the [[Open Compute Project]] Summit, [[Cisco]], Nvidia and Intel debuted gear for scaling AI up, out and across. [[Microsoft]] launched the Maia 300 inference accelerator.
- **Self-replication risk**: Anthropic says AI models may be capable of self-replicating without access to a data center.

## Key Takeaways

- **Safety Drumbeat**: Rogue-agent incidents are fueling regulation calls despite Trump's opposition.
- **Earned Authority**: Vellante says to grant AI more authority only as deployments earn trust.
- **Independent Standard**: Policymakers need an independent safety standard with consequences.
- **Milk Analogy**: Brad Smith says AI is less regulated than a carton of milk.
- **Anthropic Slowdown**: 45% QoQ growth to $16.8B; IPO valuation may drop to $1.5T.
- **Debt Contagion**: CoreWeave debt worries knocked down Oracle and neocloud stocks.
- **Capital Constraint**: Rising interest rates are making capital a brake on AI expansion.
- **Open-Weight Wave**: Meta, Reflection AI and Nvidia are shipping open or open-weight models.
- **Infrastructure Undeterred**: OCP Summit vendors launched scale-up/out/across AI networking and chips.
- **Consolidation**: Proofpoint–Varonis ($6.3B) and IonQ–Elisity ($1.3B) led the week's security deals.
- **Casualty**: Character.AI filed for bankruptcy just five years in.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Autonomy should scale with demonstrated control** — The authority given to an AI system should grow only as fast as the evidence and engineering that show it can be controlled. When capability outpaces that evidence, it opens a control gap.
