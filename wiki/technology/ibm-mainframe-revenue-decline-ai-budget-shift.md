## Summary

[[IBM]] reported a 42% year-over-year decline in mainframe revenue, sending its stock down ~25% — one of its worst single-quarter drops in decades. IBM attributed the decline to enterprise customers deferring mainframe purchases as they redirect IT budgets toward AI infrastructure. The market rejected this "timing issue" framing, raising questions about whether AI capex is structurally crowding out legacy hardware.

## Core Concepts

- **[[IBM]] Mainframe** — z-series hardware business; cornerstone of IBM's enterprise revenue for decades
- **[[AI Budget Cannibalization]]** — phenomenon where AI infrastructure spending crowds out traditional IT hardware purchases
- **[[Legacy Hardware Cycle]]** — mainframe upgrade cycles subject to deferral when competing investment priorities emerge
- **[[Enterprise AI Capex]]** — corporate spending on GPUs, AI cloud, and accelerators displacing traditional IT

## Key Takeaways

- **-42% mainframe revenue**: worst quarterly decline in recent memory; stock dropped ~25%
- **IBM's framing**: "timing issue" — customers deferring, not abandoning mainframes
- **Market verdict**: investors didn't buy the timing story; reaction suggests structural concern
- **AI cannibalization**: AI hardware budgets directly competing with mainframe refresh cycles
- **IBM counter**: z-series supports on-premises AI inference for regulated industries
