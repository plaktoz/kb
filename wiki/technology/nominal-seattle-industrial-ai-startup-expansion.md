## Summary

[[Nominal]], a Los Angeles-based startup whose software helps engineers build, test, and validate complex hardware systems, is opening a permanent Seattle office and plans to more than double its 11-person local team by year-end. The move follows an $80 million Founders Fund-led round that valued the four-year-old, ~200-employee company at $1 billion, and reflects Seattle's growing pull as a hub for companies operating at the intersection of AI and the physical world.

## Core Concepts

- [[Nominal]] — startup building data-supply-chain software for hardware engineering teams (instrumentation, acquisition, storage, analysis, reporting); ~75 customers, about half in defense
- [[Founders Fund]] — led Nominal's $80M round at a $1B valuation; also backs fellow Seattle entrant Armada
- [[Industrial AI]] — the broader trend of AI-and-physical-world startups (Nominal, Overland AI, Carbon Robotics, Brinc, Stoke Space) clustering in Seattle
- [[Anduril Industries]] — Nominal's Head of Product Stephen Slattery and CEO Cameron McCord both previously worked there; informal advice from Anduril co-founder Matt Grimm shaped the Seattle decision
- Seattle as a tech hub — draws on aerospace heritage and dense engineering talent pool, also attracting Anthropic, OpenAI, and expanded Anduril operations

## Key Takeaways

- Nominal is relocating Head of Product Stephen Slattery from LA to lead the new Seattle office
- Seattle team of 11 expected to more than double by end of 2026, reaching 20-25, then 40-50 by end of 2027
- Nominal raised $80M from Founders Fund at a $1B valuation shortly before the Seattle expansion
- About half of Nominal's 75 customers are in defense; four of five major US defense contractors use its software
- Seattle office will be full cross-functional (engineering, sales, customer success, design), not just an engineering outpost
- Nominal sees growing opportunity in energy (nuclear, fusion, batteries) and AI data center infrastructure
