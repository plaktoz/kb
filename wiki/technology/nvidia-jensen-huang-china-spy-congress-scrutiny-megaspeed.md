## Summary

U.S. intelligence reportedly assessed that China's Ministry of State Security recruited Megaspeed executive Alice Huang to gather information on [[Jensen Huang]], prompting bipartisan congressional scrutiny. [[Nvidia]]'s board opened an independent investigation after Huang disclosed in a staff memo that he had been intimate with her, while the company maintains no secrets or national-security information were compromised. Three House Republican chairs asked Huang for a closed-door briefing on Nov. 18, and Democrats are calling for investigations or testimony.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Jensen Huang]]**: Nvidia CEO who disclosed the relationship in a memo to staff and says he will continue leading without interruption.
- **[[Megaspeed]]**: Singapore-based tech company the U.S. has suspected of helping China circumvent AI chip sales prohibitions; Alice Huang is an executive there.
- **[[Nvidia Board Investigation]]**: Independent inquiry into Huang's involvement with Alice Huang, which the board says will examine all available information.
- **[[China Ministry of State Security]]**: Reportedly recruited Alice Huang, per an intelligence memo received by Trump administration policymakers last spring.
- **[[AI Chip Export Controls]]**: Underlying policy context; lawmakers also questioned the Commerce Department's clearance of New Matrix to assemble Nvidia chips in Beijing.
- **[[Congressional Oversight]]**: House GOP chairs and Democrats such as Sen. [[Chris Coons]] and Rep. Seth Magaziner are pressing for briefings, investigations, and testimony.

## Key Takeaways

- **Intelligence Memo**: Administration received an intelligence memo last spring identifying Alice Huang as recruited by China's MSS.
- **Board Response**: Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation, saying it has no reason to believe secrets were compromised.
- **Congressional Ask**: Three House GOP chairs requested a closed-door briefing on Nov. 18, a day after earnings.
- **Bipartisan Pressure**: Democrats separately call for investigation; Magaziner wants Commerce Secretary Lutnick to testify too.
- **Administration Scrutiny**: Lawmakers question what the Trump administration knew, including Rubio's role overseeing the memo.
- **New Matrix Report**: Times found little evidence of operations at a Chinese firm cleared to assemble Nvidia chips.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central controversy facing Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang?
**A**: U.S. intelligence reportedly assessed that China's Ministry of State Security recruited Megaspeed executive Alice Huang to gather information on him, and Huang disclosed an intimate relationship with her. Lawmakers from both parties are demanding briefings and investigations.

**Q2**: How have Nvidia and its board responded?
**A**: The board opened an independent investigation, stating it has no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised. Nvidia also said Huang's interactions were consistent with ordinary customer meetings and that he will keep leading without interruption.

**Q3**: What upcoming events could shape the fallout?
**A**: Huang has been asked to attend a closed-door briefing on Nov. 18, the day after Nvidia reports third-quarter earnings. Democrats are also seeking testimony from Commerce Secretary Lutnick and plan investigations when they take Senate control in January.
