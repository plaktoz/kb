## Summary

President [[Donald Trump]] formally announced a "Super Intelligence Force," a federal AI task force meant to coordinate government efforts so America keeps leading the world in AI while protecting Americans' interests. The announcement gave few details and set out no specific actions or oversight powers, even though some tech CEOs have called for federal regulation. The task force will be led by [[Jay Clayton]], the expected White House AI czar, who argues that being first in AI is the best way to handle risks from adversaries.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Super Intelligence Force]]**: A federal task force that coordinates the government's work with consumers, public interest groups, religious organizations, critical infrastructure providers, and "Super Intelligence Companies."
- **Leadership team**: [[Jay Clayton]] (Director of National Intelligence), [[Andrew Ferguson]] (FTC Chairman), [[Emil Michael]] (Under Secretary of Defense for Research and Engineering), and [[Scott Kupor]] (OPM Director).
- **[[AI Czar]]**: Clayton is expected to serve as the White House's new AI czar.
- **Predecessor events**: Trump promised an AI task force last month, then met tech CEOs and signed a self-policing "constitution" with six companies. See [[trump-tech-ceos-ai-self-policing-accord-sep2026]] and [[trump-super-intelligence-rebrand-naming-poll-sep2026]].
- **"Risk of not being first"**: Clayton argues that global AI dominance leaves the U.S. better equipped to handle both known and unknown risks from adversaries.
- **Financial-industry risk model analogy**: Clayton pointed to risk models built with the [[Federal Reserve]] and [[SEC]] as a template, saying "mechanisms developed in dialogue" would emerge.
- **Role of Congress**: The task force will review current law and discuss actions [[Congress]] could take.

## Key Takeaways

- **Thin on details**: No specific actions or oversight powers announced for the task force.
- **Dual mandate**: Promote U.S. AI dominance while protecting American lives and interests.
- **Four-person leadership**: Clayton, Ferguson, Michael, and Kupor will lead the force.
- **Dominance as safety**: Clayton says "the risk of not being first is high."
- **Finance-style template**: Risk mechanisms would be co-developed with industry, like Fed and SEC models.
- **Legislative review**: The task force will examine current law and possible congressional action.
- **Follows self-policing accord**: Comes a week after six companies signed voluntary AI standards.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Security Dilemma]]**: Clayton argues that leading the race is itself the safety strategy, because falling behind adversaries raises risk. This logic pushes toward speed over restraint, which matches the task force's lack of announced oversight.

**Q2**: Who leads the task force, and what risk philosophy does its head express?
