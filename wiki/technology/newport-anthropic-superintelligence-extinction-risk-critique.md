## Summary

Cal Newport responds to the resignation of Anthropic engineer Jacob Coxon and public statements from current staff — including alignment head Evan Hubinger estimating a greater than 10% probability that AI kills all humans within a decade — arguing that a company openly admitting it may be building extinction-level technology and continuing anyway is categorically unacceptable. Newport distinguishes between safe, constrained coding agents (billions of interactions, near-zero severe incidents) and a narrow dangerous class of long-horizon, unsupervised, fully-equipped agents that pose real existential risk. He calls the continued development of the latter a product of "technological salvation ideology" rather than financial necessity.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Technological Salvation Ideology]]**: Newport's term for the Silicon Valley worldview — held by leaders like [[Dario Amodei]] — that frames building potentially apocalyptic superintelligence as a quasi-religious calling rather than a commercial decision, insulating the choice from normal cost-benefit scrutiny.
- **[[Long-Horizon Unsupervised LLM Agents]]**: Newport's technical category for the dangerous class of AI: agents with access to powerful hacking tools, guardrails removed, minimal safety constraints, and extended autonomous operation — distinct from the constrained coding agents used by millions today.
- **[[AI Alignment]]**: The unsolved problem of ensuring AI systems act in accordance with human values; Anthropic's own alignment head acknowledged the company currently lacks a plan to solve it.
- **[[Evan Hubinger]]**: Anthropic's head of Alignment Science, who publicly stated a personal estimate of >10% probability that AI kills all humans within a decade while continuing to work on the systems in question.
- **[[Jacob Coxon]]**: Anthropic engineer who resigned, alleging the company is "racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives."
- **[[Gary Marcus]]**: AI critic cited by Newport as the source of the consumer boycott recommendation directed at Anthropic and OpenAI.

## Key Takeaways

- Evan Hubinger: >10% personal estimate that AI kills all humans within a decade.
- Hubinger acknowledged Anthropic lacks a plan to solve alignment — yet continues.
- Senior Anthropic employees express more concern about safety than junior ones.
- Standard coding agents: billions of interactions, essentially zero high-severity incidents.
- Dangerous agent class: hacking tools + no guardrails + no human oversight + long horizons.
- Newport: abandoning dangerous agent configurations would have negligible revenue impact.
- Newport endorses consumer boycott (Gary Marcus) and calls for Congressional action.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Pascal's Mugging]]**: Hubinger's >10% extinction estimate is a case where even a seemingly low probability demands extreme caution — a 10% chance of human extinction should override ordinary commercial logic entirely, making the "we continue anyway" stance structurally indefensible.
- **[[Technological Determinism]]**: Newport's "technological salvation ideology" critique identifies a belief that the technology *will* be built regardless — turning a choice into an inevitability and removing moral agency from developers, which is precisely the framing Newport rejects.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Newport's core argument about Anthropic's situation?
**A**: Anthropic's own senior staff publicly admit they may be building an extinction-level technology and lack a plan to prevent catastrophic outcomes, yet continue development — Newport argues this is categorically unacceptable and not justified by financial necessity.

**Q2**: What distinguishes the dangerous AI agent class Newport describes from safe coding agents?
**A**: Dangerous agents combine powerful hacking tools, removed guardrails, minimal safety constraints, and extended autonomous operation without human oversight; standard coding agents are heavily constrained and have produced near-zero high-severity incidents across tens of millions of interactions.

**Q3**: What actions does Newport call for, and what is his rationale?
**A**: Newport endorses a consumer boycott of Anthropic and OpenAI and calls for Congressional action, arguing that continued development of the dangerous agent class is driven by ideological rather than financial reasons and could therefore be halted without meaningful revenue loss.
