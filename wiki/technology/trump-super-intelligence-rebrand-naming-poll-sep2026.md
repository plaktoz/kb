## Summary

President [[Donald Trump]] signed an executive order and ran social-media polls to officially rebrand "artificial intelligence" as "Super Intelligence," then signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence with six tech CEOs the same day. The accord's four layers of self-policing controls echo [[Dario Amodei]]'s September "pace the frontier" essay, but carries no enforcement mechanism, penalties, or timeline. Researchers like [[Nick Bostrom]] note the new term technically describes hypothetical systems beyond human intelligence, not today's chatbots, making the branding premature.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Super Intelligence Rebrand]]**: Trump ran two social-media polls (X and Truth Social) pitting "Superior," "Extreme," "Supreme," and "Super" Intelligence against each other before signing an executive order replacing "AI" with "Super Intelligence" in federal usage.
- **[[White House Accord on Super Intelligence]]**: Subtitled "Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities," signed Sept. 29, 2026 by [[Dario Amodei]], [[Greg Brockman]], [[Jensen Huang]], [[Elon Musk]], [[Sundar Pichai]], and [[Mark Zuckerberg]] at a White House "Super Intelligence Luncheon."
- **Four Layers of Controls**: Internal monitoring, internal dev teams maintaining those controls, independent external auditors, and independent board-level committees — all self-administered with no outside enforcement.
- **[[Nick Bostrom]]**: Philosopher who originally defined superintelligence as hypothetical systems surpassing human intelligence — not the chatbots and spam filters the new government terminology now covers.
- **Amodei's "Pace the Frontier" Essay**: [[Dario Amodei]]'s Sept. 12 three-part plan — permanent evaluator access, shared safety standards, and a government-granted antitrust waiver for coordination — forms the backbone of the accord's substance.
- **Non-signatories**: [[Apple]] sent no representative at all; [[Microsoft]] and [[Amazon]] attended the luncheon but did not sign.

## Key Takeaways

- **No enforcement**: The accord has no penalties, timeline, or definition of "independent" auditor.
- **Branding came from polls**: "Super Intelligence" beat "Superior Intelligence" 65% to 35% in a Truth Social poll.
- **Term is premature**: Bostrom's definition describes future hypothetical systems, not current generative AI.
- **Traces to Amodei's plan**: The accord operationalizes ideas Amodei proposed publicly just weeks earlier.
- **Antitrust backdrop**: A class-action suit alleges a coordinated AI slowdown by major labs violates antitrust law.
- **Apple absent**: Apple sent no one, breaking from its usual presence at Trump tech summits.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Framing Effect]]**: Renaming "AI" to "Super Intelligence" changes nothing about what the underlying software does — it only reshapes public perception of its capability and risk, a textbook case of framing substituting for substance.
