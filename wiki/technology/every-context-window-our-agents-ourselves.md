## Summary

Every's "Context Window" newsletter covers several AI developments: updated ChatGPT workflows for the post-Codex-merge unified app, an executive AI Q&A distilling 33 questions from ~400 executives, and a model review of Deft. The most striking piece is mathematician [[Terence Tao]]'s "proof indigestion" problem — AI now generates valid-seeming proofs faster than humans can verify them, requiring humans to act as translators between machine results and usable mathematical knowledge.

## Core Concepts

- **[[ChatGPT]] Workflow Update**: Katie Parrott rebuilt Every's guide after [[OpenAI]] merged Codex into ChatGPT mid-July 2026, producing 16 revised workflows for the unified app.
- **Executive AI Adoption Q&A**: Natalia Quintero and Mike Taylor answered 33 questions submitted by ~400 executives covering strategy, skeptic management, tool selection, governance, and team restructuring.
- **[[KateBench]]**: A copyeditor trained on 30,000 edits; highlighted for showing that a high acceptance rate can still hide the edits a human must redo — measuring acceptance alone understates rework.
- **[[AI Skill Cloning]]**: Companies are capturing employee judgment as reusable AI skills — a practice Every is piloting internally.
- **[[Deft Model]]**: A model designed to solve AI prose sameness; Katie Parrott's review found it less predictable, but dense output and hallucination-prone.
- **[[Proof Indigestion]]**: [[Terence Tao]]'s term for the backlog created when AI produces more apparently valid proofs than mathematicians can verify; "central ideas have yet to be extracted" from results.
- **AI-to-Human Compression**: Tao condensed a 90,000-line AI-generated proof of [[Sendov's Conjecture]] to ~15,000 lines of human-readable reasoning using surprisingly basic tools.

## Key Takeaways

- **ChatGPT unified**: 16 workflows recast after Codex merge into one app.
- **KateBench insight**: High acceptance rate masks hidden human rework.
- **Deft tradeoff**: Less predictable prose, but dense and hallucinates.
- **Executive Q&A scale**: ~400 executives submitted 33 distilled questions on AI adoption.
- **Proof indigestion**: AI outpaces human verification capacity in mathematics.
- **Tao as translator**: 90,000-line proof compressed to 15,000 human-readable lines.
- **Humans as intermediaries**: Future role may be translating machine results, not generating them.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: KateBench's high acceptance rate obscures true edit quality — when acceptance becomes the metric, it stops measuring what matters (rework burden on humans).
- **[[Bottleneck Thinking]]**: In math and knowledge work alike, the bottleneck has shifted from proof generation to proof verification and comprehension — AI accelerates generation but humans remain the throughput constraint on understanding.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does Terence Tao mean by "proof indigestion"?
**A**: AI now generates more apparently valid mathematical proofs than the global community of mathematicians can verify, creating a growing backlog of results whose "central ideas have yet to be extracted."

**Q2**: How does KateBench illustrate the limits of simple acceptance-rate metrics?
**A**: A high acceptance rate can still hide the edits a human has to redo — it measures how often output is accepted, not how much invisible correction work follows acceptance.

**Q3**: What does Tao's compression work on Sendov's Conjecture suggest about the human role in AI-augmented mathematics?
**A**: Humans may increasingly serve as translators — taking vast machine-generated outputs and distilling them into compact, human-readable knowledge that the broader community can actually use and build on.
