## Summary

The FTC, joined by 22 states, has sued Amazon for secretly inflating advertising costs over seven years through a hidden surcharge mechanism that converted its marketed second-price auction into an effective first-price auction. Amazon allegedly inserted a fake bidder — internally called a "soft reserve price" — to drive up winning bids, causing advertisers to pay their full bid roughly 80% of the time. The scheme allegedly affected over 1 million brands and may have generated tens of billions in extra revenue for Amazon.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Second-Price Auction]]**: An auction format where the winner pays one cent above the second-highest bid; Amazon advertised this model to [[Sponsored Products]] and [[Sponsored Brands]] advertisers, encouraging high bidding by promising cost caps.
- **[[Soft Reserve Price]]**: Amazon's internal name for the hidden surcharge — essentially an *invented auction participant* (fake bidder) injected to inflate winning prices beyond what genuine competition would produce.
- **[[FTC Antitrust Enforcement]]**: The [[Federal Trade Commission]] coordinated a multi-state lawsuit against [[Amazon]], alleging deceptive advertising practices that harmed over 1 million small and mid-size businesses.
- **[[Amazon Advertising]]**: Amazon's ad business — encompassing Sponsored Products, [[Sponsored Brands]], and Display ads — generated over $68 billion in revenue last year, making ad monetization central to Amazon's overall business model.

## Key Takeaways

- FTC + 22 states sued Amazon for secretly inflating ad auction prices since 2019.
- Amazon told advertisers it ran a second-price auction; in practice, winners paid full bid ~80% of the time.
- A hidden "soft reserve price" acted as an invented fake bidder to drive up costs.
- Over 1 million brands — including 500,000+ small businesses — were allegedly affected.
- Amazon earned $68B+ in ad revenue last year; called the lawsuit "misguided."
- The 22 states include California, New York, Florida, Texas equivalents, and others.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Principal-Agent Problem]]**: Amazon acted as the auctioneer (agent) for advertisers (principals) and secretly redesigned the auction to extract more value for itself — a textbook case of misaligned incentives when the intermediary controls both the rules and the marketplace.
- **[[Information Asymmetry]]**: Advertisers had no visibility into the soft reserve price mechanism; Amazon's opacity gave it structural power to charge more than a transparent market would allow.
