## Summary
Google is retiring [[Gemini]]'s "Gems" custom-assistant feature, automatically converting them into "skills" starting November 17, 2026, as rivals like [[Meta]] push all-in-one AI agents such as Muse. The article frames this as part of a recurring pattern of Google rebranding and reorganizing AI features rather than keeping them stable, and argues the new "skills" interface remains less approachable than simple chat-based agents.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Gemini Gems]]**: Feature debuted in 2024 letting users build custom AI assistants (e.g. learning coach, coding partner, running coach) without repeating instructions each time.
- **[[Skills (Gemini)]]**: The successor concept Gems are being converted into, invoked via a forward-slash command in a task thread.
- **[[Google]]** product strategy: pattern of frequent AI feature rebranding/reorganization, likened to its historical habit of running multiple overlapping messaging apps.
- **[[Meta]]**'s Muse: cited as a competing all-in-one AI agent that Gemini's skills model is implicitly responding to.

## Key Takeaways
- **Migration date**: Gems become skills starting November 17, 2026; Google handles conversion automatically.
- **No user action needed**: Existing Gems continue working normally until the cutover.
- **Origin**: Gems launched in 2024 with premade examples (learning coach, brainstorming assistant, career guide, coding partner, editor) plus user-built and shareable versions.
- **UX critique**: Skills require typing a forward slash to select, which the article says appeals more to engineers than everyday users.
- **Pattern of instability**: Author frames this as consistent with Google's long-running habit of reorganizing AI/product features rather than keeping them stable.
- **Prior coverage**: 9to5Google first reported the Gems phase-out over the preceding weekend.
