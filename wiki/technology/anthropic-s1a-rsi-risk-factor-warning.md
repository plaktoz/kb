## Summary

Anthropic's revised S-1A adds risk-factor language saying it may imminently meet its [[Responsible Scaling Policy]] definition of "dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI", and that it has previously failed to recognize capability leaps from AI-assisted R&D until after the fact. No current model is designated as crossing the threshold, and Anthropic says it is considering policy updates to keep humans in control of frontier model development. TechCrunch notes that an S-1 is both disclosure and marketing, but argues the claim deserves to be taken seriously.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Recursive Self-Improvement]] (RSI)**: The point at which AI becomes so good at training itself that capabilities accelerate dramatically; seen as peril by doomers and promise by enthusiasts.
- **[[Anthropic]] S-1A**: The revised IPO filing whose Risk Factors section contains the new RSI statements.
- **[[Responsible Scaling Policy]]**: Anthropic's framework for monitoring and mitigating risks, including the "dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI" threshold.
- **AI-Accelerated R&D**: Using AI systems within Anthropic's own research, which may outpace safety, alignment and security measures.
- **Human Oversight**: Anthropic does not expect to safely let Claude autonomously build successive frontier models without meaningful human supervision.
- **[[Julie Bort]]**: TechCrunch author who frames the disclosure and its skeptical reading.

## Key Takeaways

- **Imminent Threshold**: Internal estimates suggest Anthropic may soon meet its RSI definition.
- **Not Yet Crossed**: No current model is designated as exceeding the threshold.
- **Delayed Recognition**: Past AI-built models showed capability leaps not recognized at the time.
- **Safety Lag**: Capabilities may advance faster than safety, alignment and security measures.
- **Human Control**: Anthropic is considering RSP updates to keep humans in frontier development.
- **Skeptic View**: An S-1 is both disclosure and marketing.
