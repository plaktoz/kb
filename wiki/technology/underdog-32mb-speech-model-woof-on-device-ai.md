## Summary
Startup Underdog released a browser game demoing Woof, a 31-million-parameter, 32MB open speech-generation model that runs offline on most devices. It follows Bark, a 167MB speech-to-text model, as part of a push for private, locally run, American-made open models. The founder predicts today's most capable models will run on devices people already own within months.

## Core Concepts
- [[Underdog]]: startup building small open models for [[Edge AI]] devices, with a waitlisted local app.
- [[Woof Speech Model]]: 31M-parameter text-to-speech model, 32MB, runs in the browser without internet.
- [[Bark Speech-to-Text Model]]: 167MB transcription and dictation model released weeks earlier.
- [[Sigil Wen]]: founder, who wanted a self-built alternative to cloud notetakers like Granola.
- [[Open-Weight Models]] and [[Private AI]]: user-owned data and local inference as the pitch against cloud providers.

## Key Takeaways
- **Tiny Footprint**: Woof is only 32MB and 31 million parameters.
- **Offline Operation**: The model runs in-browser on most devices without internet access.
- **Open Release**: The speech model is open for anyone to download.
- **Companion Model**: Bark, a 167MB speech-to-text model, launched weeks earlier.
- **Larger Option**: A 27-billion-parameter model runs on laptops.
- **Founder Claim**: Today's most capable models will run on owned devices within months.
- **Realistic Scope**: Small models likely won't replace big ones but may drive local assistants.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Underdog demonstrating with its Woof game?
**A**: That a 32MB, 31-million-parameter speech-generation model can run in the browser on most devices without an internet connection. The model is open for anyone to download.

**Q2**: What motivated the founder to build on-device models?
**A**: Sigil Wen wanted his own system using AI models that run on users' devices and let them own their data, instead of notetakers like Granola.

**Q3**: Where could such small models be useful, according to the article?
**A**: They likely won't replace bigger models, but may be enough to drive local assistant experiences or create personalized podcasts.
