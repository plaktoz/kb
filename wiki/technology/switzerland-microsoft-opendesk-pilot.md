## Summary

Switzerland's federal government has launched a CHF 9 million pilot to replace [[Microsoft 365]] with the open-source [[openDesk]] platform on 3,000 workstations (~7% of the federal workforce), targeting completion by end of 2027. The initiative is driven by data sovereignty concerns under US cloud law, single-vendor lock-in risk, and rising proprietary licensing costs. Success could trigger full expansion to all 54,000 federal workstations.

## Core Concepts

- **[[openDesk]]**: A German open-source collaboration suite used as the Microsoft 365 replacement; covers document editing and email but has limitations for large-scale video conferencing.
- **[[Digital Sovereignty]]**: The policy goal of keeping government data and infrastructure free from foreign legal jurisdiction and vendor dependency.
- **[[EMBAG Law]]**: Switzerland's 2024 law requiring federal agencies to publish government software as open source.
- **Proof-of-Concept (PoC BOSS)**: A 172-employee trial completed September 3, 2026, that validated openDesk's core functionality before the broader pilot launch.
- **[[Vendor Lock-in]]**: The strategic risk of dependence on a single proprietary vendor for critical infrastructure.
- **[[Microsoft]]**: Incumbent provider being displaced; investing CHF 325M+ in Swiss AI and cloud infrastructure partly to address sovereignty concerns.

## Key Takeaways

- **Scope**: 3,000 workstations now; potential expansion to 54,000 if pilot succeeds.
- **Timeline**: Full migration targeted by end of 2027; military Cyber Command aims to complete by October 2026.
- **Parallel deployment**: openDesk runs alongside Microsoft 365 — not an outright cutover.
- **Three sovereignty drivers**: US cloud law exposure, single-vendor continuity risk, rising licensing costs.
- **Legal mandate**: EMBAG Law (2024) and December 2025 federal declaration make open source and digital sovereignty policy.
- **Precedent**: France and Germany have pursued similar open-source migration initiatives.
- **Microsoft counter**: CHF 325M+ Swiss investment in AI/cloud infrastructure to retain government trust.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Vendor Lock-in]]**: Switzerland's reliance on Microsoft 365 across 54,000 workstations exemplifies how switching costs accumulate over time — the pilot's parallel-run strategy is a direct response to this trap.
- **[[Optionality]]**: Running openDesk alongside Microsoft 365 rather than replacing it outright preserves the ability to reverse course, reflecting a classic option-value approach to high-stakes infrastructure change.
