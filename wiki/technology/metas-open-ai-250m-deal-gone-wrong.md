## Summary
Meta released Glimmer, an open-weight AI model anyone can download and run locally, alongside a Zuckerberg manifesto arguing AI should be "for everyone" — while keeping its more powerful Muse Spark model locked behind APIs. The same news cycle covered a collapsed $250M deal between VideoVerse and Minute Media amid allegedly forged documents and lawsuits.

## Core Concepts
- [[Open-Weight Models]] (Glimmer) vs. closed API-gated models (Muse Spark) as Meta's dual AI strategy.
- [[AI Democratization Rhetoric]] and its tension with keeping flagship models closed.
- Deal collapse risk illustrated by the [[VideoVerse]]–[[Minute Media]] $250M acquisition falling apart amid forged documents.

## Key Takeaways
- **Dual-Track Release**: Meta released open-weight Glimmer while keeping the more capable Muse Spark API-only.
- **Manifesto**: Zuckerberg published a 6,500-word letter arguing AI should be broadly accessible, not controlled by a few labs.
- **Deal Collapse**: A $250M VideoVerse–Minute Media acquisition fell apart amid allegedly forged documents and multiple lawsuits.
- **Related Threads**: Anthropic's text watermarking drew user pushback; Amazon's Texas data center raised grid-strain concerns.

**Q2**: What deal collapse does the episode cover, and why?

**Q3**: What's the broader implication for evaluating "open AI" claims?
