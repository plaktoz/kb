## Summary
LinkedIn is rolling out a "Seems like AI slop" button so users can flag posts that appear to be low-quality, AI-generated content, as part of a broader push to reduce "AI slop" on the platform. The move mirrors an industry-wide response to rising bot and AI content volume, following similar moves by Substack and Pangram and the shutdown of Digg's Reddit competitor.

## Core Concepts
- [[AI Slop]] — low-quality, artificially generated content flooding online platforms
- [[LinkedIn]] introducing a user-facing reporting button and new classifiers
- [[Content Moderation]] via user signal collection and automation defenses
- [[Bot Traffic]] — Cloudflare reports bot traffic now exceeds human-generated web traffic
- [[Substack]] partnering with Pangram to flag AI-written content
- [[Digg]] shut down its Reddit competitor due to bot flooding
- Hari Srinivasan, LinkedIn's Chief Product Officer

## Key Takeaways
- **Report Button**: New "Seems like AI slop" button lets users flag suspected AI content.
- **Automation Defenses**: LinkedIn blocks "hundreds of thousands" of automated comment attempts daily.
- **New Classifiers**: AI models will identify slop to reduce it in outside-network recommendations.
- **Private Flagging**: Dashboards will privately notify users whose posts seem inauthentic.
- **Feature Swap**: "Enhance your post" AI-writing tool is replaced by a proofreading-only feature.
- **Industry Signal**: Substack-Pangram partnership and Pangram's $9M raise show a broader anti-slop trend.
- **Bot Milestone**: Cloudflare says bot traffic now exceeds human traffic on the web, faster than predicted.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models
- **[[Tragedy of the Commons]]**: Cheap AI content generation lets individual posters over-exploit shared feed attention, degrading the platform's quality for everyone.

## 🃏 Review Questions
**Q1**: What is the core problem LinkedIn's new "Seems like AI slop" button addresses?
**A**: It lets users flag posts that appear to be low-quality AI-generated content, helping LinkedIn train its models to identify and reduce such "slop."

**Q2**: What data point illustrates how widespread the bot/AI content problem has become on the web?
**A**: Cloudflare reported that bot traffic now exceeds human-generated traffic on the web, a milestone reached faster than previously predicted.

**Q3**: How does LinkedIn plan to help users who use AI to merely refine (not fully generate) their posts?
**A**: LinkedIn will privately flag in a user's dashboard when their content seems inauthentic due to heavy AI use, aiming to help them improve their own writing rather than penalize them.
