## Summary
The author, writing from inDrive, argues the most durable AI advantage comes from deliberately preserving human agency: automate the repeatable, generate insights, and leave irreversible decisions to humans. Human-in-the-loop is framed as the architecture of trust rather than a phase to automate away, since models are ready long before organizations trust them.

## Core Concepts
- [[Human Agency]]: systems should help people decide, not decide for them.
- [[Human-in-the-Loop]]: the architecture of trust at the scale of millions of decisions.
- [[Agentic AI]]: autonomous systems that reason, plan, and act on users' behalf.
- [[AI as Advisor, Not Verdict]]: dynamic pricing shown as a suggestion the rider can counter.
- [[AI as Filter]]: automation screens at scale; ambiguous cases escalate to humans.
- [[Reversibility]] of decisions: cheap-to-correct decisions suit automation; trust, livelihood, or safety decisions do not.
- [[inDrive]]: ~40% of first-line support automated; 67% of car photos and 98% of avatars AI-processed.
- [[AI Trust Gap]]: 79% agent adoption but ~11% in production; 37% trust autonomous action.

## Key Takeaways
- **Core Rule**: Automate the repeatable, create insights, assist humans on the irreversible.
- **Inform, Don't Dictate**: Surface model output as a suggestion users can counter.
- **Routine Automation**: inDrive automates roughly 40% of first-line support conversations.
- **Safety at Scale**: AI processes 67% of car photos and 98% of driver avatars.
- **Humans Keep Ambiguity**: Permanent bans, disputes, appeals, and regulatory decisions stay with people.
- **Adoption Gap**: About 79% adopt agents, yet only ~11% of deployments reach production.
- **Governance Blocks Scale**: McKinsey finds security and governance concerns hold back nearly two-thirds of companies.
- **Trust Lags Accuracy**: 48% trust AI accuracy, but only 37% trust it to act autonomously.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Match autonomy to reversibility** — Where a wrong decision is cheaply corrected, automate; where it could destroy trust, livelihood, or safety, keep human judgment in control. · *model:* [[Reversibility Principle]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the author's core principle for deploying AI?
**A**: Automate the repeatable, create insights, and assist humans in making decisions on the irreversible. Systems should help people decide more efficiently rather than decide for them.

**Q2**: What data does the article use to show a gap between AI capability and organizational trust?
**A**: 2026 surveys put enterprise agent adoption near 79% but only ~11% of deployments in production. Just 37% of organizations trust AI to act autonomously, even though 48% trust its accuracy.

**Q3**: How should a company decide which decisions to keep with humans?
**A**: Use automation as a filter, and send ambiguous or high-stakes cases such as permanent bans, disputes, and appeals to humans. The context is richer than data captures and errors fall on someone's livelihood.
