## Summary

Domain expertise — not generic prompting tricks — is the critical skill for extracting value from LLMs. Using [[Terence Tao]]'s publicly documented ChatGPT sessions as evidence, the author demonstrates that experts get qualitatively different (and better) results because they can steer the model, catch errors, and articulate exactly what they want. The core bottleneck is the human, not the model.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Domain Expertise]] as force multiplier**: Experts use LLMs to amplify existing knowledge rather than replace missing knowledge.
- **[[Terence Tao]] example**: Tao's brief, targeted prompts and willingness to redirect the model when answers seemed off illustrate expert-level LLM use — none of it replicable without deep mathematical knowledge.
- **Mental model of a codebase**: The author's own programming experience shows that knowing the codebase lets you push the [[LLM]] harder — questioning suggestions and grounding prompts in specifics.
- **Floor vs. force multiplier**: Non-experts use LLMs as a floor (getting something useful); experts use them as a ceiling-raiser.
- **[[Prompt Engineering]]**: Reframed — effective prompting is downstream of domain knowledge, not a standalone skill.

## Key Takeaways

- **Non-expert use**: LLMs provide a floor — useful output, but generic.
- **Expert use**: LLMs become a force multiplier, enabling better-than-human-alone output.
- **Human bottleneck**: The hard part is articulating what kind of solution you actually want.
- **Durable value of expertise**: Domain knowledge stays valuable as models improve — knowing what to ask for doesn't commoditize.
- **Redirecting the model**: Experts actively steer LLMs away from wrong directions; non-experts follow the model's lead.
- **Brief, targeted prompts**: Expert prompts are often shorter but more precise, grounded in deep context.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Leverage]]**: Domain knowledge is the fulcrum — the same LLM tool produces asymmetrically different results depending on the expertise applied to it, exactly as a lever produces different mechanical advantage depending on placement.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Reducing LLM skill to "prompting tricks" mistakes a proxy (prompt wording) for the underlying driver (domain knowledge), leading practitioners to optimize the wrong variable.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central claim of "LLMs Reward Expertise"?
**A**: Domain expertise is the critical skill for getting value from LLMs — not generic prompting tricks — because experts can steer the model, verify its outputs, and articulate exactly what solution they need.

**Q2**: What evidence does the author use to illustrate expert vs. non-expert LLM use?
**A**: Terence Tao's ChatGPT sessions on a counterexample to the Jacobian Conjecture, where Tao's brief, targeted messages and active redirection of the model were entirely dependent on his deep mathematical knowledge.

**Q3**: Why will domain knowledge remain valuable even as LLM models improve?
**A**: Extracting the right output requires knowing what to ask for — that judgment is a function of expertise, not model capability, so improving models does not eliminate the human bottleneck.
