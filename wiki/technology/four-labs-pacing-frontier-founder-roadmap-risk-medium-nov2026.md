## Summary

Emma Bennett argues that the frontier labs' endorsement of [[Dario Amodei]]'s "We Must Pace the Frontier" essay is, for founders, a supply announcement rather than a safety debate. Product roadmaps that assumed a 2024-2025 pace of model improvement now sit under a vendor-declared ceiling, as OpenAI's decision to withhold GPT-6.1 Astra showed. She recommends building on the paced, generally available tier, keeping the frontier as an unpromised research line, writing the risk into contracts, and repricing the pitch deck.

## Core Concepts

- **[[We Must Pace the Frontier]]**: Amodei's September 12 essay calling for slower capability gains, independent evaluator access and coordination including China.
- **[[Frontier AI Labs]]**: [[Anthropic]], [[OpenAI]] and others whose endorsement turned the pace into a collective supply choice.
- **[[Roadmap Risk]]**: Product plans that assumed quarterly frontier jumps now depend on lab decisions.
- **[[Fast Follower Strategy]]**: The "first to wrap each new model" moat loses value as the gap to incumbents shrinks.
- **[[GPT-6.1 Astra]]**: Finished flagship model withheld by OpenAI; inference for its most capable models stopped.
- **[[Platform Risk]]**: Dependency on suppliers who can change pace or pull back previews.
- **[[Two-Plan Roadmap]]**: One plan sold on the paced tier, one prepared for a frontier move.

## Key Takeaways

- **Supply Decision**: Four labs agreeing to ship slower is a supply change for downstream founders.
- **Assumption Broken**: Roadmaps assumed predictable quarterly capability jumps; the rate is now a lab choice.
- **Astra Demonstration**: OpenAI shelved a finished model and halted inference on its most capable ones.
- **Moat Redefined**: Speed-to-wrap loses value when incumbents hear about releases simultaneously.
- **Build on Paced Tier**: Put only GA, supported, priced capabilities on the critical path.
- **Contract Language**: Capability commitments contingent on model availability should say so.
- **Counterweight**: Every's team called the essay a "book club," not a "fire drill."
- **Paced Upside**: Predictable frontiers reward building well and understanding the customer.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Dependence on a supplier transfers control of your plan to them** — When a critical input is controlled by a few vendors, their pace and pullbacks become your constraint, and the business should plan around what is reliably supplied. · *model:* [[Platform Risk]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about the labs' pacing announcement?
**A**: For founders it is a supply decision, not a safety story: the labs will ship their core input more slowly than they could. Roadmaps built on the 2024-2025 pace now have a vendor-declared ceiling.

**Q2**: What evidence shows pacing is real, and why does it hurt fast-follower startups?
**A**: OpenAI declined to release GPT-6.1 Astra and stopped inference for its most capable models. Pacing narrows the gap between a capability appearing and incumbents absorbing it, so being first to wrap it is worth less.

**Q3**: What should a founder do in response?
**A**: Build on the paced GA tier, keep the frontier as an unpromised research line, add contract language for model-dependent features, and rewrite the "next-generation models will let us" slide. This means two plans: one to sell, one to prepare.
