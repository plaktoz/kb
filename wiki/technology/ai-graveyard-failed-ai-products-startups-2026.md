## Summary
TechCrunch keeps a running list of AI products and startups that shut down, pivoted, or missed expectations, from Relay and Notion Mail to Humane's AI Pin and Character.ai's bankruptcy. S&P Global Market Intelligence says about 42% of AI initiatives are abandoned by their corporate parents. The recurring causes are larger platforms absorbing standalone features, weak product-market fit, unsustainable costs, and legal or privacy backlash.

## Core Concepts
- [[Relay]]: AI workflow-automation tool, a Zapier alternative, shut down in August as OpenAI and Google built similar automation into their own tools.
- [[Character.ai]]: filed for bankruptcy in early October 2026 after lawsuits, a Disney cease-and-desist, and a reported $2.7 billion Google licensing deal; [[Disney]] is the opening bidder at $20 million for some IP.
- [[OpenAI]]: reversed its ChatGPT "super app" redesign, shut down Atlas (August 9) and Sora (March 2026), and folded Operator and DALL-E into [[ChatGPT]].
- [[Humane AI Pin]] and [[Rabbit R1]]: hyped AI hardware that struggled with performance and reviews.
- [[Microsoft Recall]] and [[Apple Intelligence]] Siri: delayed by privacy backlash and engineering problems, respectively.
- [[Notion Mail]], [[Huxe]], [[Yupp]], and [[Figgs AI]]: smaller products shut down by competition from larger platforms, weak fit, or free-tier costs.

## Key Takeaways
- **Abandonment Rate**: About 42% of AI initiatives are abandoned by their corporate parents (S&P Global).
- **Platform Absorption**: Relay, Notion Mail, and Huxe lost ground as bigger platforms built the same features.
- **OpenAI Consolidation**: Atlas, Operator, and DALL-E folded into ChatGPT; the "super app" redesign was rolled back.
- **Hardware Flops**: Humane raised $230M, then sold assets to HP for $116M.
- **Hype vs. Fit**: Rabbit sold 100,000 R1 units, but reviews called it unfinished.
- **Cost Pressure**: Sora and Figgs AI (over 1M users) struggled with operating costs.
- **Legal Fallout**: Character.ai faced teen-harm suits and Disney's cease-and-desist before bankruptcy.
- **Privacy Backlash**: Microsoft delayed Recall almost a year; a researcher later extracted its captured data.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Standalone features get absorbed by platforms that own the audience** — A product that is a feature of a larger platform's core offering loses its reason to exist once the platform builds the feature in. · *model:* [[Platform Risk]]
→ explains: Platform Absorption, OpenAI Consolidation

**T2. Early sales and attention do not prove a product works** — Hype and initial purchases can coexist with poor retention or weak performance; survival requires real product-market fit. · *model:* [[Product-Market Fit]]
→ explains: Hype vs. Fit, Hardware Flops

**T3. Usage that costs more to serve than it earns is not sustainable** — Free or heavily used services with high operating costs can fail despite large user numbers.
→ explains: Cost Pressure

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about AI product failures?
**A**: Many AI products and startups fail, with about 42% of corporate AI initiatives abandoned, often because larger platforms build similar features or because the product lacked fit, funding, or trust.

**Q2**: What happened to Relay and Notion Mail, and why?
**A**: Relay shut down in August because OpenAI, Google, and others built similar automation into their own tools. Notion Mail is shutting down because its users were handling email with separate AI agents instead.

**Q3**: What does the Rabbit R1 case imply for evaluating hyped AI products?
**A**: Strong initial sales (100,000 units) did not mean a strong product, since early reviews found it unfinished and unreliable. Sales figures alone are a poor signal of durability.
