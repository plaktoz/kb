## Summary

Jeff Su synthesizes six data-backed AI trends for 2026 sourced from McKinsey, Stanford, OpenAI, and Epoch AI, arguing that the competitive landscape has shifted from raw model performance to application-layer differentiation. AI workflows—not autonomous agents—are unlocking near-term economic value (~$3 trillion by 2030 per McKinsey), while context engineering and file management replace prompt obsession as the key user skill. Physical AI (autonomous vehicles, warehouse robots) is expanding rapidly, though humanoid robots remain roughly 15 years away.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Model Commoditization]]**: Frontier models are converging in quality; [[Epoch AI]] data shows hardware energy cost per token dropped 105,000x over 10 years, making powerful AI cheap and interchangeable.
- **[[AI Workflows]] vs. [[AI Agents]]**: Only ~10% of enterprise functions have scaled true agents (McKinsey), while 20% of enterprise AI use runs through workflow-specific tools like custom GPTs.
- **[[Context Engineering]]**: The shift from how you phrase requests to what context you provide—whoever holds your emails, docs, and calendar holds your attention (the "platform lock-in" war).
- **[[Technical Democratization]]**: MIT study confirms AI disproportionately helps lower-skill workers; 75% of enterprise users report completing tasks they literally could not before (OpenAI report).
- **[[Advertising in AI]]**: Ads are coming to ChatGPT in 2026; predicted to be display banners separate from conversation, not inline product recommendations.
- **[[Physical AI Agents]]**: Waymo has logged 100M+ autonomous miles (96% fewer crashes than humans); Amazon robots cut order-to-ship time by 78%; China leads global industrial robot deployment.
- **[[Andrej Karpathy]]** coined "the decade of agents" to push back on overblown near-term agent hype.
- **[[Jagged Frontier of AI]]**: Concept from [[Ethan Mollick]] (Wharton) — AI expertise is being reset; no one is the established expert yet, creating opportunity for fast learners.
- **[[OpenAI]]**, **[[Google Gemini]]**, **[[Anthropic]]** each hold a different competitive moat: mind share, distribution, and developer/enterprise specialization respectively.

## Key Takeaways

- **Model convergence**: No single model leads; pick based on ecosystem fit, not benchmarks.
- **Workflow over agents**: Redesigning one workflow unlocks more near-term value than chasing autonomy.
- **AI economic value**: McKinsey projects $3 trillion from workflow redesign by 2030.
- **Context is the new prompt**: AI fails not from bad wording but from missing company context.
- **Technical barrier dissolves**: Non-technical workers now build dashboards, scripts, and automations solo.
- **Ads incoming**: ChatGPT ads likely display-style banners, not conversation-embedded recommendations.
- **Physical AI is real**: Autonomous vehicles and warehouse robots already deployed at scale.
- **Humanoid robots**: MIT's [[Rodney Brooks]] estimates 15+ years before they enter daily life.
- **Commoditization analogy**: AI models becoming like electricity—ask what to use it for, not who's best.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Commoditization Curve]]**: As products converge on quality, competition shifts to distribution and integration—exactly what is happening as frontier model gaps narrow and platform lock-in becomes the moat.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Organizations chasing "AI adoption" metrics may deploy agents prematurely; McKinsey's data shows workflows outperform agents precisely because optimizing for true reliability beats optimizing for autonomy headlines.
