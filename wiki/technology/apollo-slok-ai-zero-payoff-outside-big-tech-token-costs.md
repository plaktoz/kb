## Summary
Apollo chief economist [[Torsten Slok]] reports that revenue per worker and profit margins are rising at the [[Magnificent Seven]] but flat across the other 493 [[S&P 500]] companies, so AI has delivered no visible financial payoff outside tech. Because providers charge by token, AI bills climb with usage and mainstream firms increasingly treat token spend as a cost center. Unless AI shows measurable gains in productivity, revenue or costs, the extra spending could squeeze margins and slow provider revenue.

## Core Concepts
- [[Apollo Global Management]]: Slok's Daily Spark analysis is the source of the central finding.
- [[Magnificent Seven]] vs. the S&P 493: Gains in revenue per worker and margins are concentrated in the former.
- [[Token Costs]]: Usage-based [[AI Pricing]] makes bills rise with requests and [[AI Agents]] workloads.
- [[AI Productivity]]: Slok finds no clear evidence AI boosts productivity growth.
- [[Cost Center]]: Mainstream firms now view token spending as a cost, not a growth driver.
- [[PwC India]] fintech survey: 84% of respondents had seen no measurable financial results from AI.
- [[Agentic Payments]]: 41% cite customer trust and adoption as the biggest scaling barrier.
- [[Nvidia]] Open Agent Safety Platform: A security stack to control AI agents.
- [[Sam Altman]]: Called token costs a "huge issue" for customers.

## Key Takeaways
- **Concentrated Gains**: Margins and revenue per worker rise at Mag 7, flat across 493 others.
- **Zero Payoff**: Slok says AI has delivered "zero financial payoff" outside tech.
- **Token Cost Pressure**: Usage-based billing grows with employee requests and agent workloads.
- **Cost Center View**: Established firms prefer cheaper models when they can do the job.
- **Supplier Risk**: Provider revenue depends on customers seeing productivity, revenue or cost gains.
- **Survey Evidence**: 84% of surveyed Indian fintechs report no measurable AI financial results.
- **Usage Curbs**: Microsoft, Meta and Amazon have moved to rein in AI usage.
- **Infrastructure Bill**: Amazon, Google, Meta and Microsoft have committed enormous sums to data centers, chips and power.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Spending persists only while measurable returns accompany it** — Customers keep paying for a technology at the same pace only if they see corresponding gains in productivity, revenue or operating costs; otherwise it becomes a cost center and weighs on margins. · *model:* [[Productivity Paradox]]
→ explains: Zero Payoff, Cost Center View, Supplier Risk

**T2. A supplier's revenue is its customers' cost** — Selling more compute and model access lifts provider sales while raising customer bills, so the boom is only durable if customer economics improve. 
→ explains: Token Cost Pressure, Supplier Risk, Usage Curbs

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Slok's central claim about AI and corporate America?
**A**: AI has delivered no financial payoff outside tech: revenue per worker and margins rose at the Magnificent Seven but stayed flat across the other 493 S&P 500 companies. He says the AI story will be decided in the S&P 493.

**Q2**: Why do token costs threaten corporate margins?
**A**: Many providers charge by token usage, so bills climb as employees make more requests and AI agents process data and handle more complex tasks. Without measurable business gains, the added spending weighs on margins.

**Q3**: What does this imply for AI providers and adopters?
**A**: Providers need customers to see productivity, revenue or cost gains to sustain spending, while adopters face the challenge of widening AI use without letting operating costs spiral.
