## Summary

[[Google]]'s new technique for producing [[Magic States]] — a "key bottleneck" consuming ~95% of a quantum computer's capacity — cuts error rates by a factor of 40, narrowing the gap to a quantum threat against [[Bitcoin]]. Research platform [[Bull Theory]] argues no existing machine can break Bitcoin's security, since it would take roughly 1,200–1,450 logical qubits versus today's best of ~70–96. Experts and [[Polymarket]] bettors see the threat as years away, but roughly 6.26 million BTC sit in quantum-vulnerable addresses with no community consensus on what to do.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Magic States]]**: A resource-intensive component of [[Quantum Computing]]; Google's technique reduces errors 40x at 99.9% accuracy but still needs larger-scale demonstration.
- **[[Logical Qubits]]**: [[Google Quantum AI]]'s March white paper estimated 1,200–1,450 logical qubits needed to break Bitcoin's security.
- **Current hardware**: Google's [[Willow Chip]] (Dec 2024) has 105 qubits; [[IBM]] reported 70 logical qubits; [[QuEra Computing]] ran algorithms with up to 96 logical qubits.
- **Roadmaps**: IBM targets 200 logical qubits by 2029 and 2,000 by 2033.
- **Attack vector**: Private keys could be derived from exposed [[Public Key Cryptography|public keys]] in 9 minutes, per Google.
- **Expert view**: [[Jameson Lopp]] of [[Casa]] estimates at least another decade before such a machine exists.
- **Prediction markets**: Polymarket assigns 1% odds by year-end and 11% by end of 2027.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Security is a moving gap, not a fixed state** — A cryptographic system is secure only as long as the capability required to break it exceeds attackers' available capability; engineering advances that cut resource requirements narrow that gap even when no break is yet possible.
