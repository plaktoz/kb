## Summary

[[Nominal]], an LA-based industrial software startup valued at $1 billion, is establishing a permanent Seattle office to tap the region's deep pool of aerospace and infrastructure engineering talent. The company's software helps engineers build, test, and validate complex hardware systems, serving defense, energy, robotics, and automotive customers. Seattle's expansion reflects a broader trend of AI-meets-physical-world companies clustering in the Pacific Northwest.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nominal]]**: Industrial software platform managing the end-to-end data supply chain for hardware engineering teams — from instrumentation through acquisition, storage, analysis, and reporting.
- **[[Industrial AI]]**: AI applications targeting the physical world — hardware validation, defense systems, robotics, and energy infrastructure — contrasted with purely digital-domain AI.
- **[[Founders Fund]]**: Venture capital firm that led Nominal's $80 million Series funding at a $1B valuation; also backs [[Armada]] (AI infrastructure).
- **[[Seattle Tech Ecosystem]]**: Growing hub for industrial and defense tech startups, anchored by aerospace heritage (Boeing legacy) and proximity to defense contractors; home to [[Overland AI]], [[Carbon Robotics]], [[Brinc]], and [[Stoke Space]].
- **[[Anduril Industries]]**: Defense tech company whose alumni network (including COO Matt Grimm) is influencing Nominal's Seattle market entry; Anduril has also expanded Seattle-area operations.
- **[[Reindustrialization]]**: Macro thesis driving Nominal's market opportunity — global demand for new industrial capacity in energy, defense, and AI data center infrastructure.

## Key Takeaways

- Nominal raised $80M at $1B valuation; 75 customers, ~half in defense sector.
- Seattle office: 11 employees now, targeting 20–25 by end of 2026, 40–50 by end of 2027.
- Head of Product Stephen Slattery (ex-Anduril) relocated from LA to lead Seattle office.
- Four of five traditional U.S. defense primes use Nominal's software.
- Growth sectors beyond defense: energy (nuclear, fusion, batteries), AI data center infrastructure.
- Seattle appeal: aerospace talent density, proximity to customers like Boeing and defense contractors.
- Anthropic, OpenAI, Armada, and Anduril have also expanded Seattle engineering presence recently.

## First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Talent Clustering]]**: Seattle's aerospace heritage created a self-reinforcing talent pool for hardware/infrastructure engineers — Nominal's CEO explicitly cites this density as irreplicable, illustrating why geographic agglomeration compounds over time rather than dispersing.
