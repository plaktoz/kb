## Summary
Meta released a new open-weight AI model, Muse Glimmer, alongside a lengthy essay from CEO Mark Zuckerberg arguing that open-sourcing AI is a fairer path than concentrating advanced AI control among a few institutions. He also called for faster U.S. energy buildout and defended AI "distillation" against Trump administration crackdowns on Chinese labs.

## Core Concepts
[[Meta]] released [[Muse Glimmer]] under a permissive open-source license, plus developer access to a more powerful model, [[Muse Spark]] 1.2. [[Mark Zuckerberg]] argues in his essay that if AI "superintelligence" is controlled by a small number of companies, governments, or even AI itself, the outcomes favor those institutions over individuals — an implicit contrast with [[OpenAI]] and [[Anthropic]]'s more closed approach. He calls for the U.S. to speed [[Energy Infrastructure]] buildout to compete with China, and defends [[Model Distillation]] as a legitimate form of learning, pushing back on the Trump administration's efforts to restrict Chinese labs like [[DeepSeek]] from using it.

## Key Takeaways
- **New models**: Muse Glimmer (lightweight, runs on a PC) is open-sourced; Muse Spark 1.2 gets developer API access.
- **Philosophical stance**: Zuckerberg frames open source as protecting individual power against institutional concentration of AI.
- **Veiled rivalry**: Essay implicitly critiques labs "focused on building AI for companies, governments, or other institutions" — read as OpenAI/Anthropic.
- **Policy ask**: Calls for faster U.S. energy capacity buildout to stay competitive with China on AI.
- **Distillation defense**: Argues distillation (training smaller models on stronger models' outputs) is legitimate learning, opposing crackdowns aimed at Chinese firms.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of Zuckerberg's essay?
**A**: Open-sourcing advanced AI is fairer and safer than letting a small number of institutions control it, because concentrated control naturally favors those institutions over individuals.

**Q2**: What specific models did Meta release, and how are they positioned?
**A**: Muse Glimmer, a lightweight open-source model that runs on a personal computer, plus developer access to the more powerful Muse Spark 1.2.

**Q3**: What policy implication does Zuckerberg draw regarding AI distillation?
**A**: He argues authorities should not treat distillation as harmful since "you can learn from anything you can observe," pushing back on U.S. efforts to restrict Chinese AI firms from the practice.
