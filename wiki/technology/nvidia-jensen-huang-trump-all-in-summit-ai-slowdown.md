## Summary

At the [[All-In Summit]] in Los Angeles, [[Nvidia]] CEO [[Jensen Huang]] received a live call from President [[Trump]] while onstage, with the discussion centering on [[Dario Amodei]]'s recent push to slow AI capability development. Huang broke from [[Elon Musk]] and [[Sam Altman]] — who have endorsed Amodei's position — firmly siding with Trump's dismissal of AI pacing concerns. A Gallup poll cited in the piece underscores that public resistance to data centers is largely practical (environmental and cost-of-living concerns), not ideological.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Jensen Huang]]**: Nvidia CEO; publicly rejected Amodei's call to slow AI development, calling the concern overblown and aligning with Trump's framing.
- **[[Dario Amodei]] Pacing Proposal**: Anthropic CEO's recent argument that AI capability advancement should be slowed to let safety work catch up — opposed by Huang and Trump at the summit.
- **[[All-In Summit]]**: Annual conference hosted by the [[All-In Podcast]] besties, including White House advisers [[David Sacks]] and [[David Friedberg]]; a platform where tech-industry and political power intersect.
- **[[Trump]] on AI Slowdown**: Characterized opposition to rapid AI development as a "hoax" and suggested it serves adversarial interests, particularly [[China]].
- **[[Data Center]] Public Opposition**: A Gallup poll found ~70% of Americans oppose local data center construction — over half due to environmental resource use, ~20% due to cost-of-living concerns.

## Key Takeaways

- **Huang's stance**: "We're not going to let that happen, sir" — Nvidia's CEO explicitly supports unrestricted AI acceleration.
- **Musk and Altman break from Huang**: Both have endorsed Amodei's call for pacing, an unusual cross-industry alignment.
- **Trump's framing**: Calls AI slowdown advocacy a "hoax" tied to political adversaries or China.
- **Public concern is practical**: 70% oppose local data centers; top reasons are environmental resource use and cost-of-living, not abstract AI fears.
- **Political venue**: Live Trump call during a summit co-hosted by White House advisers signals how intertwined AI industry and political power have become.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Regulatory Capture]] (inverted)**: Huang and Trump's opposition to pacing mirrors the regulatory-capture critique from the other direction — just as incumbents sometimes push regulation to slow challengers, dominant infrastructure players (Nvidia sells the GPUs) have an equally direct financial incentive to oppose any slowdown.
- **[[Overton Window]]**: The live Trump call at an industry summit — normalizing direct political intervention in AI acceleration debates — shifts the window of acceptable discourse, making pro-pacing positions harder to advance publicly.
