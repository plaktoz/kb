## Summary

Nvidia is negotiating a multi-billion-dollar equity investment in [[Perplexity]] that would push its valuation above $30 billion, extending Nvidia's pattern — already seen with Groq, Poolside, and Enfabrica — of directly bankrolling the companies that buy its chips rather than just selling to them.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nemotron Coalition]]**: Nvidia's group of open-model co-development partners, which already includes Perplexity alongside Mistral and Thinking Machines Lab.
- **[[Vendor Financing (AI)]]**: Nvidia extending its reach beyond chip sales by directly investing in the companies that consume its compute, blurring supplier and investor roles.
- **Model ownership split**: The article contrasts companies buying vs. building their own models — Thomson Reuters spent $40M over two years building legal AI on Alibaba's open Qwen weights rather than paying OpenAI or Anthropic monthly, while OpenAI is pushing Codex toward accountants and doctors with limited individual uptake so far.

## Key Takeaways

- **Deal size**: A Nvidia-Perplexity deal would value Perplexity above $30B, over 50% above its funding round a year ago.
- **Revenue growth**: Perplexity's annualized recurring revenue roughly tripled in 2026, from under $250M to over $750M.
- **Pattern repeats**: Nvidia has already backed Groq, Poolside, and Enfabrica in similar equity deals.
- **Parallel story**: Hugging Face was separately reported in acquisition talks at a $13B valuation — a company that once turned down a $500M Nvidia investment offer.
- **Codex adoption gap**: OpenAI's Codex sees under 1% usage among individual subscribers versus 98% usage internally.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Circular Financing (Vendor Financing)]]**: When a chip supplier funds the equity of its own customers, reported demand becomes harder to separate from financially engineered demand — the same dynamic already flagged in Nvidia's OpenAI data-center guarantee.
