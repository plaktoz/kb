## Summary

Rillet, an [[AI-Native]] accounting platform built for [[AI Agents]] rather than humans, raised $100M at a $1B valuation within 48 hours of an informal board update, driven by triple-digit revenue growth and 600 customers actively migrating off legacy ERP systems like Oracle and NetSuite.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI-Native Software]]**: Rillet was architected for AI agents to perform bookkeeping directly, with humans working alongside the agents rather than the agents assisting humans — a structural inversion of how most enterprise software adds AI as a feature layer.
- **[[Governance Layer for AI Agents]]**: A feature letting accountants audit every decision an AI agent makes — which numbers it pulled and how it calculated them — built only recently because agents became capable of reliable multi-step workflows over long time horizons.
- **[[Model Routing]]**: Customers can redirect Rillet's AI requests to their foundational model of choice (e.g., OpenAI or Anthropic), with a harness preventing those models from training on customer data.
- **Legacy ERP Displacement**: Rillet's growth comes from customers actively replacing Oracle, NetSuite, Sage Intacct, SAP, and Workday rather than piloting alongside them.

## Key Takeaways

- **48-hour unicorn round**: Existing investors Iconiq and Sequoia moved to close a new round within two days of a board update showing doubled quarterly annualized revenue.
- **Customer mix**: ~50% of customers migrated from Intuit, 30% from NetSuite/Sage Intacct, 20% from Oracle/SAP/Workday/Microsoft — signaling broad displacement across the ERP market, not one incumbent.
- **Structural driver**: A persistent US accountant shortage (declining accounting-degree completions since 2010; 61% of finance leaders report hiring difficulty) is pulling demand toward AI-assisted platforms rather than AI simply cutting headcount.
- **Regulatory constraint**: Public companies currently require a human to approve every AI-agent transaction — a compliance guardrail the company expects will evolve as the technology matures.
- **No net job-loss claim**: The BLS projects accounting-related roles to grow 5%+ (72,800 jobs) through 2034, with AI shifting accountants toward advisory/analytical work rather than eliminating the role.

**Q2**: What specific product feature addresses the trust gap in letting AI agents handle accounting?

**Q3**: What does Rillet's growth suggest about the broader enterprise software market?
**A**: Legacy ERP incumbents (Oracle, NetSuite, SAP, Workday) face real displacement risk from AI-native challengers, not just incremental feature competition — reinforcing why software stocks broadly dipped earlier in 2026 on AI disruption fears.
