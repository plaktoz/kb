## Summary

The Baeldung Design Patterns Series is a curated collection of guides organized into four categories: Creational, Structural, Behavioral, and Other Architectural patterns. Each pattern addresses a recurring design problem in object-oriented software with a proven solution. The series serves as an index and reference point linking to individual deep-dive articles.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Creational Patterns]]**: Patterns for object creation — [[Singleton]], [[Factory Method]], [[Abstract Factory]], [[Builder Pattern]], [[Prototype Pattern]].
- **[[Structural Patterns]]**: Patterns for object composition and relationships — [[Adapter Pattern]], [[Bridge Pattern]], [[Composite Pattern]], [[Decorator Pattern]], [[Facade Pattern]], [[Flyweight Pattern]], [[Proxy Pattern]].
- **[[Behavioral Patterns]]**: Patterns for object communication and responsibility — [[Observer Pattern]], [[Strategy Pattern]], [[Command Pattern]], [[Template Method]], [[Iterator Pattern]], [[Chain of Responsibility]], [[State Pattern]], [[Mediator Pattern]], [[Visitor Pattern]], [[Memento Pattern]].
- **[[Architectural Patterns]]**: Higher-level structural patterns — [[MVC]], [[CQRS]], [[Event Sourcing]], [[Repository Pattern]], [[Domain-Driven Design]].
- **[[Gang of Four]]**: The canonical source of the 23 design patterns from the 1994 book "Design Patterns: Elements of Reusable Object-Oriented Software."

## Key Takeaways

- Design patterns are reusable solutions to recurring design problems — vocabulary for communicating design intent.
- Creational patterns decouple object creation from usage — improves testability and flexibility.
- Structural patterns define how classes and objects compose into larger structures.
- Behavioral patterns clarify how objects communicate and distribute responsibility.
- Architectural patterns operate at a higher level, shaping entire application structures.
- Patterns are a starting point, not a prescription — over-engineering via forced patterns is a known anti-pattern.
