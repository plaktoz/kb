## Summary

[[Nvidia]] is negotiating to guarantee roughly $250 billion in financing for a 10-gigawatt [[OpenAI]] data center campus in Ohio, on top of a separate potential $350 billion GPU-purchase financing deal — a combined exposure of about $600 billion to a single customer that has never turned a profit. The structure has drawn sharp criticism as a form of [[Circular Financing]], since a chip supplier would effectively be underwriting its own customer's ability to buy its products.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Circular Financing]]** — Nvidia's balance sheet backstops OpenAI's debt, which OpenAI uses to buy Nvidia chips, raising concerns the AI capex boom is partly self-referential
- **[[Vendor Financing]]** — the $250B guarantee would be nearly six times larger than [[Google]]'s prior ~$44B data center rent guarantee, the previous industry high-water mark
- **[[Off-Balance-Sheet AI Commitments]]** — Big Tech's aggregate off-balance-sheet AI obligations are estimated at $1.65 trillion, with [[Meta]] alone carrying ~$420B
- **[[Safe Superintelligence]]** — Nvidia has also funded [[Ilya Sutskever]]'s AI lab, which in turn purchases Nvidia hardware, extending the same financing pattern

## Key Takeaways

- **Scale**: combined Nvidia exposure to the Ohio project could reach ~$600B across financing and chip-purchase guarantees
- **Credit workaround**: OpenAI lacks investment-grade credit, so Nvidia's balance sheet substitutes as collateral for lenders
- **Bear case**: investor Michael Burry increased his Nvidia short, calling the arrangement "circular" financing of OpenAI's own chip spending
- **Industry-wide**: AI infrastructure spending is projected to exceed $700B in 2026 and $3T cumulative through 2028
- **Still tentative**: negotiations remain early-stage with no signed agreement as of publication

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Circular Reference Risk]]**: when a supplier finances its own customer's purchases of its products, reported demand and revenue can inflate without corresponding external capital actually entering the system — the same structural pattern that concerned analysts around vendor financing in the dot-com telecom buildout.
