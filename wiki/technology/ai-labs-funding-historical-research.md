## Summary

Frontier AI models have reached a threshold where pairing them with professional historians could yield genuine advances in historical knowledge. The most tractable problems involve cryptography, cross-lingual text tracing, and connecting findings siloed across niche subfields. The author proposes three interventions: digitize archives, give historians free API access, and collaboratively identify "millennium problems" for history.

## Core Concepts

- [[Frontier AI Models]] — GPT-6 Sol, [[Opus 5.5]], and GPT-6 Astra are now capable enough to contribute meaningfully to historical scholarship.
- [[Digital Humanities]] — the intersection of computational methods and historical research, now supercharged by large language models.
- [[Archival Research]] — the process of studying primary historical sources; AI's tractability depends on how fully digitized these sources are.
- [[Cognitive Offloading]] — risk of AI substituting for historian reasoning rather than augmenting it; the author flags this as a failure mode.
- [[Enigma Cipher]] — WWII German encryption system; a 1941 message was cracked by Astra by surfacing archival notes experts had missed.
- [[John Dee]] — Elizabethan occultist; his *Liber Loagaeth* manuscript was analyzed by Astra, which found it largely nonsense with a pattern of laziness by scryer [[Edward Kelley]].
- [[Samuel Hartlib]] — 17th-century intellectual networker; Opus 5.5 analyzed his papers to identify a likely source Newton drew upon for alchemical knowledge.
- [[Newton]] — Isaac Newton's use of anagram-codes for ingredients was cross-referenced against Hartlib's papers, revealing a shared but differently encoded term.

## Key Takeaways

- **New capability threshold**: Frontier models now advance historical knowledge, not just process it.
- **Best-fit problems**: Fully digitized, multilingual, cryptographic, cross-subfield — clearly verifiable outcomes.
- **Enigma win**: GPT-6 Astra cracked a 1941 German message by surfacing archival notes experts missed.
- **Dee manuscript**: AI found *Liber Loagaeth* is largely nonsense; laziness pattern detected after a specific date.
- **Newton–Hartlib link**: Opus 5.5 identified a probable source Newton drew on via matching anagram-codes.
- **Charles V letters**: Partial decipherment validated model capability, even though letters were already decoded.
- **Three interventions**: Digitize archives; free API/compute for historians; define "millennium problems" for history.
- **Cognitive offloading risk**: AI as writing substitute = bad; AI as horizon-expander = genuinely mind-expanding.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Comparative Advantage]]**: Historians hold interpretive judgment and contextual expertise; AI holds multilingual recall and exhaustive pattern matching — pairing them exploits the comparative advantage of each rather than substituting one for the other.
- **[[Fermi Estimation]]**: The proposal to define "millennium problems" in history mirrors mathematics' Hilbert problems — decomposing a vast domain into tractable, falsifiable sub-problems so progress can be measured and celebrated.
