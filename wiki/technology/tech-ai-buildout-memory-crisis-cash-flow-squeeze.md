## Summary

Big Tech's Q2 2026 earnings show [[Amazon]], [[Alphabet]], and [[Tesla]] all reporting negative free cash flow while [[Meta Platforms]]'s cash generation dropped 91%, as a memory-chip supply crunch drives [[AI Capex]] costs even higher than already-aggressive plans. Wall Street's reaction diverged sharply — [[Microsoft]] rallied on efficient spending and Azure growth while [[Apple]] slid on a weak forecast tied to memory-driven price hikes — reflecting growing scrutiny of whether the AI buildout will pay off.

## Core Concepts

- [[AI Capex]] — projected to reach $765B across megacaps in 2026, rising to nearly $1.2 trillion by 2027 per [[Goldman Sachs]]
- [[Memory Chip Crisis]] — insatiable AI processor demand from a small set of memory vendors is driving costs up faster than hyperscalers expected
- [[Free Cash Flow]] — Amazon (-$7.6B trailing 12-month), Meta (-91% YoY), and Alphabet (negative for the first time ever) all under pressure from AI spend
- [[Micron]] — memory vendor benefiting from the shortage; [[Elon Musk]] publicly thanked Micron for a "significant allocation on reasonable terms"
- [[Open-Weight Models]] — Chinese AI labs' cheaper open-weight releases are narrowing the performance gap with [[OpenAI]] and [[Anthropic]], adding a competitive risk to the AI trade

## Key Takeaways

- **Amazon capex raised to $220B** for the year, the highest among the four hyperscalers
- **Alphabet's free cash flow went negative** for the first time in company history despite 82% cloud growth
- **Microsoft's best day since 2008** came from pairing an earnings beat with increased capex guidance, not spending less
- **Apple is uniquely exposed** to the memory crunch since it's core to every consumer device; further iPhone price hikes are expected
- **JPMorgan calls it "one big AI trade"**: Wall Street is separating AI winners from losers by scrutinizing capex-to-return ratios, not just revenue beats

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Narrative vs Numbers]]**: identical AI-spending increases produced opposite stock reactions (Microsoft up, Meta/Apple down) depending on whether the spend came with visible revenue proof — the market isn't pricing AI capex itself, it's pricing evidence of monetization.
