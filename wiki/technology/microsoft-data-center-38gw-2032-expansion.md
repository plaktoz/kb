## Summary

Microsoft plans to expand its global data center capacity more than threefold — from roughly 12 gigawatts to over 38 gigawatts by 2032 — to resolve severe server shortages. AI-dedicated capacity is expected to grow from ~2 GW today to roughly one-third of the 38 GW total. The push follows a 2025 pause on expansion that triggered cascading service disruptions across Microsoft's customer base.

## Core Concepts

- [[Microsoft]] scaling data center capacity from ~12 GW to 38+ GW by 2032 (owned + leased)
- [[AI Infrastructure]] buildout: AI-dedicated capacity growing from ~2 GW to ~12–13 GW
- [[Capital Expenditure]]: $145 billion spent last fiscal year on infrastructure
- [[Hyperscalers]] arms race: [[Alphabet]], [[Amazon]], and [[Meta]] collectively earmarked ~$2.4 trillion for computing infrastructure
- [[Intel]] CPU-heavy "East US 3" cluster near Atlanta coming online
- Regulatory headwinds: governors in Texas and New York halting new data center construction over energy and land-use concerns

## Key Takeaways

- **Capacity target**: Microsoft aims to surpass 38 GW total power by 2032.
- **AI share**: AI-dedicated capacity expected at roughly one-third of the 38 GW total.
- **Capex**: $145 billion in capital expenditures last fiscal year.
- **2025 pause fallout**: Temu defected to Oracle; GitHub suffered an eight-hour outage; Xbox capped cloud gaming.
- **East US 3**: New Atlanta-area cluster relies on Intel CPUs rather than GPUs.
- **Regulatory risk**: Texas and New York governors have halted new data center projects.
- **MSFT stock**: Up ~1.6% year-to-date at publication.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Bottleneck Principle]]**: Microsoft's 2025 capacity pause created a downstream bottleneck that surfaced across unrelated products (cloud deals, gaming, developer tools) — illustrating how shared infrastructure constraints propagate unpredictably through a product portfolio.
- **[[Arms Race Dynamics]]**: Hyperscalers collectively committing trillions to infrastructure creates a winner-takes-most dynamic where falling behind on capacity directly translates to lost enterprise deals, not just slower growth.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Microsoft's data center capacity target and timeline?
**A**: Microsoft aims to expand from roughly 12 gigawatts to over 38 gigawatts by 2032, with AI-dedicated capacity expected to reach about one-third of that total.

**Q2**: What operational disruptions resulted from Microsoft's 2025 capacity pause?
**A**: Temu signed a cloud deal with Oracle after Microsoft couldn't supply capacity; GitHub suffered an eight-hour outage rerouting traffic through rival AWS; Xbox capped cloud game streaming time.

**Q3**: What regulatory headwind could constrain Microsoft's expansion plans?
**A**: Governors in Texas and New York have moved to halt new data center construction projects due to public opposition over energy consumption and land use, introducing uncertainty into Microsoft's internal projections.
