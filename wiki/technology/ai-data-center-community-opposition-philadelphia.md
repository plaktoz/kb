## Summary

The AI data center construction boom is triggering organized community resistance in cities with histories of industrial harm, most visibly in Philadelphia where activists are pushing for a moratorium on new data centers. Proposed sites in neighborhoods already burdened by refinery pollution have galvanized environmental justice groups who link past industrial exposure to family illness and death. The opposition mirrors a growing national trend — moratoriums now in place in Denver, Indianapolis, Asheville, Charlotte, Reno, and New York — driven by concerns over energy consumption, air pollution from diesel backup generators, and lack of community consent.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Environmental Justice]]**: Communities like Grays Ferry in Philadelphia — already scarred by a now-closed oil refinery that exploded in 2019 — face proposed [[AI Data Center]] siting on top of existing industrial burden; activists such as [[Philly Thrive]] connect past pollution to documented cancer and illness clusters.
- **[[Data Center Moratoriums]]**: A coordinated national policy response; cities and states including Denver, Indianapolis, Asheville, Charlotte, Reno, and New York have adopted or proposed pauses on new large data center construction.
- **[[AI Energy Consumption]]**: [[BloombergNEF]] projects U.S. data centers could consume more natural gas than Germany and Japan combined by 2035 — nearly double prior estimates — making energy infrastructure a central flashpoint for local opposition.
- **[[Diesel Generator Pollution]]**: Pennsylvania data centers typically include hundreds of diesel backup engines; [[Clean Air Council]] attorney [[Annie Fox]] notes these generators are increasingly activated during grid stress events, adding sustained air quality harm.
- **[[Community Consent]] in Infrastructure Siting**: NY Governor Hochul's framing — "These data centers...should only be built in places that want them" — signals a shift toward consent-based siting norms for AI infrastructure.

## Key Takeaways

- **Philadelphia sites**: Two candidate locations identified, one in Grays Ferry — already polluted by former refinery.
- **Philly Thrive**: Environmental justice group whose leaders personally lost family members to refinery-linked illness.
- **BloombergNEF projection**: U.S. data centers may use more natural gas than Germany + Japan combined by 2035.
- **Diesel pollution**: Hundreds of diesel generators per facility; activated increasingly during grid stress.
- **Moratorium trend**: Denver, Indianapolis, Asheville, Charlotte, Reno, and NY have all adopted or proposed moratoriums.
- **Rally attendance**: Over 100 people attended the No Data Centers in Philly rally at City Hall on September 14, 2026.
- **NY precedent**: Governor Hochul's executive order halts new large data center permits statewide.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Cumulative Impact]]**: Grays Ferry illustrates how communities already above environmental burden thresholds are disproportionately targeted for new industrial siting — each new facility compounds existing harm rather than distributing it equitably.
- **[[Tragedy of the Commons]]**: Local air and grid capacity are shared resources; individual data center operators capturing cheap land and power impose costs (pollution, grid stress) that are borne collectively by nearby residents rather than priced into the project.
