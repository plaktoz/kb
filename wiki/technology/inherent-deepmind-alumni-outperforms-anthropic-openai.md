## Summary

London AI lab [[Inherent]], founded by [[Google DeepMind]] alumni, says its AI agent "Faraday" outperformed much larger models from [[Anthropic]] and [[OpenAI]] at independently replicating published scientific research findings, despite running on a comparatively tiny 27-billion-parameter model. The company credits reinforcement learning and a focus on "research taste" rather than accuracy alone.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Faraday (AI agent)]]**: Inherent's research-replication agent, built on the 27B-parameter [[Qwen 3.6]] model, benchmarked against Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 and OpenAI's GPT-5.5.
- **[[Research Taste]]**: Inherent's success metric beyond raw accuracy — an instinct for which experiments are worth running and how to design them well.
- **[[Reinforcement Learning]]**: Inherent's core training method, rewarding good outcomes rather than spelling out explicit rules, chosen to generalize better across scientific domains.
- **[[Paper Replication]]**: A standard human PhD-training exercise Inherent uses to benchmark AI agents, testing whether they can reproduce published findings without being told the answer.
- **[[Garden Leave]]**: The UK practice of barring departing employees from joining rivals for months, which Inherent's co-founder has publicly called to end.

## Key Takeaways

- Faraday beat larger Anthropic and OpenAI models at replicating research despite its small size.
- Inherent emerged from stealth weeks earlier with a $50 million seed round.
- The company prioritizes reinforcement learning over imitating documented scientific process.
- Inherent uses OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Codex for coding rather than building its own tool.
- Headcount, currently a dozen, is planned to grow to 20-25 by year-end.
