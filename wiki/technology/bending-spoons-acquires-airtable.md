## Summary

Bending Spoons agreed to acquire Airtable for $1.29 billion in cash ($2.25 billion implied equity value), its first acquisition since its own July 2026 Nasdaq IPO — a price roughly 89% below Airtable's $11 billion 2021 peak valuation, illustrating how sharply the market for high-growth SaaS companies has reset.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Bending Spoons]]**: A Milan-based software roll-up (Nasdaq: BSP) that acquires and operates established digital products indefinitely, with a portfolio spanning Vimeo, Evernote, WeTransfer, Eventbrite, Meetup, and AOL.
- **[[SaaS Valuation Reset]]**: Airtable's $1.29B sale price compares starkly to its $11B 2021 Series F valuation, reflecting the broader repricing of high-growth private software companies since the 2021 peak.
- **[[No-Code Database Platform]]**: Airtable functions as part database, part workflow engine, and part internal application platform — making it operationally difficult for customers to migrate away from.
- **[[AI-Native Data Layer]]**: The deal lands as buyers increasingly expect database platforms to support AI that understands operational context and acts across live records, not just forms and automations.

## Key Takeaways

- **Deal terms**: $1.29 billion cash purchase price; $2.25 billion implied equity value including Airtable's cash reserves.
- **Valuation gap**: Airtable's 2021 Series F valued it near $11 billion pre-money — an ~89% drop to the current sale price.
- **First acquisition post-IPO**: Bending Spoons raised $1.7 billion in its July 2026 Nasdaq IPO before pursuing this deal.
- **Still open**: No confirmed closing date, and no announced changes to Airtable's pricing, roadmap, or support as of the deal announcement.
- **Customer risk**: Airtable users are advised to inventory dependency on the platform (linked records, automations, integrations) rather than assume immediate disruption.
