## Summary

Waymo has rapidly expanded its robotaxi service from 3 to 15 U.S. cities in one year, now completing 500,000 paid rides weekly with a ~4,000-vehicle fleet. Its newest vehicle, the "Ojai" (a modified Zeekr RT minivan), is central to its cost-reduction and scaling strategy. Current Chinese-vehicle tariffs are partially offsetting the cost savings, but analysts project ~5,100 Ojai vehicles will be imported by year-end.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Waymo]]**: [[Alphabet]]'s autonomous vehicle subsidiary, now the dominant commercial robotaxi operator in the U.S.
- **[[Ojai robotaxi]]**: Waymo's 6th-generation self-driving vehicle — a modified [[Zeekr RT]] minivan manufactured by [[Geely]]-owned [[Zeekr]] in China, fitted with Waymo's sensor suite and [[Google Gemini]] AI assistant at an Arizona facility.
- **[[Autonomous Vehicle Fleet Scaling]]**: Waymo's geographic concentration strategy — ~80% of vehicles in California and Texas — prioritizing density in high-demand corridors before broader rollout.
- **[[Chinese-vehicle tariffs]]**: U.S. import tariffs on Chinese-built vehicles that partially erode the cost advantages of sourcing the Ojai from Zeekr.
- **[[MoffettNathanson]]**: Research firm projecting ~5,100 Ojai imports by end of 2026.

## Key Takeaways

- **Rapid City Expansion**: 3 cities in Sept 2024 → 15 U.S. cities by Sept 2026.
- **Ride Volume**: 500,000 paid rides per week at current scale.
- **Fleet Size**: ~4,000 vehicles total; ~80% in California and Texas.
- **Texas Surge**: Fleet grew 49% in three weeks to 1,102 vehicles as of Sept 24, 2026.
- **Ojai Vehicle**: Zeekr RT minivan stripped of Chinese connected-car tech, fitted with Waymo's 6th-gen system in Arizona.
- **Gemini Integration**: Google's [[Gemini AI]] serves as the in-car assistant for Ojai riders.
- **Tariff Headwind**: Chinese-vehicle import tariffs offset some cost savings from the Ojai supply chain.
- **Next Targets**: Florida and Las Vegas flagged as likely expansion markets.
- **Analyst Projection**: ~5,100 Ojai vehicles expected by year-end 2026.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Economies of Scale]]**: Waymo's strategy of concentrating fleet density in fewer cities before expanding mirrors the principle that unit economics improve with volume — the Ojai sourcing reflects a deliberate push down the cost curve once ride density validates a market.
- **[[Constraint Theory]]**: The tariff on Chinese-built vehicles represents a binding constraint that limits how fast cost reductions can translate to competitive pricing — identifying and resolving this bottleneck is the next lever in Waymo's scaling path.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Waymo's current scale in terms of cities served and weekly ride volume?
**A**: As of September 2026, Waymo operates in 15 U.S. cities and averages 500,000 paid rides per week with a ~4,000-vehicle fleet.

**Q2**: What is the Ojai, and how is it manufactured?
**A**: The Ojai is a modified Zeekr RT minivan built in China by Geely-owned Zeekr, shipped to the U.S. without Chinese connected-car technology, then outfitted with Waymo's 6th-generation self-driving system at an Arizona facility.

**Q3**: How do tariffs affect Waymo's Ojai-based cost strategy?
**A**: Current U.S. tariffs on Chinese-built vehicles offset the cost savings intended from sourcing the Ojai from Zeekr, limiting the financial benefit of the lower-cost supply chain in the near term.
