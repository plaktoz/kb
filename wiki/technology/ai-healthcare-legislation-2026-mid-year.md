## Summary
The Transparency Coalition's 2026 Mid-Year Report documents 84 new AI laws enacted in 27 states in the first half of 2026. Healthcare is the most active legislative battleground: 7 states restricted AI in medical authorization decisions, and 5 states banned AI therapy chatbots. The laws establish that AI can assist but not replace licensed human professionals in clinical decision-making.

## Core Concepts

- [[AI Healthcare Legislation]] — 14+ new laws in 2026 regulating AI use in medical settings
- [[Prior Authorization AI]] — 7 states now require human physician review of AI-generated insurance denials
- [[AI Therapy Chatbot Bans]] — Colorado, Maine, Rhode Island, Tennessee, Vermont prohibit AI from delivering standalone psychotherapy
- [[Patient Consent]] — Iowa requires verbal disclosure before AI transcribes clinical recordings
- [[Human-in-the-Loop]] — emerging legal standard in healthcare: AI can flag or assist, but humans must decide on coverage and clinical matters
- [[Transparency Coalition]] — organization tracking AI legislation across U.S. states

## Key Takeaways

- **84 AI laws in 27 states** in H1 2026 — fastest state-level AI legislation pace ever recorded
- **Core legal principle**: AI cannot be the *sole* basis for denying healthcare coverage or delivering therapy
- **Therapy chatbot bans follow a standard playbook**: prohibition grounded in existing professional licensing law
- **Iowa model**: recording consent + AI disclosure is a replicable framework for other states
- **2025 pioneers**: Illinois and Nevada were first to ban AI therapy chatbots; 2026 saw the model spread to 5 more states

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Accountability Gap]]**: Healthcare AI legislation is fundamentally about closing the accountability gap — when an AI denies a claim and someone is harmed, who is responsible? The laws force a named human professional into the decision loop.
