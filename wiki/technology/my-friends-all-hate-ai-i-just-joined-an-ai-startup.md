# My Friends All Hate AI; I Just Joined an AI Startup

## Summary

Rachel Thomas, [[fast.ai]] co-founder, explains her return to AI work at [[Answer.AI]] after burning out and earning a microbiology degree. She validates widespread frustration with AI slop, gameable metrics, and overhyped lab claims, but argues the major labs' vision isn't the only path forward. Her new project, [[SolveIt]], is built to keep users editing AI output directly rather than ceding control to an automated chatbot.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Slop]]**: Low-quality, AI-generated content flooding the internet and disrupting norms like student essays.
- **[[fast.ai]]**: The organization [[Rachel Thomas]] co-founded with [[Jeremy Howard]] in 2016.
- **[[Answer.AI]]**: fast.ai's evolution, where Thomas now works on [[SolveIt]].
- **[[SolveIt]]**: A tool named after [[George Pólya]]'s 1945 problem-solving framework, designed for direct user editing of AI responses.
- **[[Center for Applied Data Ethics]]**: An institution Thomas founded at USF before leaving the field.
- **AI industry concentration**: The idea that a handful of labs ([[OpenAI]], [[Anthropic]], [[Google]], [[xAI]]) do not own the definition of AI.

## Key Takeaways

- **Legitimate Grievances**: AI slop, gameable ed-tech metrics, and skill atrophy are real problems.
- **Overhyped Claims**: AI lab executives routinely overstate current capabilities.
- **Career Arc**: Thomas left AI in 2023, earned an MS in Microbiology-Immunology, then returned.
- **Alternative Vision**: [[SolveIt]] keeps humans editing AI output instead of automating it away.
- **Core Belief**: Major AI labs "don't own AI" — thousands work outside that dominant narrative.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Circle of Control]]**: Thomas can't change how frontier labs hype capabilities or flood the internet with slop, so she redirects her effort toward what she can shape — building [[SolveIt]] around human control rather than automation.
- **[[Sturgeon's Law]]**: Her acknowledgment that most AI content and products are low-quality ("slop," gameable metrics) mirrors the idea that the bulk of any output in a fast-growing field is mediocre, which doesn't invalidate the value of the genuinely useful remainder.
