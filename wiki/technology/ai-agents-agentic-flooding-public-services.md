## Summary

AI tools are driving massive surges in public service applications globally, a pattern researcher Chris Schmitz terms "agentic flooding." The growth is consistent across 11 jurisdictions — flat submissions before 2022, then rapid acceleration — and is driven primarily by legitimate claimants whose administrative burden has been removed by AI. Schmitz frames this as an opportunity to rethink how public services are structured for the AI era, not a crisis.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Agentic Flooding]]**: The phenomenon where [[AI Agents]] dramatically increase the volume of public service complaints, petitions, and applications by lowering the administrative friction for eligible claimants.
- **Reduced Friction Effect**: Previously complex paperwork deterred entitled claimants; AI removes that barrier, surfacing latent demand that was always there.
- **[[Public Services]] Redesign**: The surge signals a need to fundamentally restructure government service capacity and intake systems for an AI-assisted world.
- **Legitimacy of Claims**: Schmitz's 84-case study found the vast majority of AI-assisted submissions came from people genuinely entitled to what they were claiming — distinguishing agentic flooding from spam.

## Key Takeaways

- **UK Housing Ombudsman**: Complaints more than doubled since ChatGPT launch (2,600 → 7,000+).
- **CFPB**: Saw 5x complaint growth in the same period.
- **Global Pattern**: Similar surges in Brazilian and German government petitions.
- **Research Scope**: 84 cases across 11 jurisdictions, presented at AI Ethics and Society conference.
- **Not Spam**: The flood is legitimate demand — not abuse — unlocked by lower friction.
- **Structural Implication**: Public services must scale capacity or redesign intake to match AI-era demand.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Latent Demand]]**: The complaints were always there; AI didn't create new demand but revealed suppressed demand that paperwork complexity was hiding. The surge is a measurement artifact of friction removal, not a genuine spike in grievances.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]] (inverted)**: Systems designed with friction as an implicit capacity control will break when that friction is removed — the original metric (submission volume) was never the true signal of service need.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is "agentic flooding" and what evidence supports it?
**A**: Agentic flooding is the pattern where AI tools cause massive increases in public service submissions; evidence includes the UK housing ombudsman doubling complaints (2,600 → 7,000+) and the CFPB seeing 5x growth, consistent across 84 cases in 11 jurisdictions.

**Q2**: Why are the AI-assisted submissions considered legitimate rather than spam?
**A**: Researcher Chris Schmitz found that "the vast majority of cases are people who are entitled to claim for something, claiming for that thing" — AI is surfacing latent demand from genuine claimants, not fabricating fraudulent claims.

**Q3**: How should governments respond to agentic flooding?
**A**: Schmitz argues it should prompt a fundamental rethinking of how public services are structured for the AI era, framing it as an opportunity to better serve entitled claimants rather than a crisis to suppress.
