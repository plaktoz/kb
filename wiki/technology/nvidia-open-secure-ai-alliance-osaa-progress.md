## Summary

The week-old [[Open Secure AI Alliance]] (OSAA), an industry group spearheaded by Nvidia that has already grown to over 120 companies, launched a working group called the Shared AI Findings Exchange (SAFE) to publish shared AI-cybersecurity incident-reporting guidelines. Notably, Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google have not joined, despite OpenAI and Google having signed the original open letter that spawned the alliance.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Open Secure AI Alliance]] (OSAA)**: An Nvidia-led coalition of 120+ companies (including Adobe, BlackRock, Cisco, Intel, Microsoft, Visa) formed to promote open-source approaches to AI safety and security.
- **[[Shared AI Findings Exchange]] (SAFE)**: OSAA's working group, managed by [[The Linux Foundation]], focused on confidential AI-cybersecurity incident reporting and blame-free post-incident analysis.
- **Open vs. Closed AI Safety**: The group's core thesis — "openness may be one of the most important paths to AI safety and security" — stands in contrast to the more closed approaches of Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google's frontier labs.

## Key Takeaways

- **Fast formation-to-action**: OSAA went from formation to publishing open-comment proposals within one week, developed at the Black Hat cybersecurity conference.
- **Member contributions**: Nvidia (Garak vulnerability scanner), Okta (agent identity), Red Hat (agent governance), Amazon (Strands Agents, Cedar authorization language).
- **Notable absences**: Anthropic never signed the founding letter; OpenAI and Google signed the letter but have not joined the alliance itself, despite releasing open-weight models of their own.
- **Context**: The alliance follows reports the Trump administration considered banning Chinese open-weight models, which triggered industry pushback via a 200+ company open letter.
