## Summary
A Bank of Korea survey finds South Korean workers spend about $2.1 billion a year of their own money on generative AI and save far more time than last year, yet the correlation between time saved and throughput is only 0.08 and total factor productivity is not rising notably. Researchers say individual efficiency gains need process, organizational and workforce changes to reach firm-level output.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Bank of Korea]] AI Diffusion Report**: "AI Diffusion and Productivity Effects: Wider, More Efficient, but Still Shallow", based on 16,342 workers and a 2,024-person panel.
- **[[Generative AI]] Adoption**: Work-related use rose from 51.8% to 66.5%, the highest among compared countries (US 43.0%).
- **[[Total Factor Productivity]]**: The observable indicator Oh Sam-il says is not increasing significantly.
- **[[Complementary Assets]]**: Compensation systems and organizational restructuring needed for technology to realize its potential.
- **[[Productivity Paradox]]**: Time savings do not convert into output, a diagnosis echoed by Fed Chair [[Kevin Warsh]].
- **Shallow Use**: Workers with more core tasks use AI less, though quality improves when they do.

## Key Takeaways
- **Time Saved**: Work-time reduction rose from 3.8% to 9.2%; 1.5 to 3.7 hours weekly.
- **Personal Cost**: 28.4% of AI-using workers pay subscriptions themselves, about $37 a month.
- **Annual Spend**: About 2.8 trillion won (roughly $2.1 billion) borne by workers.
- **Weak Link**: Time-savings correlation with throughput is 0.08; with quality 0.09.
- **Where Time Goes**: 63.1% to core or extra work; 23.7% to non-work uses.
- **Potential Output**: Maximum boost if all saved time is reinvested rose from 1.0% to 2.6%.
- **US Parallel**: 65% of US AI-adopting firms use it for three or fewer tasks.
- **Policy Focus**: Raise quality of use and embed AI in core tasks, not just diffusion.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Individual efficiency gains convert to organizational output only through complementary changes** — Faster work yields no extra output if the workload stays fixed; process redesign, structure, and redeployment must accompany the technology. · *model:* [[Complementary Assets]]
→ explains: Weak Link, Where Time Goes, Policy Focus

**T2. Productivity effects of a general-purpose technology lag adoption** — Usage metrics can lead observable productivity indicators, so researchers expect the translation to arrive with a time lag. · *model:* [[Long and Variable Lags]]
→ explains: Time Saved, Potential Output, US Parallel

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the Bank of Korea report?
**A**: Korean workers use and pay for AI more than workers in comparable countries and save more time, but corporate productivity shows no clear improvement. The path from saved time to organizational output is not working yet.

**Q2**: What data point shows the gap between time saved and performance?
**A**: The correlation between time-savings rate and throughput increase is just 0.08 (up from 0.00), and 0.09 for quality improvement. About 23.7% of saved time goes to non-work uses.

**Q3**: What should companies and workers do in response?
**A**: Companies should embed AI into core tasks and change work processes and organization, while workers should learn task-suited usage and verify results. Policy should target quality of use rather than diffusion alone.
