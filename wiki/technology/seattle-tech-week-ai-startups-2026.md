## Summary

Seattle Tech Week drew over 250 events centered on AI, revealing a regional scene buzzing with job seekers and Bay Area investors scouting for opportunities. A standout insight emerged around enterprise AI: companies building on top of frontier models are using intelligence trained on web-scraped data, not the 95% of proprietary data locked inside their own organizations. Founders and investors also debated Seattle's identity as a tech hub, outcomes-based pricing models, and whether AI is actually saving time.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Foundation Models]] vs. Vertical AI**: [[Oumi]] CEO Manos Koukoumidis argued that enterprises building on frontier models are renting intelligence irrelevant to their own data — raising whether they are AI companies or application companies.
- **[[Enterprise Data Moat]]**: The 95% of organizational data not on the web represents a structural advantage that generic [[LLM]] training cannot access.
- **[[Outcomes-Based Pricing]]**: [[Madrona Venture Group]] partner Sabrina Albert described the shift from per-seat software licensing to paying for delivered outcomes as a fundamental pricing model change.
- **[[Seattle Tech Ecosystem]]**: [[PitchBook]] analysis undercounts Seattle because large engineering offices (e.g., [[OpenAI]], [[Anthropic]]) are counted as Bay Area companies despite significant local presence.
- **[[AWS Bedrock]]**: [[Clarify]] CEO Patrick Thompson shifted from [[Anthropic]] direct API to AWS Bedrock citing reliability, while running smaller models locally for low-level tasks.
- **[[Mistral AI]]**: CMO Brian Hall (ex-Microsoft, AWS, Google) expressed skepticism that AI will ultimately save time.

## Key Takeaways

- **Data Gap**: Frontier models trained on 5% of world data; enterprise's 95% sits unindexed internally.
- **AI vs. App**: Building on others' models may make you an app company, not an AI company.
- **Cost Escalation**: One startup's [[Anthropic]] bill tripled in three months before pivoting to [[AWS Bedrock]].
- **Headcount Paradox**: [[OpenAI]] legal still needs more people despite massive productivity gains from AI.
- **Outcomes Pricing**: SaaS seat model shifting toward pay-for-outcome as AI enables measurable delivery.
- **Seattle Undercounting**: PitchBook VC rankings miss Seattle's real footprint due to HQ attribution rules.
- **Long-Horizon Research**: Seattle's research institutions tackle 10-50 year problems — framed as a competitive edge, not a weakness.
- **Founder Focus**: Stop marketing yourself to investors; build a valuable company and investors will find you.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Build vs. Buy]]**: The frontier model dilemma is a classic build-vs-buy question at the intelligence layer — renting general capability vs. owning domain-specific intelligence trained on proprietary data.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When AI productivity becomes a metric to optimize, teams may generate outputs that look productive without delivering genuine business value — the concern implicit in Brian Hall's quip that "we're gonna laugh" about AI saving time.
