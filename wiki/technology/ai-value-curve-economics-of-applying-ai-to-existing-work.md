## Summary

McKinsey's AI value curve compares the cost of a task done today with the full cost of doing it with AI (tokens plus human review and rework), estimating a $2.52 trillion annual global opportunity. The strongest economics come from augmenting high-skilled, higher-wage work, spread across sectors beyond tech and finance, and they vary by market because labor costs differ. Because AI costs can swing the value pool sharply, leaders should manage cost alongside adoption and plan redeployment of freed-up people.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Value Curve]]**: Bottom-up model across Region x Industry x 831 occupations, comparing fully loaded labor cost per hour with AI cost per hour.
- **[[Effective Automatable Hours]]**: Automatable share (from [[McKinsey Global Institute]] 2026 estimates) times employment and productive hours.
- **Full cost of AI**: Token costs plus non-token costs such as human review, rework, and risk; one-time transformation spend is excluded.
- **Augmentation of expertise**: Executives, lawyers, financial managers, nurses, and developers gain most by offloading routine preparation, analysis, and drafting.
- **[[Local Economics of AI]]**: Same use case can pay off in the US but not where labor is cheaper, as with sales and administrative support.
- **[[AI Cost Sensitivity]]**: A 30% rise in AI cost cuts the US pool by $320B of $960B and the global pool from $2.52T to $1.67T.
- **[[AI FinOps]]**: Managing model routing, context length, token governance, and cost per useful outcome.
- **[[Agentic AI]] economics**: An agent creates value when its success probability exceeds verification time divided by human-do-it time (David Tepper).

## Key Takeaways

- **Value, Not Adoption**: Organizations track users, copilots, and tokens, not whether AI creates economic value.
- **Size of Prize**: About $2.52 trillion annual global opportunity from task-level automation or augmentation.
- **Sector Pools**: Financial services $530B, education $410B, tech/media/telecom $360B, healthcare $310B.
- **Expertise First**: Highest per-hour AI advantage sits in management, legal, healthcare, and technical roles.
- **Negative Cases**: Pharmacy technicians and entertainment attendants cost more with AI at today's wages.
- **Cost Fragility**: A 30% AI cost increase removes about a third of the global pool.
- **Rising Spend**: Enterprise LLM spending reportedly tripled over 12 months by end of 2025.
- **Redeploy Early**: Without redeployment targets, freed productivity disappears into slack.
- **Infrastructure Hurdle**: $7T AI capital by 2030 needs roughly $760B annual profit pools.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Economic viability of automation depends on the ratio of AI cost to the labor cost it replaces** — The same task is worth automating where labor is expensive and not where it is cheap, so value pools are local and shift when either cost moves. · *model:* [[Comparative Advantage]]
→ explains: Expertise First, Negative Cases, Cost Fragility

**T2. Value only counts net of verification and rework** — An AI output is worth its price only after the human effort to check and correct it is subtracted from the savings.
→ explains: Size of Prize, Negative Cases

**T3. Stress-test decisions against the opposite cost trajectory** — A business case assuming falling prices should be tested against rising prices, since small cost shifts swing the value pool sharply.
→ explains: Cost Fragility, Rising Spend

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does the AI value curve measure?
**A**: It compares the cost of a task as performed today with the full cost of doing it with AI, including human review and rework, to show where AI is economically worthwhile. McKinsey estimates a $2.52 trillion annual global opportunity.

**Q2**: How sensitive is the value pool to AI costs?
**A**: A 30% rise in effective AI cost removes $320 billion of the $960 billion US baseline and shrinks the global pool from $2.52 trillion to $1.67 trillion.

**Q3**: What should leaders do with these findings?
**A**: Map opportunities at task level, prioritize by area under the curve, manage AI costs alongside adoption, stress-test against cost reversal, and plan redeployment before deployment.
