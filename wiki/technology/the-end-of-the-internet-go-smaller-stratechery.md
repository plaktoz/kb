## Summary

The Stratechery author argues the open, public Internet he grew up with is effectively dead: blogs and social media were each undone by free distribution and infinite competition, and AI-driven hacking now makes running your own public site or app untenable. In each case the survivable answer is to go smaller, with subscriptions for niche writing, group chats for social life, and private apps built for oneself and trusted groups. He ends optimistically, citing Venkatesh Rao's "Cognitive Mercantilism" and de Tocqueville's associations to argue that many small, local markets open new room for creation.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Aggregation Theory]] and discovery**: Google won the blog era by offering discovery, since distribution was free; social media later became a second [[Aggregator]].
- **[[Subscription Business Model]]**: Niche blogging survives by limiting audience and charging, e.g. [[Andrew Sullivan]]'s low-substitutability writing.
- **[[Group Chats]]**: Social networking moved from public feeds to private, high-trust groups after public platforms became engagement-tuned entertainment (TikTok).
- **[[AI Cybersecurity Vulnerabilities]]**: A week of PHP remote-code-execution bugs, compromised CA keys, and Node.js, GitLab, Java, Cisco, LiteLLM and Railway issues.
- **[[Small Software]]**: AI lets individuals and teams build apps for themselves, making tiny audiences economically viable.
- **[[Cognitive Mercantilism]]**: [[Venkatesh Rao]]'s essay framing AI as freeing minds from "subsistence cognition", parallel to the end of subsistence farming.
- **[[Alexis de Tocqueville]] and associations**: American voluntary associations as the model for many small markets.

## Key Takeaways

- **Free Distribution**: When distribution is free, discovery becomes the most valuable part of the value chain.
- **Infinite Competition**: Openness gives everyone an opportunity, which means infinite competition and poor business economics.
- **Go Small (Blogs)**: Subscriptions work by deliberately limiting audience to those who pay for a niche.
- **Community Doesn't Scale**: Public social platforms drift toward engagement-tuned entertainment, while group chats keep trust.
- **AI Attackers**: AI has limitless time and substitutes iteration for cleverness, so vulnerabilities will surge.
- **Long-Run Optimism**: Software rewritten by AI and defended by agents should eventually be more secure.
- **Public Internet Decline**: It becomes a battle zone (social media) or wasteland (everything else); self-hosting is "madness".
- **Software for Few**: AI makes one-person or one-team software viable, a potentially larger market.
- **Many Local Markets**: Smallness at scale means infinite local markets rather than one global market.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Openness invites unlimited competition, so value migrates to what stays scarce** — When supply and distribution are free, abundance erodes returns for producers; value accrues to the scarce layer (discovery) or to deliberately scarce, hard-to-substitute offerings.
→ explains: Free Distribution, Infinite Competition, Go Small (Blogs)

**T2. Community quality degrades as audience scales** — Platforms open to everyone start out special and spiral toward engagement optimization; trust and connection survive only in bounded groups.
→ explains: Community Doesn't Scale, Many Local Markets

**T3. Exposure is bounded by attacker capacity** — Software built for "did it work?" rather than security was protected mainly because attackers were limited by human time and ingenuity; removing that limit makes any public surface risky.→ explains: AI Attackers, Public Internet Decline, Software for Few

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about the Internet's future?
**A**: The open, public Internet is dead, undone by free distribution, infinite competition, and AI-driven security threats. The viable response across blogs, social media, and software is to go smaller, toward private or niche groups.

**Q2**: Why does AI make running a public site or app untenable?
**A**: Previously attackers were limited by human time and ingenuity; AI has limitless time and can substitute iteration for cleverness. A single week saw PHP, CA key, Node.js, GitLab, Java, Cisco, LiteLLM, and Railway security issues.

**Q3**: How does the author suggest people should respond to AI-enabled software creation?
**A**: Build apps for yourself and a small group of trusted friends or colleagues rather than chasing the App Store, as Stratechery has done for podcast production, task tracking, and calendars. Small audiences become a larger market for software at scale, the "group chat era" of software.
