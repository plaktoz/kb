## Summary

Despite warnings from investors like [[Michael Burry]] and economist [[Dean Baker]], some Silicon Valley venture capitalists argue an AI bubble would be a net positive — the "capital destruction" of a bubble is, in their view, the price of building crucial infrastructure that a purely rational market would never finance. ## Core Concepts

- [[AI Bubble]] — contested framing: warning sign vs. necessary innovation mechanism
- [[Michael Burry]] — investor of "The Big Short" fame, has warned of an AI bubble
- [[Tomasz Tunguz]] — Theory Ventures investor arguing bubbles are "part of technology and innovation"
- [[Capital Destruction]] — the acceptable cost VCs cite for enabling breakthrough infrastructure buildouts

## Key Takeaways

- **Bull case for a bubble**: excitement and frothy capital are framed as necessary to fund risky, unproven infrastructure bets
- **"Organic financing myth"**: Touring Capital's Samir Kumar argues purely rational financing would never fund major platform shifts
- **Prominent skeptics**: Michael Burry, Dean Baker, and JPMorgan's Jamie Dimon have all raised bubble concerns
- **Article access limitation**: only the introductory section was retrievable; the piece's full reporting and counter-arguments require an NYT subscription

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Schumpeterian Creative Destruction]]**: The VC argument implicitly invokes Schumpeter's idea that speculative excess and failure are inseparable from genuine innovation cycles — bubbles misallocate capital in the short run but can leave behind infrastructure (railroads, fiber, now AI compute) that outlives the speculative mania that funded it.
