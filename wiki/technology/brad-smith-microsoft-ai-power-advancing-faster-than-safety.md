## Summary

[[Microsoft]] President and Vice Chair [[Brad Smith]] warned in a WSJ interview that the science of building more powerful AI models is advancing far faster than the science of aligning those models with human values. Smith called for mandatory "emergency brakes" in AI systems, particularly for critical infrastructure, and drew comparisons to airline and food safety regulations developed over decades. While dismissing extreme extinction-risk estimates, he cautioned that the technology gap between the global North and South could deepen inequality.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Brad Smith]]** — Microsoft President and Vice Chair; advocates for proactive AI safety governance and mandatory emergency brakes
- **[[AI Alignment]]** — the science of ensuring AI models act in accordance with human values; Smith's central concern is that it lags behind capability development
- **[[AI Safety Regulation]]** — Smith supports airline/food-safety-style mandatory standards, independent safety evaluations before frontier model releases, and product liability obligations
- **[[Emergency Brakes (AI)]]** — proposed mandatory failsafe mechanisms in AI systems, especially for critical infrastructure, to allow humans to halt or override AI actions
- **[[Digital Divide (AI)]]** — global disparity in AI adoption: 29% usage in the global North vs 16% in the global South; could worsen existing inequality
- **[[AI and the Generalist Professional]]** — Smith's vision of AI elevating workers from narrow repetitive tasks toward broader, multi-disciplinary problem-solving

## Key Takeaways

- **Capability-safety gap**: AI power science outpaces AI alignment science — Smith's core warning.
- **Emergency brakes**: Mandatory failsafes required for AI in critical infrastructure.
- **Regulatory analogy**: Airline and food safety rules took decades; AI governance needs similar rigor.
- **Independent evaluations**: Frontier models should face safety reviews before public release.
- **Product liability**: Existing law already forbids knowingly shipping unsafe products.
- **Extinction risk**: Smith rejects a 10% figure but prefers over-caution to under-action.
- **Positive use cases**: Faster wildfire detection and AI-assisted medical documentation cited.
- **Digital divide**: North/South AI adoption gap (29% vs 16%) risks widening global inequality.
- **Generalist revival**: AI could free workers from narrow specialization toward broader roles.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Collingridge Dilemma]]**: Smith's warning illustrates the classic bind — AI's impacts are hard to predict early (when it's still controllable) but hard to control later (when impacts are clear). His push for emergency brakes is a direct attempt to preserve controllability before the window closes.
- **[[Precautionary Principle]]**: "I'd rather have us worry too much than do too little" — Smith explicitly invokes precautionary logic, accepting false-positive costs (over-regulation) to guard against catastrophic false negatives (under-regulation of existential risk).
