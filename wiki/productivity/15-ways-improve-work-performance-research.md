## Summary

A research-backed guide to improving work performance centers on five core levers: setting specific SMART goals, prioritizing ruthlessly with the Eisenhower Matrix, protecting deep-focus time, communicating clearly, and seeking regular feedback. It grounds each recommendation in cited research rather than generic advice — for instance, specific goal-setting improves performance by 11–25% and it takes 23 minutes to regain focus after an interruption.

## Core Concepts

- **[[SMART Goals]]**: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound — replacing vague directives ("improve the customer experience") with concrete, trackable targets improves performance by 11–25% (Locke & Latham, *A Theory of Goal Setting & Task Performance*).
- **[[Eisenhower Matrix]]**: A four-quadrant prioritization tool (urgent/important) used to identify the top 3 weekly priorities and ruthlessly deprioritize low-leverage work.
- **[[Interruption Cost]]**: Employees are interrupted every 11 minutes on average and take 23 minutes to regain focus afterward (UC Irvine research) — the core justification for protecting 2–3 hours of daily uninterrupted work.
- **[[Pareto Principle]]** applied to knowledge work: roughly 20% of tasks generate 80% of meaningful results (Harvard Business Review).
- **[[Feedback Frequency Effect]]**: Employees who receive weekly feedback are 3.6x more likely to be highly engaged than those receiving only annual feedback (Gallup, 2021).

## Key Takeaways

- **Goal-setting alone improves performance by 11–25%** when goals are specific and challenging rather than vague.
- **Track goal-completion rate against a 70–80% target**: 100% completion suggests goals were too easy; below 60% suggests they're too ambitious or poorly scoped.
- **Review performance signals weekly or bi-weekly**, not monthly — monthly reviews create too long a feedback loop for meaningful course correction.
- **Productivity killers to avoid**: serial multitasking, perfectionism on low-leverage tasks, unbounded meetings, overcommitting without renegotiating, and working without a plan.
- **Tactical changes (time-blocking, eliminating distractions) show results within 2–3 weeks**; structural changes (goal alignment, skill development) typically take 60–90 days to show measurable impact.
