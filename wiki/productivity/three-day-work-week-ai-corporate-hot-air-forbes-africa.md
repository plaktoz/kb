## Summary

Business leaders predict AI will shrink the work week to about three days, but the author argues this is wishful thinking. Employers are unlikely to hand productivity gains back as time off, history shows past technologies lengthened working hours, and cutting 40% of hours requires roughly 67% more output per hour. The three-day week looks more like "corporate hot air" than the future of work.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Three-Day Working Week]]** — The prediction that [[AI Productivity]] could compress five days of work into three.
- **[[Four-Day Working Week]]** — Made to work by a small number of organizations; many found the practicalities harder than promised.
- **Predictors** — [[Jeff Bezos]], [[Eric Yuan]], [[Jamie Dimon]] and [[Bill Gates]] have floated three to three-and-a-half day weeks.
- **[[Productivity Gains]]** — Employers who spend millions on AI expect profit, output and cost returns.
- **[[Technology and Working Hours]]** — Computers, email and smartphones left people more connected and often working longer.

## Key Takeaways

- **Predictions**: Yuan says a three-day week within five years; Dimon says three-and-a-half days.
- **Gains capture**: More output per hour does not mean employers grant extra days off.
- **Historical precedent**: Computers, email and smartphones increased connectivity and, often, working hours.
- **Employer ROI**: Firms investing millions in AI expect higher profits, output and lower costs.
- **The maths**: Five days to three cuts hours 40%, needing about 67% more output per hour.
- **Current reality**: The five-day week remains entrenched; few organizations sustain four days.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Efficiency gains go to whoever pays for the technology** — Productivity improvements do not automatically become worker leisure; the party that funds the investment expects the return as profit, output or lower cost. · *model:* [[Principal-Agent Problem]]
→ explains: Gains capture, Employer ROI, Historical precedent

**T2. Output per hour must rise by the inverse of the hours cut** — Cutting hours by 40% while keeping total output requires about 1/0.6, or roughly 67% more output per hour.
→ explains: The maths, Current reality

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the author's central argument about a three-day work week?
**A**: It is unlikely anytime soon, despite predictions from business leaders. Employers will probably keep AI productivity gains rather than reward staff with extra days off.

**Q2**: What calculation shows how demanding a three-day week would be?
**A**: Moving from five days to three cuts hours by 40%. Employees would need to be roughly 67% more productive per hour to finish the same work.

**Q3**: What does the history of workplace technology imply for AI?
**A**: Computers, email and smartphones made tasks easier but left workers more connected and often working longer hours. AI could follow the same pattern, especially since firms expect a return on their AI spending.
