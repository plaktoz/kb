## Summary
A software developer and conference speaker shares seven counterintuitive productivity habits — centered on doing less rather than more — that she credits with preventing burnout while still shipping consistent work, framed as an antidote to "hustle culture."

## Core Concepts

- [[Sustainable Pace]] — "do less than you could," an old Scrum principle applied to personal life: finding a workload comfortable enough to sustain indefinitely, not one that only works because breaks are coming
- [[Protected Rest]] — deliberately not working weekends or after-hours admin tasks, on the premise that a fully relaxed brain is what creates room for creativity, not continuous grinding
- [[MVP Thinking Applied to Life]] — "deliver only what you're asked for," borrowing the minimum-viable-product concept from software to avoid unpaid, unrequested extra effort
- [[Embarrassingly Achievable Goals]] — deliberately setting goals so modest they can't trigger avoidance, as a direct countermeasure to procrastination-inducing, vague, oversized ambitions

## Key Takeaways

- **Do Less Than You Could**: pace work like a sustainable sprint rather than filling every open slot the moment it appears.
- **Don't Work on Weekends**: protecting true rest time (not just closing the laptop, but skipping admin/emails too) is what allows creative ideas to surface.
- **Plan Your Week in Advance**: dump tasks into an LLM to generate a prioritized daily plan, while still deliberately under-scheduling.
- **Set Embarrassingly Achievable Goals**: vague, huge goals trigger avoidance; concrete, realistic, self-dependent goals ("get an internship," not "become Staff Engineer") keep momentum.
- **Deliver Only What You're Asked For**: extra, unrequested polish is often wasted effort or actively counterproductive; agree on and charge for true scope additions.
- **Know What "Done" Means Before You Start**: define a completion criterion up front so open-ended goals ("learn JavaScript") don't drag on forever.
- **Reply to Small Things Immediately**: anything answerable in under five minutes should be handled within the hour to prevent backlog buildup.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Minimum Viable Product]]**: The author explicitly imports the MVP concept from software development into personal productivity — "deliver only what's asked for" mirrors shipping the smallest thing that satisfies the requirement rather than gold-plating.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Seven counterintuitive habits — most centered on doing and working less rather than more — sustain long-term productivity better than constant hustle, because they prevent the burnout that eventually collapses output entirely.

**Q2**: What specific mechanism does the author give for why weekend rest boosts (rather than costs) productivity?
**A**: A fully relaxed, unoccupied brain is what generates new ideas and creative momentum; filling every spare moment with admin work leaves no mental space for that process to happen.

**Q3**: How would someone apply the "embarrassingly achievable goals" tip in practice?
**A**: Replace a vague, intimidating goal ("become a Staff Engineer") with a concrete, self-dependent, achievable one ("get an internship" or "complete this tutorial and pass its test"), since a goal your brain doesn't perceive as achievable triggers avoidance rather than action.
