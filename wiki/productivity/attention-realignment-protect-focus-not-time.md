## Summary
Decades of time management have optimized the wrong resource: you can protect calendar hours and still lose them to a wandering mind, because attention, not time, determines where you actually spend your life. As AI makes intelligence abundant, the core problem shifts from information access to a "selection crisis" — deciding what deserves to enter our minds — and leaders, individuals, and organizations alike must treat attention like capital. Pereira argues that undivided human presence and deliberately preserved "cognitive wildernesses" will become the scarce, defining advantages of an AI-saturated future.

## Core Concepts

- [[Attention Management]] — reframing self-management from "how do I fit more in?" to "what deserves access to my consciousness?"
- [[Deep Work]] — [[Cal Newport]]'s claim that undistracted focus is becoming rarer exactly as it grows more valuable
- [[Poverty of Attention]] — [[Herbert A. Simon]]'s 1971 observation that "a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention"
- [[Selection Crisis]] — the modern problem is no longer access to answers but choosing what to attend to
- [[William James]] — "My experience is what I agree to attend to" (*The Principles of Psychology*, 1890); attention constructs perceived reality
- [[Continuous Partial Attention]] — [[Linda Stone]]'s term for being perpetually tethered to external inputs
- [[Outsourced Prioritization]] — when notifications and AI assistants, not you, decide what you think about next
- [[Organizational Culture]] — [[Edgar Schein]]: what leaders systematically attend to, measure, and control embeds culture more than mission statements
- [[Algorithmic Curation]] — AI filters that summarize, recommend, and personalize reality, reducing serendipity and weak signals
- [[Matthew Crawford]] — *The World Beyond Your Head*: attention as a shared resource and a struggle for human agency
- [[Cognitive Wilderness]] — unoptimized mental spaces where serendipity, ambiguity, and discovery live
- [[Sherry Turkle]] — *Reclaiming Conversation*: devices erode empathy; unfragmented presence is a remedy
- [[Attention Budget]] — treating personal and organizational attention with the same reverence as financial capital

## Key Takeaways

- **Wrong resource**: Calendars track time; attention determines whether you were actually present.
- **Selection crisis**: AI removes access friction; the hard problem is choosing what enters the mind.
- **Attention shapes reality**: Repeatedly consuming outrage or problems makes the world look that way.
- **Outsourced prioritization**: Each notification lets something external decide your next thought.
- **Leadership = deciding what matters**: You can't lead if your attention is led by everything else.
- **Culture follows attention**: Employees believe where leaders' attention goes, not the PowerPoint slide.
- **AI risk**: Danger isn't only AI thinking for us, but deciding what we think about.
- **Efficiency vs. discovery**: What looks like waste is sometimes exactly where discovery lives.
- **Attention budgets**: Before adding a KPI, ask what people will stop noticing.
- **Productivity redefined**: From accumulation to intentional subtraction.
- **Presence as advantage**: Undivided attention signals "you matter more than everything competing for my mind."
- **H2H experiment**: For 7 days, each morning write "Today, my attention deserves to belong to…"
- **Observe hijackers**: Track who or what repeatedly captures your attention through the day.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Opportunity Cost]]**: Pereira's "what will people stop noticing?" before adding a KPI is opportunity-cost reasoning applied to a finite attention budget — every new focus displaces something else.
- **[[Via Negativa]]**: Redefining productivity as "intentional subtraction" rather than accumulation is improvement by removal — protecting focus by eliminating inputs, not adding systems.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central argument about time management versus attention?
**A**: We have been managing the wrong resource — protected calendar time is worthless if attention wanders, so in an AI-abundant world the decisive skill is protecting focus, not just time.

**Q2**: What does "outsourced prioritization" mean, and how does it connect to leadership?
**A**: It's when notifications, dashboards, and AI assistants decide what you think about next, hijacking agency; since leadership is deciding what matters, you can't lead if your attention is constantly led by external inputs.

**Q3**: How would you apply the article's H2H experiment?
**A**: For seven days, before opening any email or feed, write "Today, my attention deserves to belong to…", then neutrally observe what repeatedly hijacks your mind to discover who or what actually governs your attention.
