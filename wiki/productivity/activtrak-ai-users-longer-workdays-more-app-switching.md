## Summary

ActivTrak's analysis of 9,140 knowledge workers across 467 enterprises found AI users have workdays up to 90 minutes longer than non-users, with more application switching and shorter focus sessions among heavy users. AI embedded in primary work applications is associated with far less switching than browser-based tools. ActivTrak concludes that integrating AI into workflows matters more than raw usage.

## Core Concepts

- [[ActivTrak Productivity Lab]]: behavioral-data research group; data covers April 1 to September 25, 2026, not self-reported surveys.
- [[Heavy AI Users]]: more than 45 minutes and 5+ sessions per day; contrasted with light, regular, and non-users.
- [[Application Switching]]: heavy users switch 1.1 times per hour versus 0.95 for non-users.
- [[Swivel-Chair Tax]]: friction from moving between standalone browser-based AI tools and work applications.
- [[Embedded AI]]: AI within primary work applications, linked to about 0.6 switches per hour.
- [[Focus Sessions]]: uninterrupted focus averages 37.8 minutes for heavy users versus 48.2 for light users.
- [[After-Hours Work]]: the "third shift", defined by productive screen time outside scheduled hours.

## Key Takeaways

- **Longer Days**: AI users' workdays span 8.4 to 9.1 hours versus 7.6 for non-users.
- **Third Shift**: Heavy users' longer workdays add 18 minutes of after-hours work daily.
- **Switching**: Heavy AI users switch applications 15.2% more often than non-users.
- **Focus Loss**: Heavy users' focus sessions are 22% shorter than light users'.
- **Browser Tax**: Browser-based AI users switch 4.2 times per hour versus 0.95 for non-users.
- **Embedded Advantage**: Embedded AI users average just 0.6 switches per hour.
- **Role Split**: Software engineers favor embedded AI; operations managers rely on browser-based tools.
- **Meetings**: Heavy users spend 3% of work time in meetings versus 5% for non-users.
- **Leadership Goal**: Treat AI usage as a means to measurable productivity gains.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Every switch between tools carries a cost to focus** — Standalone tools force extra hops between applications, fragmenting attention; integrating a capability into the existing workflow removes the hops. · *model:* [[Context-Switching Cost]]

**T2. Usage is a proxy, not the outcome** — Counting adoption or usage does not measure productivity; value must be tied to measurable gains in efficiency and performance. · *model:* [[Goodhart's Law]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is ActivTrak's central finding about AI and work?
**A**: AI users have workdays up to 90 minutes longer than non-users, with more app switching and shorter focus sessions for heavy users, so usage alone does not guarantee productivity.

**Q2**: How does the way AI is accessed affect application switching?
**A**: Employees using AI embedded in their primary applications average 0.6 switches per hour, while browser-based AI users switch between 1.7 and 4.2 times per hour.

**Q3**: What should leaders do based on these findings?
**A**: Integrate AI directly into existing applications and treat AI usage as a means to measurable gains in productivity, efficiency, and business performance rather than a goal itself.
