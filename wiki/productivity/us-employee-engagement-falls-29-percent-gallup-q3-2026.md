## Summary
Gallup's Q3 2026 report finds U.S. employee engagement at 29%, the lowest since 2011 and the first reading below 30% since then. Role clarity and feeling cared for at work fell alongside it, with fully remote and hybrid employees dropping most. Gallup does not establish that AI adoption or remote work caused the decline, but flags whether communication and manager support are keeping pace with change.

## Core Concepts
- [[Employee Engagement]]: Gallup's measure, 29% engaged, 53% not engaged, 18% actively disengaged.
- [[Gallup]]: tracker of U.S. engagement since 2000, when it stood at 26%.
- [[Role Clarity]]: share strongly agreeing they know what is expected fell from 49% to 46%.
- [[Manager Support]]: share feeling cared for by someone at work fell from 40% to 37%.
- [[Remote Work]] and [[Hybrid Work]]: remote engagement fell 5 points, hybrid 2 points, on-site about 1.
- [[AI Adoption]]: the remote and hybrid engagement advantage at AI-adopting organisations narrowed.

## Key Takeaways
- **Long Decline**: Engagement peaked at 36% in 2020 and was 31% in 2024.
- **Quarterly Drop**: Down two points, about 3.2 million fewer engaged workers.
- **Disengagement**: 71% of workers are not engaged or actively disengaged.
- **Clarity Gap**: Role clarity is well below the 61% recorded in 2015.
- **Work Location**: Remote and hybrid declines exceeded the margin of error; on-site did not.
- **Causation Caveat**: Findings do not show AI or remote work caused the decline.
- **HR Response**: Clarify expectations, communicate regularly, and support employees through tech changes.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Technology adoption does not by itself improve the employee experience** — Tools change how work is organised, so expectations, communication, and support must be actively updated alongside them. Otherwise clarity and engagement can erode.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did Gallup find about U.S. employee engagement in Q3 2026?
**A**: Engagement fell to 29%, down two points from the previous quarter and the lowest since 2011. Actively disengaged workers rose to 18% and 53% were not engaged.

**Q2**: Which groups and measures declined most, and how reliable is the evidence?
**A**: Fully remote employees dropped five points and hybrid employees two, both beyond the margin of error. Role clarity fell to 46% and feeling cared for to 37%.

**Q3**: What should managers do in response?
**A**: Make responsibilities and performance standards clear, communicate regularly (especially with remote and hybrid teams), and explain how new tools like AI fit into roles with practical support.
