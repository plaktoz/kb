# How to Actually Finish What You Need to Get Done

## Summary

Marc Zao-Sanders, author of *Timeboxing: The Power of Doing One Thing at a Time*, argues that [[Timeboxing]] merges a to-do list with a calendar by assigning dedicated time blocks to specific tasks, producing gains neither tool delivers alone. He recommends a short morning planning ritual, three standard block sizes, and reserving most of the day for both work and personal commitments, while building in slack for the roughly 10% of plans that inevitably change. He frames the entire method around a single word: agency.

## Core Concepts

- [[Timeboxing]]: scheduling dedicated calendar blocks for specific tasks rather than keeping a separate to-do list.
- [[Marc Zao-Sanders]]: CEO/co-founder of Filtered.com and author of the book on timeboxing.
- [[Time Management]]: the broader discipline this technique sits within, contrasted with plain calendaring or plain to-do lists.
- [[Agency]]: the underlying psychological benefit Zao-Sanders says timeboxing provides.

## Key Takeaways

- **Definition**: [[Timeboxing]] is "a calendar multiplied by the to-do list."
- **Morning ritual**: Spend 15 minutes each morning planning the day's blocks.
- **Block sizes**: Use three standard durations — 15, 30, and 60 minutes.
- **Coverage**: Timebox roughly 70% of the day, including exercise and family time.
- **Flexibility**: Build in slack since about 10% of plans change unexpectedly.
- **Focus**: Reduces moment-to-moment decision fatigue about what to work on next.
- **Record-keeping**: The calendar doubles as a log of what was actually accomplished.
- **Collaboration**: Committing to "timeboxed for Thursday at 3pm" replaces vague promises.
- **Distraction reset**: Say "one thing at a time" aloud, then return to the current calendar block.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Implementation Intentions]]**: Pre-committing to when and where a task happens (a specific calendar slot) removes in-the-moment decision-making, which is the core mechanism timeboxing exploits.
- **[[Parkinson's Law]]**: Assigning a fixed block (15, 30, or 60 minutes) to a task constrains it from expanding to fill unlimited time, explaining why timeboxing sharpens focus.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of Zao-Sanders' timeboxing method?
**A**: Combining a to-do list with a calendar — scheduling specific tasks into dedicated time blocks — produces productivity gains beyond what either tool offers on its own.

**Q2**: What specific structure does he recommend for implementing timeboxing?
**A**: A 15-minute morning planning session, three block sizes (15, 30, 60 minutes), roughly 70% of the day scheduled including personal time, and slack for the ~10% of plans that change.

**Q3**: How can timeboxing improve collaboration and handling distraction?
**A**: Telling colleagues a task is "timeboxed for Thursday at 3pm" replaces vague commitments with concrete ones, and when distracted, repeating "one thing at a time" helps redirect attention back to the current block.
