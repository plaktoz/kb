## Summary

The most durable productivity gains come from habits that remove friction — batched decisions, protected slack time, a trimmed daily task list, and a two-minute nightly shutdown — rather than habits that add new tools or routines, because friction-removing habits keep working precisely when a week gets chaotic.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Decision Batching]]**: Settling recurring low-stakes choices (meals, meeting times) once per week instead of relitigating them daily — ten daily 30-second decisions cost five minutes lost to nothing, versus a single ten-minute weekly session.
- **[[Scheduling Slack]]**: A calendar with real unbooked time (even 30 minutes) absorbs disruptions; a fully booked calendar treats every disruption as a crisis because there's nowhere for it to go.
- **[[Two-List System]]**: Separating "must happen today" from "someday" tasks, since mixing them makes every day feel impossible before it starts.
- **[[Shutdown Ritual]]**: A two-minute end-of-day note of what's carrying over to tomorrow, which lets the mind stop holding onto open loops overnight — most residual work stress comes from unfinished things circling mentally, not the work itself.

## Key Takeaways

- Before optimizing a recurring task, ask whether it needs to happen at all — a task removed entirely costs nothing forever, while a task merely sped up still costs time every week.
- A five-minute weekly reset (reviewing the week ahead) removes the need to re-decide priorities each morning.
- A single protected daily buffer block gives urgent items somewhere to land without displacing already-planned work.
- None of the seven habits require new tools or a routine overhaul — they're durable because they reduce pressure rather than add another thing to maintain, so they keep paying off during a bad week specifically.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of the article?
**A**: The productivity habits that actually hold up under a busy week are ones that remove friction (fewer repeated decisions, less overbooking, fewer unnecessary tasks) rather than habits that add new structure or tools.

**Q2**: What specific habit addresses lingering work-related stress after hours?
**A**: A two-minute "shutdown ritual" — writing down what's carrying over to tomorrow before finishing work — which lets the mind stop holding onto unfinished tasks overnight.

**Q3**: How should someone decide whether to optimize or eliminate a recurring task?
**A**: Ask whether anyone would notice if the task stopped happening entirely; if not, remove it rather than simply trying to do it faster, since elimination saves time permanently while speed-ups only reduce a recurring cost.
