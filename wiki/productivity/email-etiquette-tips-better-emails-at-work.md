## Summary

Professional email etiquette has no standardized training, yet mastering it significantly affects how competent you appear to colleagues. Well-crafted emails save the reader's time by surfacing only relevant information and eliminating unnecessary back-and-forth threads. Jeff Su, presenting for [[Harvard Business Review]], distills eight actionable tips drawn from his experience as a management consultant.

## Core Concepts

- [[Email Etiquette]] — unspoken rules that signal professional competence
- [[Call to Action]] in subject lines — specificity reduces cognitive load for recipients
- [[Email Thread Management]] — one thread per topic preserves context
- [[Recipient Management]] — explicitly noting additions/removals in CC/BCC
- [[Bottom Line Up Front]] (BLUF) — leading with the main point before context
- [[Active Summarization]] — restating a disorganized sender's points to confirm understanding
- [[Hyperlinking]] — embedding clean links instead of raw URLs
- [[Undo Send]] — leveraging delay windows to catch errors before delivery

## Key Takeaways

- **CTA in subject line**: Specify action + estimated time (e.g., "5-min survey feedback for Project X").
- **One thread per topic**: Separate emails for the same project clutter inboxes and lose context.
- **Announce recipient changes**: Add a top line noting who was added or removed and why.
- **Main point first**: Lead with the request; push context to after so senior readers can act fast.
- **Summarize disorganized emails**: Bucket themes before replying to confirm understanding.
- **Hyperlink links**: Use Cmd+K / Ctrl+K — cleaner and reduces URL copy-paste errors.
- **Default to Reply, not Reply All**: Contains damage if you send a rushed or mistaken reply.
- **Set undo-send to 30 seconds**: Gives time to catch errors after hitting send.

## First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Cognitive Load Reduction]]**: Every structural choice — BLUF ordering, specific CTAs, clean hyperlinks — minimizes the mental work the reader must do, which is the root mechanism behind all eight tips.
- **[[Murphy's Law]]**: The undo-send tip is an explicit acknowledgment that mistakes will happen; designing the system (30-second delay, Reply default) to limit blast radius is applied defensive design thinking.
