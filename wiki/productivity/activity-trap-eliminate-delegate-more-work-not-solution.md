## Summary

Throwing more work at feeling overloaded makes things worse; the fix is to ruthlessly eliminate or delegate low-value tasks and focus on high-value work. Delegation only works when it is systematized, trusted, and paired with a clear definition of a "win". Most failures are system problems, not people problems.

## Core Concepts

- [[Time Management]]: reframed as deciding what to eliminate, not managing everything better.
- [[Delegation]]: handing tasks to people or technology, via services like Fiverr and Upwork.
- [[Parkinson's Law]]: a task fills the time allotted to it.
- [[Unique Ability]]: [[Dan Sullivan]]'s concept of focusing on what you are exceptionally good at.
- [[Micromanagement]]: pushes delegates into "minimum expectation mode".
- [[Standard Operating Procedures]]: the "last time method" documents a task while doing it once more.
- [[Systems Thinking]]: most problems are system breakdowns rather than people failures.

## Key Takeaways

- **Eliminate First**: "More is never the solution to too much"; cut tasks that don't move the needle.
- **Busy Is Not Productive**: Busyness can be an admission of failure, not a badge of honor.
- **Input-Output Multiple**: Results are some multiple of effort, which can be near zero.
- **Hourly Value**: Time on $10/hour work never produces $1,000/hour results.
- **Delegate to People or Tech**: Specialists are usually better and happier doing tasks you dislike.
- **Time Expansion**: Scheduling eight hours for a task makes it take eight hours.
- **Last Time Method**: Do the task once more, documenting each step while the delegate watches.
- **Realistic Expectations**: Expect delegates to reach about 80% first, then improve with reps.
- **Avoid Micromanaging**: Constant takeovers push good employees into doing the bare minimum.
- **Define the Win**: Clear daily success criteria raise morale and reduce attrition.
- **Blame Systems**: Roughly 80% of problems are system problems, yet people get blamed.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Time spent on low-value work displaces high-value work** — Each hour has a value set by the best work it could do, so filling it with low-dollar tasks caps results. Selectivity, not volume, drives growth. · *model:* [[Opportunity Cost]]
→ explains: Eliminate First, Hourly Value, Delegate to People or Tech, Input-Output Multiple

**T2. Work expands to fill the time allotted** — A task's duration tracks the time budgeted for it, so tight allotments shorten it. · *model:* [[Parkinson's Law]]
→ explains: Time Expansion, Busy Is Not Productive

**T3. Failures are usually system breakdowns, not individual faults** — Documenting and iterating a process yields consistent results and makes errors fixable without blame. · *model:* [[Systems Thinking]]
→ explains: Blame Systems, Last Time Method, Avoid Micromanaging, Define the Win

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central argument of "the activity trap"?
**A**: Piling on more work doesn't solve overload; people should eliminate or delegate everything that doesn't move the needle and focus on high-value work.

**Q2**: How does the "last time method" of delegation work?
**A**: You do the task one last time while documenting each step as the delegate watches. The delegate then performs it from the notes, and the steps are clarified wherever they get confused.

**Q3**: How should you set expectations and treat a person you have delegated to?
**A**: Expect about 80% of your quality at first and improvement with practice, and don't micromanage or swoop in. Otherwise they slip into "minimum expectation mode".
