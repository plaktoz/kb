## Summary
A New York Fed survey of 1,835 college-educated workers finds roughly half of in-office days are dominated by Zoom calls and Slack, with little in-person contact. The researchers call this "in-office remote work" and say it undercuts the collaboration case for office mandates. They still conclude that enforcing adherence is what delivers the face-to-face contact workers want.

## Core Concepts
- [[In-Office Remote Work]]: commuting to the office only to take video calls and answer messages from a desk.
- [[Return-to-Office Mandates]]: three-, four- or five-day policies justified by collaboration, productivity and mentorship.
- [[Federal Reserve Bank of New York]] research by [[Natalia Emanuel]] and [[Emma Harrington]].
- [[Distributed Teams]]: teams spread across more offices, plus fully remote colleagues, leave in-office staff without colleagues nearby.
- [[Mandate Compliance and Enforcement]]: workers "no-showing" and employers not enforcing mandates.
- [[Workplace Isolation]] and its mental health repercussions.

## Key Takeaways
- **No Face Time**: 48.8% had no in-person meetings on their most recent office day.
- **Absent Teammates**: 70.6% had at least one teammate out of the office.
- **Virtual Meetings in Person**: 37% had a virtual meeting although all participants were in the same office.
- **Remote Share**: Roughly one in four workers are fully remote, per BLS data.
- **Conservative Estimate**: September was chosen because office attendance is higher after Labor Day.
- **Policy Implication**: Authors urge adherence to mandates, since it delivers the face-to-face contact workers crave.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. A policy delivers its intended benefit only if the behavior it targets actually changes** — Mandating attendance does not produce collaboration if the in-office experience still consists of virtual interaction. Outcomes depend on how the policy is carried out, not its stated goal.
→ explains: No Face Time, Virtual Meetings in Person, Policy Implication

**T2. The value of a shared location depends on who else is there** — An office delivers its social benefit only when colleagues are co-present; spread-out teams and absentees erode it. *(implied)*
→ explains: Absent Teammates, Remote Share

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the NY Fed's central finding about in-office workers?
**A**: Roughly half of in-office workers experience their days as effectively remote, dominated by Zoom calls and Slack with little in-person contact. This weakens the collaboration rationale for office mandates.

**Q2**: What data points show how little in-person interaction happens on office days?
**A**: 48.8% had no in-person meetings on their latest office day, 70.6% had a teammate absent, and 37% had a virtual meeting even though all participants were in the same office.

**Q3**: What do the authors recommend employers do in response?
**A**: They do not say to give up on mandates; ensuring adherence helps deliver the face-to-face contact workers want. They also note post-pandemic organizational structure may not suit mandates and can cause pushback.
