## Summary

A PwC survey of over 2,000 UK workers found 28% cite workload pressure and 25% cite poor management as barriers to productivity, compared with just 13% citing a lack of skills or training. Experts argue employers should examine job design, workload and decision rights before assuming more training is the fix, while noting gaps in access to development resources and future-skills clarity.

## Core Concepts

- **[[PwC]]** Workforce Transformation Survey: Surveyed 2,000+ UK workers on productivity barriers.
- **[[Workload]]**: Cited by 28% of workers as the top barrier to productivity, ahead of management and skills gaps.
- **[[Job Design]]**: Teresa Rose (ConsultHer) argues employers must assess workload, workflows, decision rights and role clarity before adding training.
- **[[Skills Gap]]**: Only 13% cited lack of skills/training as a barrier; notably higher among Gen Z (20%) than millennials (13%) or Gen X (11%).
- **[[CIPD]]**: Ben Willmott links sustained workplace pressure to reduced cognitive capacity and decision-making ability.
- **[[Line Management]]**: Better managers correlate with lower reported workplace pressure, higher motivation, and lower intent to leave, per CIPD research.

## Key Takeaways

- **Workload tops the list**: 28% cite workload pressure as holding back productivity.
- **Management matters**: 25% point to poor management or decision-making.
- **Skills gap smaller**: Only 13% cite lack of skills/training as a barrier.
- **Gen Z skills gap**: 20% of Gen Z cite skills gaps, vs 13% millennials, 11% Gen X.
- **Confidence vs access**: 60% feel confident learning new skills, but only half have adequate L&D access.
- **Future clarity gap**: Fewer than half (48%) understand what skills they'll need.
- **Fix design, not just training**: Experts urge auditing job design and workflows before adding training.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Theory of Constraints]]**: Teresa Rose's warning that "training people harder will not fix badly designed work" reflects the core insight that improving a non-bottleneck resource (individual skill) does nothing for overall throughput when the real constraint is workload, workflow design, or decision rights.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the PwC survey on workplace productivity barriers?
**A**: Workload pressure (28%) and poor management or decision-making (25%) were cited far more often than lack of skills or training (13%) as barriers to employee productivity.

**Q2**: What generational difference did the survey find regarding skills gaps?
**A**: 20% of Gen Z workers cited a lack of skills or training as a productivity barrier, compared with 13% of millennials and 11% of Gen X workers.

**Q3**: What do experts recommend employers do before investing further in training?
**A**: Audit workload, workflows, priorities, decision rights and role clarity first, since poorly designed work undermines productivity regardless of how much additional training is provided.
