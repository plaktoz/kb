## Summary

[[Cognizant]]'s study "New Frontiers, New Frictions: Mapping the DNA of AI Adoption" identifies an "[[Activation Gap]]": four in five employees want to use AI more than their jobs allow, so the main barrier is turning willingness into effective adoption rather than winning people over. The study finds that up to 82% of workers say their AI tools don't fit their needs, and that personal disposition predicts AI behavior far better than organizational rank. Cognizant argues that context-specific tools and persona-tailored training are the clearest path to closing the gap and to capturing a share of a potential ~$6 trillion global GDP gain.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Activation Gap]]**: The divide between how much employees think AI can help them and how much their jobs let them use it.
- **[[Context Engineering]]**: Shaping AI tools to a team's rules, workflows and execution patterns. Along with customization and integration, the study names it as the lever for closing the gap.
- **Disposition over hierarchy**: How a person relates to technology, risk and work is roughly 6x more predictive of AI behavior than their place in the org chart.
- **Employee archetypes**:
  - **Navigators** are willing users blocked by organizational barriers.
  - **Stalwarts** want a clear business case before adopting.
  - **Accelerators** are the heaviest users.
  - **Architects** adopt fastest.
  - **Precisionists** are highly active users and the most satisfied group.
  - **Holdouts** are the least inclined to use AI.
- **[[Shadow AI]]**: 87% of Architects turn to unsanctioned tools when approved options fall short.
- **Training gap**: How effective training feels and how often it is completed both vary sharply by archetype. The study treats this as a separate problem from poor tool fit.
- **People**: [[Ravi Kumar S]] (Cognizant CEO) and [[Surya Gummadi]] (Cognizant President, Americas).

## Key Takeaways

- **Eager adopters**: 80% of employees want to use AI more than their jobs permit.
- **Macro stakes**: A uniform 5% labor-productivity gain could add ~$6T to world GDP.
- **Disposition matters**: Disposition is ~6x more predictive of AI behavior than hierarchy.
- **Leverage point**: Navigators plus Stalwarts make up 72% of the workforce.
- **Poor tool fit**: 72–82% of every archetype say their AI tools don't meet their needs.
- **Even fans unhappy**: Precisionists, the most satisfied group, report only 28% tool satisfaction.
- **Context missing**: 33% of Accelerators and Architects cite missing context and customization as their top limiter.
- **Training gap**: Architects rate training 81% effective with 83% completion; Holdouts 56% and 53%.
- **Shadow tools**: 87% of Architects use unsanctioned tools when approved ones fall short.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Fit to context limits adoption more than willingness does** — Once people are willing to use a tool, how widely it is used depends on how well the tool fits the specific workflows and rules of the work. Generic tools stall where context-specific ones perform.
→ explains: Eager adopters, Poor tool fit, Even fans unhappy, Context missing, Shadow tools

**T2. Disposition predicts technology behavior better than position** — How someone relates to technology, risk and work shapes their adoption more than where they sit in the hierarchy. Interventions targeted by disposition therefore reach more people than ones targeted by role.
→ explains: Disposition matters, Leverage point, Training gap

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the "Activation Gap" and what does Cognizant say is the main barrier to AI's economic promise?
**A**: It is the gap between how much employees believe AI can help them and how much their jobs allow them to use it. The main barrier is turning employees' existing willingness into effective adoption, not persuading them to adopt.

**Q2**: What evidence shows that tool dissatisfaction isn't limited to skeptics?
**A**: Between 72% and 82% of every archetype say their AI tools don't meet their needs. Even Precisionists, the most satisfied group, report only 28% satisfaction, and 87% of Architects turn to unsanctioned tools.

**Q3**: How should a company apply these findings to speed up AI adoption?
**A**: Target the Navigator and Stalwart archetypes (72% of workers), provide context-specific, customized tools, and tailor training to different personas. Treat tool fit and training as two separate problems to fix.
