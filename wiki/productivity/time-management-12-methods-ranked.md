## Summary
A tiered ranking of 12 time-management methods from simplest (2-Minute Rule) to most comprehensive (GTD, Weekly Review Hybrid), matched to the specific problem each solves — procrastination, poor prioritization, overcommitting, context-switching, or complex-project management.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Ivy Lee Method]]**: Write the 6 most important tasks for tomorrow each evening, ranked by priority, and complete them strictly in order — a century-old prioritization technique.
- **[[Time Blocking]]**: Assigning every hour to a specific task or category, with 2-3 hour blocks reserved for deep work; the guide calls it the method most recommended by productivity researchers.
- **[[Getting Things Done (GTD)]]**: David Allen's five-step system — Capture, Clarify, Organize, Reflect, Engage — anchored by a non-negotiable weekly review.
- **[[Eisenhower Matrix]]**: Categorizing tasks by urgency and importance into do/schedule/delegate/eliminate quadrants.
- **[[80/20 Rule (Pareto Principle)]]**: Identifying the ~20% of activities producing ~80% of results and concentrating effort there.

## Key Takeaways
- Method choice should match the specific failure mode: Pomodoro/Eat the Frog for procrastination, Eisenhower/ABCDE for prioritization struggles, 1-3-5/Ivy Lee for overcommitting, Time Blocking/Themed Days for context-switching, GTD/Weekly Review for complex projects.
- 15 minutes of planning is framed as saving roughly 2 hours of reactive work.
- Stanford research cited shows multitasking can cut productivity by up to 40%.
- The Pomodoro Technique's default 25/5 cadence can be lengthened to 50/10 for deep-work sessions.

## 🃏 Review Questions
**Q1**: What is the guide's core philosophy about time management?
**A**: That the goal isn't acquiring more time but building a system that directs the time you already have toward what actually matters — not squeezing more in, but making sure the right things get your best attention.

**Q2**: Which method would the guide recommend for someone who struggles most with procrastination versus someone who struggles with context-switching?
**A**: For procrastination, Pomodoro or Eat the Frog; for context-switching, Time Blocking or Themed Days — method choice is matched to the specific underlying problem.
