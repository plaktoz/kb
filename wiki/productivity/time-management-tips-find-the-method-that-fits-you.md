## Summary

A research-backed guide argues that most people fail at time management not due to lack of discipline, but because they adopt techniques mismatched to their actual bottleneck. The guide frames time management as a diagnostic exercise — identify whether the core problem is prioritization, scheduling, execution, or energy management before choosing a method. The right intervention depends on the specific failure mode: someone who struggles to start needs different tools than someone who struggles to protect focus time already scheduled.

## Core Concepts

- **Diagnostic approach to [[Time Management]]**: Identify the root bottleneck before selecting a technique
- **Four failure modes**: [[Prioritization]] (deciding what matters), [[Scheduling]] (fitting tasks into the day), [[Execution]] (staying focused once started), [[Energy Management]] (matching tasks to peak mental states)
- **[[Pomodoro Technique]]**: Lowers the activation barrier for people who struggle with starting tasks
- **[[Calendar Blocking]]**: Protects scheduled focus time and requires boundary-setting
- **Mismatch hypothesis**: Trending productivity systems fail because they are adopted wholesale rather than matched to individual needs

## Key Takeaways

- **Root cause first**: Diagnose your specific bottleneck before picking a system.
- **Activation barrier**: [[Pomodoro Technique]] works for people who struggle to start, not all.
- **Calendar blocking**: Best for those who lose focus time to interruptions, not procrastination.
- **Energy management**: Match cognitively demanding tasks to peak mental clarity windows.
- **One-size-fits-all fails**: Trending systems succeed for those whose problem matches the system's design.
- **Four buckets**: Prioritization, scheduling, execution, and energy are distinct problems requiring distinct fixes.

## First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Adopting a popular system because it worked for someone else optimizes for the appearance of productivity rather than addressing the actual constraint — the metric (system use) diverges from the goal (output).
- **[[Theory of Constraints]]**: Time management fails when effort is applied anywhere other than the binding constraint; identifying whether the bottleneck is prioritization, scheduling, execution, or energy determines which intervention will actually lift throughput.
