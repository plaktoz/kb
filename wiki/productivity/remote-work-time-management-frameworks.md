## Summary

A remote-work productivity guide argues that 40% of remote workers struggle with productivity and work-life balance because remote work removes the implicit boundaries and social cues of an office. It compiles frameworks (Pomodoro, time blocking, the "windowed work" approach, the Eisenhower Matrix) and ten concrete tactics for protecting focus and boundaries when working from home.

## Core Concepts

The **windowed work approach** lets people schedule work in blocks that fit their natural energy rhythm rather than a rigid 9-to-5 — the guide cites a stat that 73% of professionals report higher productivity when working in self-chosen windows. This complements the **[[Pomodoro Technique]]** (25-minute focused intervals) and the **[[Eisenhower Matrix]]** for prioritization, and pairs with the "52/17 rule" (52 minutes of work, 17-minute break) as an alternative interval-based structure.

## Key Takeaways

- **Boundary erosion is the core remote-work risk**: lack of physical/social separation between work and home blurs focus and rest.
- **Windowed work**: scheduling around personal energy rhythms beats a fixed 9-to-5 for many remote workers.
- **Dedicated workspace + "dressing for work"** are physical/behavioral cues that switch the brain into work mode.
- **Meeting-free deep-work blocks** should be treated as sacred and protected from interruption.
- **Results over hours**: remote work success should be measured by output, not time clocked.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Remote workers struggle with time management primarily because remote work removes the natural boundaries of an office, not because of a lack of tools or discipline.

**Q2**: What specific technique does the article propose as an alternative to a fixed 9-to-5 schedule?
**A**: The "windowed work approach," which schedules work in blocks that match a person's natural energy rhythm (e.g., early morning or late night) rather than a rigid fixed schedule.

**Q3**: What practical boundary-setting tactics does the article recommend for remote workers?
**A**: Setting a dedicated workspace, communicating availability clearly with the team, using distraction-blocking apps during work hours, and setting an alarm to signal the end of the workday to avoid after-hours email/Slack checking.
