## Summary

The Charter AI Pulse survey of 5,002 US desk workers finds that only 10.5% say AI makes both their work and life much better, and the strongest predictor of who gets there is agency, meaning a say in how AI fits their job. Most top drivers of benefit are conditions managers control, yet managers and workers see a 23 to 30 point gap in what managers say they do versus what teams observe. The author argues for redesigning recurring work around outputs and steps with the people who do it, building verification into workflows, and making AI use safe to show.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Charter AI Pulse]]**: Survey of 5,002 US desk workers, managers, and executives who use AI; the "thriving" group is 10.5%.
- **[[Agency (AI at Work)]]**: Feeling involved in how AI fits your job; top predictor of benefit, ranking above usage frequency, while running background agents ranks 25th.
- **[[OVER Framework]]**: Lead with AI's method: Outputs, Verbs, Evaluate (AI-first, human-first, hybrid), Route to the right tool.
- **[[Workflow Literacy]]**: Understanding work at the level of outputs and steps, which makes specific AI guidance possible.
- **[[Swimlane Process Maps]]**: Phil Kirschner's critique: map the work first, attach "who" last as a label expected to change.
- **[[Demonstration Gap]]**: Difference between what managers say they do (54% give specific AI guidance) and what workers see (24.6%).
- **[[DRAFT Check]]**: Verification routine: Doubt the confidence, Reasoning, Alternatives, Facts and sources, Take ownership; scaled to stakes.
- **[[Secret Cyborgs]]**: [[Ethan Mollick]]'s term for people who hide their AI use.
- **[[AI Burnout]]**: Checking and fixing AI output is the top cited source (20.7%); hiding AI use is the top factor separating rising from falling burnout.

## Key Takeaways

- **Thriving Minority**: Only 10.5% say AI makes both work and life much better.
- **Agency Gap**: Managers are 1.8x likelier than workers to feel agency (67% vs 37%).
- **Frequency Second**: Multiple daily users benefit twice as often, but agency ranks higher.
- **Manager Levers**: 11 of the top 14 benefit factors are manager-controlled conditions.
- **Guidance Mismatch**: Policy guidance is most provided (45.4%) but rarely most useful (13.4%).
- **Training Gap**: Hands-on tool training is most useful (32.5%), yet only 39.6% receive it.
- **Executive Overtrust**: Executives trust AI over own judgment twice as often as workers (32.8% vs 16.3%).
- **Verification Barriers**: Time pressure (43.7%) and not knowing how to verify (38.7%) lead.
- **Hidden Use**: 35.9% hide AI use; top reason is fear of looking less capable (49.3%).
- **Time Savings Fate**: Only 7.9% put saved time toward rest; 17.6% do more of the same.
- **Automation Underuse**: Gallup shows 77% positive effect for coding and automation, but only 16% use AI for automation.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. People adopt change better when they help shape it** — Benefit from a workplace change tracks how much say the affected people have in it, more than how often or how deeply they use it. Redesigning your own recurring work puts judgment where the knowledge is.
→ explains: Agency Gap, Frequency Second, Automation Underuse

**T2. Concealment follows perceived penalty for disclosure** — When people suspect that revealing efficiency or tool use will be used against them, hiding it is a rational response, and it compounds strain. Making the work visible and deciding in advance what saved time is for removes the incentive.
→ explains: Hidden Use, Time Savings Fate

**T3. Guidance is only as specific as the unit of work it targets** — Managers can give actionable direction only at the level of outputs and steps, not job descriptions; vague encouragement leaves a perceived gap between what leaders think they provide and what teams see.
→ explains: Guidance Mismatch, Training Gap, Manager Levers

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does Charter's data identify as the strongest predictor of benefiting from AI at work?
**A**: Agency, or feeling involved in how AI fits into your job, ranks above usage frequency and far above advanced use such as running background agents (25th on the list).

**Q2**: What evidence shows managers and workers see AI support differently?
**A**: 54% of managers say they give specific guidance on when and how to use AI, but only 24.6% of workers see it, part of a 23 to 30 point demonstration gap.

**Q3**: How does the author suggest giving people a real say while building verification into the workflow?
**A**: Have people redesign their own recurring work with the OVER framework, mapping steps before assigning who does them, and flag decision points and approvals as human checkpoints with checks scaled to the stakes.
