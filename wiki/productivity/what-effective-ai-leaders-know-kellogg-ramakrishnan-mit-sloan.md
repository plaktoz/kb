## Summary

[[MIT Sloan]] professors [[Katherine Kellogg]] and [[Rama Ramakrishnan]] argue that effective leaders of agentic AI understand agents at an "atomic level," invest in domain experts who validate agent output, and prepare to manage agents more than people. Building agents is quick, but validating them is slow and falls to overworked experts, so it becomes the bottleneck.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Agentic AI]]**: Agents that perform many tasks in parallel, requiring a shift from sequential to parallel work management.
- **[[Mental Models]] of AI**: Ramakrishnan says knowing how AI works "under the hood" lets executives balance pragmatism and ambition and move beyond automation to reimagining the business.
- **Validation**: Domain experts must check critical agent outputs during development and in use, even when agents validate other agents.
- **[[Agent Management]]**: Kellogg predicts people will become "managers of agents, not of people," needing new training and job descriptions.
- **[[Katherine Kellogg]]**: MIT Sloan professor teaching a course on leading agentic transformation, with a five-phase implementation framework.
- **[[Rama Ramakrishnan]]**: Professor of the practice with a talk, "The 7 AI Mindsets of Highly Effective Executives."

## Key Takeaways

- **Understand Agents**: Executives need a mental model of how AI works, not just what it does.
- **Validation Gap**: Developing an agent takes little time; validating its output takes much more.
- **Expert Overload**: Overworked domain experts drop validation first, tightening the bottleneck.
- **Fixes**: Reward validation in reviews and promotions, form dedicated teams, create hybrid build-and-validate roles.
- **Resourcing**: Critical agent projects can't be a side-of-the-desk effort.
- **New Role**: Leaders will manage parallel agents, needing growth mindset and training.

## 🧠 Core Principles

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central argument about leading agentic AI?
**A**: Effective leaders understand how agents work at a fundamental level, invest in domain experts, and prepare to manage agents. Doing so lets them pursue ambitious reimagining of the business rather than just automation.

**Q2**: Why does validation become a bottleneck with AI agents?
**A**: Agents are fast to build, but validating their output takes far more time, and more agents working simultaneously add complexity. Kellogg observes that overworked experts drop validation first, which tightens the bottleneck.

**Q3**: How can organizations keep validation from collapsing?
**A**: Count validation work in performance reviews, promotions, and rewards, create dedicated teams, and move some experts into hybrid build-and-validate roles. Fully resourced teams are needed rather than side-of-the-desk effort.
