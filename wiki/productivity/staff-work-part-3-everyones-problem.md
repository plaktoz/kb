## Summary

The final part of the [[Staff Work]] series argues that staff work is a shared responsibility, not just the drafting officer's: managers who silently rewrite bad drafts or leave vague comments ("sharpen this") without explaining the underlying reasoning save time in the moment but prevent the officer from actually improving, producing the same category of problem in the next submission. The frame borrowed from Josh Seiden is **outcomes over output** — a corrected paper is the output, but a better decision and a more capable officer are the actual outcomes.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Outcomes Over Output]]** — evaluating whether feedback produced a better decision-maker over time, not just whether a single document got fixed
- **[[Silent Rewriting]]** — a manager's shortcut of replacing a bad draft entirely without showing the officer *how* the thinking changed, which fixes the immediate document but teaches nothing for next time
- **[[Vague Feedback Trap]]** — broad comments like "rethink" or "sharpen the angle" are fast to write but leave the officer unable to tell whether the actual problem is the objective, the evidence, the structure, or the wording
- **[[Split-Ownership of Decisions]]** — everyone who reviews, edits, or clears a paper shares responsibility for the decision it produces, not just the original drafting officer

## Key Takeaways

- **Study tracked changes instead of clicking "accept all"**: red comments and redlines reveal what moved, what was cut, and why — but only for someone who actually reads them
- **A small comment can point at a bigger problem**: a question about one statistic can mean an entire claim lacks real evidence
- **Reply with the answer and the edit together**, not "noted, will check" — this avoids a second round after the answer has already been found
- **Match guidance to the individual officer**: some need written comments, others need to talk through the issue, others need a live co-editing session to see the reasoning in real time
- **A manager's steer can be wrong too**: inferring what a senior person wants and guiding the team on that inference can misfire — owning that mistake matters as much as correcting the officer's work would
