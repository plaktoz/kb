## Summary

Workslop is AI-generated work that looks polished but lacks the substance to move a task forward, and it taxes both productivity (rework) and trust between colleagues. Repeated hand-offs of unreviewed AI output can cause organizational knowledge decay, so the author argues for quality standards, rewarding results rather than AI usage volume, and making human judgment visible.

## Core Concepts

- [[Workslop]]: AI output that masquerades as good work; popularized by BetterUp Labs and Stanford's Social Media Lab ([[Kate Niederhoffer]], [[Jeff Hancock]]).
- [[Botsitting]] vs. botshitting: supervising and correcting AI output versus forwarding it without responsible review.
- [[Knowledge Decay]]: [[Matthias Holweg]] and [[Thomas Davenport]] (HBR, June 2026) describe errors accumulating as people pass along AI output unexamined.
- [[Large Language Models]]: generate responses by predicting word sequences, so fluency does not guarantee truth or fit.
- [[Human Authorship]]: specificity, judgment, and accountability as the differentiator when polished content is abundant.
- [[AI Adoption Metrics]]: measuring tool usage or content volume rather than value created.

## Key Takeaways

- **Prevalence**: 40% of surveyed workers received workslop in the prior month.
- **Rework Offset**: A Workday study cited says 37% of AI productivity gains are lost to rework.
- **Cost**: About $186 per employee per month in lost productivity (HBR estimate cited).
- **Trust Tax**: Nearly half viewed senders as less capable, trustworthy, or creative.
- **Knowledge Decay**: Chained AI hand-offs let errors accumulate and blur what the organization truly knows.
- **Quality Standard**: Verifiable sources, explicit uncertainty, human accountability, and a clear purpose.
- **Incentives**: Reward outcomes and oversight, not the volume of AI use.
- **Visible Judgment**: Ask whether something here only you could have contributed.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. A proxy that becomes the target stops tracking value** — Measuring AI success by tools adopted or content produced rewards usage rather than value, so teams can produce more while deciding no better. · *model:* [[Goodhart's Law]]
→ explains: Incentives, Prevalence

**T2. Unverified work shifts cost rather than removing it** — When one person generates content and another must check it, the work has changed hands, not disappeared; real productivity is value delivered after review.
→ explains: Rework Offset, Cost, Trust Tax

**T3. Errors compound when each stage of a chain skips verification** — If each recipient passes along output without independent reasoning, gaps accumulate and the system loses the ability to separate knowledge from output.
→ explains: Knowledge Decay, Quality Standard

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is workslop, and why does the author call it a tax on trust?
**A**: Workslop is AI-generated work that looks professional but lacks the substance to advance a task. Beyond rework, it makes colleagues doubt each other's competence and honesty, so nearly half see senders as less capable or trustworthy.

**Q2**: How does knowledge decay occur according to Holweg and Davenport?
**A**: One person outsources reasoning to AI, the recipient does the same, and so on down the chain. Each stage looks professional while errors and gaps accumulate, weakening the human processes that catch mistakes.

**Q3**: What three practices does the article recommend to avoid workslop?
**A**: Set quality standards for AI content (sources, uncertainty, accountability, purpose), reward results rather than volume of AI use, and make your own judgment and authorship visible.
