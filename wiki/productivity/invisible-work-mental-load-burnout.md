## Summary

Most leaders assess employee workload through hours, deadlines, and output, but this misses [[Mental Load]] — the ongoing cognitive and emotional effort of managing tasks, anticipating problems, and keeping work moving that never appears on a timesheet. Employees who carry the most invisible coordination burden, often the most reliable and attuned team members, are systematically undercompensated for it and are at elevated risk of burnout, disengagement, and turnover.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Mental Load]]** — cognitive and emotional labor distinct from visible task output: remembering unwritten deadlines, anticipating needs before being asked, managing team emotional temperature
- **[[Invisible Labor Distribution]]** — the tendency for conscientious, high-attunement employees to absorb disproportionate coordination work without it being tracked or rewarded

## Key Takeaways

- **Blind spot**: standard workload metrics (hours, deadlines, output) systematically miss mental load
- **Who's affected**: often the most reliable and team-attuned employees, not the visibly overworked ones
- **Consequence**: unrecognized mental load is a direct driver of burnout, disengagement, and turnover
