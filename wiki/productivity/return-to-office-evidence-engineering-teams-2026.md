## Summary

Rigorous causal research on 2026 return-to-office mandates shows they raise senior-employee attrition 13-15 percentage points and disproportionately push out high performers, while general RTO research (Gallup, FlexJobs) measures satisfaction rather than the engineering-specific metrics — deployment frequency, PR throughput, incident response — leaders actually need. Microsoft and GitHub's own DevEx research found deep, uninterrupted work is the single strongest lever for developer output, a resource offices structurally reduce.

## Core Concepts

- [[Return-to-Office Mandate]] — five-day mandates remain the exception (Amazon, JPMorgan) versus a three-day hybrid norm (Google, Meta, Apple, Microsoft)
- [[Deep Work]] — Microsoft/GitHub's DevEx research finds it the single strongest lever for developer output, ahead of tooling or feedback speed
- [[Senior Employee Attrition]] — Chicago/Michigan resume study found RTO mandates dropped senior-employee share by 4-15 points at Microsoft, Apple, and SpaceX
- [[Follow-the-Sun Coverage]] — distributed teams gain structural code-review and on-call advantages that co-location eliminates
- [[Desirable Difficulties]] — Bjork's framework underlying why office mandates without clear purpose fail to improve output

## Key Takeaways

- **Senior attrition spike**: Baylor/Pittsburgh research found a 13-14 point rise in abnormal turnover after RTO mandates, worst among women and high performers
- **High performers leave first**: Gartner found intent-to-stay dropped 16% among high performers under strict mandates, double the average
- **No productivity payoff shown**: DORA metrics (deployment frequency, lead time) show no consistent gains after RTO mandates
- **Hiring pool shrinks**: remote postings draw 20% to 10-20x more applicants than location-bound roles (50M+ postings, 28 countries)
- **Ctrip RCT precedent**: Nicholas Bloom's randomized trial found a 13% performance gain for home-based workers, driven by fewer interruptions
- **Real estate vs retention tradeoff**: full RTO carries the highest hiring/retention cost with no consistent productivity offset for engineering-specific work

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Adverse Selection]]**: Because senior engineers with the most external options are also the most likely to leave after a strict mandate, blanket RTO policies systematically drive out exactly the people a company can least afford to lose — a selection effect, not a random attrition bump.
- **[[Desirable Difficulties]]**: The DevEx finding that uninterrupted deep-work blocks (not office proximity) drive output reflects Bjork's broader principle that the conditions producing durable skill or output are often the opposite of what feels easiest or most collaborative in the moment.
