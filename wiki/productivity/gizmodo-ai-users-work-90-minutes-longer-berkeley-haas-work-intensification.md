## Summary
An [[ActivTrak Productivity Lab]] analysis of more than 9,000 employees at 467 companies found AI users' workdays span 8.4 to 9.1 hours versus 7.6 for non-users, even as figures like Bill Gates and Jensen Huang predict shorter workweeks. The article offers several readings of the data and pairs it with a Berkeley Haas study finding AI intensifies work rather than freeing time. See also [[activtrak-ai-users-longer-workdays-more-app-switching]] and [[ai-users-longer-workdays-association-not-causation-tech-insider]] for other takes on the same study.

## Core Concepts
- [[ActivTrak Productivity Lab]]: source of the April to September analysis; [[Gabriela Mauch]] leads it.
- [[Heavy AI Users]]: more than 45 minutes across at least five sessions per day; 18 extra after-hours minutes.
- [[Shorter Workweek]] debate: Gates, Huang, and Bernie Sanders's 32-hour workweek call versus the data.
- [[Work Intensification]]: the Haas finding that AI made work faster, broader, and longer.
- [[UC Berkeley Haas School of Business]]: eight-month ethnographic study at a 200-person tech company.
- [[Application Switching]] and focus: heavy users switch more and have shorter uninterrupted work stretches than light users.

## Key Takeaways
- **Longer Days**: AI users' days span 8.4 to 9.1 hours versus 7.6 for non-users.
- **Sample**: More than 9,000 employees at 467 companies, April to September.
- **After Hours**: Heavy users work 18 extra minutes after hours versus non-users.
- **Fragmented Focus**: Heavy users switch apps more and have shorter uninterrupted sessions.
- **Competing Readings**: Long-hours workers may adopt AI, AI may invite more work, or AI output needs sorting.
- **Haas Finding**: Employees worked faster, took broader tasks, and extended into more hours.
- **Sustainability Warning**: Self-driven expansion resets expectations until extra effort becomes standard performance.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Efficiency gains get absorbed by expanded demand** — When a tool lowers the effort of producing work, people take on more, so total workload grows rather than shrinks. · *model:* [[Jevons Paradox]]
→ explains: Haas Finding, Longer Days, Sustainability Warning

**T2. A correlation can have several causal explanations** — A link between AI use and long hours can reflect reverse causation, where long-hours workers adopt AI, rather than AI causing the long hours. · *model:* [[Correlation vs Causation]]
→ explains: Competing Readings, Longer Days

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the ActivTrak analysis find about AI users' workdays?
**A**: AI users' workdays span 8.4 to 9.1 hours versus 7.6 for non-users, up to 90 minutes longer, despite predictions that AI would shorten the workweek.

**Q2**: What did the Berkeley Haas study add?
**A**: An eight-month study at a 200-person tech company found AI made work more intense rather than freeing time, with faster pace, broader tasks, and more hours, often unasked.

**Q3**: What risk does this pose for employers who see a short-term productivity boost?
**A**: Because the expansion feels self-driven at first, expectations can reset so that extra effort becomes the standard, making the pace hard to sustain as the workday grows denser and more demanding.
