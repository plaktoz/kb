## Summary

The article argues that burnout is an operational and business issue for community financial institutions, not just a wellness concern. It cites modeled cost estimates and recommends addressing root causes (workload, workable time off, manager capacity, recovery after intense projects) and tracking capacity alongside performance.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Employee Burnout]]**: depleted employee capacity that often stays hidden because people keep working until the problem is obvious.
- **Modeled cost**: a 2025 [[American Journal of Preventive Medicine]] model estimates roughly $4K per hourly nonmanager, $10.8K per manager, $20.7K per executive, and $5.04MM a year for a hypothetical 1,000-employee US company. These are modeled, not measured, figures.
- **[[Mercer Global Talent Trends]]**: 2026 report finds 44% of employees globally thriving, down from 66% in 2024.
- **[[Community Financial Institutions]]**: the article's audience, where burnout affects productivity, staffing and sustained performance.
- **[[Workload Review]]**: ask which recurring tasks, approvals, meetings and reports add little value, and what can stop, wait or be simplified.
- **[[Manager Capacity]]**: managers juggling customers, operations and staff development need room to manage.
- **[[Project Recovery Planning]]**: intensive initiatives need a recovery plan, such as staggered time off, not just a completion date.
- **Capacity metrics**: review overtime, unused vacation, absenteeism, voluntary turnover and feedback together.

## Key Takeaways

- **Business Issue**: Burnout affects productivity, staffing and the ability to sustain performance.
- **Modeled Cost**: About $5.04MM yearly for a hypothetical 1,000-person company.
- **Thriving Decline**: Employees thriving globally fell from 66% to 44% since 2024.
- **Workable Time Off**: Coverage and documentation matter more than accrued leave.
- **Recovery Plans**: Intensive projects need recovery built in, not just deadlines.
- **Specific Questions**: Ask what work follows people home; assign an owner to act.
- **Combined Metrics**: No single metric establishes burnout; use patterns to start conversations.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Output can mask depleting capacity** — Sustained performance can continue while capacity erodes, so output metrics alone give late warning; capacity signals must be tracked alongside them. · *model:* [[Leading vs. Lagging Indicators]]

**T2. Extraordinary effort must not become the normal operating model** — Demanding periods are sustainable only when followed by recovery and reduced load.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does the article treat burnout as a business issue?
**A**: Burnout affects productivity, staffing and the ability to sustain performance, and its costs are large even when only modeled. Employees often keep working until the problem is obvious.

**Q2**: What cost estimates does the article cite, and what caveat applies?
**A**: A 2025 model estimates about $4K to $20.7K per employee a year depending on role, or $5.04MM for a hypothetical 1,000-employee US company. They are modeled estimates, so institutions should not simply multiply headcount by them.

**Q3**: How should an institution start reducing burnout?
**A**: Pick one team or process with persistent pressure, remove a specific source of unnecessary work, and check whether employees gain breathing room. Track overtime, unused vacation, absenteeism, turnover and feedback together.
