## Summary

There is a wide gap between how executives perceive AI rollout and how employees actually experience it — leaders assume enthusiasm while most workers feel anxious. [[Jamil Zaki]] argues that empathetic leadership closes this gap: employees with caring managers innovate and adopt new technology more readily, while low psychological safety leads to shallow AI-generated work or even quiet sabotage of AI initiatives.

## Core Concepts

- **Executive-employee perception gap**: Leaders systematically overestimate employee enthusiasm for AI tools relative to how employees actually feel (anxious, uncertain).
- **[[Psychological safety]]**: Without it, employees are less likely to experiment with new AI tools, more likely to produce shallow AI-generated output, and in some cases actively resist or sabotage AI initiatives.
- **[[Empathetic leadership]]**: Framed as both a human good (social connection improves well-being) and a business lever (empathic companies see harder-working, more collaborative, more innovative employees).

## Key Takeaways

- **Involve, don't announce**: Leaders should involve employees in shaping AI strategy rather than simply rolling it out top-down.
- **Train frontline managers**: Since managers are the direct interface for AI adoption, investing in their empathetic-leadership skills has outsized impact.
- **Use AI to deepen human connection**: The article frames AI's ideal role as supplementing rather than replacing human relationships at work.
- **Sabotage risk is real**: The piece explicitly names "active sabotage" as a downside of poor psychological safety around AI rollouts — a stronger claim than typical "resistance" framing.
