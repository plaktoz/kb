## Summary

Centric Consulting's Joseph Ours argues that AI tools such as [[Microsoft 365 Copilot]] should take over shallow work (meeting notes, inbox triage, status updates) so professionals can spend more time on deep work. He recommends adopting AI gradually through small, low-risk pilots. He also warns that AI can add busywork, so success should be judged by valuable output rather than speed.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Deep Work]] vs. [[Shallow Work]]**: Deep work pushes people to their cognitive limits and produces their best work. Shallow work is tedious, and perpetual overbooking lets it crowd out what matters.
- **[[Microsoft 365 Copilot]]**: AI built into Outlook (thread summaries, draft replies, early-access agentic email and calendar triage), Teams (discussion summaries, alignment, action items) and Sales (CRM data from Dynamics 365 Sales and Salesforce).
- **AI tool categories**: Content creation ([[ChatGPT]], [[Claude]], Jasper), text enhancement (Grammarly), image generation (MidJourney, Adobe Firefly), note-taking (Copilot Notebooks), transcription (Otter) and presentations.
- **[[AI Adoption]] approach**: Start with a small, low-risk pilot, then integrate tools gradually and evaluate regularly.
- **Personalization**: Tune tools to your writing style, work style and project methodology to maximize gains.
- **Human-AI balance**: Overreliance risks complacency and lost [[Critical Thinking]]. The article points to [[Change Management]] and workforce reskilling for team-wide gains.

## Key Takeaways

- **Interruption Load**: Microsoft telemetry shows Microsoft 365 users interrupted about every two minutes in core hours.
- **Target Shallow Work**: Meeting notes, inbox triage and status updates are the first tasks to automate.
- **Copilot Integration**: AI embedded in Outlook, Teams and Sales avoids switching apps.
- **Measured Rollout**: Pick tasks, add tools one at a time, evaluate regularly, adjust.
- **Busywork Risk**: A study of 164,000 workers found AI raised administrative tasks over 90%, cut deep work almost 10%.
- **Right Metric**: Measure valuable work produced, not how fast small tasks clear.
- **Beyond Tools**: Team-wide gains need workforce preparation and new skills, not just software.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Speed on small tasks is a poor proxy for value** — Optimizing how fast tasks clear can speed up the wrong work and displace valuable work, so output should be judged by the value produced. · *model:* [[Goodhart's Law]]
→ explains: Busywork Risk, Right Metric

**T2. Attention is finite, so low-value work displaces high-value work** — When shallow tasks and interruptions fill the day, little room remains for focused work; offloading shallow work protects capacity for deep work. · *model:* [[Opportunity Cost]]
→ explains: Interruption Load, Target Shallow Work

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central argument about AI and productivity?
**A**: AI should take over shallow work such as meeting notes and inbox triage so people can focus on deep work. Success should be judged by valuable output, not by speed.

**Q2**: What evidence shows AI can backfire on productivity?
**A**: A study of 164,000 knowledge workers found AI tools increased administrative tasks by more than 90 percent while reducing deep work by almost 10 percent.

**Q3**: How should a team start adopting AI tools?
**A**: Begin with a small, low-risk pilot. Identify tasks to automate or augment, integrate tools gradually, and regularly evaluate their effectiveness, adjusting as needed.
