## Summary

When demand surges beyond a team's capacity, leaders face a painful dilemma: absorb excess work themselves or push an already-stretched team harder — and both approaches carry real costs. Executive coach Melody Wilding illustrates this trap through a chief legal counsel who, after rapid company acquisitions, responded to team overload by taking on the extra work personally, sacrificing the strategic focus his role required. The article argues that leaders must resist the instinct to absorb overflow and instead address capacity constraints structurally.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Work Absorption Trap]]**: A pattern where leaders personally take on work their team cannot complete, which feels helpful but erodes the leader's ability to perform strategic, high-leverage work.
- **[[Team Capacity]]**: The sustainable throughput of a team; when exceeded, neither individual heroics nor pushing the team harder creates a durable solution.
- **[[Strategic Leadership]]**: The expectation that senior leaders focus on high-level direction and influence, not task execution — a function undermined by absorbing operational overflow.
- **[[Melody Wilding]]**: Executive coach, professor of human behavior at Hunter College, and author of *Managing Up*.
- **[[Rapid Acquisitions]]**: A common organizational trigger for sudden workload spikes, as integration creates compounding demands across legal, compliance, and operations functions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Work Absorption**: Leaders instinctively absorb overflow, but this crowds out strategic work.
- **Both Paths Costly**: Overloading the team or the leader both carry significant downsides.
- **Role Misalignment**: A chief legal counsel working nights on contracts is not doing the job; the role requires strategic leverage.
- **Structural Fix Needed**: Capacity constraints must be solved at the organizational level, not through individual heroics.
- **Acquisitions as Trigger**: Rapid M&A activity is a prime driver of sudden, unsustainable team overload.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Opportunity Cost]]**: Every hour a senior leader spends on tactical overflow is an hour not spent on strategy and leadership — the article's core mechanism is exactly this trade-off made visible through Chris's experience.
- **[[Delegation Ladder]]**: Absorbing work rather than delegating, escalating, or re-scoping inverts healthy organizational design; the fix must address the underlying structure, not just redistribute individual burden.
