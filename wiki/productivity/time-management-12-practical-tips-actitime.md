## Summary
The article argues most people have a time awareness problem rather than a time management problem, and offers 12 practical habits, from tracking time to weekly reviews. It cites research linking stronger time management to lower stress and higher job satisfaction. It closes with common mistakes and advice to pick two or three tips and apply them for a few weeks.

## Core Concepts
- [[Time Tracking]]: recording where hours go for one or two weeks before changing anything.
- [[Time Blocking]]: assigning calendar blocks to types of work, popularized by [[Cal Newport]] in "Deep Work".
- [[Task Batching]] and [[Context-Switching Cost]]: grouping similar work to avoid repeated startup costs.
- [[Parkinson's Law]]: work expands to fill available time; time limits and the [[Pomodoro Technique]] counter it.
- [[Two-Minute Rule]] from [[David Allen]]'s Getting Things Done, and the [[Eisenhower Matrix]].
- [[Energy Management]]: matching task difficulty to peak hours and taking real breaks.

## Key Takeaways
- **Evidence**: A 158-study meta-analysis tied time management to lower distress and higher satisfaction.
- **Track First**: Log one to two weeks of time to find the real breakdown.
- **Three Priorities**: Write three daily priorities before opening email.
- **Batch and Block**: Group similar tasks and protect 90-minute blocks for key work.
- **Switching Penalty**: Task switching can eat up to 40% of productive time.
- **Time Limits**: Parkinson's Law means 25-30 minute caps sharpen focus.
- **Breaks**: Top performers worked about 52 minutes, then rested 17.
- **Weekly Review**: A 15-20 minute review keeps the other habits from staying theoretical.
- **Mistakes**: Multitasking, skipping planning, and confusing activity with progress.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Task switching carries a recovery cost** — Shifting attention between tasks consumes productive time, so grouping similar work and cutting interruptions preserves output. · *model:* [[Context-Switching Cost]]
→ explains: Switching Penalty, Batch and Block, Mistakes

**T2. Work expands to fill the time allotted** — An open-ended allowance lets a task grow; a fixed limit creates pressure that concentrates effort. · *model:* [[Parkinson's Law]]
→ explains: Time Limits

**Q2**: Why does batching tasks and reducing context switching help?
