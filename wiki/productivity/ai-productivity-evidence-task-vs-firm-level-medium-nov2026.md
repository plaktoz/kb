## Summary

The author argues that conflicting AI productivity studies mostly agree once sorted by level: task-level studies show large gains, while company and labor-market studies show little effect so far. The gap is largely organizational, because gains shift the bottleneck from producing work to verifying it. Teams that capture value measure outcomes, track quality alongside speed, invest in verification, map the AI frontier, and redesign workflows.

## Core Concepts

- **[[METR]] developer studies**: 2025 study found developers 19% slower with AI despite believing they were faster; a 2026 follow-up points toward a speed-up but with unreliable data.
- **[[Productivity Paradox]]**: Individual gains fail to appear in company results until work is redesigned around new tools.
- **[[Jagged Frontier]]**: In a BCG study of 758 consultants, AI helped inside its capability and hurt outside it.
- **[[Verification Bottleneck]]**: Addy Osmani's "comprehension debt" and Charity Majors' call to shift trust to tests, evals, and telemetry.
- **[[Workslop]]**: Polished AI output that doesn't advance the task, received by 40% of surveyed workers.
- **[[Klarna]] case**: AI assistant now handles 80% of chats, with a hybrid human fallback.
- **[[Microsoft]] Claude Code and Copilot rollout**: Adopters merged 24% more pull requests.

## Key Takeaways

- **Perception Gap**: Developers believed AI sped them up 20% after being 19% slower.
- **Developer Throughput**: Microsoft adopters merged 24% more PRs; daily users saw 50%.
- **Novice Gains**: Support agents improved 14% on average, 34% for novices.
- **Jagged Frontier**: Consultants were 19 points less likely to be correct outside AI's capability.
- **Firm-Level Null**: About 9 in 10 executives report no productivity impact yet.
- **Entry-Level Signal**: Employment of 22-25-year-olds in AI-exposed jobs is 19% below trend.
- **Organizational Factors**: Microsoft attributes 67% of reported AI impact to organizational factors.
- **Quality Costs**: Workslop took about two hours each to handle.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Local gains don't become aggregate gains without process redesign** — Tools pay off at the firm level only after organizations restructure work around them; individual adoption alone yields personal time savings. · *model:* [[Productivity Paradox]]
→ explains: Firm-Level Null, Organizational Factors

**T2. Speeding up one stage moves the bottleneck downstream** — Faster code generation shifts the limiting step to review, testing, and trust. · *model:* [[Theory of Constraints]]
→ explains: Developer Throughput, Quality Costs

**T3. Capability is uneven, so performance depends on task fit** — The same tool can raise performance on tasks inside its frontier and lower it outside. · *model:* [[Jagged Frontier]]
→ explains: Jagged Frontier, Novice Gains

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about AI productivity evidence?
**A**: The studies are more consistent than they appear: task-level gains are often large, while firm-level gains haven't shown up yet. The gap is mostly organizational.

**Q2**: What evidence shows self-reports can mislead?
**A**: METR's 2025 study found developers took 19% longer with AI but still believed it sped them up by 20%. METR also warns people have overestimated AI's effect on their time before.

**Q3**: What should teams do to capture AI gains?
**A**: Measure shipped outcomes rather than self-reports, track quality next to speed, invest in verification, map where the model's frontier lies, and redesign workflows rather than just issuing licenses.
