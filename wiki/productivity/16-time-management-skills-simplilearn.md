## Summary

Simplilearn outlines 16 core time-management skills — planning, prioritized (not exhaustive) to-do lists, delegation, SMART goals, and the 20-minute focus rule among them — alongside five prioritization strategies: the Eisenhower Matrix, ABC ranking, time blocking, weighted scoring, and the Pareto Principle.

## Core Concepts

- [[Eisenhower Matrix]] — sorting tasks by urgency and importance into four quadrants to decide what to do, schedule, delegate, or drop
- [[Weighted Scoring]] — ranking tasks by a score combining importance, urgency, impact, and effort rather than a single criterion
- [[Pareto Principle]] — roughly 80% of results come from 20% of effort; used here to justify concentrating on high-value activities
- [[20-Minute Rule]] — a focused sprint block used to force progress on a task before deciding whether to continue or stop

## Key Takeaways

- **Common failure modes**: poor planning, lack of organization, feeling overwhelmed, procrastination, distractions, difficulty saying no, and low self-discipline.
- **Core skills**: making a plan, prioritized lists over to-do lists, starting early, chunking tasks, deliberate decision-making, delegation, SMART goals, realistic deadlines, noticing drift, boundary-setting, minimizing distractions, stress management, avoiding multitasking, the 20-minute rule, taking breaks, and building a consistent personal system.
- **Prioritization toolkit**: Eisenhower Matrix, ABC prioritization, time blocking, weighted scoring, and the Pareto Principle, used together with regular review as priorities shift.
- **Multitasking specifically flagged**: research cited shows it hampers productivity versus sequential single-tasking.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What's the difference between a prioritized list and a plain to-do list, per this framework?
**A**: A prioritized list ranks tasks by importance and is worked in that order, producing a sense of progress and focus, versus an unordered to-do list that risks working on whatever is easiest rather than what matters most.

**Q2**: How does the Pareto Principle apply to task prioritization?
**A**: Since roughly 80% of results come from about 20% of effort, identifying and prioritizing that high-leverage 20% produces disproportionately better outcomes than treating all tasks as equally valuable.

**Q3**: What is the 20-minute rule and what problem does it solve?
**A**: A focused 20-minute sprint on a task with a decision point at the end (continue or stop) — it counters procrastination by making the initial commitment small and concrete rather than open-ended.
