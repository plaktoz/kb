## Summary

A sourced statistics roundup finds that most workers lack any formal time-management system, spend just 20% of the workday on activities that actually matter, and lose over 10 hours a week to meetings alone — while better time management is strongly linked (self-reported) to lower stress and higher confidence, focus, and performance.

## Core Concepts

- [[Interruption Recovery Cost]] — each interruption costs about 25 minutes to fully recover focus from, compounding across ~60 daily interruptions
- [[Meeting Load by Seniority]] — time spent in meetings scales sharply with seniority: 6% of the week for an average employee, 35% for middle management, 50% for upper management, 72% for a CEO
- [[Skill-Based Work Share]] — employees spend only 27% of their time on the work they were actually hired to do, per an Asana study
- [[Unrecorded Task Cost]] — unrecorded quick tasks (untracked emails, meetings, document searches) cost the US economy an estimated 50 million productive hours per day ($7.4B/day)

## Key Takeaways

- **No system**: 82% of people have no dedicated time-management system; 25% simply react to things as they arise.
- **Low-value time**: the average worker spends 51% of the workday on tasks of little to no value (~20 hours/week).
- **Meetings**: 72% of meetings are considered unproductive, costing businesses an estimated 24 billion hours and $37 billion annually; attention during a meeting drops from ~91% to 64% after 15 minutes.
- **Email**: the average worker checks email 15x/day and spends 2+ hours managing it, 1.8 of which is spent on irrelevant messages.
- **Perceived benefits**: 90% believe better time management would raise productivity; 91% believe it would lower stress and improve focus, goal achievement, decision-making, and confidence.
- **Interruption cost**: employees can recover up to 40 hours a month simply by better managing interruptions, which otherwise take ~25 minutes each to recover from.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What share of the average workday goes to genuinely high-value, skill-based work?
**A**: Only about 27% of the day, per an Asana-sourced statistic cited in the article — the rest goes to email, coordination, meetings, and social media.

**Q2**: How does meeting burden scale with organizational seniority?
**A**: Sharply — from about 6% of the week for an average employee up to 72% of the week for a CEO, with middle and upper management in between at 35% and 50%.

**Q3**: What is the estimated productivity cost of unproductive meetings specifically?
**A**: About 24 billion hours and $37 billion annually across businesses, driven partly by the fact that attention during a meeting falls from roughly 91% to 64% after the first 15 minutes.
