## Summary

Federal workforce reforms in 2025 drove measurable increases in employee burnout and a sharp drop in engagement scores, and the author argues agencies should treat employee wellbeing and workers'-compensation risk management as linked rather than separate concerns.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Partnership for Public Service]] 2025 Public Service Viewpoint Survey**: Developed after the government discontinued its long-running Federal Employee Viewpoint Survey; found overall engagement scores fell to 32 out of 100.
- **Burnout-to-claims pipeline**: Higher stress and burnout are linked to more complex workers'-compensation claims and longer recovery timelines, even when the underlying injury isn't directly caused by workplace stress.
- **[[Sedgwick Government Solutions]]**: The author's employer, a workers'-compensation case-management provider advocating for early intervention tied to workforce wellbeing data.

## Key Takeaways

- Overall federal employee engagement scores fell to 32/100, with more than half of employees reporting worse engagement over the past year.
- Employees remained committed to public service despite the drop — the disconnect was specifically between dedication to the work and confidence in the systems supporting them.
- Injured employees in high-stress environments require more behavioral-health support within workers'-comp claims and face added challenges returning to work.
- Recommended focus areas: clearly defined job roles with manageable workloads, positive organizational culture, and reducing stigma around accessing health care.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Federal agencies should treat employee burnout as a workforce risk-management issue, not just an employee-experience issue, because it directly affects workers'-compensation outcomes and organizational continuity.

**Q2**: What specific data shows the scale of the engagement problem?
**A**: Overall federal employee engagement scores fell to 32 out of 100 per the 2025 Public Service Viewpoint Survey, with more than half of employees reporting worsened engagement year-over-year.

**Q3**: How does burnout concretely affect workers'-compensation claims?
**A**: Employees under higher stress tend to have more complex claims, need greater behavioral-health support, and face more difficulty coordinating a return to work — even when their injury isn't directly caused by workplace stress.
