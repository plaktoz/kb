## Summary

Gallup's Q3 2026 survey of 21,648 U.S. employees finds that frequent AI users spend less time gathering information and writing, while collaboration, customer work and meetings mostly stay the same. Frequent use rose to 32% of employees, and fewer than one in three in AI-adopting organizations say AI has raised daily output expectations.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Frequent AI Use]]**: using AI a few times a week or more; 32% of U.S. employees in Q3 2026, up two points, with half of them using it daily (16% of all employees).
- **[[Remote-Capable Roles]]**: desk-based jobs are the heaviest users, at 49% frequent use versus 22% in on-site roles.
- **Leaders vs. individual contributors**: among remote-capable workers, leaders use AI frequently more often (57% vs. 46%), and 64% of leaders hold remote-capable jobs versus 46% of contributors.
- **Time reallocation**: frequent users spend less time on information gathering (52%), writing (47%), data analysis (38%) and administrative tasks (29%).
- **Human-centered work stays stable**: 82% report no change in customer time, 78% in coworker collaboration, 83% in meetings.
- **[[Mental Effort]]**: 65% of frequent users report no change; 21% report less and 14% more.
- **[[Performance Expectations]]**: 29% in AI-adopting organizations say AI raised daily output expectations; 33% of frequent users feel pressure to use AI to keep up.
- **Skill shift**: emphasis may move toward [[AI-Assisted Work Review]], applying information in context and working with others.

## Key Takeaways

- **Usage Growth**: 53% of employees use AI at least a few times a year.
- **Time Savings**: 52% of frequent users spend less time gathering information.
- **Review Burden**: About one in five spend more time reviewing and correcting work.
- **Stable Collaboration**: Time with coworkers and customers largely unchanged by AI.
- **Effort Unchanged**: 65% of frequent users report the same mental effort.
- **Expectations**: Only 29% in AI-adopting organizations say output expectations rose.
- **Role Gap**: Leaders and remote-capable workers use AI far more than others.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Gallup's central finding about how AI changes work time?
**A**: Frequent users mostly spend less time gathering information and writing, while most other activities stay about the same. Time with coworkers and customers is especially stable.

**Q2**: What data show how AI use differs across worker groups?
**A**: 49% of remote-capable employees use AI frequently versus 22% of on-site employees. Among remote-capable workers, 57% of leaders use it frequently versus 46% of individual contributors.

**Q3**: What implication does Gallup draw for organizations?
**A**: Skills may shift toward reviewing AI-assisted work, applying information in context and collaborating, which affects hiring and development. Evidence that AI is raising output expectations remains limited.
