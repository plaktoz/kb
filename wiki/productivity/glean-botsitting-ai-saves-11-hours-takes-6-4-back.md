## Summary
Glean's Work AI Index 2026 finds workers save about 11 hours a week with AI but spend 6.4 hours "botsitting": feeding it context, checking output, and rerunning prompts. Only 13% say their organization performs significantly better. Tech leaders argue the gap closes through work design, expertise, and shared agent supervision, not more tokens or tools.

## Core Concepts
- [[Botsitting]]: the work of supplying missing context, verifying output, debugging mistakes, and rerunning prompts; coined by [[Glean]]'s Work AI Index.
- [[Botshitting]]: passing along AI output unchecked; more likely at companies with recent AI-attributed layoffs.
- [[Context Tax]]: time spent re-explaining projects across tools; APIs and [[Model Context Protocol]] give connectivity, not context.
- [[Tool Sprawl]]: 77% of AI users switch tools weekly, and a third use four or more.
- [[Mark Hoffman]]: Glean Work AI research lead; says domain expertise decides who succeeds with AI.
- [[Fabien Potencier]]: Upsun executive; reports more individual productivity but not more output.
- [[Sherif Mansour]]: Atlassian head of AI; describes work shifting from doing to architecting.
- [[Shared Agent Sandboxes]]: moving agents from private laptops into shared cloud environments so teams supervise together.
- [[Work Design]]: treating AI as a work-design problem rather than a procurement one.

## Key Takeaways
- **Time Math**: 11 hours saved weekly, 6.4 hours spent botsitting.
- **Org Gap**: Only 13% say their organization performs significantly better.
- **Context Fatigue**: Each 10% more time feeding context makes workers 25% likelier to feel worn out.
- **Retention Risk**: Frequent botsitters are 73% more likely to seek another job.
- **Tool Sprawl**: Multi-tool users are 35% more likely to botsit frequently.
- **Individual vs. Team**: "More productivity but not more output" after unlimited coding-agent access.
- **Token Spend**: No correlation between productivity and token spend; measure quality too.
- **Expertise Matters**: Senior engineers and managers get more value from AI than juniors.
- **Traceability**: Atlassian shows the responsible human behind every agent action, plus citations.
- **Vendor Caveat**: Glean sells context tooling, so the diagnosis doubles as a pitch.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Measured proxies detach from outcomes** — When a proxy such as token usage is treated as the goal, it stops tracking real productivity; quality must be measured alongside it. · *model:* [[Goodhart's Law]]
→ explains: Token Spend, Individual vs. Team

**T2. Tools amplify the skill already present** — AI multiplies existing judgment and expertise, good or bad, so output quality tracks the user's domain knowledge. · *model:* [[Leverage]]
→ explains: Expertise Matters

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is botsitting and why does it matter?
**A**: Botsitting is the work of feeding AI context, checking output, debugging mistakes, and rerunning prompts, averaging 6.4 hours a week. It eats much of the 11 hours AI saves, which helps explain why only 13% report organizational gains.

**Q2**: What evidence links context work and tool sprawl to worker strain?
**A**: Each 10% more time feeding context makes workers 25% more likely to feel worn out, and multi-tool users are 35% more likely to botsit frequently. Frequent botsitters are 73% more likely to be job hunting.

**Q3**: How can organizations make AI supervision worthwhile?
**A**: Treat AI as a work-design problem: measure quality as well as productivity, move agents into shared sandboxes, show the human behind agent actions, and train people on basics. High achievers use AI for grunt work and keep their own judgment where it matters.
