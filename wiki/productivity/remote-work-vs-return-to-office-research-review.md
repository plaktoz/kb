## Summary
A review of 14 studies on remote work and return-to-office (RTO) policy finds no single study resolves the debate, but together they show [[Hybrid Work]] of two to three office days has become the stable post-pandemic equilibrium — avoiding most downsides of full remote while preserving the flexibility workers value most. The research agrees office attendance has stabilized 30-40% below pre-pandemic levels with no sign of reverting, but clashes sharply on whether full-remote work helps or hurts productivity and well-being.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Hybrid Work]]** — two to three days in office; the arrangement 6 in 10 remote-capable workers prefer over any other.
- **[[Return-to-Office Mandates]]** — policies requiring on-site work; the research doesn't support them as a clear productivity fix.
- **[[Trip.com]] randomized controlled trial** (Bloom, Han & Liang, *Nature*) — two-year RCT finding zero performance impact from hybrid alongside a 33% attrition reduction.
- **[[Nicholas Bloom]]** and coauthors — Stanford-linked researchers whose Indian IT-firm study found full-remote output productivity fell 8-19% even as hours rose.
- **[[Cognitive Reserve]]**-adjacent well-being findings — remote work is a "double-edged sword," easing commute stress while raising isolation.

## Key Takeaways
- **14 studies span methodologies**; no single one settles the RTO debate on its own.
- **Hybrid RCT result**: Trip.com found zero performance impact plus 33% less attrition after two years.
- **Full-remote productivity fell 8-19%** in a single Indian IT firm study, even as hours increased.
- **Tech workers valued remote access at ~25% of pay** in real job-offer decisions, far above survey estimates.
- **Office attendance stabilized 30-40% below pre-pandemic levels**, per McKinsey, Gallup, and BLS data.
- **53% of remote workers** report it's harder to feel connected to coworkers (Pew Research).
- **Managers who tried hybrid updated their views positive** after experiencing it firsthand.
- **17% of workers who quit** did so specifically because working arrangements changed (McKinsey).

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Office attendance is an easy-to-measure proxy for productivity, but the research shows presence and output are decoupled — mandating the proxy doesn't recover the underlying goal.
- **[[Streetlight Effect]]**: Most RTO research measures what's easy to observe — satisfaction, attendance — rather than the harder engineering- or output-specific metrics that would actually settle the debate.

## 🃏 Review Questions
**Q1**: What does the review of 14 studies conclude about the RTO vs. remote debate?
**A**: No single study settles it, but together they show hybrid work of two to three office days has become the stable equilibrium, avoiding most downsides of full remote while preserving the flexibility workers value most.

**Q2**: What did the Trip.com randomized controlled trial find?
**A**: After two years, hybrid workers showed zero performance difference versus fully in-office workers, while attrition fell 33%.

**Q3**: Why do the full-remote productivity findings conflict with other data showing productivity gains?
**A**: The 8-19% productivity decline came from a single Indian IT firm under mandatory pandemic-era full remote — an extreme condition — while U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics data found industries that adopted remote work most heavily saw total factor productivity gains, suggesting remote's effect is highly context-dependent.
