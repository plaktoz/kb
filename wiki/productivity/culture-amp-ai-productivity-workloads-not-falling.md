## Summary

Culture Amp's first AI at Work Benchmark (data representing ~112,000 employees) finds 71% of employees feel more productive with AI — 93% of power users — yet workload perceptions barely differ by usage intensity (72% of power users vs. 69% of non-users say workload is reasonable). The article frames this as the "AI dividend" question: if AI creates capacity, who benefits, and is every saved hour simply refilled with more work? Employees are experimenting and understand the risks, but leadership direction, manager guidance and career visibility lag behind.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Culture Amp]] AI at Work Benchmark**: Groups employees into non-users, light users, daily users and power users; measures perceptions, not objective output or hours.
- **[[AI Dividend]]**: The capacity AI frees up, which organizations can allocate to more output, better quality, innovation, development time or reduced pressure.
- **Workload creep**: Saved hours immediately replaced with new tasks, so efficiency becomes the new baseline expectation.
- **[[Workslop]]**: Prior research found two-thirds of employees spend up to six hours a week correcting poor AI-generated work — AI can save one worker time while creating work for another.
- **Encouragement–direction gap**: 85% say their organization encourages AI experimentation, but only 58% say leaders explained where the company is headed with it.
- **[[Amy Lavoie]]**: VP of People Science at Culture Amp; argues returns will go to organizations that decide explicitly what to stop doing with reclaimed time, and that leaders owe employees candor over certainty.
- **Career visibility decline**: Awareness of internal career opportunities fell 10 points since July 2025, the largest single-year move in the benchmark.
- **Broader [[AI ROI]] measurement**: Quality, workload, rework, skills development, customer outcomes and sustainable productivity — not just adoption and time saved.

## Key Takeaways

- **Perceived productivity**: 71% feel more productive with AI; 93% of power users.
- **Flat workloads**: Reasonable workload 72% power users vs. 69% non-users.
- **Motivation gap**: 72% of power users feel motivated to go further vs. 57% non-users.
- **Causation caveat**: Motivated employees may simply be likelier to adopt AI.
- **Strategy gap**: 85% encouraged to experiment; only 58% told the direction.
- **Manager gap**: Only 60% say managers share practical AI examples.
- **Risk awareness**: 86% understand AI risks; 67% non-users to 95% power users.
- **Trust**: 80% trust their organization to use AI responsibly.
- **Weak infrastructure**: Systems and processes supporting AI scored 64%.
- **Career fog**: Career-opportunity awareness down 10 points; development satisfaction steady at 66%.
- **Recommendation**: Decide what the AI dividend is for before pushing adoption.
- **Recommendation**: Measure capacity and rework, not just adoption rates.
- **Recommendation**: Protect reclaimed time for learning and human development.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Parkinson's Law]]**: Work expands to fill the time available — without an explicit decision on where saved hours go, AI-created capacity is absorbed by new tasks, leaving workloads flat.
- **[[Jevons Paradox]]**: Making each task cheaper to complete increases total task demand rather than reducing total effort, which explains why higher perceived productivity doesn't lower perceived workload.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of Culture Amp's AI at Work Benchmark?
**A**: Employees feel markedly more productive with AI (71%, rising to 93% for power users), but their workload perceptions barely change with usage, raising the question of where AI-created capacity goes.

**Q2**: What gap exists between AI encouragement and leadership direction?
**A**: 85% of employees say their organization encourages AI experimentation, but only 58% say leaders have explained where the company is headed with it — a 27-point gap — and only 60% say managers share practical examples.

**Q3**: How should a leader act on these findings?
**A**: Explicitly decide what the AI dividend is for — output, quality, development or reduced pressure — measure capacity and rework rather than just adoption, and watch for workload creep so every saved hour isn't automatically refilled.
