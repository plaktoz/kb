## Summary

MyPerfectResume's Visibility at Work report, a national survey of 1,000 U.S. workers, finds 70% believe highly visible employees are rewarded more than those who quietly produce strong results. More than half (56%) have used tactics to appear busy, and 1 in 5 spend at least a quarter of their workday managing appearances rather than doing meaningful work. Workers attribute the pressure to a need to constantly prove their value, job insecurity, burnout, and workplace monitoring.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Workplace Visibility]]** — the perception that being seen as active and available is rewarded over quiet, high-quality output; 25% say "definitely," 45% "somewhat."
- **[[Pseudo-Productivity]] / appearance management** — behaviors aimed at looking busy: delaying simple tasks, sending status messages to appear active, staying logged in longer, stretching out tasks, taking on visible busywork, scheduling unnecessary meetings.
- **[[Always-On Culture]]** — immediate message responses as a visibility tactic; 84% have responded instantly to signal attentiveness.
- **Idle anxiety** — 82% have worried that appearing "too idle" could hurt job security or career growth.
- **[[Workplace Surveillance]] and trust** — 30% blame excessive monitoring and 28% say employees don't feel trusted by management.
- **[[Jasmine Escalera]]** — MyPerfectResume career expert, who urges making contributions visible through clear results and communication rather than performed busyness.

## Key Takeaways

- **70% say** visibility is rewarded more than quiet strong results.
- **56% have used** at least one tactic to appear busy; 44% never have.
- **Most common tactic**: delaying simple tasks to appear busy longer (25%).
- **65% spend** some of the workday managing appearances; 20% spend 25%+.
- **77% feel** pressure to look productive or available; 30% often or constantly.
- **Top cited cause**: pressure to constantly prove value (50%).
- **43% would feel** less pressure to appear busy in a stronger job market.
- **Stress sources**: actual workload 38%; appearing productive/available combined 35%.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When visible activity becomes the proxy managers reward, workers optimize for looking busy (delaying tasks, staged responsiveness) rather than the results the proxy was meant to measure.
- **[[Signaling Theory]]**: Without a trusted way to observe output, workers send costly but cheap-to-fake signals of effort — instant replies, staying online — to show commitment and protect job security.
