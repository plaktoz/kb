## Summary

By mid-2026, a handful of large employers have moved to full five-day RTO mandates, yet the share of the US workforce actually working from home has barely budged — reflecting that mandates are concentrated among a small number of highly visible firms. Research consistently shows RTO mandates drive measurable senior-talent attrition and hiring friction while delivering no proven gain in engineering-specific metrics like deployment frequency or code quality. For engineering leaders, the evidence favors periodic in-person offsites over blanket mandates.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Return to Office]] (RTO) mandates**: Policies requiring employees to work on-site, adopted by companies like [[Amazon]], [[JPMorgan Chase]], [[Microsoft]], and [[Meta]] at varying day counts
- **[[Distributed Engineering Teams]]**: Software teams operating asynchronously across locations; structurally dependent on deep, uninterrupted work and async collaboration
- **[[Deep Work]]**: 2–4 hour uninterrupted blocks essential for debugging, architectural design, and infrastructure work — the primary productivity lever for engineers
- **[[Work-From-Home]] (WFH) Research**: Stanford's Survey of Business Uncertainty (led by [[Nicholas Bloom]]) tracking actual WFH rates vs. mandate rhetoric
- **[[Senior Talent Attrition]]**: University of Chicago/Michigan study finding RTO mandates caused 4–15 point drops in senior employee share, with departures flowing to direct competitors
- **[[Developer Experience]]**: [[Microsoft]] and [[GitHub]]'s joint research finding deep-work time is the single strongest lever for individual developer output
- **[[Hiring Pool]] size**: Remote postings attract 10–20x more applicants than equivalent in-person roles (Liao, Administrative Science Quarterly)

## Key Takeaways

- **RTO adoption gap**: Fortune 100 mandates rose sharply, but WFH share barely moved (21.2% → ~20.8%)
- **Actual utilization**: Office utilization at 56% in 2026, still below pre-pandemic 61%
- **Senior attrition**: RTO mandates caused 5-pt (Microsoft), 4-pt (Apple), 15-pt (SpaceX) drops in senior staff share
- **Abnormal turnover**: 13–14 pp rise in turnover at S&P 500 firms post-RTO; highest among women and skilled workers
- **Hiring friction**: Vacancy duration rose 23%, hire rates fell 17% at RTO-mandating firms
- **Deep work advantage**: Developers with dedicated deep-work time felt 50% more productive (2,000+ dev study)
- **Productivity evidence gap**: Most RTO research measures attendance/satisfaction, not code velocity or MTTR
- **In-person sweet spot**: Highest value for team formation, architectural sessions, and conflict resolution — not sprint work
- **Replacement cost**: Losing one employee costs 50–200% of annual salary (SHRM estimate)
- **High-performer intent**: Under strict mandates, 16% of high performers plan to leave — double the average rate

## First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Attendance metrics proxy for productivity, so mandating office presence optimizes for presence — not output — while actual engineering velocity remains unmeasured and unchanged.
- **[[Opportunity Cost]]**: A single-metro location requirement trades a global candidate pool for a local one, paying a permanent hiring-cost premium that compounds every quarter the mandate is in force.
