## Summary
The Pomodoro Technique — 25 minutes of focused work followed by a 5-minute break, with a longer break every four cycles — works by lowering the activation energy needed to start a task and by building recovery into the work rhythm so attention doesn't degrade over long stretches. The method requires choosing a single specific task, protecting the session as indivisible, and treating breaks as mandatory rather than optional.

## Core Concepts
Developed by [[Francesco Cirillo]] in the late 1980s, the [[Pomodoro Technique]] structures work into fixed, bounded sessions. It works via two mechanisms: lowering the activation energy for starting (committing to "25 minutes on the report," not finishing it) and protecting sustained concentration through built-in breaks, since [[Attention]] degrades over hours of uninterrupted effort. It also leverages [[Parkinson's Law]] — a visible time constraint sharpens focus and makes the cost of distraction more apparent. The technique pairs naturally with [[Time Blocking]], scheduling several Pomodoros as a protected calendar window.

## Key Takeaways
- **Structure**: 25 minutes focused work, 5-minute break; longer break (15-30 min) every four cycles.
- **Single task rule**: Choose one specific task before starting — vague goals waste the session's early minutes.
- **Indivisibility**: If interrupted for more than a few minutes, the session doesn't count and must restart.
- **Interruption protocol**: "Inform, negotiate, schedule, call back" — capture non-urgent interruptions as a task and return to work.
- **Common failure modes**: working multiple tasks per session, skipping breaks, and starting the timer without a planned task.
- **Adaptable length**: 25 minutes is a starting point; deep writing or complex programming may warrant 45-50 minute sessions after the base habit is established.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Activation Energy]]**: Framing a session as "25 minutes on X" rather than "finish X" reduces the psychological barrier to starting, the same way lowering a reaction's activation energy makes it proceed more readily.
- **[[Parkinson's Law]]**: Work expands to fill the time available; a hard 25-minute boundary compresses effort and makes low-value distractions visibly costly in real time.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of the Pomodoro Technique?
**A**: Bounding work into fixed 25-minute sessions with mandatory breaks lowers the barrier to starting difficult tasks and protects sustained concentration better than open-ended work sessions.

**Q2**: What mechanism explains why breaks are not optional?
**A**: Attention degrades over hours of uninterrupted effort; the 5-minute break resets focus so a later session stays nearly as sharp as the first, rather than producing diminishing returns.

**Q3**: How should someone apply this if a genuine interruption occurs mid-session?
**A**: End the session (it doesn't count), use the "inform, negotiate, schedule, call back" approach to defer the interruption, then restart a fresh Pomodoro later.
