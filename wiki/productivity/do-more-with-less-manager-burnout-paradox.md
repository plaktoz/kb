## Summary

Across industries, "do more with less" staffing policies have quietly loaded managers with undisclosed second jobs — emotional labor, therapist-like support, and change translation — that their job descriptions never accounted for. An eight-month Berkeley Haas study found heavy AI users at a 200-person tech company reported higher burnout than non-users, since AI expanded task loads rather than shrinking them, compounding a workforce-wide retention and resilience crisis.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Productivity Paradox]] (staffing variant)** — automation and AI tools are marketed to buy back time, but Berkeley Haas research found they instead expanded what people do, with heavy AI users reporting more burnout than non-users.
- **Undisclosed second job** — managers absorbing emotional/relational labor (nursing director example: "50% of my job is now therapist") without formal recognition or staffing support.
- **[[Resource-Loss Spiral]]** — digital overload and eroded trust push employees toward "work less, make more" self-protective responses as they recognize their own replaceability.
- **Generational resilience gap** — Gen Z has overtaken millennials as the most burned-out generation (74% moderate-to-intense burnout per Aflac), with job insecurity as a top stressor for 65-75% of younger workers (APA).
- **Leadership perception gap** — APA Workforce data shows senior leaders feel optimistic about change while frontline teams report overwhelm and constant rework.

## Key Takeaways

- **Berkeley Haas 8-month study**: heavy AI users at a 200-person tech company reported higher burnout than non-users.
- **74% of Gen Z** report moderate to intense burnout, surpassing millennials.
- **65% of workers aged 26-43** and 75% of workers aged 18-25 cite job insecurity as a top stressor (APA).
- **Root cause is leadership practice**, not individual failure — e.g., a misguided staffing policy reshaping onboarding and expectations.
- **Fix requires workload audits** that account for emotional and task labor, not another wellness workshop.
- **Resilience is built through daily, repeated practices** (trust, predictable decisions, psychological safety), not one-off training.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Staffing policies optimized for headcount-efficiency metrics ("do more with less") ignore the unmeasured emotional labor that keeps teams functioning, so the target metric improves while the real system quietly breaks.
