## Summary

The cost of context switching sits between the effort a team puts in and the output it produces, so hours reports and task counts never capture it. Personal focus habits fall short because tools, handoffs, notifications and meetings build interruptions into the work. The lasting fix is redesigning how work reaches people, with AI absorbing summaries, routing and status updates.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Context-Switching Cost]]**: The recovery cost of moving attention between tasks, tools or conversations before the first is finished.
- **[[Gloria Mark]]**: UC Irvine researcher: 23 minutes 15 seconds on average to return to a task after an interruption; roughly half of interruptions are self-interruptions.
- **[[Tool Sprawl]]**: One of four sources of switching; a ticket may pass through a helpdesk, customer database, billing system and chat.
- **[[Handoffs]]**: Each person a task passes through adds a wait and a restart.
- **[[Notification Norms]]**: Senders decide when receivers' attention moves; Microsoft's 2025 Work Trend Index found the most-pinged employees interrupted about every two minutes.
- **[[Loaded Hourly Cost]]**: Pay plus benefits and overhead; an input to pricing switching cost.
- **[[AI Agents]]**: Can take over summarizing, routing and status updates that pull people off their work.
- **[[Systems Thinking]]**: Treating interruptions as a product of work design rather than individual discipline.

## Key Takeaways

- **Invisible Gap**: Hours measure input and task counts measure output; switching cost lands in neither.
- **Recovery Time**: 23 minutes 15 seconds to resume a task after an interruption (Mark).
- **Daily Toggling**: A 2022 HBR study found about 1,200 switches a day, costing just under four hours weekly.
- **Productive Time Lost**: APA's 2006 summary says switching can cost up to 40% of productive time.
- **Four Sources**: Tool sprawl, handoffs, notification norms, and meetings cut into the day.
- **Pricing Formula**: Switches per hour × recovery hours × loaded hourly cost × headcount.
- **Worked Example**: Ten support agents losing six minutes an hour at $60 lose $60 an hour.
- **Redesign Flow**: Fewer tools, batched handoffs, team-level protected blocks, and AI handling switching work.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why do standard productivity metrics miss the cost of context switching?
**A**: Hours measure what went in and task counts measure what came out, while the cost of getting from one to the other lands in neither column. A full calendar and a flat output line can therefore coexist.

**Q2**: What four inputs let a team price its own switching cost?
**A**: Switches per hour, recovery time in hours, loaded hourly cost, and headcount. In the example, a 10-person support team losing six minutes an hour at $60 loses $60 an hour.

**Q3**: How should a team reduce context switching rather than telling people to focus harder?
**A**: Change how work reaches people: fewer tools per task, batched handoffs, team-level protected blocks, and AI handling summaries, routing and status updates. Pick one team and one source, change it, and re-measure a month later.
