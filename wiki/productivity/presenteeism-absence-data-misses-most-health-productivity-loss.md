## Summary

HR Director's coverage of Vitality's Global Health and Productivity Index 2026 (with [[RAND Europe]]) argues that U.S. employers relying on absence records track the smaller problem: of 63.4 productive days lost per worker each year to poor health, 53.9 (85%) are lost to [[Presenteeism]] while only 9.5 come from absence. U.S. benefits lean toward treatment rather than prevention, and the index points to manager care and targeted risk identification as the main levers. See also [[vitality-workforce-health-productivity-index-2026]] for the press-release coverage of the same study.

## Core Concepts

- [[Presenteeism]] — showing up but performing below one's best due to poor physical or mental health; the dominant share of health-related productivity loss.
- [[Vitality Global Health and Productivity Index]] — converts missed time and reduced on-the-job performance into full working-day equivalents; 9,206 workers surveyed across the U.S., U.K., Germany, Japan and Singapore (2,079 U.S., Feb. 21–Mar. 16, 2026).
- [[Absence Tracking]] — captures roughly one in seven days lost to poor health, leaving most of the loss invisible.
- [[Treatment vs. Prevention Gap]] — U.S. employers offer broad health support, but only 18% of workers can access employer-organized screenings.
- [[Manager Care]] — workers who feel their manager genuinely cares lose nine fewer productive days a year.
- [[AI-Related Concern]] — one of five stacking risk areas, alongside mental wellbeing, physical health, work strain and lifestyle.
- [[Christian van Stolk]] (RAND Europe) and [[Maia Surmava]] (CEO, Vitality U.S.) — argue presenteeism has the greater impact and benefits systems should shift toward earlier risk identification.
- [[Rust-Out]] — another way employees can be present without being fully engaged, distinct from [[Burnout]].
- [[Commercial Interest Bias]] — Vitality sells health engagement programs, so it has a commercial stake in the findings.

## Key Takeaways

- **Presenteeism dominates**: 53.9 of 63.4 lost days (85%) occur while working.
- **Absence is small**: absence accounts for only 9.5 days, about one in seven.
- **Above peers**: U.S. 63.4 days vs. 54.8 five-market average; only Germany higher (69.3).
- **Economic cost**: poor workforce health costs the U.S. an estimated $2.33 trillion yearly.
- **Self-reported impact**: 32% say health medium/highly impacts on-the-job productivity.
- **Recent absence**: 14% missed over four hours of work last week for health.
- **Health burden**: 29% obese (vs. 18% average); 65% report moderate/serious distress.
- **Screening gap**: only 18% of U.S. workers access employer-organized health screenings.
- **Manager lever**: caring managers linked to nine fewer lost days per year.
- **AI concern cost**: high AI-concern workers (12.9%) lose 27.6 extra days yearly.
- **Stacked risk**: high risk in all five areas means ~119 lost days a year.
- **Source caveat**: Vitality has commercial interest; RAND Europe partnered independently.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Measuring the visible undercounts the invisible** — When an organization tracks only the loss that is easy to observe (absence), the larger, harder-to-see loss (reduced performance at work) goes unmanaged, so it ends up addressing the smaller problem. · *model:* [[Streetlight Effect]]
→ explains: Presenteeism dominates, Absence is small

**T2. Risk factors stack** — Productivity losses from separate risk areas accumulate, so a worker exposed to many risks at once loses far more than one exposed to any single risk.
→ explains: Stacked risk, AI concern cost

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central argument about how U.S. employers track health-related productivity loss?
**A**: Absence records capture only about one in seven days lost to poor health; 85% of the loss comes from presenteeism, so absence-focused employers are tracking the smaller problem.

**Q2**: What evidence does the index give for stacking risks?
**A**: Across five risk areas (mental wellbeing, physical health, AI-related concern, work strain, lifestyle), a worker at high risk in all five loses an estimated 119 productive days a year, close to half a working year.

**Q3**: Where does the index suggest employers start to reduce presenteeism?
**A**: With managers, since workers who feel their manager cares lose nine fewer days a year, and by shifting benefits toward earlier risk identification and prevention such as screenings, which only 18% of U.S. workers can access.
