## Summary

This analysis of the [[ActivTrak Productivity Lab]] study (9,140 workers, 467 enterprises) stresses that AI users' workdays run up to 90 minutes longer, but the finding is an association, not proof that AI causes longer days. It separates confirmed from unconfirmed claims, places the result in the context of the [[Productivity Paradox]] and [[Jevons Paradox]], and urges leaders to tie AI usage to measurable outcomes. See also [[activtrak-ai-users-longer-workdays-more-app-switching]] for the study's own framing.

## Core Concepts

- [[ActivTrak Productivity Lab]]: behavioral work-data research group; led by [[Gabriela Mauch]].
- [[Heavy AI Users]]: more than 45 minutes across at least five sessions per day.
- [[Correlation vs Causation]]: the study shows a link, not a cause; workload, ambition, or deadlines could explain it.
- [[Productivity Paradox]]: Solow's 1987 observation that computers showed up everywhere except productivity statistics.
- [[Jevons Paradox]]: efficiency gains can raise total consumption, so saved time gets absorbed by more work.
- [[Application Switching]] and [[Focus Sessions]]: heavy users switch more and focus in shorter stretches.
- [[AI Adoption Metrics]]: seats and prompts are easy to count but say little about business results.

## Key Takeaways

- **Workday Gap**: AI users' days span 8.42-9.08 hours versus 7.58 for non-users.
- **Headline Caveat**: The 90-minute figure is the top of the range, not an average.
- **Sample**: 9,140 knowledge workers, 467 enterprises, April 1 to September 25, 2026.
- **After Hours**: Heavy users work 18 more minutes after hours; share is 32.3% versus 31.9%.
- **Meetings**: Heavy users spend 3% of active time in meetings versus 5%.
- **Association Only**: Study cannot show AI causes longer days; reverse and third-factor stories fit.
- **Tools Unnamed**: The report does not name AI products, so no vendor claims hold.
- **Usage Is Not Outcome**: Mauch says leaders should treat usage as a means to measurable gains.
- **Practical Steps**: Pick one outcome per deployment; monitor workday span; protect focus blocks.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Association does not prove causation** — A link between two measures can reflect reverse causation, a third factor, or chance; only controlled before-and-after comparisons can show cause. · *model:* [[Correlation vs Causation]]
→ explains: Association Only, Headline Caveat, Workday Gap

**T2. Usage is a proxy, not the outcome** — Measuring adoption rewards more usage, not better results; value must be tied to measurable gains. · *model:* [[Goodhart's Law]]
→ explains: Usage Is Not Outcome, Practical Steps

**T3. Efficiency gains get absorbed by expanded demand** — When a tool lowers the cost of producing work, teams ask for more, so the day grows to fit new capacity. · *model:* [[Jevons Paradox]]
→ explains: Workday Gap, After Hours

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the study's central finding, and what does it not show?
**A**: AI users' workdays run up to 90 minutes longer than non-users'. It shows an association only, not that AI causes the longer days.

**Q2**: What data supports the finding, and how are heavy users defined?
**A**: The analysis covers 9,140 knowledge workers at 467 enterprises over roughly 25 weeks. Heavy users spend more than 45 minutes across at least five AI sessions per day.

**Q3**: How should leaders act on the findings?
**A**: Treat AI usage as a means to measurable productivity gains, picking one outcome per deployment, tracking workday span, and protecting focus blocks.
