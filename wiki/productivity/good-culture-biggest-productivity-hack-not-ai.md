## Summary

Gregor Ojstersek argues that organizational culture is a stronger productivity driver than AI tooling, because AI amplifies existing conditions — good or bad. Executives chasing competitor "10x productivity" claims create FOMO-driven pressure that undermines psychological safety, while the real leadership question is how to build an environment where great people do their best work and AI multiplies them.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Conway's Law]]**: Systems mirror organizational communication structures; product quality and productivity are reflections of culture first, tooling second.
- **[[Psychological Safety]]**: Top-down AI mandates erode it by signaling distrust and generating performance anxiety over narratives that are often marketing.
- **[[AI Amplification Effect]]**: AI does not fix broken processes — it accelerates them. Bad [[Communication]] gets worse; bad [[Software Architecture]] gets worse.
- **[[Bottom-Up Adoption]]**: Because the AI landscape changes too rapidly for top-down mandates, knowledge must flow laterally across teams through voluntary experimentation.
- **[[FOMO-Driven Leadership]]**: CEOs who react to competitor productivity claims without scrutinizing their origin (often product partnerships) make culture-damaging decisions.

## Key Takeaways

- AI amplifies existing culture — bad communication + AI = worse communication.
- [[Conway's Law]] means org structure determines system quality more than tooling.
- "10x productivity" claims are often marketing tied to product partnerships.
- Forcing AI top-down fails; lateral knowledge sharing is the right vector.
- Culture health markers: clear ownership, psychological safety, outcome rewards, blame-free retrospectives.
- The real question: build an org where great people thrive, then multiply with AI.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Conway's Law]]**: Organizations ship what their communication structure allows — AI tooling layered on poor communication reproduces and amplifies those structural flaws at scale.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When executives chase a competitor's "10x productivity" headline, productivity becomes the target metric and teams optimize for its proxies (AI usage stats, throughput numbers) rather than actual output quality.
- **[[Leverage Points]]**: Culture sits higher in the system hierarchy than tooling; changing a high-leverage point (culture) produces compounding returns that low-leverage changes (adding an AI tool) cannot replicate.
