## Summary

Lenny Rachitsky compiles ten specific, durable time-management tactics he uses daily — from calendaring to-dos and the two-minute rule to fiercely protected deep-work blocks and a deliberate practice of saying no — drawn from books like *Getting Things Done*, *Deep Work*, and *Indistractable*.

## Core Concepts

- [[Calendar-as-To-Do-List]] — slotting tasks directly into calendar events rather than a separate app
- [[Two-Minute Rule]] — from David Allen's [[Getting Things Done]]: do it immediately if it takes under two minutes
- [[Deep Work]] — Cal Newport's concept of focused, cognitively demanding, undistracted work, protected via recurring weekly blocks
- [[Async-First Communication]] — converting meeting requests into email threads to avoid the setup/teardown cost of synchronous meetings
- [[LNO Framework]] — Shreyas Doshi's Leverage/Neutral/Overhead task classification used to decide what to delegate

## Key Takeaways

- **Calendar to-dos** instead of app-based lists — "your calendar is your personal roadmap"
- **1-3 top tasks per morning**, written down before opening the to-do list, to prevent easy tasks from crowding out important ones
- **Deep-work blocks need 30-60 minutes to enter** and must never be scheduled over — 2-3 recurring weekly blocks were the author's highest-leverage habit
- **"No meetings before 3pm"** rule protects peak-focus hours for generative work
- **Async pivot template**: "Could we start async over email? What are you most looking for my input on?" turns most meetings into 5-minute tasks
- **Saying no requires prioritizing AND communicating** the tradeoff to whoever made the ask — silently absorbing work or silently declining both fail

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Loss Aversion]]**: The "waiting for" list and two-minute rule both work by externalizing open loops from working memory — Zeigarnik-effect-style unfinished tasks otherwise consume background cognitive load even when not actively being worked on, so writing them down converts anxiety into a retrievable record.
