## Summary

The Two-Hour Rule asks founders to protect two focused hours each weekday, ideally before communication opens, for the single most important task. Two hours is long enough to produce real work (500+ hours a year) yet small enough to defend. The article offers a four-week rollout and long-term rules for guarding the block.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Two-Hour Rule]]** — Two protected, single-task hours per weekday, same time and place
- **[[Deep Work]]** — Zero notifications, phone in another room, Slack and email closed
- **[[Time Blocking]]** — A calendar block ("Deep Work") treated as immovable; meeting requests inside it rejected
- **[[Habit Loop]]** — Same time, same desk, same coffee and ritual to lock in the habit
- **[[Environment Design]]** — The physical space triggers the mental state
- **Founder examples** — [[Bill Gates]] (strategic memos), [[Jeff Bezos]] (long-form documents), [[Paul Graham]] (essays), [[Naval Ravikant]] (reading and thinking)
- **[[Compounding]]** — 2 hours x weekdays = 500+ hours a year, about three months of focused work

## Key Takeaways

- **Why two hours**: Meaningful output, compounding progress, and a schedule defensible during chaos.
- **Timing**: Before Slack, email and meetings, e.g. 6:30-8:30 a.m.
- **Single task**: One most-important thing, not three.
- **Founder obstacles**: Constant urgency, felt obligation to be reachable, guilt about "slacking".
- **Rollout**: Block week 1, tell team week 2, add anchor week 3, track output week 4.
- **Defense**: Reject all meeting requests in the block for the first year.
- **Maintenance**: Review weekly; audit quarterly how well the block was defended.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Small, consistent blocks compound** — A modest daily time commitment, protected without exception, accumulates into a large volume of focused work. · *model:* [[Compounding]]

**T2. Fixed cues speed entry into focus** — Doing the same activity at the same time and place builds anticipation, so the brain enters the work state faster. · *model:* [[Habit Loop]]

**T3. Focus requires protected conditions** — Focus survives only when interruption channels are closed before the day's reactive demands begin. · *model:* [[Environment Design]]
