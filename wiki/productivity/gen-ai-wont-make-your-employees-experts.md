## Summary

Generative AI accelerates novice task completion but does not close the performance gap between novices and experts — the underlying judgment, pattern recognition, and error-detection that define expertise are not transferred by AI tooling. Organizations betting that AI-assisted juniors equal experienced seniors are likely trading hard-to-recover institutional knowledge for short-term cost savings.

## Core Concepts

- [[Expertise Gap]] — the persistent performance difference between novices and experts, even with AI augmentation
- [[David Autor]] — MIT economist; research shows gen AI "shortens time for novices to gain competence"
- [[AI-Augmented Novice]] — worker using gen AI to perform tasks above their raw skill level
- [[Tacit Knowledge]] — the judgment and pattern recognition that experts hold and AI cannot replicate
- [[Workforce Strategy]] — risk of eliminating expert roles in favor of cheaper AI-assisted juniors
- [[Skill Atrophy]] — organizational capability degradation when expert roles are cut rather than developed

## Key Takeaways

- **Gap persists**: AI narrows the novice-expert gap but does not eliminate it.
- **Novices get faster**: Time-to-competence shrinks with AI — but peak capability ceiling stays lower.
- **Expert judgment survives**: Quality discrimination, edge-case handling, and strategic framing remain expert advantages.
- **Organizational risk**: Cutting expert roles for AI-augmented juniors destroys hard-to-rebuild capabilities.
- **Applicable to copywriting, software**: Both fields cited as industries where this pattern is actively playing out.
