## Summary

"Using AI" is now a baseline expectation, not a differentiator; the real competitive advantage lies in four meta-skills layered on top: knowing when to delegate versus collaborate versus go manual ([[Cockpit Rule]]), designing AI-native workflows that compound over time, crafting narratives that transform information into meaning ([[Storytelling]]), and intentionally preserving cognitive sharpness by doing some work without AI ([[Manual Override]]). Each skill is grounded in research from Wharton, Harvard, BCG, MIT, and McGill. The core thesis is that AI will atrophy thinking only if left unchecked by deliberate habits.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Cockpit Rule]]**: Mental model for AI task delegation — autopilot (AI handles solo), collaboration (human + AI iterate), or manual (human only). Developed via [[Ethan Mollick]]'s agentic cost-benefit framework.
- **Agentic Cost-Benefit Framework** ([[Ethan Mollick]], Wharton): Compares human baseline time, AI probability of success, and AI process time to choose the right mode.
- **Build the Rails**: Workflow design as the new competitive advantage — breaking recurring deliverables into steps and assigning each to the right AI mode. Backed by Harvard/BCG study of 758 consultants (centaurs and cyborgs outperformed unstructured AI users by 19 percentage points).
- **Centaurs vs Cyborgs**: Centaurs divide tasks cleanly between human and AI; cyborgs integrate AI into every micro-step. Both outperform ad-hoc AI users.
- **[[Storytelling]] Moat**: AI can generate information but not meaning. The [[ABT Framework]] (And-But-Therefore, [[Randy Olsen]]) and [[SCQA Framework]] (Situation-Complication-Question-Answer, McKinsey/BCG) both work by introducing conflict then resolving it.
- **[[Manual Override]]**: Intentionally working without AI to prevent cognitive atrophy. McGill GPS study and Microsoft/Carnegie Mellon research show over-reliance degrades independent reasoning.
- **Think First, Prompt Second**: Form your own analysis before engaging AI — preserves independent reasoning while still benefiting from AI speed.
- **Interrogate the Output**: Actively challenge AI answers with counterarguments and what-if scenarios rather than passively consuming them.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI proficiency is now table stakes** — not a differentiator on its own.
- **Cockpit Rule**: Match delegation mode (autopilot/collab/manual) to task context and AI capability.
- **Process beats model**: Harvard/BCG — structured AI users outperformed unstructured by 19 percentage points.
- **Single-prompt vs workflow**: Andrew Ng example — code success rate jumped from 48% to 95% with a multi-step workflow.
- **Storytelling = conflict + resolution**: ABT and SCQA frameworks both rely on introducing tension, then resolving it.
- **GPS brain study (McGill)**: Heavy GPS reliance caused measurable changes in spatial navigation circuits.
- **Radiologist anchoring**: AI-first review led to anchoring bias; human-first + AI-second maintained accuracy.
- **AI tutoring upside**: World Bank — 6 weeks of structured AI tutoring equaled 2 years of traditional schooling.
- **Habits, not hardware**: AI harms thinking only when it changes behavior; the tool is neutral.

## First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When "AI usage" becomes the performance target, workers optimize for prompting volume rather than actual output quality — exactly why structured process (rails) matters more than raw AI access.
- **[[Cognitive Atrophy]]**: Competencies not exercised degrade — the weightlifting-belt analogy illustrates that outsourcing every cognitive step to AI is structurally equivalent to never training stabilizer muscles; periodic manual override is the deliberate practice that maintains the underlying capability.
- **[[Information Commodity Trap]]**: In a world where anyone can generate information instantly, scarcity shifts to meaning-making — storytelling becomes more valuable, not less, as AI lowers the cost of raw content production.
