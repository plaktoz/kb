## Summary

Effective time management comes from controlling priorities rather than every minute of the day — organizing the schedule around the highest-value tasks, protecting peak-energy hours, and reducing decision friction through batching, time-blocking, and delegation.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Priority-Based Scheduling]]**: Determine the most valuable tasks before checking email or messages, which otherwise let other people's priorities set the day's agenda.
- **[[Time Blocking]]**: Assigning specific periods to specific activities reduces the cognitive cost of deciding what to do next and protects focus from interruption.
- **[[Task Batching]]**: Grouping similar low-focus activities (email, paperwork, calls) into dedicated windows reduces the mental cost of switching between unrelated task types.
- **[[Chronotype-Aware Scheduling]]**: Reserving peak-energy hours for demanding creative/analytical work and lower-energy periods for routine tasks, since concentration ability varies predictably across the day.

## Key Takeaways

- Distinguish "busy" from "productive": completing many small tasks (like clearing an inbox) doesn't necessarily advance high-value goals.
- Leave deliberate flexibility in the schedule rather than filling every slot — unexpected disruptions are absorbed by slack, not squeezed out of already-tight time.
- Set personal deadlines ahead of actual due dates, broken into milestones, since waiting for external deadlines invites last-minute work.
- Effective delegation requires communicating the desired outcome and deadline clearly, not just assigning a task and hoping for the best.
- Environmental changes (removing phone access, blocking distracting sites during focus periods) reduce reliance on willpower to avoid distraction.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of the article?
**A**: Time management improves most by controlling priorities — deciding what matters before the day starts — rather than trying to micromanage every minute of the schedule.

**Q2**: What specific technique reduces the mental cost of switching between unrelated tasks?
**A**: Task batching — grouping similar low-focus activities like email, paperwork, and calls into dedicated time windows instead of interleaving them throughout the day.

**Q3**: How should someone apply chronotype awareness to their daily schedule?
**A**: Identify when their concentration naturally peaks and reserve that window for demanding creative or analytical work, saving lower-energy periods for routine tasks and shielding peak hours from low-value meetings.
