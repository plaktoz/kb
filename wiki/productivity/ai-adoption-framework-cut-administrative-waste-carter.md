## Summary
A healthcare entrepreneur argues that AI's value lies in returning time to people by automating repetitive administrative work, not in replacing them. His framework starts with the biggest bottleneck, automates one to three tasks, measures hours before dollars, and rolls out in phases. Scorecards and a human-centered approach keep adoption measurable and sustainable.

## Core Concepts
- [[Administrative Automation]]: using AI for charting, scheduling, and insurance verification.
- [[Theory of Constraints]]: finding the repetitive task that consumes the most hours first.
- [[Phased Rollout]]: one department, one workflow, one success metric at a time.
- [[AI Scorecard]]: tracking hours saved, response speed, errors removed, and team sentiment monthly.
- [[Employee Burnout]]: reduced by removing repetitive work.
- [[Human-Centered AI]]: technology handles repetition; people handle relationships.
- [[Dr. Sterling L. Carter]]: author, who built therapy, staffing, and hospice companies.

## Key Takeaways
- **Find Bottlenecks**: Spend a week identifying repetitive, low-judgment tasks before buying any technology.
- **Start Small**: Automate only the one to three most time-consuming administrative processes.
- **Hours Before Dollars**: Measure productive hours returned before measuring cost savings.
- **Documentation Example**: AI transcription cut documentation from 30 minutes to five per appointment.
- **Scorecards**: Review hours saved, response time, errors, and team feelings monthly.
- **Burnout**: Burnout begins with repetitive work, so removing it reduces burnout.
- **Phased Rollout**: Expand only after one workflow delivers consistent results on one metric.
- **People First**: Technology strengthens human judgment rather than replacing it.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Constrain effort to the biggest bottleneck** — Returns are highest where the most time is consumed by the least judgment-demanding work, so improvement should start there rather than spread evenly. · *model:* [[Theory of Constraints]]
→ explains: Find Bottlenecks, Start Small, Documentation Example

**T2. Limit simultaneous changes to keep results attributable** — When many changes launch together, it is hard to tell which worked and which flopped, so measured, incremental expansion yields reliable data for larger decisions.
→ explains: Start Small, Scorecards, Phased Rollout

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central argument of the article?
**A**: AI should be adopted to solve a real operational problem by returning time to people, and doing so works best through disciplined, measured steps rather than broad transformation.

**Q2**: What specific evidence does the author give that automation creates measurable capacity?
**A**: AI-assisted transcription cut documentation from 30 minutes to five minutes per appointment, creating recovered hours that teams can use to serve more customers or improve quality.

**Q3**: How would you apply the framework to launch AI in your own organization?
**A**: Spend a week listing repetitive tasks, automate the one to three biggest, and begin with one department, one workflow, and one success metric. Track a monthly scorecard before expanding.
