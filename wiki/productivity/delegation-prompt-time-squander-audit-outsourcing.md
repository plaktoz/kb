## Summary

[[Thanh Pham]] argues that the expensive default is doing every task that shows up without asking whether it should be yours, and that the main difficulty with [[Delegation]] is remembering to do it at all. His fix is a recurring "What can I delegate?" prompt every 2-3 days, a [[Time Squander Audit]] to automate small recurring tasks, Brooks's three levels of delegation, and a fixed $200/month budget for buying back time. The real payoff is recovered mental energy and attention rather than clock time, so you should start with task services and later move to a part-time [[Executive Assistant]].

## Core Concepts

- **Recurring delegation prompt**: A repeating task in your project management tool asking "What can I delegate?" so delegation stays top-of-mind.
- **[[Time Squander Audit]]**: Identify small recurring manual tasks (bill paying, attachment hunting, schedule spreadsheets) and automate them via auto-pay, [[Keyboard Shortcuts]], or apps like [[TeamSnap]].
- **Brooks's three levels of delegation**: Easy (tasks you hate or are bad at), Medium (tasks you can do but don't enjoy), Hard (tasks you're good at or enjoy that still eat mental bandwidth).
- **The $200 rule**: A fixed monthly budget for buying back time replaces hard-to-calculate personal ROI and makes outsourcing a small experiment.
- **Task services vs. dedicated EA**: Services like [[Fancy Hands]] handle one-off work; a dedicated [[Executive Assistant]] compounds value by learning preferences and anticipating needs.
- **[[Energy Management]]**: Track how you feel before and after handing off a task; energy, not hours, shows whether it worked.
- **Walk-in hack**: Have an EA (or yourself) phone fully booked restaurants, because human requests can unlock capacity that digital interfaces hide.

## Key Takeaways

- **Default trap**: Doing tasks just because you know how skips the "should I?" question.
- **Schedule the question**: Set "What can I delegate?" to recur every 2-3 days.
- **Small tasks compound**: Tiny manual chores together drain hours, energy and attention.
- **Audit action**: List top 3 time squanders; automate the worst one this week.
- **Leverage lives at Level 3**: Most people stop at Level 1; real leverage comes from Level 3.
- **Skill isn't a reason**: Being good at a task doesn't justify keeping it.
- **Budget, not ROI**: $200/month for grocery delivery, cleaning or errand freelancers.
- **EA starting point**: Begin at 5-8 hours/week, part-time, not a full salary.
- **First EA tasks**: Inbox triage and calendar blocking to protect hours for real work.
- **Hidden payoff**: Outsourcing cooking saves ~5 hours plus meal-planning headspace.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Comparative Advantage]]**: Brooks's Level 3 says that being good at a task is not enough reason to keep it; what matters is the bandwidth it costs you relative to higher-value work.
- **[[Opportunity Cost]]**: Every small task you keep uses attention that could go to creative work or family, which is why the article measures success in recovered energy, not saved minutes.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: According to Thanh Pham, why do people keep doing work they should have handed off?
**A**: Their default is to start any task they know how to do without asking whether it should be theirs. The real difficulty is remembering to outsource at all, so the delegation question has to come up on a schedule.

**Q2**: What are Brooks's three levels of delegation, and where does true leverage come from?
**A**: Level 1 is tasks you hate or are bad at, Level 2 is tasks you can do but don't enjoy, and Level 3 is tasks you're good at or even enjoy that still eat disproportionate mental bandwidth. Most people stop at Level 1, but true leverage comes from Level 3.

**Q3**: How would you start outsourcing if you've never done it?
**A**: Set a fixed $200 monthly budget for buying back time and run one specific annoyance through a task service like Fancy Hands this week. Once you've seen the value, hire a part-time EA for 5-8 hours a week to handle inbox triage and calendar blocking.
