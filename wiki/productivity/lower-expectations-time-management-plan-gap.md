## Summary

Time management coach [[Elizabeth Grace Saunders]] argues that the gap between what you plan and what you actually accomplish is the biggest factor in how satisfied you feel with your [[Time Management]]. Because no plan can perfectly predict how long work will take, the fix is not to abandon planning but to adjust expectations so imperfect days still feel like success. Planning's real value is prioritization, working ahead, and judging interruptions — not guaranteeing everything gets done.

## Core Concepts

- **Plan-vs-actual gap**: The distance between intended and completed work drives perceived time management success, even when essentials get done.
- **Inherent planning uncertainty**: Email surges, client emergencies, glitchy systems, and [[Learning Curve]] effects on unfamiliar work make minute-level estimates impossible; related to the [[Planning Fallacy]].
- **Expectation adjustment**: Set expectations so you can keep commitments and feel good despite imperfect execution.
- **Purpose of planning**: [[Prioritization]], working ahead on big projects instead of last-minute rushing, and deciding whether new demands merit [[Context Switching]] away from the plan.
- **Self-judgment**: Failing your own expectations can make you grade yourself an "F" regardless of actual output.

## Key Takeaways

- **Satisfaction Driver**: Plan-vs-actual gap is the biggest factor in time management satisfaction.
- **Self-Grading Trap**: Missing self-set expectations feels like failure even when essentials ship.
- **Don't Quit Planning**: Frustration with results calls for adjusted expectations, not abandoning plans.
- **Unpredictable Durations**: Email spikes, emergencies, and glitches defeat precise time estimates.
- **Learning Curve X Factor**: Unfamiliar tasks add unpredictable time from learning.
- **Planning's Real Job**: Prioritize now, work ahead, and evaluate whether interruptions deserve attention.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Satisfaction is measured against expectation, not output** — How successful you feel about your time depends on the gap between what you expected and what you achieved, so identical output can feel like success or failure depending on the expectation.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does Saunders identify as the biggest factor in how satisfied people feel with their time management?
**A**: The gap between what they plan to do and what they actually accomplish.

**Q2**: Why can't even skilled planners execute plans perfectly?
**A**: They can't know task durations to the minute because of email surges, client emergencies, glitchy systems, and learning curves on unfamiliar work.

**Q3**: If your plans keep falling short, what should you do instead of abandoning planning?
**A**: Adjust your expectations and use planning for prioritizing, working ahead on big projects, and judging whether new demands are worth a diversion.
