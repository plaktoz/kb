## Summary
Suzanne Coles of Curzon Consulting argues that AI productivity gains do not become economic value automatically; management decisions about roles, workflows and resources convert released capacity into returns. She separates disappointing AI returns into delayed, stranded and unproven value, and offers five leadership questions to decide whether to continue, redesign or stop.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Delayed Value]]**: credible benefits still maturing, which may justify continued investment against clear milestones.
- **[[Stranded Value]]**: demonstrated improvements blocked by operating structures, resource allocations or management decisions.
- **[[Unproven Value]]**: insufficient evidence that expected improvements are materialising, demanding reassessment of original assumptions.
- **[[Capacity Fragmentation]]**: saved minutes scattered across shifts, locations and specialist roles cannot be pooled into a redesigned shift or reduced contract.
- **Five benefit types**: productivity improvement, [[Cashable Savings]], [[Cost Avoidance]], profitable growth and strategic value; these must not be double-counted.
- **[[Finance and Operations Alignment]]**: Finance sets baselines and tests assumptions; Operations knows which processes can change; one accountable business leader owns the outcome.
- Evidence cited: [[McKinsey]] 2026 global survey, a 66-firm field experiment, and the [[Erik Brynjolfsson]] customer-support study.

## Key Takeaways
- **Survey Gap**: 80% reported personal productivity gains; only 37% saw enterprise-level EBIT impact.
- **Decision Creates Return**: Process improvement creates opportunity; the business decision creates the return.
- **Automation Case**: 20–30% effort cut left operating costs flat because roles stayed unchanged.
- **Field Experiment**: 7,137 workers saved about two email hours weekly, with no task-mix shift.
- **Fragmented Capacity**: 15 minutes saved across 40 people is ten theoretical, not actionable, hours.
- **Support Study**: AI assistant raised issues resolved per hour 15%, strongest for less-experienced agents.
- **Last Mile Cost**: Removing the last 20–30% of manual effort may need heavy redesign and controls.
- **Patience vs Waiting**: Stalled adoption or flat operational measures should trigger reassessment, not more waiting.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Saved capacity is not realised value until a decision converts it** — Efficiency gains create only potential capacity; unless someone decides how to use or remove that capacity and it is measured, the benefit disappears into existing activity. · *model:* [[Opportunity Cost]]
→ explains: Decision Creates Return, Automation Case, Fragmented Capacity, Survey Gap

**T2. Each additional increment of automation costs more than the last** — The first 70–80% of manual effort is removed quickly, while the remainder needs disproportionate redesign, controls and management effort, so stopping short can be the better investment. · *model:* [[Diminishing Returns]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why do AI productivity gains often fail to show up in business performance?
**A**: Improvements in how people work only become economic value when management makes decisions about roles, workflows and resources. Without those decisions, released capacity is absorbed into existing activity and the value stays stranded.

**Q2**: What distinguishes delayed, stranded and unproven value, and why does it matter?
**A**: Delayed value is still maturing, stranded value is demonstrated but blocked by operating structures or decisions, and unproven value lacks evidence of improvement. Each calls for a different response (keep investing against milestones, change processes and resources, or reassess assumptions), so treating all as a timing problem encourages waiting when action is needed.

**Q3**: How should leaders apply the article's framework when approving or reviewing an AI investment?
**A**: State which type of value is sought, identify where value is already stranded, test whether capacity is genuinely usable, and judge whether further redesign is commercially justified. Also check whether patience is disciplined or just waiting, with one accountable leader agreed jointly by Finance and Operations.
