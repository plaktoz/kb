## Summary
ClickUp's guide frames [[Deep Work]] as a trainable skill built through deliberate scheduling, environment control, and task batching, emphasizing that recovery time between focus sessions is as important as the sessions themselves — both for individuals and teams.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Focus as a Trainable Skill]]**: Like a muscle, deep-work capacity grows through deliberate practice, starting with short sessions and building toward longer sustained blocks — reinforcing the incremental-capacity-building framing common across deep-work literature.
- **[[Context-Switching Cost]]**: The guide highlights that switching between apps and tasks carries a measurable cognitive cost, motivating the recommendation to consolidate work into a single clean workspace during focus blocks.
- **[[Shared Team Norms]]**: Beyond individual tactics, the article argues organizations benefit from collective focus norms (e.g., meeting-light days) that protect focus time at the team level, not just for individuals.

## Key Takeaways
- **Schedule and defend**: Set a dedicated deep-work schedule and treat it as non-negotiable on the calendar.
- **Environment control**: Close the inbox, silence notifications, and signal unavailability to reduce interruption risk.
- **Batch shallow work**: Group email, status updates, and scheduling into separate windows so they don't fragment focus blocks.
- **Reduce app-switching**: Use a single tool/workspace where possible to minimize the cognitive cost of context switching.
- **Pair goals with sessions**: Give each deep-work block a defined outcome rather than leaving it open-ended.
- **Recovery matters**: Deep work is demanding and unsustainable without scheduled rest and hard stop points between sessions.
