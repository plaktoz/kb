## Summary
Organizations widely measure individual employee engagement, but team engagement, a shared state of vigor, dedication, and absorption, is a distinct dimension that surveys largely miss. Virtual and hybrid work and human-AI teams make this gap more consequential. The author argues that engagement surveys should be complemented with team engagement measures.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Employee Engagement]]**: emotional, cognitive, and behavioral connection to work; [[William Kahn]] tied it to meaningfulness, safety, and availability.
- **[[Job Demands-Resources Model]]**: demands drive exhaustion while resources drive motivation; engagement (vigor, dedication, absorption) fluctuates as they change.
- **[[Team Engagement]]**: a shared positive work-related psychological state emerging from team interaction (Torrente et al., 2012), measured differently from individual engagement.
- **[[Virtual and Hybrid Teams]]**: remote work can lift individual engagement while team virtuality correlates negatively with team engagement.
- **[[Human-AI Teams]]**: mixed evidence on communication, coordination, cognition, and trust with AI teammates.
- **[[Gallup]] engagement research**: annual benchmark and a $10 trillion productivity-loss estimate.

## Key Takeaways
- **Prevalence**: Two-thirds of surveyed organizations measure engagement regularly (HR Research Institute, 2025).
- **Economic Cost**: Gallup estimates low engagement cost about $10 trillion in 2025, 9% of global GDP.
- **Market Size**: Engagement software valued at $1.22 billion in 2025, projected to quadruple by 2034.
- **Different Questions**: Individual surveys ask about role clarity and recognition; team measures ask about collective energy.
- **Remote Paradox**: Remote workers report higher engagement, yet 90 global teams showed virtuality lowering team engagement.
- **Human-AI Teams**: Nearly half of leaders prioritize AI teammates in the next 12-18 months.
- **Recommendation**: Complement employee engagement surveys with team engagement measures.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Properties of a whole are not revealed by measuring its parts** — Collective states emerge from member interactions, so a strong individual-level reading does not imply a strong team-level one. Each level needs its own measurement. · *model:* [[Emergence]]
→ explains: Different Questions, Remote Paradox, Recommendation

**T2. Engagement is a balance of demands and resources that shifts over time** — Sustained effort demands push toward exhaustion while resources push toward motivation, so engagement fluctuates as the balance changes. · *model:* [[Job Demands-Resources Model]]
→ explains: Prevalence, Economic Cost

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central argument?
**A**: Individual employee engagement is heavily measured, but team engagement is a distinct, overlooked dimension. Organizations should complement employee surveys with team engagement measures.

**Q2**: What evidence shows individual and team engagement can diverge?
**A**: Gallup found remote employees report higher engagement than on-site ones, while research on 90 global business teams (976 individuals) found team virtuality negatively related to team engagement.

**Q3**: Why does team engagement matter more now?
**A**: Virtual and hybrid work and human-AI teams change how people interact, and individual surveys may miss problems in collaboration, cohesion, and collective motivation. Leaders need to watch the quality of human interaction as AI teammates are added.
