## Summary

This video presents five time optimization habits centered on deliberate scheduling rather than reactive task completion, arguing that a meaningful week is built by proactively carving out time for promises, people, enjoyment, and profit rather than letting incoming requests fill the calendar. The habits range from a weekly planning ritual (the Big 4) to daily task interrogation and a binary filter for new commitments. The overarching principle is that fewer, higher-consequence tasks done with full attention produce more satisfaction and impact than a packed reactive to-do list.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Weekly Big 4 Framework]]**: A weekly planning template with four columns — Promises, People, Personal Enjoyment, and Profit — to ensure each week advances what matters across work, relationships, joy, and long-term earnings.
- **[[Capture and Interrogate]]**: A daily overwhelm-reduction technique that lists all potential tasks then rates each on a 0–100 consequence scale; only items rated above 90 make the final cut.
- **[[Prioritizing Thought Experiment]]**: A mental scenario where an urgent personal obligation forces you to compress an 8-hour workday into 60 minutes, forcing your genuine top priority to surface immediately.
- **[[Dedicated Days]]**: Theming each weekday around one of the five or fewer responsibilities that drive 80% of career success, enabling deep-mode focus without constant context-switching.
- **[[Hell Yeah Filter]]**: A binary commitment test: if a proposed project or event doesn't generate an immediate "hell yeah," the default answer is no.
- **[[Kanban Board]]**: Used as the physical/digital container for the Weekly Big 4, with one column per category (Trello or Notion).

## Key Takeaways

- **Reactive weeks feel wasteful**: Responding only to inbound requests and repeating routine tasks builds no future value.
- **Weekly Big 4**: Plan one meaningful action per column (Promises, People, Enjoyment, Profit) every week.
- **0–100 consequence rating**: Tasks rated below 90 should be delegated, postponed, or dropped entirely.
- **Imagination over discipline**: The 1-hour thought experiment bypasses rationalization and surfaces true priorities.
- **80/20 on responsibilities**: Fewer than five tasks drive 80%+ of career success — identify and protect time for them.
- **Dedicated day themes**: Single-mode days increase output by eliminating context-switching overhead.
- **Hell Yeah or no**: Future commitments that only somewhat appeal will feel like obligations when they arrive.
- **Block calendar time**: Even 15 minutes for a priority item is enough to move the needle over a week.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Pareto Principle]]**: The dedicated-days habit is a direct application of the 80/20 rule — identifying the critical few responsibilities and protecting disproportionate time for them explains why theming days outperforms balanced daily multitasking.
- **[[Hell Yeah Filter]]**: Derek Sivers' heuristic encodes an asymmetric cost-benefit insight: lukewarm commitments consume full calendar cost but deliver only partial enthusiasm and value, so the bar must be set high enough to screen them out before they accumulate.
