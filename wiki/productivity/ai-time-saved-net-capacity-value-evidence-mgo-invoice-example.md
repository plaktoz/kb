## Summary
Reported AI time savings overstate value until review, rework and other workflow costs are netted out, as a hypothetical invoice pilot shows: 100 gross hours become 60 net hours, of which only 40 were used for documented work. Leaders should define the value they want, test the workflow and its safeguards, and close the "defensibility gap" between what management claims and what evidence supports. Control evidence and value evidence are separate and both are needed before expanding an AI use case.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Net Capacity Released]]**: Gross time saved minus added review and correction hours.
- **[[Defensibility Gap]]**: The distance between what management claims or permits and what the evidence supports.
- **[[AI Governance]]**: Assessing risk, validating controls, and confirming evidence supports the proposed scope; MGO frames this as AI Control to Value.
- **[[Shadow AI]]**: AI used without required approval or outside approved boundaries; recorded separately from AI type.
- **[[Total Cost of Ownership]]**: Licenses, usage, integration, review, rework, monitoring and recovery; a blank cost is not zero.
- **[[Bounded Pilot]]**: A test of one hypothesis under defined limits for data, users, authority, cost and time.
- **[[Human Review]]**: Reviewers need time, information and ability to intervene to catch consequential errors.

## Key Takeaways
- **Net Not Gross**: 100 gross hours less 30 review and 10 correction gives 60.
- **Attribution Limits**: Backlog fell 500 to 350, but not all attributable to AI.
- **Capacity Is Not Cash**: Labor-rate math does not prove budget savings.
- **Gartner Gap**: 75% target productivity; only 22% scaled AI across units.
- **Test The Workflow**: Policy that AI cannot pay invoices does not prove it cannot.
- **Two Evidence Types**: Strong controls don't prove ROI; good results don't excuse exposure.
- **Shadow AI Cost**: IBM breach study: $670,000 higher costs, an association only.
- **Decision Record**: Bring scope, results, full costs, safeguards, approver and review date.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Gross gains overstate value until the cost of verifying them is netted out** — A productivity gain is the output released after subtracting the extra review, correction and rework that the new process creates. · *model:* [[Net vs Gross Benefit]]
→ explains: Net Not Gross, Capacity Is Not Cash

**T2. A claim is only as strong as the evidence that supports it** — Decisions to expand authority should rest on documented results, costs and tested safeguards, and unknowns must stay visible rather than assumed zero. Neither value evidence nor control evidence substitutes for the other.
→ explains: Test The Workflow, Two Evidence Types, Attribution Limits

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about AI time savings?
**A**: Time saved is not the same as value created; leaders must account for review, rework and other costs to know net capacity, and have evidence tying results to the investment.

**Q2**: How does the invoice example illustrate this?
**A**: A 100-hour screening saving becomes 60 net hours after 30 hours of review and 10 of correction, and only 40 of those hours went to documented work, so no cash savings were demonstrated.

**Q3**: What should a leader bring to a meeting to decide on expansion?
**A**: One decision record covering the next decision, permitted scope, observed results and full costs, tested safeguards and gaps, and the approver, conditions and review date.
