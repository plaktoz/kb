## Summary

Adults with [[ADHD]] struggle at work because the condition impairs [[Executive Function]] (planning, prioritizing, starting tasks, managing time, holding information in mind), not because of laziness. The fix is to stop relying on willpower and build external systems: fewer distractions, visible time, smaller first steps, and one place to track tasks. When tips aren't enough, ADA workplace accommodations and professional treatment (medication, therapy, skill-building) fill the gap.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Executive Function]]**: The brain skills ADHD affects. This explains last-minute rushing, losing track of time, forgetting verbal instructions, and [[Hyperfocus]] on interesting work while urgent work waits.
- **Focus as filtering**: ADHD focus problems come from paying attention to everything at once. The goal is to cut down what competes for attention: close extra tabs, put the phone away, work full-screen.
- **[[Body Doubling]]**: Working next to someone, in person or on a video call, makes it easier to start and keep going.
- **Parking Lot Note**: A running doc for stray thoughts so you keep the thought without breaking focus.
- **[[Time Blindness]]**: Time doesn't feel real until a deadline is close. Countered with visual timers, short sprints (see [[Pomodoro Technique]]), fake early deadlines, doubled estimates, and [[Time Blocking]].
- **Interest-based motivation**: ADHD brains respond to interest, novelty, challenge, and urgency. Boring tasks can be turned into races or mini challenges.
- **Energy matching**: Do demanding work in your best focus window (often mid-morning, especially on medication) and push admin to slower hours.
- **External memory**: Set up tomorrow before leaving, give every object one home, confirm verbal instructions in writing, and check the inbox in batches.
- **[[Americans with Disabilities Act]] (ADA)**: ADHD may count as a disability when it substantially limits major life activities. Employers with 15+ employees must then provide reasonable accommodations.
- **[[Job Accommodation Network]] (JAN)**: A free resource with ADHD-specific accommodation ideas.

## Key Takeaways

- **Not Laziness**: ADHD adults often spend extra effort to get the same result.
- **Shrink Field of View**: One window, no phone, only the tabs you need.
- **Noise on Purpose**: Brown noise or one repeated playlist trains a "lock in" cue.
- **Visual Timers**: Watching time shrink creates gentle urgency better than a clock does.
- **Short Sprints**: 20–25 minutes of work, 5 minutes of break; start with 10 if needed.
- **Fake Deadlines**: Set Wednesday for a Friday deadline and tell someone.
- **Double Estimates**: Block an hour for a task you think takes 30 minutes.
- **Top Three**: No more than three must-do tasks per day.
- **Tiny First Step**: Open the template and type the title to get going.
- **One Task System**: Tasks spread across many tools get forgotten.
- **Batch Inbox**: Check messages at set times, e.g. 9:00, 12:00, 3:30.
- **Common Accommodations**: Quiet space, written instructions, flexible or remote work, check-ins.
- **Disclosure Is Optional**: Share on a need-to-know basis; informal requests may not require it.
- **Seek Help When**: You keep missing deadlines, get poor reviews, or medication wears off early.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Extended Mind]]**: Every tactic moves planning, memory, and time-tracking out of the head and into timers, lists, calendars, and written recaps, which compensates for weak executive function instead of fighting it with willpower.
- **[[Activation Energy]]**: Tiny first steps, body doubling, and fake deadlines all lower the cost of starting, which the article calls the hardest part of a task.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does the article say ADHD makes work hard, and what is its core fix?
**A**: ADHD impairs executive function: planning, prioritizing, starting tasks, managing time, and working memory. The fix is to rely on external systems and structure instead of willpower, supported by accommodations and treatment when needed.

**Q2**: What techniques does the article recommend for "time blindness"?
**A**: Make time visible with a shrinking visual timer, work in 20–25 minute sprints, set fake earlier deadlines with outside check-ins, double time estimates, and block calendar time for focus work.

**Q3**: How could an employee with ADHD get support without fully disclosing a diagnosis?
**A**: They can start with informal requests, such as written follow-ups after meetings, without mentioning ADHD. Formal ADA accommodations generally require giving HR enough information, often with provider documentation, to show a qualifying limitation.
