## Summary
Two productivity frameworks adapted for new teachers: "Full Capture" (from David Allen's Getting Things Done) to offload every task/idea into one trusted system, and "Multi-Scale Planning" (from Cal Newport's Deep Work) which cascades semester → weekly → daily plans so every minute of non-teaching work gets deliberately scheduled like a lesson.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Full Capture]]**: The brain treats "written down in a trusted system" the same as "already done" — closing the open loop that otherwise causes background anxiety about forgotten tasks.
- **[[Multi-Scale Planning]]**: Cascading three planning horizons — a semester plan (term-level commitments and success criteria), a weekly plan (deadlines and progress toward semester goals), and a daily plan (every minute assigned a job) — each level feeding the one below it.
- **[[Timeblocking]]** (Cal Newport): Treating non-teaching tasks (grading, planning, reports) exactly like scheduled lessons — a specific time slot worked with total focus, rather than left as vague, deprioritized to-dos.

## Key Takeaways
- One trusted capture system (notebook, Todoist, Trello, planner, index cards) should be reviewed daily for 10-30 minutes; time-sensitive items go straight to a calendar.
- The method doesn't reduce total workload — it makes teachers feel less busy by ensuring nothing gets overlooked and everything gets a deliberate slot.
- When a crisis pulls a teacher away from a planned block, the correct response is to simply update the plan and schedule a new block, not to abandon the system.
- Timeblocking explicitly includes protected time for lunch, breaks, and reading — it isn't meant to fill every minute with work.

## 🃏 Review Questions
**Q1**: Why does "Full Capture" reduce mental overhead even before any task is completed?
**A**: Because the brain can't distinguish between a task being done and it being reliably stored in a trusted system — writing it down closes the "open loop" and removes the background cognitive load of trying to remember it.

**Q2**: What are the three planning horizons in Multi-Scale Planning, and how do they relate to each other?
**A**: Semester, weekly, and daily plans — each feeds the next down the chain: the semester plan sets term-level goals, the weekly plan breaks those into weekly deadlines and progress steps, and the daily plan allocates every minute based on the weekly plan.

**Q3**: According to the article, does timeblocking reduce a teacher's total workload?
**A**: No — it doesn't reduce the amount of work, but it can make teachers feel significantly less busy because nothing is overlooked and everything has a deliberate, scheduled place.
