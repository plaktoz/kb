## Summary

Only 12.2% of US adults meet CDC-recommended daily fruit intake, and research links poor diet quality to a 66% higher rate of reported workplace productivity loss versus a potential 20% productivity gain from healthier eating — driven mainly by how fruit's fiber content stabilizes blood sugar and sustains cognitive performance across the workday.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Blood Sugar Stability]]**: Fruit's natural sugars are packaged with fiber, which slows absorption and prevents the sharp glucose spikes and crashes that processed snacks cause — the mechanism behind the common mid-afternoon productivity dip.
- **[[Nutrient-Cognition Link]]**: Blueberries' anthocyanins accumulate in brain regions tied to learning and memory; citrus flavonoids show neuroprotective properties tied to slower cognitive decline; polyphenol-rich fruits support blood flow to the brain.
- **[[Convenience Gap]]**: The primary barrier to fruit consumption isn't awareness of benefits but access friction — washing, cutting, and storing fresh fruit discourages workplace consumption even when benefits are well understood.

## Key Takeaways

- **Adoption gap**: Only 12.2% of US adults meet daily fruit intake recommendations; just 9.3% meet vegetable recommendations.
- **Productivity link**: Employees with unhealthy diets are 66% more likely to report productivity losses; healthier diets are linked to productivity gains up to 20%.
- **Blueberries**: Regular consumption linked to improved working-memory task performance in research participants.
- **Citrus**: Higher intake associated with slower cognitive decline over time; vitamin C supports neurotransmitter production tied to mood and focus.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Most US workers fall well short of recommended fruit intake, and this nutritional gap has a measurable, quantifiable cost to workplace cognitive performance and productivity.

**Q2**: What is the specific mechanism linking fruit intake to sustained afternoon productivity?
**A**: Fruit's fiber slows sugar absorption into the bloodstream, avoiding the rapid blood-sugar spike-and-crash cycle that refined-sugar snacks cause and that produces the common mid-afternoon energy/productivity dip.

**Q3**: What's the practical barrier preventing people from acting on this information despite knowing the benefits?
**A**: Convenience, not awareness — fresh fruit requires prep (washing, cutting, storing) that creates enough friction to discourage regular workplace consumption, which is why frictionless-access interventions (like on-site smoothie stations) are positioned as the solution.
