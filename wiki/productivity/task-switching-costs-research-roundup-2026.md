## Summary

Multiple 2025-2026 research threads converge on the same finding: workers sustain only a few minutes of true focus before switching tasks, and each switch carries a measurable, compounding cost to productivity that self-initiated interruptions (not external ones) mostly drive.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Attention Residue]]**: Sophie Leroy's research shows part of your attention stays stuck on an unfinished task when you switch to a new one, so the new task receives only partial cognitive resources.
- **[[Task-Switch Reconfiguration Cost]]**: Moving between demanding tasks requires the brain to unload the first task's rules/goals and load the second's — a measurable delay the APA estimates can cut effective productivity by up to 40% on complex work.
- **[[Self-Initiated Interruption]]**: A CHIWORK '25 study (Talypova et al.) found workers sustained an average of just six minutes of focused work before switching, with self-initiated interruptions accounting for nearly 60% of off-task time — more than external interruptions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Six-minute focus window**: Workers deviated from their main task roughly every 3.5 minutes once brief on/off-task switches were counted (Talypova et al., CHIWORK '25).
- **Recovery time**: Gloria Mark's UC Irvine field studies found it takes an average of 23 minutes 15 seconds to return to a task at the same engagement level after a typical interruption.
- **Productivity cost**: APA's review estimates switch costs reduce effective productivity by up to 40% on complex work.
- **Open-office effect**: Bernstein and Turban found open-plan offices reduced face-to-face interaction by ~70%, pushing workers toward electronic communication instead — undermining the "collaboration" rationale for open floor plans.
- **Countermeasure**: Physical/visible focus signals (noise-cancelling headphones, do-not-disturb indicators) can recreate boundaries that open environments remove.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim across this research?
**A**: Sustained focus is rare and fragile — workers switch tasks every few minutes, largely by their own initiative, and each switch carries a real, measurable productivity cost rather than being a neutral or harmless habit.

**Q2**: What specific data point best quantifies the recovery cost of an interruption?
**A**: Gloria Mark's field studies found it takes an average of 23 minutes 15 seconds to return to a task at the same level of engagement after a typical interruption.

**Q3**: What's the practical implication for someone trying to protect deep work time?
**A**: Since most interruptions are self-initiated rather than externally imposed, the fix is less about eliminating outside noise and more about building personal environmental cues (visible focus signals, phone out of reach) that make staying on task the path of least resistance.
