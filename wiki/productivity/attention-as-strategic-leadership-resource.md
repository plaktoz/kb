## Summary
Attention is a finite organizational resource that leaders must actively protect and manage — not just an individual concern. Organizations that reward responsiveness while penalizing concentration inadvertently undermine performance quality; leaders who demonstrate focused, disciplined work patterns create a cultural permission structure for deep work. The organizations that thrive in complex environments will be those that manage attention more effectively than their competitors.

## Core Concepts

- [[Attention Management]] — the deliberate allocation and protection of cognitive bandwidth at the individual and organizational level
- [[Deep Work]] — sustained, uninterrupted concentration on cognitively demanding tasks; [[Cal Newport]]'s framework
- [[Cognitive Switching Cost]] — the mental overhead of moving between tasks; frequent interruptions accumulate to significant productivity drag
- [[Decision Fatigue]] — degraded decision quality from depleted cognitive resources; worsened by constant interruption
- [[Attention as Organizational Resource]] — reframing attention alongside capital, talent, technology, and time in leadership thinking

## Key Takeaways

- **Core insight**: Organizational performance depends on the quality of collective attention, not just people and processes
- **Hidden cost**: Constant availability degrades strategic thinking, produces reactive decisions, and reduces time for coaching
- **Leaders direct attention**: Every meeting, report, and initiative competes for employee cognitive bandwidth — leaders shape this
- **Organizational design**: Reduce unnecessary meetings; establish predictable communication norms; protect focused work blocks
- **Cultural signal**: Leader behavior (multitasking in meetings vs. focused listening) becomes the organizational norm regardless of policy
- **Strategic leadership requires space**: Complex problems require reflection and systems thinking — impossible in constant-interruption environments
- **Competitive advantage**: Organizations managing attention better than competitors will outperform on reasoning, creativity, and judgment

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Attention Economy]]**: Internal organizations face the same scarcity dynamics as external media — attention is the scarce resource, and every initiative, meeting, and notification is competing for the same finite pool.
- **[[Modeling (Organizational Learning)]]**: Leaders who demonstrate focused listening and disciplined prioritization don't need to write policies about deep work — they encode the norm through their behavior, which employees copy as a survival strategy.
