## Summary

The author redesigned weekly planning around personal energy patterns rather than letting meetings and errands claim the calendar first. The method layers high-energy windows, immovable commitments, and protected focus blocks, then adds buffer gaps and AI-assisted weekly reviews to keep the week resilient to disruption. The author frames this as a personal pattern, not research, and encourages testing it against your own energy log.

## Core Concepts

- **Three-Layer Weekly Plan**: Map energy windows, then fixed commitments, then focus blocks in the best open windows, before anything discretionary.
- **[[Energy Management]]**: Find the windows of natural energy and concentration; leave them unassigned until fixed commitments are on the page.
- **[[Deep Work]] Protection**: Reserve focus time first (3 hours per week as a starter, building toward 1 hour per workday), before accepting more meetings.
- **Weekly Review Questions**: Four questions on drains, what deserves more space, what gets skipped, and what can be automated or batched with [[AI]].
- **Calendar Buffers**: 15- or 30-minute gaps between meetings absorb overruns, bumped work, and recovery.
- **AI Meeting Agent**: An agent drafts follow-up emails and checks a meeting-action task database twice a day; the human reviews and owns what is sent.

## Key Takeaways

- **Plan Order**: Energy windows first, fixed commitments second, focus blocks third, errands last.
- **Voice Review**: Have an AI ask the four review questions one at a time; answer aloud.
- **Shrink, Don't Drop**: Turn a skipped 30-minute block into a 15-minute one you will keep.
- **Protected Mornings**: Block the strongest windows (here Mon/Tue 7 a.m.-noon) so others cannot book over them.
- **Buffer Gaps**: Add one 15-30 minute gap where a disruption would cascade; watch it a week.
- **Human Ownership**: Agents prepare drafts; get consent for meeting capture and review before sending.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Unreserved time is claimed in arrival order** — A calendar left open fills with whatever arrives first, so scarce high-value capacity must be reserved before discretionary demands are accepted.
→ explains: Plan Order, Protected Mornings

**T2. Slack absorbs shocks** — Margin in a schedule lets an ordinary disruption stay local instead of becoming a week-long backlog. · *model:* [[Margin of Safety]]
→ explains: Buffer Gaps, Shrink, Don't Drop

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core change the author made to weekly planning?
**A**: Instead of giving meetings and errands first claim on the calendar, the author plans around energy windows first, then fixed commitments, then focus blocks, with lower-priority items fitted in the remaining space.

**Q2**: How do buffer gaps work in a meeting-heavy day?
**A**: The author leaves 15- or 30-minute gaps between most meetings to catch meetings that run long, hold bumped work, or serve as recovery, which keeps a schedule change from becoming a week-long backlog.

**Q3**: How would you apply this to a block you keep skipping?
**A**: Keep the commitment but shrink it, for example turning a 30-minute appointment into a 15-minute one you will actually keep, and change only that one thing for the coming week.
