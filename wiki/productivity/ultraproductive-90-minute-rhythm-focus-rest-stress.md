## Summary
Eric Partaker and Dr. Christian Poensgen's book *Ultraproductive* argues that humans are built to pulse, not run continuously, so sustainable performance means working with the body's 90-minute [[Ultradian Rhythms]] across focus, rest, and stress. Each area gets one strategy: three-phase deep focus, a work ethic paired with a rest ethic, and short bursts of stress interrupted often. The three areas reinforce each other, and the approach is explicitly anti-hustle culture.

## Core Concepts
- [[Ultradian Rhythms]]: the roughly 90-minute cycle used to structure focus, rest, and stress.
- [[Eric Partaker]] and [[Christian Poensgen]]: authors of *Ultraproductive: The 90-Minute Method to Optimize Focus, Sleep, and Stress*.
- [[Deep Work]] phases: Fight (first 5-10 minutes of resisting distraction), Flow (effortless focus), and Fadeout (stop as energy wanes).
- Work ethic plus rest ethic: the race car driver analogy of accelerator (stress system) and brake (relaxation system).
- [[Acute vs Chronic Stress]]: short-term stress channels energy and focus; chronic stress leads to burnout.
- Stress interruptors: sleep (deep and REM), breaks every 90 minutes, walks, long-exhale breathing, quarterly vacations.
- [[Hustle Culture]] and "9-9-6" as the approach's explicit counterpoint.

## Key Takeaways
- **Distraction Baseline**: The average person shifts attention every 47 seconds.
- **Fatigue and Stress**: 85% feel tired; 76% of Americans report chronic stress.
- **Focus Payoff**: 90 minutes of deep focus per day doubles average daily productivity.
- **Fight Phase**: Getting into deep focus takes five to ten difficult minutes.
- **Fadeout Phase**: Stop when energy wanes, rather than pushing until the task is done.
- **Rest Ethic**: Peak performance needs a great rest ethic, not just work ethic.
- **Stress Bursts**: Use acute stress deliberately, then interrupt it often.
- **Compounding Areas**: Improving one of focus, rest, or stress lifts the other two.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Performance is rhythmic: exertion needs matching recovery** — Humans are organisms that pulse, so sustained output depends on alternating focused effort with real rest rather than staying "always on" in a half-working, half-resting state.
→ explains: Focus Payoff, Fadeout Phase, Rest Ethic, Compounding Areas

**T2. Stress helps in short doses and harms when it persists** — Acute stress sharpens focus and energy, but it becomes burnout when it is chronic, so it must be interrupted regularly. · *model:* [[Hormesis]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of the *Ultraproductive* approach?
**A**: Humans are designed to pulse, so sustainable peak performance comes from working and living in rhythm with the 90-minute ultradian cycle across focus, rest, and stress, not from hustle.

**Q2**: What are the three phases of deep focus, and why does each matter?
**A**: Fight (the first 5-10 minutes of resisting distraction), Flow (effortless, hard-to-interrupt focus), and Fadeout (stopping as energy wanes so you can recuperate and refocus later). Fadeout is the one most often neglected.

**Q3**: How should you handle stress in practice?
**A**: Use acute stress deliberately during deep focus, then toggle it off by pulling away from the work: sleep well, break every 90 minutes, take walks, practice long-exhale breathing, and plan quarterly vacations.
