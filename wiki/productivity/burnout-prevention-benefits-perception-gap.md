## Summary

A 2026 UK employee benefits study finds a large perception gap — 82% of employers believe they support wellbeing, but only 67% of employees agree — with flexible working and physical wellbeing benefits identified as the most effective, practical levers against burnout.

## Core Concepts

- [[Wellbeing Perception Gap]] — the divide between what employers believe they offer and what employees actually experience and value.
- [[Benefit Utilization vs Availability]] — a benefit listed on an intranet isn't the same as one employees understand, trust and actually use.
- [[Wellbeing Infrastructure]] — moving from ad hoc wellbeing initiatives to accessible, relevant support built into everyday working life.

## Key Takeaways

- **Perception gap**: 82% of UK employers think they support wellbeing; only 67% of employees feel that support.
- **Most effective interventions**: Flexible working (72% of employees) and work-life balance benefits like gym access (48%) rank highest for burnout prevention.
- **Fitness benefit gap**: 89% of employees with fitness benefits say they help wellbeing, but only 41% currently have access.
- **Root cause caveat**: High workload and workplace stress are cited nearly 3x more than any other barrier — benefits alone can't fix structural overwork.
- **What works**: Benefits that fit real (hybrid/shift) lives, early intervention, and a culture that normalizes using support before crisis point.

**Q2**: Which specific benefits did employees rate as most effective for preventing burnout?
**A**: Flexible working arrangements (72%) and work-life balance benefits like gym memberships and physical wellbeing programs (48%).

**Q3**: Why can't benefits alone solve workplace burnout, per this research?
**A**: High workload and workplace stress are cited as barriers to wellbeing nearly three times more often than any other factor — a structural issue benefits packages don't fix on their own.
