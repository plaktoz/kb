## Summary

[[Korn Ferry]] leadership consultants describe five small daily rituals CEOs use to reduce [[Decision Fatigue]]: a morning "forest bath" for setting intentions, a weekend [[30-Minute Rule]] limiting email to protect recovery time, a "find the skeptic" practice of deliberately inviting the most critical voice after big meetings, ending each day with a top-three [[Priority List]], and a brief daily positive self-talk ritual. The consistent theme is that small, repeatable habits — not willpower alone — sustain executive performance.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Decision Fatigue]]** — the depletion of decision-making quality over a day of repeated choices, which structured habits help offset
- **[[30-Minute Rule]]** — limiting weekend work email to 30 minutes to protect recovery time, a post-pandemic executive norm
- **[[Find the Skeptic]]** — deliberately giving the most critical person in the room the last word after a meeting or presentation to surface hidden risk
- **[[Priority List]]** — writing down the next day's top three priorities at end of day to reduce next-morning decision load

## Key Takeaways

- **Forest bath ritual**: a solo morning walk to set intentions, adaptable even without literal access to a forest.
- **30-minute weekend email rule**: protects recovery time while still allowing minimal responsiveness.
- **"One last question" tactic**: intentionally surfaces skepticism to counter groupthink after key meetings.
- **End-of-day top-3 list**: reduces next-day decision paralysis by pre-committing to priorities.
- **Daily 5-minute pep talk**: counters self-criticism and sets a positive tone before the workday starts.
