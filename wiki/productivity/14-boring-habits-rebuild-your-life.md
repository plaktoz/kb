## Summary
Fourteen small, unglamorous habits — spanning health, productivity/mindset, social life, and money — are backed by research showing outsized compounding effects, from short bursts of vigorous exercise to automated savings and pre-bed brain dumps.

## Core Concepts
- [[Habit Stacking]] and small-consistent-action compounding over dramatic overhauls.
- [[Zeigarnik Effect]] — unfinished tasks occupying working memory, addressed by writing tomorrow's task down before bed.
- [[Decision Fatigue]] reduction via automation (savings, closing browser tabs).
- [[Getting Things Done]] (David Allen) — weekly review and two-minute-task principles.

## Key Takeaways
- **Exercise Intensity**: 15 min/week vigorous activity linked to 18% lower mortality risk (Harvard Health, ~72,000 adults).
- **Pre-Meal Water**: ~500ml before meals improves satiety and supports weight loss.
- **Phone Out of Bedroom**: 87% of Americans sleep with phones nearby despite evidence linking this to worse sleep.
- **Post-Lunch Walk**: A 10-minute walk after lunch lowers blood glucose more than one 30-minute walk any time of day.
- **Reading for Stress**: 6 minutes of reading can cut stress by up to 68% (University of Sussex).
- **Automate Savings**: Thaler and Benartzi's research shows automated savings programs significantly boost savings by removing friction.
- **Two-Minute Tidy**: Nightly two-minute tidying counters clutter-linked cortisol elevation (UCLA, 32 dual-income families).

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models
- **[[Compounding]]**: None of the 14 habits produce dramatic short-term change; their value comes from small, consistent repetition accumulating over time — the same logic that governs compound interest.
