## Summary

Oregon Community Credit Union (OCCU) is moving most of its nearly 700 employees to a 32-hour workweek at full pay by the end of 2027, following a 2023 call-center pilot. CEO Greg Schumacher says the model only works when each team first redesigns its work, and a team transitions only after proving readiness through measurable service results. The call center saw tenure rise from nine months to 3.1 years and call wait times fall from 5:47 to 1:17.

## Core Concepts

- **[[32-Hour Workweek]]**: OCCU's "100-80-100" model: 100% of the work, in 80% of the time, for 100% of the pay.
- **[[Oregon Community Credit Union]] rollout**: Team-by-team expansion; 15 teams and 188 employees by end of 2026, including a Roseburg branch pilot started Oct. 1.
- **[[Workflow Redesign]]**: Teams remove unnecessary steps, meetings and handoffs, use technology better, and cross-train before changing schedules.
- **Readiness gating**: Teams must show coverage, cross-training and service measures before transitioning; the calendar does not set the pace.
- **Paired metrics**: Attendance, retention and workload are tracked alongside wait times, abandonment, transfers and first-call resolution.
- **Coverage design**: Branches stagger schedules rather than closing a day or cutting hours; days off are not always Monday or Friday.
- **[[Employee Retention]]**: The call-center pilot was meant to make jobs more attractive and cut turnover.

## Key Takeaways

- **Retention**: Call-center average tenure rose from about nine months to 3.1 years.
- **Absences**: Average daily unplanned absences fell from five employees to 1.6.
- **Service**: Call wait time fell from 5:47 to 1:17; abandonment from 16.30% to 6.86%.
- **Headcount**: Call center runs on 51 full-timers versus a 67 peak, via normal attrition.
- **No new hires**: The model relies on schedules, cross-training and eliminated work, not added staff.
- **Not a scheduling exercise**: The hard work happens before schedules change.
- **Meeting discipline**: Staggered schedules force stricter meeting norms and better documentation.
- **Exclusions**: Executives are excluded; management transitions only after individual contributors.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Shorter hours require redesigned work** — Compressing unchanged work into fewer hours just moves pressure around; the work itself (steps, meetings, handoffs) must change first.
→ explains: Not a scheduling exercise, No new hires, Meeting discipline

**T2. Pair human and service measures to catch displaced pressure** — Tracking employee measures and outcome measures together shows whether improvement is real or has simply been shifted to another person or team.
→ explains: Retention, Service, Absences

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is OCCU's central claim about making a 32-hour workweek work?
**A**: It is not a scheduling change; teams must first redesign workflows, cross-train, and build coverage. A team transitions only after demonstrating it can maintain or improve service and results.

**Q2**: What evidence did the call-center pilot produce?
**A**: Average tenure rose from nine months to 3.1 years, unplanned absences fell from five to 1.6 a day, wait times dropped from 5:47 to 1:17, and abandonment fell from 16.30% to 6.86%.

**Q3**: How should a branch with required in-person coverage adopt the model?
**A**: Keep branch hours unchanged and stagger schedules, cross-train staff and align coverage with member demand, while tracking wait times, appointment availability and workload, as in the Roseburg pilot.
