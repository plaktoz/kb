## Summary

Smart Brevity, by VandeHei, Allen, and Schwartz, argues that modern information overload demands a radical rethink of how we communicate in writing. The core method is to front-load every message with one surprising, value-packed sentence and then give readers the option to go deeper. By pairing this principle with attention-grabbing subject lines and structural "attention magnets," writers consistently get their messages read rather than skimmed or deleted.

## Core Concepts

- [[Smart Brevity]] — a writing framework that treats the reader's attention as a scarce, perishable resource
- [[Information Overload]] — average workers receive 75 more emails per day than a decade ago; patience for new messages is at an all-time low
- [[Inverted Pyramid Writing]] — lead with the most important fact, then layer in context and details
- [[Attention Magnets]] — four structural devices (axiom headers, paragraph breaks + bold text, emojis, visuals) that guide skimmers through a message
- [[Dopamine-Driven Engagement]] — a great opening sentence delivers a small neurochemical reward that buys a few more seconds of attention
- [[Subject Line Optimization]] — 80% of email success is determined before the body is opened

## Key Takeaways

- **Lead with value**: give readers the one new thing they need in the first sentence.
- **Smart Brevity template**: This happened → Why it matters → Here are the details.
- **Subject lines**: be provocative and specific in six words or fewer.
- **Active voice**: use "who did what" format with one-syllable words where possible.
- **Axiom headers**: bold headers like "Why it matters" signal the message is worth reading.
- **Attention magnets**: axiom headers, bold text, emojis, and visuals guide skimmers down the page.
- **Say it aloud**: polish subject lines and opening sentences until they spark visible interest.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Scarcity Principle]]**: Attention is finite and depletes with each new demand — Smart Brevity treats every word as a cost, which explains why front-loading value outperforms conventional warm-up openers.
- **[[Signal-to-Noise Ratio]]**: The framework is a direct application of maximizing signal per unit of reader effort; every structural device (bold, bullets, short words) lowers noise and raises signal density.
