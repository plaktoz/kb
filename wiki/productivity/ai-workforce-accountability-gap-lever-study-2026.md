## Summary
A Lever Talent survey of 484 senior leaders at US and Canadian companies with 100 to 999 employees found that 45% have no single person accountable for how employees and AI work together. Firms with a dedicated owner were far more likely to call AI extremely valuable (55% versus 22% of others). The report ties clear task division and error ownership to higher value, but it does not establish that these practices cause better outcomes.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Human-AI Collaboration]]**: How employees and AI divide work, and who oversees the result.
- **[[Workforce Orchestrator]]**: The designated leader Lever Talent proposes to coordinate people, technology, and processes.
- **[[Agentic AI]]**: Autonomous systems that complete multiple tasks, where 61% of adopters cannot confirm human review of every workflow.
- **[[AI Governance]]**: Policies, inventories, and review processes that track AI's impact on the workforce.
- **[[Human Resources]]**: Involved in AI decisions in only 18% of surveyed companies before tool selection.
- **[[Lever Talent]]**: Source of the Co-Intelligent Workforce Report: 2026 Baseline.

## Key Takeaways
- **Accountability Gap**: 45% of mid-sized firms lack one leader for human-AI work.
- **Owner Effect**: 55% with a dedicated owner call AI extremely valuable, versus 22%.
- **Unowned Risk**: Only 10% of firms with no owner call AI extremely valuable.
- **Defined Roles**: 51% with clear task and error ownership report extreme value.
- **HR Exclusion**: Just 18% involve HR before selecting AI technologies.
- **Tech-Only Investment**: Only 6% of tech-only investors call AI extremely valuable.
- **Agentic Oversight**: 61% using agentic AI cannot confirm human review of workflows.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Value from a tool tracks whether someone owns how work is split between people and the tool, and who corrects its errors.** — Owner Effect and Defined Roles show it.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the Lever Talent study?
**A**: Nearly half of mid-sized companies have no single person accountable for how employees and AI work together, even though nearly all have adopted AI.

**Q2**: What data supports the value of clear ownership?
**A**: 55% of firms with a dedicated owner describe AI as extremely valuable, compared with 22% of other companies and 10% where nobody owns the responsibility.

**Q3**: What should an organization do with this finding?
**A**: Name one accountable owner, define which tasks AI should perform and where human judgement stays essential, and assign who corrects automated mistakes.
