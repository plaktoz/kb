## Summary

The biggest obstacle to AI adoption is not access to the technology but the time to learn it, because experimentation otherwise competes with sprint commitments, meetings and deadlines. Drawing on a two-day AI hackathon, the author argues that adoption works when experimentation targets meaningful business problems, time is truly protected, learning is collaborative, and scoring rewards measured outcomes. Useful discoveries must then be carried forward into shared playbooks and real project paths.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Adoption]]**: Real adoption means turning individual discoveries into organizational capabilities, not just handing out tools such as an AI coding assistant.
- **[[Protected Learning Time]]**: Two full build days with sprint work formally removed, backed by executive sponsorship, centrally issued calendar blocks and manager buy-in.
- **[[Hackathon]]**: Structured around two themes: near-term measurable business impact, and radically faster delivery from idea to customer value in under an hour.
- **[[Mob Programming]]**: Participants watch colleagues work with AI in real time, so a technique can spread across a team within minutes.
- **[[Cross-Functional Teams]]**: Engineering, support and sales surface different problems, and the relationships outlast the event.
- **[[Scoring Rubric Design]]**: Impact was 35% of the score and required a measured before-and-after result.
- **[[Model Context Protocol]]**: API access, MCP servers, integrations, licenses, tokens and model capacity were prepared before the build clock started.
- **[[AI Security Guardrails]]**: Sandboxes with synthetic data, least-privilege access, no sensitive data in prompts, and humans approving AI-generated changes.
- **[[Shared Playbook]]**: Agents, integrations, skills and prompt patterns captured so the next team does not rediscover them.

## Key Takeaways

- **Time Is the Barrier**: The main obstacle to AI adoption is finding time to learn, not access.
- **Pick Real Problems**: Focus on a few business-relevant themes; mundane, repetitive processes are often highest value.
- **Day One Builds**: Pre-configure access, tokens, integrations and capacity so no hours are lost to setup.
- **Standardized Access**: Common toolchains emerged, making peer help and later support easier.
- **Protect the Time**: Remove sprint work and signal leadership backing; add mentor office hours and help channels.
- **Team Sport**: Mob programming plus cross-functional teams spread techniques quickly.
- **Measured Results**: Teams cut code-to-customer delivery to under an hour, rule creation from three weeks to under 24 hours, and an eight-hour video workflow to 30 minutes.
- **Carry Momentum**: Shared playbooks and paths forward keep the best ideas from vanishing into a backlog.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Measured outcomes steer behavior** — People optimize for whatever leaders reward, so a scoring rubric functions as strategy; rewarding measurable business results produces them, while rewarding flashy demos produces flashy demos. · *model:* [[Incentive Alignment]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does the author identify as the biggest obstacle to employee AI adoption?
**A**: Not access to the technology, but the time to learn to use it effectively, since experimentation competes with sprint commitments, meetings and deadlines.

**Q2**: How did the hackathon's scoring rubric shape results?
**A**: Impact was 35% of the score and needed a measured before-and-after result, which produced concrete gains such as rule creation dropping from three weeks to under 24 hours.

**Q3**: How can an organization make protected learning time effective rather than symbolic?
**A**: Pair calendar blocks with executive sponsorship and manager buy-in, prepare infrastructure and security in advance, offer mentor support, and capture outputs in a shared playbook with a path forward for the best projects.
