## Summary

Liz Wiseman's *Multipliers* distinguishes two leader archetypes — Multipliers who amplify their team's intelligence and Diminishers who suppress it — based on research with 150 leaders across organizations worldwide. Multipliers operate from the belief that their team is already talented and simply needs ownership and space, while Diminishers assume they are the smartest in the room and therefore over-direct. The book prescribes five disciplines (Spot, Set, Stage, Stop, Shift) that systematically transfer thinking and ownership back to the team.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Multipliers vs Diminishers]]**: Two leader types differentiated by whether they expand or constrain the collective intelligence of their teams. Diminishers unintentionally drain energy by over-directing; Multipliers create conditions where people operate at full capacity.
- **[[Multiplier Mindset]]**: The belief that the team is already packed with talent, great ideas, and knowledge — and that giving people ownership will cause them to make great decisions independently.
- **[[Liz Wiseman]]**: Researcher and author who studied 150 multiplier and diminisher leaders via 360° interviews; coined the Multipliers framework.
- **Five Multiplier Disciplines (5 S's)**:
  - **Spot** — Identify each person's effortless genius, even outside their official role, and publicly leverage it.
  - **Set** — Issue challenges that stretch the team beyond their comfort zone ("mission impossible" goals).
  - **Stage** — Convene structured debates for high-stakes decisions rather than dictating answers.
  - **Stop** — Resist solving team members' problems; respond with "I don't know — what do you think?"
  - **Shift** — Return ownership immediately after offering input; remain a coach, not the player.
- **[[Talent Leverage]]**: Actively scanning for and amplifying natural strengths to grow engagement and capability across the team.

## Key Takeaways

- **Diminishers try to help** but become black holes that drain team energy and initiative.
- **Same person, different leader**: Derek's Navy story shows performance is context-dependent, not fixed.
- **Morning multiplier email**: Daily self-reminder that the team has talent and needs ownership to grow.
- **Spot genius publicly**: Celebrating a team member's natural skill causes it to expand and inspires others.
- **Set impossible missions**: Stretch goals unlock capabilities teams didn't know they had (72% stock increase example).
- **Stage debates**: Ask "what would you decide and why?" before making high-stakes calls.
- **"Disagree and commit"**: Winning the debate isn't the goal — getting buy-in for execution is.
- **Stop solving problems**: Reduce leader airtime in meetings so team members fill the space with solutions.
- **Shift ownership**: Always have an exit plan after offering help; say "you're still the lead on this."

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Autonomy and Mastery]]**: Wiseman's Multiplier disciplines map directly onto self-determination theory — people perform best when they have ownership (autonomy) and are stretched toward growth (mastery), which Diminishers systematically remove.
- **[[Pygmalion Effect]]**: The multiplier mindset operationalizes the Pygmalion effect — when leaders genuinely believe in their team's capability, they create conditions (challenge, space, visibility) that cause the team to rise to those expectations.
