## Summary

Supercut research of 547 US and UK agent users finds workers spend a median 12 hours of a 40-hour week briefing and reviewing [[AI Agents]], a "[[Handoff Tax]]" of about 30%. The hardest thing to convey is background knowledge, not task steps, so enterprises need better ways to capture and pass on organisational context. The study was funded and designed by Supercut, which sells a screen-recording product, so it describes active agent users only.

## Core Concepts

- [[Handoff Tax]]: Time spent briefing agents, supplying context, following up and reviewing output.
- [[AI Agents]]: 49% of surveyed users hand a task to an agent before a colleague.
- [[Supercut]]: Vendor that ran the report "Briefing AI Agents: We show people, we tell agents".
- [[Tacit Knowledge]]: Background knowledge, company conventions and edge cases are hardest to explain.
- [[Context Transfer]]: Moving rules, history and permissions to an agent safely.
- [[Model Context Protocol]]: Supercut exposes recordings to agents through an MCP server.
- [[AI Governance]]: Approval thresholds and a named human owner for each agent's output.
- [[David Okuniev]]: Supercut co-founder: "We brief colleagues by showing them, and we brief agents by writing essays."

## Key Takeaways

- **Time Cost**: Median 12 hours weekly, about 30% of the working week.
- **Briefing Grows**: 45% spend more time briefing than when they started.
- **Review Shrinks**: 47% spend less time reviewing what agents return.
- **Self-Completion**: 72% did a task themselves because explaining it took too long.
- **Hardest Context**: 55% cite background knowledge; 40% company rules.
- **Medium Gap**: 78% show colleagues in person; only 14% give agents video.
- **ROI Blind Spot**: Counting only execution time ignores briefing and fixing effort.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Delegation cost includes context transfer** — The true cost of handing off work is briefing plus review plus correction, not just execution time; speed gains in execution can be erased upstream.
→ explains: Time Cost, Briefing Grows, ROI Blind Spot

**T2. Background knowledge resists transfer** — The knowledge hardest to hand over is the unwritten why, history and rules, not the task steps. · *model:* [[Tacit Knowledge]]
→ explains: Hardest Context, Medium Gap

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the Supercut research?
**A**: Active agent users spend a median 12 hours a week, about 30% of their week, briefing and reviewing AI agents, a "handoff tax" that undercuts the assumption of frictionless delegation.

**Q2**: What do workers find hardest to convey to agents?
**A**: Background knowledge (55%), then company conventions and rules (40%), then exceptions, edge cases and visual information (35% each).

**Q3**: How should enterprises change their ROI calculations for agents?
**A**: Measure the full cost, including time assembling the brief and fixing results, since an agent finishing in two minutes is no win if a human spends 15 minutes on setup.
