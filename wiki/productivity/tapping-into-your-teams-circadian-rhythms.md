## Summary

Circadian rhythms are stable biological dispositions, not lifestyle choices — and corporate culture's preference for morningness actively degrades performance from those with different chronotypes. Stefan Volk argues that scheduling high-stakes work against an employee's natural energy peak increases error rates and inter-team friction. Effective managers align task demands with chronotype rather than imposing uniform schedules.

## Core Concepts

- [[Chronobiology]] — science of biological time and circadian rhythms
- [[Chronotype]] — individual's biologically determined peak energy time: "larks" (morning), "night owls" (evening), "intermediate finch" (afternoon)
- [[Morningness Bias]] — corporate culture's systemic preference for early schedules, disadvantaging non-larks
- [[Task-Energy Alignment]] — matching cognitively demanding work to an individual's natural performance peak
- [[Stefan Volk]] — Professor of Management, University of Sydney Business School
- [[Body, Heart and Mind in Business Research Group]] — Stefan Volk's research group at U of Sydney

## Key Takeaways

- **Chronotypes are biological**: Not habits, not preferences — stable dispositions you can't train away.
- **Three chronotype clusters**: Morning (lark), afternoon (intermediate), evening (night owl).
- **Morningness bias**: Most orgs schedule important work at 9am — harmful for ~60% of employees.
- **Scheduling = performance lever**: Aligning task demands to energy peaks improves output quality and reduces errors.
- **Friction source**: Chronotype misalignment causes interpersonal tension mistaken for personality conflict.
