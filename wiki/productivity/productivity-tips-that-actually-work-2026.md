## Summary

A no-nonsense list of 19 unglamorous productivity habits — protecting deep work, eating the frog, managing energy rather than just time, and using AI as leverage rather than a crutch — framed around the idea that effective productivity advice is usually boring because it works.

## Core Concepts

- [[Eat the Frog]] — doing the most-avoided, highest-value task first thing, before email or meetings crowd it out.
- [[Energy Management]] — matching hard, judgment-heavy work to peak-energy hours and saving light admin for natural energy dips, rather than treating all hours as interchangeable.
- [[Shutdown Routine]] — a deliberate end-of-day ritual (review what's finished, capture what's unresolved, choose tomorrow's first task) that gives the workday a clean psychological ending.
- [[AI as Leverage]] — using AI for context-setting, drafting and option-generation while keeping judgment and final decisions with the human, rather than outsourcing thinking entirely.

## Key Takeaways

- **Better lists, not longer ones**: separate what needs doing today from what merely feels urgent; break vague tasks ("write report") into concrete actions ("draft intro," "pull sales figures").
- **Protect deep work with real boundaries**: block calendar time, close the inbox, start with 60-90 minute single-task sessions.
- **Busy isn't the same as effective**: ask "what actually matters today" rather than "what can I get done today."
- **Manage energy, not just time**: not every hour is equal — assign hard, judgment-heavy tasks to peak-energy windows.
- **Small environmental fixes compound**: natural light, less clutter, and phone out of the room measurably support focus without requiring an elaborate setup.
