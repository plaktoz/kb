## Summary

[[Cal Newport]] argues that AI's apparent failure to improve workplace productivity isn't a new problem created by AI — it's an existing dysfunction AI is magnifying. A survey of 6,000 digital workers found AI saves 11 hours/week but only 13% reported improved company performance, a gap Newport traces to [[botsitting]], tool-switching overhead, and [[pseudo-productivity]]. He frames AI's real opportunity as finally forcing organizations to fix work processes that were already broken by email and Slack.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Pseudo-Productivity]]** — Newport's term for visibly performing work rather than producing value; predates AI; now worsened by it
- **[[Botsitting]]** — time spent waiting on AI agents or monitoring AI outputs; hidden overhead in AI adoption
- **[[Workplace Theater]]** — organizational norm of signaling activity over delivering outcomes
- **[[AI Productivity Gap]]** — 11 hrs/week saved by AI vs. only 13% improved company performance; gap driven by pre-existing dysfunction

## Key Takeaways

- **11hrs saved, 13% improved**: AI saves individual time but rarely translates to organizational performance gains
- **Botsitting**: waiting on AI agents creates hidden overhead that undercuts time savings
- **Tool-switching cost**: 60% of workers use multiple AI tools; toggling between them adds friction
- **Not a new problem**: Newport diagnosed pseudo-productivity and overload in *Slow Productivity* (2024), before AI
- **Real opportunity**: AI novelty is drawing executive attention to workflow problems that have gone unaddressed for years
