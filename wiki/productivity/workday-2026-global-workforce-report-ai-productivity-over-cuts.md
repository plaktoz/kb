## Summary

[[Workday]]'s October 2026 [[Workday Global Workforce Report]] finds more business leaders expect [[Artificial Intelligence|AI]] to raise output from existing staff (40%) than to cut headcount (28%), with roles changing around employees rather than employees changing jobs. Employer skill demand is shifting away from basic prompting toward building AI tools, workflow automation and [[AI Engineering]], while internal mobility stalls and mentions of management and L&D skills in job requisitions decline. Workday stresses that its combined survey, workforce and recruiting data are descriptive, not causal.

## Core Concepts

- [[Workday Global Workforce Report]] — combines Workday workforce, employee-sentiment and recruiting datasets with a September survey of 6,001 respondents (~1,780 decision-makers) across the US, UK, Canada, Australia, France and Germany.
- [[AI Productivity vs. Headcount Reduction]] — leaders' expectations skew toward productivity gains and role changes over workforce cuts; multiple answers were allowed, so both can coexist.
- [[AI Skills Demand Shift]] — requisitions from 554 enterprise employers on [[Workday Recruiting]] show basic AI skills peaking in January 2026 and applied AI skills rising; technology was the exception, with both rising.
- [[Internal Mobility]] — lateral moves fell at 57% of employers and promotion rates stayed flat; blockers include paused roles, unsupportive managers and selection processes seen as unfair.
- [[Phil Willburn]] — Workday VP of people systems, intelligence & support: "Employees may not be changing jobs, but their jobs are changing around them."
- [[AI Literacy]] and [[Upskilling]] — 79% of workers know which skills they need, but only 66% feel employer-supported in building them.

## Key Takeaways

- **Productivity over cuts**: 40% expect higher staff output; 28% expect fewer employees.
- **Role change**: 34% expect changed roles or work; 33% expect new roles/skills.
- **Low no-impact view**: only 8% expect AI to have no significant impact.
- **Concentrated decline**: largest employers drive net headcount decline; typical employer growth ≈ zero.
- **Basic skills cooling**: basic AI skill demand fell 25% after a January 2026 peak.
- **Applied skills rising**: AI tool-building, automation and engineering demand rose 51% (Sep 2025–Jul 2026).
- **Support gap**: 79% know needed skills; 66% feel supported; 65% confident adapting.
- **Mobility stalled**: lateral moves fell at 57% of employers; promotions flat.
- **Staying put**: 49% didn't seek internal moves; 44% of them were satisfied.
- **Soft-skill demand falling**: management skill mentions down 7%; L&D mentions down 13%.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Descriptive data cannot assign cause** — Co-occurring trends (headcount decline alongside AI adoption, falling demand for a skill) do not establish why they happened; a drop in advertised demand may mean a skill is less useful or simply assumed. · *model:* [[Correlation vs Causation]]
→ explains: Concentrated decline, Basic skills cooling

**T2. Totals can hide the typical case** — An aggregate change can be driven by a few large units while the median unit barely moves, so the total misdescribes most participants' experience.
→ explains: Concentrated decline

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the report's central finding about how leaders expect AI to affect their workforce?
**A**: More leaders expect AI to raise productivity from existing staff (40%) than to reduce headcount (28%), with many also expecting changed or new roles.

**Q2**: How is employer demand for AI skills changing, according to Workday's requisition data?
**A**: Demand for basic skills like simple prompting peaked in January 2026 and then fell 25%, while demand for building AI tools, automating workflows and AI engineering rose 51% between September 2025 and July 2026.

**Q3**: What does the report imply leaders should do as jobs change?
**A**: Per Willburn, leaders should be honest about what is changing and give people a way to keep up, which matters given that only 66% feel supported in skill development and internal mobility has stalled.
