## Summary

Thomas Ptacek offers two strict rules for using LLMs as copyeditors without letting them erode your writing voice: never adopt any phrasing the model suggests, and explicitly suppress its tendency to encourage your drafts. LLMs are useful for systematic editing passes — flagging passive voice, filler words, structural problems — but only if you rewrite every correction yourself.

## Core Concepts

- **[[LLM Copyediting]]**: Using AI to flag writing problems rather than generate fixes — preserving author voice by refusing to adopt any model-generated phrasing.
- **[[Homogenization Risk]]**: Frontier models tend toward polished, headline-y uniformity; even "preferred" suggestions subtly push prose toward processed blandness.
- **[[Sycophancy in LLMs]]**: Models default to praising drafts, reinforcing first-draft instincts and short-circuiting genuine revision.
- **Fresh instance evaluation**: Using a new model session (without editing context) to compare old vs. new drafts — avoiding the sycophancy trap of a model that remembers the original.
- **[[Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace]]**: Referenced as a resource that makes copyediting a systematic, near-mechanical process.

## Key Takeaways

- **Rule 1 — No LLM words**: Any specific phrasing the model offers is disqualified, even if you like it.
- **Rule 2 — Suppress encouragement**: Explicitly forbid praise; stay skeptical of any that slips through.
- **Use LLMs for flagging**: Passive voice, filler words ("very," "actually"), repeated phrasing, misplaced paragraphs.
- **Rewrite yourself**: Ask the model to flag, rewrite the section yourself, then evaluate.
- **Fresh model for comparison**: New instance evaluates old vs. new — no editing memory means no sycophancy.
- **Tool, not authority**: Ptacek ignored GPT-5's note that the piece was "20% too long" — use the tool, don't obey it.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: If you optimize for "sounding good to an LLM," you start writing for the model's aesthetic rather than your own — Ptacek's ban on LLM phrasing is a direct structural defense against this drift.
- **[[Skin in the Game]]**: Forcing yourself to rewrite every flagged section keeps the voice and risk yours; outsourcing the fix externalizes authorship without you noticing.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Ptacek's core argument about LLM-generated phrasing?
**A**: Any wording the model suggests should be disqualified outright, because you cannot reliably detect all the subtle ways it pushes your prose toward polished blandness.

**Q2**: Why does Ptacek recommend using a fresh model instance for draft comparison?
**A**: A model that participated in editing will sycophantically favor the version it helped create; a fresh instance evaluates old vs. new without that bias.

**Q3**: What writing tasks are LLMs actually well-suited for, according to Ptacek?
**A**: Flagging overuse of passive voice, filler words, and repeated phrasing; identifying paragraphs that belong elsewhere; and running systematic editing passes from a personal checklist.
