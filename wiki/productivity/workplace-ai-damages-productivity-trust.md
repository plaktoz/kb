## Summary

A Stanford/BetterUp Labs study finds that 40% of US employees encountered AI-generated "workslop" in the past month — polished-looking but low-substance content that someone else must clean up — costing an estimated $186 per employee per month in lost productivity and damaging trust between colleagues.

## Core Concepts

"[[Workslop]]" is content that appears professional but lacks the depth or accuracy to actually move work forward, forcing recipients to fact-check, rewrite, or follow up. The [[Stanford Social Media Lab]] and [[BetterUp Labs]] study found workslop is bidirectional — 18% flows from workers up to managers, while 16% flows from managers down — creating a mutual-reliance dynamic that erodes [[Workplace Trust]]. Despite mixed evidence of real productivity gains (a UK government M365 Copilot trial found no definitive boost; Asana found 64% of British employees view AI agents as unreliable), employees still face organizational pressure to adopt AI tools.

## Key Takeaways

- **Prevalence**: 40% of US employees encountered workslop in the last month.
- **Cost**: Sorting through AI-generated slop costs employers roughly $186 per employee per month in lost productivity.
- **Trust damage**: 42% of recipients trust the sender less after receiving AI-written material; over a third judge the sender less intelligent or creative.
- **Bidirectional flow**: Workers send 18% of workslop up to managers; managers send 16% down to workers.
- **Weak productivity evidence elsewhere**: A 3-month UK government trial of M365 Copilot found no definitive productivity boost, and 64% of British employees view AI agents as unreliable (Asana study).
