## Summary

Inc.'s *Modern CEO* collected self-reported calendars from nine CEOs across retail, tech, healthcare and professional services. Most put direct reports and senior leaders first or second, with customers a close priority, and some blend the two by bringing employees along to client meetings.

## Core Concepts

- **[[CEO Time Allocation]]** — how chief executives split their calendars among direct reports, customers, and thinking and networking time.
- **[[Direct Reports and Leadership Team]]** — the top or second-ranked use of time for nearly all respondents (about 25-50 percent).
- **[[Customer Time]]** — [[Vimal Kapur]] of Honeywell spends about 40 percent with customers and partners, and uses it to test ideas before big decisions.
- **[[Nohria and Porter CEO Time Study]]** — a 2018 Harvard Business School study in which assistants coded CEOs' time in 15-minute increments; more rigorous than self-reporting.
- **[[Back-to-Back Meetings]]** — [[Kelly Cook]] of David's Bridal uses stacked 15- and 30-minute meetings with her executive team.

## Key Takeaways

- **Leaders First**: Direct reports and senior leadership ranked No. 1 or No. 2 for nearly all CEOs.
- **Pacsun and Mission North**: [[Brieane Olson]] spends about 30 percent with leaders; [[Tyler Perry]] about 25 percent coaching.
- **Hands-On Outlier**: [[René Saúl]] of Kapital devotes 50 percent to direct reports and their teams.
- **Customers as Reality Check**: Honeywell's CEO spends 40 percent with customers; it "keeps me honest".
- **Full Attention**: [[Raj Mehra]] of Sage brings a notebook, not a laptop, to meetings.
- **Method Caveat**: The data is self-reported, unlike the coded 2018 HBS study.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Where do most CEOs in the survey spend the largest share of their time?
**A**: On their direct reports and senior leadership, which ranked No. 1 or No. 2 for nearly all respondents, with customers also a top priority.

**Q2**: What specific allocations did the CEOs report?
**A**: Pacsun's CEO spends about 30 percent with leadership, Mission North's about 25 percent coaching reports, Kapital's 50 percent with reports and teams, and Honeywell's and Sage's CEOs about 40 percent with customers.

**Q3**: Why does Honeywell's CEO spend so much time with customers, and how reliable is this data?
**A**: He says field time with sales teams keeps him honest and lets him test ideas before product or acquisition decisions. The figures are self-reported, unlike the 2018 HBS study that had assistants code CEO time.
