## Summary

Unstructured "blank canvas" days lead people to default to [[Fake Work]]: easy, fun, non-essential tasks that keep them busy without moving their goals forward. Pham argues the root cause is a constraints problem rather than a discipline problem, and that time boundaries, goal-tagging, parking low-priority tasks, and public accountability make it harder to swap real work for fake work. As AI automates execution, deciding what to do becomes the key human skill, because otherwise "AI will help you do the wrong things faster."

## Core Concepts

- **[[Fake Work]]**: Any task that doesn't align with core goals, company objectives, or core values. Fixing a typo is fake work unless it serves a value like excellence.
- **[[Constraints Create Clarity]]**: Strict time boundaries, such as a named, protected 9–11 AM [[Deep Work]] block for Goal X, force prioritization by removing the option to wander.
- **[[Focus Block]]**: Schedule your top priority on the calendar the same way you'd schedule a meeting.
- **[[Goal-Tagging]]**: Brooks' method of tagging every task with the goal it supports. A task that can't be tagged is likely fake work, and the tags map where time actually goes.
- **[[Someday/Maybe List]]**: A parking lot for tasks that don't serve your top 3 goals. Parking reduces [[Cognitive Load]] and guilt without the sense of failure that deleting brings.
- **[[30/30 Rule]]**: Spend 30 minutes daily on a task not due for at least 30 days. This is Q2 (important, not urgent) work.
- **[[Accountability Effect]]**: Declaring priorities to a team or peer creates a social contract. The article suggests [[Wildly Important Goals]] (WIG) meetings or a 15-minute weekly check-in.
- **[[Incentive Design]]**: Incentives drive behavior more than culture. Rewarding lines of code or hours logged produces verbose, inefficient work.
- **[[Prioritization as a Future Skill]]**: A Barclays report highlights prioritization as the key future-of-work skill as AI takes over routine execution.

## Key Takeaways

- **Core reframe**: the problem is a lack of constraints, not a lack of discipline.
- **Audit**: check your to-do list against your quarterly goals to expose fake work.
- **Protect blocks**: name each block and tie it to a specific goal.
- **Tag or cut**: tasks without a goal tag don't belong on the main list.
- **One tweak**: move 10 low-priority items to a Someday/Maybe list this week.
- **30/30 Rule**: 30 minutes daily on work due 30+ days out.
- **Public goals**: Brooks' execution improved once his goals were declared to the team.
- **Metric trap**: line-count KPIs push developers toward knowingly wasteful, verbose code.
- **AI warning**: without prioritization, AI just accelerates the wrong work.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Parkinson's Law]]**: Work expands to fill the time available. An empty calendar invites drift, and "if the game is hours logged, you'll fill the hours." Imposed time boundaries are the countermeasure.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Once lines of code or hours logged become the target, people optimize the proxy (verbose code, filled hours) instead of real outcomes. That is why Pham says to change the metrics or make real contributions visible.
- **[[Eisenhower Matrix]]**: The 30/30 Rule explicitly targets Quadrant 2 (important, not urgent) work, which urgent fake work otherwise crowds out.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does Pham argue that empty calendars make people less productive?
**A**: Without boundaries, people drift toward fake work, meaning quick, interesting, or top-of-list tasks that don't advance core goals. He frames this as a constraints problem rather than a discipline problem.

**Q2**: How does Goal-Tagging work, and what does it reveal?
**A**: You tag every new task in your task manager with the specific goal it supports. Any task you can't tag is likely fake work or a distraction, and the tags show where time actually goes versus where you think it goes.

**Q3**: What concrete first step does the article recommend, and why does prioritization matter more with AI?
**A**: Move 10 low-priority items off your main list to a Someday/Maybe list this week. As AI automates routine execution, the human role shifts to deciding what to do, and without that skill AI just helps you do the wrong things faster.
