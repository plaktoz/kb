## Summary

A ranked roundup of 15 time-management methods for knowledge workers — time blocking, the Pomodoro Technique, the Eisenhower Matrix and others — grounded in research showing structured approaches like time blocking beat unstructured to-do lists, while task-switching carries a measurable productivity cost.

## Core Concepts

- [[Time Blocking]] — scheduling specific calendar slots for tasks; a 2018 Journal of Consumer Research study found time-blockers complete 30% more tasks than unstructured to-do list users.
- [[Pomodoro Technique]] — 25-minute focused work intervals separated by 5-minute breaks, with a longer 15-30 minute break after four cycles.
- [[Task-Switching Cost]] — switching between tasks can cost 20-40% in lost productivity compared to sustained focus.
- [[Deep Work]] — a state of distraction-free concentration that enables learning hard things and producing quality work quickly, popularized by Cal Newport.

## Key Takeaways

- **Deep work is scarce**: a 2026 study of 500,000+ hours of remote work found only 51% of work time goes to deep work tools, versus 34% to communication tools and 15% to meetings.
- **Breaks help cognition**: a 2026 UC Irvine study found short, regular breaks improve cognitive function by up to 15% versus uninterrupted work.
- **Time blocking works**: people who time block complete 30% more tasks than those working from unstructured lists.
- **Switching is costly**: task-switching can cost 20-40% in lost productivity.
- **Quick-start protocol**: time block the calendar, tackle the hardest task before email/Slack, batch low-energy tasks, use a timer for avoided work, and protect one 90-minute deep-work block each morning.
