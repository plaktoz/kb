## Summary

Drawing on Pavel Simonov's Need-Informational Theory of Emotions, this article argues workplace emotions are diagnostic signals of how confident employees are in achieving meaningful goals, not problems to be suppressed. It introduces the 5D Framework (Detect, Determine, Diagnose, Decide, Design) for leaders to decode emotions into root causes and targeted interventions, framed against Gallup data showing disengagement costs the global economy ~$10 trillion annually.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Need-Informational Theory of Emotions]]** — Pavel Simonov's model defining emotion as a function of (1) the importance of a goal and (2) one's estimated probability of achieving it; stronger emotion signals a more important goal paired with lower confidence.
- **[[5D Framework]]** — Detect (what emotion?), Determine (what goal?), Diagnose (what's missing?), Decide (individual, managerial, or systemic cause?), Design (what intervention builds confidence?).
- **Emotions as diagnostic signals** — stress signals resource gaps, anxiety signals uncertainty, anger signals blocked goals, disappointment signals expectation gaps, frustration signals unclear paths to a goal.
- **[[Employee Engagement]]** — Gallup's 2026 State of the Global Workplace: only 20% globally engaged, 16% actively disengaged, with 40% stressed, 23% sad, 22% angry, 22% lonely.

## Key Takeaways

- **Disengagement costs ~$10 trillion globally**, roughly 9% of global GDP, excluding burnout/turnover/healthcare costs.
- **91% of employers plan to increase mental health investment**, but often treat emotion itself as the problem rather than a signal.
- **5 diagnostic emotion types** map to specific missing conditions (resources, clarity, control, expectations, guidance).
- **5D Framework sequence**: detect and diagnose before deciding whether the cause is individual, managerial, or systemic, then design a targeted intervention.
- **Interventions should change conditions**, not just reduce the emotional reaction itself.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Signaling Theory]]**: The article reframes workplace emotions as informational signals about goal-achievability rather than noise to suppress — the emotion itself carries diagnostic content about missing resources, clarity, or control.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Workplace emotions are diagnostic signals about employees' confidence in achieving meaningful goals, not problems to eliminate, and leaders should decode them rather than just try to reduce them.

**Q2**: What is the mechanism behind Simonov's theory of emotion, according to the article?
**A**: Emotion is a function of how important a goal is to a person and how confident they are of achieving it — the more meaningful the goal and the lower the confidence, the stronger the emotional response.

**Q3**: How would a manager apply the 5D Framework to a frustrated employee?
**A**: Detect the frustration, determine the goal behind it, diagnose what's missing (e.g., unclear process or insufficient skills), decide whether the cause is individual, managerial, or systemic, then design an intervention — such as clearer guidance — that increases confidence the goal is achievable.
