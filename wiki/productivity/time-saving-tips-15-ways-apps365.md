## Summary
Time is lost in small leaks such as interruptions, task switching, searching for information and unproductive meetings, not in big chunks. The article offers 15 practical habits for work and home, from time audits and time blocking to batching, templates, automation and AI. It ends with a daily routine and the claim that saving 20 minutes a day wins back more than 80 hours a year.

## Core Concepts
- [[Time Audit]]: log tasks for five workdays and tag them to find the biggest drain.
- [[Context-Switching Cost]]: task switching can cost up to 40% of productive time, per the American Psychological Association.
- [[Time Blocking]] and [[Deep Work]]: protected 60-90 minute focus blocks with notifications off.
- [[Batching]] of email, messages, errands and cooking to switch only once.
- [[Two-Minute Rule]] from [[David Allen]]: do tasks under two minutes now.
- [[Automation]] and [[Generative AI]] for repeat work, templates and summaries.
- [[Meeting Overload]] and [[Information Search Waste]], backed by Microsoft, [[Asana]] and [[Atlassian]] data.

## Key Takeaways
- **Interruptions**: Heavily pinged Microsoft 365 users are interrupted every 2 minutes, about 275 times a day.
- **Switching Cost**: Task switching can cost up to 40% of productive time.
- **Recovery Time**: Returning to a project after an interruption can take up to 25 minutes.
- **Searching**: Leaders and teams waste 25% of their time searching for answers.
- **Meetings**: Individual contributors spent 3.7 hours weekly in unproductive meetings in 2024, up 118% since 2019.
- **AI Savings**: Generative AI users saved 5.4% of work hours, about 2.2 hours in a 40-hour week.
- **Breaks**: Top performers in a 2025 DeskTime study worked about 75 minutes, then rested 33.
- **Compounding**: Saving 20 minutes daily recovers over 80 hours a year.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Switching between tasks carries a hidden cost** — Each change of task or interruption costs recovery time on top of the interruption itself, so fewer switches preserve more productive time. Grouping similar work and protecting focus blocks reduce the number of switches. · *model:* [[Context-Switching Cost]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Where does the article say most time is lost?
**A**: Most time is lost in small pieces, not big chunks: messages, task switching, searching for information and chores. The article says the day is not too short, it is leaking.

**Q2**: What evidence does the article give for the cost of task switching?
**A**: It cites the American Psychological Association figure that switching can cost up to 40% of productive time. It also cites Gloria Mark's finding that returning to a project after an interruption can take up to 25 minutes.

**Q3**: How would you apply the article's daily routine?
**A**: Plan tomorrow's top three tasks in the evening, do the hardest task in a 60-90 minute focus block with notifications off, and check email at set times. Log your time at day's end and batch errands and meal prep weekly.
