# Managing Office Politics: The Three Networks Framework

**Source:** Harvard Business Review IdeaCast — Linda A. Hill & Kent Lineback, *Being the Boss: The 3 Imperatives for Becoming a Great Leader*
**Category:** [[Productivity]] | Management | Networking

---

## Core Insight

Organizations are inherently political entities. Politics arise from three structural realities built into all organizations:
1. **Diversity of perspectives** — people see problems differently
2. **Interdependence** — you need others to get anything done
3. **Scarce resources** — tough allocation decisions breed conflict

Ignoring organizational politics doesn't make you principled — it leaves you powerless. As Hill notes: *"Powerlessness corrupts."* Without influence, you can't act on what you believe is right.

---

## The Three Networks Every Manager Needs

### 1. Operational Network
- Purpose: Get the team's daily work done
- Includes people you depend on and who depend on you right now
- Most managers build this one naturally

### 2. Strategic Network
- Purpose: Scan the environment for opportunities, threats, and what's really happening across the organization
- Helps you prepare for the future
- Often neglected but critical in dynamic environments

### 3. Developmental Network
- Purpose: Access the work experiences you need to grow
- Key principle: *"Who you know determines what you get to do, and what you get to do determines what you get to know"*
- Requires cultivation — you can't shortcut it by asking for mentorship upfront; build credibility first

> Networks can and should overlap to reduce the time cost of cultivating each.

---

## Building Relationships Strategically

- Don't limit your network to people you like or people like you — this is especially limiting in global organizations
- Personal chemistry is optional; professional relationships are not
- Use low-stakes moments (task forces, cross-functional projects) to meet people across the org before you need them
- Build relationships *before* problems arise — you can't forge trust mid-crisis

**The "competent jerk" trap:** Being technically competent buys you early inclusion. Sustained abrasiveness causes people to quietly exclude you, eroding your expertise through isolation.

---

## How Others Perceive and Experience You

Signs your network is broken:
- Frequently blindsided by decisions
- Excluded from pre-meeting meetings
- Things you want to get done don't get done despite good logic

Key question to ask: *"How do people experience themselves when they're with me?"* — not just whether they like you.

---

## Navigating Toxic Politics

When politics in your organization become genuinely dysfunctional (people pursuing personal wins over organizational interests):
- Don't try to beat them at the game — you won't win
- Keep your head down, focus on your group's work
- The only durable counter to bad-faith actors is building your *own* source of influence

---

## Practical Implications

- Treat network-building as part of the job, not a distraction from it
- Proactively share your priorities with key stakeholders so they understand your intent before you ask for favors
- Aim for long-term reciprocal partnerships, not transactional exchanges
- If you find yourself consistently lacking resources or excluded from decisions, audit your relationships — not just your work quality
