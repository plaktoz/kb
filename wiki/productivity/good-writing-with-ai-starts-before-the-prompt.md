## Summary

Mike Taylor, head of evals at Every, outlines a 12-step AI-assisted writing workflow that cuts a 1,200-word piece from a full day's work down to under two hours. The workflow is structured around four stages — pre-writing, AI drafting, human refinement, and editorial collaboration — with significant human judgment required at every stage. Taylor's central point is that AI compresses drafting time but the surrounding work of finding ideas, gathering material, and deciding if a draft delivers remains irreducibly human.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI-Assisted Writing]]**: Using multiple models ([[Claude]], [[GPT-6 Astra]], Fable) fed a personal style guide and context, then assembling the best parts from each.
- **Pre-writing discipline**: Title-first thinking, source collection, skeleton outline, and voice recording via the [[Monologue App]] before any AI involvement.
- **[[Human-in-the-Loop]]**: Self-editing accounts for ~20–30% of draft changes; conclusions are almost always rewritten from scratch.
- **Editorial collaboration**: Accepting grammar/style corrections while defending edits that dilute voice or weaken ideas.

## Key Takeaways

- **Title first**: Starting with the title anchors the piece before any drafting begins.
- **Voice recording**: Dictating a rough monologue via the Monologue app captures natural tone.
- **Multi-model comparison**: Running the same prompt through several models then assembling the best parts.
- **Personal style guide**: Feeding AI a style guide constrains output toward the author's voice.
- **Self-editing threshold**: Expects to change 20–30% of an AI draft during human refinement.
- **Conclusions stay human**: Claude's conclusions are rarely adequate; Taylor rewrites them.
- **Time saving is real**: A 1,200-word piece cut from a full day to under two hours.
- **Ideas still require humans**: "I still have to bring something worth saying and care enough to get it right."

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Separation of Concerns]]**: Taylor's workflow cleanly separates idea generation, raw material collection, AI drafting, and human judgment — each stage has a distinct owner (human vs. AI), which prevents the quality failures that come from conflating them.
- **[[Pareto Principle]]**: AI handles roughly 70–80% of draft production; focused human effort on the remaining 20–30% (refinement, conclusions, voice defense) delivers the quality that makes the piece worth publishing.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Taylor's core argument about where AI fits in the writing process?
**A**: AI compresses drafting time, but the surrounding work — finding ideas, gathering material, and judging whether a draft delivers — remains human work that cannot be delegated.

**Q2**: What does Taylor's self-editing step look like, and what does he always rewrite from scratch?
**A**: He changes approximately 20–30% of the AI draft during human refinement, and he almost always rewrites the conclusion because Claude's version is rarely adequate.

**Q3**: How would a writer apply this workflow to their own long-form piece?
**A**: Start with a title and skeleton outline, record a voice monologue to capture tone, feed context plus a style guide to multiple models, assemble the best parts, then apply a full human read-through focused on voice, idea strength, and rewriting the conclusion.
