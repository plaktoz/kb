## Summary

Mike Taylor presents a three-phase writing workflow — before, during, and after prompting — that positions AI as a drafting accelerator while reserving the distinctly human work for both ends of the process. The workflow reduces a 1,200-word piece from a full day to under two hours without sacrificing reader engagement. The throughline is that AI compresses drafting time but cannot replace the judgment needed to find ideas, gather material, or craft an emotionally resonant conclusion.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI-Assisted Writing]]**: Using multiple models (e.g., [[Claude]], Fable, GPT-6 Astra) to generate draft candidates, then combining the strongest elements rather than accepting any single output whole.
- **Pre-prompt preparation**: Writing a compelling title first, gathering sources, building a structural skeleton, and recording a spoken monologue of raw thoughts before touching an AI model.
- **[[Personal Style Guide]]**: Feeding a style guide alongside context into the prompt to constrain AI output toward the author's voice.
- **Post-draft self-editing**: Reading end-to-end repeatedly and expecting to change approximately 20–30% of the AI-generated text.
- **Working with editors**: Accepting grammar corrections readily while defending edits that dilute the core idea or voice.

## Key Takeaways

- **Title-first discipline**: Write a compelling title before drafting to sharpen focus.
- **Spoken monologue**: Record raw thoughts aloud to capture unfiltered ideas as pre-prompt input.
- **Multi-model approach**: Feed the same context to several models, then cherry-pick the best.
- **Style guide as constraint**: Include a personal style guide in the prompt to preserve voice.
- **20–30% self-edit rate**: Expect and plan for significant post-AI revision.
- **Conclusions are hard**: AI-generated conclusions often fall flat; write the final paragraph yourself for emotional resonance.
- **Efficiency gain**: 1,200-word piece cut from a full day to under two hours with no reader engagement drop.
- **Defend your edits**: Accept grammar feedback freely; push back when an editor's change dilutes your core argument.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Division of Labor]]**: Taylor's workflow explicitly partitions cognitive tasks — human judgment handles idea generation and emotional resonance, AI handles prose throughput — matching each agent to its comparative advantage.
- **[[Garbage In, Garbage Out]]**: The quality of pre-prompt preparation (title, sources, skeleton, spoken monologue) directly bounds the quality of the AI draft; poor input produces drafts that require heavy revision or are unusable.
