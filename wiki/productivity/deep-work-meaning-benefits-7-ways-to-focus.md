## Summary
Deep work — Cal Newport's term for distraction-free, cognitively demanding concentration — contrasts with the "shallow work" (email, scheduling) that consumes 60% of knowledge workers' time. Building a deep-work habit involves choosing a philosophy, creating rituals, prioritizing high-impact tasks, and gradually expanding focus capacity from ~1 hour to a 4-hour daily maximum.

## Core Concepts
- [[Deep Work]] vs. [[Shallow Work]] (Cal Newport's distinction).
- [[Attention Residue]] — the cognitive cost of task-switching.
- Four deep-work philosophies: rhythmic, journalistic, monastic, bimodal.
- The daily "[[Shut-Down Procedure]]" for sustainable focus.

## Key Takeaways
- **Time Split**: Only 51% of remote work time is spent in deep-work tools; 34% in communication tools.
- **Coordination Tax**: 60% of knowledge workers' time goes to coordination, not strategic work.
- **Notification Cost**: 80% of knowledge workers keep their inbox open; 3 in 4 feel daily pressure to multitask.
- **Capacity Builds Gradually**: ~1hr/day for beginners, up to a 4-hour daily maximum after months of practice.
- **Rituals Matter**: Fixed location, time, and internet/phone rules cue the brain into focus mode.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is deep work and why does it matter?
**A**: Distraction-free, cognitively demanding concentration that lets people learn hard things and produce high-value work quickly — contrasted with logistical "shallow work" that dominates most workdays.

**Q2**: What data shows the scale of the shallow-work problem?
**A**: Only 51% of remote work time is spent in deep-work tools, versus 34% in communication tools and 15% in meetings; 60% of knowledge workers' time goes to coordination.

**Q3**: How would someone build a deep work habit?
**A**: Pick a philosophy (e.g., rhythmic blocks), create a fixed ritual to cue focus, reduce notifications, and grow capacity gradually from about 1 hour a day toward a 4-hour daily maximum over months.
