## Summary
An LSE Inclusion Initiative and Protiviti survey of 3,001 professionals finds 74% now use AI at work and 48% worry about being judged for *not* using it enough, versus 20% who worry about being judged for using it. Users save an average of 19% of their week but reinvest 72% of it in other work, raising the risk of "workload creep". AI training strongly lifts adoption and time savings, yet AI use shows no consistent link to perceived job performance.

## Core Concepts
- **[[London School of Economics]] / The Inclusion Initiative**: Third annual report, *Beyond the Technology: Who Really Captures AI's Gains at Work*, with [[Protiviti]]; survey run March to May 2026.
- **[[Workload Creep]]**: AI-created capacity absorbed by more output and broader responsibilities rather than freed time.
- **[[AI Dividend]]**: Where the saved time goes (higher-value work, innovation, development, wellbeing) matters as much as how much is saved.
- **[[AI Training]]**: Trained employees show higher adoption and larger time savings; access is uneven across generations ([[Generation Z]] vs [[Baby Boomers]]).
- **Perceived Job Performance**: How employees believe leaders see their work; tied to human traits, not AI use.
- **[[Grace Lordan]]**: LSE researcher who warns efficiency may simply raise the baseline of expectations.

## Key Takeaways
- **Adoption Surge**: 74% use AI at work, up from 29% in 2024.
- **Fear of Lagging**: 48% fear judgment for under-using AI; 20% for using it.
- **Time Saved**: Users save 19% of their week and reinvest 72% of it.
- **Expanding Scope**: 50% of time savers use the hours for new responsibilities or projects.
- **Training Effect**: 93% adoption among trained staff versus 50% among untrained.
- **Savings Gap**: Trained employees save 22% of hours versus 13% untrained.
- **Uneven Access**: 66% of Gen Z received AI training versus 40% of baby boomers.
- **Performance Disconnect**: No consistent link between AI adoption and perceived job performance.
- **Human Drivers**: Conscientiousness, resilience, confidence and willingness to learn predict perceived performance.

## 🧠 Core Principles

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the study's central finding about AI productivity gains?
**A**: AI users save about 19% of their week but reinvest 72% of it in other work, so gains risk being absorbed as "workload creep" instead of benefiting employees or higher-value work.

**Q2**: What evidence shows training matters for AI adoption?
**A**: Adoption is 93% among AI-trained employees versus 50% among untrained ones, and trained staff save 22% of working hours versus 13%.

**Q3**: What should leaders do in response?
**A**: Be explicit about where AI adds value and how freed time should be used, directing capacity toward higher-value work, innovation, development and wellbeing instead of just raising output baselines.
