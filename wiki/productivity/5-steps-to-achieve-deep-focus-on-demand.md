## Summary

Deep focus is the foundation of exceptional work, and it can be engineered on demand through five sequential, science-backed steps. The framework aligns work sessions to the brain's natural [[Ultradian Cycles]], primes attention through intentional writing, triggers neurochemical focus via visual fixation, sustains flow using Goldilocks timers, and extends sessions beyond perceived limits using the "twice then quit" protocol. Together these steps allow practitioners to reliably hit [[Flow State]] during their two strongest daily focus windows.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Ultradian Cycles]]**: ~90-minute biological attention cycles continue during waking hours, producing two notably stronger focus windows per day; identifying and protecting them is the prerequisite for deep work.
- **[[Reticular Activating System]] (RAS)**: Physically writing session intentions activates the RAS, causing the brain to filter distractions and elevate task importance — handwriting is more effective than typing.
- **[[Norepinephrine]] and [[Acetylcholine]]**: Neurochemicals that drive attention and alertness; visual fixation on a nearby point for 30 seconds boosts their release, creating a neurochemical on-ramp to focus.
- **[[Goldilocks Principle]] applied to timers**: A shot-clock timer set in the zone between anxiety (too short) and boredom (too long) reliably induces flow; mini milestones provide the target.
- **[[David Goggins]] 40% Rule**: Perceived exhaustion arrives at only 40% of true capacity; pushing through resistance — via the "twice then quit" technique — releases a further spike of acetylcholine.
- **[[Cal Newport]]'s Shallow Work**: Low-value tasks (email, texts, miscellaneous requests) are the primary enemy of deep focus sessions; the shot-clock technique creates urgency that crowds them out.

## Key Takeaways

- **Track ultradian focus ratings**: Set 90-minute alarms for a day; score each window 1–10 to identify your two prime windows.
- **Limit caffeine, sugar, and large meals** to preserve natural focus rhythms during tracking.
- **Write intentions on paper**: Half a sheet, folded; fill it with session-specific detail before starting.
- **Visual lock-in**: Fix gaze 12–18 inches away for 30 seconds immediately before beginning deep work.
- **Goldilocks timer**: Set a countdown for a mini milestone in 5–30 minutes; neither too easy nor too stressful.
- **Twice then quit**: When you feel spent, commit to exactly two more sprints — then fully disconnect.
- **Protect your prime windows**: Negotiate meetings, decline lunch invitations; treat these blocks as non-negotiable.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Parkinson's Law]]**: Work expands to fill available time — the shot-clock timer directly constrains that expansion by imposing an artificial deadline on each mini milestone, forcing concentration.
- **[[Progressive Overload]]**: The "twice then quit" technique applies the gym principle of going slightly beyond perceived limits to build capacity over time, using acetylcholine release as the neurochemical reward for doing so.
