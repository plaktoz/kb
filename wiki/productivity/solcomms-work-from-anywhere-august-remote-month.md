## Summary

NYC PR firm SolComms normally requires four days a week in the office, but it let all 53 employees work from anywhere for a month as its first "Work from Anywhere August." Employees asked for more remote days, and CEO [[Bruno Solari]] gave them a full month of flexibility instead of adding a weekly home day. Survey responses showed higher productivity, better work-life balance and strong demand to repeat the perk, and revenue rose modestly. The move runs against a broader [[Return-to-Office Mandate]] trend at large employers.

## Core Concepts

- **Time-boxed flexibility**: SolComms offered a month of full remote work rather than a permanent extra remote day each week, which avoided loosening the year-round [[Hybrid Work]] policy.
- **[[Remote Work]] guardrails**: Rules covered fixed 9-to-5:30 EST hours, cameras on, office dress for meetings, frequent Slack status updates, regular breaks, and no sensitive calls or screens in public.
- **Six-month rollout**: Leadership came up with the idea in January, announced it in February, and spent six months preparing best practices. The firm's 80-plus clients were also told about the plan.
- **"Internal pressure to be available"**: Remote staff felt tied to their computers and found it hard to step away. Solari sees this as a common problem with remote work.
- **RTO tightening elsewhere**: [[Uber]] is cutting about 3,300 corporate jobs and tightening office rules, and [[Disney]] now requires more employees in the office four days a week.
- **[[Flex Index]] data**: The share of Fortune 500 companies requiring five office days rose from 13% to 24% between mid-2024 and mid-2025.
- **[[Owl Labs]] data**: 25% of full-time US workers are hybrid, 65% are fully onsite and 10% are fully remote.

## Key Takeaways

- **Survey results**: 76% felt more productive and 90% kept high work-life balance.
- **Unanimous demand**: 100% of survey respondents want the program back next year.
- **Small sample**: Only 21 of 53 employees answered the post-month survey.
- **Revenue bump**: Revenue rose 4% from July to August and 12% from August to September.
- **Biggest draw**: Employees most valued cutting their commute.
- **Downside**: Remote staff felt "chained to a desk" and took fewer natural breaks.
- **Leader consistency**: Solari would have felt like a hypocrite mandating office time while he was in Spain.
- **Commitment**: The program is confirmed for August 2027.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Selection Bias]]**: Only 21 of 53 employees answered the survey, and those who enjoyed the month were probably more likely to respond. The 76%, 90% and 100% figures likely overstate how the whole company felt.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did SolComms try, and what was the result?
**A**: The four-days-in-office PR firm let all 53 employees work from anywhere for the month of August. Survey respondents reported higher productivity and work-life balance, all of them wanted it repeated, and revenue rose modestly.

**Q2**: Why did Solari offer a full remote month instead of an extra weekly work-from-home day?
**A**: Employees asked for more remote days every year, but Solari wanted to protect the in-person culture he values. Concentrating the flexibility in one month gave staff full freedom without permanently weakening the four-day office norm.

**Q3**: What lesson does SolComms' experience offer leaders designing remote-work perks?
**A**: Prepare in advance: clear guidelines, client communication and months of planning helped the trial succeed. Leaders also need to tell staff that being first to respond is not the same as delivering results, so remote work doesn't create pressure to be always available.
