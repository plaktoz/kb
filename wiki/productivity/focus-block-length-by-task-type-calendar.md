## Summary
The right focus-block length depends on the task: 20-30 minutes for admin, 60-90 for writing and coding, and 90-120 for strategy and research. Short blocks rarely reach deep flow, and every interruption resets the ramp-up, so the article pairs a simple default schedule with a one-week self-test and an environment built to protect long blocks.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Focus Block]]**: A calendared window of single-task work, sized to the kind of work rather than a fixed number.
- **[[Time Blocking]]**: Scheduling the day so priority work gets a protected block and admin gets small, bounded ones.
- **[[Deep Work]]**: Sustained, high-cognitive-load work that needs runway before output becomes meaningful.
- **[[Flow State]]**: Blocks under 30 minutes rarely reach it.
- **[[Context-Switching Cost]]**: Each interruption resets the ramp-up to focus.
- **Personal testing framework**: A Monday-to-Friday experiment from 25-minute to 2-hour blocks, ending with a mixed day.
- **Restorative breaks**: Walking, water, stretching, or non-work conversation rather than another cognitive load.
- **[[Environment Design]]**: Phone out of the room, full-screened tool, one monitor, a physical cue, and a pre-committed opening move.

## Key Takeaways
- **Task Matching**: Admin 20-30 min; writing, coding, design 60-90; strategy and research 90-120.
- **Reading Limit**: Learning and reading blocks beyond 45-60 minutes lose retention.
- **Reactive Work**: Support and sales calls fit 15-25 minute blocks between meetings.
- **Ramp-Up**: Blocks under 30 minutes rarely reach deep flow.
- **Weekly Test**: Try 25, 50, 90, and 120-minute blocks Monday to Thursday, then mix Friday.
- **Simple Default**: One 90-minute morning block, one 60-minute afternoon block, two 25-minute admin blocks.
- **Break Quality**: Rest should be physical or social, not more cognitive load.
- **Phone Placement**: Even a silenced phone in eyeline reduces cognitive capacity, per University of Texas research.
- **Opening Move**: Decide the first action before the block starts.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Interruptions reset the ramp-up to deep focus** — Entering a focused state takes time, and each interruption forces that cost to be paid again, so fragmented time yields shallow output. · *model:* [[Context-Switching Cost]]
→ explains: Ramp-Up, Phone Placement, Opening Move

**T2. Block length should match the cognitive demand of the task** — Light, reactive work fits short blocks, while demanding creative or strategic work needs longer runway; reading and learning are capped by falling retention.
→ explains: Task Matching, Reading Limit, Reactive Work, Simple Default

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: How long should a focus block be, according to the article?
**A**: It depends on the task: 20-30 minutes for admin, 60-90 for writing, coding, or design, and 90-120 for strategy and research. Reading and learning work best at 45-60 minutes.

**Q2**: Why are blocks under 30 minutes a poor fit for creative or strategic work?
**A**: They rarely reach deep flow, and every interruption resets the ramp-up. That is why context-switching is so damaging.

**Q3**: What default schedule and test does the article suggest?
**A**: Use one 90-minute morning block for top-priority work, a 60-minute afternoon block, and two 25-minute admin blocks. To tailor it, test 25-minute to 2-hour blocks across a week and compare.
