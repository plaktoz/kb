## Summary

Fifteen Fast Company Executive Board members share the daily management tools and habits they actually rely on — ranging from custom dashboards and AI meeting summaries to simple written lists and prioritization frameworks. The common thread across nearly all responses is that discipline and clarity beat any single piece of software.

## Core Concepts

Across the 15 responses, [[Prioritization]] emerges as the dominant theme — whether through a "pick-one" morning ritual, a three-question daily check-in, or a formal framework separating urgent from important. Several leaders lean on AI tools like [[Claude AI]] and [[Microsoft Copilot|Copilot Studio]] for meeting summaries and automation, while others deliberately avoid software in favor of a written list or a notebook. [[ClickUp]] appears as the example of centralizing tasks into "one source of truth" to prevent blame and ambiguity across teams.

## Key Takeaways

- **Own dashboards**: Building custom reporting removes the need for a reporting layer between leaders and the business.
- **AI meeting summaries**: AI-assisted summarization captures decisions and action items with minimal labor investment.
- **Written lists over memory**: Getting everything out of your head onto a list (plus one trusted calendar-holder) sharpens execution.
- **Pick-one prioritization**: Choosing the single highest-impact task each morning beats juggling many "urgent" items.
- **The follow-up habit**: Simply circling back on commitments outperforms any dashboard or project management tool.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Effective daily leadership relies less on any specific software and more on disciplined habits like prioritization, written systems, and consistent follow-up.

**Q2**: What specific AI tools were mentioned as part of leaders' daily workflows?
**A**: Claude AI (for thinking faster and pressure-testing ideas) and Microsoft Teams with Copilot Studio (for communication and building automations around day-to-day loose ends).

**Q3**: How would you apply the "pick-one prioritization" tactic in your own work?
**A**: Every morning, choose the single task that would have the most impact if completed that day, and treat everything else as negotiable — reducing decision fatigue and clarifying what actually matters.
