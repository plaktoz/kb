## Summary
Texas A&M researchers analyzed a year of data from more than 10,000 workers and found that sit-stand desk users logged about 43 more minutes of active computer time per day than regular-desk users. The authors value that gap at nearly $9,000 per employee per year. They frame it as a "workplace effect" in which the physical environment shapes employee behavior and performance.

## Core Concepts
- [[Sit-Stand Desks]]: Height-adjustable desks that let workers alternate between sitting and standing.
- [[Workplace Effect]]: Benden's term for the direct impact of the physical work environment on behavior, health, engagement, and performance.
- [[Active Computer Time]]: Software-tracked keystrokes, words typed, mouse clicks, and typing errors.
- [[Mark Benden]]: Texas A&M School of Public Health professor and director of the Center for Worker Health.
- [[Tricia Salzar]]: Co-lead and U.S. Army Public Health Command Region, West, ergonomist.
- [[Return on Investment]]: Extra hours multiplied by a standardized wage of about $52 per hour.

## Key Takeaways
- **Active Time Gap**: 3.66 hours versus 2.95 hours of daily active computer use.
- **Annual Hours**: Roughly 171 extra hours, over four 40-hour workweeks.
- **Dollar Value**: Nearly $9,000 per employee per year at about $52 per hour.
- **Output Measures**: Sit-stand users also typed more words and logged more keystrokes.
- **Data Design**: Comfort surveys paired with objective software activity logs.
- **Next Question**: How AI may reshape the physical workspace and outcomes.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central claim of the Texas A&M study?
**A**: Workers with sit-stand desks log more active computer time than those with regular desks, suggesting the physical workplace directly influences productivity. Benden calls this the "workplace effect."

**Q2**: How was the $9,000 figure derived?
**A**: Sit-stand users averaged 3.66 versus 2.95 hours of active computer time per day, about 171 extra hours a year. Multiplied by a standardized wage of about $52 per hour, that gives nearly $9,000.

**Q3**: How might an employer use the finding?
**A**: Employers could treat height-adjustable desks as an investment with a possible return of nearly $9,000 per employee annually, beyond comfort and health benefits.
