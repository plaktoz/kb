## Summary

Jeff Su draws on eight years of corporate experience across four companies to offer six practical tips for making 1:1 meetings with your manager more productive. The framework covers three phases: structured preparation before the meeting, the [[Rose Thorn Bud Framework]] for delivering business updates during the meeting, and disciplined follow-through on action items afterward. Consistent execution of this process builds managerial trust and earns employees more autonomy in decision-making.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Managing Up]]**: The intentional practice of structuring interactions with your manager to advance your own development and credibility.
- **[[Rose Thorn Bud Framework]]**: A three-part structure for business updates — a recent win (rose), a current challenge with a proposed solution (thorn), and a new idea or learning goal (bud).
- **[[1:1 Meetings]]**: Regular, recurring conversations between an employee and their manager covering business updates and professional development.
- **[[Shared Meeting Document]]**: A single living document (spreadsheet, Notion, or doc) capturing all agendas, notes, and action items for a recurring meeting series.
- **[[Communication Style Alignment]]**: Proactively informing your manager how you prefer to receive feedback, reducing miscommunication and friction.
- **[[Google Rework]]**: Google's organizational research body whose findings support weekly-to-biweekly 1:1 cadences of 30–60 minutes.
- **[[Kim Scott]]** / [[Radical Candor]]: Management framework referenced to support the case for structured, candid 1:1s.

## Key Takeaways

- **Lock a regular slot**: Weekly or biweekly, 30–60 minutes is the research-backed sweet spot.
- **Set format expectations upfront**: Agree on how time splits between business updates and development.
- **Share your communication style**: Tell your manager what kind of feedback works best for you.
- **Use one shared document**: Single spreadsheet or doc with date, agenda, notes, and action items columns.
- **Embed the doc in the calendar invite**: Reduces friction, keeps it two clicks away.
- **Send a preread for complex topics**: Email context before the meeting for blockers or conflicts.
- **Rose = win**: Start positively; attribute successes to manager's advice when warranted.
- **Thorn = problem + solution**: Always pair a challenge with at least one proposed solution.
- **Bud = new idea or learning goal**: Invite manager input on experiments or strategic context.
- **Spend 10 min post-meeting**: Review notes, prioritize action items, schedule follow-up time.
- **Nudge your manager on overdue items**: A polite reminder signals accountability and reliability.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Accountability Systems]]**: By externalizing commitments into a shared document and calendar blocks, both parties are held to action items — the system does the remembering so willpower does not have to.
- **[[Proactive Problem Framing]]**: Presenting a thorn with a solution draft signals agency and makes it easier for the manager to say yes; it shifts the cognitive load of solutioning away from them and toward the employee, which is the essence of managing up.
