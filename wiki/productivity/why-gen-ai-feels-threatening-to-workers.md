## Summary

As [[generative AI]] takes on more cognitive, creative, and interpersonal work tasks, employees often perceive it as a threat to their competence, autonomy, and sense of belonging. The authors propose the **[[AWARE framework]]** — acknowledge, watch, align, redesign, empower — to help leaders address the psychological roots of AI resistance rather than treating adoption as a purely technical rollout.

## Core Concepts

- **Three psychological needs**: [[Self-Determination Theory]]'s competence (feeling effective), autonomy (feeling in control), and relatedness (feeling connected to others) — whether gen AI satisfies or frustrates these determines whether workers embrace or resist it.
- **[[AWARE framework]]**: Acknowledge employee concerns; Watch for adaptive/maladaptive coping behaviors; Align support systems with psychological needs; Redesign workflows around human-AI synergies; Empower workers through transparency and inclusion.
- **Covert resistance**: The article frames AI resistance as often invisible — disengagement and quiet opposition rather than open pushback — making it easy for leaders to underestimate.

## Key Takeaways

- **Adoption is psychological, not just technical**: The central argument is that successful gen AI rollout depends on emotional/social adaptation, not just providing access to tools.
- **Threatened needs predict resistance**: When AI undermines competence, autonomy, or relatedness, workers disengage or covertly resist rather than raising concerns directly.
- **AWARE as an actionable leadership checklist**: Gives leaders five concrete levers instead of a vague mandate to "manage change better."

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Generative AI feels threatening to workers because it can undermine their sense of competence, autonomy, and relatedness at work — and how leaders manage these psychological needs determines whether AI adoption succeeds or triggers resistance.

**Q2**: What framework do the authors propose, and what does it stand for?
**A**: The AWARE framework: Acknowledge employee concerns, Watch for coping behaviors, Align support with psychological needs, Redesign workflows around human-AI synergy, and Empower workers through transparency and inclusion.

**Q3**: How would a manager apply this framework when rolling out a new AI tool?
**A**: They would surface and validate employee concerns upfront, monitor whether staff are adapting well or disengaging, tailor support and training to unmet psychological needs, redesign workflows collaboratively rather than imposing them, and communicate transparently to preserve a sense of autonomy and inclusion.
