## Summary

Workers responding to a Futurism Instagram post say employer-mandated AI has lengthened their days because they must review, correct, and rebuild AI-generated output. A social services worker and an advertising worker describe checking and rework crowding out senior responsibilities and adding process steps. The account is anecdotal and sits against a backdrop of research the post says shows little productivity gain.

## Core Concepts

- [[AI Slop]]: Unedited AI-generated documents that colleagues pass along, creating repetitive content and unintended promises.
- [[AI Productivity Paradox]]: Employers promote AI for speed while employees report added hours.
- [[Futurism]]: Outlet whose Instagram post prompted the worker responses.
- [[AI Mandates]]: Management requiring AI use "at all costs".
- [[Human Review of AI Output]]: Checking and correcting generated text is the main time sink cited.
- [[AI Disclosure and Documentation]]: Agency requirement before AI-assisted marketing work is published.
- [[AI Energy and Water Demand]]: Computing and data-center cooling draw electricity and fresh water.

## Key Takeaways

- **Reported Effect**: Workers say AI added hours through review, correction, and rebuilding.
- **Unedited Output**: Colleagues skipping edits pushes cleanup onto recipients.
- **Displaced Work**: Verification crowds out the senior responsibilities one worker wanted.
- **Creative Rework**: Approved AI concepts still need rebuilding to be deliverable, editable, and owned.
- **Added Process**: Disclaimers and documentation insert steps between idea and publication.
- **Coping Tactic**: Returning AI material to its authors for corrections after checking.
- **Exploration vs Production**: AI is useful for exploring concepts, less for carrying them through.
- **Resource Cost**: AI use raises electricity and fresh-water demand.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central claim of the article?
**A**: Despite bosses pushing AI to boost productivity, some workers say it has added hours to their workday. They spend that time reviewing and correcting AI-generated material.

**Q2**: What specific examples of added work do the workers give?
**A**: A social services worker deals with unedited AI documents containing repetitive content and unintended promises. An advertising worker must rebuild approved AI concepts so the agency can deliver, edit, and scale them and own the rights.

**Q3**: How are workers responding or adapting?
**A**: The social services worker returns checked AI material to colleagues for correction. The agency requires disclaimers and documentation, and the worker separates using AI to explore concepts from carrying them through production.
