## Summary

Rather than promoting a single time-management system, this guide argues people should first diagnose their actual bottleneck — prioritization, scheduling, execution, or energy management — and then choose the matching technique. A person who struggles to start tasks needs a different intervention (e.g., a Pomodoro timer to lower the activation barrier) than someone who struggles to protect already-scheduled focus time (who needs calendar boundaries instead).

## Core Concepts

- **[[Diagnostic Time Management]]** — treating technique selection as a diagnosis-first exercise rather than adopting a trending system wholesale
- **[[Energy Management]]** — matching task type to the time of day when a person is mentally sharpest, as a distinct lever from scheduling or prioritization

## Key Takeaways

- **Match the tool to the problem**: prioritization issues, scheduling issues, execution issues, and energy issues each require a different fix
- **One-size-fits-all systems often fail** because they solve a bottleneck the user doesn't actually have
