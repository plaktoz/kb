## Summary

For work environments where deep-work blocks aren't realistic, this guide offers seven tactics calibrated for reactive, high-velocity conditions: a single committed must-do task, 25-minute micro-focus windows instead of long blocks, upfront triage of urgent-vs-important, pre-committed decision rules, energy-based scheduling, deliberate context-switch management, and a short weekly review.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Micro-Focus Windows]]**: 25-minute single-objective focus sessions as a substitute for unattainable 90-minute deep work blocks.
- **[[Urgent vs. Important]]**: A triage distinction used to prevent reactive drift — most fast-paced-environment "urgency" isn't actually important.
- **[[Pre-Committed Decision Rules]]**: Deciding in advance how to handle recurring categories of requests (e.g., meeting requests under an hour) to reduce decision fatigue.
- **[[Energy Management]]**: Scheduling demanding work within one's 2–3 hours of genuine peak cognitive capacity, treating energy — not just calendar time — as the constraint to manage.
- **[[Context Switching Cost]]**: Citing research that it takes 10–20 minutes to fully re-engage after an interruption, arguing the goal is managing switches, not eliminating them.

## Key Takeaways

- **One must-do before the day starts**: commit the night before to a single non-negotiable, meaningful task.
- **25-minute micro-focus windows** beat trying to force unrealistic 90-minute blocks in interrupt-heavy cultures.
- **Two-minute triage passes** (morning and afternoon) prevent hours of reactive drift.
- **Pre-committed rules** (e.g., batch Slack responses at fixed times) remove repeated in-the-moment decisions.
- **Peak-energy scheduling** — protecting your 2–3 highest-capacity hours for the hardest work — is called the single highest-impact scheduling move available.
- **Common mistakes**: starting the day reactively, treating all urgency as equally important, multitasking instead of sequencing, and skimping on sleep during high-pressure periods.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Standard deep-work advice fails in fast-paced environments, so productivity there depends on a different toolkit built around micro-focus windows, triage, pre-committed rules, and energy management rather than long uninterrupted blocks.

**Q2**: What specific technique replaces the traditional 90-minute deep-work block?
**A**: 25-minute micro-focus windows with a single, concrete objective — enough real focus to be useful without requiring conditions (silence, long uninterrupted time) that fast-paced environments don't provide.

**Q3**: How would someone apply the "protect energy, not just time" principle in practice?
**A**: By identifying their 2–3 hours of daily peak cognitive capacity and scheduling their most demanding work there while protecting that window from meetings, rather than just blocking arbitrary calendar time.
