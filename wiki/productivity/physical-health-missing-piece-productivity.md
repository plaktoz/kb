## Summary

TELUS Health data shows physical health is a major, under-addressed driver of workplace productivity: UK workers dissatisfied with their physical health lose 58.4 productivity days a year versus 35.5 for satisfied peers, with presenteeism — not absence — accounting for most of the hidden cost.

## Core Concepts

- [[Presenteeism]] — lost productivity while still at work due to fatigue, pain or poor sleep; accounts for ~£24B of the £51B poor mental health costs UK employers annually.
- [[Resource Caravans]] (Harvard Business Review) — daily exercise triggers mutually reinforcing next-day benefits (better sleep, more energy, sharper focus) that compound over time.
- [[Wellbeing Continuum]] — self-service perks mainly engage employees already thriving; struggling employees rarely self-initiate care, requiring employer-led outreach instead.

## Key Takeaways

- **Productivity cost**: Physically dissatisfied UK workers lose 58.4 workdays/year vs. 35.5 for satisfied workers.
- **Presenteeism dominates**: £24B of £51B in UK poor-mental-health costs comes from presenteeism, not absenteeism.
- **Compounding effect**: Regular exercise improves sleep and protein synthesis, which raises next-day energy, focus and stamina.
- **Culture matters most**: Workers who see their organisation's culture as unsupportive of wellbeing lose 24 more productivity days/year than those who see it as supportive.
- **Injury reduction**: Employees with higher physical activity have a 15% lower risk of workplace injuries.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Employee physical health is a major, underused lever for workplace productivity, with the biggest hidden cost coming from presenteeism rather than sick days.

**Q2**: What mechanism explains why daily exercise boosts next-day work performance?
**A**: HBR's "resource caravans" concept — exercise improves sleep quality and protein synthesis, which together raise energy, focus and stamina the following day.

**Q3**: Why do self-service wellness perks fail to reach the employees who need them most?
**A**: Employees who are struggling or unwell rarely self-initiate care on their own; self-service tools mainly engage people who are already thriving, so proactive, employer-led outreach is needed instead.
