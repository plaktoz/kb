## Summary
A vendor comparison (published by GoodHabitz, which ranks itself first) of seven workplace productivity training options, organised by who each suits. It argues that one-off courses fade and that training must build habits, fit the workday and be measurable by adoption. Treat the rankings as promotional, though the selection criteria are reusable.

## Core Concepts
- [[GoodHabitz]]: online human-skills platform using [[Activity-Based Learning]] (two to eight minute activities) plus a Client Success Manager.
- [[Coursera]]: accredited, credential-oriented hard skills, priced per seat.
- [[FranklinCovey]]: facilitated workshops such as The 5 Choices to Extraordinary Productivity.
- [[Getting Things Done]]: [[David Allen]]'s single method for capturing commitments and next actions.
- [[Alison]], [[Think Productive]] and [[Microsoft Learn]]: free self-paced courses, practical workshops, and tool training.
- [[Habit Formation]]: why training must continue past launch week.

## Key Takeaways
- **Interruptions**: The average Microsoft 365 user is interrupted every two minutes.
- **Selection Criteria**: Coverage of human skills, durability, workday fit, reach, visibility and value.
- **Format Fit**: Credentials suit Coursera; cohorts suit workshops; whole organisations suit continuous learning.
- **Habit Timing**: A UCL study found new behaviours took 66 days on average to become automatic.
- **MIT Sloan Study**: A 12-month garment-factory programme returned 256% ROI, but in one setting.
- **Measurement**: Judge by adoption and engagement; distrust unexplained promised ROI figures.
- **Securitas UK**: 8,115 accounts activated and 38,000+ learning hours logged (vendor-reported).
- **Manager Leverage**: Gallup: managers account for at least 70% of variance in engagement.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Behaviour change requires repetition over time** — Skills become lasting habits only through repeated practice, so a single exposure fades and training needs ongoing return visits. · *model:* [[Habit Loop]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central argument about productivity training?
**A**: The best training builds habits that outlast launch week rather than being a one-off event. It should cover human skills like focus, planning and communication.

**Q2**: What evidence does it give that habits take time to form?
**A**: A UCL study found a new behaviour took 66 days on average to become automatic. The authors conclude a single course cannot do the job alone.

**Q3**: How should an L&D leader choose between options?
**A**: Start with who the training is for, then decide between a one-off and ongoing format, then check measurable adoption and budget. For example, a credential need points to Coursera, while organisation-wide reach points to continuous learning.
