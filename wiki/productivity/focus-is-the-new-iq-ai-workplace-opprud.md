## Summary

Louise Opprud of Work Life Lab argues that as AI absorbs routine tasks, the work left to humans is complex, creative and analytical, and it needs deep concentration that a constantly interrupted working culture undermines. The scarce human skills become asking the right questions, evaluating AI output and taking responsibility for it, plus the ability to keep learning. She calls for organising work to protect focus time rather than rewarding instant replies.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Deep Focus]]**: Sustained concentration on one task; Opprud says it is a precondition for learning, creativity and complex problem-solving.
- **[[Louise Opprud]]**: Founder of Work Life Lab, with fifteen years of work on the future of work.
- **[[Flow (Psychology)]]**: Complete absorption in a task; some studies cited show up to 700 per cent higher creativity.
- **Brain fry and [[Workslop]]**: "Brain fry" is a brain overloaded with input; workslop is AI-generated work that looks finished but fails under scrutiny.
- **[[System 1 vs System 2 Thinking]]**: Kahneman's fast intuitive and slow focused modes; AI leaves the slow mode to humans.
- **[[Continuous Learning]]**: The World Economic Forum predicts 39 per cent of current skills change or become obsolete by 2030.
- **Always-on culture**: Prompt replies are treated as good colleagueship, at the expense of focus time; the author ends on an email auto-reply that batches email to a couple of times a week.
- **[[Eric Schmidt]]**: Estimates the next generation of AI could take over 90 per cent of current work tasks around 2030-32.

## Key Takeaways

- **Fragmentation Cost**: Up to 40 per cent of productive time is lost to distractions.
- **Attention Stats**: 47 seconds per screen, 2,600+ phone touches and 117 emails daily.
- **Human Role**: As AI produces more, asking, evaluating and taking responsibility matter more.
- **Hidden AI Cost**: Checking AI output demands the concentration that work culture erodes.
- **Skill Demand**: Analytical thinking, creative thinking and technological literacy top WEF lists.
- **Member Sentiment**: 52 per cent of IDA members say AI raised their job satisfaction.
- **Evening Work**: Hard thinking pushed to evenings hits the brain's weakest hours.
- **Verification Burden**: An AI-produced proof in days may need months of expert checking.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Complex cognition and learning require sustained, uninterrupted attention** — Learning new things and doing demanding tasks need a protected space for concentration; fragmentation removes the capacity regardless of how much time is nominally available.
→ explains: Fragmentation Cost, Attention Stats, Evening Work, Hidden AI Cost

**T2. Automation shifts human work toward slow, evaluative thinking** — When routine tasks are automated, what remains is judgment: framing questions, assessing outputs and taking responsibility, which is effortful slow thinking. · *model:* [[System 1 vs System 2 Thinking]]
→ explains: Human Role, Verification Burden, Skill Demand

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Opprud's central claim about AI and the workplace?
**A**: The bigger risk is not losing your job to AI but losing your focus and ability to learn, because the remaining human work needs deep concentration that today's interrupted workday undermines.

**Q2**: What data points illustrate how fragmented attention is?
**A**: People spend an average of 47 seconds on one screen before switching, touch their phones over 2,600 times a day and get 117 emails a day, and up to 40 per cent of productive time is lost to distractions.

**Q3**: What should organisations do in response?
**A**: Rethink accessibility and reward focus time instead of fast replies, and experiment with ways of organising work that create better conditions for immersion, learning and creativity.
