## Summary
The 2026 Workstyle Monitor (Jabra-sponsored, with Workstyle CIC and King's College London) finds that technology explains more of individual productivity (13.5%) than team productivity (8.9%), so AI's individual efficiency is not translating into organisational gains. Where people work matters very little; autonomy over how work is shaped is the factor that stands out. Leaders are urged to pair personal autonomy with shared "connective capability" such as preserved team context.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Workstyle Monitor]]**: multi-year longitudinal study by [[Workstyle CIC]] and [[King's College London]], sponsored by [[Jabra]]; first wave covers 1,058 mostly UK knowledge workers.
- **[[AI Productivity Paradox]]**: individual efficiency rises, but collective performance gains stay small.
- **[[Super-Individual]]**: one person plus AI doing work that once needed several specialists and handovers ([[Prithwiraj Choudhury]], LSE).
- **[[Shared Context]]**: the reasoning, caveats and expertise created in conversations, which AI can only build on if captured.
- **[[Connective Capability]]**: the organisational ability to turn individual AI capability into team capability.
- **[[Worker Autonomy]]**: control over how tasks are shaped, distinct from location flexibility.
- **[[Return-to-Office Policy]]**: location and the digital/face-to-face mix show little link to team productivity.
- **[[Trust in Workplace Technology]]**: correlates with trust in teammates (r=.40).
- **[[Neurodivergent and Disabled Workers]]**: equal productivity but lower autonomy, inclusion and vitality.

## Key Takeaways
- **Tech Explains Less For Teams**: Technology explains 13.5% of individual productivity versus 8.9% of team productivity.
- **Small AI Correlations**: AI use correlates r=.23 with better ways of working, r=.17 with individual productivity.
- **Capacity Needs Direction**: Benefits depend on autonomy over where AI-saved capacity goes, such as deeper thinking.
- **Location Barely Matters**: Digital versus face-to-face balance shows no relationship with reported team productivity.
- **Autonomy Stands Alone**: Only autonomy stays significantly linked to vitality once other work factors are controlled.
- **Choosing Location Is Not Autonomy**: The true driver is control over how tasks are shaped and executed.
- **Tech Trust And Team Trust**: Confidence in workplace technology adds 2.5% explained variation in team trust.
- **Vitality Gaps**: Disabled workers score 4.21 versus 4.89; neurodivergent 4.12 versus 4.82; women 4.67 versus men 4.87.
- **Comparable Output, Worse Conditions**: Equal productivity is often achieved in a less supportive environment.
- **Five Directives**: Expand autonomy, design around interdependency, protect collective judgement, equip managers, measure links.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Individual gains do not aggregate into team gains by default** — Capability created at the level of one person only compounds at the group level if the connections between people are redesigned to carry it. The article argues this from the weaker tech-to-team correlation and the loss of context between people.
→ explains: Tech Explains Less For Teams, Small AI Correlations, Five Directives

**T2. Downstream tools can only build on context that was captured** — When the reasoning behind decisions stays in memory, inboxes or private notes, the next person or AI agent must reconstruct it from scratch, which caps how far individual gains transfer to the team.
→ explains: Five Directives, Tech Trust And Team Trust

**T3. Saved capacity creates value only when its use is chosen** — Freed time and effort deliver benefits only if workers control where it goes, rather than defaulting to more volume of the same work.
→ explains: Capacity Needs Direction, Autonomy Stands Alone, Choosing Location Is Not Autonomy

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the Workstyle Monitor's central finding about AI and productivity?
**A**: AI is boosting individual efficiency but not collective organisational performance: technology explains 13.5% of individual productivity versus 8.9% of team productivity.

**Q2**: What evidence shows that autonomy matters more than office location?
**A**: When computer-use intensity, location flexibility, desk-based and asynchronous working, and autonomy were assessed together, autonomy was the only factor still significantly associated with worker vitality. Being able to pick where you work does not drive a sense of autonomy; control over how tasks are shaped does.

**Q3**: How should leaders act on these findings to get returns from AI?
**A**: Shift from raw tool adoption to system design that pairs personal autonomy with shared context, for example by capturing why decisions were made so records travel with the work. Also equip managers and measure the links between people as well as the individuals.
