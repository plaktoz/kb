## Summary

Mike Taylor outlines 13 principles that guided his shift from AI skepticism to regular AI-assisted publishing. His central test for publishability is whether the author can defend every idea in conversation — regardless of how the prose was drafted. He argues AI models have crossed the threshold of "worth publishing" quality, making the tooling ethically acceptable if the ideas remain genuinely yours.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI-Assisted Writing]]**: Using AI as a drafting partner while retaining idea ownership and the ability to defend every claim.
- **Input vs. Output ratio**: Prompts should be larger than the desired output — load them with your own raw material to constrain AI invention.
- **[[Idea Ownership]]**: If you cannot argue for an idea under pushback, it is not ready to publish regardless of who (or what) wrote it.
- **[[Originality]] through firsthand experience**: Writing from experiments and observations that are not yet in training data preserves genuine distinctiveness.
- **Short-form as idea incubator**: Social posts should stay human-written because they are where ideas begin and where writers feel personal stakes.

## Key Takeaways

- **Input > Output rule**: Prompt with your own material to limit AI invention.
- **Defend the ideas test**: Can you argue every idea if a reader pushes back? If yes, publish.
- **Originality source**: Write from firsthand experiments not already in training data.
- **Social posts stay human**: Short-form is where ideas originate; keep personal risk.
- **AI democratizes ideas**: Helps non-writers publish valuable thinking, cuts time significantly.
- **Quality bar crossed**: Models are now "worth publishing" quality, though controversy remains.
- **Contract note**: Taylor wrote his O'Reilly book without AI per contract — calls it his last such project.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: If polished prose becomes the publication standard, writers optimize for prose quality over idea quality — Taylor's "defend the ideas" test redirects the metric back to intellectual substance.
- **[[Skin in the Game]]**: Keeping social posts human-written preserves felt personal risk, which Taylor argues is what makes short-form ideas worth taking seriously.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Mike Taylor's core test for whether AI-assisted writing is publishable?
**A**: He asks whether he can defend every idea in conversation if a reader pushes back — if yes, it is publishable regardless of how the draft was produced.

**Q2**: What does Taylor mean by "input > output" in AI writing prompts?
**A**: The prompt should be larger than the desired output and loaded with the author's own material, which constrains the AI to the author's ideas rather than inventing new ones.

**Q3**: How does Taylor justify keeping social media posts human-written while using AI for longer pieces?
**A**: Short-form posts are where ideas begin, and feeling the personal risk of posting keeps the creative process authentic and generative.
