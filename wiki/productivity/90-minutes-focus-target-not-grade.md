## Summary
Ninety minutes of focus is a target, not a pass/fail grade: use whatever window you have, from 15 to 90 minutes, and recover away from the screen. Work around [[Ultradian Rhythms]], match hard tasks to energy peaks, and protect focus blocks early in the week. Judge each day by which outcome moved, not by tasks checked off.

## Core Concepts
- [[Ultradian Rhythms]] versus the [[Circadian Rhythm]]: about 90-minute alertness cycles inside the 24-hour cycle, after which performance declines.
- [[Pomodoro Technique]] as training wheels: build from 25 to 45, 60, then 90 minutes, and chain cycles as focus strengthens.
- [[Chronotype]] and energy audit: track focus hourly for a week (or 3 days) to find peaks and valleys.
- [[Brooks's Rule]]: aim for 90 minutes, but use any 15-, 30-, or 60-minute window you get.
- Physical breaks: leave the workstation; staying at the desk is a context switch that blocks recovery.
- Outcomes versus activity: an outcome is a clear objective that may need several tasks in one block.
- AI usage metrics (tokens, prompts) as an activity-counting trap.

## Key Takeaways
- **Block Structure**: Expect 60-90 focused minutes, a 15-30 minute reset, then a second cycle.
- **Window Flexibility**: A 15-, 30-, or 60-minute window still counts.
- **Pomodoro Progression**: Stretch 25 minutes to 45, 60, then 90 as focus strengthens.
- **Energy Audit**: Rate focus hourly for 3-7 days; put key outcomes on peaks.
- **Real Breaks**: Leave the workstation; desk-based email or social checks block recovery.
- **Early Calendar Holds**: Book focus time before meetings claim open hours.
- **Outcome Question**: Ask each evening which outcome moved forward, not how many tasks closed.
- **Metric Trap**: Counting AI tokens or prompts rewards activity, like measuring police by tickets.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Sustained effort yields diminishing returns past a threshold, so recovery is part of the work** — Performance rises and then declines in cycles; overloading a single sitting, like 100 grams of protein at once, wastes capacity. Spreading effort across repeatable cycles with real breaks sustains output. · *model:* [[Diminishing Returns]]
→ explains: Block Structure, Real Breaks, Pomodoro Progression

**T2. Counting activity is not measuring impact, and quotas invite empty output** — Metrics based on task counts, tokens, or tickets are easy to game and miss the result that mattered. · *model:* [[Goodhart's Law]]
→ explains: Outcome Question, Metric Trap

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does "90 minutes is a target, not a grade" mean?
**A**: Aim for a 90-minute focus block, but a 15-, 30-, or 60-minute window still counts, and you should use whatever window you actually have.

**Q2**: What mechanism makes breaks and block length matter?
**A**: Ultradian rhythms of about 90 minutes of rising and falling alertness mean performance declines after intense focus. Real recovery away from the workstation prepares the brain for the next cycle.

**Q3**: How should you evaluate your day and apply this tonight?
**A**: Ask which outcome moved forward rather than counting tasks. Rewrite tomorrow's top task as a single outcome and place it on a window you can honestly hold.
