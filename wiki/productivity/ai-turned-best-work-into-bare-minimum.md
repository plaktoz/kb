## Summary

AI efficiency gains at work are being converted into higher output expectations rather than returned to employees as free time, per GoTo/Workplace Intelligence research showing employees save 2+ hours daily but 60% feel pressured to use AI and 39% say reliance is making them less intelligent. Because speed is the most visible output of AI adoption, it has become the default performance proxy, obscuring the invisible judgment work of catching AI errors and producing "workslop" that takes longer to fix than doing the work well the first time. The article argues leaders must return recaptured time, measure impact over throughput, and set honest expectations rather than quietly redefining "enough."

## Core Concepts

- [[Workslop]] — fast-output, low-value AI-generated work that floods organizations when speed is the only metric measured
- [[GoTo]] and [[Workplace Intelligence]] — Pulse of Work 2026 study (2,500 employees/IT decision-makers across 10 countries)
- [[ActivTrak]] — analysis of 443 million hours of work activity across 1,100+ organizations
- [[Gallup]] — research showing frequent AI use among managers has doubled since 2023
- [[Dan Schawbel]] — author, Managing Partner of [[Workplace Intelligence]]
- [[Cognitive Load]] — the invisible judgment labor of reviewing, fact-checking, and correcting AI output

## Key Takeaways

- **Time savings get redirected**: Employees save 2+ hrs/day with AI, but organizations assign more work instead of returning that time.
- **Adoption pressure**: 60% of employees feel pressured to use AI to boost productivity.
- **Perceived intelligence cost**: 39% say reliance on AI is making them less intelligent.
- **Deep work erosion**: ActivTrak found AI doubled time on email/messaging while focused deep work fell 9%.
- **Speed becomes the proxy**: Because it's the most legible output of AI adoption, speed replaces judgment as the performance metric.
- **Workslop tax**: 43% of employees used AI content they suspected was low quality; 77% say AI-generated work takes longer to review than human work.
- **Training gap**: 65% say employers aren't equipping them with needed AI skills; 80% say training is inadequate.
- **Leader fix #1**: Actually return recaptured time to employees instead of assigning more work.
- **Leader fix #2**: Measure impact and judgment quality, not raw output throughput.
- **Leader fix #3**: Set honest expectations openly rather than quietly redefining what "enough" looks like.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Jevons Paradox]]**: Just as efficiency gains in resource use tend to increase total consumption rather than reduce it, AI-driven efficiency at work is being consumed as more assigned output rather than converted into employee time savings.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core paradox the article identifies about AI adoption at work?
**A**: AI dramatically speeds up tasks, but the time saved is not returned to employees as breathing room — it is immediately converted into higher output expectations, so workers do more in the same hours rather than less.

**Q2**: What did the ActivTrak analysis of 443 million work hours find about AI's effect on deep work?
**A**: AI adoption doubled time spent on email and messaging while focused deep work time fell by 9%, indicating AI increased busywork rather than freeing time for high-value thinking.

**Q3**: What three changes does the article recommend leaders make?
**A**: Actually return recaptured time to employees instead of assigning more work, redesign performance measurement to reward impact and judgment over raw output speed, and set honest expectations openly about sustainable workloads.
