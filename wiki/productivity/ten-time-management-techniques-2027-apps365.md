## Summary
Workdays fail from fragmentation, not laziness: Microsoft's 2025 Work Trend Index found workers are interrupted about every two minutes (~275 times a day). The article presents 10 time management techniques, a problem-to-method selection table, three combinable "stacks", and ways to use AI for logistics while keeping human judgment over priorities. Its advice is to audit first, prioritize before scheduling, protect focus, then start small and stack methods.

## Core Concepts
- [[Time Audit]]: log 3–5 days of activity in 30-minute blocks and tag by deep work, shallow work, meetings, admin, distraction.
- [[Eisenhower Matrix]] and the 4 D's (Do, Defer, Delegate, Delete), popularized by [[Stephen Covey]].
- [[Pareto Principle]]: roughly 80% of results come from 20% of efforts.
- [[Eat the Frog]] ([[Brian Tracy]]): do the hardest, most important task first.
- [[Time Blocking]]: assign every part of the day to a task type on the calendar.
- [[Timeboxing]] and [[Parkinson's Law]]: work expands to fill the time available.
- [[Pomodoro Technique]] ([[Francesco Cirillo]]): 25-minute sprints with 5-minute breaks.
- [[Task Batching]]: group similar small tasks into set windows.
- [[Getting Things Done]] ([[David Allen]]): capture, clarify, organize, reflect, engage.
- [[Personal Kanban]] with a work-in-progress limit.
- [[Context-Switching Cost]]: the hidden cost behind constant interruptions.

## Key Takeaways
- **Interruption Load**: Workers are interrupted roughly every two minutes, about 275 times daily.
- **Audit First**: Track time for 3–5 days before choosing a technique.
- **Prioritize Before Scheduling**: Eisenhower Matrix or 80/20 decides what deserves your best hours.
- **Tight Timeboxes**: Cut estimates by 20–30% to counter [[Parkinson's Law]].
- **Batching**: Check email in set windows, e.g. 11:00 and 16:00, with notifications off.
- **WIP Limit**: Cap Personal Kanban "Doing" at 2–3 cards.
- **Stacks**: Deep Work Stack is Eisenhower, time blocking, then Pomodoro inside blocks.
- **Buffers**: Leave 20–30% of the calendar unscheduled; ADHD planners add 25–50% to estimates.
- **AI Role**: Use AI for logistics (focus blocks, triage, recaps); keep priorities human.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Task switching taxes focus** — Every switch between tasks costs attention and raises error rates, so interruptions fragment output far beyond their own duration. · *model:* [[Context-Switching Cost]]
→ explains: Interruption Load, Batching, WIP Limit

**T2. Work expands to fill the time allotted** — Without a fixed limit, effort stretches to the available time, so tight boundaries force completion. · *model:* [[Parkinson's Law]]
→ explains: Tight Timeboxes

**T3. A few inputs drive most results** — Outcomes are unevenly distributed, so the best hours belong to the vital few tasks. · *model:* [[Pareto Principle]]
→ explains: Prioritize Before Scheduling

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about why workdays fail?
**A**: They fail because of fragmentation rather than laziness; interruptions about every two minutes break focus, so the right techniques turn a reactive day into deliberate progress.

**Q2**: How does timeboxing use Parkinson's Law?
**A**: Parkinson's Law says work expands to fill the time available, so timeboxing gives a task a deliberately tight limit (estimate minus 20–30%) and stops when the box ends.

**Q3**: How should someone choose and combine methods?
**A**: Match the main problem to a starting method (e.g. constant interruptions: time blocking, then batching) and stack methods once the first is mastered, using AI for logistics while keeping judgment over priorities.
