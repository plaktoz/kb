## Summary

A time-management guide for busy entrepreneurs centers on two core levers: applying the [[Pareto Principle]] to focus effort on the 20% of tasks driving 80% of results, and building a consistent daily routine with protected deep-work blocks. Delegation, boundary-setting, and eliminating digital distractions round out the approach.

## Core Concepts

The **[[Pareto Principle]]** (80/20 rule) is used as a triage tool: review daily tasks, identify the small subset that actually advances business goals, then delegate or automate the rest. This pairs with **[[Delegation]]** — sorting tasks into must-do/should-do/could-do buckets and handing off the could-do items — and the **[[Eisenhower Matrix]]** for goal prioritization by urgency and importance.

## Key Takeaways

- **80/20 triage**: Focus effort on the 20% of tasks producing 80% of results; delegate or automate the rest.
- **Routine over willpower**: A consistent daily structure with dedicated deep-work blocks reduces reliance on discipline alone.
- **Delegate deliberately**: Sort tasks into must-do/should-do/could-do, then hand off could-do items with clear instructions.
- **Distraction elimination**: Turn off notifications, use website blockers, and keep a tidy, quiet workspace.
- **SMART + Eisenhower combo**: Use SMART goals for clarity and the Eisenhower Matrix to prioritize by urgency/importance.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Busy entrepreneurs get the most leverage from combining Pareto-based task triage with a consistent, protected daily routine, rather than trying to do more of everything.

**Q2**: What specific delegation mechanism does the article recommend?
**A**: Sorting tasks into must-do, should-do, and could-do categories, then delegating or outsourcing the could-do tasks to team members or freelancers.

**Q3**: How does this apply practically to a busy work week?
**A**: Start each day by identifying the 1-2 highest-leverage tasks (the Pareto 20%), block dedicated time for them, and actively hand off or automate lower-value work rather than letting it crowd the schedule.
