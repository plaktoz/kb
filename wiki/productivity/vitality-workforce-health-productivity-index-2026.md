## Summary

Vitality's Global Health and Productivity Index 2026, conducted with [[RAND Europe]] across five markets, found U.S. workers lose 63.4 productive days a year to poor health — 85% of it through presenteeism rather than absence — costing the U.S. economy $2.33 trillion annually, the largest impact among the five countries studied. The study also identifies AI-related concern as an independent risk factor: high-concern workers lose 27.6 more productive days a year than those with no AI concern.

## Core Concepts

- [[Presenteeism]] — being physically present but underperforming due to poor health; accounts for 53.9 of the 63.4 total productive days lost by U.S. workers, far more than absence.
- [[Vitality Global Health and Productivity Index]] — a five-market (U.S., UK, Germany, Japan, Singapore) survey of 9,206 workers plus 24 expert interviews, run with RAND Europe, quantifying health-related productivity loss.
- [[AI-Related Concern]] — one of five independent risk domains the Index identifies (alongside mental wellbeing, physical health, work strain, lifestyle); concentrated among younger, less senior, and already-distressed workers.
- [[Treatment vs. Prevention Gap]] — U.S. employers offer broad health/medical benefits (49% have insurance access) but only 18% get employer-organized health screenings, skewing support toward treating illness over early prevention.

## Key Takeaways

- **63.4 productive days lost/year** by the average U.S. worker to poor health — second only to Germany's 69.3 and above the five-market average of 54.8.
- **85% is presenteeism**: 53.9 of 63.4 days lost happen while employees are still working, versus 9.5 days lost to absence.
- **$2.33 trillion** annual U.S. economic cost from poor workforce health — the largest of the five markets, versus $579B (Germany), $326B (UK), $91B (Japan), $26B (Singapore).
- **AI concern costs 27.6 extra days/year**: workers with high AI-related concern (12.9% of respondents) lose over five additional work weeks versus those with no concern, even though 56% say AI/digital tools boost their productivity.
- **Compounding risk**: a worker at high risk across all five domains (mental wellbeing, physical health, AI concern, work strain, lifestyle) loses an estimated 119 days a year — nearly half a working year.
- **Manager care matters**: workers who feel their manager genuinely cares about their wellbeing lose nine fewer productive days a year than those who don't.
- **Screening gap**: only 18% of U.S. workers have access to employer-organized health screenings despite nearly half having employer health insurance.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this research?
**A**: Poor workforce health costs U.S. employers far more through presenteeism than absence, and the Vitality/RAND Europe Index puts the total U.S. economic cost at $2.33 trillion a year — the largest among five markets studied.

**Q2**: What specific data point ties AI to workforce productivity loss in this study?
**A**: Workers with high AI-related concern (12.9% of those surveyed) lose an additional 27.6 productive days a year compared with workers who have no identified AI-related concern.

**Q3**: What should employers do differently based on these findings?
**A**: Shift investment from treatment-focused benefits toward earlier prevention and screening, support managers in showing genuine care for employee wellbeing, and specifically address AI-related anxiety among younger and less senior staff.
