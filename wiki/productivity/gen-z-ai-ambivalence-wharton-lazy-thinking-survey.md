## Summary
A Wharton, Gallup and Walton Family Foundation survey finds Gen Z is deeply ambivalent about AI: most believe it makes people lazier and less smart, yet they lead workplace adoption. Researcher Benjamin Lira Luttges attributes this to a preference for immediate rewards and the better-than-average effect, and advises employers to be intentional rather than ban AI, outsourcing "the crap, not the craft".

## Core Concepts
- **[[Gen Z]] AI Ambivalence**: High adoption alongside persistent worry about lazy thinking.
- **[[Wharton]] / Gallup Survey**: About 2,500 US adults aged 18 to 28, completed October 2025.
- **[[Hyperbolic Discounting]]**: Brains prefer smaller immediate rewards over delayed ones.
- **[[Better-Than-Average Effect]]**: Respondents see themselves as power-users immune to the downsides.
- **[[Critical Thinking Gap]]**: Beasley warns it, not an AI skills gap, threatens organizational pipelines.
- **[[Germane Cognitive Load]]**: Task-intrinsic effort that becomes learning, versus friction.

## Key Takeaways
- **Belief**: 79% say AI makes people lazier; 62% worry it makes people less smart.
- **Adoption**: 66% of ages 18-29 have used an AI chatbot, up from 55% in 2024.
- **Pessimism**: 37% of young adults expect AI's impact on them to be negative.
- **Usage Effect**: Heavier AI users worried less about intelligence and motivation.
- **Entry-Level Risk**: Fewer entry-level jobs could starve middle and upper tiers of experience.
- **Employer Guidance**: Do not ban AI; embrace ambivalence and be intentional.
- **Effort Rule**: "Outsource the crap, not the craft."

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Immediate rewards outweigh delayed costs** — People systematically favor small near-term gains over larger long-term ones, so they use tools whose costs arrive later. · *model:* [[Hyperbolic Discounting]]
→ explains: Adoption, Belief, Usage Effect

**T2. Only task-intrinsic effort produces learning** — Effort germane to the task converts into learning, while friction teaches nothing and can be offloaded safely. · *model:* [[Cognitive Load Theory]]
→ explains: Effort Rule, Employer Guidance

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What paradox does the article describe about Gen Z and AI?
**A**: Gen Z widely believes AI makes people lazier and less smart, yet leads workplace AI adoption and keeps increasing use.

**Q2**: What explanations does Lira Luttges offer for the paradox?
**A**: A bias toward immediate gratification over delayed rewards, plus the better-than-average effect, where respondents assume the harms apply to others, not themselves.

**Q3**: How should workplaces handle AI use according to the article?
**A**: Not ban it, but implement it intentionally, outsourcing friction that teaches nothing while keeping the effort that is intrinsic to the task and builds learning.
