## Summary
Amid economic pressure, geopolitical instability, and AI-driven disruption, employees struggle to prioritize career development on top of demanding workloads. Managers can help by shifting from rigid career ladders toward continuous, embedded learning framed around near-term, transferable skills.

## Core Concepts
- [[Career Development]] reframed as continuous learning rather than a fixed ladder.
- [[Manager Modeling]] — leaders demonstrating curiosity and growth (especially around AI) as a driver of team adaptability.
- [[Organizational Resilience]] built through practical, embedded skill-building.

## Key Takeaways
- **Reframe Growth**: Shift focus from career ladders to continuous, transferable skill-building.
- **Embed, Don't Add**: Development should be built into daily work, not treated as an extra task.
- **Manager Modeling**: Leaders who show curiosity about AI and new skills encourage teams to do the same.
- **Near-Term Focus**: Conversations should emphasize practical, achievable next steps over abstract long-term plans.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim?
**A**: Employees can't prioritize career growth when overwhelmed, so managers must embed development into everyday work rather than treating it as a separate obligation.

**Q2**: What specific shift in approach does the author recommend?
**A**: Moving from rigid career-ladder conversations to discussions about near-term, transferable capabilities and learning embedded in daily tasks.

**Q3**: How should a manager apply this?
**A**: Model curiosity about emerging tools like AI themselves, and frame growth conversations around practical, achievable next steps rather than distant career milestones.
