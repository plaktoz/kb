## Summary

A Software Finder survey of 1,006 workers found that although 71% feel pressure to answer work communications after hours or on vacation, only 8% were promoted and 12% got a raise or bonus because of it. Fear, not payoff, appears to sustain the habit, which is most common among managers and hybrid workers and least common among Gen Z. The average worker loses 1.7 hours a week to it, adding up to over two unpaid workweeks a year.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Always-On Culture]]** — answering calls, joining remote meetings, and replying to email outside work hours or on vacation.
- **[[Software Finder]] survey** — the 1,006-respondent data source behind the promotion, raise, and time-cost figures.
- **Fear as driver** — the article's explanation for why workers stay always-on despite little reward.
- **[[Burnout]] and [[Work-Life Balance]]** — the main outcomes the survey links to off-the-clock accessibility.
- **Demographic splits** — managers, hybrid workers, in-person workers, [[Gen Z]], millennials, and Gen X differ in how often they respond after hours.
- **[[Unpaid Availability]]** — the compounding hours spent checking work communications after hours.

## Key Takeaways

- **Promotion payoff**: Only 8% say high after-hours responsiveness directly contributed to a promotion.
- **Pay payoff**: Just 12% say it led to a raise or bonus.
- **Career impact**: Nearly 70% say availability hasn't meaningfully improved their career.
- **Pressure**: 71% feel pressure to check work communications after hours; 81% of managers do.
- **Work arrangement**: 76% of hybrid workers are always-on versus 71% of fully in-person staff.
- **Generations**: Gen Z 65%, versus 72% for both millennials and Gen X.
- **Time cost**: 1.7 hours weekly, over two full workweeks a year of unpaid availability.
- **Hidden cost**: The habit mainly raises burnout and lowers work-life balance.
