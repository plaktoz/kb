## Summary

IBM shares surged 12.4% after the U.S. government announced a proposed $1 billion grant supporting IBM's new quantum chip foundry venture, Anderon. IBM will match the grant with $1 billion plus IP and assets, establishing a 300mm quantum wafer foundry in Albany, New York. The Department of Commerce unveiled a broader $2 billion quantum computing package, with IBM as the largest recipient.

## Core Concepts

- **[[IBM]] (International Business Machines)**: Stock jumped 12.4% on the quantum foundry news, though shares remained down 14% year-to-date.
- **[[Anderon]]**: IBM's quantum chip foundry spinout, headquartered in Albany, NY; built on existing 300mm fabrication infrastructure at NY CREATES Albany.
- **[[Quantum Computing]]**: Emerging technology sector projected to generate up to $850 billion in economic value by 2040, per IBM.
- **[[Department of Commerce]]**: Announced a $2 billion package to bolster the U.S. quantum computing industry.
- **[[Quantum Chip Foundry]]**: A merchant-scale semiconductor fab dedicated to quantum processors — analogous to TSMC but for quantum hardware.
- **[[National Security and Tech Competition]]**: President Trump framed the investment as critical to maintaining U.S. technological superiority over [[China]].
- Other grant recipients: [[Rigetti Computing]], [[D-Wave Quantum]], [[Infleqtion]].

## Key Takeaways

- IBM stock gained 12.4% — its best single-day move in over a year.
- U.S. government proposed a $1B grant to IBM's new quantum foundry, Anderon.
- IBM matched with $1B investment plus IP, assets, and workforce.
- Anderon will be a 300mm quantum wafer foundry open to external customers.
- Broader DoC package totals $2B for the quantum computing sector.
- IBM has been fabricating Loon and Nighthawk processors at Albany since November 2025.
- Wedbush reiterated Buy with a $225 price target; retail sentiment shifted to "extremely bullish."
- Quantum computing economic value estimated at $850B by 2040.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Picks and Shovels Investing]]**: Rather than betting on which quantum application wins, IBM positions Anderon as the infrastructure layer — supplying quantum chips to all players — capturing value regardless of which use case dominates, much like TSMC's foundry model in classical semiconductors.
- **[[Crowding In Effect]]**: Government capital commitment signals credibility to private investors, reducing perceived risk and unlocking additional private investment — IBM's dollar-for-dollar match illustrates this dynamic directly.
