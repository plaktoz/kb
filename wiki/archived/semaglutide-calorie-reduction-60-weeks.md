## Summary

A 60-week clinical study found that [[semaglutide]] (the active ingredient in [[Ozempic]] and [[Wegovy]]) maintained a 24-30% reduction in caloric intake throughout the full observation period — suggesting the appetite-suppressing effects do not diminish quickly over time. The mechanism operates through [[GLP-1 receptor]] agonism, which modulates hunger hormones to produce natural satiety rather than willpower-based restriction. Long-term outcomes after discontinuation and cardiovascular effects remain under active study.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Semaglutide]]** — GLP-1 receptor agonist; active compound in Ozempic and Wegovy; used for type 2 diabetes and obesity
- **[[GLP-1 Receptor Agonism]]** — mechanism that modulates hunger hormones in the brain, producing sustained appetite reduction
- **[[Caloric Intake Reduction]]** — 24-30% sustained decrease over 60 weeks; occurs without conscious dietary restriction
- **[[Novo Nordisk]] / [[Eli Lilly]]** — dominant market players in the GLP-1 drug class

## Key Takeaways

- **60-week persistence**: calorie reduction sustained across full study period — not a short-term effect
- **24-30% reduction**: significant drop in caloric intake; achieved without deliberate calorie counting
- **Mechanism**: GLP-1 agonism makes satiety feel natural, unlike willpower-dependent diets
- **Side effects remain**: nausea, vomiting, GI discomfort still common
- **Post-discontinuation unknown**: weight maintenance after stopping semaglutide not directly addressed by this study
