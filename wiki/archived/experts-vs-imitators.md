## Summary

Drawing on Munger's observation that it's hard to distinguish real expertise from "someone who just has the patter down," this piece identifies five behavioral markers that separate genuine experts from skilled imitators. The key differentiators operate under pressure: experts engage with challenges and admit limits; imitators deflect, rely on jargon, and can't reframe ideas for new audiences. Popularizers — highly effective communicators — are a distinct third category frequently mistaken for experts.

## Core Concepts

- [[Charlie Munger]]'s "patter" problem: surface fluency masquerading as deep knowledge
- [[Circle of Competence]]: experts know where theirs ends; imitators can't recognize when they've exceeded theirs
- [[First Principles Thinking]]: the test that separates depth from surface knowledge under pressure
- [[Popularizers]] as a third category: excellent communicators who aren't necessarily authorities

## Key Takeaways

- **Five markers of imitators**: Shallow depth, jargon reliance, defensive to questions, hide failures, unaware of limits
- **The pressure test**: Ask for detail or reframing — imitators fail; experts engage
- **Failure transparency**: Experts openly discuss mistakes; imitators protect their projected image
- **Jargon as camouflage**: Inability to reframe for different audiences reveals surface-level understanding
- **Popularizer ≠ expert**: "The person with real expertise is often not the person who made the subject popular"
