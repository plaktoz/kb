## Summary

Research published in *Biology Methods & Protocols* (August 2026) found that patients remaining on the lowest starting doses of GLP-1 medications — sometimes called "microdosing" — still achieved meaningful weight loss over one year. Low-dose tirzepatide users lost approximately 5.5% of body weight and low-dose semaglutide users lost ~2.2%, both well below the ~12% seen at higher semaglutide doses. The authors suggest this approach may suit patients prioritizing cardiometabolic health with fewer side effects over maximal weight loss.

## Core Concepts

- [[GLP-1 Medications]] — glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists including [[Semaglutide]] (Wegovy) and [[Tirzepatide]] (Zepbound/Mounjaro)
- **Microdosing** — remaining on lowest starting doses (0.25 mg semaglutide; 2.5 mg tirzepatide) for six or more months rather than titrating up
- [[Cardiometabolic Health]] — the study framed low-dose GLP-1 use as a tool for modest metabolic improvement, not just weight loss
- [[Electronic Health Records]] — the study used EHR data from 814 semaglutide and 1,016 tirzepatide low-dose patients
- Side effect profile — sustained low-dose semaglutide showed notably lower 24-month constipation (23.3% vs. 36.3%) and nausea (39.7% vs. 50.1%) rates versus higher-dose users

## Key Takeaways

- **Low-dose tirzepatide**: ~5.5% body weight reduction at one year
- **Low-dose semaglutide**: ~2.2% body weight reduction at one year
- **High-dose semaglutide (comparison)**: ~12% weight loss — roughly 3–6x more effective
- **Fewer side effects**: Constipation and nausea significantly lower at sustained low doses
- **Not FDA-sanctioned**: Dosing outside approved titration schedules is off-label
- **Clinical caveat**: Many patients eventually plateau and require dose escalation to hit their goals
- **Study limitation**: This was not a study of intentional microdosing; some patients may simply have tolerated low doses before escalating

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Dose-Response Relationship]]**: The data illustrate that GLP-1 efficacy scales with dose, but meaningful benefit can exist well below the therapeutic ceiling — a reminder that "more is better" is not universal in pharmacology.
- **[[Risk-Benefit Tradeoff]]**: For patients whose goal is modest cardiometabolic improvement rather than maximum weight loss, accepting lower efficacy in exchange for substantially reduced side effects is a rational optimization of the risk-benefit curve.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core finding about low-dose GLP-1 use and weight loss?
**A**: Patients staying on the lowest starting doses of semaglutide or tirzepatide for six or more months still lost meaningful weight — about 2.2% and 5.5% of body weight respectively — suggesting microdosing retains some efficacy.

**Q2**: How do side effect rates compare between low-dose and high-dose semaglutide users?
**A**: At 24 months, sustained low-dose users had notably lower rates of constipation (23.3% vs. 36.3%) and nausea (39.7% vs. 50.1%) compared to higher-dose counterparts.

**Q3**: Who might be the ideal candidate for a sustained low-dose GLP-1 approach?
**A**: Patients whose primary goal is modest cardiometabolic improvement (2–7% weight reduction) with fewer gastrointestinal side effects, rather than maximum weight loss, are the profile the researchers describe as a target for this strategy.
