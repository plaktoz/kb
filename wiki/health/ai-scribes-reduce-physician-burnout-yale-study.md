## Summary

A Yale-led, multi-site study of 263 physicians and advanced practice practitioners across six U.S. health systems found that 30 days of using an [[Ambient AI Scribe]] cut physician burnout from 51.9% to 38.8%. The reduction in documentation burden also freed clinicians to give patients more undivided attention during visits.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Ambient AI Scribe]]**: AI system that listens during patient visits and automates clinical documentation, reducing the administrative burden tied to electronic health record entry.
- **[[Physician Burnout]]**: Widespread clinician dissatisfaction with [[Electronic Health Record]] documentation is a leading driver of healthcare workforce attrition; this study is notable because most burnout interventions fail to move the needle significantly.
- **[[Lee H. Schwamm]]**: Senior author, associate dean of digital transformation at Yale School of Medicine and chief digital health officer at Yale New Haven Health, framed the result as freeing providers to focus attention on patients.
- **Study design**: Multi-site trial across six U.S. health systems using the Abridge ambient AI tool, published in *JAMA Network Open*; Abridge funded data collection but had no role in study design or analysis.

## Key Takeaways

- **Burnout drop**: Physician burnout fell from 51.9% to 38.8% after 30 days of AI scribe use.
- **Attention shift**: Less after-hours documentation freed clinicians to focus more on patients.
- **Cognitive load**: Participants reported lower cognitive load alongside the burnout reduction.
- **Rare result**: Significant burnout score change is uncommon since most burnout interventions fail.
- **Independent evaluation**: Vendor (Abridge) had no role in study design, analysis, or publication decisions.
