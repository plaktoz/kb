# FDA Clears First Single-Biomarker Blood Test for Alzheimer's

## Summary

On August 24, 2026, the FDA cleared the Elecsys® Phospho-Tau (217P) Plasma (pTau217) test, developed by [[Roche]] and [[Eli Lilly]], as the first single-biomarker blood test able to both rule in and rule out amyloid pathology from one blood draw. It is intended for symptomatic adults 55+ and must be physician-ordered, not self-administered. Experts frame it as a major step toward accessible [[Alzheimer's Disease]] diagnosis, though it is not a stand-alone diagnostic tool.

## Core Concepts

- **[[pTau217]]**: Phosphorylated tau 217, a blood biomarker that rises early in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and correlates closely with brain amyloid accumulation.
- **[[Amyloid Pathology]]**: The buildup of amyloid plaques in the brain, historically confirmed via expensive, invasive methods like [[PET Scans]] and spinal taps.
- **[[Blood-Based Biomarker Testing]]**: A growing category of FDA-cleared diagnostics (now four tests) replacing or supplementing costly imaging and lumbar puncture methods.
- **[[Roche]]** and **[[Eli Lilly]]**: Co-developers of the new Elecsys pTau217 assay, built to run on Roche's existing lab infrastructure.
- **[[Rule-In Rule-Out Diagnosis]]**: The test's distinguishing feature — a single assay that both confirms and excludes amyloid pathology, unlike prior tests limited to one direction.

## Key Takeaways

- **First of its kind**: Single biomarker test supports both rule-in and rule-out from one blood draw.
- **Cleared population**: Symptomatic adults aged 55+, not for general screening.
- **Physician-only**: Must be ordered by a doctor; not available for self-ordering.
- **Diagnosis gap**: About 75% of dementia cases remain undiagnosed today.
- **Rapid progress**: Four FDA-cleared Alzheimer's blood tests now exist, versus zero just over a year ago.
- **Not stand-alone**: Requires clinical context; doesn't replace comprehensive assessment.
- **Open question**: No clear guidance yet for patients with intermediate results.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central significance of the new pTau217 blood test?
**A**: It is the first FDA-cleared single-biomarker blood test that can both rule in and rule out amyloid pathology from a single blood draw, using equipment already widespread in labs.

**Q2**: How does the test work biologically, and how are results reported?
**A**: It measures phosphorylated tau 217 in blood, which rises early in Alzheimer's and correlates with brain amyloid; results are categorized as positive, intermediate, or negative using validated cutoffs across care settings.

**Q3**: What are the practical implications and limitations of this test for patients?
**A**: It could help close the diagnosis gap (75% of dementia cases undiagnosed) by offering an accessible alternative to PET scans and spinal taps, but it is physician-ordered only, not for asymptomatic screening, and must be interpreted alongside full clinical evaluation.
