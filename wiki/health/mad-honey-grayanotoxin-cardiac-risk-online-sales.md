## Summary

"Mad honey," produced by Himalayan cliff bees (*Apis laboriosa*) from rhododendron nectar, contains [[Grayanotoxin]] — a compound capable of causing life-threatening cardiac events even in small doses. Global online marketplaces now sell it as a "psychoactive superfood," spreading consumption beyond its traditional Himalayan origins without adequate safety warnings. The trend is also accelerating the decline of the cliff bee population, threatening Himalayan pollination ecosystems.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Grayanotoxin]]**: The active toxin in mad honey, derived from [[Rhododendron]] nectar collected by [[Apis laboriosa]] (Himalayan cliff bees). Disrupts sodium channels, causing cardiovascular and neurological effects.
- **[[Mad Honey]] (bhir mauri ko maha)**: Traditional Himalayan substance now commodified globally; sold online for hundreds of dollars per kilogram and marketed as a wellness product.
- **[[Grayanotoxin Poisoning]]**: A documented medical condition with distinct symptom onset (30–60 minutes), treated with atropine and IV fluids; recovery typically within two days.
- **[[Apis laboriosa]]**: The world's largest honey bee, native to Himalayan cliffs; its population is declining due to overharvesting, habitat loss, and climate change.
- **Online Health Misinformation**: The mechanism by which dangerous substances get reframed as "superfoods" and reach global consumers who lack clinical context.

## Key Takeaways

- **Cardiac Risk**: Even a few tablespoons can drop heart rate to life-threatening levels.
- **Symptom Window**: Dizziness, nausea, and low blood pressure appear within 30–60 minutes of ingestion.
- **Treatment**: Atropine and IV fluids; most patients recover within two days.
- **Demographics**: ~72% of documented poisoning cases involve middle-aged men using it as a home remedy.
- **Stated Uses**: Most deliberate consumption targets hypertension relief or sexual function improvement.
- **Seasonal Pattern**: Poisonings peak in spring (rhododendron bloom) and secondarily in autumn.
- **Global Spread**: Confirmed cases now in South Korea, France, and Qatar — regions where doctors may not recognize the poisoning.
- **Conservation Threat**: Overharvesting compounds habitat loss and climate pressure on [[Apis laboriosa]] populations.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Information Asymmetry]]**: Online sellers market grayanotoxin-laden honey as a superfood while omitting cardiac risks — buyers cannot assess danger they don't know exists, and doctors in non-endemic regions lack pattern recognition to catch poisonings quickly.
- **[[Cobra Effect]]**: Commercializing mad honey to meet global demand directly accelerates the decline of the cliff bee population that produces it, undermining the very supply chain driving the trade.
