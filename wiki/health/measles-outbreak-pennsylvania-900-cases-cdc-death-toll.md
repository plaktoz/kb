## Summary
Pennsylvania's measles outbreak has surpassed 900 cases, the largest and fastest-growing in the nation, with 176 hospitalizations and four related deaths — the state's first measles deaths in 35 years. The [[CDC]] separately confirms only two 2026 measles deaths nationally, and it is unclear which deaths that count includes, fueling a public dispute between Pennsylvania officials and [[CDC]]/HHS leadership.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Measles Outbreak]]**: A rapidly growing outbreak centered in Pennsylvania, now over 900 confirmed cases with 114 new diagnoses in a single week.
- **[[Vaccination Rate]]**: Less than 1% of Pennsylvania's measles patients were fully vaccinated, underscoring the outbreak's link to low immunization coverage.
- **[[CDC]] reporting discrepancy**: The federal death count (two) diverges from the state's reported death toll (four), with ambiguity over which cases are recognized.
- **[[Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]]**: HHS Secretary at the center of a public back-and-forth with Pennsylvania state leaders over the CDC's death-count recognition.

## Key Takeaways
- **Scale**: 903 measles cases in Pennsylvania, 176 hospitalized, 4 deaths reported by the state.
- **Historic**: First measles deaths in Pennsylvania in 35 years.
- **Vaccination gap**: Under 1% of patients were fully vaccinated.
- **Age skew**: 31% of case-patients are children.
- **Federal-state mismatch**: CDC lists only 2 deaths nationally for 2026, versus 4 reported by Pennsylvania alone.
