## Summary
Pennsylvania's measles outbreak has surpassed 900 cases, the largest and fastest-growing in the nation, with 176 hospitalizations and four related deaths — the state's first measles deaths in 35 years. The [[CDC]] separately confirms only two 2026 measles deaths nationally, and it is unclear which deaths that count includes, fueling a public dispute between Pennsylvania officials and [[CDC]]/HHS leadership. A follow-up Annenberg Public Policy Center survey finds most Americans are confused about where [[Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] actually stands on the measles vaccine.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Measles Outbreak]]**: A rapidly growing outbreak centered in Pennsylvania, now over 900 confirmed cases with 114 new diagnoses in a single week.
- **[[Vaccination Rate]]**: Less than 1% of Pennsylvania's measles patients were fully vaccinated, underscoring the outbreak's link to low immunization coverage.
- **[[CDC]] reporting discrepancy**: The federal death count (two) diverges from the state's reported death toll (four), with ambiguity over which cases are recognized; The Washington Post reported on the unresolved gap.
- **[[Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]]**: HHS Secretary at the center of a public back-and-forth with Pennsylvania state leaders over the CDC's death-count recognition, and whose public messaging on the measles vaccine has left most Americans unsure of his actual stance.

## Key Takeaways
- **Scale**: 903 measles cases in Pennsylvania, 176 hospitalized, 4 deaths reported by the state.
- **Historic**: First measles deaths in Pennsylvania in 35 years.
- **Vaccination gap**: Under 1% of patients were fully vaccinated.
- **Age skew**: 31% of case-patients are children.
- **Federal-state mismatch**: CDC lists only 2 deaths nationally for 2026, versus 4 reported by Pennsylvania alone.
- **Public confusion**: Annenberg survey finds only 31% of adults believe Kennedy recommends measles vaccination for children, 29% believe he doesn't, and 40% are unsure.

## Update (2026-10-02, STAT — fifth death and the outbreak's unknowable true size)

STAT's Helen Branswell reports a fifth measles death in Pennsylvania. She argues that the full scope of the outbreak, in the state and nationally, "isn't known or even knowable":

- **Updated count**: 943 confirmed cases in Pennsylvania as of Wednesday, up from the 903 logged above and about a quarter of the 2026 national total.
- **Fifth death**: Pennsylvania's toll rose to five, up from the four state-reported deaths above.
- **Confirmed-only reporting**: Official tallies count only confirmed cases. About 20% of infected people need hospital care and some others see a doctor, but some never come to the attention of public health.
- **[[Amish]] communities**: Several Pennsylvania counties have large Amish populations that interact little with the wider world. Low measles vaccination rates, large families, and a tendency to seek care only when illness becomes severe make undercounting especially likely there. The same was true of last year's outbreak in Mennonite colonies in Texas.
- **Modeling still to come**: Researchers are expected to produce modeling estimates of the outbreak's true size eventually. For now, confirmed counts show only "a portion of the iceberg."
- **[[Paul Offit]]** (director, Vaccine Education Center, Children's Hospital of Philadelphia): "It is much worse than is being claimed... I think we don't know how bad."
