## Summary

A study published in *Neurology* on Sept. 8, 2026 found that menopausal women using estrogen-only [[Hormone Replacement Therapy]] may have reduced risk of dementia or Alzheimer's-related brain changes. Across two databases totaling over 20,000 women aged 50+, estrogen-only users were 35% less likely to show Alzheimer's-related brain changes at autopsy and had 39% lower odds of a dementia diagnosis with better memory test performance while living. The findings are observational, reassuring but not a basis to start hormone therapy for dementia prevention.

## Core Concepts

- [[Estrogen]] — the sole hormone used in this therapy; theorized to protect neuronal and cognitive function
- [[Hormone Replacement Therapy]] — here specifically the estrogen-only form, prescribed only to women without a uterus
- [[Surgical Menopause]] — estrogen-only therapy is used by women lacking a uterus, often due to hysterectomy
- [[Alzheimer's Disease]] — outcome measured via post-mortem amyloid protein markers and living cognitive/imaging assessment
- [[Observational Study Limitations]] — study design cannot establish causation and excluded combination therapy and vaginal estrogen users

## Key Takeaways

- **Autopsy cohort**: 2,959 women; estrogen-only users were 35% less likely to show Alzheimer's-related brain changes.
- **Living cohort**: 2,058 women; estrogen-only users had 39% lower odds of dementia diagnosis.
- **Memory performance**: Living estrogen-only users also scored better on memory tests.
- **Who qualifies**: Estrogen-only therapy is advised only for women without a uterus.
- **Excluded groups**: Study didn't cover combination estrogen-progesterone or vaginal estrogen users.
- **Missing data**: No information on treatment timing or duration was analyzed.
- **Not a green light**: Findings are reassuring but don't justify starting HRT solely for dementia prevention.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the Neurology study on estrogen-only hormone therapy?
**A**: Menopausal women using estrogen-only hormone therapy showed reduced markers of Alzheimer's-related brain changes and lower odds of a dementia diagnosis compared to non-users.

**Q2**: What specific data points support this finding across the two study databases?
**A**: In a 2,959-woman autopsy database, estrogen-only users were 35% less likely to show Alzheimer's-related brain changes; in a 2,058-woman living cohort, users had 39% lower odds of dementia diagnosis and better memory test scores.

**Q3**: What are the limitations, and what should women take away from this study?
**A**: The study is observational (no causation proof), excluded combination therapy and vaginal estrogen users, and lacked timing/duration data — so the findings are reassuring but not sufficient grounds to start hormone therapy specifically for dementia prevention.
