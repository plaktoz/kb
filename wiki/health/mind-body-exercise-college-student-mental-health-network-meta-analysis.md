## Summary

A [[Network Meta-Analysis]] of 24 RCTs compared mindfulness-based exercise, [[Tai Chi]], [[Baduanjin]], [[Yoga]], and a combined intervention on mental health in college students. Baduanjin appeared to relieve anxiety and depression, while mindfulness-based exercise helped reduce stress. No intervention significantly improved well-being or sleep quality, though the authors call these findings inconclusive. Only the abstract was available when scraped.

## Core Concepts

- [[Mind-Body Exercise]] as a non-pharmacological intervention for [[College Student Mental Health]].
- [[Network Meta-Analysis]] following PRISMA-NMA, with [[Risk of Bias]] assessed by RoB 2.
- Interventions: [[Mindfulness-Based Exercise]], [[Tai Chi]], [[Baduanjin]], [[Yoga]].
- Outcomes: anxiety, depression, stress, mental well-being, and sleep quality.
- Authors from Chengdu Sport University: [[Yang Li]], [[Lingli Jia]], [[Guohui Liu]].

## Key Takeaways

- **Evidence Base**: 24 RCTs from PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science.
- **Anxiety**: All five interventions helped; combined was largest (SMD -0.99), mindfulness-based -0.70.
- **Depression**: Only Baduanjin (SMD -0.75) and mindfulness-based exercise (-0.55) were superior.
- **Stress**: Mindfulness-based exercise (-0.67) beat yoga (-0.32).
- **Null Results**: No intervention significantly improved well-being or sleep quality.
- **Caution**: Limited evidence means null findings do not rule out real effects.
- **Conclusion**: Mind-body exercises have differential effects by outcome.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Non-significance is not proof of no effect** — A lack of statistically significant benefit under limited evidence does not rule out a genuine effect. · *model:* [[Absence of Evidence Is Not Evidence of Absence]]
→ explains: Null Results, Caution

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of this network meta-analysis?
**A**: Mind-body exercises have differential effects: Baduanjin may relieve anxiety and depression, while mindfulness-based exercise is beneficial for reducing stress.

**Q2**: What data supports the depression and stress findings?
**A**: For depression, Baduanjin (SMD -0.75) and mindfulness-based exercise (SMD -0.55) were superior. For stress, mindfulness-based exercise (SMD -0.67) outperformed yoga (SMD -0.32).

**Q3**: How should the null results for well-being and sleep be interpreted?
**A**: No intervention significantly beat controls, but the authors say this does not rule out genuine effects and remains inconclusive given limited evidence.
