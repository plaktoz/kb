## Summary
A University of Hong Kong study of more than 120,000 adults aged 60+ with osteoporosis or fragility fractures found that nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates were linked to a 16% lower risk of Alzheimer's and related dementias versus no treatment, and 24% lower versus other osteoporosis drugs. The study is observational, so it does not show the drugs prevent dementia, but it supports further research into repurposing them.

## Core Concepts
- [[Nitrogen-Containing Bisphosphonates]]: osteoporosis drugs, including alendronate and zoledronate, that slow bone breakdown.
- [[Dementia]]: affects 55 million people worldwide, projected to reach 139 million by 2050.
- [[Alzheimer's Disease]]: the main dementia type, where new treatments are costly and of limited suitability.
- [[Osteoporosis]]: independent risk factor for dementia, sharing risk factors such as age and low activity.
- [[Drug Repurposing]]: finding added benefits of existing medications.
- [[Observational Study]]: the design used, based on Hong Kong electronic medical records from 2005 to 2020.
- [[Cheung Ching-lung]]: HKUMed professor who led the research.

## Key Takeaways
- **Versus No Drug**: NBP users had 16% lower dementia risk than untreated patients.
- **Versus Other Drugs**: Dementia risk was 24% lower than with other osteoporosis medications.
- **Subgroups**: Associations looked strongest in women and hip fracture patients.
- **Number Needed**: Treating 48 patients over five years could prevent one dementia case.
- **Causation Caveat**: Observational design cannot prove the drugs prevent dementia.
- **Practical Appeal**: NBPs are already widely available with an established safety history.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the HKUMed study find about osteoporosis drugs and dementia?
**A**: Patients taking nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates had 16% lower risk of Alzheimer's and related dementias than untreated patients, and 24% lower than users of other osteoporosis drugs.

**Q2**: What data supports the finding?
**A**: Hong Kong electronic medical records from 2005 to 2020 covering over 120,000 patients aged 60+ with osteoporosis or fragility fractures. The researchers estimated treating 48 patients over five years could prevent one dementia case.

**Q3**: Why is the result not yet a reason to prescribe these drugs for dementia prevention?
**A**: It is an observational study, so it cannot show the drugs directly prevent dementia. It does provide an empirical basis for further research on whether these affordable, widely available drugs can protect the brain.
