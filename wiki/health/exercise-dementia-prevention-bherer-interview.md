## Summary
Neuropsychologist Louis Bherer argues that lifestyle, not just the search for a cure molecule, is the neglected lever against dementia, with nearly half of cases (45%) preventable through healthy habits. His Montréal lab finds that combining physical training with cognitive exercises produces clinically significant gains in attention, alertness, and memory in adults aged 50 to 95. The benefits fade within a month or so of stopping, so activities must fit daily routines.

## Core Concepts
- [[Louis Bherer]]: neuropsychologist and laboratory director at the Montreal University Institute of Geriatrics, studying exercise and cognition at the Montréal Heart Institute.
- [[Healthspan-Lifespan Gap]]: Statistics Canada reports healthy life expectancy of 66.9 years in 2023, down 3.5 years from the 70.4-year peak.
- [[Dementia Prevention]]: 14 modifiable risk factors identified, including inactivity, poor sleep, hearing loss, and psychological health.
- [[Combined Physical and Cognitive Training]]: phone memory games plus stationary bikes, treadmills, and strength work.
- [[Myokines and Exerkines]]: molecules released by working muscle that affect metabolism, inflammation, bone health, mood, and neuroplasticity.
- [[Heart-Brain Connection]]: cardiovascular and brain health are interrelated, so what is good for the heart is good for the brain.
- [[Neuroplasticity]] and the [[Hippocampus]]: imaging measures improved blood flow and structure changes.

## Key Takeaways
- **Prevention Potential**: About 45% of dementia cases could be prevented through lifestyle.
- **Widening Gap**: Rising lifespan means more years in poor health without lifestyle change.
- **Never Too Late**: Starting in later life still helps; hearing loss and depression raise risk.
- **Combined Training**: Pairing physical and cognitive exercise works best, per Bherer's lab.
- **Timeline of Gains**: Alertness improves in weeks; attention after six months; memory takes longer.
- **Dosage**: Participants train three times a week for three months to a year.
- **Fast Decay**: Training benefits disappear within a month to six weeks of stopping.
- **Practical Choices**: Running or brisk walking to a light sweat, plus strength, posture, balance work like yoga.
- **Independence**: Mobility and balance exercises reduce fear of falling and sustain independence.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Bherer's central claim about dementia?
**A**: Lifestyle habits are a neglected prevention lever; nearly half of dementia cases (45%) could be prevented by addressing 14 modifiable risk factors, rather than waiting for a cure molecule.

**Q2**: What happens when people stop their training programs, and what do the lab's studies show on timing of benefits?
**A**: Benefits disappear within a month to a month and a half, faster in older people. While training, alertness improves within weeks, attention after about six months, and memory more slowly.

**Q3**: How should an older adult apply this advice?
**A**: Choose easy-to-sustain activities like running or brisk walking to a light sweat, add strength, posture, and balance work such as yoga, and pair them with cognitive exercises.
