## Summary

A mathematical modeling study published in *npj Aging* estimated that if only somatic mutations drove aging (all other hallmarks eliminated), human median lifespan could reach 146–194 years — roughly double today's ~79 years, with a maximum range of 210–557 years. If all aging hallmarks were eliminated entirely, median lifespan could theoretically reach ~1,759 years. The study identifies heart muscle cells and neurons as the critical bottlenecks since they don't regenerate, while liver cells showed remarkable theoretical resilience.

## Core Concepts

- [[Somatic Mutations]] as the isolated aging driver in this theoretical model
- [[Lifespan Bottlenecks]]: [[Cardiomyocytes]] and [[Neurons]] don't regenerate after mutation-driven damage
- [[Healthspan vs. Lifespan]]: the study reframes the question as maximum disease-free years, not maximum years
- [[Aging Hallmarks]]: multiple interacting mechanisms — model excludes [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] and [[Chronic Inflammation]]
- Aging confirmed as multifactorial: "no single hallmark of aging is likely to account for normal human aging on its own"

## Key Takeaways

- **Somatic-only lifespan**: median 146–194 years; maximum 210–557 years
- **Theoretical maximum**: ~1,759 years if all aging hallmarks eliminated
- **Bottleneck organs**: Heart muscle cells and neurons — non-regenerating, accumulate damage fatally
- **Resilient organ**: Liver cells theoretically viable for 100,000+ years
- **Model limits**: Excludes mitochondrial dysfunction, chronic inflammation, and future mutation-reducing therapies
- **Practical levers**: [[VO2 Max]], sleep, diet, blood pressure, social connection — over supplements or peptides
- **Practical advice**: "It's what you eat, drink, think, and do"

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of this lifespan modeling study?
**A**: If all reversible aging hallmarks were eliminated except somatic mutations, human median lifespan could reach 146–194 years, with a maximum range of 210–557 years.

**Q2**: Why are neurons and heart muscle cells identified as longevity bottlenecks?
**A**: Unlike regenerative organs such as the liver, neurons and cardiomyocytes rarely regenerate, so DNA copying errors accumulate in them irreversibly over time.

**Q3**: What does the study imply for practical longevity today?
**A**: The study confirms aging is multifactorial, so practical gains come from lifestyle factors — VO2 max, sleep, diet, blood pressure, and social connection — not single-target supplements.
