## Summary

A mathematical modeling study published in NPJ Aging estimated that if only somatic mutations drove aging (all other hallmarks eliminated), human median lifespan could reach 146–194 years — roughly double today's ~79 years. If all aging hallmarks were eliminated entirely, median lifespan could theoretically reach ~1,759 years. The study identifies heart muscle cells and neurons as the critical bottlenecks since they don't regenerate, while liver cells showed remarkable theoretical resilience. Experts emphasize the model is theoretical and the practical longevity levers remain unchanged.

## Core Concepts

- [[Somatic Mutations]] as the isolated aging driver in this theoretical model
- [[Lifespan Bottlenecks]]: [[Cardiomyocytes]] and [[Neurons]] don't regenerate after mutation-driven damage
- [[Healthspan vs. Lifespan]]: the study reframes the question as maximum disease-free years, not maximum years
- [[Aging Hallmarks]]: multiple interacting mechanisms that the model simplifies to isolate somatic mutations

## Key Takeaways

- **Somatic-only lifespan**: 146–194 years if only somatic mutations remain
- **Theoretical maximum**: ~1,759 years if all aging hallmarks eliminated
- **Bottleneck organs**: Heart muscle cells and neurons — non-regenerating, accumulate damage fatally
- **Resilient organ**: Liver cells theoretically viable for 100,000+ years
- **Practical advice unchanged**: Exercise, diet, sleep, no smoking, social ties → 10–20 additional healthy years
- **Study limit**: Purely theoretical, simplified assumptions, doesn't model hallmark interactions
