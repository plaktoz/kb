## Summary
Emerging clinical research directly correlates high consumption of [[Ultra-Processed Foods]] with elevated risks of neurological and cerebrovascular decline, specifically [[Dementia]] and [[Stroke]]. Diets rich in industrial additives, refined fats, and sugars induce systemic vascular inflammation and compromise neural integrity over long time horizons. Modifying dietary patterns by substituting unprocessed whole foods presents a critical preventative strategy against cognitive deterioration.

## Core Concepts
- [[Ultra-Processed Foods]] (UPFs): Heavily formulated industrial foods containing artificial additives, emulsifiers, and high levels of refined sugar and unhealthy fats.
- [[Dementia]]: Progressive cognitive impairment impacting memory, judgment, and daily functioning linked to vascular and metabolic degradation.
- [[Stroke]]: Cerebrovascular compromise caused by disrupted blood supply to the brain, exacerbated by dietary inflammation.
- [[Neuroinflammation]]: Chronic micro-inflammatory cascades within neural pathways triggered by poor nutritional profiles.
- [[Preventative Nutrition]]: Dietary interventions aimed at reducing disease risk through whole, unrefined foods.

## Key Takeaways
- **Elevated Neurological Risk**: High UPF consumption directly correlates with higher rates of dementia and stroke.
- **Inflammatory Mechanisms**: Industrial additives and refined ingredients trigger persistent vascular and neural inflammation.
- **Dose-Response Effect**: Incremental increases in daily UPF share compound long-term neurovascular risk.
- **Preventative Substitution**: Replacing ultra-processed items with whole foods demonstrably mitigates cognitive decline.
