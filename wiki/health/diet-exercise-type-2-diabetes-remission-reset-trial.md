## Summary

The RESET for Remission randomized trial found that 54% of adults aged 18-45 with early-onset type 2 diabetes and obesity reached remission after 24 weeks on a low-calorie diet plus supervised exercise, versus about 4% with usual care. The program also produced greater fat loss while preserving muscle. Researchers caution that longer studies are needed to show whether remission and weight loss last outside a monitored trial.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Early-Onset Type 2 Diabetes]]**: Diagnosis under age 45; faster glycemic decline, earlier complications, shorter life expectancy.
- **[[Type 2 Diabetes Remission]]**: HbA1c below 6.5% with no glucose-lowering medication for at least 12 weeks; not necessarily a cure.
- **[[Low-Energy Diet]]**: 800-900 calories daily, using meal replacements (Optifast, ProtiDiet) then one conventional meal.
- **[[Resistance Training]]** and **[[Aerobic Exercise]]**: Supervised twice weekly plus one independent session.
- **[[Muscle Preservation]]**: Fat-free mass protected during rapid weight loss; relevant to sarcopenia risk.
- **[[RESET for Remission Trial]]**: Led by [[Kaberi Dasgupta]] and [[Thomas Yates]]; published in The Lancet Regional Health - Americas.
- **[[HbA1c]]**: Blood test reflecting average blood sugar over roughly three months.

## Key Takeaways

- **Remission Rate**: 27 of 50 (54%) intervention participants versus 2 of 46 (4%) controls.
- **Effect Size**: Adjusted analysis shows 21 times higher likelihood of remission.
- **Weight Loss**: Intervention lost 8.4 kg on average versus 1.2 kg in controls.
- **Fat Versus Muscle**: 6.6 kg of the extra loss was fat; muscle was preserved.
- **Side Effects**: No severe events; mild effects in 43 of 50 (constipation, headache, fatigue).
- **Trial Limits**: 96 participants, open-label, 24 weeks; durability is untested.
- **Pregnancy Relevance**: Authors suggest value for people planning pregnancy.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the RESET for Remission trial find?
**A**: Combining a low-calorie diet with supervised exercise put 54% of young adults with early-onset type 2 diabetes into remission at 24 weeks, versus about 4% with usual care.

**Q2**: How was remission defined and what was the intervention schedule?
**A**: Remission meant HbA1c below 6.5% with no glucose-lowering medication for at least 12 weeks. The intervention was 800-900 calories daily plus supervised exercise twice weekly for weeks 1-12, then weight-maintenance eating with independent exercise through week 24.

**Q3**: What caveat limits how the result should be applied?
**A**: Remission is not necessarily a cure, and the trial was short and monitored. Longer studies are needed to see whether weight loss, muscle mass, and remission persist in everyday life.
