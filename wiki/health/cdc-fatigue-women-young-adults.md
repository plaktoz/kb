## Summary

A CDC National Center for Health Statistics report found that 71.5% of U.S. adults experience fatigue on at least some days, with 16.6% reporting it frequently. Women and adults aged 18–34 are disproportionately affected, with frequent fatigue rates of 20% and 19.5% respectively. Experts link higher rates to caregiving burdens, life transitions, financial pressures, and hormonal factors such as menopause.

## Core Concepts

- [[Chronic Fatigue]] as a widespread public health concern affecting over 70% of U.S. adults
- [[CDC]] / [[NCHS]] surveillance data tracking fatigue prevalence across demographics
- [[Chronic Stress]] and its disruption of [[Cortisol]] and adrenaline as a driver of fatigue
- [[Menopause]] as a significant fatigue factor — over 85% of postmenopausal women report exhaustion
- Demographic disparities: gender, age, race/ethnicity, geography (rural vs. urban, region)

## Key Takeaways

- **Prevalence**: 71.5% of U.S. adults report fatigue on at least some days.
- **Frequent fatigue**: 16.6% experience it most or every day.
- **Women**: 20% report frequent fatigue, higher than the national average.
- **Young adults (18–34)**: 19.5% report frequent fatigue.
- **American Indian/Alaska Native**: 25.5% — highest among racial/ethnic groups.
- **Rural vs. urban**: 20.5% rural vs. 14.5% urban frequent fatigue rates.
- **Comorbidities**: 69.6% of frequent fatigue sufferers have anxiety symptoms; 46.1% cognitive difficulty; 45% depression.
- **Work impact**: 36% of frequent fatigue sufferers report work limitations.
- **Hormonal link**: Chronic stress disrupts cortisol/adrenaline; menopause is a major contributor.
- **Clinical threshold**: Consult a doctor if fatigue persists beyond a few weeks or disrupts daily functioning.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Allostatic Load]]**: Cumulative physiological wear from caregiving, financial stress, and life transitions — the very pressures disproportionately borne by women and young adults — depletes the body's stress-response systems, explaining elevated fatigue rates in these groups.
