## Summary

The CDC released clinical guidance and opened ordering for COVID-19 vaccines through the [[Vaccines for Children]] (VFC) program, nearly a month after the FDA approved the 2026-2027 vaccines, but actual availability will vary widely by state. This marks the second consecutive year of delayed VFC access, driven partly by an [[Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices]] that hasn't met since December 2025 after a federal judge found its members unlawfully appointed. A pharmacy expert warns that cost barriers, not just supply delays, keep low-income and uninsured families from vaccinating their children.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Vaccines for Children]] program**: Federal program giving free vaccines to uninsured, low-income, or American Indian/Alaska Native children.
- **[[CDC]]**: Opened VFC ordering and issued clinical guidance that differs in places from [[American Academy of Pediatrics]] and FDA labeling.
- **[[Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices]]**: Normally sets vaccination recommendations but hasn't met since December 2025 after a court found its members unlawfully appointed.
- **[[American Academy of Pediatrics]]**: Recommends COVID-19 vaccination for children 6-23 months and high-risk children 2-18; says delays are directly harming access.
- **Flu Vaccine Delays**: VFC flu vaccine deliveries are also lagging or in short supply in some states, for unclear reasons.
- **Cost as a Barrier**: Pharmacy professor [[Gretchen Garofoli]] warns families skip vaccination once costs shift out-of-pocket.

## Key Takeaways

- **Ordering Opens Late**: CDC opened VFC COVID-19 ordering nearly a month after FDA approval.
- **State Variability**: Actual availability depends on each state's implementation speed and supply.
- **Second Year Running**: This is the second consecutive year of delayed VFC vaccine access.
- **ACIP Vacuum**: Committee hasn't met since December 2025 amid unlawful-appointment ruling.
- **High Hospitalization Risk**: Infants 6-11 months had hospitalization rates on par with adults 65-74.
- **Underlying Conditions**: 42% of hospitalized children 6-23 months had an underlying condition.
- **Flu Vaccines Also Delayed**: Some VFC clinics received flu doses two weeks late, in small quantities.
- **Cost Barrier Effect**: Out-of-pocket costs measurably reduce vaccination uptake, per research cited.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Last-Mile Problem]]**: Federal approval and open ordering don't equal access — the real bottleneck is distribution through state systems down to individual clinics and families, the classic last-mile gap in public health logistics.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: The CDC opened ordering for COVID-19 vaccines in the Vaccines for Children program nearly a month after FDA approval, but real-world availability will vary widely by state due to implementation delays and limited supply.

**Q2**: What data illustrates the stakes of delayed access for young children?
**A**: CDC data show infants aged 6-11 months had a COVID-19 hospitalization rate on par with adults 65-74, and 42% of hospitalized children aged 6-23 months (October 2022-April 2024) had an underlying condition.

**Q3**: What should families and providers do given these access gaps?
**A**: Families relying on VFC, especially uninsured or low-income households, should check availability directly with providers and be aware that cost barriers alone can suppress vaccination even when supply exists.
