## Summary

A randomized trial of 121 young adults (18-24) with mild to moderately severe depressive symptoms found that an eight-week salsa dance programme produced a clinically meaningful, significantly greater reduction in depression than a waitlist control, alongside larger improvements in social anxiety and daily happiness.

## Core Concepts

**[[Social Dance]]** functions as a multimodal intervention combining physical activity, music, social interaction, and structured practice — a combination that may make it more engaging and accessible than single-mode interventions (pure exercise or pure talk therapy alone) for young adults, per the concept of [[Social Prescribing]].

## Key Takeaways

- **Clinically meaningful effect**: A 2.45-point between-group reduction on the PHQ-9 scale exceeded the clinical significance threshold.
- **Broader benefits**: Salsa participants also saw greater reductions in social anxiety and larger gains in daily happiness than controls.
- **Design**: 121 participants aged 18-24, randomized to an 8-week salsa programme or waitlist control.
- **Mechanism hypothesis**: Combining movement, music, and social interaction may lower the barrier to engagement compared to conventional interventions.
