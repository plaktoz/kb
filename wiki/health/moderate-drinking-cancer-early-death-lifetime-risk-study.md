## Summary

A US-Canada study in the Journal of Studies on Alcohol and Drugs, built on more than 7,200 scientific articles combined with national health data, estimates lifetime risks of alcohol-attributable death and disease. It found no overall protective effect at any drinking level and higher risk above one drink per day for both sexes. The authors say the findings were sidelined in the 2025-2030 Dietary Guidelines.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Alcohol-Attributable Mortality]]**: Lifetime risk of premature death and disability caused by drinking.
- **[[Katherine Keyes]] and [[Kevin Shield]]**: Co-author at Columbia Mailman School and lead author at the University of Toronto.
- **[[Dose-Response Relationship]]**: Risk begins at low consumption and rises with each additional drink.
- **[[Dietary Guidelines for Americans]]**: The 2025-2030 edition says to "limit alcoholic beverages" without a numeric amount.
- **[[Standard Drink]]**: 12 oz beer, 5 oz wine or 1.5 oz spirits.
- **[[Ischemic Heart Disease]]**: Low intake may lower this risk, but the benefit is outweighed by other harms.

## Key Takeaways

- **Evidence Base**: Over 7,200 scientific articles were evaluated and combined with national datasets.
- **No Protective Effect**: No statistically significant overall benefit at any consumption level.
- **Two Drinks Daily**: Substantially higher risk of alcohol-attributable premature death.
- **Threshold**: Above one drink per day raised risk for both men and women.
- **Net Assessment**: Possible heart and stroke benefits were outweighed, even at seven drinks weekly.
- **Population Estimates**: Results describe population risk, not individual likelihood.
- **Guideline Gap**: Authors say findings were sidelined in the 2025-2030 Dietary Guidelines.
- **Headline Figure**: Mortality reaches 1 in 25 at 14 drinks per week.

## 🧠 Core Principles

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the study's central finding about moderate drinking?
**A**: Even modest drinking is associated with higher risks of premature death, disability, cancer and heart disease, and no protective effect on overall health was observed at any level.

**Q2**: How did the researchers reach this conclusion?
**A**: They reviewed more than 7,200 articles on alcohol-linked diseases and injuries, then combined the risk estimates with national health datasets using statistical models. Combining conditions let them estimate overall burden rather than disease by disease.

**Q3**: What does this imply for how people think about "moderate" drinking?
**A**: Two drinks a day, often seen as socially moderate, carries substantially higher risk, and risk rose above one drink per day for both sexes. The authors say clearer thresholds help people make more informed decisions.
