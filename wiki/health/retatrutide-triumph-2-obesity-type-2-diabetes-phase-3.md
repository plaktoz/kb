## Summary

The Phase III TRIUMPH-2 trial, published in *The Lancet*, found that [[Retatrutide]], Eli Lilly's once-weekly triple receptor agonist, produced up to 18.8% average body-weight loss over 80 weeks in 1,152 adults with obesity and [[Type 2 Diabetes]], versus 5.1% on placebo. By activating GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptors at once, it appears to overcome the weight-loss resistance that limits single- and dual-agonist drugs like [[Semaglutide]] and [[Tirzepatide]] in people with diabetes. Benefits extended to blood sugar control and cardiovascular risk markers, though mostly digestive side effects drove 8–12% discontinuation at higher doses.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Retatrutide]]**: An experimental once-weekly injection from [[Eli Lilly]] that acts as a triple receptor agonist.
- **Triple hormone mechanism**: Mimics [[GLP-1]] (lowers blood sugar, promotes fullness) and [[GIP]] (enhances insulin response, aids weight loss), and activates [[Glucagon]] receptors to raise energy expenditure and metabolic activity.
- **Comparison drugs**: [[Semaglutide]] (Wegovy) targets one hormone; [[Tirzepatide]] (Zepbound) targets two; neither usually exceeds 15% average weight loss in people with type 2 diabetes.
- **[[TRIUMPH-2 Trial]]**: An 80-week [[Randomized Controlled Trial|randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled]] Phase III study testing 4 mg, 9 mg, and 12 mg weekly doses, titrated up from 2 mg every four weeks.
- **[[HbA1c]]**: A marker of long-term blood sugar control, used to measure glycemic benefit.
- **[[Bariatric Surgery]]**: The benchmark that the top quartile of weight loss on 12 mg approached.
- **[[GLP-1 Receptor Agonists]]**: The drug class that first made weight loss more achievable for people with obesity and diabetes.

## Key Takeaways

- **Headline Result**: 12 mg dose averaged 18.8% weight loss (~20.7 kg) vs 5.1% placebo.
- **Dose Response**: Weight loss rose with dose: 11.9%, 16.8%, 18.8%.
- **High-BMI Subgroup**: Participants starting at BMI 35+ lost 21.9% on 12 mg.
- **Surgery-Level Outcomes**: At 12 mg, 75% lost ≥10%; nearly one-third lost ≥25%.
- **Breaking the 15% Ceiling**: Semaglutide and tirzepatide rarely exceed 15% in type 2 diabetes.
- **Third Mechanism**: Glucagon agonism may explain overcoming the body's weight-loss resistance.
- **Glycemic Control**: HbA1c fell 1.4–1.5 points vs 0.45 with placebo.
- **Cardiometabolic Gains**: Lower waist circumference, systolic blood pressure, and triglycerides.
- **Side Effects**: Diarrhea up to 34%, nausea up to 28%, vomiting up to 16%.
- **Discontinuation**: 8–12% quit at higher doses vs 5% on placebo.
- **Open Question**: Benefits for other obesity-related complications still need research.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the TRIUMPH-2 trial find about retatrutide in adults with obesity and type 2 diabetes?
**A**: After 80 weeks, the 12 mg dose produced an average 18.8% body-weight loss versus 5.1% on placebo, along with better blood sugar control and cardiovascular risk markers.

**Q2**: What mechanism is thought to let retatrutide exceed the ~15% weight-loss ceiling of semaglutide and tirzepatide in diabetes?
**A**: Beyond mimicking GLP-1 and GIP, it activates glucagon receptors, adding a third mechanism that increases energy expenditure and may overcome the body's resistance to weight loss.

**Q3**: What trade-off should be weighed when considering higher retatrutide doses?
**A**: Higher doses deliver more weight loss, but digestive side effects led 8–12% of higher-dose participants to stop treatment versus 5% on placebo.
