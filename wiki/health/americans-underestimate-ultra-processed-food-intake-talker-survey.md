## Summary
A Talker Research survey of 2,000 Americans found respondents think ultra-processed foods make up 16% of their diet, while CDC data put the figure at 53%. Only 9% could correctly identify commonly purchased items as ultra-processed, and a health economist says the hidden lifetime costs could exceed $350,000. Many respondents say knowing the long-term costs might motivate them to cut back.

## Core Concepts
- [[Ultra-Processed Foods]]: foods that make up 53% of the average American diet per CDC data.
- [[Talker Research]]: ran the 2,000-person survey comparing perception with dietary data.
- [[Perception Gap]]: respondents estimated 16% intake against an actual 53%, a more than threefold disconnect.
- [[Hidden Health Costs]]: healthcare expenditure and lost productivity that [[Lindsay Allen]], a health economist, estimates could exceed $350,000 over a lifetime.
- [[Food Labeling Transparency]]: 28% put primary responsibility for reducing costs on food manufacturers.

## Key Takeaways
- **Intake Gap**: Perceived 16% versus actual 53% of diet from ultra-processed foods.
- **Identification Failure**: Only 9% correctly identified common items such as bread, cereal, deli meat.
- **Common Misses**: Sliced bread 42%, breakfast cereal 35%, deli meats 33% recognized as ultra-processed.
- **Cost Underestimate**: Respondents estimated about $128,149 lifetime spend; hidden costs may exceed $350,000.
- **Avoidance Fatalism**: 61% feel avoiding ultra-processed foods is nearly impossible.
- **Willingness to Pay**: 69% would pay nearly $1,200 more annually for higher-quality groceries.
- **Motivation Lever**: 42% say long-term cost knowledge might make them eat fewer.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Visible immediate costs crowd out hidden long-term costs** — When people weigh only what they pay now, deferred costs such as healthcare and lost productivity go unaccounted for, so choices skew toward the cheaper-looking option.
→ explains: Cost Underestimate, Motivation Lever
