## Summary

A JAMA Network Open study published October 1 found predominantly white U.S. neighborhoods had about 15% more pharmacies per 10,000 residents than communities with high proportions of people of color, amid a nationwide wave of [[Pharmacy Closures]]. Financial pressures — low margins, reduced drug payments, and workforce shortages — are driving closures, which have been linked to declines in medication adherence. The same Public Health Watch roundup covers unequal heat-related worker deaths, wildfire ozone weakening the Amazon carbon sink, and net hospital closures.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Pharmacy Deserts]] and [[Health Equity]]**: Pharmacy density is lower in communities of color and areas with more foreign-born residents; higher in areas with older residents, more vehicle ownership, and single-family homes.
- **Drivers of closures**: Low profit margins, reduced payments for prescription drugs, and [[Health Workforce Shortages]].
- **Health consequences**: Closures linked to declines in taking prescribed heart and seizure medications ([[Medication Adherence]]).
- **[[Occupational Heat Exposure]]**: JAMA study (October 5) using national death records found military, construction, and natural resources/mining workers face the highest heat-related death rates.
- **Wildfire ozone and the [[Amazon Rainforest]]**: Communications Earth & Environment study finds wildfire ozone suppresses forest carbon uptake, worsened by [[El Niño]] droughts.
- **[[Hospital Closures]]**: JAMA study finds closures outpaced openings 2010–2025, hitting rural residents hardest.
- **Other items**: Climate change may raise [[Methylmercury]] in seafood; lead and zinc in [[PM2.5]] linked to child respiratory visits.

## Key Takeaways

- **Pharmacy Gap**: White neighborhoods have ~15% more pharmacies per 10,000 residents.
- **Closure Wave**: Nearly one-third of U.S. pharmacies closed 2010–2021; 7,000+ since 2019.
- **Financial Drivers**: Low margins, reduced drug payments, and workforce shortages fuel closures.
- **Health Impact**: Closures linked to drops in heart and seizure medication use.
- **Heat Deaths**: ~2,030 heat-related deaths among working-age adults, 2021–2023; 0.3 per 100,000.
- **Highest-Risk Industries**: Military ~1, construction 0.83, mining/resources 0.78 per 100,000 annually.
- **Racial Heat Disparity**: Hispanic and Black workers had over twice the odds in some industries.
- **Surveillance Blind Spot**: Workplace tracking misses heat deaths occurring after workers leave jobs.
- **Amazon Carbon Loss**: Every 100 tons of fire carbon blocks 24 more tons of uptake.
- **Hospital Net Loss**: ~430 closed vs. ~220 opened 2010–2025; 24,000+ beds lost.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Physical access shapes health behavior** — When the distance or effort required to reach care increases, people use it less, so closures of nearby facilities translate into worse health outcomes, especially for those dependent on ongoing treatment.
→ explains: Health Impact, Pharmacy Gap, Hospital Net Loss

**T2. Thin margins concentrate service loss where resources are scarcest** — When providers operate on low margins, the least profitable sites close first, so service withdrawal clusters in already disadvantaged communities and compounds existing inequities.
→ explains: Financial Drivers, Closure Wave, Pharmacy Gap, Hospital Net Loss

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the October 1 JAMA Network Open study find about pharmacy access?
**A**: Communities with greater proportions of white residents had more pharmacies as of 2023, with predominantly white neighborhoods having about 15% more pharmacies per 10,000 residents than those with high proportions of people of color.

**Q2**: What is driving the U.S. pharmacy closure wave, and how large is it?
**A**: Low profit margins, reduced prescription drug payments, and workforce shortages; nearly one-third of pharmacies closed from 2010 to 2021, with more than 7,000 closures since 2019.

**Q3**: Why do pharmacy closures matter for patients?
**A**: Losing neighborhood pharmacies harms people who rely on medications for chronic conditions, with closures linked to declines in taking prescribed heart and seizure medications.
