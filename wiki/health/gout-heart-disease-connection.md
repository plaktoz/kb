## Summary
People with gout face elevated risk of heart disease — coronary artery disease, heart failure, and arrhythmias — driven by shared metabolic risk factors and gout's chronic low-grade inflammation, with a 2026 JAMA Internal Medicine study showing uric-acid-lowering treatment cuts five-year cardiac risk by 9%.

## Core Concepts

- [[Uric Acid Buildup]] — the root mechanism of gout: the body overproduces or under-eliminates uric acid, which can crystallize in joints and trigger immune-driven flares
- [[Shared Risk Factor Pathway]] — obesity, diabetes, and high blood pressure independently raise both uric acid levels and cardiovascular risk, meaning much of the gout-heart link may run through common upstream conditions rather than gout causing heart disease directly
- [[Between-Flare Inflammation]] — low-grade inflammation persists even between acute gout flares, damaging blood-vessel linings and promoting the fatty plaque buildup that reduces blood flow — the proposed direct mechanistic link to cardiovascular disease
- [[Mediterranean/DASH Diet Overlap]] — both diets simultaneously lower uric acid and support cardiovascular health, making dietary intervention a rare case where one lifestyle change addresses both conditions at once

## Key Takeaways

- **Treatment benefit quantified**: a March 2026 JAMA Internal Medicine study found lowering blood uric acid below 6 mg/dL with medication reduced five-year heart attack/stroke/cardiac-death risk by 9%.
- **Two medication classes**: flare-relief drugs (NSAIDs, colchicine, corticosteroids) treat symptoms; uric-acid-lowering drugs (most commonly allopurinol or febuxostat) prevent future flares and are typically recommended for anyone with 2+ flares/year.
- **Lifestyle overlap**: Mediterranean and DASH diets (vegetables, fruits, whole grains, olive oil, low-fat dairy, fish, legumes, nuts; limiting red meat and added sugar) lower uric acid and support heart health simultaneously.
- **Exercise guidance**: 150–300 minutes/week of moderate aerobic activity plus strength training at least twice weekly benefits both conditions.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Gout patients face measurably elevated cardiovascular risk, driven by both shared metabolic risk factors and gout's own chronic inflammation, and treating gout appears to meaningfully lower that cardiac risk.

**Q2**: What specific evidence links gout treatment to reduced heart-disease risk?
**A**: A March 2026 JAMA Internal Medicine study found that patients who lowered uric acid below 6 mg/dL with medication were 9% less likely to have a heart attack, stroke, or die of heart disease over five years.

**Q3**: What single lifestyle change addresses both gout and cardiovascular risk simultaneously, and why?
**A**: Following the Mediterranean or DASH diet — emphasizing vegetables, whole grains, fish, and legumes while limiting red meat and added sugar — because both diets independently lower uric acid levels and support cardiovascular health.
