## Summary

The DiSCoVeR trial of 109 adults with major depression found that a six-week, mostly home-based program combining active [[Transcranial Direct Current Stimulation]] (tDCS) with a cognitive-control video game was feasible and well tolerated across Jerusalem, Riga and Munich. However, it was no more effective than a double-sham control, with both groups improving substantially (7.3 vs. 8.2 points). The authors say the result shows why convincing control conditions are essential for understanding what drives improvement.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Transcranial Direct Current Stimulation]] (tDCS)**: Non-invasive technique delivering a weak electrical current through scalp electrodes to influence brain activity.
- **[[DiSCoVeR Trial]]**: Multicenter, sham-controlled randomized trial published in JAMA Network Open, led by Prof. Mor Nahum of Hebrew University of Jerusalem.
- **Double-Sham Control**: Sham stimulation plus a control game lacking the cognitive training exercises.
- **[[Cognitive Control Training]]**: A video game designed to strengthen attention, memory and executive control.
- **[[Placebo Effect]] and Expectations**: Participants who believed they had the control treatment improved less, regardless of actual assignment.
- **[[Remote Mental Health Care]]**: Home delivery after minimal clinic instruction as a route to accessible treatment.

## Key Takeaways

- **Feasibility**: 79% completed at least 20 of 30 sessions; nearly 2,500 sessions done.
- **Safety**: No significant difference in adverse event rates between groups.
- **No Added Benefit**: Active combination improved 7.3 points versus 8.2 for control; not significant.
- **Both Groups Improved**: Cause could be expectations, engagement, routine, ongoing care or natural change.
- **Expectation Signal**: Belief in having the control treatment tracked with less improvement.
- **Not a Verdict**: Results do not mean brain stimulation is ineffective for depression.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. An effect can only be attributed to a treatment by comparison with a convincing control.** — When a control arm improves as much as the active arm, the improvement cannot be credited to the active ingredient. · *model:* [[Placebo Effect]]
→ explains: No Added Benefit, Both Groups Improved, Expectation Signal

**T2. Accessibility and efficacy are separate questions.** — A treatment can be shown to be deliverable and safe without showing that it works better than alternatives.
→ explains: Feasibility, Safety, Not a Verdict

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the DiSCoVeR trial find overall?
**A**: Home-based tDCS plus cognitive training was feasible and well tolerated for major depression, but it did not outperform a double-sham control because both groups improved substantially.

**Q2**: What data illustrate the feasibility and the lack of added benefit?
**A**: 79% of 109 participants completed at least 20 sessions, while the active group improved 7.3 points versus 8.2 in the control group, a non-significant difference.

**Q3**: What does the study imply for testing digital and device-based treatments?
**A**: Trials need convincing control conditions to separate the effect of stimulation or training from expectations, engagement, routine and natural symptom change.
