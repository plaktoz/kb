## Summary

A University of Connecticut study in [[JAMA Pediatrics]] estimates that one in six US teenagers have moderate to severe anxiety symptoms, with heavy overlap with depression, suicidal thoughts and substance use. Nearly half of teens with severe symptoms did not seek care in the past year, and male, Black and uninsured teens were least likely to get help. Doctors advise parents to tell normal worry apart from anxiety that interferes with daily function, model coping, and use annual well-child visits and screeners to catch symptoms that are hard to see.

## Core Concepts

- **Data source**: Self-reported anxiety symptoms from more than 15,000 people aged 12-20 in the 2024 [[National Survey on Drug Use and Health]], the first year anxiety questions were included.
- **New national baseline**: Study author [[Greg Rhee]] says the data allow tracking of trends, emerging disparities and whether treatment-access efforts work.
- **Worry vs. [[Anxiety]]**: Per the [[American Academy of Pediatrics]], worry becomes anxiety when thoughts or symptoms interfere with daily function.
- **Symptoms**: The [[Child Mind Institute]] lists irritability, trouble concentrating, recurring fears, extreme self-consciousness, sensitivity to criticism, withdrawal, falling grades or school refusal.
- **[[Comorbidity]]**: Severe anxiety co-occurs with severe [[Depression]], suicidal thoughts and substance abuse.
- **Risk factors**: Female adolescents report severe symptoms at nearly twice the male rate; psychiatrist [[Chanelle Ramsubick]] points to girls learning to "perform"; [[Social Media]] overuse is linked to anxiety and loneliness.
- **Treatment gap**: Rhee cites stigma, a shortage of child mental health professionals, financial and insurance barriers, and limited culturally responsive services.
- **[[Mental Health Screening]]**: Routine screeners at annual well-child checks can detect symptoms and reduce stigma.

## Key Takeaways

- **Prevalence**: About 1 in 6 US teens have moderate to severe anxiety.
- **Comorbidity**: Nearly two-thirds with severe anxiety met severe depression criteria.
- **Suicide and Substance Risk**: Nearly half reported suicidal thoughts; nearly one-third reported substance abuse.
- **Sex Gap**: Girls nearly twice as likely to report severe anxiety symptoms.
- **Treatment Gap**: Nearly half with severe symptoms sought no care in the past year.
- **Underserved Groups**: Male, Black and uninsured teens least likely to seek care.
- **Hidden Symptoms**: Many children lack language to describe their internal world.
- **Function Threshold**: Worry becomes anxiety when it disrupts daily functioning.
- **Parent Strategies**: Model coping, create safe space, sleep, breathing, relaxation, time together.
- **Routine Screening**: Annual well-child checks with screeners destigmatize and catch problems.
- **Baseline Data**: First NSDUH anxiety questions enable future trend tracking.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Hidden internal states require systematic detection** — Conditions that cannot be seen from the outside and that sufferers cannot easily articulate go undetected unless they are routinely measured; making measurement universal also removes the stigma of being singled out.
→ explains: Hidden Symptoms, Routine Screening, Treatment Gap

**T2. You cannot manage what you have not baselined** — Trends, disparities and the effect of interventions can only be judged against an initial measurement taken the same way over time.
→ explains: Baseline Data

## 🃏 Review Questions

**A**: An estimated one in six teens have moderate to severe anxiety symptoms, and nearly half of those with severe symptoms had not sought care in the past year.

**Q2**: Which groups were most at risk or least likely to get help, according to the study?
**A**: Female adolescents were nearly twice as likely as males to report severe symptoms, while male, Black and uninsured teens were most likely not to seek care.

**Q3**: What practical steps do doctors recommend for parents?
**A**: Model healthy coping, create a safe space to talk, encourage sleep and relaxation techniques, spend time together, and use annual well-child visits to ask about mental health and request screeners.
