## Summary
University of Missouri researchers found that older adults with consistent daily routines reported less physical pain and fewer depressive symptoms, regardless of sleep quality. The authors suggest [[Circadian Rhythm]] regulation as a possible explanation. The small, survey-based study cannot prove causation.

## Core Concepts
- [[Circadian Rhythm]]: the body's 24-hour timing system, shaped by light, meals, work, social contact, and sleep cues.
- [[Insomnia]]: roughly half of adults 60 and older report symptoms, per the National Council on Aging.
- [[Social Jet Lag]]: shifting sleep schedules between workdays and days off, mimicking time-zone travel.
- [[Eunjin Tracy]]: University of Missouri assistant professor who led the analysis of survey responses.
- [[Daily Routine]]: consistent timing of waking, eating, working or studying, socializing, and bedtime.

## Key Takeaways
- **Sample**: 67 older adults surveyed; 37 reported insomnia.
- **Insomnia Effect**: Insomnia was linked to more pain and more depression.
- **Routine Effect**: Consistent routines tied to less pain and depression regardless of sleep quality.
- **Timing Cues**: Waking, eating, working, socializing, and bedtime help synchronize internal clocks.
- **Social Jet Lag**: Weekend schedule shifts may disrupt circadian rhythm; keep weekly timing consistent.
- **Limits**: Small survey sample; association only, so larger studies are needed.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Regular timing cues keep the body clock aligned** — Biological rhythms stay coordinated when daily signals arrive at predictable times, and irregular signals disrupt that coordination.
→ explains: Timing Cues, Social Jet Lag, Routine Effect

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the University of Missouri study find about daily routines?
**A**: Older adults with regular daily routines reported less physical pain and fewer depressive symptoms than those without, regardless of sleep quality.

**Q2**: What mechanism might explain the link, and what evidence limits it?
**A**: Consistent timing cues may synchronize the circadian rhythm, which influences hormones, metabolism, and mood. The study used survey data from only 67 older adults, so it shows association, not causation.

**Q3**: How could someone apply the findings?
**A**: Keep waking times, meals, and bedtimes consistent across the whole week, including weekends, to limit social jet lag.
