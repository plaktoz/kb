## Summary
Wave Neuroscience and Texas A&M researchers received FDA clearance for Magnetic e-Resonance Therapy (MeRT), an EEG-guided, AI-personalized form of transcranial magnetic stimulation for PTSD. Company-reported early trial results show a 52% symptom reduction and 68% remission, versus a 10.9% maximum remission rate in a 2020 review of PTSD medications. It would be the first FDA-cleared nonmedication treatment specifically for PTSD, though full trial data are not yet public.

## Core Concepts
- [[Magnetic e-Resonance Therapy (MeRT)]]: combines [[EEG]] brain-activity measurement with [[Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS)]] using an AI-assisted algorithm to personalize protocols.
- [[PTSD]]: affects an estimated 6% of Americans in their lifetime; ~7% of veterans are diagnosed annually.
- [[SSRIs]]: sertraline and fluoxetine are the only two FDA-approved PTSD medications; psychotherapy is hard to complete and has high dropout.
- [[Precision Medicine]]: [[Kenneth Ramos]] argues for tailoring care to individual biology (people are ~99.9% genetically alike, but the 0.1% matters).
- [[FDA Breakthrough Device Designation]]: granted in 2024; helped the project clear review in just over two years versus a typical ~10.
- [[Wave Neuroscience]] and [[Texas A&M Health Institute of Biosciences and Technology]]: developer and academic research partner.

## Key Takeaways
- **Trial Results**: 52% symptom reduction and 68% remission, per Wave Neuroscience; full findings unpublished.
- **Medication Baseline**: A 2020 review found medication remission at most 10.9%.
- **Caveat**: Figures come from different studies; not a direct head-to-head comparison.
- **Personalization**: EEG plus an AI algorithm customizes TMS to each patient's brain activity.
- **Dosing**: Five sessions weekly for five to six weeks; gains appeared to persist afterward.
- **Drug-Free Option**: First FDA-cleared nonmedication PTSD treatment, per Liberzon.
- **Fast Regulation**: [[FDA Breakthrough Device Designation]] cut clearance to just over two years.
- **Access Gap**: Insurance coverage and affordability still need to be worked out.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central claim about MeRT?
**A**: MeRT, an EEG-guided and AI-personalized form of TMS, received FDA clearance for PTSD and showed 52% symptom reduction and 68% remission in the company's early trial.

**Q2**: How does MeRT differ from conventional TMS, and what is the comparison baseline?
**A**: MeRT uses EEG measurements and an AI algorithm to tailor stimulation to each patient's brain activity rather than a standardized protocol. The baseline is a 2020 review where medications reached at most 10.9% remission, though the studies differ and are not a direct comparison.

**Q3**: Who might benefit and what barrier remains?
**A**: People who cannot tolerate SSRI side effects or drop out of psychotherapy gain a drug-free option, potentially reaching about 13 million people. Insurance coverage and affordability still need to be established.
