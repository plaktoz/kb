## Summary

A phase 3 international clinical trial (ISLEND-1) found that a once-weekly oral HIV pill combining [[Islatravir]] and [[Lenacapavir]] performed as well as the widely used daily three-drug regimen [[Biktarvy]] over 48 weeks. Over 93% of participants in both arms maintained viral suppression, with zero virological failures in the weekly group. The study, published in the *New England Journal of Medicine*, suggests a weekly pill could reduce pill fatigue and expand treatment options for people living with HIV.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Islatravir]] + [[Lenacapavir]]**: A two-drug, once-weekly oral combination tested as a simpler alternative to current three-drug daily regimens.
- **[[Biktarvy]]**: The current standard-of-care daily three-drug HIV regimen used as the comparator arm.
- **[[ISLEND-1 Trial]]**: Phase 3 global trial across 106 sites in 12 countries enrolling 607 virally suppressed adults.
- **[[Viral Suppression]]**: The primary endpoint — maintaining HIV viral load below detectable levels (< 50 copies/mL).
- **[[Pill Fatigue]]**: The psychological burden of long-term daily dosing; reduced from 365 to 52 annual doses with a weekly regimen.
- **[[Gilead Sciences]]** and **[[Merck Sharp & Dohme]]**: Co-funders of the trial.

## Key Takeaways

- **Viral Suppression**: >93% of participants in both groups maintained suppression at week 48.
- **Zero Failures**: No virological failures occurred in the weekly pill group.
- **Adherence**: Weekly arm averaged ~99% adherence vs. ~96% for the daily arm.
- **Adverse Events**: Comparable between groups; mostly mild-to-moderate.
- **Dose Reduction**: Weekly regimen cuts annual doses from 365 to 52.
- **Hepatitis B Gap**: Weekly pill lacks hepatitis B activity unlike Biktarvy; one unvaccinated participant developed hepatitis B.
- **Limitation**: Trial enrolled highly adherent, virally suppressed patients — may not generalize to those struggling with daily dosing.
- **Follow-Up Pending**: 96-week data are forthcoming.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: The trial's adherence-optimized sample (already ~96–99% compliant) means the metric of "adherence improvement" cannot be properly tested — the people most likely to benefit from weekly dosing were excluded by design.
- **[[Selection Bias]]**: Enrolling only participants who had maintained viral suppression for ≥6 months inflates efficacy estimates and limits external validity to the hardest-to-treat population.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the ISLEND-1 trial?
**A**: A once-weekly oral HIV pill combining islatravir and lenacapavir was non-inferior to the daily three-drug regimen Biktarvy, with over 93% of participants maintaining viral suppression and zero virological failures in the weekly group at 48 weeks.

**Q2**: What key limitation affects the generalizability of the ISLEND-1 results?
**A**: All participants were already highly adherent and virally suppressed for at least 6 months prior to enrollment, meaning the results may not apply to patients who struggle with daily dosing — the very group most likely to benefit from a weekly pill.

**Q3**: What practical implication does a once-weekly HIV regimen offer, and what safety gap must be addressed?
**A**: Reducing annual doses from 365 to 52 could ease pill fatigue and potentially improve adherence for some patients; however, clinicians must account for the weekly pill's lack of hepatitis B activity, which Biktarvy provides, making hepatitis B vaccination or screening essential before switching.
