## Summary
An analysis of 359,595 adults with type 2 diabetes at a Texas medical center found GLP-1 starters had lower obesity-related cancer rates than insulin or sulfonylurea starters, but not versus metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors, or DPP-4 inhibitors. Because the study is observational and the comparison groups differ in disease severity, it does not show GLP-1s prevent cancer. Treatment should follow established health needs, not potential cancer benefits.

## Core Concepts
- **[[GLP-1 Receptor Agonists]]**: Diabetes and weight-loss drugs now studied for cancer associations.
- **[[Obesity-Related Cancers]]**: Outcome tracked at three and five years.
- **Comparator drugs**: [[Metformin]], [[Insulin]], [[SGLT2 Inhibitors]], [[DPP-4 Inhibitors]], [[Sulfonylureas]].
- **[[Observational Study]]**: Patients were not randomized to treatments.
- **[[Confounding Variable]]**: Disease severity and prescribing patterns differ by drug.
- **[[Type 2 Diabetes]]**: Population, with heart and kidney complications considered.

## Key Takeaways
- **Sample**: 359,595 adults, electronic health records, January 2010 to July 2025.
- **Lower Rates**: About 1.25 points lower than insulin, 1.14 lower than sulfonylureas.
- **No Edge**: Similar rates versus metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors, and DPP-4 inhibitors.
- **Severe Diabetes**: SGLT2 inhibitors showed the lowest cancer rates.
- **Confounding**: Insulin users likely have more advanced disease at baseline.
- **Thin Long-Term Data**: Many GLP-1 users started recently, making five-year estimates less certain.
- **Practical Advice**: Don't switch drugs; keep up cancer screenings.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Association is not causation** — An observational link can arise because the compared groups differ in baseline characteristics, so it can't establish that the exposure caused the outcome. · *model:* [[Correlation vs Causation]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the 359K-person study find about GLP-1s and cancer?
**A**: GLP-1 starters had lower obesity-related cancer rates than insulin or sulfonylurea starters, but similar rates to metformin, SGLT2 and DPP-4 users. It was not a blanket win.

**Q2**: Why can't the findings be read as GLP-1s preventing cancer?
**A**: The study was observational, and drug groups differed at baseline. Insulin is often prescribed when diabetes is harder to manage, and SGLT2 inhibitors are prescribed with heart and kidney health in mind.

**Q3**: How should a GLP-1 user act on this research?
**A**: Don't change treatment based on it; choose medication by blood sugar, heart and kidney needs, talk to a provider before switching, and keep up cancer screenings.
