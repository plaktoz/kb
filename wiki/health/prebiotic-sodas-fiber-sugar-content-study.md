## Summary

A study presented at NUTRITION 2026 analyzed 108 prebiotic sodas launched between 2021–2025 and found they contain more calories and sugar than diet sodas and flavored sparkling water, though less than regular sodas. The average 12-oz can delivers 35 calories, 5g sugar, and 5g fiber, but marketing claims may overstate health benefits. Experts recommend whole food sources of prebiotics over isolated fiber added to beverages.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Prebiotic Sodas]]** — functional beverages marketed for gut health containing added inulin or other soluble fibers
- **[[Dietary Fiber]]** — prebiotic fiber in these sodas averages 5g per 12-oz can (range: ~2–9g), far below what a high-fiber diet provides
- **[[Nutrition Label Literacy]]** — consumers are advised to read labels rather than rely on "prebiotic" marketing language
- **[[Whole Food Prebiotics]]** — asparagus, bananas, oats, garlic, legumes, onions consistently outperform isolated fiber in beverages
- **[[NUTRITION 2026]]** — annual nutrition science conference (July 25–28, Washington D.C.) where the study was presented (not yet peer-reviewed)

## Key Takeaways

- Average prebiotic soda: 35 cal, 5g sugar, 5g fiber per 12-oz can.
- More sugar/calories than diet sodas and flavored sparkling water.
- Study analyzed 108 sodas launched in the U.S. from 2021–2025.
- "Prebiotic" label does not equate to a high-fiber diet.
- Best prebiotics come from asparagus, oats, garlic, legumes, and onions.
- Study not yet peer-reviewed as of publication date.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Once "prebiotic" becomes a marketing term, product formulations optimize for label appeal rather than genuine gut-health benefit — the signal degrades as it becomes the target.
