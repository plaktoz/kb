## Summary

A Lancet Global Health study of over 3 million adolescents in 140 countries finds only about 14% get the WHO-recommended hour of daily moderate-to-vigorous exercise, with a flat 15-year trend. Girls and teens in low- and middle-income countries are the least active, driven by limited opportunity, unwalkable cities, screens, and cultural taboos. Experts point to schools and structured sport as the most practical lever.

## Core Concepts

- [[World Health Organization]]: recommends at least 60 minutes a day of moderate-to-vigorous activity for adolescents.
- [[The Lancet Global Health]]: published the study, pooling surveys of 3 million+ teens across 140 countries.
- [[Fiona Bull]]: WHO physical activity unit head and study co-author; says "business as usual will not change these trends."
- [[Solveig Cunningham]]: Emory global health researcher; links adolescent baseline to adulthood activity.
- [[Andrea Ramirez Varela]]: epidemiologist who frames activity as "an issue of opportunity."
- [[Adolescent Physical Activity]]: self-reported days of 60+ minutes of activity in the past week.
- [[Gender Gap in Physical Activity]]: girls are less active than boys in virtually all countries.
- [[Built Environment and Walkability]]: unwalkable, unsafe, or polluted cities restrict activity.
- [[School-Based Sport Programs]]: structured sport time correlates with higher adolescent activity.

## Key Takeaways

- **Scale of Inactivity**: Only about 14% of adolescents report an hour of activity daily.
- **Zero-Day Group**: One in four hit 60 minutes on no day of the week.
- **Flat Trend**: Activity levels have not improved over the last 15 years.
- **Gender Gap**: 11% of girls are daily active versus 17% of boys.
- **Income Gap**: Teens in low- and middle-income countries are less active than Western peers.
- **Environmental Barriers**: Unwalkable cities, safety fears, air pollution, and screens limit movement.
- **Cultural Norms**: Puberty-era taboos and chores-as-activity shape girls' lower intensity.
- **School Lever**: Countries with more structured school sport tend to have more active teens.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Early baselines carry forward** — Activity habits set in adolescence tend to persist into adulthood, so early inactivity compounds into later costs. · *model:* [[Path Dependence]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the Lancet Global Health study on teen exercise?
**A**: Only about 14% of adolescents worldwide get the recommended hour of daily activity, and the 15-year trend is flat. Girls and teens in lower-income countries are the least active.

**Q2**: How did researchers measure activity, and what gender data stood out?
**A**: They used surveys asking how many days in the past week teens were active for 60+ minutes, covering 3 million+ teens in 140 countries. Nearly 30% of girls did no exercise all week versus 22% of boys, and 11% of girls were active daily versus 17% of boys.

**Q3**: Where do experts suggest starting to improve teen activity?
**A**: In schools, where kids spend most of their time, by building accessible and affordable structured sport programs. Redesigning cities for walkability is slower and costlier.
