## Summary

A UK Biobank study of 340,924 adults, presented at ACC.26, found heavy drinking raised mortality regardless of beverage, while low to moderate wine intake was linked to lower death risk and similar beer, cider or spirits intake to higher risk. The study is observational and the authors stress it does not show wine protects against disease. Drinking less alcohol remains generally better for health.

## Core Concepts

- **[[UK Biobank]]**: Health and lifestyle database; 340,924 adults enrolled 2006-2022, followed over 13 years on average.
- **[[Zhangling Chen]]**: Senior author at Second Xiangya Hospital, Central South University.
- **[[American College of Cardiology]] ACC.26**: Annual Scientific Session where the study was presented.
- **[[Standard Drink]]**: A 12 oz beer, 5 oz wine or 1.5 oz spirits each hold about 14 g pure alcohol.
- **[[Polyphenols]]**: Plant compounds in red wine with possible vascular benefits, not proven responsible.
- **[[Observational Study]]**: Shows associations only; cannot prove a beverage causes better or worse outcomes.
- **[[Cardiovascular Disease]]**: The cause of death where wine and other drinks diverged most.

## Key Takeaways

- **Heavy Drinking**: 24% higher all-cause, 36% higher cancer, 14% higher heart-disease mortality.
- **Wine Contrast**: Moderate wine drinkers had 21% lower cardiovascular death risk than non-drinkers.
- **Other Beverages**: Even low beer, cider or spirits intake linked to 9% higher cardiovascular death.
- **Lifestyle Gap**: Wine is drunk with meals by people reporting healthier diets.
- **Measurement**: Groups defined by grams of pure alcohol, not drink counts.
- **Limitations**: Self-reported baseline intake; UK Biobank participants are healthier than average.
- **Bottom Line**: Do not start drinking wine for heart health.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Association is not causation** — When groups differ in many habits, an outcome gap between them cannot be pinned on the one exposure being compared; lifestyle can masquerade as a beverage effect. · *model:* [[Correlation vs Causation]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the 340,924-adult study find about wine versus other alcohol?
**A**: Heavy drinking raised mortality regardless of beverage, but low to moderate wine was linked to lower death risk while similar beer, cider or spirits intake was linked to higher risk. The authors say this does not prove wine protects.

**Q2**: What figures show the cardiovascular divergence, and what might explain it?
**A**: Moderate wine drinkers had 21% lower cardiovascular death risk, while low beer, cider or spirits intake carried 9% higher risk. Possible explanations are polyphenols in red wine and wine drinkers' meal-time drinking and healthier diets.

**Q3**: How should someone act on these results?
**A**: Not by taking up wine for heart health, since the study is observational and relies on self-reported intake. The consistent message is that drinking less is better, with risks possibly higher in people with chronic or cardiovascular disease.
