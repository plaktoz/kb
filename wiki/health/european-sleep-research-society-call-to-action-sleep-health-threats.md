## Summary
A paper from the European Sleep Research Society's EDI Committee and Early Career Network warns that sleep health is a growing public health challenge in Europe. Climate change, built environments, healthcare inequality, sleep-tracking fixation and 24-hour culture combine to undermine rest. The authors call for coordinated policy action and offer recommendations organized in a PESTEL framework.

## Core Concepts
- [[European Sleep Research Society]]: source of the paper, led by [[Fran Pilkington-Cheney]] and [[Adrian Michalak]].
- [[Climate Change and Sleep]]: rising nighttime temperatures, heat waves, noise, artificial light and poor air quality disrupt sleep.
- [[Built Environment]]: dense areas and poor housing, energy poverty and displacement cause lasting sleep disturbance.
- [[Health Care Inequality]]: uneven access to medicine, diagnosis and trained sleep professionals.
- [[Orthosomnia]] and [[Sleepmaxxing]]: obsessive pursuit of "perfect" sleep metrics via wearables.
- [[Health Misinformation]]: social media shapes unrealistic expectations of sleep.
- [[24-Hour Culture]]: shift work, constant connectivity and screens erode recovery time.
- [[PESTEL Framework]]: political, economic, sociological, technological, legal, environmental recommendations.

## Key Takeaways
- **Perfect Storm**: Climate, technology, shift work, urban environments and misinformation compound each other.
- **Heat Risk**: Hotter nights may harm heart health, mental health and raise sleep apnea.
- **Care Gaps**: Access to sleep diagnosis and treatment varies widely across communities.
- **Tracker Caution**: Many sleep-tracking technologies need stronger evidence; not a substitute for professional advice.
- **Policy Call**: Treat sleep as a public health issue with multi-sector action.
- **Literacy**: Improve public education and counter misinformation.
- **Publication**: "A Call to Action" in the *Journal of Sleep Research* (2026).

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Interacting systemic pressures** — Individual outcomes like sleep are shaped by interacting environmental, social, and technological conditions, so individual awareness alone cannot fix them; coordinated structural action is needed.
→ explains: Perfect Storm, Policy Call, Care Gaps

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the paper's central claim about sleep health in Europe?
**A**: Sleep health is becoming a growing public health challenge and needs coordinated action from policymakers, health providers, educators, employers, planners and technology developers.

**Q2**: Which threats does the paper identify?
**A**: Climate change and the built environment, inequality in sleep care, fixation on sleep tracking and misinformation, and a 24-hour culture of shift work, connectivity and screens.

**Q3**: How do the authors propose turning this into action?
**A**: They call for better sleep health literacy, clearer regulation of working hours, digital tools and environmental exposures, and offer recommendations in a PESTEL framework in the appendix.
