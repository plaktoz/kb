## Summary

The brain retains the ability to form new neural pathways at any age — [[Neuroplasticity]] — and nine practical, lifestyle-based habits (mental and physical exercise, sleep, social connection, stress management, diet, and treating underlying health conditions) reliably support memory and cognitive performance, more effectively than commercial brain-training apps, which mostly improve only the specific task practiced.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Neuroplasticity]]** — the brain's capacity to form new neural pathways and connections throughout life, the basis for memory improvement at any age
- **[[Sleep and Memory Consolidation]]** — the deepest sleep stages are when the brain performs key memory-reinforcing activity; most adults need 7.5–9 hours nightly
- **[[Spaced Rehearsal]]** — reviewing learned material the same day and at increasing intervals thereafter, more effective than cramming
- **[[Hippocampus and Chronic Stress]]** — sustained stress destroys brain cells and damages the hippocampus, the region responsible for forming new memories

## Key Takeaways

- **Brain-training apps underdeliver**: evidence suggests they improve the trained task but not overall memory or intelligence
- **Physical exercise matters for the brain, not just the body**: it increases oxygen delivery and stimulates new neuronal connections
- **Social connection is a real cognitive intervention**: a Harvard study found people with the most active social lives had the slowest rate of memory decline
- **Underlying health conditions are common, overlooked causes** of memory decline: cardiovascular disease, diabetes, hormone imbalance, certain medications, untreated vision loss, and depression can all mimic or worsen memory loss
- **Practical encoding tips**: focused attention (about 8 seconds needed to encode information), multi-sensory engagement, relating new material to existing knowledge, and mnemonic devices
