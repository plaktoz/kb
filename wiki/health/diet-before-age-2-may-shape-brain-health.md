## Summary

A new study links lower sugar intake during the first 1,000 days of life — from conception to about age 2 — with lower risks of dementia and Alzheimer's disease decades later, pointing to an early developmental window with outsized long-term effects on brain health.

## Core Concepts

- **[[First 1,000 Days]]**: The developmental window from conception to roughly age 2, identified as a critical period during which sugar intake correlates with much later dementia/Alzheimer's risk.
- **Early-life nutritional programming**: The broader concept that dietary exposure in infancy can shape health trajectories decades into adulthood.

## Key Takeaways

- **Key finding**: Lower sugar intake during the first 1,000 days of life is linked to lower risk of dementia and Alzheimer's disease later in life.
- **Timeframe**: The critical window spans conception to approximately age 2.
