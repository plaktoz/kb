## Summary

The University of Auckland's Liggins Institute has launched a $25 million fundraising campaign — "In our hands: A healthy start for a healthy life" — to accelerate six research moonshots in maternal and child health. Building on 25 years of breakthroughs (including steroid therapy for preterm lung development and dextrose gel for neonatal hypoglycaemia), the campaign opens with $12 million already pledged. The six moonshots span preterm birth reduction, personalised neonatal nutrition, rare-disease genomics, living medicines from the [[Gut Microbiome]], AI-powered digital twins, and clinician-scientist training pipelines.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Preterm Birth]]** — One in 13 New Zealand babies is born prematurely (~4,500 families/year); the [[Carosika Collaborative]] is translating discoveries into nationwide clinical practice to cut this rate.
- **Personalised Neonatal Nutrition** — Researchers are developing non-invasive real-time [[Biomarkers]] to monitor individual infant growth and response to nutrition, plus [[Breast Milk]]-derived cell therapies.
- **[[Genomic Medicine]] and Rapid Genetic Diagnosis** — The Institute's programme achieves ~70% diagnosis success in critically ill children; ambition is to move from precision diagnosis to precision treatment using AI and genomics.
- **Living Medicines** — [[Microbiome]]-based probiotic treatments targeting [[Metabolic Syndrome]], developed from bacteria shown to reduce disease markers in gut microbiome research.
- **[[Digital Twin]]s in Healthcare** — AI-powered digital twins of hospitals and individual patients to simulate crises, predict illness, and guide personalised care.
- **Clinician-Scientist Pipeline** — The Clinical Research Internship Programme trains medical students early to translate clinical problems into research breakthroughs.
- **[[Professor Justin O'Sullivan]]** — Current Liggins Institute director leading the campaign.
- **[[Professor Sir Graham Liggins]]** — Pioneered steroid therapy for preterm lung development, saving millions of babies.

## Key Takeaways

- **$25M Campaign**: Launches with $12M already committed from philanthropic donors.
- **Six Moonshots**: Preterm birth, personalised nutrition, rare-disease paths, living medicines, digital healthcare, clinician scientists.
- **Preterm Birth Scale**: 1 in 13 NZ babies born prematurely; ~4,500 families affected annually.
- **Genomics Success Rate**: Rapid genetic diagnosis achieves ~70% success in critically ill children.
- **Living Medicines**: Gut microbiome bacteria identified as candidates for probiotic metabolic-syndrome therapies.
- **Digital Twins**: AI models of hospitals and children to simulate, predict, and personalise care.
- **Legacy**: Steroids for preterm lungs and dextrose gel for neonatal hypoglycaemia already revolutionised neonatal care.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Moonshot Thinking]]**: By framing each research area as a bold, independently scoped moonshot rather than incremental project, the campaign structures ambition — ensuring each strand has a clear problem, a measurable goal, and its own funding story, rather than competing for a single undifferentiated pot.
- **[[Translation Gap]]**: The explicit mission to move discoveries "from laboratory to families and into clinical practice" names the classic valley of death between bench research and patient benefit — the campaign's design (linking philanthropic capital directly to clinical-scale programmes) is a deliberate mechanism to close it.
