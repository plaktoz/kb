## Summary

NBA star Stephen Curry revealed his [[Keratoconus]] diagnosis — a progressive condition where the cornea thins and bulges into a cone shape, causing blurred vision — which went undetected until an outside observer noticed him squinting on TV. Treatment with corrective lenses transformed his vision, and he now advocates for early screening through a national awareness campaign. The article highlights that KC disproportionately affects Black and Hispanic communities and can be halted with corneal cross-linking therapy if caught early.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Keratoconus]] (KC)**: Progressive eye disease where the cornea thins and takes on an irregular cone shape, leading to blurry or distorted vision, light sensitivity, and frequent prescription changes.
- **[[Stephen Curry]]**: Twelve-time NBA All-Star diagnosed with KC at age 29 after Warriors GM Bob Myers noticed him squinting on a broadcast.
- **Corrective Lenses**: Rigid gas permeable (RGP) or scleral contact lenses are first-line management for KC symptoms.
- **[[Corneal Cross-Linking Therapy]]**: A procedure used to slow or halt the progression of keratoconus by strengthening corneal tissue.
- **Glaukos "Could it be KC?" Campaign**: Awareness initiative Curry partnered with to educate the public, particularly Black and Hispanic communities who are disproportionately affected.
- **[[Health Disparities]]**: KC has higher prevalence in Black and Hispanic communities, underlining the importance of targeted screening outreach.

## Key Takeaways

- KC is typically diagnosed in adolescence; symptoms can worsen silently without treatment.
- Curry lived with distorted vision for years, normalizing it without realizing it was abnormal.
- A casual text from an outsider, not a medical professional, triggered the diagnosis.
- Corrective lenses gave Curry an immediate "whole new world" improvement in vision.
- Biannual eye screenings are now part of Curry's routine post-diagnosis.
- KC disproportionately affects Black and Hispanic communities — targeted outreach matters.
- Curry introduced a children's book, *Eye on the Ball*, to make eye health accessible for youth.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Adaptation vs. Optimization]]**: Curry adapted to degraded vision rather than seeking its root cause — a reminder that humans optimize for continuity rather than investigating gradual decline, making slow-onset conditions easy to miss.
- **[[Signal Detection Theory]]**: The diagnosis hinged on an external observer (Bob Myers) detecting an anomalous signal (squinting) that Curry himself had normalized — illustrating how a fresh perspective can surface what self-monitoring misses.

**A**: Keratoconus is a progressive corneal condition causing blurred and distorted vision; Curry simply adapted to the deteriorating sight over time and considered it normal.

**A**: Rigid gas permeable or scleral contact lenses manage symptoms, while corneal cross-linking therapy can slow progression; Curry started with corrective lenses and described the result as transformative.
