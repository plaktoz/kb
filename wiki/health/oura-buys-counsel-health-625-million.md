## Summary
Smart ring maker Oura is buying AI primary-care start-up Counsel Health for $625 million in cash, its biggest step from sleep tracker to health care provider. The deal comes weeks after Oura postponed its IPO and alongside strong preliminary results, while questions remain about medical claims, privacy and non-hardware revenue.

## Core Concepts
- [[Oura]]: smart ring maker; about 80% of revenue from rings, 89% gross margin on subscriptions.
- [[Counsel Health]]: AI-first primary care practice pairing a medical chatbot with board-certified physicians.
- [[Muthu Alagappan]]: Counsel founder and CEO, joining Oura.
- [[Tom Hale]]: Oura CEO promising "a 24-7 doctor in your pocket."
- [[Wearables]] moving into care: rivals include [[Whoop]], Samsung Galaxy Ring, Apple Watch.
- [[AI in Healthcare]]: competitors include [[OpenAI]] (ChatGPT Health, Torch) and Amazon One Medical.
- [[GLP-1]] services: GLP-1 Insights, LillyDirect partnership and MD Live telehealth.

## Key Takeaways
- **Deal Terms**: $625 million cash, closing before year-end, over 17 times Counsel's $36 million raised.
- **Counsel Model**: Free AI chat; $29 for seven-day physician visit; $300 annual membership.
- **Integration**: Counsel has been in the Oura app since June, using ring data with consent.
- **Oura Results**: Q3 revenue up 153% to $533 million; 2026 revenue about $1.75 billion.
- **Scale**: More than six million paying members; IPO postponed in late September.
- **Competition**: Whoop added clinician video visits; OpenAI and Amazon are active in health.
- **Regulatory Tension**: Oura says the ring is not a medical device, yet promises a doctor.
- **Open Risks**: Class action over sleep-stage accuracy; researchers flag privacy and EU AI Act risk.
- **Revenue Question**: Unclear whether health services can earn revenue independent of ring sales.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Oura doing and why does it matter?
**A**: Oura is acquiring Counsel Health for $625 million cash, moving from sleep tracking toward delivering care with AI-plus-physician primary care. It is its biggest step yet into health care provision.

**Q2**: What financial context surrounds the deal?
**A**: Oura's Q3 revenue rose 153% to $533 million with a 16% adjusted EBITDA margin, and it has over six million paying members. This follows a postponed IPO that would have valued it at about $14 billion.

**Q3**: What doubts does the article raise about the strategy?
**A**: The ring is officially not a medical device, a lawsuit questions sleep-stage accuracy, researchers flag privacy risks, and it is unclear whether health services can generate revenue independent of ring sales.
