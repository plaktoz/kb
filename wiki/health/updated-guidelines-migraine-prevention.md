## Summary

The American Academy of Neurology and American Headache Society jointly updated their migraine prevention guidelines in 2026, replacing the 2012 recommendations to reflect newer therapies targeting the CGRP pathway. The updated framework covers both episodic and chronic migraine, stratifies recommendations by patient profile, and encourages an individualized treatment approach rather than a one-size-fits-all protocol. The guidelines aim to address widespread undertreatment, with migraine affecting roughly 40 million Americans.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Migraine Prevention]]**: Preventive therapy is recommended for adults with 4+ migraine days/month or significant disability, with treatment decisions individualized by evidence strength, tolerability, and patient circumstances.
- **[[CGRP Pathway]]**: The calcitonin gene-related peptide pathway is central to the updated guidelines, as newer drugs targeting it were unavailable during the 2012 recommendations.
- **[[Chronic Migraine]]**: Defined as headaches on 15+ days/month for over 3 months, with migraine features on at least 8 of those days — a key stratification point in the new framework.
- **[[American Academy of Neurology]]** and **[[American Headache Society]]**: Joint authors of the updated guidelines, also endorsed by the American Academy of Family Physicians.
- **[[Headache Diary]]**: Patient-centered monitoring tool encouraged by clinicians to evaluate therapy effectiveness; once-per-week tracking is highlighted as a threshold for migraine-related disability risk.
- **[[Medication Overuse Headache]]**: Explicitly addressed in the new guidelines, which account for acute medication overuse when stratifying treatment recommendations.

## Key Takeaways

- **Updated Framework**: 2026 guidelines replace 2012 recommendations, driven by new CGRP-targeting therapies.
- **Treatment Threshold**: Discuss prevention with patients having 4+ migraine or moderate-to-severe headache days/month.
- **Chronic Migraine Definition**: 15+ headache days/month for 3+ months, with 8+ days having migraine features.
- **Individualized Care**: Weigh evidence strength, side effects, cost, administration route, and comorbidities.
- **Special Populations**: Guidelines cover pregnant individuals and those with comorbidities as distinct subgroups.
- **Monitoring**: Reassess patients after treatment initiation within specified timeframes; encourage headache diary.
- **Undertreatment Gap**: Millions with migraine in the U.S. do not receive adequate care; guidelines aim to close this gap.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the main reason the 2026 migraine prevention guidelines replace the 2012 version?
**A**: The major update reflects the availability of newer therapies — particularly those targeting the CGRP pathway — that did not exist when the 2012 guidelines were issued.

**Q2**: According to the new guidelines, at what point should clinicians discuss preventive therapy with migraine patients?
**A**: Preventive therapy discussions are recommended for adults with 4 or more migraine days per month, 4 or more moderate-to-severe headache days per month, or migraine that significantly disrupts work or daily life.

**Q3**: How do the updated guidelines recommend monitoring treatment effectiveness?
**A**: Clinicians should reassess patients after starting treatment within specified timeframes, and keeping a headache diary is encouraged — once-per-week tracking is highlighted as a meaningful threshold for evaluating migraine-related disability risk.
