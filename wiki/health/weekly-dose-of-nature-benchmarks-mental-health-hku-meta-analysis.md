## Summary

A University of Hong Kong meta-analysis of research from 1980 to 2025 sets the first systematic weekly benchmarks for green space exposure and mental health. The dose-response relationship is nonlinear: benefits rise rapidly at low exposure, then taper off as visits grow more frequent or longer. Modest weekly contact with nature is therefore associated with meaningful benefits.

## Core Concepts

- [[Dose-of-Nature Framework]]: evaluates green space exposure on four dimensions: intensity, frequency, duration and accessibility.
- [[Dose-Response Relationship]]: curves across most reviewed studies follow a remarkably consistent pattern, per [[Bin Jiang]].
- [[Diminishing Returns]]: early, manageable exposure gives the biggest gains; further visits add smaller increments.
- [[Green Space and Mental Health]]: outcomes studied include stress, anxiety, depression and mood.
- [[Greenness Intensity]]: earlier team work in *Nature Cities*; its benefit pattern differs from frequency and duration.
- [[Urban Planning and Green Space Accessibility]]: green space access as a modifiable determinant of mental health.
- Researchers quoted: [[Bin Jiang]], [[Chris Webster]], [[Gunter Schumann]], [[Jiali Li]].

## Key Takeaways

- **Scope**: Meta-analysis of evidence published 1980-2025 on weekly visit frequency and cumulative duration.
- **Nonlinear Curve**: Benefits rise rapidly at low exposure, then taper off.
- **Low Threshold**: One hour or one visit per week reaches half the modeled maximum response.
- **Reading the Numbers**: Reference points come from separate models, not a combined schedule.
- **Duration Meaning**: Cumulative weekly time in green space, not the length of one visit.
- **Percent Meaning**: Share of estimated maximum benefit, not clinical symptom improvement.
- **Limits**: Population averages from observational studies; complement, not replace, clinical treatment.
- **Accessibility Gap**: Evidence on accessibility remains limited; quality and personal circumstances need research.
- **Urban Design**: Shaded seating, continuous paths and maintained vegetation encourage regular use.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the HKU study?
**A**: Even modest weekly contact with green space is associated with meaningful mental health benefits, with gains rising rapidly at low exposure and tapering off at higher levels.

**Q2**: What does the study say about the size of the minimum effective dose, and how should the percentages be read?
**A**: One hour or one visit per week already reaches half of the modeled maximum response. The percentages represent the share of the model's estimated maximum benefit, not a direct improvement in clinical symptoms.

**Q3**: How can individuals and urban planners apply the findings?
**A**: Individuals can start with a sustainable routine such as a weekly park walk and adjust it to their needs. Planners should build accessible, inviting green spaces and evaluate how actively residents engage with them, not just total vegetation coverage.
