## Summary

[[Maria Shriver]], founder of the [[Women's Alzheimer's Movement]] at Cleveland Clinic, has partnered with [[Eli Lilly]]'s Brain Health Matters campaign to raise awareness that women face nearly twice the [[Alzheimer's Disease]] risk as men. Shriver argues that midlife — even the 30s and 40s — is the critical window for intervention because [[Alzheimer's Disease]] pathology begins in the brain more than 20 years before symptoms appear. Up to 45% of dementia risk may be preventable through modifiable lifestyle factors, per new WHO guidelines.

## Core Concepts

- [[Women's Alzheimer's Movement]] — advocacy organization founded by Shriver at Cleveland Clinic focused on women's elevated Alzheimer's risk
- [[Alzheimer's Disease]] — neurodegenerative disease; women face nearly double the lifetime risk of men
- [[Midlife Brain Health]] — the 20-year pre-symptom window makes ages 30–50 the prime prevention period
- [[ApoE4 Gene]] — most studied genetic risk factor for late-onset Alzheimer's; two copies significantly raise risk
- [[Modifiable Risk Factors]] — lifestyle factors (exercise, sleep, diet, cognition, social engagement) that account for up to 45% of preventable dementia risk per WHO
- [[FDA Blood Tests for Alzheimer's]] — four new FDA-cleared blood tests now aid early diagnosis; three new treatments approved
- [[Eli Lilly]] — pharma partner on the Brain Health Matters awareness campaign

## Key Takeaways

- **Women's double risk**: Women face nearly twice the Alzheimer's risk compared to men.
- **Early pathology window**: Brain changes begin 20+ years before any symptoms appear.
- **Midlife is the intervention window**: Brain health habits in 30s–40s matter most.
- **45% preventable**: WHO guidelines link nearly half of dementia risk to modifiable lifestyle factors.
- **Genetic risk**: Over 100 genetic variants linked to late-onset Alzheimer's; ApoE4 is the key gene.
- **New diagnostics**: Four FDA-cleared blood tests now available for earlier detection.
- **Shriver's own habits**: Daily meditation, exercise, and Mahjong for social and cognitive stimulation.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Prevention Window]]**: The 20-year pre-symptom lead time means lifestyle interventions in midlife are acting on a disease that has already started — making early, consistent habits structurally more valuable than late-stage treatment.
- **[[Compounding Returns]]**: Lifestyle habits like exercise and sleep compound protective effects over decades, much like financial compounding — the earlier they start, the greater the neurological dividend by the time risk peaks.
