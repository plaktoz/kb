## Summary

A fast-growing US [[Cyclosporiasis]] outbreak has spread to at least 47 states and sickened thousands (led overwhelmingly by Michigan), while an initial lettuce-recall theory was retracted by the FDA as a false positive, leaving the actual contamination source still unidentified as of this report.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Cyclosporiasis]]**: An infection caused by the microscopic parasite *Cyclospora*, spread via food or water contaminated with feces; symptoms (watery/explosive diarrhea, cramping, nausea, bloating) begin within about 7 days of exposure and can persist for weeks untreated.
- **[[False Positive Contamination Trace]]**: The FDA initially linked the outbreak to Taylor Farms iceberg lettuce (used by some Taco Bell locations) and the supplier issued a voluntary recall, but the FDA later determined the lab result was a false positive due to the technical difficulty of detecting Cyclospora — illustrating how outbreak source-tracing can mislead even after a recall is issued.
- **[[Foodborne Outbreak Surveillance]]**: The CDC routinely tracks cyclosporiasis (which typically rises each spring/summer) and coordinates with state health departments; Michigan's case count jumped from a typical ~50/year baseline to 14,277 within about seven weeks, the single largest cluster nationally.

## Key Takeaways

- At least 47 states, DC, and Puerto Rico have reported cases; a separate 1,681 lab-confirmed cases were travel-related, acquired outside the US.
- Two deaths in Michigan were linked to the outbreak, both in patients with significant pre-existing conditions worsened by dehydration; both illnesses predated the (later-retracted) lettuce recall.
- Person-to-person transmission is unlikely — the parasite requires 1–2 weeks outside the body to become infectious after passing in a bowel movement.
- Prevention centers on food handling: washing hands and produce, scrubbing firm fruit/vegetables, cutting away damaged areas, and refrigerating cut produce within 2 hours.
- Treatment is typically antibiotics; the primary danger is dehydration, especially for the very young, very old, chronically ill, or immunocompromised.

**Q3**: What practical steps reduce individual risk during a produce-linked outbreak like this one?
**A**: Wash hands before and after handling raw produce, rinse all fruits and vegetables under running water before cutting or eating, scrub firm produce with a clean brush, and refrigerate cut or cooked produce within two hours.
