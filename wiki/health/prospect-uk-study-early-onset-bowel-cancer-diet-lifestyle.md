## Summary

PROSPECT-UK is a new observational study, funded by Cancer Research UK through Cancer Grand Challenges, that aims to recruit 250,000 UK adults aged 18 to 50. It will look at how diet, lifestyle and environment might contribute to the global rise in early-onset bowel cancer. The study is new in collecting brand-level data on the foods people actually eat, which lets researchers look past broad labels like "ultra-processed". It will link this data to long-term electronic health records.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Early-Onset Bowel Cancer]]**: [[Colorectal Cancer]] diagnosed before age 50. It is still rare (more than 9 in 10 cases occur in people over 50), but incidence is rising worldwide while rates stabilise or fall in older groups.
- **[[PROSPECT-UK]]**: Led by [[King's College London]] in collaboration with [[ZOE]]. Participants complete short online questionnaires on diet, lifestyle, environment and health, and consent to link their electronic health records. A small subgroup will also provide detailed diet diaries plus blood and stool samples.
- **[[Cancer Grand Challenges]]** / **PROSPECT team**: The wider global, interdisciplinary research group. It is funded by Cancer Research UK, the US [[National Cancer Institute]], The Bowelbabe Fund and France's INCa, and studies both population-level risk patterns and changes inside the body and its cells.
- **[[Ultra-Processed Foods]]**: About 60% of UK calorie intake comes from highly processed foods. These differ widely in ingredients, processing methods and nutritional composition, even within the same food category, so PROSPECT-UK collects data at brand level.
- **[[Gut Microbiome]]**: PROSPECT-UK joins other PROSPECT work in studying the gut's bacteria, fungi and viruses, which may raise or lower cancer risk.
- **[[Inflammatory Memory]]**: A PROSPECT project at Harvard found signs that cells' "memories" of inflammation could influence how bowel cancer develops. This could open up new prevention strategies.
- **[[Biological Ageing]]**: Other PROSPECT sub-teams have used existing population data to explore links between early-onset bowel cancer and changes in how younger generations age.
- **People**: [[Sarah Berry]] (PROSPECT-UK lead researcher, King's College London), [[Rosiered Brownson-Smith]] (research associate, King's College London), [[Andrew Chan]] (one of PROSPECT's two co-leads).

## Key Takeaways

- **Recruitment Target**: 250,000 UK participants aged 18 to 50.
- **Global Trend**: Early-onset rates rose in 27 of 50 countries studied (2024 PROSPECT analysis).
- **England Outlier**: Fourth-fastest rise globally from 2007 to 2017, the fastest in Europe.
- **UK Incidence**: Rates in 25 to 49-year-olds up more than 50% since the early 1990s.
- **Why So Large**: The disease is rare in young people, so spotting patterns needs a huge cohort.
- **Brand-Level Diet Data**: The first study to capture the specific food items people eat.
- **Observational Limits**: Can find associations and likely culprits, but cannot prove causes on its own.
- **Out of Scope**: Genetics and biology may also contribute, but PROSPECT-UK doesn't study them.
- **No Proven Driver Yet**: Evidence doesn't yet single out any one diet or lifestyle factor.
- **General Risk Reducers**: More fibre and whole foods, less red and processed meat, staying active, a healthy weight, limited alcohol and no smoking.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Correlation vs Causation]]**: PROSPECT-UK is an observational study. It can link exposures to outcomes but cannot prove what causes them, so its role is to narrow down suspects for later research that can test causes.
- **[[Law of Large Numbers]]**: Early-onset bowel cancer is rare, so only a very large cohort produces enough cases to separate real risk patterns from noise. That is why the study needs 250,000 people.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is PROSPECT-UK trying to find out, and how?
**A**: It aims to identify which diet, lifestyle and environmental changes might be driving the rise in bowel cancer among under-50s. It will recruit 250,000 UK adults aged 18 to 50 and link their questionnaire data to electronic health records.

**Q2**: What sets PROSPECT-UK's diet data apart from earlier research on ultra-processed foods?
**A**: It collects detailed, brand-level information on the specific foods people eat. This matters because the processed foods behind about 60% of UK calorie intake differ substantially in ingredients, processing and nutrition, even within the same category.

**Q3**: Until PROSPECT-UK reports, what can individuals reasonably do about bowel cancer risk?
**A**: No single factor has been proven to drive the early-onset rise. General risk-reducing habits still apply: eat more fibre, fruit, vegetables and whole foods, eat less red and processed meat, stay active, keep a healthy weight, limit alcohol and don't smoke.
