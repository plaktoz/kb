## Summary
A three-year study of 6,537 Australian adolescents found that higher fruit intake was linked to lower risk of meeting screening thresholds for depression and anxiety, while higher sugar-sweetened beverage (SSB) intake was linked to greater risk. Vegetable intake showed no significant association. The observational design and self-reported diet mean causation cannot be established.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Common Mental Disorders]]**: depression and anxiety defined by screening thresholds ([[PHQ-A]], [[PROMIS Anxiety-Pediatric]]), not clinical diagnoses.
- **[[Sugar-Sweetened Beverages]]**: sports drinks, soft drinks, and cordials counted in cups per day.
- **[[Fruit and Vegetable Intake]]**: servings of 150 g fruit and 75 g vegetables per day.
- **[[Health4Life Trial]]**: the school-based cohort across 71 schools in New South Wales, Queensland, and Western Australia.
- **[[Longitudinal Cohort Study]]**: surveys at baseline (Grade 7) and after one, two, and three years.
- **[[Inflammation]] and [[Cortisol]]**: proposed but untested pathways linking SSBs to mood.

## Key Takeaways
- **Fruit**: Each extra daily serving linked to 13% lower depression and 7% lower anxiety risk.
- **SSBs**: Higher intake linked to greater risk in both sexes, stronger for male anxiety.
- **Vegetables**: No statistically significant association in adjusted models.
- **Consistency**: SSB associations were more consistent than fruit associations.
- **Fragile Results**: Complete-case analyses lost some fruit associations.
- **Mechanism Untested**: Inflammatory biomarkers and cortisol are hypotheses only.
- **Limits**: Self-reported diet and unmeasured confounding prevent causal claims.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Association is not causation** — Observational links between diet and symptoms cannot show that changing diet prevents disorders, since unmeasured confounders and reporting error remain. · *model:* [[Correlation vs Causation]]
→ explains: Limits, Fragile Results

**T2. More exposure, more risk** — Risk shifted incrementally with each additional serving, the pattern expected of a graded exposure effect. · *model:* [[Dose-Response Relationship]]
→ explains: Fruit, SSBs

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the study find about diet and adolescent mental health?
**A**: Adolescents who ate more fruit and drank fewer sugar-sweetened beverages were less likely to screen positive for depression and anxiety over three years.

**Q2**: How large was the fruit association, and how was it measured?
**A**: Each additional daily serving (150 g) was linked to 13% lower depression and 7% lower anxiety risk, using self-reported intake across 6,537 adolescents with validated symptom scales.

**Q3**: What are the implications and caveats for prevention?
**A**: If confirmed, school programs and policy could target dietary habits as part of prevention, but the observational design, self-reporting, and confounding mean the findings cannot show dietary change prevents disorders.
