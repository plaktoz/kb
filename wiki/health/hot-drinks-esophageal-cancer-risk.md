## Summary

A large UK study of nearly 1 million adults found that drinking beverages at "very hot" temperatures triples the risk of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), while drinking 6+ cups daily raises SCC risk by 71%. The WHO's IARC classifies drinks above 65°C (149°F) as "probably carcinogenic," and researchers estimate that lowering drink temperature could prevent roughly 440 UK esophageal cancer cases annually.

## Core Concepts

- [[Esophageal Squamous Cell Carcinoma]] — cancer type most strongly linked to heat exposure; distinct from adenocarcinoma, which showed no significant temperature-related risk increase
- [[Thermal Injury and Chronic Inflammation]] — repeated heat exposure to the esophageal lining triggers chronic inflammation, a known driver of carcinogenesis; the esophagus has fewer pain receptors than the mouth, masking the ongoing damage
- [[IARC Carcinogenicity Classification]] — the WHO's International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies beverages above 65°C as "probably carcinogenic to humans" (Group 2A)
- [[Million Women Study]] and [[UK Biobank]] — large prospective cohorts used to track beverage habits and cancer outcomes over a decade-plus follow-up

## Key Takeaways

- **Risk magnitude**: "Very hot" drinkers have 3x the SCC risk versus "warm" drinkers (pooled RR: 3.17).
- **Volume effect**: Drinking 6+ cups daily correlates with ~71% higher SCC risk.
- **Adenocarcinoma unaffected**: Temperature had no significant impact on the other main esophageal cancer subtype.
- **Prevention estimate**: ~14% of UK SCC cases (~440/year) could be prevented by cooling drinks before sipping.
- **Practical threshold**: Allow hot drinks to cool 3–5 minutes; avoid if the liquid would burn your tongue.
- **Competing risk factors**: Tobacco, heavy alcohol, obesity, and chronic acid reflux remain larger overall drivers of esophageal cancer.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Dose-Response Relationship]]**: Both temperature intensity ("very hot" vs. "hot" vs. "warm") and frequency (6+ cups/day) independently scaled risk — a hallmark of causal mechanisms rather than confounding, strengthening the case that thermal injury drives SCC.
- **[[Absence of Signal as Signal]]**: Adenocarcinoma showed no temperature link, which helps isolate the mechanism to mucosal heat damage rather than a systemic metabolic effect — ruling out hypotheses and pointing toward localized cellular injury.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core finding linking hot beverages to esophageal cancer?
**A**: Drinking "very hot" beverages triples the risk of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma compared to drinking "warm" beverages, according to a study of nearly 1 million UK adults.

**Q2**: Why does the esophagus sustain damage from hot drinks that people may not notice?
**A**: The esophagus has fewer pain receptors than the mouth, so the repeated thermal injury that causes chronic inflammation — a cancer precursor — often goes unfelt.

**Q3**: What practical step does the evidence recommend, and how meaningful is the population-level impact?
**A**: Allowing hot drinks to cool 3–5 minutes before drinking could prevent roughly 440 esophageal cancer cases per year in the UK alone, representing about 14% of all SCC cases there.
