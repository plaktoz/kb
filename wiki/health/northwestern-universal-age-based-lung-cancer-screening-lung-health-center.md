## Summary
Northwestern Medicine researchers are studying universal, age-based lung cancer screening, similar to breast and colorectal programs, because current criteria miss most patients. In its first year, the Lung Health Center screened nearly 1,000 people and found six early-stage cancers, including two stage 1 cases in never-smokers. The team recommends screening adults aged 40 to 85.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Lung Cancer Screening]]**: Current [[USPSTF]] guidelines cover ages 50 to 80 with at least a 20 pack-year smoking history.
- **[[Low-Dose CT Scan]]**: The screening test used to detect nodules early.
- **[[Northwestern Medicine Lung Health Center]]**: A research program at the Canning Thoracic Institute aiming to screen 10,000 people over five years.
- **Universal age-based screening**: Proposed for ages 40 to 85, modeled on breast and colorectal programs.
- **[[Early Detection]]**: Finding cancer before symptoms, enabling surgery and sometimes same-day discharge.
- **People: [[Ankit Bharat]]**, [[Andrew Arndt]], [[Benjamin Seides]], and patient Cathy Hartmann.

## Key Takeaways
- **Eligibility Gap**: Only 35% of treated lung cancer patients would have met USPSTF criteria (JAMA, Nov 2025).
- **Who Is Missed**: Two-thirds of those ineligible were women and never-smokers.
- **First-Year Results**: Nearly 1,000 screened; 54% had nodules or findings warranting surveillance.
- **Cancers Found**: Six early-stage cancers diagnosed and treated, two in never-smokers.
- **Comparable Yield**: Detection rate is comparable to mammography programs (5.4 cancers per 800).
- **Smoking History**: 40% had smoking history but were ineligible under current criteria.
- **Proposed Expansion**: Ages 40 to 85 could detect 94% of cases and prevent over 26,000 deaths yearly.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. A screening program only finds what its eligibility criteria let in** — If the criteria exclude a large share of people who develop the disease, those cases are detected late regardless of test quality. Detection yield depends on who is screened.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of the Northwestern Medicine research?
**A**: Current lung cancer screening criteria miss most patients, so a universal age-based program (ages 40 to 85), like those for breast and colorectal cancer, should be considered.

**Q2**: What evidence shows current criteria are too narrow?
**A**: A JAMA study found only 35% of treated lung cancer patients would have qualified under USPSTF criteria, and two-thirds of those who would not were women and never-smokers.

**Q3**: What does the first-year Lung Health Center experience imply?
**A**: Broader screening can yield cancers at a rate comparable to established programs, including stage 1 cancers in never-smokers who would otherwise have waited for symptoms.
