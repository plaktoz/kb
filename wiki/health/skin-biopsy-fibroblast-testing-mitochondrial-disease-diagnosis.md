## Summary

A large international study led by Children's Hospital Colorado and CU Anschutz, published in *EMBO Molecular Medicine*, finds that functional testing of fibroblasts grown from a small skin biopsy can often diagnose primary mitochondrial disease. In many cases this removes the need for more invasive muscle or liver biopsies. Functional testing fills a gap left by genetic testing, which finds a definitive cause in only about 50–65% of cases and often returns variants of uncertain significance.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Mitochondrial Disease]]**: A group of genetic disorders that impair the body's ability to produce energy. They affect about 1 in 5,000 people and can involve multiple organs.
- **Limits of genetic testing**: [[Whole-Genome Sequencing]] is usually the first step, but it often returns inconclusive [[Variants of Uncertain Significance]].
- **[[Functional Diagnostic Testing]]**: Tests that measure how well mitochondria perform inside cells, which helps separate affected from unaffected patients.
- **[[Fibroblasts]]**: Cells cultured from a small skin biopsy. They can often supply enough diagnostic information on their own, so a [[Muscle Biopsy]] or liver biopsy may not be needed.
- **Researchers**: Led by [[Johan Van Hove]] and [[Marisa W. Friederich]] of the Children's Colorado Mitochondrial Laboratory.
- **Study scale**: Samples came from more than 30 medical centers across North America, Europe, Australia, and New Zealand. The authors describe it as the first comprehensive evaluation of these tests' clinical performance.
- **Clinical translation**: Nearly all the tests are now offered by the Children's Colorado Mitochondrial Diagnostic Laboratory, one of North America's largest providers of this testing.

## Key Takeaways

- **Prevalence**: Mitochondrial diseases affect roughly 1 in 5,000 people.
- **Genetic gap**: Genetic testing finds a definitive cause in only about 50–65% of cases.
- **Less invasive**: Skin-biopsy fibroblasts can often replace muscle or liver biopsies.
- **Scale**: Samples came from more than 30 centers on four continents.
- **Complementary role**: Functional testing helps resolve inconclusive genetic results.
- **Publication**: Van Hove et al., *EMBO Molecular Medicine* (2026), DOI 10.1038/s44321-026-00497-3.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the study's main finding?
**A**: Comprehensive functional testing of fibroblasts from a small skin biopsy can often diagnose mitochondrial disease. That means more invasive muscle or liver biopsies may not be needed in many cases.

**Q2**: Why is functional testing needed alongside genetic testing?
**A**: Genetic testing finds a definitive cause in only about 50–65% of cases and often returns variants of uncertain significance. Functional tests measure how mitochondria actually perform inside cells.

**Q3**: What does this mean for patients and families?
**A**: Getting critical diagnostic information from a simple skin biopsy makes diagnosis more accessible and less burdensome. Patients may avoid invasive procedures and get clearer answers when genetic results are inconclusive.
