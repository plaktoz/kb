## Summary
A two-year double-blind trial from Keck Medicine of USC (eBioMedicine) gave 365 at-risk adults aged 55–80 either 2,000 mg DHA daily or a placebo. DHA reached the brain, rising 17% in cerebrospinal fluid, yet it produced no cognitive advantage and did not prevent hippocampal shrinkage. The researchers say this doesn't make omega-3s or fish unimportant, and that overall healthy habits remain the best tool for reducing Alzheimer's risk.

## Core Concepts
- [[Fish Oil Supplements]]: A market exceeding $1 billion yearly in the US for omega-3 products.
- [[Docosahexaenoic Acid (DHA)]]: Omega-3 fatty acid that is a major component of brain cell membranes.
- [[APOE4]]: Strongest known genetic risk factor for late-onset Alzheimer's; carried by 47% of participants.
- [[Cerebrospinal Fluid]]: Used to confirm DHA reached the central nervous system (+17% at six months).
- [[Hippocampal Shrinkage]]: Brain-imaging marker of memory-region decline; not prevented by supplements.
- [[Double-Blind Placebo-Controlled Trial]]: Design that reduces bias in judging treatment effects.
- [[Mediterranean Diet]]: Possible context in which omega-3s work better than as an isolated supplement.
- [[Alzheimer's Disease Prevention]]: Researchers emphasise exercise, sleep and balanced diet.

## Key Takeaways
- **Null Result**: No memory, thinking or brain-shrinkage benefit after two years.
- **Target Engagement**: DHA in cerebrospinal fluid rose 17% on average.
- **Trial Design**: 365 adults aged 55–80, double-blind, 2,000 mg DHA daily.
- **High-Risk Group**: 47% carried APOE4; all rarely ate fish.
- **Not Anti-Omega-3**: Findings concern high-dose DHA supplements alone, not fish in the diet.
- **Next Question**: Whether diet pattern, health, genetics and age affect omega-3 use.
- **Recommended Approach**: Regular exercise, quality sleep and a balanced diet.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Hitting a biological target is not the same as improving the outcome that matters** — Raising brain DHA levels did not translate into better cognition or less hippocampal shrinkage, so the intermediate marker cannot stand in for clinical benefit. · *model:* [[Surrogate Endpoint Fallacy]]
→ explains: Target Engagement, Null Result

**T2. Nutrients may act through the whole pattern, not in isolation** — Researchers suspect omega-3s work best alongside other nutrients in a balanced diet and with overall health. · *model:* [[Reductionism]]
→ explains: Next Question, Not Anti-Omega-3

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the USC trial conclude about fish oil and brain health?
**A**: High-dose DHA supplements gave no measurable cognitive benefit or protection against hippocampal shrinkage in at-risk older adults over two years.

**Q2**: What evidence showed the supplement reached the brain yet still failed?
**A**: DHA in cerebrospinal fluid rose an average 17% after six months, but cognitive test scores and hippocampal size didn't differ from placebo.

**Q3**: What should people take from the result?
**A**: Don't rely on a single supplement to prevent Alzheimer's; the researchers advise regular exercise, quality sleep and a balanced diet. The findings don't imply that omega-3s or eating fish are unimportant.
