## Summary

A Mass General Brigham study of 131 patients found that as the number of modifiable cardiovascular risk factors (dyslipidemia, hypertension, diabetes, obesity, smoking) increased, so did the number of high-risk "vulnerable" coronary plaques, likely mediated by vascular inflammation. Non-modifiable factors like age and family history were linked to more stable plaque types instead, suggesting targeted risk-factor management could reduce heart attack risk.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Cardiovascular Disease]]** — condition under study via coronary plaque composition
- **[[Coronary Plaque]]** — specifically thin-cap fibroatheroma and lipid-rich "vulnerable" plaque types
- **[[Optical Coherence Tomography]]** — imaging technique used to analyze 534 plaques across 131 patients
- **[[Vascular Inflammation]]** — proposed mechanism linking modifiable risk factors to plaque vulnerability
- **[[Mass General Brigham]]** — institution behind the Heart and Vascular Institute study

## Key Takeaways

- **More modifiable risk factors** correlated with more vulnerable plaques.
- **Among patients with 4+ risk factors**, half of plaques were high-risk.
- **Non-modifiable factors** linked to stable, not vulnerable, plaque types.
- **Vascular inflammation** likely mediates the modifiable-risk-factor link.
