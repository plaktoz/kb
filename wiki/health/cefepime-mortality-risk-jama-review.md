## Summary

A review published in *JAMA Network Open* found that cefepime, a widely used cephalosporin antibiotic, is associated with an 18% higher mortality risk in adults compared with other [[β-Lactam Antibiotics]]. Across 11,726 cefepime recipients, 6.6% died versus 6.2% among those on other β-lactams — roughly 4 additional deaths per 1,000 patients. Despite this signal, experts note cefepime remains a critical option for severe, complex infections where broad-spectrum coverage is needed.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Cefepime]]** — a fourth-generation [[Cephalosporin]] antibiotic used for serious bacterial infections including febrile neutropenia, pneumonia, and meningitis.
- **[[β-Lactam Antibiotics]]** — the antibiotic class containing cefepime, penicillins, and carbapenems; a cornerstone of hospital-acquired infection treatment.
- **[[JAMA Network Open]]** — the peer-reviewed journal publishing this multi-national mortality review.
- **[[Antibiotic Stewardship]]** — prescribing antibiotics only when necessary to reduce resistance and minimize harm, emphasized by experts given cefepime's mortality signal.
- **[[Multi-Drug-Resistant Pathogens]]** — a growing global threat that makes judicious use of broad-spectrum antibiotics like cefepime especially important.
- **[[Febrile Neutropenia]]** — one of the primary indications for cefepime, showing ~19% elevated mortality risk in this review.

## Key Takeaways

- **Mortality signal**: Cefepime users had 18% higher mortality risk vs. other β-lactams in adults.
- **Absolute difference**: ~4 extra deaths per 1,000 patients (6.6% vs. 6.2%).
- **Children unaffected**: No significant mortality difference found in pediatric patients.
- **Condition-specific risks**: UTI (+33%), febrile neutropenia (+19%), severe bacterial infections (+19%), pneumonia (+11%), meningitis (+10%).
- **Still clinically viable**: Experts say cefepime remains a legitimate option for life-threatening broad-spectrum needs.
- **Caution on interpretation**: Varied ages, conditions, and subjective category labels complicate firm conclusions.
- **Stewardship imperative**: Antibiotics should only be prescribed when necessary given rising resistance.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Base Rate Neglect]]**: The absolute difference (4 deaths/1,000) is easily overshadowed by the relative 18% figure — clinicians must weigh both to make sound prescribing decisions rather than anchoring on the headline risk ratio.
- **[[Risk-Benefit Tradeoff]]**: Cefepime's broad-spectrum coverage addresses infections where alternatives may fail; the mortality signal doesn't eliminate its role but demands that the tradeoff be explicitly weighed against severity and patient condition.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core finding of the JAMA Network Open review on cefepime?
**A**: Adults taking cefepime had an 18% higher mortality risk compared to those on other β-lactam antibiotics, translating to roughly 4 additional deaths per 1,000 patients.

**Q2**: Which patient condition showed the highest elevated mortality risk associated with cefepime in this review?
**A**: Urinary tract infection showed the highest elevated mortality risk at approximately 33%, followed by febrile neutropenia and severe bacterial infections at ~19% each.

**Q3**: How should clinicians apply this mortality signal in practice?
**A**: The review authors recommend considering the mortality signal when framing cefepime's role in clinical practice guidance, while experts emphasize it remains viable for life-threatening infections requiring broad-spectrum coverage — judicious prescribing and antibiotic stewardship remain essential.
