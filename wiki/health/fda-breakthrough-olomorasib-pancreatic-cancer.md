## Summary
The FDA granted Breakthrough Therapy designation to Eli Lilly's [[Olomorasib]], a next-generation KRAS G12C inhibitor, as a monotherapy for adult patients with advanced [[Pancreatic Cancer]] carrying a KRAS G12C mutation who have already received prior systemic therapy — a genetic subgroup that has historically had very limited treatment options.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Olomorasib]]** — investigational, potent, highly selective next-generation [[KRAS G12C]] inhibitor from Eli Lilly
- **[[KRAS G12C Mutation]]** — a specific genetic driver mutation targetable by this class of inhibitors, identified via an FDA-approved companion test
- **[[Breakthrough Therapy Designation]]** — FDA pathway to expedite development/review of drugs showing early evidence of substantial improvement over existing therapies
- **[[LOXO-RAS-20001 Trial]]** — the open-label Phase 1/2 study (NCT04956640) providing the preliminary evidence behind this designation

## Key Takeaways
- **Difficult-to-treat population**: pancreatic cancer patients with KRAS G12C mutations face especially limited options once disease progresses.
- **Designation basis**: encouraging preliminary Phase 1/2 results in patients who had already received at least one prior systemic therapy.
- **Broader pipeline**: olomorasib is also being studied combined with pembrolizumab as a first-line treatment for KRAS G12C-mutant advanced non-small cell lung cancer (SUNRAY-01 trial).
- **Company statement**: Lilly Oncology's Jacob Van Naarden called it reflective of "the critical need for new treatment options" in this setting.

## 🃏 Review Questions
**Q1**: What did the FDA grant to olomorasib, and for which patient population?
**A**: Breakthrough Therapy designation, for adult patients with advanced pancreatic cancer bearing a KRAS G12C mutation who have received at least one prior systemic therapy.
**Q2**: What class of drug is olomorasib, and how is the eligible patient population identified?
**A**: It is a next-generation KRAS G12C inhibitor; eligible patients are identified by an FDA-approved test for the KRAS G12C mutation.
**Q3**: What trial provided the preliminary evidence supporting this designation?
**A**: The open-label, multicenter Phase 1/2 LOXO-RAS-20001 study (NCT04956640).
