## Summary

A modeling analysis presented at the [[American Heart Association]]'s Hypertension Scientific Sessions 2026 estimates that raising [[Hypertension]] control across 20 countries to the level of high-performing U.S. health systems (~80% of eligible patients) could prevent more than 518,000 cardiovascular deaths a year — more than any other preventive service studied. [[Statins]] for high cholesterol and glucose control in [[Diabetes]] were the next most effective strategies, and lower-middle-income countries had the largest gaps and opportunities. The findings are a preliminary, non-peer-reviewed abstract.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Hypertension]]**: The most prevalent and modifiable risk factor for [[Cardiovascular Disease]]; untreated, it damages blood vessels and leads to coronary artery disease, heart failure, enlarged left heart, and metabolic syndrome.
- **[[Non-Communicable Diseases]] (NCDs)**: Cause roughly three in five deaths globally, ~73% of them in low- or middle-income countries; CVD accounts for more than diabetes, cancers, or chronic respiratory illness.
- **Six preventive services modeled**: Blood pressure control, statin treatment when indicated, glucose control, and screening for colon, breast, and cervical cancer.
- **Study design**: Statistical models across the 20 countries with the most NCD deaths, using data from the [[Global Burden of Disease]] network, NCD Risk Factor Collaboration, [[WHO]] STEPwise, and WHO Cancer Screening databases; no low-income countries included.
- **Targets**: Blood pressure below 140/90 mm Hg (the AHA 2025 guideline threshold for starting medication; treated target is below 130/80), statins per the 2026 AHA/ACC dyslipidemia guideline, HbA1c below 7.0%, and USPSTF cancer screening intervals.
- **People**: [[Scott Pilla]] (Johns Hopkins) presented the study; [[Daniel W. Jones]], chair of the 2025 guideline committee, commented independently.
- **Limitations**: Limited preventive-care data for some countries, and no specific strategies offered for improving access.

## Key Takeaways

- **Top Lifesaver**: Hypertension control could prevent 518,476 CVD deaths annually.
- **Runners-Up**: Statins prevent 340,682 deaths; glucose control prevents 309,520 annually.
- **Cancer Screening**: Colon screening at 80% could prevent 206,767 deaths yearly.
- **Income Split**: BP control saves ~223,000 deaths each in upper- and lower-middle-income countries.
- **Control Gap**: Only about one in five hypertensive adults have controlled blood pressure.
- **Statin Gap**: Just 16% of people with high cholesterol use statins.
- **Screening Gap**: Only 14%–26% receive routinely indicated cancer screenings.
- **LMIC Opportunity**: Lower-middle-income countries show the lowest control and screening rates.
- **Preliminary**: Abstract not peer-reviewed; findings await full publication.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Preventable deaths scale with risk prevalence times the coverage gap** — The lives a preventive service can save depend on how common and harmful the risk factor it targets is and how far current coverage falls short of achievable coverage, so a highly prevalent, poorly controlled risk factor yields the largest gains.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the study's central finding about preventive services?
**A**: Among six preventive services modeled across 20 countries, improving blood pressure control would save the most lives, preventing more than 518,000 cardiovascular deaths annually.

**Q2**: How large is the current gap in hypertension control in the studied countries?
**A**: Only about one in five adults with hypertension had blood pressure below 140/90 mm Hg, versus a modeled target of roughly 80% as in high-performing U.S. health systems.

**Q3**: Where should health systems focus to reduce NCD deaths, according to this analysis?
**A**: Expanding hypertension control first, then statin use and glucose control, with lower-middle-income countries offering the largest opportunities given their low control and screening rates.
