## Summary
A Lancet Psychiatry network meta-analysis of 115 randomized trials (30,000+ people) found escitalopram, duloxetine and agomelatine associated with better sustained response than fluoxetine. The authors of this commentary stress the results are far from definitive: many trials had high risk of bias and few followed people beyond six months. Group averages also cannot say which drug will suit an individual.

## Core Concepts
- [[Antidepressants]]: [[Escitalopram]] (Lexapro), [[Duloxetine]] (Cymbalta) and [[Agomelatine]] (Valdoxan) compared against [[Fluoxetine]] (Prozac).
- [[Network Meta-Analysis]]: 115 [[Randomized Controlled Trials]]; the main analysis pooled 35 head-to-head trials.
- [[Treatment Acceptability]]: measured by trial drop-out rate, a limited proxy for patient experience.
- [[Risk of Bias]]: more than half of the main-analysis studies were rated high risk.
- [[Antidepressant Discontinuation]]: guidelines suggest continuing 6-12 months after response, then tapering gradually with a doctor.

## Key Takeaways
- **Headline Result**: Three drugs beat [[Fluoxetine]] on sustained response; two also had lower drop-out.
- **Weak Evidence Base**: Over half of main-analysis studies carried a high risk of bias.
- **Short "Long Term"**: Direct comparisons typically followed people about six months; only six ran longer.
- **Averages Not Individuals**: Group results can't predict which drug works best for one person.
- **Acceptability Proxy**: Drop-out rate misses side-effect trade-offs and withdrawal-driven continuation.
- **Practical Advice**: If your current antidepressant works well, this study is no reason to switch.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Group averages don't determine individual outcomes** — Results that hold on average across a population cannot tell you what will work best for any one member of it. · *model:* [[Flaw of Averages]]
→ explains: Averages Not Individuals, Practical Advice

**T2. Conclusions are only as reliable as the underlying studies** — Pooling many studies does not remove bias or sparse data in the inputs, so differences may reflect study flaws rather than real effects. · *model:* [[Garbage In, Garbage Out]]
→ explains: Weak Evidence Base, Short "Long Term"

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the new review find about long-term antidepressant effectiveness?
**A**: Escitalopram, duloxetine and agomelatine were associated with a greater likelihood of sustained response than fluoxetine, and escitalopram and duloxetine also had fewer drop-outs. The results are far from definitive.

**Q2**: Why are the findings less reliable than they first appear?
**A**: More than half of the studies in the main analysis had a high risk of bias, some drugs had very few studies, and comparisons typically followed people for only about six months.

**Q3**: How should someone already taking an antidepressant use these results?
**A**: If their medication is working well, the findings are not a reason to switch. Anyone considering starting, changing or stopping should discuss it with their doctor, since averages can't pick the best drug for an individual.
