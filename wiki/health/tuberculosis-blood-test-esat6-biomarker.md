## Summary

Researchers presenting at [[ADLM 2026]] found that a highly sensitive blood test measuring circulating [[ESAT-6]] — a marker produced directly by the tuberculosis bacterium — can accurately distinguish active TB from latent infection, prior exposure, and other lung diseases, potentially replacing sputum-dependent diagnosis for patients who can't produce a sample. Since a quarter of the world's population carries TB bacteria and early detection is critical to reducing transmission, a reliable non-sputum blood test could meaningfully speed diagnosis and treatment initiation.

## Core Concepts

- **[[ESAT-6]]** — a bacterial biomarker (6-kDa early secreted antigenic target) that, unlike immune-response markers, comes directly from *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, giving it a stepwise, dose-dependent signal across the infection spectrum
- **[[Non-Sputum TB Diagnostics]]** — diagnostic tools not dependent on a sputum sample, needed because many patients with early or low-bacterial-burden disease can't produce one

## Key Takeaways

- **Study size**: 217 patients across active TB, latent TB, TB-exposed, lung cancer, and healthy control groups
- **Signal gradient**: ESAT-6 levels rose in a clear stepwise pattern from uninfected/exposed → latent → active TB
- **Outperformed existing markers**: beat two common blood-based markers at discriminating between patient groups
- **Not yet clinical-ready**: the study design means further prospective research is needed before real-world deployment
