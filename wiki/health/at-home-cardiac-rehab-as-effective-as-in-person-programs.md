## Summary

An updated Cochrane review of 107 randomized controlled trials (26,886 people with coronary heart disease) found exercise-based [[Cardiac Rehabilitation]] cuts heart attack risk by 28% and all-cause hospital admissions by around 35%. A companion analysis of 27 studies found home- and digitally-supported rehab programs deliver clinical benefits comparable to in-person, facility-based programs.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Cardiac Rehabilitation]]**: Structured, exercise-based programs for people with [[Coronary Heart Disease]] shown to reduce heart attacks and hospitalizations versus no structured exercise.
- **[[Cochrane Review]]**: The systematic-review methodology underlying this analysis, led by senior author Rod Taylor, PhD (University of Glasgow) and lead author Grace Dibben.
- **[[Home-Based Rehabilitation]]**: Digitally-supported or remote-coached programs (e.g. smartwatches, remote coaching) that matched facility-based programs on clinical outcomes across 27 studies.
- **[[Strength Training]]**: Flagged by cardiologist Rigved Tadwalkar as an underused complement to aerobic exercise, supporting long-term longevity and metabolic health, especially in older adults.
- **[[World Health Organization]]**: Reported that cardiovascular disease caused an estimated 19.8 million deaths in 2022, ~85% from heart attack or stroke.

## Key Takeaways

- **Heart attack reduction**: Exercise-based rehab linked to a 28% relative risk reduction in heart attacks.
- **Fewer hospitalizations**: All-cause hospital admissions dropped by around 35%.
- **Numbers needed to treat**: One heart attack prevented per 71 participants within 6–12 months.
- **Home care works**: 27 studies show home/digital rehab matches in-person clinical outcomes.
- **Underfunded therapy**: Cardiac rehab is "inexpensive and cost-effective" yet remains underused.
- **Strength training gap**: Aerobic exercise dominates programs; strength training is underemphasized.
- **Safety confirmed**: Properly prescribed, gradually progressed exercise is safe post-heart attack.
- **Study limitation**: Only 17% of participants across the reviewed trials were women.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Access as a Force Multiplier]]**: Structured exercise's clinical effect only compounds at population scale if delivery barriers (distance, cost, scheduling) are removed — the review's core finding that home-based delivery matches in-person outcomes shows the therapy's benefit was previously capped by access, not efficacy.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the updated Cochrane review on cardiac rehabilitation?
**A**: Exercise-based cardiac rehab reduces heart attack risk by 28% and all-cause hospital admissions by about 35% in people with coronary heart disease, and home-based/digital delivery is just as effective as in-person programs.

**Q2**: What evidence supports the claim that cardiac rehab meaningfully prevents heart attacks?
**A**: Across 107 trials with 26,886 participants, one heart attack was prevented for every 71 people enrolled in exercise-based rehab within 6 to 12 months.

**Q3**: What practical implication follows from the home-based rehab findings?
**A**: Digital tools like smartwatches and remote coaching can extend cardiac rehab to patients who face barriers to attending in-person facility sessions, potentially closing an access gap given the therapy is inexpensive but historically underfunded.
