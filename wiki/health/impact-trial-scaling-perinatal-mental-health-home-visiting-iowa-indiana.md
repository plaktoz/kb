## Summary

A formative evaluation published in *BMC Health Services Research* describes how researchers adapted an implementation facilitation strategy to scale the [[Mothers and Babies]] cognitive-behavioral intervention across state home visiting programs in Iowa and Indiana. Because home visiting programs vary widely in operations, staffing, and funding, the team used community- and partner-engaged methods to identify barriers and state-specific priorities. The result is the IMPACT Implementation Support Package, built around four core performance outcomes for delivering perinatal mental health care.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Perinatal Depression]] and anxiety**: Common, potentially serious complications of pregnancy and childbirth that contribute to [[Maternal Mortality]] and health disparities, especially among BIPOC populations.
- **[[Home Visiting Programs]] (HVPs)**: Community health links already reaching thousands of low-income and marginalized families each year, but heterogeneous in structure.
- **[[Mothers and Babies]] intervention**: An evidence-based [[Cognitive Behavioral Therapy|cognitive-behavioral]] program being scaled within HVPs.
- **IMPACT trial**: Implementing Mental Health Programs Across Communities in Iowa & Indiana, led by [[Elissa Z. Faro]] (Hackensack Meridian CDI) with co-PI [[Kelli Ryckman]] (Indiana University).
- **[[Implementation Mapping]]**: A modified version was used with providers, state agencies, and program recipients to adapt [[Implementation Facilitation]].
- **[[Social Determinants of Health]]**: Prioritized in in-person meetings in Des Moines and Indianapolis and in English/Spanish virtual panels.
- **[[Implementation Science]]**: The broader discipline of moving evidence-based interventions into real-world delivery systems.

## Key Takeaways

- **Program Mapping**: Over 500 home visiting programs mapped across Indiana and Iowa.
- **Screening Is Routine**: Over 90% of 72 surveyed HVPs screen for perinatal depression.
- **Engagement Barriers**: Fear of judgment and scheduling logistics deter participation.
- **Social Networks Matter**: Involving family and broader social connections aids engagement.
- **Indiana Priorities**: Staff workload and integration into existing workflows.
- **Iowa Priorities**: Communication systems, training infrastructure, mental health consultations.
- **Support Package**: Four outcomes — staff growth, system integration, mental health understanding, trust.
- **Equity Stakes**: Perinatal mood disorders drive maternal mortality and BIPOC disparities.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Scaling an intervention requires adapting delivery to local context** — An evidence-based intervention does not transfer unchanged into heterogeneous delivery systems; differences in operations, staffing, funding, and local priorities determine what support each site needs.
→ explains: Indiana Priorities, Iowa Priorities, Support Package, Program Mapping

**T2. Uptake depends on removing engagement barriers, not just identifying need** — Screening that detects a problem does not by itself deliver care; perceived judgment, logistics, and lack of trust limit whether people engage with available help.
→ explains: Screening Is Routine, Engagement Barriers, Social Networks Matter

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What was the goal of the IMPACT formative evaluation?
**A**: To adapt an implementation facilitation strategy so the Mothers and Babies cognitive-behavioral intervention could be scaled within state home visiting programs across Iowa and Indiana.

**Q2**: What did the survey of home visiting programs reveal, and which barriers did participants report?
**A**: Over 90% of 72 responding programs routinely screen for perinatal depression, while participants cited fear of judgment and scheduling logistics as key engagement barriers.

**Q3**: Why did the study produce state-specific insights rather than one uniform rollout plan?
**A**: The states differed in priorities — Indiana focused on staff workload and workflow integration, Iowa on communication, training, and mental health consultation — so implementation support had to address each context.
