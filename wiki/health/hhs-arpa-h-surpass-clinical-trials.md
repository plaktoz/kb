## Summary
HHS, through [[ARPA-H]], launched SURPASS (Simulation-augmented, Real-time Platform Adaptive Seamless Trials) along with three complementary projects — STACK, COMMONS, and CINCH — to modernize [[Clinical Trials]] using predictive computational models, real-time data analysis, shared infrastructure, and automation. The goal is to cut the decade-plus timelines, $2 billion average costs, and 90%+ failure rate of current drug development while reducing patient burden and strengthening U.S. leadership in clinical research.

## Core Concepts
- **[[ARPA-H]]**: The Advanced Research Projects Agency for Health, the HHS unit funding and coordinating this clinical-trial modernization push.
- **[[SURPASS]]**: The flagship program building continuous, adaptive platform trials via a phaseless design engine (digital twins, predictive simulation), a continuous inference engine (real-time, always-valid statistical analysis), and an agentic operations layer (automated trial startup and operations).
- **[[Digital Twin]]**: A predictive computational model of a trial used to simulate clinical and operational outcomes before launch, central to SURPASS's "phaseless design engine."
- **[[Platform Trial]]**: An adaptive trial design that shares infrastructure and control groups across treatment arms, enabling faster, cheaper evidence generation.
- **STACK, COMMONS, CINCH**: Three complementary ARPA-H projects — STACK expands domestic clinical trial site capacity using AI; COMMONS builds privacy-by-design national data infrastructure for trial enrollment; CINCH helps patients contribute real-world data and find relevant trials.
- **[[Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]]**: HHS Secretary who framed the initiative as removing "delays, duplication, and unnecessary costs" from U.S. clinical trials.

## Key Takeaways
- **Status quo problem**: Clinical development often takes over a decade, costs up to $2 billion, and fails more than 90% of the time.
- **Three SURPASS technical areas**: phaseless design engine, continuous inference engine, and agentic operations layer.
- **Site capacity**: STACK aims to convert research-naive sites into trial-capable sites using AI-driven activation.
- **Data infrastructure**: COMMONS builds national-scale, privacy-by-design consent and data access infrastructure.
- **Patient-centered design**: CINCH focuses on patient-contributed real-world data and faster matching to appropriate trials.
- **Stated goal**: Enable more patients to join trials more likely to benefit them, while generating rigorous evidence faster and with fewer participants.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Bottleneck Theory]]**: SURPASS explicitly targets the slowest-moving constraints in drug development — fixed phase sequencing, large control groups, and fragmented site/data infrastructure — rather than optimizing peripheral steps, reflecting the principle that system throughput is governed by its tightest bottleneck.
