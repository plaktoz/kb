## Summary

With the CDC's [[ACIP]] failing to issue new COVID-19 or RSV recommendations for the 2026–2027 respiratory season, four major U.S. medical organizations jointly released their own guidance on September 2, 2026. The [[AMA]] and University of Minnesota's Vaccine Integrity Project reviewed hundreds of studies and published findings in *JAMA* the same day, concluding vaccines still protect against severe illness with no new safety concerns. The guidance void stems from HHS dismissing ACIP members last year and a subsequent court challenge that has not restored expert leadership.

## Core Concepts

- **[[ACIP]] Absence**: The CDC's Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices failed to issue flu, COVID, or RSV recommendations for 2026–2027, leaving a policy vacuum.
- **Independent Medical Guidance**: Four organizations stepped in — [[AAP]] (children), [[AAFP]] (adults), [[ACOG]] (pregnant people), and [[IDSA]] (immunocompromised individuals).
- **[[AMA]] Review**: The American Medical Association partnered with the University of Minnesota's Vaccine Integrity Project to independently review hundreds of studies, publishing in *JAMA*.
- **[[Vaccine Safety]]**: No new safety concerns were identified; vaccines continue to protect against severe illness from flu, COVID-19, and RSV.
- **HHS Policy Context**: HHS dismissed ACIP members, triggering a court challenge; expert leadership has not been restored as of September 2026.

## Key Takeaways

- **Flu**: Everyone 6 months+; enhanced formulation recommended for adults 65+.
- **COVID**: All adults 19+; adults 65+ receive two seasonal doses; children 6–23 months; higher-risk youth 2–18.
- **RSV**: Adults 75+; adults 50–74 at elevated risk; during pregnancy; infants under 8 months.
- **Science unchanged**: Emory epidemiologist Benjamin Lopman — "The science is as strong as ever."
- **Institutional gap**: CDC/ACIP absence is a policy failure, not a scientific one.
- **Coordinated response**: Four specialty societies covered distinct high-risk populations collectively.
- **JAMA validation**: Independent review found consistent protection, no new safety signals.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Institutional Redundancy]]**: When one governing body fails, distributed expert organizations can fill the gap — this is a real-world demonstration of why redundant institutions matter in public health systems.
- **[[Appeal to Authority vs. Evidence]]**: The guidance underscores that vaccine safety rests on peer-reviewed evidence, not on any single agency's imprimatur — separating institutional authority from scientific validity.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did four major medical organizations issue their own vaccine guidance for 2026–2027?
**A**: The CDC's ACIP failed to issue new COVID-19 or RSV recommendations for the season after HHS dismissed its members, leaving a policy vacuum that the AAP, AAFP, ACOG, and IDSA filled.

**Q2**: What did the AMA's independent review of vaccine studies find?
**A**: The AMA, partnering with the University of Minnesota's Vaccine Integrity Project, reviewed hundreds of studies published in *JAMA* and found that vaccines still protect against severe illness with no new safety concerns identified.

**Q3**: How should high-risk adults approach COVID-19 vaccination for 2026–2027?
**A**: Adults 65+ are recommended two seasonal COVID doses; all adults 19+ should receive at least one dose, and higher-risk youth aged 2–18 are also included in the guidance.
