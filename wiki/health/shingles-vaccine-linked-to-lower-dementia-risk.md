# Shingles vaccine linked to 24% lower dementia risk

## Summary

A study of over 500,000 older adults entering skilled nursing facilities found that those who received the recombinant [[Shingrix]] shingles vaccine had a 24% lower risk of developing dementia over four years than unvaccinated peers. The findings, using a "target trial emulation" method on Medicare data, add to a growing body of evidence linking [[Herpes Zoster Vaccination]] to neuroprotective effects, though the study cannot prove causation.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Shingrix]]**: The only recombinant shingles vaccine (RZV) currently on the market, introduced in 2017, studied here for its association with dementia risk.
- **[[Target Trial Emulation]]**: A statistical technique used to mimic the conditions of a randomized clinical trial using observational data when a real trial isn't feasible.
- **[[Dementia]]**: The health outcome tracked; researchers compared diagnosis rates between vaccinated and unvaccinated cohorts.
- **[[Neuroprotection]]**: The broader hypothesis that vaccines preventing viral or inflammatory insults (like shingles) may also protect cognitive function.
- **[[Kaley Hayes]]**: Assistant professor at Brown University's School of Public Health and lead author of the study.

## Key Takeaways

- **Reduced Risk**: Vaccinated adults had a 24% lower risk of dementia vs. unvaccinated adults.
- **Large Sample**: Analysis covered 509,926 adults age 66+ across 5,500+ nursing facilities.
- **Absolute Numbers**: Dementia occurred in 18.8% of vaccinated vs. 24.6% of unvaccinated participants.
- **Real-World Impact**: About 1 in 17 dementia cases potentially prevented, per the study author.
- **Consistent Evidence**: Results align with earlier studies of an older shingles vaccine and dementia risk.
- **Causation Unproven**: Vaccinated people were slightly younger/healthier; adjustments didn't fully explain the effect.
- **Funding Disclosure**: Study funded by GlaxoSmithKline, Shingrix's manufacturer, though GSK had no control over design or analysis.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of this study?
**A**: Older adults in skilled nursing facilities who received the Shingrix shingles vaccine had a 24% lower risk of being diagnosed with dementia over four years compared to unvaccinated adults.

**Q2**: What method did researchers use, and what specific numbers support the finding?
**A**: They used target trial emulation on Medicare data from over 500,000 adults; dementia developed in 18.8% of vaccinated participants versus 24.6% of unvaccinated participants.

**Q3**: What is a key limitation, and what should readers take from it?
**A**: The study cannot prove Shingrix causes lower dementia risk since vaccinated people were healthier on average; further clinical trials are needed before treating vaccination as a proven dementia-prevention strategy.
