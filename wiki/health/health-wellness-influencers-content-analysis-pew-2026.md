## Summary

Pew Research Center analyzed 535,512 Instagram, TikTok and YouTube posts from 6,334 health and wellness influencers (February to April 2026). It found that their content leans toward healthy-living tips such as fitness, nutrition and habits rather than medical topics. Product promotion is widespread, appearing in 38% of posts, and influencers rarely cite outside sources to back up health claims.

## Core Concepts

- **Study design**: [[Pew Research Center]] analyzed the audio, video and captions of posts. Topics were grouped into "healthy living" and "medical issues."
- **Healthy living (just over half of posts)**: [[Fitness]] (25%), healthy habits (20%), [[Nutrition]] (19%; 42% of nutrition posts mention [[Protein]]), and physical appearance (14%).
- **Medical issues (about a third of posts)**: Medical conditions (19%), prescription or professional treatments (14%), and personal experiences with a condition or the healthcare system (11%).
- **Most-mentioned conditions**: [[Cancer]] appears in 8.5% of condition posts. [[Diabetes]], anxiety/depression, substance abuse and [[ADHD]] each appear in 5% or more.
- **[[Influencer Marketing]]**: 38% of posts promote a product or service, and 92% of influencers do so at least occasionally. Promotion is most common in supplement posts (66%), men's health and appearance (56% each), and nutrition and [[Longevity]] (52% each).
- **Credibility signals**: Only 9% of posts cite an outside source, and 11% warn of a direct health harm. The two often go together.
- **Off-topic content**: 30% of posts are not about health at all, and 89% of influencers made at least one such post.

## Key Takeaways

- **Scale**: 535,512 posts from 6,334 influencers over three months.
- **Healthy-living tilt**: About 50% of posts cover healthy living vs. about 33% medical.
- **Promotion everywhere**: 38% of posts promote; about a third of influencers promote in most posts.
- **Supplements most commercial**: 66% of supplement posts include a promotion.
- **Go-to topics**: About 60% of influencers focus on one main topic; fitness leads at 25%.
- **Common pairing**: 17% of influencers mostly post fitness plus healthy habits.
- **Odd pairing**: 7% combine appearance and professional treatments, such as cosmetic surgeons.
- **Thin sourcing**: Only 9% of posts cite outside expertise.
- **Who promotes most**: Dieticians, alternative-health practitioners, authors, business owners and coaches.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Incentive-Caused Bias]]**: Promotions cluster in supplements, appearance and longevity (52–66% of posts). Topics that are easiest to sell are also the most promoted, so viewers should read that advice in light of the influencer's commercial incentives.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the main finding about what health and wellness influencers post?
**A**: They focus more on healthy-living tips (just over half of posts) than on medical conditions or treatments (about a third), and product promotion is widespread.

**Q2**: How common is product promotion, and where is it concentrated?
**A**: 38% of posts promote a product or service, and 92% of influencers do so at least occasionally. Promotion is most common in supplement posts (66%), followed by men's health, appearance, nutrition and longevity.

**Q3**: What should a viewer keep in mind when evaluating influencer health claims?
**A**: Only 9% of posts cite an outside source and many are promotional, especially for supplements and appearance. Claims should be checked against independent expertise rather than taken on the influencer's personal credibility.
