## Summary

A meta-analysis of 21 randomized controlled trials (8,449 participants) published in the *British Journal of Sports Medicine* found that structured exercise after cancer treatment significantly reduces recurrence risk and cancer-specific mortality. Aerobic exercise alone was associated with a 29% lower relative risk of death, while participants who followed any structured exercise program were 17% more likely to survive without cancer recurrence.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Cancer Survivorship]]** — the phase of care after active cancer treatment ends, during which lifestyle factors like exercise can influence long-term outcomes.
- **[[Aerobic Exercise]]** — structured cardiovascular activity (e.g., walking, cycling, swimming) that increases cardiac output, oxygen delivery, and immune function.
- **[[Cancer Recurrence Risk]]** — the likelihood that cancer returns after initial treatment; the study found exercise programs reduced this risk measurably.
- **[[Antitumor Immune Response]]** — the immune system's capacity to detect and destroy cancer cells, potentially enhanced by regular aerobic activity.
- **Cancers studied** — breast, colorectal, and pancreatic cancers were the most represented in the included trials.

## Key Takeaways

- **17% greater likelihood** of surviving without cancer recurrence in exercisers.
- **26% lower risk** of cancer-specific death for structured exercisers vs. controls.
- **Aerobic exercise alone**: 29% lower relative risk of death.
- **Combined aerobic + resistance training**: ~25% lower death risk.
- **Resistance training alone** was not statistically significant.
- Exercise boosts cardiac output, immune cell activity, and may impair cancer cell metabolism.
- Programs should be tailored to individual fitness level, treatment history, and coordinated with an oncology team.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Hormesis]]**: Moderate physiological stress from aerobic exercise triggers adaptive responses — improved immune surveillance and metabolic efficiency — that specifically disadvantage cancer cell survival, illustrating why a controlled stressor produces outsized protective benefits.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of this meta-analysis on exercise and cancer outcomes?
**A**: Structured exercise after cancer treatment reduces recurrence risk by 17% and cancer-specific death risk by 26%, with aerobic exercise alone driving the strongest survival benefit.

**Q2**: Which exercise modality showed the strongest statistical effect, and which was not significant?
**A**: Aerobic exercise alone showed a 29% lower relative risk of death; combined aerobic and resistance training showed ~25%; resistance training alone was not statistically significant.

**Q3**: How should a cancer survivor apply these findings practically?
**A**: Work with an oncology care team to design a structured aerobic exercise program tailored to individual fitness level and treatment history, prioritizing consistent cardiovascular activity over resistance training alone.
