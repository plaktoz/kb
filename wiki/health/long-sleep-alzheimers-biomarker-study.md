## Summary

A [[Framingham Heart Study]] analysis of 2,410 participants found that sleeping more than 8.5 hours a night was associated with elevated blood levels of [[p-tau181]], a biomarker linked to [[Alzheimer's Disease]], following a J-shaped curve where both very short and very long sleep correlated with higher biomarker levels. Researchers caution the observational, cross-sectional design cannot establish whether long sleep causes early neurodegeneration or is itself an early symptom of it, since Alzheimer's pathology can disrupt sleep-regulating brain regions years before memory symptoms appear.

## Core Concepts

- **[[p-tau181]]** — a plasma biomarker of phosphorylated tau protein reflecting Alzheimer's-related brain pathology
- **[[J-shaped Sleep-Biomarker Curve]]** — both short (<6h) and long (>8.5-9h) sleep durations associate with elevated tau biomarkers relative to average sleep
- **[[Glymphatic System]]** — the brain's waste-clearance pathway during deep sleep, which may be impaired by fragmented long sleep
- **[[Sleep as Cause vs. Symptom]]** — competing hypotheses on whether long sleep drives neurodegeneration or reflects early pathology disrupting sleep regulation

## Key Takeaways

- **Long sleep (>8.5h) tied to elevated p-tau181**, the only biomarker with a statistically significant association.
- **J-shaped relationship**: both short and long sleepers showed higher biomarker levels than average (6-9h) sleepers.
- **Sleep quality, not just duration, may matter**: long time in bed can mask fragmented, non-restorative sleep.
- **Causality remains unresolved**: this cross-sectional study can't distinguish long sleep as cause versus early symptom.
- **Alzheimer's pathology can precede symptoms by 15-20 years**, potentially disrupting sleep regulation long before memory issues appear.
