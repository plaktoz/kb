## Summary

A survey study of 6,607 adults with autoimmune or rheumatic disease, published October 12 in eClinicalMedicine, found women wait longer for a correct diagnosis and are more often told their symptoms are mental health or psychosomatic. Nearly 32% of women waited four years or more versus about 26% of men, and 43.3% of women reported mental-health misattribution versus 32.7% of men. Researchers cite presentation differences, research gaps, fragmented care and unconscious bias, and urge clinicians to consider autoimmune disease in women and patients to advocate for themselves.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Diagnostic Delay]]**: About 32% of women waited four years or more from symptom onset versus roughly 26% of men; almost 50% of men were diagnosed within a year versus 40% of women.
- **Visit burden**: 26.2% of women had 11 or more visits before diagnosis versus 18.4% of men.
- **Misattribution**: Women more often reported symptoms mistaken for mental health issues (43.3% vs. 32.7%) or psychosomatic causes (33.9% vs. 21.5%); no sex difference for misdiagnosis with another physical disease.
- **Disease dependence**: Conditions with clear signs or definitive lab tests (type 1 diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis) had lower misdiagnosis; autoimmune encephalitis and undifferentiated connective tissue disease exceeded 50%.
- **[[Anchoring Bias]] in records**: Once a psychiatric explanation enters the record, later clinicians may read new symptoms through that lens.
- **Study limits**: Online survey, 91.2% female, mostly UK respondents, self-reported.

## Key Takeaways

- **Longer Waits**: Women face longer paths to diagnosis despite being nearly twice as likely to have an autoimmune disease.
- **Psychiatric Mislabeling**: Women were far likelier to be told it was mental or psychosomatic.
- **Test-Clear Diseases Fare Better**: Definitive tests reduce misdiagnosis.
- **Bias Compounds**: Early psychiatric labels can color later care.
- **Self-Advocacy Tools**: Keep a symptom timeline, bring family history, ask for a next-step plan, seek a second opinion.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**A**: Women with autoimmune diseases face longer delays to a correct diagnosis and are more often misattributed to mental health or psychosomatic conditions than men.

**Q2**: What data illustrate the gap?

**Q3**: What can patients do?
**A**: Keep an accurate symptom timeline, bring family history, ask what else could explain symptoms and for a plan for next steps, get a second opinion, and keep working with a primary care clinician.
