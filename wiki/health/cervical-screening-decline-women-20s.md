## Summary

A 2026 study published in *JAMA Network Open* found that nearly 32% of women ages 21–29 had never undergone [[Cervical Cancer]] screening as of 2023, up sharply from 12.5% in 2005. The never-screened rate also rose among older women (ages 30–65), from 5.3% to 14.8% over the same period. The findings coincide with increasing incidence of regional- and distant-stage cervical cancer among women ages 30–64.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Cervical Cancer Screening]]**: Regular testing using primary [[HPV]] tests every five years, recommended starting at age 25 by the [[American Cancer Society]].
- **[[Health Disparities]]**: Screening gaps vary significantly by race, ethnicity, education, and insurance status — [[Asian Women]] showed the highest never-screened prevalence, and uninsured women faced notably higher risk.
- **[[HPV Vaccination]]**: Reduces but does not eliminate cervical cancer risk; regular screening remains necessary even for vaccinated individuals.
- **[[Preventive Care Barriers]]**: Cost, transportation, insurance gaps, discomfort, and misinformation all contribute to declining screening uptake.

## Key Takeaways

- **Screening decline**: 32% of women ages 21–29 never screened in 2023, up from 12.5% in 2005.
- **Older women also affected**: Never-screened rate tripled among women ages 30–65 (5.3% → 14.8%).
- **Racial disparity**: [[Asian Women]] have the highest never-screened prevalence in both age groups.
- **Insurance gap**: Uninsured women 30–65 significantly more likely to have never been screened.
- **Cancer incidence rising**: Regional/distant-stage cervical cancer grew 2%+ annually after 2017 in 30–64 age group.
- **Screening recommendation**: [[HPV]] test every 5 years starting at age 25, per [[American Cancer Society]].
- **Vaccination caveat**: [[HPV Vaccination]] reduces but does not eliminate risk — screening still required.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Prevention Paradox]]**: The benefits of cervical screening are invisible to those who never develop cancer — making it easy to deprioritize a painless, preventive test until the disease is already advanced.
