## Summary

Two years after the FDA rejected MDMA therapy for PTSD, big pharma is investing billions in psychedelic medicines, helped by clearer regulatory guidance and positive late-stage data. The MDMA failure taught the industry how to build stronger submission packages, though researchers stress that safety questions remain and progress is not inevitable.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Eli Lilly]] / [[AtaiBeckley]]** — Lilly's plan to acquire AtaiBeckley for up to $3.8 billion; lead asset BPL-003 targets treatment-resistant depression.
- **[[AbbVie]] / [[Gilgamesh Pharmaceutical]]** — a $1.2 billion 2025 acquisition of bretisilocin, a short-acting psychedelic.
- **[[MDMA-Assisted Therapy]]** — Lykos Therapeutics' 2024 FDA rejection (now Resilient Pharmaceuticals, which has resubmitted).
- **[[Functional Unblinding]]** — trial participants and researchers guessing who received the active psychedelic; addressed in the FDA's July psychedelic-trial guidance.
- **[[Psilocybin Therapy]]** — [[Compass Pathways]] reported positive results from two late-stage trials for treatment-resistant depression.
- **[[Ibogaine]] and [[5-MeO-DMT]]** — "dark horse" compounds now drawing attention; an April executive order highlights ibogaine.
- **[[War on Drugs]]** — the force that stifled the 1950s-60s wave of psychedelic research.

## Key Takeaways

- **Big Pharma Bets**: Lilly (up to $3.8B) and AbbVie ($1.2B) acquired psychedelic developers.
- **Regulatory Clarity**: Clearer global guidance is credited for persistence despite the MDMA rejection.
- **MDMA Lessons**: FDA flagged functional unblinding and therapy-versus-drug effects as key trial flaws.
- **Late-Stage Data**: Definium's LSD pill cut generalized anxiety symptoms significantly in late-stage studies.
- **Next-Gen Design**: Researchers aim for shorter-acting drugs with fewer hallucinations.
- **Policy Tailwinds**: April executive order streamlines research; SAMHSA urges states to prepare for approval.
- **Caution**: Adverse-effect extent and needed effect duration remain unknown; history shows booms can be crushed.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why are pharma firms still investing billions in psychedelics despite the FDA's MDMA rejection?
**A**: Regulators have given clearer guidance, late-stage trials show promising results, and the MDMA failure showed companies what a strong submission package needs.

**Q2**: What specific problems did the FDA raise about the Lykos MDMA application?
**A**: Functional unblinding, where participants and researchers could guess who got the active drug, and difficulty disentangling MDMA's effects from the accompanying psychotherapy.

**Q3**: What caution do researchers attach to the field's current momentum?
**A**: Progress is not inevitable: the 1950s-60s enthusiasm was stifled by the war on drugs, and adverse effects and required effect duration are still poorly understood.
