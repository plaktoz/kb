## Summary
A Lancet series led by the University of Sheffield finds the life expectancy gap between men and women is widening disproportionately among disadvantaged, ethnic minority and lower socioeconomic groups. The authors attribute this to industries that prey on men and to policy gaps that let harmful influences like the online manosphere grow unchecked. They call for national men's health strategies worldwide.

## Core Concepts
- [[Men's Health Gap]]: life expectancy and illness burden differ widely among groups of men.
- [[Manosphere]]: extremist online culture that has grown unchecked amid gaps in support services.
- [[Commercial Determinants of Health]]: the alcohol, tobacco and sports betting industries profit from men's harm.
- [[National Men's Health Strategies]]: introduced in [[Ireland]] (2008) and [[England]] (2025); elsewhere sparse and underfunded.
- [[Systemic Drivers of Health]]: racism, poverty and class inequality, which strategies tend to overlook.
- [[Jim Catto]]: University of Sheffield urologist who led the series.
- [[The Lancet]]: the journal that published the series.

## Key Takeaways
- **Widening Gap**: The male-female life expectancy gap grows most among disadvantaged and minority groups.
- **Industry Harm**: Alcohol, tobacco and sports betting industries turn over billions from men's harm.
- **Policy Gaps**: Weak policy and services let harmful online influences grow unchecked.
- **Strategy Coverage**: Only Ireland (2008) and England (2025) are cited as having national strategies.
- **Individual Focus**: Existing efforts focus on individual behavior over systemic drivers.
- **Recommendations**: Better data on marginalized men, stable long-term funding, positive masculinity.
- **Equity Framing**: Catto says closing men's health gaps does not take away from women.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the Lancet men's health series?
**A**: Men are suffering poor health and dying younger, with the life expectancy gap between men and women widening most among disadvantaged populations, ethnic minorities and lower socioeconomic groups.

**Q2**: What drivers does the series identify?
**A**: The alcohol, tobacco and sports betting industries prey on men, while gaps in public policy and support services let influences like the online manosphere grow unchecked. Existing efforts focus on individual behavior rather than systemic drivers such as racism, poverty and class inequality.

**Q3**: What do the authors recommend?
**A**: They call for global rollout of national men's health strategies, with better data on marginalized men, stable long-term funding and positive masculinity. Professor Catto says this does not take away from women and paves the way for health equity for all.
