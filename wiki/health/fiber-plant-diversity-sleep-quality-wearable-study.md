## Summary
A Nature Health study of 3,598 mostly healthy adults found that diets higher in fiber and in plant-food variety were associated with better sleep architecture. The article advises caution in treating these associations as definitive treatments for sleep disorders. Eating a larger share of calories shortly before bed was linked to slightly longer sleep but a higher nighttime heart rate.

## Core Concepts
- [[Dietary Fiber]]: Higher fiber density is linked to more deep and REM sleep and less light sleep.
- [[Sleep Architecture]]: The mix of deep, REM, and light sleep stages measured via wearables.
- [[Dietary Phytochemical Index]]: A higher score is linked to more REM sleep and less light sleep.
- [[Plant-Based Diet]]: Variety of whole-plant foods is associated with lower nighttime heart rates.
- [[Gut Microbiome]]: [[Marie-Pierre St-Onge]] suggests plant-forward diets may raise short-chain fatty acid production, affecting sleep-related neurotransmitters.
- [[Meal Timing]]: A larger share of calories in the six hours before sleep is linked to a raised nighttime heart rate.
- [[Hagai Rossman]]: Study author who notes diet choices influence sleep architecture.

## Key Takeaways
- **Study Scale**: 3,598 adults and 4,793 person-nights of wearable sleep data.
- **Deep Sleep**: Higher fiber density linked to 0.59 percentage-point more deep sleep.
- **REM Sleep**: Higher fiber density linked to 0.76 percentage-point more REM sleep.
- **Heart Rate**: Fiber linked to a 1.14 bpm lower average nighttime heart rate.
- **Plant Diversity**: A wider variety of whole-plant foods tracks with better sleep measures.
- **Late Eating**: Late calories added about 7.7 minutes of sleep but raised heart rate.
- **Caution**: Findings are associations, not definitive treatments for sleep disorders.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Association does not establish a treatment effect** — Observational links between diet and sleep cannot show that changing diet will fix sleep disorders, so findings should be read with caution. · *model:* [[Correlation vs Causation]]
→ explains: Caution, Deep Sleep, REM Sleep

**T2. More of a measure is not the same as better quality** — Longer sleep after late eating came with a higher heart rate, so duration alone does not signal improved sleep quality.
→ explains: Late Eating, Heart Rate

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core finding about diet and sleep?
**A**: Diets rich in fiber and a variety of whole plant foods are associated with improved sleep quality. The article cautions against reading this as a definitive treatment for sleep disorders.

**Q2**: What specific evidence links fiber to sleep?
**A**: In 3,598 adults, higher fiber density was associated with 0.59 percentage-point more deep sleep, 0.76 more REM sleep, 1.35 less light sleep, and a 1.14 bpm lower nighttime heart rate.

**Q3**: How might this be applied in practice?
**A**: Physicians could recommend fiber-rich foods such as beans, lentils, whole grains, berries, vegetables, nuts, and seeds. Finishing meals at least three hours before bed may also help, though sleep quality was not that study's main focus.
