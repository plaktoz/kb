## Summary
A UTHealth Houston study of over 2 million elective surgery patients found that those on [[GLP-1 Receptor Agonists]] had modestly lower odds of major cardiovascular events (11%) and postoperative infections (8%). The design was observational, so causation is not established, and the study did not assess aspiration risk. Researchers call the results reassuring and hypothesis-generating, not a reason to start or stop the drugs.

## Core Concepts
- [[GLP-1 Receptor Agonists]]: semaglutide and tirzepatide-based drugs, originally developed for type 2 diabetes blood sugar control.
- [[Josephine Tolan]]: lead author at McGovern Medical School, UTHealth Houston; proposes [[Inflammation Modulation]] as a mechanism.
- [[EPIC COSMOS]]: electronic health record database spanning 1,700+ hospitals and 40,000+ clinics.
- [[MACE]]: major adverse cardiovascular events (heart attack, myocardial injury, stroke, all-cause death).
- [[Perioperative Medicine]]: the debate over withholding GLP-1s before surgery due to delayed gastric emptying and [[Aspiration Risk]].
- [[Jessica Duncan]]: obesity medicine physician who stresses the small absolute differences and observational limits.

## Key Takeaways
- **Cohort Size**: 2,028,601 adults, 145,610 on GLP-1 therapy, surgeries 2016–2024.
- **Cardiovascular Effect**: MACE in 1% of users versus 1.1% of non-users (11% lower odds).
- **Infection Effect**: Infections in 4.9% of users versus 5.3% of non-users (8% lower odds).
- **Plausible Mechanism**: Anti-inflammatory effects may blunt surgery's inflammatory response.
- **Causality Caveat**: Observational design cannot prove the drugs caused the reductions.
- **Unknowns**: Timing of drug discontinuation before surgery and aspiration outcomes were not reported.
- **Patient Guidance**: Tell surgical teams which GLP-1, dose, and last dose; follow instructions.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Association is not causation** — An observed difference between groups in observational data cannot by itself establish that the exposure produced the outcome. · *model:* [[Correlation vs Causation]]

**T2. Small absolute differences can matter at scale** — A tiny per-person risk reduction can be clinically relevant when multiplied across millions of cases. · *model:* [[Relative vs. Absolute Risk]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the study find about GLP-1 use before elective surgery?
**A**: GLP-1 users had 11% lower odds of major cardiovascular events and 8% lower odds of postoperative infections than matched non-users. The authors cautioned the design was observational and cannot prove causation.

**Q2**: What data support the finding, and what is the proposed mechanism?
**A**: The EPIC COSMOS database covered 2,028,601 surgery patients (145,610 on GLP-1s), with MACE at 1% versus 1.1% and infections at 4.9% versus 5.3%. The proposed mechanism is that GLP-1s modulate inflammatory pathways around surgery.

**Q3**: How should patients and doctors act on these results?
**A**: Patients should tell their surgical and anesthesia teams their GLP-1, dose and last dose, and follow clinician instructions without starting or stopping on their own. Doctors may favor individualized perioperative decisions but should not start the drugs solely to prevent surgical complications.
