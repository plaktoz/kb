## Summary

The FDA has approved [[Moderna]]'s mFLUSIVA (mRNA-1010), the first [[mRNA Vaccine]] approved for seasonal influenza, for adults 50 and older. In a phase 3 trial of 40,000+ adults, mFLUSIVA demonstrated approximately 27% greater relative efficacy versus standard-dose flu vaccines for the 50–64 age group. The vaccine is expected to be available for the 2026–2027 respiratory season and represents a potential step-change in flu vaccine manufacturing speed.

## Core Concepts

- **[[mFLUSIVA]] (mRNA-1010)**: [[Moderna]]'s first mRNA-based seasonal flu vaccine; FDA-approved for adults 50+.
- **[[mRNA Vaccine]] Platform**: Same underlying technology as [[COVID-19 mRNA Vaccines]]; enables faster redesign and production compared to traditional egg-based methods.
- **[[FDA Accelerated Approval]]**: 65+ cohort received accelerated approval based on immunogenicity data; confirmatory real-world evidence still required.
- **Strain-Matching Problem**: Traditional [[Egg-Based Flu Vaccine]] manufacturing takes 6+ months, limiting strain-matching accuracy. mRNA's faster cycle could address this recurring limitation.
- **[[CDC]] Flu Burden**: ~45,000 U.S. flu deaths estimated in 2024–2025; vaccination prevented ~12,000 of those deaths.

## Key Takeaways

- **FDA Approval**: First mRNA flu vaccine approved for adults 50+.
- **Superior Efficacy**: ~27% higher relative efficacy vs. standard-dose vaccines for ages 50–64.
- **Accelerated Approval Caveat**: 65+ group still requires confirmatory real-world evidence post-approval.
- **Manufacturing Speed**: mRNA vaccines can be produced faster than egg-based alternatives, aiding strain-matching.
- **Availability**: Expected for the 2026–2027 flu season.
- **Side Effects**: Similar to other vaccines — fatigue, headache, muscle pain, fever; rare anaphylaxis.
- **Not a Replacement**: Experts position mFLUSIVA as an additional option; getting any appropriate flu vaccine remains the priority.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Technology S-Curve]]**: mFLUSIVA marks the inflection point where mRNA platform technology — validated by COVID-19 vaccines — begins expanding into adjacent disease domains, following the classic S-curve of platform adoption.
- **[[Precautionary Principle]]**: The accelerated approval pathway for the 65+ group exemplifies the regulatory tension between speed of access and certainty of evidence — real-world confirmatory trials are required precisely because the benefit-risk calculus is highest for the most vulnerable population.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core significance of mFLUSIVA's FDA approval?
**A**: It is the first mRNA-based seasonal flu vaccine approved in the U.S., demonstrating that the mRNA platform validated by COVID-19 vaccines can be extended to influenza prevention.

**Q2**: How did mFLUSIVA perform versus standard-dose flu vaccines in the phase 3 trial?
**A**: In a 40,000+ adult trial, mFLUSIVA showed approximately 27% higher relative efficacy versus standard-dose vaccines for the 50–64 age group; the 65+ group received accelerated approval based on immunogenicity, with confirmatory evidence still pending.

**Q3**: Why might mRNA technology reshape seasonal flu vaccine responses over time?
**A**: Unlike egg-based vaccines that require 6+ months to manufacture, mRNA vaccines can be redesigned and produced faster, potentially improving strain-matching accuracy — a recurring weakness of traditional flu vaccine production cycles.
