## Summary
A study in *Women's Health Issues* found 32% of postpartum women self-reported needing mental health care, well above the 10%–20% estimated from screenings and diagnoses. Three-quarters of those who could not get care cited logistical barriers such as lack of time, child care, or information. The authors argue policy should address these barriers and let women report their own needs.

## Core Concepts
- [[Postpartum Mental Health]]: mental health conditions are the leading cause of pregnancy-related death in the US, yet almost all such deaths are considered preventable (CDC, 2017–2019).
- [[Self-Reported Need]] vs. [[Clinical Screening]]: asking women directly surfaces need among those who may not meet clinical criteria or have access to diagnosis.
- [[Pregnancy Risk Assessment and Monitoring System]]: 2022 data from over 13,000 women, representative of postpartum women in 16 states.
- [[Barriers to Care]]: time off work, affordable child care, and finding where to go for help.
- [[Rebecca Gourevitch]] and [[Marie E. Thoma]]: University of Maryland School of Public Health researchers leading the study.
- [[Paid Family Leave]], [[Telemedicine]]: policies suggested to remove logistical barriers.

## Key Takeaways
- **Self-Reported Need**: 32% of women said they needed postpartum mental health care.
- **Screening Gap**: Screening and diagnosis-based studies estimate only 10%–20% have postpartum mood and anxiety disorders.
- **Logistical Barriers**: Three-quarters of women who lacked care cited logistical barriers.
- **Top Barrier**: Not having time to get care was the biggest logistical barrier.
- **Equity Gaps**: Access was lower for Medicaid-covered, uninsured, Hispanic, and Asian/Pacific Islander women.
- **Likely Undercount**: Stigma, discrimination, and unrecognized symptoms may suppress self-reports.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. The measurement method sets the measured need** — Prevalence estimates depend on what is counted: screening and diagnosis capture only people who meet criteria and reach services, while self-report captures need that falls outside both.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the study's central finding about postpartum mental health care need?
**A**: 32% of women self-reported needing mental health care, higher than the 10%–20% estimated from screenings and diagnoses. Asking women directly reveals more need than previously known.

**Q2**: What evidence shows what stops women from getting care?
**A**: Among women who could not access needed care, three-quarters cited logistical barriers, with lack of time the biggest. The data came from over 13,000 women in the 2022 PRAMS survey across 16 states.

**Q3**: What policies does the lead author say follow from this?
**A**: Gourevitch calls for investing in policies that remove logistical barriers, such as paid family leave, paid sick leave, affordable child care, and telemedicine. She also says women should be able to report their own mental health needs alongside current screening tools.
