## Summary
A large international genetic study of over 1 million people, supported by the [[National Institutes of Health]], identified 58 DNA segments linked to [[Endometriosis]], 27 of them not found before. The researchers credit the diversity of participants across racial backgrounds for part of the new discoveries. The authors say the data could guide better diagnostics, risk assessment and treatments for a chronic condition with no cure.

## Core Concepts
- [[Endometriosis]] affects roughly 1 in 10 women, an estimated 190 million reproductive-age women worldwide, per the [[World Health Organization]].
- **Genetic discovery**: 58 DNA segments linked to the condition, including 27 newly identified.
- **Shared genetics**: Endometriosis shares genetic links with 135 other health conditions or traits.
- **Candidate genes**: 314 genes may be associated with the condition.
- **Multi-ancestry design**: Combining biological data across people with different ancestries broadened the findings.
- **Symptoms**: Severe pain, heavy menstrual bleeding, infertility and chronic pelvic pain.

## Key Takeaways
- **Study Scale**: Over 1 million participants across diverse racial backgrounds.
- **New Findings**: 58 DNA segments linked to endometriosis, 27 never reported before.
- **Shared Genetics**: Links to 135 other conditions or traits.
- **Candidate Genes**: 314 genes may be associated with the disease.
- **Prevalence**: Affects about 1 in 10 women, roughly 190 million worldwide.
- **No Cure**: Early diagnosis and effective treatment remain limited in many settings.
- **Research Direction**: Findings could guide diagnostics, risk tools and treatments.
