## Summary

[[Daraxonrasib]] (Rasonque), already FDA-approved for metastatic pancreatic cancer, showed promising results in a Phase 1-2 trial for previously treated, advanced *RAS*-mutant [[Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer]] (NSCLC). Tumor shrinkage occurred in over 30% of participants overall, rising to 42% in an exploratory subgroup with no prior docetaxel exposure. A Phase 3 randomized trial is now planned, potentially expanding targeted RAS therapy to a major lung cancer population with few options.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Daraxonrasib]] (Rasonque)** — RAS(ON) multiselective inhibitor; blocks active RAS across multiple proteins and mutations, unlike single-mutation-specific drugs
- **[[Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer]] (NSCLC)** — accounts for ~85% of all lung cancer cases; ~30% carry *RAS* mutations
- **[[RAS Gene Mutations]]** — keep cell-growth signals permanently "on," driving cancer progression; historically difficult to target therapeutically
- **[[RAS(ON) Inhibition]]** — novel mechanism targeting the *active* form of RAS across multiple mutation types, potentially reaching patients with currently untreatable RAS variants
- **[[Phase 1-2 Clinical Trial]]** — enrolled 136 adults with previously treated, advanced *RAS*-mutant NSCLC; doses ranged 10–400 mg daily

## Key Takeaways

- **Overall response rate**: Tumors shrank in 31–37% of participants depending on dose level.
- **Best subgroup**: 42% tumor shrinkage, 89% disease control rate (160–220 mg, no prior docetaxel, n=38).
- **Durability**: Median response duration 11.5 months; median progression-free survival 8.3 months.
- **Survival**: Median overall survival 16 months in the best-performing subgroup.
- **Safety**: Nearly all patients had adverse events; >50% had grade 3+ events (pneumonia, diarrhea, rash, anemia).
- **Mechanism advantage**: Multiselective design may cover multiple RAS variants, broadening eligible patients.
- **Next step**: Phase 3 randomized trial planned to confirm results vs standard of care.
- **Published**: *New England Journal of Medicine*.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Drug Repurposing]]**: Targeting a shared molecular driver (RAS mutations) across cancer types — rather than organ-specific biology — allows a drug proven in pancreatic cancer to rationally expand into lung cancer, illustrating how mechanism-first oncology multiplies the value of a single approved therapy.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of this Phase 1-2 trial for daraxonrasib in NSCLC?
**A**: Tumors shrank in over 30% of all participants and in 42% of the best-responding subgroup (no prior docetaxel, 160–220 mg), with an 89% disease control rate — suggesting meaningful clinical activity in treatment-resistant RAS-mutant lung cancer.

**Q2**: Why does daraxonrasib's multiselective mechanism matter for lung cancer patients?
**A**: By blocking active RAS across multiple mutation types rather than a single variant, the drug may reach patients with currently untreatable RAS variants, broadening access to targeted therapy beyond what single-mutation inhibitors can offer.

**Q3**: What is the clinical implication if Phase 3 confirms these results?
**A**: Oncologist Nilesh Vora noted it "could give us another tool to improve both the quality and length of life" for cancer patients — adding a targeted oral option for the ~30% of NSCLC patients carrying RAS mutations who currently lack effective targeted therapies.
