## Summary

Everyday memory lapses like forgetting names or misplacing keys are frequently mistaken for early dementia, but neuroscientist [[Tommy Wood]] says most such lapses stem from stress, fatigue, or inattention rather than neurodegeneration. Many causes of apparent cognitive decline — vitamin deficiencies, thyroid problems, medication side effects, depression, and kidney or liver issues — are treatable and reversible if caught early.

## Core Concepts

- [[Tommy Wood]] — professor of pediatrics and neuroscience at the [[University of Washington]] who distinguishes stress-driven forgetfulness from true neurodegenerative memory loss.
- [[Memory Formation Stages]] — encoding, consolidation, and retrieval; stress and sleep deprivation typically impair retrieval, while [[Neurodegenerative Disease]] impairs encoding itself.
- [[Dementia]] — clinically defined as cognitive decline severe enough to impair independent daily functioning; [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and [[Vascular Dementia]] account for 70–90% of cases.
- [[Reversible Cognitive Decline]] — includes vitamin B12/B1 deficiency, [[Hypothyroidism]], medication side effects (sedatives, anticholinergics, polypharmacy), [[Pseudodementia]] from depression or anxiety, and kidney/liver or electrolyte imbalances.
- [[Dementia Prevention]] — at least 50% of dementia cases are considered preventable through lifestyle and health risk factor modification, including hearing and vision correction.

## Key Takeaways

- **Retrieval vs. encoding**: Stress/fatigue impairs retrieving stored memories; dementia impairs encoding them in the first place.
- **Self-awareness as a signal**: Noticing your own memory lapses is reassuring — people with true neurodegenerative decline often don't notice.
- **50% preventable**: At least half of dementia cases can be prevented via lifestyle and health risk factor changes.
- **Reversible mimics**: B12/B1 deficiency, thyroid issues, medication side effects, and depression can all mimic dementia symptoms.
- **Physical health matters**: Hearing aids and cataract surgery directly reduce cognitive decline risk.
- **Belief drives action**: Expecting decline discourages preventive behavior; believing prevention is possible encourages it.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Encoding vs. Retrieval Failure]]**: Treating "forgetting" as a single phenomenon obscures that stress-based forgetting (retrieval failure) and dementia (encoding failure) are mechanistically distinct problems requiring different responses — the article's core diagnostic frame.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about everyday memory lapses like forgetting names or losing keys?
**A**: Most such lapses are caused by stress, fatigue, or inattention rather than dementia, and many apparent cognitive symptoms stem from reversible, treatable conditions.

**Q2**: What mechanism distinguishes ordinary forgetfulness from neurodegenerative memory loss?
**A**: Ordinary forgetfulness is usually a retrieval problem — the memory was encoded but is hard to access under stress — while neurodegenerative disease typically prevents the memory from being encoded at all.

**Q3**: What should someone do if they notice increasing memory lapses?
**A**: Seek early clinical evaluation for reversible causes (vitamin deficiencies, thyroid problems, medication effects, depression) and address treatable physical health issues like hearing or vision loss, since noticing the problem is itself a good sign and early intervention can prevent further decline.
