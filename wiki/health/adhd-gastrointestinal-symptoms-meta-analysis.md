## Summary

A Warwick Medical School meta-analysis of 23 studies covering over 1.9 million participants found that people with ADHD have significantly higher odds of every gastrointestinal (GI) symptom category investigated, including constipation, IBS, abdominal pain, and diarrhea. The overall elevated GI symptom odds for those with ADHD was 48% compared to neurotypical peers. No single causal mechanism has been confirmed, but gut-brain axis disruption, irregular eating habits, stress, and stimulant medication side effects are all proposed contributors.

## Core Concepts

- [[ADHD]] (Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder) — neurodevelopmental condition linked to broad GI symptom burden
- [[Gut-Brain Axis]] — bidirectional communication pathway between the central nervous system and the gastrointestinal tract
- [[Meta-Analysis]] — systematic pooling of 23 studies with 1.9 million+ participants
- [[Microbiome]] — gut microbial composition proposed as a mediating variable
- [[Emotional Dysregulation]] — ADHD-linked stress response that may affect gut motility
- [[Stimulant Medications]] — ADHD treatments (e.g., amphetamines) with known GI side effects

## Key Takeaways

- **Overall GI risk**: ADHD associated with 48% higher odds of GI symptoms vs. neurotypical peers.
- **Constipation**: 97% higher odds — largest elevated risk category found.
- **Diarrhea**: Over 2x higher odds; encopresis over 4x higher odds.
- **IBS**: 58% higher odds; abdominal pain 88% higher odds; dyspepsia 52% higher odds.
- **Mechanisms unclear**: Gut-brain axis, diet irregularity, stress, and medications all implicated.
- **Medication impact**: Stimulants can cause nausea, appetite suppression, and abdominal pain.
- **Practical advice**: Regular meals, hydration, fiber, exercise, and reminders for consistency.
- **Research gap**: Longitudinal studies on microbiome + clinical variables needed to establish causality.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Bidirectional Causality]]**: The gut-brain axis operates in both directions — ADHD may worsen GI symptoms, but GI dysfunction may also exacerbate ADHD symptoms, making it impossible to identify a single root cause without longitudinal data.
- **[[Implementation Intention]]**: Baker's observation that "knowing what to do and being able to do it consistently are not always the same thing" maps precisely onto why standard dietary advice fails ADHD patients — executive function deficits undercut behavioral follow-through.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core finding of the Warwick Medical School meta-analysis on ADHD and GI symptoms?
**A**: People with ADHD have 48% higher overall odds of gastrointestinal symptoms compared to neurotypical individuals, with elevated risks found across every GI symptom category examined in the 23-study meta-analysis of 1.9 million+ participants.

**Q2**: What specific GI symptom showed the highest elevated odds in people with ADHD, and by how much?
**A**: Constipation showed the highest elevated odds at 97% higher likelihood, followed by abdominal pain at 88% and encopresis at over 4x higher odds compared to neurotypical peers.

**Q3**: What are the practical implications for clinicians and ADHD patients regarding GI health?
**A**: Clinicians should recognize and not dismiss GI complaints in ADHD patients, discuss medication-related GI side effects, and support patients in building consistent routines around meals, hydration, and fiber — using reminders to compensate for executive function challenges.
