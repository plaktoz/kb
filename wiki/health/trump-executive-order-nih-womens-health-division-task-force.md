## Summary
President Trump signed an executive order, spearheaded by Melania Trump, creating a White House women's health task force and a new women's health division within the NIH. The division must be established within 90 days, works alongside the existing Office of Research on Women's Health, and prioritizes translating research into clinical care. The order arrives amid administration NIH cuts, a mifepristone safety review, and other contested women's health policy moves.

## Core Concepts
- [[Executive Order]] creating a White House task force led by [[Susie Wiles]] and Health Secretary [[Robert F. Kennedy Jr]].
- New [[National Institutes of Health]] women's health division, coordinated with the [[Office of Research on Women's Health]] (est. 1990).
- [[Sex as a Biological Variable]] in grant accountability and [[Clinical Trials]] inclusion of women and girls.
- Understudied conditions: cardiovascular disease, cancer, autoimmune disease, endometriosis, menopause, Alzheimer's, osteoporosis.
- Context: [[Mifepristone]] safety review, [[Hormone Replacement Therapy]] warning removal, [[Title X]] funding conditions.

## Key Takeaways
- **New Division**: NIH women's health division must be established within 90 days.
- **Task Force**: Led by Susie Wiles and RFK Jr.; first meeting held Oct 19.
- **Focus**: Speed diagnosis, improve treatment, translate discoveries into clinical practice and policy.
- **Biden Orders Revoked**: Replaces March 2024 order and November 2023 memorandum on women's health.
- **Accountability**: Grant recipients must consider sex as a biological variable.
- **Criticism**: Medical groups call the mifepristone review politically motivated.
- **Policy Context**: Administration also pursued NIH cuts and downsized the federal health workforce.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does the executive order create?
**A**: A White House task force on women's health and a new women's health division within the NIH, to be established within 90 days and coordinated with the existing Office of Research on Women's Health.

**Q2**: What conditions and requirements will the division focus on?
**A**: Understudied conditions affecting women uniquely or disproportionately, such as cardiovascular disease, endometriosis, menopause and Alzheimer's. It also boosts women's inclusion in clinical trials and holds grantees accountable for considering sex as a biological variable.

**Q3**: What context complicates how the order is received?
**A**: The administration has pushed NIH and healthcare workforce cuts, launched a mifepristone safety review criticized as political, and removed a hormone therapy warning that led to shortages.
