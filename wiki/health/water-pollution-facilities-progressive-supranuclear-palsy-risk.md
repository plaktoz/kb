## Summary

A Neurology study of more than 500,000 UK participants found that people living within about three miles of industrial facilities emitting water pollutants were more likely to develop [[Progressive Supranuclear Palsy]] (PSP). The association held for pesticides, inorganic and organic pollutants, and heavy metals, but it does not prove causation. The overall risk of PSP remains low regardless of where people live.

## Core Concepts

- [[Progressive Supranuclear Palsy]]: a rare, fast-progressing movement disorder within [[Atypical Parkinsonism]], sharing symptoms with [[Parkinson's Disease]] but responding differently to treatment.
- [[Environmental Water Pollution]]: emissions from wastewater treatment plants, intensive aquaculture, chemical plants and thermal power stations.
- [[Pollutant Types]]: heavy metals (lead, mercury, cadmium), pesticides (atrazine, simazine, diuron), organic pollutants (benzene, toluene) and inorganic pollutants (phosphorus, nitrogen, fluorides).
- [[UK Biobank-Style Cohort Study]]: 500,000+ people followed up to 25 years; 221 developed PSP, each matched to 10 controls by age and sex.
- Researcher: Charlie Weige Zhao, MD, of Harvard Medical School.

## Key Takeaways

- **Proximity Risk**: Living within ~3 miles of polluting facilities linked to higher PSP likelihood.
- **Pesticides**: 66% higher PSP likelihood near pesticide-emitting facilities after adjustment.
- **Inorganic Pollutants**: 60% higher likelihood near facilities emitting phosphorus, nitrogen, fluorides.
- **Organic Pollutants**: 52% higher likelihood near benzene, ethyl benzene, toluene emitters.
- **Heavy Metals**: 38% higher likelihood near lead, mercury, cadmium emitters.
- **No Association**: Chlorinated solvents and persistent organic pollutants showed no link.
- **Distance and Duration**: Risk fell with distance up to ~6 miles and varied with residence length.
- **Limitations**: Job histories unavailable; facilities emitted multiple pollutants, so single effects were not isolable.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Association is not causation** — An observed link between exposure and outcome does not establish that the exposure causes the outcome; confounders and mixed exposures can produce the same pattern. · *model:* [[Correlation vs Causation]]
→ explains: Limitations, Proximity Risk

**T2. Risk scales with exposure** — If an exposure plays a causal role, risk should vary with its intensity, so a gradient by distance or duration strengthens the case. · *model:* [[Dose-Response Relationship]]
→ explains: Distance and Duration

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the study find about water-pollution sources and PSP?
**A**: People living within about three miles of industrial facilities emitting water pollutants were more likely to develop PSP. The study shows only an association, not proof of causation.

**Q2**: What evidence suggested a gradient in risk?
**A**: Risk decreased in proportion to distance from the facility, up to about six miles, and the length of time living nearby also seemed to play a role.

**Q3**: Why can't the study isolate the effect of a single pollutant, and what does that imply?
**A**: Many facilities emitted more than one pollutant and job-related exposure data were incomplete, so further studies are needed to confirm the findings.
