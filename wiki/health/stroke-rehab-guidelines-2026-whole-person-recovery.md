## Summary

The 2026 American Stroke Association guidelines update stroke rehabilitation standards, calling for therapy to begin within 48 hours of medical stability while avoiding high-intensity exercise in the first 24 hours. The updated framework takes a whole-person approach, integrating physical, cognitive, communication, and mental health care through coordinated multidisciplinary teams. Caregiver support and quality-of-life concerns — including sleep, pain, and sexuality — are formally incorporated for the first time.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Neuroplasticity]]**: Post-stroke, neuroplasticity genes remain elevated for one to three months, creating a heightened window for learning and recovery — the biological rationale for early mobilization.
- **[[American Stroke Association]]**: Issued the *2026 Guideline for Adult Stroke Rehabilitation and Recovery*, published in the journal *Stroke*, replacing 2016 recommendations.
- **[[Multidisciplinary Care]]**: Recovery requires coordinated teams addressing physical, cognitive, communication, and mental health dimensions simultaneously.
- **[[Post-Stroke Depression]]**: Affects roughly one in three survivors; up to a quarter develop anxiety — both impede rehabilitation progress.
- **[[Caregiver Burden]]**: An estimated 2.7 million Americans care for stroke survivors; nearly 44% of caregivers show depression symptoms (2025 systematic review).
- **[[Teach-Back Method]]**: Active educational technique recommended for caregiver instruction — families practice hands-on care and confirm understanding by repeating it back.

## Key Takeaways

- **Early mobilization**: Rehab should start within 48 hours of medical stability.
- **Avoid high intensity early**: No moderate-to-high exercise in the first 24 hours post-stroke.
- **Neuroplasticity window**: Brain is more receptive to recovery for 1–3 months after stroke.
- **Mental health screening**: One-in-three survivors get depression; screening is now standard.
- **Whole-person scope**: Sleep, pain, sexuality, and role changes are addressed in guidelines.
- **Caregiver involvement**: Include caregivers early; repeat education over time as crises reduce absorption.
- **Employment and parenting**: Younger stroke survivors require rehab plans accounting for work and child-rearing.
- **Stroke burden**: ~800,000 Americans affected annually; fourth-leading cause of death.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Window of Opportunity]]**: The elevated neuroplasticity gene expression (1–3 months post-stroke) is a time-bounded biological window — starting therapy early exploits the mechanism while it is active, not after it fades.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Focusing rehabilitation solely on measurable physical outcomes risks neglecting harder-to-quantify factors (mental health, sexuality, caregiver wellbeing) that equally determine long-term quality of life — the guideline's whole-person expansion corrects for exactly this narrowing.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central update in the 2026 ASA stroke rehabilitation guidelines?
**A**: Rehab should begin within 48 hours of medical stability, using a whole-person approach that covers physical, cognitive, communication, and mental health needs through multidisciplinary care.

**Q2**: Why does starting therapy early matter neurologically?
**A**: Neuroplasticity genes activated by stroke remain elevated for one to three months, creating a period of enhanced brain receptivity to learning — early therapy exploits this neural window before it closes.

**Q3**: How should clinicians and families handle caregiver education during the acute crisis phase?
**A**: Educational information should be repeated over time because families in crisis absorb less initially; hands-on practice and teach-back techniques reinforce understanding more effectively than one-time verbal instruction.
