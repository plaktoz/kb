## Summary

A prospective real-world study of 3,856 CT angiography (CTA) examinations at Northwell Health found that an FDA-cleared AI algorithm (Aidoc's aiOS) detected 55 brain aneurysms initially missed by radiologists — a 39% relative increase in detection. While AI showed higher sensitivity (84.6% vs. 71.8%), radiologists were more precise in confirming true positives (PPV 92.7% vs. 78.2%), suggesting the two work best as complementary partners. The study does not yet establish whether increased detection translates to improved patient outcomes.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Aidoc aiOS]]** — FDA-cleared AI algorithm deployed for brain aneurysm triage on CT angiography
- **[[CT Angiography (CTA)]]** — imaging modality used to visualize blood vessels; the study's primary diagnostic tool
- **[[Sensitivity vs. Specificity]]** — AI favored sensitivity (catching more true cases); radiologists favored precision (fewer false positives)
- **[[Positive Predictive Value (PPV)]]** — measure of how often a flagged finding is a true positive; radiologists scored 92.7% vs. AI's 78.2%
- **[[Complementary AI in Medicine]]** — framing AI as augmentation for clinicians rather than replacement
- **[[Northwell Health]]** — large health system where the prospective study was conducted
- **[[Journal of the American College of Radiology]]** — publication venue for the study

## Key Takeaways

- **Detection boost**: AI found 55 aneurysms radiologists missed; a 39% relative increase.
- **Radiologist edge**: Radiologists caught 30 aneurysms AI missed; higher precision overall.
- **High agreement**: AI and radiologists agreed on over 96% of all examinations.
- **False positives**: 46 of 101 AI-only findings turned out to be false positives.
- **Setting matters**: Strongest AI benefit in inpatient/ED settings; outpatient benefit limited.
- **Small aneurysm catch**: AI primarily found small, clinically significant missed lesions.
- **Not a replacement**: Study supports AI as a complementary tool; physicians retain final authority.
- **Outcome gap**: Increased detection has not yet been shown to improve patient outcomes.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Division of Labor]]**: AI and radiologists each have a distinct comparative advantage — AI's higher sensitivity catches missed cases while radiologist precision filters false positives — making collaboration strictly better than either alone.
- **[[Base Rate Neglect]]**: AI's false positive rate is higher in outpatient settings where aneurysm prevalence is lower, illustrating how the same tool's real-world value shifts dramatically with the base rate of the condition being screened.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the Northwell Health brain aneurysm AI study?
**A**: An FDA-cleared AI algorithm detected 55 brain aneurysms initially missed by radiologists across 3,856 CTA exams, representing a 39% relative increase in detection compared to radiologists alone.

**Q2**: How do AI sensitivity and radiologist precision compare, and why does that matter clinically?
**A**: AI had higher sensitivity (84.6% vs. 71.8%), catching more true aneurysms, but radiologists had higher PPV (92.7% vs. 78.2%), meaning fewer false alarms — the two metrics trade off and together explain why a collaborative model outperforms either alone.

**Q3**: What limitation does the study acknowledge, and what does it imply for clinical adoption?
**A**: The study does not establish whether higher detection rates lead to better patient outcomes, meaning the clinical benefit of acting on AI-flagged small aneurysms remains unproven and warrants further research before changing treatment protocols.
