## Summary

The [[Heart Failure Society of America]]'s HF Stats 2026 report finds that heart failure mortality in the US has hit a new peak. Age-adjusted death rates rose about 37% from 2010 to 2023, reversing more than a decade of progress, and HF contributed to more than 450,000 deaths in 2023. The burden is shifting toward younger adults: hospitalization rates are rising among people under 65 while falling among older adults. The report presents 12 principal findings, covering underused proven therapies, persistent disparities, rising costs and a strained specialist workforce.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Heart Failure]] (HF)**: About 7.7 million US adults (roughly 3% of adults) live with HF. That number is projected to reach 11.4 million by 2050, driven by aging and by rising [[Obesity]], [[Hypertension]] and other cardiometabolic risk factors.
- **HF Stats 2026**: An annual epidemiology report published in the [[Journal of Cardiac Failure]] and on HFStats.org. The 2026 committee was chaired by [[Eiran Gorodeski]] of University Hospitals Harrington Heart & Vascular Institute.
- **Stage A HF**: The "at-risk" stage. About one-third of US adults without symptomatic HF already meet its criteria.
- **[[Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy]] (GDMT)**: In real-world data, only 18% of more than 3 million patients with [[HFrEF]] receive quadruple GDMT.
- **Underdiagnosed cardiomyopathies**: [[ATTR-CM]] (transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy), [[Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy]] and genetic cardiomyopathies. Genetic causes account for an estimated 16–36% of [[Dilated Cardiomyopathy]].
- **Advanced HF and [[Cardiogenic Shock]]**: Stage D HF affects an estimated 350,000–500,000 adults, and cardiogenic shock complicates 60,000–120,000 hospitalizations a year.
- **[[Health Disparities]]**: Black individuals have the highest age-adjusted HF incidence and mortality. Outcomes also vary by state and between rural and urban areas.
- **Workforce gap**: There were 1,523 certified Advanced HF and Transplant Cardiology physicians as of June 2025, and fewer applicants are entering the Match per position.

## Key Takeaways

- **Deaths**: HF contributed to 452,573 US deaths in 2023 (14.6% of all-cause deaths).
- **Reversal**: Age-adjusted HF mortality rose about 37% from 2010 to 2023. Early 2024 data show the rise continuing.
- **Young adults**: Crude HF mortality rose 121% among ages 25–44 (2001–2020).
- **Middle age**: Crude HF mortality rose 96% among ages 45–64 over the same period.
- **Hospitalization split**: Rates rose for adults under 65 and fell for those 65+ (2010–2022).
- **Lifetime risk**: Nearly 1 in 4 Americans will develop HF.
- **Therapy gap**: Only 18% of HFrEF patients receive quadruple GDMT.
- **Costs**: Estimates range from $46B (direct and indirect, 2020) to $312B (broader healthcare costs).

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Implementation Gap]]**: Gorodeski notes that treatment advances have not reduced the population-level burden. With quadruple GDMT used in only 18% of HFrEF patients, proven therapies are not reaching most of the people who need them, so mortality still rises.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the headline finding of HF Stats 2026?
**A**: US heart failure mortality is at a new peak, up about 37% (age-adjusted) from 2010 to 2023, and younger adults show some of the most concerning increases.

**Q2**: What evidence shows HF is shifting toward younger Americans?
**A**: From 2010 to 2022, HF hospitalizations rose among adults under 65 and fell among those 65 and older. Crude mortality rose 121% for ages 25–44 and 96% for ages 45–64 from 2001 to 2020.

**Q3**: Where does the report suggest the biggest gains could come from?
**A**: Better prevention, earlier recognition and wider use of proven therapies, since only 18% of HFrEF patients receive quadruple GDMT. Addressing disparities and workforce shortages would also help.
