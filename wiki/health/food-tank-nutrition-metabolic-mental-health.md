## Summary

At Food Tank's Capitol Hill Luncheon, experts including [[Dariush Mozaffarian]] and [[Eric Verdin]] argued that diet drives metabolic, cognitive, and mental health outcomes as much as physical disease risk, citing that [[Ultra-Processed Food]] makes up 50% of the average American diet and is linked to chronic inflammation and mental health conditions. Speakers called the current system "sickcare" rather than healthcare and urged more research and policy investment into nutrition and metabolic approaches to serious illness, including mental illness. The event highlighted rare bipartisan momentum around food-as-medicine policy.

## Core Concepts

- [[Ultra-Processed Food]] — makes up ~50% of average American nutrition; engineered for palatability, linked to disease and poor mental health
- [[Food is Medicine]] — Tufts institute and movement framing nutrition as a clinical intervention
- [[Chronic Inflammation]] — mechanism Eric Verdin links poor diet to impaired brain function
- [[Dariush Mozaffarian]] — Director of the Food is Medicine Institute at Tufts
- [[Eric Verdin]] — Buck Institute researcher on nutrition's role in metabolism and aging

## Key Takeaways

- Ultra-processed foods account for roughly 50% of average American nutrition
- 90% of the $4.5T spent on US healthcare in 2022 went to chronic disease patients
- Strong evidence links ultra-processed diets to mental health conditions
- Ketogenic diet cited as an example of a metabolic approach to bipolar disorder
- Speakers call the current system "sickcare," urging more prevention and nutrition research
- Rare bipartisan political momentum exists around food-as-medicine policy
