## Summary

State legislators across the U.S. have responded to concerns from patients and medical professionals by enacting 84 new AI laws in 27 states in 2026, as documented by the TCAI Mid-Year Report. Key areas include restricting AI in insurance authorization decisions, banning AI therapy chatbots, and requiring informed patient consent for AI use. These laws collectively establish that human clinicians must retain final authority over medical decisions, even when AI tools are involved.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI in Healthcare]]**: Growing deployment of AI tools in insurance decisions, therapy, and medical record-keeping — now subject to state-level guardrails.
- **[[Medical Authorization]]**: Seven states passed laws requiring AI-driven insurance decisions to be reviewed and approved by a licensed physician or clinician.
- **[[AI Therapy Chatbots]]**: Five states banned commercial AI chatbot therapy services, following Illinois and Nevada's 2025 precedent.
- **[[Patient Consent]]**: States such as Iowa mandate verbal disclosure when AI recording tools are used in medical appointments.
- **[[Scope of Practice]]**: Utah clarified that AI does not qualify as a licensed "innovation" within a medical clinic's permitted scope.
- **[[Healthcare AI Regulation]]**: Broad state-level legislative trend toward explicit human oversight and accountability in AI-assisted care.

## Key Takeaways

- **84 new AI laws** enacted across 27 states in 2026 per TCAI Mid-Year Report.
- **7 states** restricted AI use in health insurance authorization decisions.
- **AI cannot be the sole basis** for denying, delaying, or modifying healthcare services (Washington SB 5395).
- **Denials must involve a licensed physician** — not just an algorithm (Alabama SB 63, Colorado HB 1139).
- **5 states banned AI therapy chatbots** commercially, building on 2025 laws.
- **Tennessee SB 1580**: AI systems cannot advertise as qualified mental health professionals.
- **Iowa HB 475**: Verbal disclosure required before AI records any medical appointment.
- **Utah SB 150**: AI is not classified as a medical "innovation" under scope-of-practice rules.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Human-in-the-Loop]]**: These laws operationalize the principle that consequential decisions — denial of care, mental health treatment — require human accountability at the point of final judgment, not just process automation.
- **[[Precautionary Principle]]**: Legislatures are acting ahead of widespread harm evidence, restricting AI in high-stakes clinical contexts before failures accumulate — a classic precautionary stance in novel technology governance.
