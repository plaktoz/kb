## Summary

A study published Aug. 26, 2026, in [[JAMA Network Open]] found that adult children of [[Centenarians]] had a 42% lower risk of dying at any given age and lived about three years longer than people whose parents died at 85 or younger. They also had lower risks of [[Heart Disease]] and [[Hypertension]], advantages that held after adjusting for lifestyle factors, though not all findings were statistically significant across analyses. A healthy lifestyle remains the best way to improve longevity odds regardless of genes.

## Core Concepts

- **Pooled cohort analysis**: Data from the [[LonGenity Study]], the [[New England Centenarian Study]] (US), and the [[UK Biobank]], following participants for 16–29 years, analyzed identically to test consistency.
- **Comparison groups**: 2,319 adult children of centenarians vs. 1,711 people whose parents died at 85 or younger.
- **[[Familial Longevity]]**: Living to 100 appears to run in families and may confer a healthier old age on offspring.
- **[[Confounding Variable]] adjustment**: Results held after accounting for smoking, diet, exercise, alcohol use, and education.
- **Statistical caution**: In some analyses only the high blood pressure finding stayed [[Statistical Significance|statistically significant]].
- **[[Healthy Aging]]**: Findings may help identify what drives healthy aging.

## Key Takeaways

- **Mortality Risk**: Children of centenarians had a 42% lower risk of death at any age.
- **Lifespan Gain**: They lived about three years longer on average.
- **Heart Disease**: 33% lower risk of heart disease.
- **Blood Pressure**: 32% lower hypertension risk, developing roughly five years later.
- **No Cancer Benefit**: Long-lived parents were not linked to lower cancer risk.
- **Lifestyle Adjusted**: Advantages persisted after controlling for smoking, diet, exercise, alcohol, education.
- **Interpret Cautiously**: Some longevity and heart findings fell just short of significance.
- **Lifestyle Still Matters**: Healthy habits remain the best lever, "good genes" or not.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Adjust for confounders before attributing an effect** — An observed association between parental longevity and offspring health only supports a familial cause if it survives controlling for shared lifestyle factors that could independently explain it. · *model:* [[Confounding Variable]]
→ explains: Lifestyle Adjusted, Mortality Risk, Heart Disease

**T2. Strength of evidence bounds strength of conclusions** — When findings fall short of statistical significance in some analyses, conclusions must be held tentatively, with support coming from consistency across studies rather than any single result.
→ explains: Interpret Cautiously, Blood Pressure
