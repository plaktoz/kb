## Summary

Perimenopause affects roughly 2 million American women annually, typically beginning in the mid-40s and lasting 4–8 years, yet women correctly identify their menopause stage only about half the time. Common symptoms span physical, emotional, and metabolic domains, but experts caution against assuming all midlife symptoms are hormonal. Lifestyle adjustments and targeted treatments — including hormones, birth control, or antidepressants — can address specific presentations.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Perimenopause]]**: The transition phase before [[Menopause]], marked by hormonal fluctuations and wide-ranging symptoms over 4–8 years.
- **[[Hot Flashes]] and Night Sweats**: Among the most recognizable vasomotor symptoms driven by declining [[Estrogen]] levels.
- **Emotional Symptoms**: Anxiety and irritability are most common; women with prior [[PMS]], [[Postpartum Depression]], or mood disorders face elevated risk.
- **Metabolic Changes**: Weight gain occurs even without behavioral changes, requiring proactive dietary and exercise adjustments.
- **[[Dr. Stephanie Faubion]] (Mayo Clinic)**: Emphasizes not attributing every midlife symptom to perimenopause — some (e.g. heart palpitations, itchy skin) may signal unrelated serious conditions.
- **Diagnostic Confusion**: A recent survey found women correctly identified their menopause stage only ~50% of the time, highlighting widespread lack of awareness.

## Key Takeaways

- Roughly 2 million American women enter perimenopause each year.
- Typical onset is mid-40s; the transition lasts 4–8 years.
- Core symptoms: hot flashes, night sweats, sleep disruption, mood changes, irregular periods.
- Vaginal dryness and reduced sexual desire are also common.
- Prior PMS or postpartum depression raises emotional symptom risk.
- Not all midlife symptoms are perimenopause — rule out other conditions.
- Key lifestyle steps: exercise, diet adjustment, monitor BP/blood sugar/cholesterol, quit smoking.
- Treatments vary by symptom: hormones, birth control, or antidepressants.
- Ask your doctor directly: "Do you think I'm in perimenopause?"

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Base Rate Neglect]]**: Women misidentify their menopause stage ~50% of the time, likely because they reason from vivid individual symptoms rather than population-level stage prevalence — the classic failure to anchor on base rates before updating on symptoms.
- **[[Symptom Attribution Bias]]**: Defaulting to a single salient explanation (perimenopause) for all midlife symptoms can mask unrelated, potentially serious conditions — reinforcing the need to consider competing hypotheses before concluding.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is perimenopause and how prevalent is it?
**A**: Perimenopause is the hormonal transition phase before menopause, affecting roughly 2 million American women each year, typically beginning in the mid-40s and lasting 4–8 years.

**Q2**: Why should women not assume all midlife symptoms are perimenopause?
**A**: Dr. Stephanie Faubion warns that symptoms such as heart palpitations or itchy skin may signal unrelated and sometimes serious conditions, so evaluation for other causes is important.

**Q3**: What practical steps can a woman take if she suspects perimenopause?
**A**: She should ask her doctor directly whether she is in perimenopause, describe her symptoms, and proactively adjust lifestyle factors — exercise, diet, blood pressure monitoring, and smoking cessation — while discussing treatment options tailored to her specific symptoms.
