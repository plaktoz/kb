## Summary
A University of New Hampshire study of visitors to College Woods found that nature visits improved mood and raised self-reported restoration scores by nearly 30%. Perceived biodiversity and pleasant natural sounds predicted well-being better than measured species counts. Educational "Meet the neighbors!" signs modestly boosted perceived biodiversity and positive mood.

## Core Concepts
- [[Lauren Ferguson]]: lead researcher, UNH assistant professor of recreation management and policy.
- [[Perceived Biodiversity]]: visitors' perception predicted well-being more than actual bird and mammal species counts.
- [[Nature Soundscapes]]: birdsong, rustling leaves and flowing water strongly predicted well-being.
- [[Restorative Environments]]: relaxation and recovery from mental fatigue.
- [[Educational Signage]]: the "Meet the neighbors!" sign highlighting 20+ mammals, 50 birds and 110 plants and trees.
- [[Landscape and Urban Planning]]: journal where the study was published; implications for park managers and planners.

## Key Takeaways
- **Restoration Gain**: Self-reported restoration rose nearly 30% after visits.
- **Perception Over Count**: Perceived biodiversity predicted well-being better than measured species numbers.
- **Sound Matters**: Pleasant natural sounds were another strong predictor.
- **Signage Effect**: Signs raised perceived biodiversity and were linked to better mood.
- **Limits of Signs**: No meaningful link to fewer negative emotions or restoration.
- **Seasonality**: Restoration greater in spring and summer; mood improved year-round.
- **Study Design**: Surveys before and after visits, June 2023 to May 2024, plus wildlife cameras and bird surveys.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Perception mediates benefit** — The benefit people draw from an environment depends on what they notice and perceive, not only on its objective properties; raising awareness can increase the benefit.
→ explains: Perception Over Count, Signage Effect, Sound Matters

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does the UNH study conclude about why nature walks improve mental health?
**A**: Sounds experienced, awareness of biodiversity and educational signage about species help explain the benefit. Perceived biodiversity predicted well-being better than actual species counts.

**Q2**: What data supports the restorative effect of the woods visit?
**A**: Visitors surveyed before and after visits in College Woods reported more positive emotions and fewer negative ones. Restoration scores rose by nearly 30% on average.

**Q3**: How can park managers apply the findings?
**A**: Biodiversity education signs are a simple, inexpensive tool to raise awareness and boost positive mood, though they did not show a clear link to restoration or reduced negative emotions.
