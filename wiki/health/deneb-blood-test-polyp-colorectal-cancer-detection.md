## Summary

An experimental blood test called DENEB demonstrates superior performance for detecting precancerous polyps and early-stage colorectal cancer noninvasively, according to a multicenter study published in *The Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology*. DENEB detected 92% of stage I–III colorectal cancers and 81% of advanced adenomas, far outperforming the FDA-approved Shield test's 13% polyp detection rate. While not a replacement for colonoscopy, DENEB represents a meaningful step toward accessible screening tools that catch cancer before it forms.

## Core Concepts

- **[[DENEB Blood Test]]**: An experimental [[Liquid Biopsy]] that detects both colorectal cancers and precancerous polyps (advanced adenomas) noninvasively from a blood draw.
- **[[Colorectal Cancer]] Screening Gap**: Colorectal cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related death in Americans under 50; accessible, affordable screening tests are critical for closing the gap among unscreened adults.
- **Advanced Adenomas**: Precancerous polyps that, if detected early, can be removed before they develop into invasive cancer — historically difficult for blood tests to identify.
- **[[Shield Test]]**: The currently FDA-approved blood-based colorectal cancer screening test; detects only ~13% of precancerous polyps compared to DENEB's 81%.
- **[[SimpleScreen CRC]]**: Freenome's second FDA-approved blood-based CRC screening test (July 2026); similar cancer-detection performance but weak on polyp detection.
- **Colonoscopy**: The gold standard for colorectal cancer screening — neither DENEB nor existing blood tests are intended to replace it.

## Key Takeaways

- **DENEB sensitivity**: 92% of stage I–III colorectal cancers detected.
- **Polyp detection**: 81% of advanced adenomas detected — a major leap over existing blood tests.
- **Shield comparison**: FDA-approved Shield detects only ~13% of precancerous polyps.
- **Study scale**: Over 1,500 adults tested across China, Japan, and Spain.
- **Mortality benefit**: A separate JAMA Network study found screening reduces colorectal cancer mortality by up to 43%.
- **Not a replacement**: DENEB is positioned as a complement to, not a substitute for, colonoscopy.
- **Expert quote**: Ajay Goel notes most blood tests "have struggled to detect these precursor lesions" — DENEB's polyp sensitivity is its key differentiator.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Prevention vs. Detection]]**: Catching precancerous polyps before they become cancer is categorically different from detecting existing cancer — DENEB's polyp sensitivity shifts the test from a diagnostic tool to a genuine prevention tool, embodying the principle that upstream intervention yields compounding returns.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Prior blood tests optimized for cancer-detection sensitivity metrics, inadvertently neglecting polyp detection; DENEB resets the target to the clinically more valuable upstream signal.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is DENEB's core claim over existing blood-based colorectal cancer screening tests?
**A**: DENEB detects 81% of advanced adenomas (precancerous polyps) — far higher than the FDA-approved Shield test's 13% — making it the first blood test to offer meaningful early-intervention capability before cancer forms.

**Q2**: What detection rates did DENEB achieve in the multicenter Lancet study?
**A**: DENEB detected 92% of stage I–III colorectal cancers and 81% of advanced adenomas across a study of more than 1,500 adults in China, Japan, and Spain.

**Q3**: How should DENEB be positioned relative to colonoscopy if it gains approval?
**A**: DENEB is not intended to replace colonoscopy, which remains the gold standard; it would serve as a more accessible, noninvasive screening option — particularly valuable for reaching the large population of eligible adults who currently avoid colonoscopy.
