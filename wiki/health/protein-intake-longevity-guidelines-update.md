## Summary

A June 2026 perspective paper argues official [[Protein Intake Guidelines]] target disease prevention rather than long-term health, suggesting physically active adults, older adults, and pregnant people could benefit from roughly double the current baseline (up to ~1 g/lb of body weight). Registered dietitians interviewed recommend a more practical range of 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day depending on activity level, cautioning that higher protein intake should not crowd out fiber and other nutrients, and that safety upper limits (~2 g/kg/day) and kidney health matter for specific populations.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Protein Intake Guidelines]]** — current RDA of 0.75-0.8 g/kg/day was set to prevent deficiency, not optimize muscle health or healthy aging
- **[[Personalized Protein Targets]]** — needs vary by age, activity level, health status, and medications (e.g., GLP-1 users)
- **[[TMAO]]** — trimethylamine N-oxide; elevated by high red/processed-meat intake, linked to cardiovascular risk
- **[[Protein Source Quality]]** — whether protein comes from processed meat versus whole foods matters as much as total quantity

## Key Takeaways

- **Guidelines may be outdated**: current RDA targets deficiency prevention, not longevity optimization.
- **Practical range: 1.2-1.6 g/kg/day** for most people focused on muscle preservation and metabolic health.
- **Upper safe limit ~2 g/kg/day** for healthy adults with normal kidney function; lower, individualized targets needed for chronic kidney disease.
- **Source matters**: processed/red meat-heavy protein intake raises TMAO and cardiovascular risk versus whole-food sources.
- **Protein alone isn't a longevity plan**: must be paired with resistance training and regular physical activity.
