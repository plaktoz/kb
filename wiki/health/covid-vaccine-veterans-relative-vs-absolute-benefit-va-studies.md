## Summary
A VA study of 931,943 veterans found that last season's COVID vaccine plus flu shot cut COVID hospitalizations 43.6% versus flu shot alone, but the absolute gain was only about three fewer hospitalizations per 10,000 people. The article argues both numbers matter, that low COVID circulation shrank the absolute benefit, and that observational data cannot prove causation.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Relative vs. Absolute Risk]]**: A 43.6% relative drop equals about 3 fewer admissions per 10,000 over 180 days.
- **[[Observational Study]]**: VA health-record data cannot prove the vaccine caused every difference.
- **[[Confounding Variable]]**: Vaccinated and comparison groups may differ in ways records miss.
- **[[mRNA-1283 (mNexspike)]]**: Moderna's vaccine showed 47.0% relative advantage against hospitalization versus Pfizer-BioNTech in adults 65+, partly Moderna-funded.
- **[[Vaccine Co-administration]]**: A VA study of about 2.5 million adults found no added risk across 46 adverse reactions.
- **[[CDC Vaccination Guidance]]**: 2026-2027 vaccine recommended for adults 18+; two doses for 65+.
- **[[Department of Veterans Affairs]]**: Source of the health-record data.

## Key Takeaways
- **Relative Benefit**: 43.6% fewer COVID hospitalizations and 31.7% fewer emergency visits over six months.
- **Absolute Benefit**: About three fewer hospitalizations and seven fewer ER visits per 10,000 people.
- **Season Matters**: Low COVID incidence shrinks absolute benefit; a high-transmission winter would prevent more stays.
- **High-Risk Gain**: Adults 65+ and those with chronic disease benefit most.
- **Product Comparison**: mNexspike's hospitalization edge had a wide 95% CI of 5.4% to 74.8%.
- **Generalizability**: Veterans are mostly older men; results may not transfer to younger adults or women.
- **Same-Day Shots**: COVID and flu shots together showed no added adverse-reaction risk.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Relative and absolute effects answer different questions** — A large relative reduction can correspond to a small absolute benefit when baseline event rates are low; the same relative protection prevents more events when baseline risk is higher. · *model:* [[Relative vs. Absolute Risk]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the VA study find about COVID vaccination and hospitalizations?
**A**: Veterans who got the updated COVID vaccine with a flu shot had 43.6% fewer COVID hospitalizations over six months than flu-shot-only veterans. Emergency visits were 31.7% lower.

**Q2**: Why was the absolute benefit only about three fewer hospitalizations per 10,000?
**A**: COVID was less common last season, so fewer people were at risk of hospitalization. A high-transmission winter would give the same relative protection more events to prevent.

**Q3**: How should this inform decisions about getting the shot?
**A**: Both numbers matter. The benefit is greatest for adults 65+ and people with chronic conditions or weakened immunity, and they can ask clinicians about preferred products and timing.
