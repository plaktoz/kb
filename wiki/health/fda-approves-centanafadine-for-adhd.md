## Summary

The FDA approved centanafadine (Simtriyo), developed by Otsuka Pharmaceutical, as the first-in-class norepinephrine, dopamine, and serotonin reuptake inhibitor ([[NDSRI]]) for [[ADHD]] in adults and children aged 6 and older (>20 kg). Approval was based on four phase 3 trials demonstrating statistically significant symptom reduction versus placebo at week 6 across pediatric, adolescent, and adult populations. Post hoc analyses further showed improvements in executive function domains and emotional dysregulation.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Centanafadine]] (Simtriyo)**: First FDA-approved [[NDSRI]] — a triple reuptake inhibitor of norepinephrine, dopamine, and serotonin — for [[ADHD]] treatment.
- **[[ADHD]]**: A complex, heterogeneous neurodevelopmental disorder affecting school, work, and relationships across the lifespan; treated across stimulant and nonstimulant drug classes.
- **Nonstimulant ADHD treatment**: Centanafadine expands nonstimulant options alongside existing agents like [[atomoxetine]], with low dependence and abuse potential.
- **[[Executive Function]]**: Post hoc analyses showed improvements in time management, planning/prioritization, task initiation/completion, and working memory.
- **[[Emotional Dysregulation]]**: Improvement noted in this domain, a commonly underaddressed dimension of ADHD symptomatology.
- **[[Otsuka Pharmaceutical]]**: Developer of centanafadine; submitted NDA in November 2025, accepted for priority review January 2026.
- **[[ADHD Rating Scale-5]] (ADHD-RS-5)**: Primary endpoint used in pediatric/adolescent trials; significant improvement in high-dose cohorts at week 6.
- **[[ADHD Investigator Symptom Rating Scale]]**: Primary endpoint for adult trials; both 200 mg and 400 mg doses showed clinically meaningful improvement at week 6.

## Key Takeaways

- **First-in-class**: Centanafadine is the first FDA-approved [[NDSRI]] for [[ADHD]].
- **Broad approval**: Covers adults and children aged ≥6 years weighing >20 kg.
- **Phase 3 evidence**: Four trials across children, adolescents, and adults met primary endpoints.
- **Dosing**: Adult doses of 200 mg and 400 mg both showed significant symptom reduction.
- **Executive function gains**: Post hoc data support improvement in planning, working memory, and task completion.
- **Safety**: Main adverse effects are decreased appetite and headache; rash/nausea also seen in younger patients.
- **Low abuse potential**: Characterized as having low dependence and abuse potential — a key differentiator from stimulants.
- **Priority review**: NDA filed November 2025, accepted for priority review January 2026.
