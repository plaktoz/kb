# Isometric Exercise Blood Pressure — Frequency Study

## Summary

Isometric exercises (e.g. wall squats, planks) performed as infrequently as once a week can produce short-term reductions in blood pressure, according to a BMJ Open Sport and Exercise Medicine study of 100 adults. Three sessions per week produced the greatest and most sustained systolic improvements, while stopping entirely reversed gains. The findings offer a pragmatic option for people with limited time, showing that blood pressure benefits do not require daily exercise.

## Key Takeaways

- **Minimum effective dose:** Even once-a-week isometric exercise maintained short-term blood pressure reductions.
- **Optimal frequency:** Three sessions per week yielded the greatest sustained systolic blood pressure improvement.
- **Reversal risk:** Stopping isometric exercise entirely reversed blood pressure gains within four weeks.
- **Mechanism:** Isometric training reduces vascular resistance by helping blood vessels relax and open up.
- **Protocol used:** Four × 2-minute wall squat holds with 2-minute rest intervals per session.
- **Population studied:** 100 adults aged ~37 with normal-to-high-normal systolic BP (120–140 mm Hg).
- **Outperforms alternatives:** Prior research shows isometric training reduces BP more than aerobic exercise or some drug treatments.
- **Beginner entry point:** Even 10–20 minutes per session, twice a week, is a sufficient starting point.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1. What is the core claim of this study?**
A: Isometric exercise performed as little as once a week can maintain short-term reductions in blood pressure, and three times a week best sustains long-term improvements.

**Q2. What is the likely mechanism by which isometric exercise lowers blood pressure?**
A: Isometric training primarily reduces vascular resistance — it helps blood vessels relax and dilate, lowering the resistance blood encounters as it flows through them.

**Q3. How would you apply these findings if you have a busy schedule and can only exercise once a week?**
A: Perform four 2-minute wall squat holds (with 2-minute rests) once a week to maintain short-term BP reductions, then aim to increase to three sessions per week when schedule allows to lock in lasting gains.
