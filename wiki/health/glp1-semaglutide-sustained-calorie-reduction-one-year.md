## Summary

A 60-week RCT (n=120) published in the American Journal of Clinical Nutrition found semaglutide users consumed 24–30% fewer calories than placebo at every measurement point over the full study period, with an average 15.1% body weight loss vs. 3.4% for placebo. Crucially, calorie reduction persisted even as hunger and food preoccupation improvements became statistically insignificant by weeks 40–60 — suggesting the mechanism shifts over time. Experts emphasize the medication is a tool, not a standalone solution, and works best alongside lifestyle change.

## Key Takeaways

- **Calorie reduction**: 24–30% fewer calories consumed vs. placebo at every time point
- **Weight loss**: 15.1% (semaglutide) vs. 3.4% (placebo) over 60 weeks
- **Mechanism shift**: Hunger improvement fades by week 40–60 but calorie reduction persists
- **Expert caution**: "Some patients may become slightly less sensitive to its effects" over time
- **Side effects**: nausea, vomiting, GI discomfort still common
- **Post-discontinuation unknown**: weight maintenance after stopping semaglutide not directly addressed by this study
- **Best practice**: Medication + sustainable dietary and lifestyle changes — not a standalone
