## Summary

A multi-center review of 84 patients aged 21 or younger found that colorectal cancer in adolescents and young adults is usually diagnosed at Stage III or IV, even though most patients have no family history of the disease. Symptoms such as rectal bleeding and unexplained weight loss are often overlooked because the cancer is rare at that age, and the delays combine with aggressive tumor biology to produce high relapse and death rates. The authors call for taking these symptoms seriously in young patients and for coordinated pediatric-adult care with tailored treatment plans.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Early-Onset Colorectal Cancer]]**: Colorectal cancer in young people. Study author [[Erica Arnold]] describes it as "a different disease" from colorectal cancer diagnosed in older adults.
- **[[Pediatric Surgical Oncology Research Collaborative]]**: The research group behind the study "Clinical Presentation and Epidemiology of Pediatric and Adolescent Colorectal Carcinoma," which covered diagnoses from 2012 to 2023.
- **[[Diagnostic Delay]]**: Because colorectal cancer is rare in young people, clinicians may not suspect it when symptoms appear, so the disease has often progressed by the time it is found.
- **Warning symptoms**: Lower back pain, abdominal pain, changes in bowel habits, weight loss and [[Rectal Bleeding]].
- **[[Cancer Staging]]**: Most patients already had Stage III or IV disease at diagnosis.
- **[[Pediatric-to-Adult Care Transition]]**: As adult-type cancers show up at younger ages, pediatric and adult medical teams need to coordinate care.
- **Venue**: The findings were presented at the [[American Academy of Pediatrics]] 2026 National Conference & Exhibition in San Diego (Oct. 2–6, 2026).

## Key Takeaways

- **Cohort**: 84 patients aged 21 or younger, diagnosed 2012–2023.
- **Family history**: Only 20% had a first- or second-degree relative with CRC.
- **Late stage**: Most had Stage III or IV disease at diagnosis.
- **Recurrence**: More than one-third later developed recurrent disease.
- **Mortality**: Nearly half died from their diagnosis.
- **Red flags**: Unexplained weight loss and rectal bleeding in young patients warrant evaluation.
- **Care model**: Coordinated pediatric-adult teams and tailored treatment plans are recommended.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Base Rate Neglect]]**: Clinicians reasonably judge colorectal cancer unlikely in a young patient because the base rate is low, but when the base rate is rising, that same reasoning causes real cases to be missed until they are advanced.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding about colorectal cancer in young patients?
**A**: Colorectal cancer in patients aged 21 or younger is becoming more common and is usually diagnosed late, at Stage III or IV, with aggressive behavior.

**Q2**: What data from the 84-patient study show how serious the disease is?
**A**: Only 20% of patients had a family history, most had Stage III or IV disease at diagnosis, more than one-third relapsed, and nearly half died.

**Q3**: What should clinicians and young patients do differently?
**A**: They should take symptoms like unexplained weight loss and rectal bleeding seriously instead of dismissing them because of age. Pediatric and adult teams should also coordinate tailored treatment.
