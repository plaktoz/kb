## Summary

A UC San Diego randomized trial found that healthy adults on a one-month isocaloric [[Vegan Diet]] showed measurable [[DNA Methylation]] changes versus a meat-rich diet, including markers of reduced inflammation and a slowed [[Epigenetic Clock]]. Researchers stress the 48-person, one-month study is preliminary and cannot yet show the changes are lasting or disease-preventing.

## Core Concepts

- [[Vegan Diet]] — the isocaloric intervention studied against a meat-rich diet over four weeks.
- [[DNA Methylation]] — the epigenetic process measured across 800,000+ genome sites.
- [[Epigenetic Clock]] — statistical tool estimating biological age from methylation patterns.
- [[Biological Aging]] — the outcome researchers found evidence of slowing.
- [[Inflammation]] — vegan group showed markers (lower neutrophils, higher CD4 T cells) of reduced inflammation.
- [[Jerome Mertens]] — co-senior author, UC San Diego School of Medicine.
- [[Max Storz]] — co-senior author, University of Freiburg Medical Center.

## Key Takeaways

- **Study Design**: 48 adults, isocaloric vegan vs. meat-rich diet for 4 weeks after a 1-week standardization period.
- **Epigenome Shift**: Analyzed over 800,000 DNA methylation sites pre- and post-intervention.
- **Inflammation Markers**: Vegan group showed lower predicted neutrophils, higher regulatory CD4 T cells.
- **Slower Epigenetic Clock**: Vegan diet associated with slowed biological aging estimate.
- **Diet Composition, Not Calories**: Effects tied to food choice, not calorie reduction.
- **Caveats**: Small sample and short duration; longer studies needed to confirm lasting benefit.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core finding of the UC San Diego study?
**A**: A one-month vegan diet produced measurable epigenetic changes associated with reduced inflammation and a slower biological aging clock compared with a meat-rich diet.

**Q2**: How did researchers measure "biological aging" in this study?
**A**: They used epigenetic clocks that estimate biological age from DNA methylation patterns across more than 800,000 genome sites.

**Q3**: Why do researchers caution against overinterpreting the results?
**A**: The trial was small (48 people) and lasted only one month, so it can't yet prove the changes are lasting or that they prevent disease such as cancer.
