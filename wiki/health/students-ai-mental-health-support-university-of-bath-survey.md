## Summary
A University of Bath survey of 2,020 students found one in four had used generative AI for mental health support in the prior six months, rising to more than a third among those with very low wellbeing. The peer-reviewed study in JMIR Mental Health highlights both potential for wider access and risks around accuracy, privacy and crisis responses. The researchers caution about a single-university sample and are expanding to a national study.

## Core Concepts
- [[Generative AI]] and [[ChatGPT]]: used by students as advice source, personal therapist or coach.
- [[Student Mental Health]]: use was highest among students with very low wellbeing or a declared disability.
- [[University of Bath]] and [[Jeffrey Lambert]]: study team and lead author.
- [[AI Safety in Mental Health]]: risks include inaccurate information, privacy concerns and poor responses in a crisis.
- [[Cross-Sectional Study]]: the design, with a 9.1% response rate at one university.
- [[Student Support Guidance]]: Bath published guidance on benefits, risks and when to seek help.

## Key Takeaways
- **Usage Rate**: One in four surveyed students used generative AI for mental health support.
- **Highest Need**: Over a third of students with very low wellbeing used it.
- **Disability Link**: Students with a declared disability were more likely to use it.
- **Uses**: Advice when distressed, understanding mental health, and personal therapist or coach.
- **Risks**: Inaccurate information, privacy concerns and poor crisis responses.
- **Study Limits**: 9.1% response rate and a single university may not be representative.
- **Institutional Response**: Universities should help students understand AI's safety, privacy and limits.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the University of Bath study find?
**A**: One in four of 2,020 surveyed students had used generative AI for mental health support in the previous six months, with use highest among those with very low wellbeing.

**Q2**: Why should the findings be read cautiously?
**A**: Only 9.1% of invited students responded, and the study was at a single university, so results may not represent UK students.

**Q3**: What should universities do in response?
**A**: Recognize that students already use AI and help them understand its safety, privacy and limits, as Bath did with dedicated guidance, while noting AI is not a replacement for professional or crisis support.
