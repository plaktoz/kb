## Summary
A randomized trial of 138 adults with prediabetes or early type 2 diabetes found that a machine-learning-guided personalized diet gave no significant extra benefit over standard low-fat, high-fiber guidance when both started alongside metformin. HbA1c, weight, visceral fat, and blood pressure improved similarly in both arms. Smaller-than-planned enrollment and missing comparison arms limit the conclusions.

## Core Concepts
- [[Personalized Nutrition]]: algorithm using clinical, glucose-monitoring, and microbiome data to predict meal responses.
- [[Metformin]]: first-line glucose-lowering drug, started with both diets at 1.5 g/day.
- [[Type 2 Diabetes]] and prediabetes: the study population.
- [[Randomized Controlled Trial]]: the PREDICT trial, 151 randomized, 138 treated.
- [[HbA1c]]: primary outcome; [[Glycemic Variability]] a secondary measure.
- [[Gut Microbiome]]: richness, diversity, and short-chain fatty acids analysed exploratorily.
- [[Garvan Institute of Medical Research]] and [[Weizmann Institute of Science]]: lead institutions.

## Key Takeaways
- **Primary Outcome**: HbA1c fell 0.26% (personalized) versus 0.29% (standard); no difference.
- **Body Measures**: Weight fell 4.6 kg versus 4.4 kg; visceral fat and blood pressure similar.
- **Glycemic Variability**: Slightly greater drop with personalized diet, likely not clinically meaningful.
- **Carbohydrate Confound**: Personalized arm cut carbohydrates while standard arm raised them.
- **Microbiome Decline**: Richness fell in both groups despite better glycemia.
- **Limitations**: COVID cut enrollment from 264; no metformin-only or diet-only arms.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. A difference between arms cannot be attributed to the intervention when another variable also differs** — The personalized arm's glucose-variability gain may reflect lower carbohydrate intake rather than personalization. · *model:* [[Confounding Variable]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Did the AI-guided diet beat standard healthy eating alongside metformin?
**A**: No. Both diets produced comparable, significant reductions in HbA1c, weight, visceral fat, and blood pressure, with no significant difference in glycemic outcomes.

**Q2**: Why is the small glucose-variability advantage hard to interpret?
**A**: Carbohydrate intake fell in the personalized group but rose in the standard group. It is unclear whether personalization or lower carbohydrates caused the improvement.

**Q3**: What does the trial suggest for practice, and what limits it?
**A**: It tentatively supports accessible, guideline-based nutrition with metformin. Limits are the smaller-than-planned sample, no metformin-only or diet-only arms, and a mostly female, well-educated cohort.
