## Summary

A Nature study of 844,108 adults supports a two-step obesity definition: confirm excess fat using BMI plus waist measures, then separate clinical obesity (organ or function impairment) from preclinical obesity (risk only). Clinical obesity carried substantially higher mortality, while most preclinical cases stayed stable over 10 years, enabling more proportionate, personalized care.

## Core Concepts

- [[Body Mass Index]] is a rough size measure that misclassifies body fat in both directions.
- [[Waist Circumference]] and [[Waist-to-Height Ratio]] help confirm excess fat and show abdominal fat distribution.
- [[Clinical Obesity]] is excess fat impairing organ function or daily activities; [[Preclinical Obesity]] is excess fat with organs still working normally.
- [[Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology Commission]] proposed the framework that the Nature study tested.
- [[Francesco Rubino]] of King's College London is the senior author.
- [[Personalized Medicine]]: matching treatment intensity to individual risk, including costly new obesity drugs.

## Key Takeaways

- **Study Scale**: 844,108 adults across China, the UK, the US and Spain.
- **BMI Errors**: BMI alone missed obesity-level fat in up to 8.6% and over-called up to 3.1%.
- **Mortality Link**: Obesity predicted death when BMI was paired with waist measures, not BMI alone.
- **Clinical Share**: Clinical obesity was 23% to 31% of cases, prevalence 5.6% to 10.9%.
- **Stability**: 77% to 91% of preclinical cases did not become clinical within 10 years.
- **Not Harmless**: Preclinical obesity carries modest extra risk; "stable" does not mean "ignore".
- **Treatment Matching**: Clinical obesity needs prompt treatment; preclinical needs prevention matched to individual risk.
- **Drug Allocation**: Better diagnosis helps costly obesity medicines reach those who need them.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Measure the harm, not the proxy** — A proxy measure such as BMI misclassifies individuals when it diverges from the underlying condition; diagnosis should follow the actual harm to organs. · *model:* [[Goodhart's Law]]
→ explains: BMI Errors, Mortality Link

**T2. Match intervention intensity to individual risk** — Blanket rules over-treat the healthy or under-treat the ill; separating current disease from future risk allows proportionate care.
→ explains: Clinical Share, Stability, Treatment Matching, Drug Allocation

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central claim of the new obesity framework?
**A**: Obesity diagnosis should combine BMI with waist measures to confirm excess fat, then distinguish clinical obesity (organ or function impairment) from preclinical obesity (risk without impairment). Health risk follows excess fat and organ harm, not the number on the scale.

**Q2**: What evidence shows preclinical obesity is different from clinical obesity?
**A**: Clinical obesity carried substantially higher mortality, while preclinical obesity showed only a modest increase. Between 77% and 91% of people with preclinical obesity stayed free of clinical obesity over 10 years.

**Q3**: How would this change care for people with obesity?
**A**: Clinical obesity gets prompt, effective treatment, while preclinical obesity gets prevention and monitoring matched to individual risk such as age, family history or prediabetes. It also helps direct costly new obesity medicines to those who need them most.
