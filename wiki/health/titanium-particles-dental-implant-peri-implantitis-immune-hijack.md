## Summary
A Rutgers study in PNAS Nexus finds that bacterial biofilms corrode titanium dental implants, releasing particles that bind bacterial toxin and trap macrophages in chronic inflammation, destroying jawbone. This explains why antibiotics often fail against peri-implantitis, which affects 10-20% of implant patients. The calcium channel TRPC1 emerged as the first credible drug target, since disabling it prevented disease in mice.

## Core Concepts
- [[Peri-implantitis]]: infection around dental implants that damages jawbone; antibiotics succeed in fewer than half of cases.
- [[Titanium Particle Corrosion]]: acidic biofilms and metal scalers release billions of particles smaller than a red blood cell.
- [[Macrophages]]: immune cells that engulf bacteria but cannot digest titanium, stalling in prolonged inflammation.
- [[Lipopolysaccharide]]: a bacterial toxin that coats the particles, making them look like large undigestible bacteria.
- [[Interleukin-1 Beta]]: inflammatory molecule overproduced by stuck macrophages; also linked to rheumatoid arthritis and Alzheimer's.
- [[TRPC1]]: macrophage calcium channel that drives the damaging response.
- [[Georgios Kotsakis]]: senior author, Rutgers School of Dental Medicine.

## Key Takeaways
- **Prevalence**: 10-20% of implant patients develop peri-implantitis; cost exceeds $1 billion annually.
- **Antibiotic Failure**: Same drugs work on natural-tooth infections but succeed in under half of implant cases.
- **New Cause**: Corroded titanium, not only bacteria, drives the disease.
- **Immune Impairment**: Exposed macrophages engulfed fewer than half as many bacteria.
- **Drug Target**: TRPC1-lacking mice had smaller abscesses, less inflammation, and cleared bacteria.
- **Next Step**: NIH-funded work is testing drugs acting on this pathway in human cells.
- **Prevention**: Nonabrasive cleaning replaces metal scalers that scrape implant surfaces.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why do antibiotics often fail against peri-implantitis?
**A**: Corroded implant titanium releases particles that bind bacterial toxin and trap macrophages in chronic inflammation, so the immune system cannot clear bacteria even when antibiotics are used.

**Q2**: What evidence identifies TRPC1 as a target?
**A**: Titanium particles activate TRPC1 in macrophages, and mice lacking it developed much smaller abscesses, fewer inflammatory molecules, and cleared bacteria effectively.

**Q3**: What should implant patients and dentists do now?
**A**: Rely on regular professional cleaning with nonabrasive methods rather than metal scalers, which can scrape the implant and release more particles.
