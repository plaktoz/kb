## Summary

Olympic gymnast [[Simone Biles]] disclosed her diagnoses of both [[Atopic Dermatitis]] and [[Seborrheic Dermatitis]], having initially mistaken scalp flaking and itching for dandruff. After hesitating to seek care due to embarrassment, she consulted a dermatologist following public discussion of her symptoms during the Paris Olympics. She found relief with [[Zoryve]] (roflumilast), a steroid-free topical PDE4 inhibitor, and partnered with its manufacturer for their "Skin to Believe In" campaign.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Atopic Dermatitis]]**: A chronic inflammatory skin condition causing itching and rash, worsened by stress, weather extremes, and individual triggers.
- **[[Seborrheic Dermatitis]]**: A scalp-focused skin condition causing flaking and itching, easily mistaken for dandruff.
- **[[Zoryve]] (roflumilast)**: A steroid-free topical [[PDE4 Inhibitor]] available in foam (scalp) and cream (skin) formulations; used to manage eczema flares.
- **[[Arcutis Biotherapeutics]]**: Manufacturer of Zoryve; partnered with Biles on the "Skin to Believe In" campaign.
- **Stress as a flare trigger**: Intense training schedules and high-performance stress are significant contributors to eczema flares; reducing training load can help.
- **[[Angela Lamb, MD]]**: Dermatologist who diagnosed and treated Biles, confirming stress and environmental triggers.

## Key Takeaways

- **Misdiagnosis risk**: Scalp eczema is commonly mistaken for simple dandruff.
- **Embarrassment barrier**: Stigma around skin conditions delays patients seeking professional care.
- **Steroid-free option**: Zoryve (roflumilast) offers a non-steroidal treatment path for eczema.
- **Stress management**: Social time, massages, and evening baths helped Biles reduce flare frequency.
- **Training intensity link**: Stepping back from intense daily training meaningfully lowered Biles's stress and flares.
- **Advocacy message**: Biles urges patients not to "silently suffer" and to seek dermatological care.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Stigma as a healthcare barrier]]**: Embarrassment about visible symptoms creates delayed care-seeking behavior — a reminder that perceived social judgment suppresses rational health decisions even in high-profile individuals.
