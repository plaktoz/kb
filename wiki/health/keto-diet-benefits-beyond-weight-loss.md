## Summary
A randomized clinical trial in *Cell Metabolism* found that a ketogenic diet produced greater improvements in liver fat and prediabetes reversal than Mediterranean or plant-forward diets among people with obesity, despite similar weight loss across all three groups. Keto participants also avoided the expected rise in blood fat and cholesterol despite very high fat intake. An outside endocrinologist cautioned the trial was small and short, and that weight loss and adherence remain the more important long-term factors.

## Core Concepts

- [[Ketogenic Diet]]: a very low-carbohydrate (4%), high-fat (73%) eating pattern tested against two other diets.
- [[Mediterranean Diet]]: moderate-carb (50%), moderate-fat (35%) comparison diet with a strong long-term track record.
- [[Plant-Forward Diet]]: high-carb (70%), low-fat (15%) comparison diet.
- [[Insulin Sensitivity]] and [[Prediabetes]] reversal as key clinical outcomes.
- [[Fatty Liver Disease]] (liver fat reduction) as a primary endpoint.
- [[Dr. Max Petersen]]: lead author of the study.
- [[Dr. Yesika Garcia]]: outside endocrinologist offering cautionary commentary.

## Key Takeaways

- All three diets produced similar body fat loss (~10% body weight over 4-5 months).
- Keto group liver fat fell **67%** vs. **45%** in the other two diets combined.
- About half of keto participants no longer met [[Prediabetes]] criteria.
- Only **29%** on Mediterranean and **7%** on plant-forward reversed prediabetes.
- Keto participants saw no rise in blood fat or cholesterol despite 73% fat intake.
- Trial was small (42 participants) and short (4-5 months), limiting generalizability.
- [[Mediterranean Diet]] still has stronger long-term evidence for diabetes and heart disease prevention.
- Diet **adherence** matters more than diet type when choosing an approach.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What was the core finding of the study comparing keto, Mediterranean, and plant-forward diets?
**A**: In people with obesity, prediabetes, and fatty liver disease, keto produced greater reductions in liver fat and higher rates of prediabetes reversal than the other two diets, even though weight loss was similar across all groups.

**Q2**: What specific data points supported keto's advantage on liver and metabolic markers?
**A**: The keto group averaged a 67% reduction in liver fat versus 45% in the other diets, and about half of keto participants no longer met prediabetes criteria, compared to 29% (Mediterranean) and 7% (plant-forward).

**Q3**: How should someone weigh these results when choosing a diet for metabolic health?
**A**: Given the trial's small size and short duration, and since the Mediterranean diet has a stronger long-term safety and prevention record, the choice should prioritize which diet a person can realistically adhere to over time.
