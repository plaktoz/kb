## Summary
The UAE's cabinet-approved National Strategy for Healthy Nutrition 2031 responds to alarming population health data: 54% of adults have high cholesterol, 22% are obese, 96% exceed WHO sodium guidelines. The strategy targets food reformulation, sugar taxation, vitamin supplementation, and nutrient fortification, projecting AED 1.45bn ($394.8m) in healthcare savings over 15 years.

## Core Concepts

- [[UAE National Strategy for Healthy Nutrition 2031]] — comprehensive government framework approved by UAE cabinet
- [[Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs)]] — responsible for 55% of UAE deaths; cardiovascular disease alone: 34%
- [[Food Reformulation]] — industry project to reduce sodium, fat, and sugar content across food products
- [[Sugar Taxation]] — second phase launched early 2026; targets sugary drinks and energy drinks
- [[Food Fortification]] — mandatory micronutrient additions to address vitamin and mineral deficiencies identified by NNS

## Key Takeaways

- **96% of UAE adults exceed WHO sodium intake** (5,000mg/day vs recommended 2,000mg) — most acute nutritional gap
- **AED 40bn/year** spent treating diet-related health issues; strategy projects AED 1.45bn savings in 15 years (3.6% ROI)
- **Three-pillar approach**: (1) shift food sector toward healthier alternatives, (2) raise awareness, (3) strengthen preventive health
- **First National Nutrition Survey** provided the data foundation for targeted interventions
- **Ban on Partially Hydrogenated Oils (PHOs)** is the strongest supply-side regulation in the strategy
- **Children under 5** are a priority population for early nutritional intervention

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Prevention vs Treatment]]**: The UAE strategy is built on a classical public health first principle — preventing NCDs through diet is cheaper than treating them. The 15-year projected savings (AED 1.45bn) vs annual NCD treatment cost (AED 40bn) quantifies the leverage ratio of prevention over cure.
