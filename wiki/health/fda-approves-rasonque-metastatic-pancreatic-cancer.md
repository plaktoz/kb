## Summary
The FDA approved [[Rasonque]] (daraxonrasib) on August 26, 2026, to treat [[Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma]] — the most common form of pancreatic cancer. A Phase 3 clinical trial showed patients on Rasonque survived an average of 13.2 months versus 6.7 months for those receiving chemotherapy alone. The drug works as a "molecular glue" targeting RAS gene mutations, which drive tumor growth in over 90% of pancreatic adenocarcinoma cases.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Rasonque]] (daraxonrasib)** — newly FDA-approved oral once-daily pill for metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma
- **[[RAS Gene Mutations]]** — oncogenic driver mutations present in over 90% of pancreatic adenocarcinoma; Rasonque's primary target
- **[[Molecular Glue]]** — drug mechanism binding to cellular protein [[Cyclophilin A]], forming a complex that blocks RAS from signaling cancer cell growth
- **[[Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma]]** — most prevalent subtype of pancreatic cancer; accounted for over 490,000 deaths globally in 2024 (sixth leading cause of cancer mortality)
- **[[Phase 3 Clinical Trial]]** — pivotal trial demonstrating nearly doubled median overall survival (13.2 vs 6.7 months) versus chemotherapy alone

## Key Takeaways
- **Survival benefit**: 13.2 months median survival vs 6.7 months on chemotherapy alone.
- **Broad target**: RAS mutations drive tumor growth in >90% of pancreatic adenocarcinoma patients.
- **Mechanism**: Acts as a "molecular glue" — binds cyclophilin A to block RAS signaling.
- **Oral delivery**: Once-daily pill, unlike intravenous chemotherapy that harms healthy cells.
- **Side effects**: Rash, mouth sores, nausea, diarrhea, and fatigue.
- **Open questions**: First-line efficacy, drug resistance over time, real-world access at ~$500K/year cost.
- **Global burden**: Pancreatic cancer caused 490,000+ deaths in 2024; sixth leading cancer killer.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Targeted Therapy vs Broad-Spectrum Attack]]**: Rasonque exemplifies the shift from cytotoxic chemotherapy (harm all fast-dividing cells) to mechanism-specific inhibition — precisely blocking the RAS pathway that >90% of these tumors depend on, which is why the survival improvement is so dramatic.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core finding of the Phase 3 trial that led to Rasonque's approval?
**A**: Patients on Rasonque survived an average of 13.2 months versus 6.7 months for those on chemotherapy alone — nearly double the median overall survival.

**Q2**: How does Rasonque's mechanism differ from standard chemotherapy?
**A**: Rasonque acts as a "molecular glue," binding to cyclophilin A to form a complex that blocks RAS signaling in cancer cells, while chemotherapy indiscriminately harms healthy and cancerous cells alike.

**Q3**: What outstanding challenge could limit Rasonque's real-world impact?
**A**: Its annual cost of nearly $500,000 raises significant access concerns, and questions remain about its effectiveness as a first-line treatment and how it handles drug resistance over time.
