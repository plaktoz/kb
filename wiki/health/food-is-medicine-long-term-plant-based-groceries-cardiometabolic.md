## Summary

A [[Mass General Brigham]] study of a long-running [[Food Is Medicine]] program found that adults receiving weekly plant-based grocery packages for an average of 2.7 years showed dose-dependent reductions in diastolic blood pressure, body weight, and BMI. Most prior Food Is Medicine research examined only weeks-to-months effects, so this study adds evidence for longer-duration interventions. The authors advocate "high potency" programs that deliver frequent, high-quality, high-quantity food over long periods.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Food Is Medicine]]** — providing nutritious foods to improve management of chronic metabolic diseases such as [[Type 2 Diabetes]] and [[Hypertension]]; most prior studies looked only at short-term impacts.
- **Program setting** — the [[Mass General Brigham Revere Community Health Center]] Food Pantry, run in collaboration with the [[Greater Boston Food Bank]], which has provided nearly 379,000 pounds of food to MGB Revere this year alone.
- **Study design** — before-and-after health data from 68 adult participants who received an average of 97 plant-based grocery packages over 2.7 years; published in *[[Preventing Chronic Disease]]*.
- **Package composition** — dietitian-designed per the [[USDA Dietary Guidelines]] 2020: fresh fruits and vegetables, canned beans, nut butter, and whole grains; enough for three meals per day per household member for one week, offered weekly indefinitely.
- **[[Dose-Response Relationship]]** — every 100 packages received was associated with a 3.21 mmHg drop in diastolic blood pressure, 2.6 kg weight loss, and 1.04 kg/m² BMI reduction.
- **[[Hemoglobin A1c]]** — no significant improvement across participants.
- **Researchers** — senior author [[Lauren Fiechtner]], MD, MPH, and first author [[Jacob Mirsky]], MD, MA, medical director for the Food Pantry.

## Key Takeaways

- **Long duration**: Participants received packages for an average of 2.7 years.
- **Small cohort**: 68 adults, averaging 97 plant-based grocery packages each.
- **Diastolic BP**: −3.21 mmHg per 100 packages received.
- **Weight**: −2.6 kg per 100 packages received.
- **BMI**: −1.04 kg/m² per 100 packages received.
- **Systolic BP**: Reductions seen among participants with elevated baseline blood pressure.
- **No A1c benefit**: Hemoglobin A1c showed no significant improvement.
- **Full meal coverage**: Packages supplied three meals daily per household member weekly.
- **Policy push**: Authors urge frequent, high-quality, high-quantity food over long periods.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Dose-Response Relationship]]**: Larger improvements tracking more packages received strengthens the case that the food itself drives the effect, which matters because the study is a small before-and-after analysis without a described control group.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What was the central finding of the Mass General Brigham long-term Food Is Medicine study?
**A**: Adults receiving weekly plant-based grocery packages for an average of 2.7 years showed dose-dependent reductions in diastolic blood pressure, body weight, and BMI.

**Q2**: How large were the associated improvements per 100 food packages received?
**A**: Every 100 packages was associated with a 3.21 mmHg reduction in diastolic blood pressure, a 2.6 kg weight reduction, and a 1.04 kg/m² BMI reduction, though hemoglobin A1c did not significantly improve.

**Q3**: What does the study imply for how Food Is Medicine programs should be designed?
**A**: Programs should be "high potency" — delivering frequent, high-quality, high-quantity food packages over long durations rather than short weeks-to-months interventions, to give patients the greatest chance of benefiting.
