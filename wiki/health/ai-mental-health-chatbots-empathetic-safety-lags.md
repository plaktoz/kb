## Summary

A scoping review of 21 studies across 11 countries finds that LLM-based mental health chatbots are generally well-accepted and feel empathetic, but fail to consistently deliver sustained clinical benefit or safety. Despite high initial user satisfaction, attrition rises over multi-week interventions and concerns persist around crisis response, over-reliance, and clinically misaligned outputs. Standardized UX metrics, co-design with clinicians, and rigorous safety protocols are essential before broader clinical deployment.

## Core Concepts

- **[[LLM-Based Chatbots]]**: Unlike rule-based predecessors, [[Large Language Model]] chatbots simulate personalized, empathetic therapeutic dialogue using free-flowing conversation.
- **[[Mental Health Treatment Gap]]**: ~25% of people globally experience a mental health condition, yet ~85% receive no adequate treatment due to stigma, cost, and provider shortages.
- **[[Cognitive Behavioral Therapy]] (CBT)**: The dominant therapeutic framework underlying most chatbot interventions; most sessions used mindfulness, emotion regulation, and cognitive restructuring.
- **[[Acceptability vs. Efficacy]]**: High user satisfaction and moderate-to-high acceptability did not reliably translate into stronger clinical outcomes.
- **[[Attrition in Digital Interventions]]**: Initial engagement spikes but declines over multi-week periods — a persistent challenge for sustained digital mental health tools.
- **[[Crisis Response Limits]]**: Users expressed concern about chatbots' ability to appropriately handle acute mental health crises, a critical safety gap.
- **[[Data Privacy in Digital Health]]**: Users flagged privacy risks as a barrier to trust, particularly when sharing sensitive mental health disclosures.

## Key Takeaways

- **Treatment Gap**: 85% of people with mental health conditions receive no adequate care globally.
- **Study Scope**: 21 studies, 11 countries, 2023–2025; sample sizes 5–527 participants.
- **Primary Targets**: Depression and anxiety were the most common intervention targets.
- **Intervention Format**: 67% text-based; others used voice, avatar, or augmented reality modalities.
- **Engagement Drop-off**: High initial uptake declines significantly over 2–8 week interventions.
- **Empathy Gap**: Perceived empathy did not consistently produce better clinical results.
- **Trust Erosion**: Generic or misaligned responses led to disengagement and reduced trust.
- **Safety Concerns**: Over-reliance on bots, inadequate crisis handling, and privacy risks remain unresolved.
- **Path Forward**: Co-design with users and clinicians, validated long-term UX metrics, transparent safety standards required.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When user satisfaction scores become the target metric for chatbot design, developers optimize for perceived empathy rather than clinical efficacy — exactly why high acceptability ratings coexist with weak outcome data.
- **[[Engagement Decay]]**: Digital products exploit novelty to drive initial uptake, but without intrinsic therapeutic value, engagement reverts to baseline — the attrition pattern observed across nearly all multi-week studies.
