## Summary

A UK trial published in *The Lancet Oncology* found that the STAMINA lifestyle program — structured exercise, dietary guidance, and behavioral coaching — reduced quality-of-life deterioration by up to one-third in men on androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) for prostate cancer. Over 700 men across 15 NHS sites participated, with 80%+ completing their sessions, reporting better cancer-specific quality of life and less fatigue than standard-care recipients. Experts caution that the 12-month study measured quality of life rather than survival, and that structured delivery matters as much as the recommendations themselves.

## Core Concepts

- **[[STAMINA Program]]** — UK lifestyle intervention combining supervised exercise, dietary guidance, and behavioral support to counter ADT side effects
- **[[Androgen Deprivation Therapy]] (ADT)** — hormone-blocking treatment that lowers testosterone; side effects include fatigue, erectile dysfunction, mood changes, and bone density loss
- **[[Exercise Oncology]]** — field studying how structured physical activity can mitigate cancer treatment side effects and improve outcomes
- **[[Prostate Cancer]]** — the trial focused on men receiving ADT, a common systemic treatment for advanced prostate cancer
- **[[American Cancer Society]]** — recommends 150+ min/week of moderate aerobic activity and a plant-rich diet for cancer survivors
- **[[Nuffield Health]]** — plans to expand a STAMINA-derived program to breast and bowel cancer patients at 50,000 facilities by 2030

## Key Takeaways

- **Trial Scale**: 700 men, median age 71, across 15 NHS England facilities.
- **Quality-of-Life Gain**: STAMINA reduced QoL deterioration by up to one-third vs. standard care.
- **Adherence**: Over 80% of participants completed scheduled sessions.
- **ADT Side Effects Targeted**: Program counters fatigue, erectile dysfunction, mood changes, bone loss.
- **Diet Component**: Leafy greens, legumes, and healthy fats support prostate health.
- **Exercise Mechanism**: Aerobic exercise releases endorphins, positively affecting overall health.
- **ACS Recommendation**: ≥150 min/week moderate aerobic activity + resistance training for survivors.
- **Limitation**: Study measured 12-month QoL, not long-term cancer survival outcomes.
- **Structured Delivery Matters**: Coaching and accountability amplify the benefits of dietary/exercise advice alone.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Dose-Response Relationship]]**: The STAMINA results illustrate that lifestyle interventions, like drugs, have a dose-response — structured, supervised delivery produces measurably larger benefits than information alone, reflecting the first-principles principle that mechanism and delivery matter as much as the intervention itself.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: The expert caveat that "information alone may not be sufficient" echoes the principle that knowing a recommendation is not the same as optimizing for it — behavior change requires systems, not just knowledge.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the STAMINA trial?
**A**: The STAMINA lifestyle program — supervised exercise, dietary guidance, and behavioral support — reduced quality-of-life deterioration by up to one-third in prostate cancer patients on androgen deprivation therapy compared to standard care.

**Q2**: Why does ADT create a specific need for lifestyle intervention, and what does STAMINA target?
**A**: ADT blocks testosterone and causes fatigue, erectile dysfunction, mood changes, and bone density loss; STAMINA was designed to counteract these side effects through structured physical activity and diet.

**Q3**: What does the STAMINA trial imply for how lifestyle advice should be delivered to cancer patients?
**A**: Structured, coached delivery of exercise and dietary guidance produces measurably better outcomes than information alone — the program's 80%+ completion rate and QoL gains suggest that accountability and support systems are essential components, not optional add-ons.
