## Summary
A small randomized controlled trial found that adults with metabolic dysfunction-associated liver disease (MASLD) who ate 200g raw tomatoes plus 50g tomato sauce daily for 6 weeks saw greater reductions in liver fat than a tomato-free control group, though other markers (liver stiffness, lipids, enzymes, BMI) showed no significant difference.

## Core Concepts
- [[MASLD]] (metabolic dysfunction-associated liver disease) and hepatic steatosis as the trial's target condition.
- [[Lycopene]] and carotenoid content as a plausible but unconfirmed mechanism.
- The gap between [[Hypothesis-Generating Research]] and clinically actionable findings.

## Key Takeaways
- **79 Adults, 6 Weeks**: Small exploratory RCT comparing tomato intervention vs. control.
- **Liver Fat Down More**: Hepatic steatosis declined in both groups but more in the tomato group.
- **No Broader Benefit Shown**: No significant difference in liver stiffness, lipids, enzymes, or BMI.
- **Weight Loss Still Wins**: ~10%+ sustained weight loss remains the strongest evidence-backed intervention for steatohepatitis and fibrosis.
- **Limited Generalizability**: Study excluded BMI over 30, though most real-world MASLD patients have obesity or diabetes.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the study's core finding?
**A**: Daily tomato consumption was associated with greater liver fat reduction than a tomato-free diet in adults with MASLD, over 6 weeks.

**Q2**: What limits confidence in this finding?
**A**: The trial was short (6 weeks), excluded higher-BMI patients typical of real-world MASLD, combined raw tomatoes with sauce (so the active component is unclear), and showed no improvement on liver stiffness, enzymes, or body composition.

**Q3**: What should someone with MASLD take from this?
**A**: Tomatoes can be a reasonable part of a healthy diet, but sustained weight loss remains the intervention with the strongest evidence — tomatoes shouldn't substitute for it.
