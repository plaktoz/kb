## Summary
A Maastricht University Medical Center randomized crossover trial found that eating 60 grams of unsalted, skin-roasted peanuts daily for 16 weeks improved insulin responsiveness in memory-related brain regions of healthy adults aged 60 to 75. The result builds on a 2025 study by the same group that linked peanuts to higher cerebral blood flow and better verbal memory. The researchers suggest several peanut components, not one nutrient, may drive the effect.

## Core Concepts
- [[Brain Insulin Signaling]]: insulin helps nerve cells grow, survive, and communicate, and may help protect against processes associated with [[Alzheimer's Disease]].
- [[Age-Related Cognitive Decline]]: the aging brain may become less responsive to insulin, which has been linked to cognitive decline.
- [[Peanuts]]: unsalted, skin-roasted, 60 g/day (about two servings) for 16 weeks.
- [[Cerebral Blood Flow]]: the 2025 study found a 3.6% increase and a 5.8% gain in verbal memory.
- [[Arterial Spin Labeling MRI]]: ASL-MRI tracked regional blood flow after intranasal insulin to gauge insulin responsiveness.
- [[Randomized Controlled Crossover Trial]]: each participant ate peanuts for one phase and avoided them for another, then switched.
- [[L-Arginine]] and [[Nitric Oxide]]: peanuts are rich in L-arginine, a precursor of nitric oxide that has been reported to facilitate learning and memory.
- Researchers: [[Peter Joris]] (senior author), [[Lucia Kerkhof]]; [[NUTRIM]] at Maastricht University Medical Center.

## Key Takeaways
- **Brain Insulin Response**: Daily peanuts improved insulin responsiveness in right-hemisphere memory regions, including the hippocampus.
- **Regions Affected**: Lingual gyrus, hippocampus, parahippocampal gyrus, and posterior cingulate gyrus showed the change.
- **Study Design**: 31 healthy adults aged 60-75 in a randomized crossover trial over 16 weeks.
- **Earlier Findings**: A 2025 study found 3.6% higher cerebral blood flow and 5.8% better verbal memory.
- **Measurement**: ASL-MRI after nasal insulin, plus abdominal MRI for liver fat and CANTAB cognitive tests.
- **Multiple Contributors**: Unsaturated fats, fiber, vitamins, minerals, phytochemicals, and L-arginine may all contribute.
- **Funding Disclosure**: The Peanut Institute Foundation funded it and reportedly had no role in design, analysis, or writing.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the Maastricht study find about daily peanut consumption in older adults?
**A**: Eating unsalted, skin-roasted peanuts daily for 16 weeks improved insulin responsiveness in memory-related brain regions of the right hemisphere. The researchers suggest this may contribute to the cognitive benefits of peanut intake.

**Q2**: How did researchers measure brain insulin responsiveness, and what design did they use?
**A**: They used ASL-MRI to track regional cerebral blood flow changes after intranasal insulin, within a randomized controlled crossover trial of 31 adults aged 60-75. Participants ate 60 g of peanuts daily for 16 weeks and avoided them for 16 weeks, in either order.

**Q3**: How should the findings be interpreted, given the funding and the possible mechanism?
**A**: The Peanut Institute Foundation funded the work, though the researchers say it had no role in design, analysis, or the manuscript. They attribute the benefits to multiple peanut components rather than a single nutrient, so the effect should not be credited to any one ingredient.
