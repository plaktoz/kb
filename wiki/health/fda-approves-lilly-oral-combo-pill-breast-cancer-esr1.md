## Summary

The FDA approved a combination oral therapy — [[Inluriyo]] (imlunestrant) plus [[Verzenio]] (abemaciclib) by [[Eli Lilly]] — on September 18, 2026 for adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative advanced or metastatic breast cancer carrying an [[ESR1 mutation]] that progressed after prior hormone therapy. The EMBER-3 trial showed the combination extended median progression-free survival to 11.1 months versus 5.5 months on Inluriyo alone, and doubled tumor shrinkage rates (35% vs. 15%). ESR1 mutations, rare at diagnosis, emerge in 10–50% of hormone therapy-resistant metastatic cases.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Inluriyo]] (imlunestrant)**: Selective [[Estrogen Receptor]] degrader (SERD) that blocks and degrades estrogen receptors; standalone FDA approval September 2025, now approved in combination.
- **[[Verzenio]] (abemaciclib)**: [[CDK4/6 inhibitor]] that blocks proteins driving cancer cell proliferation; approved since 2017.
- **[[ESR1 Mutation]]**: Mutation in the estrogen receptor gene linked to hormone therapy resistance; present in ~1% of tumors at diagnosis, rising to 10–50% in resistant metastatic cases.
- **[[EMBER-3 Trial]]**: Phase III clinical trial of 159 participants with ESR1 mutations evaluating the combination versus monotherapy.
- **[[Guardant360 CDx]]**: FDA-approved liquid biopsy test for detecting ESR1 mutations via blood sample.
- **Competitive context**: [[AstraZeneca]]'s [[Etcamah]] (camizestrant) received accelerated FDA approval September 4 for ESR1 mutations detected during first-line treatment.

## Key Takeaways

- **Approval date**: FDA approved Inluriyo + Verzenio combination on September 18, 2026.
- **Indication**: ER+/HER2- advanced or metastatic breast cancer with ESR1 mutation, post-hormone therapy.
- **PFS benefit**: Median 11.1 months (combo) vs. 5.5 months (monotherapy) — roughly doubled.
- **Tumor shrinkage**: 35% response rate (combo) vs. 15% (monotherapy).
- **Survival data**: Overall survival results not yet available.
- **ESR1 prevalence**: Rare at diagnosis (~1%), common in hormone therapy-resistant metastatic disease (10–50%).
- **Companion diagnostic**: Guardant360 CDx blood test detects ESR1 mutations.
- **Side effects**: Diarrhea (86%), neutropenia, liver toxicity, blood clots; ~6% discontinuation rate.
- **Competition**: AstraZeneca's camizestrant also recently approved for ESR1+ breast cancer.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this FDA approval for Inluriyo plus Verzenio?
**A**: The combination of imlunestrant and abemaciclib significantly extends progression-free survival in ER+/HER2- metastatic breast cancer patients with ESR1 mutations who have progressed on hormone therapy, roughly doubling median PFS compared to imlunestrant alone.

**Q2**: What specific clinical evidence supports the approval, and why does ESR1 mutation prevalence matter?
**A**: The EMBER-3 trial (159 ESR1-mutant participants) showed 11.1 vs. 5.5 months median PFS and 35% vs. 15% tumor shrinkage; ESR1 mutations affect 10–50% of hormone therapy-resistant metastatic cases, making this a clinically significant population despite rarity at initial diagnosis.

**Q3**: How would an oncologist identify which patients are candidates for this combination therapy?
**A**: Clinicians would use the FDA-approved Guardant360 CDx blood test to detect ESR1 mutations in patients with ER+/HER2- advanced or metastatic breast cancer who have already progressed on endocrine therapy.
