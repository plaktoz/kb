## Summary

The FDA approved four updated [[COVID-19]] vaccines on August 27 for the 2026–2027 season, each targeting the [[XFG Variant]] (also called "Stratus"), the most prevalent circulating strain globally. The approved vaccines come from [[Pfizer-BioNTech]], [[Moderna]], and [[Novavax]], covering both mRNA and protein-based platforms. Vaccination is recommended especially for adults 65 and older and those with underlying health conditions.

## Core Concepts

- **[[COVID-19]] vaccine annual update cycle** — vaccines are reformulated each fall to match dominant strains, mirroring the influenza vaccine cadence
- **[[XFG Variant]]** (Stratus) — a highly transmissible [[Omicron]] sublineage; does not cause more severe illness than prior variants
- **[[SW2 Variant]]** — the most prevalent U.S. strain at ~21% as of late August; closely related to XFG, so cross-protection is expected
- **[[mRNA Vaccine]] technology** — enables rapid reformulation to match emerging strains, per [[William Schaffner]] (Vanderbilt University)
- **[[Novavax]] protein-based vaccine** — provides a non-mRNA option for those who prefer it
- **[[Monica Gandhi]]** (UCSF) — noted that XFG and SW2's genetic similarity makes XFG-targeting vaccines effective against both

## Key Takeaways

- **4 vaccines approved**: COMIRNATY (Pfizer), SPIKEVAX (Moderna), MNEXSPIKE (Moderna), NUVAXOVID (Novavax)
- **Target strain**: JN.1-lineage [[XFG Variant]]; high transmissibility but not increased severity
- **Primary recommendation**: Adults 65+ and those with qualifying conditions
- **Broader eligibility**: Anyone wanting prevention may also vaccinate
- **COVID-19 spread**: CDC estimated cases growing in 47 states as of late August
- **High-risk conditions**: Chronic kidney/lung disease, heart conditions, diabetes, obesity, liver disease, pregnancy
- **mRNA advantage**: Rapid strain-matching capability cited as key strength

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Evolutionary Arms Race]]**: Annual vaccine reformulation reflects the ongoing co-evolution between viral mutation and immune protection — each season's update is a deliberate catch-up move in a continuous adaptive competition between pathogen and medicine.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core reason the FDA approved new COVID-19 vaccines for 2026–2027?
**A**: To match the updated vaccines to the XFG (Stratus) variant, currently the most prevalent COVID-19 strain globally, as part of the annual fall reformulation cycle.

**Q2**: Why is the XFG-targeted vaccine expected to protect against the SW2 variant too?
**A**: Dr. Monica Gandhi (UCSF) explained that XFG and SW2 are closely related strains, so the vaccine targeting XFG should provide meaningful cross-protection against SW2 despite it being the dominant U.S. strain at 21% prevalence.

**Q3**: Who should prioritize getting the updated COVID-19 vaccine?
**A**: Adults 65 and older and people with underlying conditions such as chronic kidney/lung disease, heart conditions, diabetes, obesity, liver disease, or pregnancy — as well as those in close contact with high-risk individuals.
