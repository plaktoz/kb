## Summary

An Oxford Population Health analysis of 1.4 million adults in England's Our Future Health program (2022–2025) found that 37% had hypertension and that 59% of them did not know it. Half of the people taking blood-pressure medication still had readings of 140/90 mmHg or higher, and awareness, treatment and control have barely improved in decades. The authors argue that current NICE guidelines are "not fit for purpose" and call for proactive, population-wide detection, with treatment started based on predicted cardiovascular risk.

## Core Concepts

- [[Hypertension]] ("silent killer"): defined by [[World Health Organization]] criteria as systolic ≥140 mmHg, diastolic ≥90 mmHg, or taking blood-pressure-lowering medication.
- [[Our Future Health]]: the UK's largest health research program, used here for clinic-measured blood pressure readings.
- [[Oxford Population Health]] study, published in [[BMJ Public Health]] and led by medical statistician [[Wenyu Liu]].
- The care cascade: prevalence → awareness/diagnosis → treatment → control.
- [[NICE Guidelines]]: require several measurements to confirm a diagnosis and, in some cases, lifestyle changes before medication. The authors say this delays detection and control.
- [[NHS Health Check]]: younger adults are not currently eligible, yet more than 15% of them have hypertension.
- Expert voices: [[Iain Turnbull]] (Our Future Health, GP) and [[Bryan Williams]] ([[British Heart Foundation]]).
- Related: [[Cardiovascular Disease]], [[Stroke]], [[Heart Attack]], [[Modifiable Risk Factors]]

## Key Takeaways

- **Prevalence**: 37% of 1.4 million English adults met the hypertension criteria.
- **Unaware majority**: 59% of people with hypertension were undiagnosed and untreated.
- **Treatment gap**: 50% of medicated participants were still at or above 140/90 mmHg.
- **Age gradient**: 15% at ages 18–39, rising to 71% at 80 and over.
- **Sex gap**: Age-standardised rates were 42% in men and 29% in women.
- **Young adults missed**: Over 15% have hypertension but aren't eligible for NHS Health Checks.
- **Stagnation**: Awareness, treatment and control in England have barely improved in decades.
- **Guideline critique**: Multi-reading confirmation and lifestyle-first rules delay control.
- **Proposed fix**: Proactive detection and treatment based on predicted cardiovascular risk.
- **Stakes**: High blood pressure is linked to about half of UK heart attacks and strokes.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Rule of Halves]]**: Losses compound at each stage of the care cascade (undiagnosed → untreated → uncontrolled). Here 59% of cases were unaware and 50% of treated cases were uncontrolled, so only a small share of all hypertensives are actually controlled.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the main finding about hypertension detection in England?
**A**: More than a third of adults in the Our Future Health cohort had hypertension. Almost 6 in 10 of them (59%) didn't know it: they had never been diagnosed and were not taking medication.

**Q2**: Which data point shows that diagnosis and treatment alone don't solve the problem?
**A**: Among participants taking one or more blood-pressure medications, half (50%) still had readings of 140/90 mmHg or higher, so their hypertension was not controlled.

**Q3**: What changes do the researchers recommend?
**A**: They call for a more proactive, population-wide approach to detection, including younger adults not covered by NHS Health Checks. They also suggest starting treatment based on predicted cardiovascular risk, not blood-pressure levels alone, instead of the current multi-step NICE approach.
