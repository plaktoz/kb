## Summary
A preliminary American Heart Association-funded study of 110 adults with Type 2 diabetes and high blood pressure found that an 8-month medically tailored meals program improved diet quality, food security, HbA1c, and blood pressure. Gradually tapering the number of meals did not reduce the benefits when paired with a heart-health education program. The findings are an unreviewed single-site abstract, so larger multi-site trials are needed.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Food Is Medicine]]**: Interventions that provide healthy food to treat chronic disease, traditionally aimed at advanced or terminal conditions.
- **[[Medically Tailored Meals]]**: Home-delivered meals designed for a medical condition; effective but expensive.
- **[[Dose-Response Relationship]]**: The study tested standard dosing (10 meals a week) against a gradual taper to 5 per week.
- **[[Health Care by Food]]**: American Heart Association initiative that funded the work and aims to embed nutrition interventions in healthcare.
- **[[Life's Essential 8]]**: The AHA heart-health framework used in the education program, alongside dietitian counseling and cooking demonstrations.
- **[[Food Insecurity]]**: Affected nearly 14% of U.S. households in 2024 and compounds poor diet.
- **[[Nour Makarem]]**: Lead author, Columbia University Mailman School of Public Health.

## Key Takeaways
- **Sample**: 110 adults, average age 63, in New York City, 2024-2026.
- **Design**: 10 meals weekly for 8 months versus a taper from month 4 to 5 per week.
- **Diet Quality**: Improved by about 3 points on a Mediterranean-style scale in both groups.
- **Food Security**: Participants had more than twice the chance of a higher food security status.
- **Blood Sugar**: HbA1c fell by about 0.5%.
- **Blood Pressure**: Systolic and diastolic both fell by about 4 mm Hg.
- **Taper Result**: Tapering produced similar effects to the standard dose with education support.
- **Limitations**: Single site, not peer-reviewed, preliminary until published.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Support can be tapered without losing benefit when it builds the participant's own capability** — Reducing the supplied dose still held outcomes when education, counseling, and cooking skills carried healthy eating at home.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the study find about medically tailored meals for adults with Type 2 diabetes and high blood pressure?
**A**: An 8-month program improved diet quality and food security and lowered HbA1c and blood pressure. Researchers say earlier use of such programs could help prevent worsening disease.

**Q2**: What did tapering the meal dose show, and what were the main measured effects?
**A**: Cutting from 10 to 5 meals per week over five months did not reduce benefits when paired with heart-health education. HbA1c fell about 0.5% and blood pressure about 4 mm Hg in both groups.

**Q3**: What are the implications and caveats?
**A**: A taper could make costly meal programs more sustainable and scalable. The results come from one New York City site in a non-peer-reviewed abstract, so larger multi-site trials are needed.
