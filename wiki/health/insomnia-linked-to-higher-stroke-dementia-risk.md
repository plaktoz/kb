## Summary

A comprehensive review in *Sleep Medicine Reviews* links insomnia to a 26% higher risk of stroke, a 28% increased risk of all-cause hospitalization, and elevated risks of depression, suicide, and dementia. Sleep disruption promotes chronic sympathetic nervous system overactivation, elevated cortisol, systemic inflammation, and impaired glymphatic clearance of amyloid-beta and tau. While the observational findings demonstrate significant associations rather than direct causation, experts emphasize timely clinical intervention when sleep issues impair daily functioning.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Insomnia]]**: Chronic sleep difficulty affecting approximately 22% of adults despite adequate sleep opportunity.
- **[[Stroke Risk]]**: Meta-analysis of six studies (1.3M+ individuals) linked insomnia to a 26% increased stroke likelihood.
- **[[Dementia]] and [[Alzheimer's Disease]]**: Meta-analyses linked chronic insomnia with neurodegeneration, potentially via impaired clearance of [[Amyloid-Beta]] and [[Tau Protein]].
- **[[Sympathetic Nervous System]]**: Persistent sleep disturbance causes hyperarousal, elevating adrenaline, cortisol, and systemic inflammation.
- **[[Depression]] and Mental Health**: Insomnia increases rumination and executive dysfunction, raising risks of depression and suicidal ideation.
- **[[Alex Dimitriu]]** and **[[Christopher Yi]]**: Medical experts emphasizing hyperarousal feedback loops and observational study cautions.

## Key Takeaways

- **26% higher stroke risk**: Meta-analysis of 1.3M+ people found insomnia significantly elevates stroke incidence.
- **28% increased hospitalization**: People with insomnia face higher rates of all-cause hospital admissions.
- **Sympathetic overactivation**: Chronic sleep loss triggers fight-or-flight hyperarousal, raising adrenaline, cortisol, and blood pressure.
- **Compromised vascular health**: Elevated cortisol and inflammation disrupt glucose and lipid metabolism, driving plaque formation.
- **Elevated dementia risk**: Sleep disruption impairs clearance of amyloid-beta and tau proteins from the brain.
- **Mental health vulnerability**: Sleep deficits impair executive function, exacerbating rumination, depression, and suicide risk.
- **Association vs. causation**: Findings reflect strong observational associations rather than proven direct causal mechanisms.
- **Early intervention indicator**: Seek medical care when sleep issues persistently impair daily health or happiness.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the primary finding of the review regarding insomnia and major health outcomes?
**A**: A systematic review published in *Sleep Medicine Reviews* found that chronic insomnia is associated with a 26% higher risk of stroke, a 28% higher risk of hospitalization, and increased risks of dementia and depression.

**Q2**: What physiological mechanisms help explain the link between insomnia, stroke, and dementia?
**A**: Persistent insomnia triggers sympathetic nervous system overactivation and cortisol release, promoting hypertension and vascular plaque, while also impairing the brain's clearance of amyloid-beta and tau.

**Q3**: How should clinicians and individuals act on these findings given that the studies show association rather than causation?
**A**: Individuals should seek healthcare evaluation early if sleep difficulties affect daytime function or prevent obtaining 6–7 hours of sleep, enabling early mitigation of underlying cardiovascular and metabolic risk factors.
