## Summary
A Penn State College of Medicine trial (FAST-2, published in PLOS One) found that a four-minute daily resistance routine improved chair-stand performance and balance in adults over 65 within 12 weeks. Participants followed the routine on 81% of days, suggesting short workouts are easier to sustain. The measured outcomes were physical-performance tests, not falls or nursing-home admissions.

## Core Concepts
- [[FAST-2 Trial]]: 97 participants (average age 74) randomised to the routine or no intervention for 12 weeks.
- [[Resistance Training]]: Muscle work against body weight or elastic bands.
- [[Four-Minute Routine]]: Push-ups, chair stands, two-arm rows and stair stepping, 30 seconds each with 30 seconds rest.
- [[Progressive Overload]]: Participants advanced from easier variations (wall push-ups) to harder ones (higher stepper).
- [[Functional Fitness Tests]]: Chair-stand and one-leg balance tests that predict falls, walking difficulty and nursing-home risk.
- [[Exercise Adherence]]: 81% of days completed; short duration lowers barriers of time and discomfort.
- [[Penn State College of Medicine]]: Led by Christopher Sciamanna.

## Key Takeaways
- **Minimal Dose**: Four minutes a day produced meaningful gains in 12 weeks.
- **Chair Stands**: 4.2 more stands in 30 seconds.
- **Balance**: 3.6 more seconds on one leg.
- **Sit-to-Stand Speed**: 2.3 seconds faster.
- **Adherence**: Participants exercised on 81% of days.
- **Low Baseline**: Participants averaged about 18 minutes of exercise per week beforehand.
- **Participation Gap**: Fewer than one in five older adults meet strength-training guidelines.
- **Caveat**: Study measured performance, not falls or nursing-home outcomes.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Shrinking the effort required raises the odds that a habit is actually done** — Exercise planning (reps, sets, rest, frequency) is complex and demanding, so making the routine short removes much of the friction and sustains participation. · *model:* [[Activation Energy]]
→ explains: Adherence, Minimal Dose

**T2. Strength gains continue only if the load increases as ability grows** — Participants were encouraged to move to harder variations as they improved. · *model:* [[Progressive Overload]]
→ explains: Chair Stands, Balance

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the FAST-2 study find?
**A**: A four-minute daily strength routine significantly improved strength, balance and mobility in adults over 65 within 12 weeks.

**Q2**: What were the routine and the measured results?
**A**: Four exercises of 30 seconds each with 30 seconds rest (push-ups, chair stands, rows, stair stepping). Participants did 4.2 more chair stands, balanced 3.6 seconds longer and sped up sit-to-stand by 2.3 seconds.

**Q3**: Why does the short duration matter practically?
**A**: Time, discomfort and workout-planning complexity deter many older adults; participants completed the routine on 81% of days. The study did not directly test falls or nursing-home outcomes.
