## Summary
Yale researchers pooled 51 clinical studies and over 110 DNA methylation biomarkers to see which interventions lower biological age, finding that metformin, semaglutide, anti-TNF therapies, and diet plus exercise do, while over-the-counter supplements generally do not. The work, published in Nature Medicine, could speed up evaluation of anti-aging therapies. It remains unproven that lower epigenetic age means longer life or less age-related disease.

## Core Concepts
- [[Epigenetic Clocks]]: blood-based tools that read [[DNA Methylation]] patterns to estimate biological age.
- [[Biological Age]]: functional age that may differ from chronological age.
- [[Metformin]], [[Semaglutide]] and [[Anti-TNF Therapy]]: the drugs linked to the strongest reductions.
- [[Diet and Exercise]]: Mediterranean, low-fat and low-carb diets plus physical activity.
- [[Biomarker Validation]]: whether changes in markers predict long-term health.
- People: [[Raghav Sehgal]] (lead author) and [[Albert Higgins-Chen]] (senior author), Yale School of Medicine.

## Key Takeaways
- **Scope**: 51 clinical studies and more than 110 methylation biomarkers, including 16 major clocks.
- **Drugs**: Metformin, semaglutide and anti-TNF therapies showed the strongest biological age reductions.
- **Lifestyle**: Exercise plus a healthy diet consistently lowered epigenetic age.
- **Supplements**: Over-the-counter supplements and some procedures showed no measurable reduction.
- **Clock Quality**: Newer clocks outperformed older ones; people with disease showed larger changes.
- **Open Question**: Lower epigenetic age is not shown to mean longer life or less disease.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. A proxy marker is not the outcome** — A change in a biomarker only matters if it is shown to predict the outcome it stands in for; until validated, it cannot be read as longer or healthier life. · *model:* [[Surrogate Endpoint Fallacy]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the Yale study find about which anti-aging interventions work?
**A**: Metformin, semaglutide, anti-TNF therapies, and diet plus exercise reduced epigenetic age, while over-the-counter supplements generally did not.

**Q2**: How did the researchers reach this conclusion?
**A**: They combined 51 clinical studies and compared before-and-after measurements across more than 110 DNA methylation biomarkers, including 16 major epigenetic clocks.

**Q3**: Why can't these results yet be used to claim people will live longer?
**A**: Scientists have not established that lowering epigenetic age reduces long-term risk of age-related disease; causation, persistence and mechanisms are still unknown. If validated, the markers could shorten evaluation of therapies from decades to months.
