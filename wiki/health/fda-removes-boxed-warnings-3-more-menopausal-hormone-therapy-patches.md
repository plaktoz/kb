## Summary

On October 30, 2026, the [[FDA]] approved updated labeling for 3 more menopausal hormone therapy (MHT) products, all estradiol transdermal patches, removing cardiovascular disease, breast cancer, and probable dementia statements from their boxed warnings. This follows February 2026 changes to 6 other MHT products, and the FDA again acted without an advisory committee meeting. One expert welcomes the change but stresses that clinicians now bear more responsibility for individualized risk counseling.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Menopausal Hormone Therapy]]**: Approved for moderate to severe hot flashes, vaginal dryness and discomfort, and bone loss prevention; about 2 million of roughly 41 million US women aged 45-64 received a prescription in 2020.
- **[[Boxed Warning]] Removal**: Labeling changes dropped safety statements on cardiovascular disease, breast cancer, and probable dementia; 29 drug companies have submitted proposed labeling changes.
- **[[Estradiol Transdermal Patches]]**: The 3 products come from 2 applicants; the FDA did not name brands or manufacturers. Six manufacturers make FDA-approved estradiol patches.
- **[[Shared Decision-Making]]**: [[Susan Reed]] argues clinicians must understand risks for special populations based on baseline cardiovascular and breast cancer risk and age.
- **[[Drug Supply and Access]]**: The FDA reports no imminent risk of nationwide shortage and says market output of patches has doubled in 12 months.
- **[[HHS]] Women's Health Executive Order**: HHS says the action supports the "Advancing Women's Health to Improve Outcomes in All Phases of Life" order signed October 19, 2026.

## Key Takeaways

- **Scope**: 3 estradiol patches lose boxed warnings on cardiovascular disease, breast cancer, probable dementia.
- **Process**: No advisory committee meeting; limited reasoning given in press briefing, per The Guardian.
- **Trajectory**: Follows February 12, 2026 action covering 6 products across 4 therapy categories.
- **Open Questions**: Reed asks about safety when started over ages 60-65 and with oral progestogens.
- **Clinician Burden**: Careful shared decision-making is "even more important than before."
- **Supply**: Patch output doubled in 12 months; gels and creams also monitored.
- **Generics and OTC**: FDA approving generic MHT products; exploring OTC vaginal estrogen.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the FDA change on October 30, 2026?
**A**: It approved updated labeling for 3 estradiol transdermal patch products, removing statements on cardiovascular disease, breast cancer, and probable dementia from their boxed warnings. This follows a similar February 2026 action on 6 other MHT products.

**Q2**: What did the FDA say about patch availability?
**A**: It said it is not aware of an imminent risk of a nationwide shortage and is working with all six patch manufacturers, whose efforts have roughly doubled market output over the last 12 months.

**Q3**: How does removing boxed warnings change what clinicians must do, according to Susan Reed?
**A**: Clinicians must take responsibility for understanding risks for special populations based on baseline cardiovascular and breast cancer risk and age, and rely on open communication and careful shared decision-making.
