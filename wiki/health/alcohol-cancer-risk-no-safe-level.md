## Summary

Three recent studies confirm that alcohol consumption — even at low levels — meaningfully raises cancer risk and mortality. Alcohol-attributable cancer deaths in the US more than doubled between 1990 and 2023, and a meta-analysis of 843 studies found moderate-to-strong evidence linking even low intake to multiple cancer types. Experts conclude that abstinence is the only truly safe level of alcohol consumption.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Alcohol-Attributable Cancer]]**: Deaths rose from 11,361 (1990) to 23,126 (2023) in the US, a doubling in three decades.
- **[[Colorectal Cancer]]**: Leading alcohol-linked cancer among men ages 20–54; heavy drinkers (14+ drinks/week) face a 25% higher risk and a 95% higher rectal cancer risk.
- **[[Breast Cancer]]**: Leading alcohol-linked cancer among women ages 20–54.
- **[[Dose-Response Relationship]]**: Even low alcohol consumption shows moderate-to-strong evidence of harm across 20 health conditions, including cancers of the lip, oral cavity, larynx, [[Pharyngeal Cancer|pharynx]], and colorectum.
- **[[Pharyngeal Cancer]]**: Risk rises by at least 105% with regular alcohol intake.
- **[[Liver Disease]] and [[Pancreatitis]]**: Also strongly linked to alcohol consumption in the meta-analysis.
- **[[Cessation Benefit]]**: Former heavy drinkers showed no elevated colorectal cancer risk, suggesting quitting reverses much of the risk.

## Key Takeaways

- **Deaths doubled**: US alcohol-related cancer deaths went from ~11K (1990) to ~23K (2023).
- **No safe level**: Low consumption still links to cancers of lip, oral cavity, larynx, pharynx, and colorectum.
- **Pharyngeal cancer**: Risk increases by at least 105% with alcohol use.
- **Heavy drinking**: 14+ drinks/week raises colorectal cancer risk 25%; rectal cancer risk 95%.
- **Cessation helps**: Former heavy drinkers had no elevated colorectal cancer risk in 14.5-year study.
- **Expert verdict**: "Abstinence is the safest and truest bet" for anyone asking about safe consumption levels.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Dose-Response Fallacy]]**: Common belief that "a little is fine" breaks down when the dose-response curve has no safe threshold — alcohol fits this pattern for cancer, where even low doses carry measurable risk.
- **[[Reversibility]]**: The finding that former heavy drinkers show normalized risk illustrates that biological harm is often reversible, making the decision to quit high-value even after years of use.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding about alcohol's relationship to cancer risk?
**A**: Even low alcohol consumption is linked to multiple cancer types, and alcohol-attributable cancer deaths in the US more than doubled from 1990 to 2023 — with no established safe level of consumption.

**Q2**: What specific risk increase did the large cohort study (88,092 participants) find for heavy drinkers (14+ drinks/week)?
**A**: Heavy drinkers had a 25% higher colorectal cancer risk and a 95% higher rectal cancer risk compared to light drinkers over approximately 14.5 years.

**Q3**: What implication does the cessation finding carry for current heavy drinkers?
**A**: Former heavy drinkers showed no elevated colorectal cancer risk, suggesting that stopping drinking can meaningfully reverse cancer risk even after prolonged heavy use.
