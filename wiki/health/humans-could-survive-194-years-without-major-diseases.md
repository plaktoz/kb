## Summary

A study published in NPJ Aging used mathematical modeling to estimate human lifespan limits when aging is driven solely by [[Somatic Mutations]] — genetic changes that accumulate in non-reproductive cells over time. If all aging hallmarks were eliminated, median lifespan could theoretically reach ~1,759 years; retaining only somatic mutations, it falls to 146–194 years, roughly double today's ~79 years. Outside experts caution the model is purely theoretical and does not capture how aging hallmarks interact.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Somatic Mutations]]**: Random genetic changes in non-reproductive cells that accumulate with age and are the sole aging driver modeled in this study.
- **[[Aging Hallmarks]]**: The suite of biological processes that drive aging; the study isolates somatic mutations by hypothetically eliminating all others.
- **[[Lifespan Bottlenecks]]**: [[Heart Muscle Cells]] (cardiomyocytes) and [[Neurons]] are identified as critical bottlenecks because they cannot regenerate after mutation-driven damage.
- **[[Liver Cell Resilience]]**: Liver cells, which regenerate readily, showed theoretical longevity of 100,000+ years, illustrating how regenerative capacity mitigates mutation accumulation.
- **[[NPJ Aging]]**: The peer-reviewed journal in which the mathematical modeling study was published.

## Key Takeaways

- **Theoretical ceiling**: Eliminating all aging hallmarks → median lifespan ~1,759 years.
- **Somatic mutation limit**: Retaining only somatic mutations → 146–194 year median lifespan.
- **Current baseline**: Today's human median lifespan is ~79 years.
- **Critical organs**: Heart and brain cells are non-regenerative, making them primary bottlenecks.
- **Liver resilience**: Regenerative liver cells could theoretically last 100,000+ years.
- **Expert caveat**: Model uses simplified assumptions; real-world hallmark interactions are ignored.
- **Practical advice unchanged**: Exercise, whole foods, sleep, social ties, no smoking add 10–20 healthy years.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Weakest Link Principle]]**: The system's lifespan is bounded by its least resilient component — non-regenerating neurons and cardiomyocytes set the ceiling, regardless of how durable other tissues are.
- **[[Counterfactual Reasoning]]**: By mathematically isolating one aging driver (somatic mutations) and removing all others, the study uses controlled counterfactuals to estimate each factor's independent contribution to mortality.
