## Summary

A USC Leonard Davis School of Gerontology study found that older adults who followed the [[Mediterranean Diet]] more closely had higher blood levels of the mitochondrial microproteins [[Humanin]] and [[SHMOOSE]], plus lower oxidative stress markers. The researchers propose these microproteins as a new pathway linking diet to heart and brain protection. The study is small and observational, so it shows association, not causation.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Mitochondrial Microproteins]]**: Small proteins encoded in mitochondrial DNA open reading frames, not nuclear DNA; [[Humanin]] (identified 2003) and [[SHMOOSE]] (identified more recently) are the two studied here.
- **[[Mediterranean Diet]]**: Olive oil, fish, beans, lentils, fruits and vegetables; linked to lower risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and cognitive decline.
- **[[Oxidative Stress]] and [[Nox2]]**: Higher humanin tracked with lower Nox2 activity, an enzyme that generates reactive oxygen species.
- **[[Biomarkers]] and [[Precision Nutrition]]**: Humanin and SHMOOSE might indicate diet adherence and guide personalised dietary strategies.
- **People**: [[Roberto Vicinanza]] (study lead) and [[Pinchas Cohen]] (senior author, who has studied mitochondrial microproteins for 20+ years).
- **[[Alzheimer's Disease]]**: A genetic variant of SHMOOSE is tied to higher risk; the normal version appears to protect neurons from amyloid damage.

## Key Takeaways

- **Core Finding**: Closer Mediterranean diet adherence was associated with higher humanin and SHMOOSE levels.
- **Food Links**: Olive oil, fish and legumes tracked with humanin; olive oil and fewer refined carbs with SHMOOSE.
- **Oxidative Stress**: Adherent participants showed lower oxidative stress indicators.
- **Humanin-Nox2 Link**: Higher humanin tracked with lower Nox2 activity, a possible cardioprotective route.
- **Biomarker Potential**: Humanin and SHMOOSE could eventually signal diet adherence and cellular response.
- **Study Limits**: Small, observational study; cannot show the diet raises these proteins or prevents disease.
- **Next Step**: Test whether adopting the diet raises the proteins and improves heart or brain outcomes.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Association does not establish causation** — A relationship observed between two measurements, without intervention, cannot show that one produces the other; other health and lifestyle factors may drive both. · *model:* [[Correlation vs Causation]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the USC study find about the Mediterranean diet and mitochondrial microproteins?
**A**: Older adults who followed the diet more closely had significantly higher blood levels of humanin and SHMOOSE, along with lower oxidative stress indicators.

**Q2**: Which foods were linked to which microprotein, and what enzyme connection was found?
**A**: Olive oil, fish and legumes were linked to humanin; olive oil and fewer refined carbohydrates to SHMOOSE. Higher humanin also tracked with lower Nox2 activity, which generates reactive oxygen species.

**Q3**: Why can't these results yet be used to recommend the diet for raising these proteins?
**A**: The study was small and observational, so it cannot show that the diet causes the higher levels or that the proteins prevent disease. Future work must test whether adopting the diet actually raises them and improves outcomes.
