## Summary

The first phase of the international [[REACT Study]], published in *The New England Journal of Medicine*, used advanced imaging on more than 16,800 adults aged 18 to 70 with no known cardiovascular disease. It found artery plaque in 57.1% of them, including about 1 in 13 adults under 30. Standard cardiovascular risk scores identified only a small share of those who already had silent [[Atherosclerosis]], which suggests that imaging the disease directly could improve on risk estimates. Experts say the findings call for attention to heart health, not panic or screening for everyone.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Atherosclerosis]]**: Fatty deposits, cholesterol, and other substances build up inside arteries, often silently for years, before a [[Myocardial Infarction|heart attack]] or [[Stroke]].
- **[[REACT Study]]**: An ongoing Danish-Spanish study mapping how common silent plaque is across adulthood. Its next phase will test whether earlier detection improves prevention.
- **Risk estimation vs. direct detection**: Prevention has relied on estimated risk from age, blood pressure, and cholesterol, without knowing whether disease was already present.
- **[[Cardiovascular Risk Scores]]**: These tools flagged only a small proportion of people who already had silent plaque.
- **Sex differences**: Men developed plaque 5 to 10 years earlier than women. Women's steepest increase came between ages 40 and 60.
- **[[Portable Ultrasound]]**: Could eventually help find hidden plaque earlier, but more clinical trials are needed.
- **Key researchers**: [[Borja Ibáñez]] (CNIC, Spain) and [[Henning Bundgaard]] (Rigshospitalet, Copenhagen). Independent comment came from [[Sirisha Vadali]] (HonorHealth).
- **Related**: [[ckm-syndrome-young-adults-cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic]] and [[modifiable-risk-factors-coronary-plaque-heart-attack]].

## Key Takeaways

- **Plaque is common**: 57.1% of healthy adults aged 18–70 had artery plaque.
- **Starts young**: About 1 in 13 adults aged 18–29 already had silent atherosclerosis.
- **Rises with age**: About 9 in 10 adults aged 60–70 had plaque.
- **Risk scores miss cases**: Traditional scores caught only a small share of people with plaque.
- **Men earlier**: Men developed plaque 5–10 years before women.
- **Detection decades early**: Atherosclerosis can be found decades before symptoms appear.
- **No universal screening yet**: The study does not show that screening everyone prevents heart attacks.
- **Lifestyle basics**: Avoid tobacco, stay active, and eat vegetables, fruits, beans, whole grains, nuts, and fish.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Map vs. Territory]]**: Risk scores built from age, blood pressure, and cholesterol are a model of disease, not the disease itself. REACT shows that this map flags only a small share of the people whose arteries already have plaque.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the REACT study's central finding?
**A**: Silent artery plaque is common in adults with no known cardiovascular disease. It was present in 57.1% of more than 16,800 participants aged 18 to 70, including about 1 in 13 adults under 30.

**Q2**: Why does the study challenge how cardiovascular risk is usually assessed?
**A**: Common risk scores based on factors like age, blood pressure, and cholesterol identified only a small share of the people who already had silent atherosclerosis. That suggests many cases go undetected with traditional assessment alone.

**Q3**: How should a healthy young adult act on these findings?
**A**: Take heart health seriously without panicking, and base any extra testing on individual risk factors, since universal screening is not yet proven to help. Avoid tobacco, stay active, and eat a diet rich in plants and fish while limiting saturated fat and sodium.
