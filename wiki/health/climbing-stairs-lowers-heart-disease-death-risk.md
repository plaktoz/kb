## Summary
A meta-analysis of 9 studies covering 480,479 people found frequent stair-climbers had a 39% lower risk of dying from cardiovascular disease and 24% lower risk of dying from any cause, with benefits becoming apparent around 6 flights (~60-70 steps) per day.

## Core Concepts
- [[Vigorous Physical Activity]] accessible without structured exercise, via everyday stair-climbing.
- [[Cardiovascular Disease]] as the leading global cause of death, with cases nearly doubling 1990-2019.
- Observational study limitations — association vs. causation.

## Key Takeaways
- **Large Meta-Analysis**: 9 studies, 480,479 participants, 14-year median follow-up.
- **39% Lower CVD Death Risk**: Frequent stair-climbers vs. rare stair-climbers.
- **Threshold Effect**: ~6 flights (60-70 steps) a day is where benefit becomes apparent; more generally helps more.
- **Accessible**: Stair-climbing is available to the 25%+ of adults worldwide who don't meet recommended activity levels.
- **Not Causal Proof**: Self-report measurement and lack of wearable-tracker data limit certainty; regular stair-climbers may be healthier for other reasons too.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the study's core finding?
**A**: People who frequently climb stairs have a 39% lower risk of cardiovascular death and 24% lower risk of death from any cause compared to those who rarely do.

**Q2**: What's the practical threshold identified?
**A**: Around 6 flights (roughly 60-70 steps) per day appeared to be the point where cardiovascular risk reduction became apparent, with more stairs generally producing greater benefit.

**Q3**: What's the practical takeaway for someone with limited exercise time?
**A**: Everyday stair-climbing is a low-cost, accessible way to gain measurable cardiovascular benefit, though people with mobility issues should use alternatives like brisk walking or swimming.
