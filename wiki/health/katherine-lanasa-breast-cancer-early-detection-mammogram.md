## Summary

Actress [[Katherine LaNasa]], star of HBO Max's *The Pitt*, was diagnosed with stage 1 breast cancer in February 2023 after a routine mammogram caught it with no symptoms present. She underwent surgery and radiation, is now cancer-free, and partnered with [[Novartis]]' *Your Attention, Please* campaign to advocate for regular breast cancer screening. Experts note that only half of eligible women receive annual mammograms, despite a 99% five-year survival rate when breast cancer is caught early.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Breast Cancer Early Detection]]**: Routine [[mammogram]] screening identified LaNasa's stage 1 tumor before any symptoms appeared, illustrating the life-saving potential of preventive imaging.
- **[[Screening Barriers]]**: Cost, time, transportation, and fear prevent roughly half of eligible women from getting annual mammograms, per [[Dr. Francisco Esteva]] of [[Northwell's Lenox Hill Hospital]].
- **[[Personal Risk Assessment]]**: Family history and personal medical history are key inputs doctors use to tailor screening recommendations and detect elevated breast cancer risk.
- **[[Novartis Your Attention Please Campaign]]**: A public awareness initiative LaNasa joined to encourage women to prioritize regular breast health screenings.

## Key Takeaways

- LaNasa had no symptoms when stage 1 breast cancer was found via routine mammogram.
- She underwent surgery plus ~3 weeks of daily radiation; complications lasted ~6 months.
- Five-year relative survival rate is **99%** when breast cancer is caught at stage 1.
- Only ~50% of eligible women get annual mammograms, per Dr. Esteva.
- **Barriers to screening**: cost, time, transportation, and fear.
- Family and personal history are critical factors in risk-based screening conversations.
- LaNasa's cancer journey shaped her empathetic portrayal of a nurse on *The Pitt*.
- A friend she encouraged to screen was later found to have a large mass — and survived.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Prevention vs. Treatment]]**: Catching disease before it progresses is almost always cheaper, less invasive, and more survivable — LaNasa's 99% survival stat vs. late-stage outcomes makes this concrete.
- **[[Availability Bias]]**: Fear of the diagnosis (a vivid, negative outcome) paradoxically deters screening that would catch cancer early, when it is most treatable — people avoid the test rather than face a potentially bad result.
