## Summary

A Columbia University study found adults who lost 90 minutes of sleep nightly for six weeks gained about one pound on average versus periods of adequate rest, with no significant change in diet or exercise tracked. An OB-GYN not involved in the study warned that women in menopause may be especially vulnerable, since sleep disruption often coincides with slowing metabolism at that life stage.

## Update (mechanism and expert context, 2026-08-19)

Source: https://www.nationalgeographic.com/health/article/can-lack-of-sleep-cause-weight-gain (Hannah Singleton, National Geographic)

Additional detail on the same Columbia University Vagelos College of Physicians and Surgeons trial, published in Annals of Internal Medicine:

- **Study design**: 95 adults who normally slept ≥7 hours were asked to delay bedtime by 90 minutes while keeping the same wake time, for six weeks, ultimately losing about 80 minutes of sleep per night.
- **Additional outcomes**: participants gained ~1 lb on average, showed a small increase in waist circumference, and became more sedentary — about 17 more minutes per day inactive, with larger effects in men and postmenopausal women.
- **Proposed mechanism**: sleep deprivation is thought to disrupt ghrelin (hunger signal) and leptin (fullness signal), though researchers say the full pathway "isn't understood completely."
- **Context**: nearly 35% of Americans get less than 7 hours of sleep a night (CDC); this study's mild, chronic-like sleep loss more closely resembles everyday experience than prior lab studies using extreme short-term deprivation.
- **Limitations**: the 6-week trial can't determine whether effects compound over months/years; it excluded people with diagnosed sleep disorders or diabetes, so effects in those populations remain unknown.
- **Expert framing**: Yale's Brian Wojeck notes most sleep-clinic patients are chronically, not just mildly, sleep-deprived, so real-world effects may be larger than the study shows. Both experts frame sleep as one part of a "trifecta" with diet and exercise for weight management, not a standalone fix.
