## Summary

A 175-person, five-month trial published in Frontiers in Nutrition found that regular almond consumption improved both sleep latency and sleep efficiency in people with poor sleep quality, with the almond group showing significantly higher melatonin concentrations and fewer reported sleep disturbances than a calorie-matched non-almond snack group.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Sleep Latency]]**: How quickly a person falls asleep after going to bed — one of the two measures this study found improved with regular almond consumption.
- **[[Sleep Efficiency]]**: The proportion of time in bed actually spent sleeping — the second measure shown to improve in the almond group.
- **[[Neuroendocrine Regulation]]**: The researchers attribute almonds' sleep benefit to micronutrients that support sleep via muscle relaxation and neuroendocrine pathways, evidenced by elevated melatonin in blood testing.

## Key Takeaways

- **Study design**: 175 participants (ages 21-55) with poor sleep quality; 88 ate almonds regularly, 87 ate a calorie-matched non-almond snack, over five months.
- **Measurement**: Brain-wave recording, blood tests, and self-reported sleep scores were used to assess outcomes.
- **Result**: Almond group reported fewer sleep disturbances, easier sleep onset, and significantly higher melatonin concentrations by blood test; the clearest benefit was in perceived (subjective) sleep quality.
- **Funding caveat**: The trial was supported by the Almond Board of California — a relevant conflict-of-interest note when weighing the findings.
- **Practical dosing**: About 23 almonds (one ounce) is a standard serving; almonds are calorie-dense, so portion awareness matters.
