## Summary

[[Information Processing Theory]] models human cognition as three sequential stages — sensory memory, working memory, and long-term memory — borrowing a computer metaphor of input, processing, and storage that made complex mental functions easier to teach and apply. Originating with [[George A. Miller]]'s discovery that working memory holds "seven plus or minus two" items, the framework remains widely used in instructional design and corporate training, despite criticism that it oversimplifies emotion, motivation, and the brain's parallel (not linear) processing.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Sensory Memory]]** — a one-to-two-second perceptual filter; only attended details move forward for further processing
- **[[Working Memory]]** — limited to roughly seven items; chunking, repetition, and visual aids expand its effective capacity
- **[[Long-Term Memory]]** — durable storage whose retrievability depends on how well information was encoded, via connections to prior knowledge and varied recall practice
- **[[Chunking]]** — grouping related information into meaningful units to reduce cognitive load during encoding

## Key Takeaways

- **Learning is staged, not instant**: effective instructional design mirrors how the brain naturally filters, processes, and stores information
- **Connection drives retention**: linking new material to existing knowledge or real-world application makes it stick
- **The computer metaphor has limits**: human cognition is messier, more emotional, and more parallel than a CPU's input-output model
- **Applied use**: spaced repetition, adaptive quizzing, and scenario-based training all lean on this framework's stages

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Working Memory Bottleneck]]**: because working memory can only hold a handful of items at once, any learning design that doesn't chunk or scaffold information will lose the learner regardless of how "important" the content is — this is the same constraint that makes [[Cognitive Load Theory]] and chunking-based study techniques effective elsewhere in this vault.
