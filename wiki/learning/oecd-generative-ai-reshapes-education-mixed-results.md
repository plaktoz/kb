## Summary
The [[OECD]] Digital Education Outlook 2026 finds that education-specific [[Generative AI]] tools grounded in learning science outperform general-purpose chatbots in classrooms. General-purpose AI can improve written work quality without lifting exam performance, while GenAI-powered tutoring shows stronger gains, especially in mathematics.

## Core Concepts
- **[[OECD]]** — publisher of the Digital Education Outlook 2026 report on AI in classrooms
- **[[Generative AI]]** — rapidly adopted by students and teachers due to easy access and intuitive design
- **[[AI Tutoring Tools]]** — GenAI-powered tutors shown to enhance teacher capacity and student outcomes
- **[[Productive Struggle]]** — a learning process AI risks undermining when it replaces effortful practice

## Key Takeaways
- **Education-specific AI** grounded in learning science beats general-purpose chatbots.
- **General-purpose AI** may improve writing quality without boosting exam scores.
- **AI tutoring tools** show the strongest gains in mathematics outcomes.
- **Risk**: replacing [[Productive Struggle]] may weaken deep understanding and focus.
- **Policy guidance**: prioritize pedagogically sound AI that is rigorously evaluated.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Optimizing for easy AI-assisted output (better-written essays) can decouple from the actual target — improved exam performance and understanding.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core finding of the OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026 regarding AI tools in education?
**A**: Education-specific AI tools grounded in learning science deliver stronger outcomes than general-purpose chatbots.

**Q3**: What risk does the report highlight when AI is used to support learning?
