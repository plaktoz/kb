## Summary
The UK Department for Education announced national curriculum reforms giving financial literacy a much stronger role, with pupils learning the "big three" concepts of inflation, compound interest, and diversification of risk. Maths reforms will focus on mastery of fundamentals and applying them to real-life money topics such as mortgages, savings, and scams. The financial literacy curriculum is being tested in classrooms by The Richmond Project for teaching from 2028.

## Core Concepts
- [[Financial Literacy]]: managing money, judging value, spotting scams, and understanding payslips, interest rates, and mortgages.
- [[The Big Three]]: [[Inflation]], [[Compound Interest]], and [[Diversification]] of risk.
- [[National Curriculum]] reform: primary and secondary financial lessons, plus a rebalanced GCSE foundation tier with financial mathematics and statistics.
- [[Mastery Learning]] in maths: cut unnecessary content, sequence topics better, and have pupils reason and explain their thinking.
- [[Young People and Work Report]] by [[Alan Milburn]]: the challenge the government is acting on.
- [[Lucy Powell]]: Education Secretary on connecting classroom learning to the real world.
- [[The Richmond Project]] ([[Rishi Sunak]]): numeracy charity testing the curriculum with the DfE.
- [[The 93% Club]]: argues financial knowledge should not depend on family and network.

## Key Takeaways
- **Online Spending**: 71% of children aged 7 to 17 buy online; 67% of them unsupervised.
- **Learning Gap**: Only a third of children recall useful money lessons at school.
- **Parent View**: 48% of parents think too little time is spent on money management.
- **Reasoning Focus**: Pupils should explain the reasoning behind calculations, not only perform them.
- **Practical Examples**: Compare supermarket prices, test discounts, calculate part-time earnings, model savings growth.
- **Secondary Topics**: Why prices rise, how savings grow, spotting scams, navigating mortgages.
- **Timeline**: Classroom testing now; schools teach the curriculum from 2028.
- **Equity Argument**: 45% of working-class students said others questioned university because of debt.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core change announced in the curriculum reform?
**A**: Financial literacy gets a much stronger role, with all pupils taught the "big three" of inflation, compound interest, and diversification of risk, linked to maths and real-life topics.

**Q2**: What evidence motivates the reform?
**A**: 71% of 7-to-17-year-olds buy online, and 67% of those do so unsupervised, yet only a third recall learning useful money lessons at school. Nearly half of parents (48%) say too little time goes to financial management.

**Q3**: Why do supporters say this should be taught in schools?
**A**: The 93% Club argues financial knowledge should not depend on family and network, calling it an easy win for equality of opportunity. Young people can then make decisions like going to university for themselves.
