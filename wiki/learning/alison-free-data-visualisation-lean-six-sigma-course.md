## Summary

[[Alison]] launched a free, CPD-accredited, intermediate-level course on data visualisation for [[Lean Six Sigma]] practitioners. It argues the persistent challenge is communicating what data means, not a lack of data. The course covers data preparation, Lean Six Sigma chart types, dashboards, and before-and-after visuals that show and sustain improvement.

## Core Concepts

- [[Lean Six Sigma]] and the [[DMAIC]] methodology, built on evidence-based decisions.
- [[Data Visualisation]] as the bridge between analysis and action.
- [[Pareto Chart]], trend chart, histogram, and scatter chart as the standard Lean Six Sigma visuals.
- [[Data Quality]] assessment and baseline process measures in Excel.
- [[KPI Dashboards]] with clear targets and status thresholds.
- Before-and-after visuals and monitoring tools to prove and sustain improvement.
- [[Danielle Verde Nolasco]] (course reviewer) and [[Mike Feerick]] (Alison founder and CEO).

## Key Takeaways

- **Communication Gap**: Process professionals struggle to convey what data means, not to find it.
- **Data First**: Course starts with data sources, table structure, and quality correction.
- **Chart Selection**: Pareto prioritizes problems; trend, histogram, and scatter charts cover time, distribution, and relationships.
- **Dashboard Design**: Arrange reports so key findings surface immediately, not buried.
- **Proving Change**: Before-and-after visuals show an intervention worked.
- **Sustaining Gains**: Monitoring tools keep improvement visible and prevent quiet reversal.
- **Access**: Free, CPD-accredited, certificate on completion; suits Yellow and Green Belts.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Evidence drives action only when it is legible** — Data changes decisions only when it is communicated in a form the audience can grasp; raw numbers alone do not move people.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What problem does the course address?
**A**: Lean Six Sigma professionals often struggle to communicate what their data means. Spreadsheets of numbers do not drive action; clear visuals do.

**Q2**: Which visuals does the course teach, and what is each used for?
**A**: Pareto charts prioritize problems, trend charts track performance over time, histograms show distribution, and scatter charts explore relationships between variables.

**Q3**: How does the course help demonstrate and sustain an improvement project's results?
**A**: Learners apply before-and-after visuals to show the intervention's impact and build monitoring tools that keep the improvement visible over time.
