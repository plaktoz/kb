## Summary
Cognitive science shows students learn best through active engagement and productive struggle — conditions current AI tools may undermine by encouraging reliance over independent thinking. The author cites falling test scores, reduced peer interaction, and unequal AI usage by income to argue schools should delay widespread adoption pending real evidence.

## Core Concepts
- [[Productive Struggle]] as a necessary condition for durable learning, threatened by AI over-reliance.
- The "[[Zone of No Development]]" — when AI assistance replaces the struggle and social support that drive learning.
- [[AI-Driven False Confidence]] — overestimating one's own knowledge after AI-assisted work.
- Emerging [[AI Equity Gap]] in education by household income.

## Key Takeaways
- **Lower Ownership**: Students using LLMs show less effort and less sense of ownership over their work.
- **False Learning**: Students using GPT scored 17% lower on a math test than non-AI users once the tool was removed.
- **Less Peer Interaction**: Generative AI use reduces student-to-student collaboration.
- **Equity Gap**: 20% of teens in households earning under $30K do most homework with AI, vs. 7% in households earning over $75K.
- **Policy Ask**: Delay broad adoption, restrict AI in foundational reading/writing/math, prioritize teacher-facing (not student-facing) AI tools.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When "getting the answer" becomes the target rather than learning, students optimize for output via AI shortcuts rather than the underlying skill the assignment was meant to build.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim?
**A**: Current AI tools may weaken the conditions (active engagement, struggle, peer collaboration) that cognitive science shows are necessary for real learning.

**Q2**: What evidence supports this?
**A**: Students who used GPT for math practice scored 17% lower than non-AI users once the tool was removed, indicating a false sense of learning rather than genuine skill acquisition.

**Q3**: What should schools do in response?
**A**: Delay widespread student-facing AI adoption pending pilot evidence, restrict use in foundational instruction, and prioritize AI tools that support teachers rather than replace student effort.
