## Summary

A study-skills guide explains Ebbinghaus's Forgetting Curve — humans forget roughly 50% of new information within an hour and 80% within a day — and outlines evidence-based strategies (spacing, previewing, active learning, sleep, diet, exercise) to slow decay and improve retention and recall.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Forgetting Curve]]**: Hermann Ebbinghaus's 19th-century finding that memory decays most steeply immediately after learning, with retention improved by meaningful content, well-organized presentation, and active engagement — see also [[Retrieval Practice]].
- **[[Spaced Learning]]**: Practicing or retrieving information distributed over time (rather than massed together) slows forgetting and is critical to memory consolidation.
- **[[Sleep and Memory Consolidation]]**: Sleep is one of the strongest predictors of academic success, helping fix and consolidate memories; both under- and oversleeping (≤5 hrs or ≥9 hrs) is linked to worse cognitive test performance.
- **[[Previewing (Pre-Learning)]]**: Reviewing course materials before a lecture increases retention and comprehension and prepares learners to engage more actively in class.

## Key Takeaways

- **~50% of new information is forgotten within an hour**, ~80% within 24 hours, and only ~5% is recalled after 30 days without revisiting it.
- **Active learning outperforms passive learning for retention** — techniques like applying, explaining, evaluating, and creating reinforce recall more than rereading.
- **Physiological self-care directly affects memory**: sleep consolidates memories and prevents decay; energy foods (nuts, berries) support focus while processed foods, sugar, and caffeine undermine it; moderate-to-vigorous exercise releases dopamine that sharpens learning.
- **Spacing beats cramming**: distributing practice over time is proven to slow the forgetting process more effectively than repeated short-interval review.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Memory decays predictably and rapidly according to the Forgetting Curve, but specific strategies — spacing, active learning, previewing, and physiological self-care — can meaningfully slow that decay and improve retention.

**Q2**: What does Ebbinghaus's Forgetting Curve research show about the timeline of memory loss?
**A**: Roughly 50% of newly learned information is forgotten within an hour, nearly 80% within 24 hours, and without revisiting the material, only about 5% is recalled after 30 days.

**Q3**: How would a student apply "spacing" and "previewing" together in a real study routine?
**A**: Preview upcoming lecture material briefly beforehand to prime attention and comprehension, then review and retrieve that same material again at increasing intervals afterward (spaced learning) rather than cramming it all in one sitting.
