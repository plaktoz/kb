## Summary

The science of learning is an interdisciplinary field drawing on cognitive science, neuroscience, psychology, and education to produce evidence-based insights into how people acquire, retain, and apply knowledge. It challenges historically tradition- and intuition-driven educational practices by offering research-backed frameworks for instructional design and school leadership decisions. Practical applications include structured literacy, retrieval practice, spaced repetition, and whole-learner approaches to student development.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Science of Learning]]** — interdisciplinary field asking what research tells us about effective teaching and learning; replaces trend-driven or assumption-based practice.
- **[[Evidence-Based Instruction]]** — aligning teaching methods with replicable research findings rather than vendor claims or popular trends.
- **[[Science of Reading]]** — the most visible application: structured literacy approaches emphasizing [[Phonemic Awareness]], phonics, vocabulary, fluency, and comprehension.
- **[[Retrieval Practice]]** — requiring students to actively recall information over time (low-stakes quizzes, cumulative review) rather than passive re-reading.
- **[[Spaced Repetition]]** — distributing practice across time to strengthen long-term retention; see also [[Spaced Learning]].
- **[[Cognitive Science]]** / **[[Neuroscience]]** — foundational disciplines informing memory, attention, and motivation research applied to classrooms.
- **[[Learning Styles Myth]]** — the debunked notion that instruction should match visual, auditory, or kinesthetic preferences; research does not support tailoring instruction this way.
- **[[Whole-Learner Development]]** — recognizing that social, emotional, relational, and developmental factors shape academic outcomes alongside academic content.
- **[[Educational Equity]]** — using research-aligned instruction to identify needs earlier, close literacy gaps, and evaluate whether systems work for all learners.

## Key Takeaways

- **Interdisciplinary roots**: Science of learning draws on cognitive science, neuroscience, psychology, and education.
- **Replaces tradition**: Most historical schooling practices rested on intuition or trends, not research.
- **Structured literacy wins**: States adopting phonics-based reading instruction are driven by learning-science evidence.
- **Learning styles debunked**: No research supports matching instruction to visual/auditory/kinesthetic preferences.
- **Retrieval beats re-reading**: Low-stakes quizzes and spaced review outperform passive review for retention.
- **Whole learner matters**: Social, emotional, and relational factors are research-proven drivers of achievement.
- **Leadership implications**: Principals and superintendents should interrogate whether initiatives are research-backed before adoption.
- **Equity tool**: Evidence-based literacy instruction can reduce early achievement gaps across demographics.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[First Principles Thinking]]**: The science of learning strips away inherited educational assumptions and rebuilds practice from what cognition research actually demonstrates — the same reasoning from evidence over tradition that defines first-principles analysis.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When "learning style" becomes the target, teachers optimize for stylistic match rather than actual retention — precisely what the evidence-based movement corrects by shifting the metric to measured learning outcomes.
