## Summary
AI use among students is near universal, but understanding of responsible use lags adoption, with early signs of overreliance. Oxford University Press argues institutions should embed AI literacy directly, and describes its own six-part module as a ready-to-use resource for instructors. The piece is a commentary by a publisher promoting its own product.

## Core Concepts
- [[AI Literacy]]: skills to evaluate generative AI content and usage responsibly, valuable across disciplines and careers.
- [[Generative AI]] in study: research, idea generation, organising thinking, refining drafts, and feedback, not just writing assignments.
- [[Overreliance on AI]]: dependence and erosion of [[Critical Thinking]].
- [[Academic Integrity]]: distinguishing AI use that supports learning from use that undermines it.
- [[Oxford University Press]] AI literacy module: interactive video and assessments in six parts, authored with Dr Anand Rao and Stefan Bauschard.
- [[Prompt Engineering]]: constructing and iteratively refining prompts ethically.
- [[Skills Gap]] between employer demand for AI skills and graduate preparation.

## Key Takeaways
- **Near-Universal Use**: 95% of UK students report using AI in at least one way (HEPI).
- **Early Dependence**: 21% find problems harder without AI; 20% depend on it for better work.
- **Time Pressure**: 67% of US undergraduates work for pay, driving AI efficiency-seeking.
- **Faculty Unprepared**: 68% of faculty say institutions haven't prepared them to use GenAI for teaching.
- **Graduate Readiness**: 62% of faculty think graduates are unprepared in GenAI understanding and use.
- **Skills Gap**: 51% of new graduates aren't actively building AI skills despite employer demand.
- **Module Structure**: Six parts: fundamentals, integrity, pros/cons, critical thinking, prompting, course-specific activity.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central argument about students and AI?
**A**: AI adoption among students is near universal but outpaces understanding of responsible use, so institutions need to embed AI literacy to counter overreliance and prepare students for work.

**Q2**: What evidence shows early signs of AI dependence and a readiness gap?
**A**: In the Digital Education Council survey, 21% find it harder to work through problems without AI and 20% depend on it for better work. In the AAC&U/Elon report, 62% of faculty think graduates are unprepared and 68% say their institutions haven't prepared them to teach with GenAI.

**Q3**: How does OUP propose instructors address AI literacy without leading the discussion themselves?
**A**: Instructors can assign a ready-made six-part module with interactive video and assessments, including a course-specific activity, on Oxford platforms. OUP also hosts faculty webinars.
