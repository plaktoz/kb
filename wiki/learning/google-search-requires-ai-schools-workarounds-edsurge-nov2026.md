## Summary
Google says that from early 2027 its AI tools and Search are off by default for K-12 accounts, and schools that block the AI features lose Search too. Districts and filtering companies were already hiding AI results through plug-ins and filters, while big-city districts weigh switching search engines. Politicians call the policy monopoly power, while some education nonprofits see a long-requested off switch explained poorly.

## Core Concepts
- [[Google Search]]: AI is built into the technology, so blocking AI features also blocks Search for schools.
- [[AI Overviews]] and [[AI Mode]]: the features districts want to suppress.
- [[Fairfax County Public Schools]]: built a browser plug-in hiding AI answers before Google's announcement.
- [[Niles Community Schools]]: published the free Chrome extension Hide AI Overview on GitHub, built by [[Jim Tyler]].
- [[Securly]]: web filter that announced Nov. 4 it blocks AI Overviews and AI Mode.
- [[Zohran Mamdani]] and [[Ed Markey]]: criticize the policy as a monopolist forcing AI on children.
- [[Patricia Burch]]: argues the key decisions are made in product design and contracts.
- [[InnovateEDU]]: [[Erin Mote]] says student-facing AI should be off by default.

## Key Takeaways
- **Policy**: Announced Nov. 3, AI tools and Search off by default for K-12 from early 2027.
- **Lock-Together**: Schools that block Google's AI features lose Search access.
- **Big Districts**: New York City, Los Angeles, and Chicago are weighing dropping Google.
- **Workarounds**: Plug-ins and commercial filters already hide AI Overviews and AI Mode.
- **Upstream Control**: Burch says decisions happen in product design and contract stages.
- **Advocate View**: Off-by-default is what districts asked for, but communicated poorly.
- **Deadline**: Google must answer Markey's letter by Nov. 30.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Defaults decide outcomes, so who sets the default holds the power** — Making AI off by default and school-controlled shifts decisions to districts, which advocates say is where they belong. The article presents this as the reason advocates welcome the change. · *model:* [[Default Effect]]
→ explains: Policy, Advocate View

**T2. Choices are shaped upstream, before the decision point** — Burch argues what students are exposed to is determined in product design and the contract process, so late concessions matter less. · *model:* [[Choice Architecture]]
