## Summary

[[UNESCO]], with the Ministry of Education and Higher Education and Palestine Technical University College - Kadoorie, has opened two vocational training units in the West Bank, funded by Japan. The units in Tulkarm and Jenin specialise in beautification and fashion design and can host up to 80 students in grades 11 and 12. They are part of a project to expand skills training and livelihoods for Palestinian youth in the West Bank and Gaza.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Technical and Vocational Education and Training]] (TVET)**: Hands-on training alongside formal education, aimed at employment and self-employment.
- **[[UNESCO]]**: Implementer of the project *Supporting Livelihoods and Economic Recovery through Accelerated Skills Training for Palestinian Youth*.
- **[[Government of Japan]]**: Funder of the two units.
- **[[Palestine Technical University College - Kadoorie]]**: Partner institution, with the Ministry of Education and Higher Education.
- **[[Mental Health and Psychosocial Support]] (MHPSS)**: Activities for young people and caregivers in Gaza.
- **[[Technical Learning Space]]**: Being established in Gaza for crisis-affected youth aged 15 and above.

## Key Takeaways

- **Locations**: Deir Al-Ghusun School in Tulkarm and Jenin Secondary School for Girls.
- **Specialisations**: Beautification and fashion design.
- **Capacity**: Up to 80 students in grades 11 and 12.
- **Beyond equipment**: Learning resources, instructor capacity-building and work-oriented programmes.
- **Target groups**: Particularly young women and persons with disabilities.
- **Barriers addressed**: Economic hardship, mobility restrictions and crisis-related disruptions.
- **Gaza component**: MHPSS activities and a new Technical Learning Space.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did UNESCO and its partners launch in the West Bank?
**A**: Two equipped vocational training units in Tulkarm and Jenin, specialising in beautification and fashion design. They can accommodate up to 80 students in grades 11 and 12, with funding from Japan.

**Q2**: What does the project provide beyond the physical training units?
**A**: It supports learning resources, instructor capacity-building and practical, work-oriented programmes, and strengthens institutional capacities. It also works to broaden access despite economic hardship, mobility restrictions and crisis disruptions.

**Q3**: How does the Gaza component complement the West Bank work?
**A**: In Gaza the project integrates mental health and psychosocial support for young people and caregivers and is establishing a Technical Learning Space for crisis-affected youth aged 15 and above. Together these aim to promote inclusive skills development and resilience across the West Bank and Gaza.
