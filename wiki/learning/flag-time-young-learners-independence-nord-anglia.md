## Summary
Nord Anglia Education reports that Flag Time, a 20-minute daily routine for children aged three to six, improved learning behaviours across 22 measures by an average of 42%. Children work on personalised activities with teachers withholding answers, then reflect on what they achieved and what to change.

## Core Concepts
- [[Flag Time]]: daily activity in which each child follows a personalised flag to a problem, idea, skill, or challenge.
- [[Independent Learning]]: making choices, planning, and persevering without relying on adults.
- [[Productive Struggle]]: teachers let children work things out and learn from mistakes instead of giving answers.
- [[Metacognition]]: end-of-activity reflection on achievements, difficulties, and next-time changes.
- [[Nord Anglia Education]]: international schools organisation running the research programme.
- [[Learning Dispositions]]: curiosity, trying, getting stuck, and trying again.
- [[Boston College]] collaboration: related work helping students control their own learning.

## Key Takeaways
- **Self-Awareness**: Understanding own strengths and weaknesses scores rose 130%.
- **Planning**: Ability to plan what to do rose 127%.
- **Perseverance**: Persistence when tasks are difficult improved 76%.
- **Independence**: Working and deciding independently rose 55%.
- **Study Scale**: 115 children, 54 teachers, 23 schools, 22 behaviours; average gain 42%.
- **Earlier Comparison**: An earlier year showed greater progress than a comparison group.
- **Teacher Role**: Teachers hold back answers and encourage different approaches.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Capability grows when learners do the thinking** — Children build independence and perseverance when given regular chances to decide and solve problems, with adults withholding the answer. · *model:* [[Desirable Difficulty]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does the Nord Anglia research claim about Flag Time?
**A**: A 20-minute daily routine can help children as young as three become more confident, independent learners. Scores across 22 learning behaviours improved by an average of 42%.

**Q2**: How does Flag Time work, and what specific gains were measured?
**A**: Each child follows a personalised flag to an activity while teachers encourage working things out rather than giving answers, then children reflect on the activity. Self-awareness rose 130%, planning 127%, perseverance 76%, and independent decision-making 55%.

**Q3**: What does the study imply for how adults should support young children's learning?
**A**: Give children regular opportunities to think, decide, and solve problems themselves, since they can build these skills from an early age. Reflection at the end of an activity builds awareness of how they learn.
