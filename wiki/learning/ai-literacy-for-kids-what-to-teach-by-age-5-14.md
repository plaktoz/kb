## Summary
AI literacy for children means understanding what AI is, how it works, its limits, and how to use it responsibly, not just operating chatbots. The article lays out an age-by-age progression from ages 5 to 14, built alongside computational thinking and coding. Schools should fold AI into a broader K-12 computer science pathway rather than replace it.

## Core Concepts
- [[AI Literacy]]: the ability to evaluate, understand, interact with and use AI responsibly.
- [[Computational Thinking]]: sequencing, patterns, decomposition and debugging, the base for later AI education.
- [[Algorithms]]: step-by-step instructions, introduced at ages 7-8 through recipes and directions.
- [[Machine Learning]]: introduced at ages 9-10 as data used to find patterns and make predictions.
- [[Generative AI]]: explored at ages 11-12 with prompting, training data, bias, privacy and copyright.
- [[Algorithmic Bias]]: studied alongside ethics, deepfakes and misinformation at ages 13-14.
- [[Python]]: the text-based language suggested for the middle-school transition.
- [[K-12 Computer Science Curriculum]]: the pathway in which AI literacy should be embedded.

## Key Takeaways
- **Not Just Tool Use**: Access to AI tools does not automatically make students AI literate.
- **Ages 5-6**: Distinguish human from artificial intelligence; practice sequencing, patterns and debugging.
- **Ages 7-8**: Learn algorithms, if/then decisions, loops and variables; computers follow rules.
- **Ages 9-10**: Learn data, classification and accuracy; challenge the belief that AI is always right.
- **Ages 11-12**: Check AI answers against reliable sources; prompting is not the whole curriculum.
- **Ages 13-14**: Shift from AI users to creators; debate ethics and build chatbots.
- **Coding Link**: Coding reveals AI as built by people from algorithms, data and models.
- **Gradual Pathway**: Embed AI concepts in existing computer science courses, not separate grade courses.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Advanced understanding builds on fundamentals** — Complex concepts should be introduced in an order matched to the learner's development, because students cannot grasp advanced ideas before the basics beneath them. · *model:* [[Scaffolding (Learning Theory)]]
→ explains: Ages 5-6, Ages 7-8, Ages 9-10, Gradual Pathway

**T2. Using a system is not understanding it** — Operating a tool without knowing how it works leaves a user unable to judge its output, so literacy requires knowing mechanism and limits, not only skill at getting answers.
→ explains: Not Just Tool Use, Ages 11-12, Coding Link

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does the article say AI literacy for kids actually involves?
**A**: Understanding what AI is, how it works at a basic level, what it can and cannot do, and how to use it responsibly, not just using tools like ChatGPT or Claude.

**Q2**: How does the focus change between ages 9-10 and ages 11-12?
**A**: At 9-10 students learn data, classification and that AI can be wrong; at 11-12 they work with generative AI, bias and prompting, and verify AI answers against reliable sources.

**Q3**: How should schools implement AI literacy?
**A**: Introduce AI concepts gradually within a K-12 computer science and digital literacy pathway, building computational thinking and coding first rather than replacing CS with AI education.
