## Summary

[[Preply]]'s first annual Progress Report surveyed 5,050 working professionals in nine countries. It found that 88% use AI to learn and 76% spend more time learning than two years ago, yet that activity often doesn't turn into usable real-world skills. Preply calls this the "[[Progress Gap]]": the distance between the progress learners feel while learning and their confidence using the skill in a real situation. Learners overwhelmingly want AI to support their learning rather than lead it, and they trust a blend of AI and human expertise for long-term progress.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Progress Gap]]**: The disconnect between progress felt during learning and confidence applying the skill in real life. 49% kept a learning routine for months but still struggled to apply it.
- **AI as starting point**: 37% name [[AI Tools]] as a primary learning method, ahead of employer training (34%) and online courses (33%). Common uses are explanations, summaries, translation, corrections and structuring learning.
- **Access ≠ progress**: 66% agree that access to information doesn't automatically lead to progress, and 55% say it's harder than ever to identify what genuinely helps them improve.
- **Human–AI division of labor**: AI reduces friction early on by explaining, summarizing, translating and generating exercises. Humans provide accountability, spot misconceptions, adapt practice to context and judge whether the learner can perform independently.
- **Generational divide**: [[Gen Z]] has the highest AI adoption (92%) but the widest gap. The survey cannot say whether AI causes the difference.
- **[[AI Fluency]]**: The article connects the findings to the growing focus on practical workforce capability rather than counts of training completions or tool usage.
- **Methodology**: An online [[Dynata]] survey completed June 26, 2026. Respondents were employed or self-employed adults aged 18–55 in the US, UK, Spain, France, Germany, Italy, Poland, Japan and Argentina. Results are self-reported perceptions, not measured skill gains.

## Key Takeaways

- **Busy, not better**: 63% feel busy learning without truly improving.
- **More time, same results**: 52% spend more time learning without always seeing better results.
- **Speed boost**: 60% of daily AI users learn faster than expected, versus 31% of non-users.
- **Support role**: 90% want AI supporting rather than leading their learning.
- **Blended trust**: 75% prefer AI plus human expertise, 14% traditional learning without AI, and 11% fully AI-led.
- **Consistency drivers**: Human feedback (46%), clear goals (44%) and real-world use (42%) far outrank AI reminders (18%).
- **Gen Z gap**: 71% busy without improving, versus 56% of Gen X.
- **Gen Z abandonment**: 68% abandoned learning last year, versus 38% of Gen X.
- **Seniority split**: 47% of senior leaders use AI to learn daily, versus 31% of non-managerial staff.
- **Country spread**: Argentina and Poland have the highest adoption (93%), the US and Japan the lowest (85%).
- **Employer implication**: Measure real-world performance, not time spent, completions or streaks.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: The report warns employers against treating time spent, course completions or learning streaks as measures of progress. Once activity metrics become the target, they stop tracking actual skill, which helps explain how 63% can feel busy without improving.
- **[[Fluency Illusion]]**: Instant AI explanations and summaries make material *feel* understood. That inflates the sense of progress during learning without building the recall and judgment needed under real conditions, which is exactly the gap Preply measures.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the "Progress Gap" that Preply identified?
**A**: It is the distance between the progress people feel while learning and their confidence using the skill in a real situation. 49% kept a learning routine for months but still struggled to apply the skill.

**Q2**: What do learners say drives learning consistency, and how does AI rank?
**A**: Human feedback or accountability ranks highest (46%), followed by clear goals (44%) and the ability to use learning in real situations (42%). AI reminders or tools rank much lower at 18%.

**Q3**: How should employers redesign workplace learning in light of these findings?
**A**: They should stop treating time spent, completions or streaks as enough. Instead they can pair AI explanations with simulations, supervised practice, manager feedback or live coaching, and judge outcomes by whether employees can do the work with less assistance.
