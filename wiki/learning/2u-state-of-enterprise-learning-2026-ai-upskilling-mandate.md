## Summary
2U's State of Enterprise Learning 2026, conducted by NewtonX with 507 L&D and workforce strategy leaders, finds 83% of employers prioritize AI capabilities but only 29% have built mature ones. The 15% of "AI transformation leaders" stand out mainly through a strong leadership mandate and deeper measurement of learning outcomes. Formal ownership of AI upskilling (HR vs. L&D vs. IT) barely mattered, accounting for just 1% of influence.

## Core Concepts
- **[[2U]] / [[edX Enterprise]]** — publisher of the report; enterprise learning business connecting companies with university learning
- **[[NewtonX]]** — research firm that surveyed 507 leaders (June–July 2026) and ran 10 expert interviews
- **[[AI Upskilling]] Mandate** — executive support, clarity on capabilities to develop, plus budget and capacity
- **[[AI Transformation Leaders]]** — 15% of companies combining mature AI capability with high confidence in L&D delivery
- **[[Learning Measurement]] Depth** — tracking outcomes beyond course participation
- **[[Leadership Development]] Integration** — building AI and leadership capabilities as one connected effort
- **[[Chris Kelly]]** (NewtonX) and **[[Andy Morgan]]** (2U) — quoted on the findings

## Key Takeaways
- **Ambition Gap**: 83% prioritize AI capabilities; only 29% have mature ones.
- **Weak Mandates**: 78% of employers lack a strong mandate to lead AI upskilling.
- **Low L&D Confidence**: Only 26% highly confident L&D can deliver needed capabilities.
- **Stronger Mandates**: Leaders are 3.9x as likely to have a strong upskilling mandate.
- **Deeper Measurement**: Leaders track 1.6x as many learning-success measures.
- **Leadership Link**: Leaders integrate AI and leadership development at 2.7x the rate.
- **Disconnected Efforts**: 85% don't develop AI and leadership capabilities together.
- **Driver Weights**: Mandate 59%, measurement 34%, formal ownership just 1% of influence.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Capability follows mandate, not org-chart ownership** — Organizational capability gets built when leadership supplies direction, clarity, and resources; deciding which team formally owns the work builds nothing on its own.
→ explains: Ambition Gap, Weak Mandates, Low L&D Confidence, Stronger Mandates, Driver Weights

**T2. Measuring outcomes rather than activity enables real capability building** — Tracking whether learning changes workforce readiness, not just participation, separates programs that build capability from course catalogs.
→ explains: Deeper Measurement, Driver Weights

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What gap does 2U's State of Enterprise Learning 2026 report identify?
**A**: AI capabilities are a workforce priority for 83% of employers, but only 29% have mature AI capabilities, and 78% lack a strong mandate to lead AI upskilling.

**Q2**: Which factors most explained "AI transformation leader" status in NewtonX's analysis?
**A**: Mandate strength accounted for 59% of combined influence and measurement depth 34%, while formal ownership of AI upskilling accounted for just 1%.

**Q3**: What should a company debating whether HR, L&D, or IT owns AI upskilling do instead?
**A**: Focus on securing a clear leadership mandate, capacity to act, and measures showing whether learning moves workforce readiness, since ownership alone barely matters.
