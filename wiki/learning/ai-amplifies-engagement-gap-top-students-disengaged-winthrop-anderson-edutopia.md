## Summary

A small sliver of highly motivated students use AI to deepen learning, but for the vast majority it acts as a demotivating shortcut, widening the gap between engaged and disengaged students. Research by Rebecca Winthrop and Jenny Anderson finds about 65% of middle and high schoolers are in Passenger or Resister mode, where AI invites outsourcing. Evidence from Brookings, a Chinese study of 27,000 teens, and the Khanmigo trial shows that motivation determines whether AI helps.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Rebecca Winthrop]]** and **[[Jenny Anderson]]**: Authors of *The Disengaged Teen*; warn AI may amplify differences between engaged and disengaged students.
- **[[Student Engagement Modes]]**: Four modes (Passenger, Resister, Achiever, Explorer) from a survey of 65,000+ students.
- **[[Brookings Institution]]**: Premortem study concluded AI's risks in K-12 education overshadow its benefits.
- **[[Khanmigo]]**: AI tutor used only about a third of study days; many students tried to coax out answers.
- **[[Homework Outsourcing]]**: In a study of ~27,000 Chinese teens, AI users scored 20% lower on closed-book tests.
- **[[Intrinsic Motivation]]** and **[[Purpose]]**: [[David Yeager]] says connecting schoolwork to purpose sustains engagement.
- **[[AI Vulnerability Spectrum]]**: Principal Andrew Miller's approach: ask what thinking a task requires.

## Key Takeaways

- **Engagement Split**: About half are Passengers, 15% Resisters, 30% Achievers; about 4% report Explorer-type experiences.
- **Dependency**: 16% of AI homework users struggle to start without it.
- **Test Penalty**: AI-using Chinese teens scored 20% lower on closed-book tests.
- **Brookings Verdict**: Risks of generative AI in children's education overshadow benefits.
- **Motivation Matters**: Khanmigo used only about a third of study days; Sal Khan cited motivation as the lesson.
- **Classroom Responses**: Blue books, oral exams, in-class practice, or disclosed and graded AI use.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Tools amplify existing motivation rather than create it** — When effort is optional, a tool that removes effort benefits those already motivated and lets the unmotivated skip the learning. · *model:* [[Matthew Effect]]
→ explains: Engagement Split, Motivation Matters, Dependency

**T2. The brain takes the path of least resistance** — Learning is effortful and the brain seeks the most energy-efficient route to a goal, so a shortcut that completes the task is hard to resist. · *model:* [[Cognitive Offloading]]
→ explains: Dependency, Test Penalty

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about AI and students?
**A**: AI supercharges a small group of motivated students but demotivates most others, widening the equity gap between engaged and disengaged learners.

**Q2**: What evidence shows AI use can hurt learning?
**A**: In a study of ~27,000 Chinese teens, AI users who outsourced homework scored 20% lower on closed-book tests, and Khanmigo was used only about a third of study days, with students trying to extract answers.

**Q3**: How can schools respond?
**A**: Return to in-class practice with blue books and oral exams, or set acceptable AI levels per assignment, and connect schoolwork to students' sense of purpose.
