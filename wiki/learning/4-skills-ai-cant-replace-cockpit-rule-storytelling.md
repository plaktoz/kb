## Summary

As AI becomes table stakes, the real differentiator is knowing when and how to use it — not just that you can. Jeff Su outlines four durable skills: a decision framework for AI delegation, workflow design that compounds over time, human storytelling as a moat, and deliberate "manual override" to prevent cognitive atrophy. The argument is backed by research from Wharton, Harvard, BCG, MIT, and McGill.

## Core Concepts

- **[[The Cockpit Rule]]**: A mental model with three modes — autopilot (delegate fully), collaboration (iterate together), and manual (do it yourself). Mode selection depends on [[Ethan Mollick]]'s agentic cost-benefit framework: human baseline time, AI probability of success, and AI process time.
- **Build the Rails**: Competitive advantage shifts from doing the work to designing the process. Like laying bullet-train tracks, upfront workflow design lets [[AI Workflow Design]] run at scale. Harvard/BCG study of 758 consultants showed "centaurs" (clear task handoffs) and "cyborgs" (fully integrated AI) outperformed unstructured AI users by 19 percentage points.
- **[[Storytelling as Moat]]**: Information is now a commodity; meaning is not. Frameworks like the [[ABT Framework]] (And–But–Therefore) and [[SCQA Framework]] (Situation–Complication–Question–Answer) introduce conflict and resolution — the core of persuasive narrative.
- **Manual Override**: Intentionally avoiding AI for certain tasks to prevent [[Cognitive Atrophy]]. Research from McGill (GPS and navigation), Microsoft/CMU (over-reliance on AI), and radiologists shows that passive AI consumption degrades independent reasoning.

## Key Takeaways

- **Cockpit Rule**: Match autopilot/collaboration/manual to task complexity and AI capability.
- **Cost-Benefit**: Delegate when human time > AI time and you can easily verify output.
- **Build Rails**: Process design beats prompt skill; workflows compound as AI improves.
- **ABT/SCQA**: Conflict-then-resolution structure makes information emotionally actionable.
- **Think First, Prompt Second**: Form your own analysis before querying AI.
- **Interrogate Output**: Ask "how would I verify this?" and steelman the counterargument.
- **Harvard/BCG**: Structured AI users outperformed unstructured by 19 percentage points.
- **World Bank**: 6 weeks of AI tutoring = 2 years of traditional schooling — with guidance.
- **MIT**: Students using ChatGPT without guidance scored 17% worse on exams.
- **GPS analogy**: Over-reliance on GPS caused measurable brain changes in navigation ability.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When AI output becomes the default deliverable, workers optimize for producing content rather than thinking — the exact failure mode "Manual Override" is designed to counter.
- **[[Use It or Lose It]]**: Cognitive skills atrophy without deliberate practice, just like muscles; delegating all reasoning to AI is functionally identical to letting a capability decay through disuse.
- **[[Comparative Advantage]]**: The cockpit rule is an applied version — allocate tasks to whoever (human or AI) has the lower opportunity cost, and reserve high-stakes judgment calls for the party with irreplaceable context.
