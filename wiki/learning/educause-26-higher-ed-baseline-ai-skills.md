## Summary
Students get mixed messages: many courses restrict AI while employers want AI-ready graduates. EDUCAUSE Senior Researcher Nicole Muscanell argues higher education should teach a few baseline AI skills (using AI-enabled interfaces and evaluating outputs) rather than overcommitting to restructuring around AI. Because disruption will keep coming, institutions should instead build the structural capacity to manage change proactively.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Nicole Muscanell]]** — EDUCAUSE senior researcher who argues no one yet knows what the future of work will look like
- **[[EDUCAUSE]] 2026 Students and Technology Report** — finds 53% of students face restrictive AI course policies
- **[[World Economic Forum]] Future of Jobs Report 2025** — lists AI and big data among the fastest-growing skills employers expect to need through 2030
- **[[Baseline AI Skills]]** — base-level knowledge: navigating a typical AI user interface and reading its outputs, without understanding [[Large Language Models]] science
- **Role-Dependent AI Depth** — a data scientist needs advanced AI capability; a healthcare professional mainly needs tool use and [[Output Evaluation]]
- **[[Assessment Validity]]** — AI revives old questions about whether multiple-choice tests measure learning and workforce readiness
- **[[Institutional Change Management]]** — staffing shortages, growing workloads, and limited faculty time block proactive planning
- Related: [[AI Literacy]], [[AI Fluency]], [[Career Readiness]]

## Key Takeaways
- **Mixed Messages**: 53% of students report restrictive AI policies; employers want AI-ready graduates.
- **Employer Demand**: WEF ranks AI and big data among fastest-growing skills through 2030.
- **Baseline, Not Expertise**: Most fields need interface use and output interpretation, not LLM science.
- **Role Variation**: Required AI depth differs sharply between data scientists and clinicians.
- **Usability Goal**: Tools should become user-friendly so professionals needn't become AI experts.
- **Don't Overcommit**: Leaders should avoid restructuring education around one specific technology.
- **Faculty Time Gap**: Few instructors have integrated AI thoughtfully; they lack time to learn tools.
- **Change Capacity**: Institutions must create space for proactive preparation as disruptions keep coming.

## 🧠 Core Principles

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does Nicole Muscanell argue higher education should do about AI skills?
**A**: Teach a few baseline AI skills, mainly using AI-enabled interfaces and evaluating outputs, rather than overcommitting to restructuring education around AI.

**Q2**: What evidence shows students are receiving mixed messages about AI?
**A**: EDUCAUSE's 2026 report found 53% of students had restrictive AI course policies, while the WEF lists AI and big data among the fastest-growing skills employers need through 2030.

**Q3**: Why should institutions focus on structural change management rather than AI specifically?
**A**: Disruption will keep coming and AI is just one instance, so building capacity for proactive preparation despite staffing and workload limits pays off across future changes.
