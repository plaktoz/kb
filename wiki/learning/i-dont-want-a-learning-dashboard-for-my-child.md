## Summary

Rachel Thomas argues that AI ed-tech doubles down on the same flaw as high-stakes testing: treating measurable proxies as learning itself. Applying Goodhart's Law, she shows that metric-optimizing systems — whether standardized tests or AI dashboards — narrow curricula, destroy intrinsic motivation, and crowd out the teacher-student relationships that actually drive learning. The solution requires deliberately combining technology with human connection, not substituting one for the other.

## Core Concepts

- [[Goodhart's Law]] — "when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure" — central thesis
- [[Metric-Driven Learning]] — curricula optimized for testable proxies rather than genuine comprehension or retention
- [[Reading for Pleasure Decline]] — 9-year-olds reading daily for fun: 53% (2012) → 39% (2022)
- [[No Child Left Behind]] — U.S. policy originating from Texas high-stakes testing; later debunked
- [[AI Ed-Tech]] — AI tools optimizing metrics too effectively, compounding the quantification problem
- [[Teacher-Student Relationship]] — qualitative, unquantifiable bond that research identifies as the most powerful learning variable
- [[Rachel Thomas]] — fast.ai co-founder; data/ML professional; homeschools her daughter

## Key Takeaways

- **Goodhart's Law in education**: Tests became the target, not the measure — curricula narrowed accordingly.
- **AI amplifies the flaw**: AI is "too effective at optimizing metrics" — makes the quantification trap worse.
- **Novel reading cut**: Short passages replaced whole books in dominant curricula — measurability over depth.
- **Texas blueprint**: No Child Left Behind spread a flawed model nationally; effects persist.
- **Human connection is irreplaceable**: Teacher relationships can't be quantified — and that's exactly why they work.
- **Solution**: Technology + creativity + genuine human connection — not any one alone.
