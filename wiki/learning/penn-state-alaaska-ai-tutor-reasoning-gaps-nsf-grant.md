## Summary

Penn State's ALAASKA AI tutor gives targeted hints, questions, and knowledge checks rather than solved answers. An early analysis of its first classroom use found that 21 of 119 student-AI interactions showed reasoning in the chat that was absent from the submitted answer. A two-year, $199,009 NSF grant now funds a controlled comparison of two sections of the same large computing course.

## Core Concepts

- **[[ALAASKA]]**: Adaptive Learning for All through AI-Powered Student Knowledge Assessment; estimates current understanding from a student's response and returns a tailored prompt.
- **[[Suman Saha]]**: Penn State associate teaching professor leading the project.
- **[[AI Tutoring]]**: conversational support designed as a learning scaffold rather than an answer generator.
- **[[NSF Research Initiation in Engineering Formation]]**: grant program funding the evaluation (award 2605386).
- **[[Leonhard Center for the Enhancement of Engineering Education]]**: Penn State unit hosting co-PIs Stephanie Cutler and Ibukun Osunbunmi.
- **[[Process-Based Assessment]]**: examining chat interactions rather than only the final submitted answer.

## Key Takeaways

- **Early Finding**: 21 of 119 interactions showed reasoning missing from the submitted answer.
- **Caveat**: Early analysis, not peer reviewed; sample selection and student count unreported.
- **Tool Design**: Returns hints, questions, or checks instead of solved answers.
- **Risk Named**: Generative AI can make it easier for students to bypass reasoning.
- **Funded Design**: One section uses ALAASKA; the other completes equivalent LMS activities.
- **Open Question**: Tests whether conversational support, not AI use generally, improves learning.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Support that preserves the learner's own reasoning builds skill, while answer-giving bypasses it** — Saha argues AI works best as a scaffold that keeps students doing the reasoning, and warns that it can otherwise let them skip it. · *model:* [[Scaffolding (Learning Theory)]]
→ explains: Tool Design, Risk Named

**T2. Isolating an effect requires a comparison against what would have happened without it** — The funded design compares ALAASKA with equivalent non-AI activities to test the conversational support itself, not AI use in general. · *model:* [[Counterfactual Thinking]]
→ explains: Funded Design, Open Question

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is ALAASKA and what early signal did it produce?
**A**: ALAASKA is a Penn State AI tutor that returns targeted hints and checks rather than solved answers. In 21 of 119 recorded interactions, the chat showed reasoning absent from the student's submitted answer.

**Q2**: How will the NSF-funded phase test whether it works?
**A**: It compares two sections of the same large computing course, one using ALAASKA and one doing equivalent activities through the LMS. Data include surveys, performance records, interaction logs, and coded reflections.

**Q3**: What does the finding imply for how instructors assess work?
**A**: Saha says instructors may learn more by examining the problem-solving process in a chat than by grading only the final answer. The early result is not peer reviewed, so it is a prompt for testing rather than proof.
