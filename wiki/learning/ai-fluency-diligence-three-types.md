# AI Fluency: Diligence Competency

**Source:** Anthropic AI Fluency Framework & Foundations Course, Lesson 10
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QbLf2zb3oPc

## Overview

Diligence is the fourth core competency in the [[AI Fluency Framework Four Ds]] — the dimension that addresses the ethical and safety aspects of AI collaboration. While Delegation, Description, and Discernment focus on effectiveness and efficiency, Diligence ensures AI use is rigorous, transparent, and accountable.

At its core, diligence is about **taking responsibility** for your AI interactions. Analogy: like driving a car responsibly — not just getting from A to B, but following rules and considering how your actions affect others on the road.

## Three Types of Diligence

### 1. Creation Diligence
Being **critical and intentional** about which AI systems you choose to work with and how you work with them.

Key questions to ask before using an AI system:
- How is this system trained and built? What data was used?
- Who owns the data I'm inputting? Who may access it once shared?
- How am I protecting the privacy and security of myself and others?
- Does this interaction align with my personal, professional, and organizational values?

> Example: Before sharing sensitive company information with an AI, check whether the service has appropriate data-protection policies and whether your organization permits such sharing.

### 2. Transparency Diligence
Being **open and accurate** about AI's role in your work with everyone who needs to know.

- Different settings (personal, academic, creative, professional) have different disclosure expectations.
- Transparency is not just rule-following — it maintains trust and respect in relationships.
- People have the right to know when AI has played a significant role in content creation or decisions that affect them.

> Example: If AI helped draft a team proposal, letting colleagues know which parts were AI-assisted enables honest collaboration.

### 3. Deployment Diligence
Taking **informed responsibility** for the outputs you use or share after they've been created with AI assistance.

Responsibilities include:
- Verifying facts and checking for biases
- Ensuring accuracy and appropriate usage rights
- Meeting all relevant professional/ethical standards — the same standards that would apply if no AI was involved

> Example: A journalist using AI to draft an article must still verify every fact and source before publishing.

## Navigating Diligence

Diligence is not always straightforward because different contexts and stakeholders have different expectations. Practical guidance:
- Develop personal guidelines aligned with your own ethics and values.
- In professional contexts, familiarize yourself with organizational policies and industry standards.
- Stay informed — legal and regulatory frameworks around AI are still evolving.

## Key Takeaway

The three types work together:
- **Creation** → thoughtful selection of AI systems and working methods
- **Transparency** → honesty about AI's role in your work
- **Deployment** → accountability for what you create with AI

We all want AI that is fair, safe, and beneficial to society. Our own behaviors play a key role in making this happen.
