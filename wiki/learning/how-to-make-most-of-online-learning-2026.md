## Summary

Ten evidence-backed strategies — from goal-setting and dedicated schedules to active recall and AI-assisted study — for closing the gap between enrolling in online courses and actually finishing them, motivated by MIT research showing average completion rates hover around just 15%.

## Core Concepts

- [[Active Recall]] — pausing to recall material without looking, rather than passively watching; passive learners retain only about 5% of lecture content versus up to 75% with active engagement (National Training Laboratories).
- [[Feynman Technique]] — explaining a concept in simple language to identify and fill gaps in understanding, developed by physicist Richard Feynman.
- [[Spaced, Scheduled Study]] — consistent, scheduled study sessions outperform marathon or irregular sessions; a Journal of Educational Psychology study found scheduled learners retain up to 50% more information.
- [[AI-Assisted Personalized Learning]] — using AI tutors, summarization tools and spaced-repetition flashcard generators as a "thinking partner" rather than a shortcut that bypasses comprehension.

## Key Takeaways

- **The completion problem is a strategy problem**: MIT research puts average online course completion around 15%, not because of poor content but because of missing structure.
- **Goal-setting matters**: learners with specific, written goals are 42% more likely to complete online courses (Coursera Learning Report).
- **Distraction costs 23 minutes**: UC Irvine research found it takes an average of 23 minutes to regain full focus after a distraction, making a dedicated, distraction-free study space a high-leverage change.
- **Community drives completion**: learners who feel a sense of community are 83% more likely to complete online courses (Stanford).
- **AI can compound results**: AI-driven personalized learning can improve retention by up to 50% and cut time to reach learning objectives by up to 40% (McKinsey).
