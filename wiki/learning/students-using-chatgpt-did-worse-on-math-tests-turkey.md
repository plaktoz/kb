## Summary

A [[University of Pennsylvania]] experiment with nearly 1,000 high schoolers in Turkey found that students who used [[ChatGPT]] during math practice solved far more practice problems correctly but then scored worse on related tests than peers who practiced alone. Plain ChatGPT users scored 17% worse on tests, while students who worked on their own performed the same on practice and tests. Researchers told [[The Hechinger Report]] that students were using the chatbot as a "crutch" that can "substantially inhibit learning."

## Core Concepts

- **Three-arm experiment**: Students practiced math problems either with standard [[ChatGPT]], with an [[AI Tutor]] version of ChatGPT, or on their own, then took tests without the tools.
- **Practice vs. test performance gap**: AI assistance raised practice scores sharply, but those gains did not carry over to independent test performance.
- **[[Cognitive Offloading]]**: Researchers describe students leaning on the chatbot as a "crutch" instead of doing the learning themselves.
- **[[AI in Education]]**: The findings caution against equating AI-assisted output with real learning gains.
- **Related evidence**: See [[Bastani et al. PNAS Study]] and [[Desirable Difficulty]] for fuller treatments of the same experiment and mechanism.

## Key Takeaways

- **Study scale**: Nearly 1,000 Turkish high schoolers, run by University of Pennsylvania researchers.
- **ChatGPT practice boost**: Plain ChatGPT users solved 48% more practice problems correctly.
- **AI tutor practice boost**: AI tutor users solved 127% more practice problems correctly.
- **Test penalty**: ChatGPT users scored 17% worse on tests than non-users.
- **Control consistency**: Students working alone performed the same on practice and tests.
- **Crutch effect**: Researchers say chatbot reliance can "substantially inhibit learning."

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Assisted performance is not learning** — When a tool does the cognitive work during practice, practice scores rise but the skill doesn't develop, so performance drops once the tool is gone. · *model:* [[Cognitive Offloading]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What was the central finding of the Turkish high school ChatGPT experiment?
**A**: Students who used ChatGPT during math practice did better on practice problems but worse on the related tests than peers who practiced without it.

**Q2**: How large were the practice gains and the test penalty?
**A**: ChatGPT users solved 48% more practice problems correctly and AI tutor users 127% more, yet ChatGPT users scored 17% worse on tests.

**Q3**: What does this imply for judging whether an AI study tool helps students?
**A**: Practice-session performance with the tool is a misleading signal; learning should be measured on independent tests, because researchers found the chatbot can act as a crutch that inhibits learning.
