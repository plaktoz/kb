## Summary

[[World Education]], a [[JSI]] initiative, published the [[Connected Futures Playbook]] with support from [[AT&T]], a guide for nonprofits, libraries, and public agencies to weave digital skill-building into existing community services rather than run it as a standalone program. It lays out a five-stage framework and draws on strategies from organizations hosting AT&T Connected Learning Centers.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Digital Opportunity]]**: Meaningful access to essential digital services depends on connectivity, affordability, devices, and digital skills together.
- **[[Connected Futures Playbook]]**: A free, non-prescriptive toolkit with client-profile frameworks, privacy and metrics mapping, and sustainability strategies.
- **[[Digital Friction]]**: Points where technology creates a barrier for a client, treated as teachable "pivots".
- **[[AT&T Connected Learning Centers]]**: Community sites offering no-cost connectivity, hardware, coaching, and skill-building; the playbook's source of proven strategies.
- **[[Community-Based Organizations]]**: Trusted frontline groups (case managers, youth mentors, adult educators) that already help clients navigate technology.
- **[[Logic Models]]**: "Impact Roadmaps" used to turn daily tasks into measurable outcomes and funding.
- **[[Digital Navigation]]**: Culturally responsive, individualized help, backed by strict data privacy protocols.

## Key Takeaways

- **Embedded Approach**: Digital literacy treated as a thread within daily community support, not a separate program.
- **Five Stages**: Review and Discover; Ideate and Strategize; Plan for Success; Activate; Iterate and Grow.
- **Trusted Messengers**: Local relationships and trust are what no algorithm can replicate.
- **Digital Friction**: Daily technology hurdles become teachable moments tied to existing services.
- **Field Examples**: Boys & Girls Clubs in North Carolina, El Centro in Mississippi, Jewish Family Service of Dallas.
- **Scale**: AT&T's initiative spans 100 Connected Learning Centers.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Skills transfer through existing trust and real need** — People adopt new capabilities most readily when taught inside relationships they already trust, at the moment a real task demands them. Standalone programs lose that context. *(implied)*

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central argument of the Connected Futures Playbook?
**A**: Digital literacy should be treated as a thread woven into daily community services, not an isolated, standalone program. This lets organizations integrate digital opportunity into the work they already do.

**Q2**: What are the five stages of the playbook, and what does "digital friction" mean?
**A**: The stages are Review and Discover, Ideate and Strategize, Plan for Success, Activate Your Strategies, and Iterate, Improve, and Grow. Digital friction is a point where technology creates a barrier for the client, which staff turn into a teachable "pivot".

**Q3**: How have local organizations applied these principles?
**A**: A Boys & Girls Clubs career coach offered an online driver's education course in the computer lab, El Centro folded digital skills into financial literacy and home ownership programming, and Jewish Family Service of Dallas embedded tech instruction in English classes, food-bank distributions, and resume workshops.
