## Summary

UNESCO profiles teachers at Kafrreman Public School in South Lebanon who kept education going through the disruptions and displacement of the 2025-26 school year. Even while displaced themselves, teachers stayed connected with students online and on WhatsApp, prioritising psychosocial wellbeing alongside lessons. The article argues that in crises, education is about preserving connection, routine, dignity and belonging, not only maintaining learning.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Education in Emergencies]]**: Schooling continued amid uncertainty, displacement and bombing across [[Lebanon]] in 2025-26.
- **[[Teacher Resilience]]**: Teachers taught from collective shelters, temporary locations and online while themselves needing support.
- **[[Psychosocial Support]]**: Teachers focused on student wellbeing, not just academics, working closely with parents.
- **[[Remote Learning]] via low-tech channels**: Teachers used online tools and [[WhatsApp]] to check in and maintain continuity.
- **[[Crisis-Sensitive Education]]**: [[UNESCO]] commits to strengthening teacher capacity in digital learning, crisis-sensitive and psychosocial approaches.
- **People**: [[Nada Khamis]] and [[Fadia Ramadan]] (teachers), [[Fatima Rizk]] (principal), all at Kafrreman Public School.
- **Related**: [[unesco-digital-skills-literacy-educators]]

## Key Takeaways

- **Core message**: Every child belongs in school, learning alongside teachers and peers.
- **Teachers as anchors**: Teachers restore students' sense of safety, routine and hope.
- **Dual burden**: Many teachers were displaced yet kept supporting students and colleagues.
- **Calming rituals**: Teachers sang the national anthem in class to soothe fear during bombing.
- **Low-tech continuity**: Online and WhatsApp contact kept school communities connected.
- **Not normal yet**: Teachers are back in classrooms, though life has not returned to normal.
- **School mission**: Raise intellectually curious, innovative, lifelong learners despite the crisis.
- **UNESCO role**: Support teachers in digital learning and psychosocial, crisis-sensitive approaches.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs]]**: Teachers prioritised students' safety and psychosocial wellbeing first because learning cannot proceed until those foundational needs are met.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central argument about education during crises?
**A**: In crises, education is not only about maintaining learning but about preserving connection, routine, dignity and a sense of belonging for students.

**Q2**: How did Kafrreman teachers keep learning going while displaced?
**A**: They taught from collective shelters and temporary locations and stayed in contact with students online and via WhatsApp, checking in on their wellbeing and working with parents.

**Q3**: What does this imply for supporting education systems in crisis zones?
**A**: Support should go beyond curriculum to building teacher capacity in digital learning, crisis-sensitive education and psychosocial care for both learners and educators.
