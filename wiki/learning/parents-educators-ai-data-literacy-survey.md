## Summary

A survey of over 1,300 parents, educators, and school officials by Data Science 4 Everyone and Mathematica finds overwhelming agreement (88%) that all K-12 students should be data-literate before graduation, with 76% saying AI has made data education more important — while respondents prioritize problem-solving and critical thinking over technical skills like coding or prompt-writing.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Data Literacy]]**: The survey defines this as the ability to evaluate information, reason with data, and engage responsibly with AI — treated as a durable skill distinct from technical AI proficiency.
- **[[AI-Readiness Skill Prioritization]]**: Respondents ranked problem-solving, critical thinking, and core academic subjects (math, science, English) above computer programming or prompt-writing as the most important "AI-ready" skills.
- **[[Cross-Subject Integration]]**: 84% of respondents believe data literacy should be woven across multiple subjects rather than confined to math or computer science classes.
- **[[Opportunity Gap]]**: The clearest finding is a gap between how important parents/educators consider data literacy and how much opportunity they believe students currently have to develop it.

## Key Takeaways

- **88%** believe all students should be data literate before graduating high school.
- **84%** want data literacy integrated across multiple subjects, not siloed in math/CS.
- **76%** believe AI has made data education more important than ever.
- **Skill priorities**: Problem-solving, critical thinking, and core academics ranked above programming and prompt engineering.
- **Survey scale**: 1,333 respondents (parents, teachers, school leaders, state/local education officials), collected February-March 2026.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: A large majority of parents and educators believe data literacy is now essential for K-12 students, and that AI has increased — not decreased — the importance of this skill.

**Q2**: What specific percentages define the survey's key findings?
**A**: 88% support universal data literacy before graduation, 84% want it integrated across subjects, and 76% say AI has made data education more important.

**Q3**: What does the "opportunity gap" finding imply for schools?
**A**: Schools need continued investment in curriculum, instructional resources, and professional learning, since the importance placed on data literacy currently outpaces the opportunities students have to develop it.
