## Summary
AI makes young people lazier when it does their thinking and sharper when it makes them explain their thinking, according to UCL's Rose Luckin. Heavy AI use lifts homework quality but can lower unaided exam scores. Shrinking entry-level jobs add a second risk: young people may lose the chance to build the expertise needed to check AI.

## Core Concepts
- [[Rose Luckin]]: Professor emerita at UCL. She argues the problem lies in the work students are set, not in the students.
- [[Generative AI]] in education: 97% of UK children aged 8-17 and 94% of undergraduates use it.
- [[Cognitive Offloading]]: Delegating thinking to AI improves output but weakens what students learn.
- AI as a "sparring partner": A purpose-built [[AI Tutor]] that gives one step at a time more than doubled learning in a 2025 Harvard physics study.
- [[Entry-Level Jobs]] and the "bottom rung" of the career ladder: Verifying and prompting AI well takes expertise that entry-level work builds.
- [[Daniel Susskind]]'s "teach both, test both": Teach and examine children with and without AI.
- [[Daniel Reast]] and the Centre for London's *Held Back* report: Employers should replace the training value that AI removes.

## Key Takeaways
- **Adoption**: 97% of UK children aged 8-17 use AI tools; 94% of undergraduates use it for assessed work.
- **Unauthorised Use**: Secondary teachers reporting pupil AI use without permission rose from 15% (2023) to 58% (2026).
- **Performance vs Learning**: In a China study of 26,000+ students, homework improved but unaided exam scores fell up to 24%.
- **Dose Effect**: The more students used AI, the bigger the exam-score fall.
- **Sparring Partner**: A one-step-at-a-time AI tutor more than doubled learning in less time (Harvard, 2025).
- **Illusion Risk**: AI tools may make users feel cleverer than they really are.
- **Disappearing Rung**: London online job adverts fell 64% from May 2022 to February 2026.
- **Youth Unemployment**: Nearly one in four economically active 16-24-year-olds in London were out of work early 2026.
- **Training Decline**: London employer training investment fell over 45% in real terms, 2011-2024.
- **Advice**: Think of your own answer first, then use AI to challenge it.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Learning requires effortful thinking** — Output produced with a tool is not the same as capability retained without it. When a tool does the cognitive work, the performance gain does not transfer to unaided settings. · *model:* [[Desirable Difficulty]]

**T2. Expertise is built through progressive practice** — Checking and directing AI requires expertise, and expertise comes from doing junior-level work. Removing the first rung stops experienced workers from appearing later.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Rose Luckin's central claim about AI and young people's laziness?
**A**: AI makes them lazier when it does the thinking for them and sharper when it makes them explain their thinking. She argues the issue lies in the assignments set, not in the students.

**Q2**: What did the 2026 China study of 26,000+ secondary students find?
**A**: Homework got faster and better with AI, but exam scores, where AI was not used, fell by up to 24%. The more students used AI, the bigger the fall.

**Q3**: What should students and parents do in response?
**A**: Students should think of their own answer first, then use AI to challenge it. Parents should "teach both, test both": teach and examine children with and without AI, with the basics first.
