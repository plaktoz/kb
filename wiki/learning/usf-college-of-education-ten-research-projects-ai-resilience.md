## Summary

The [[University of South Florida]] College of Education put $125,000 of internal funding into 10 faculty-led research projects through three programs: CREATE 2.0 Awards, Early Career Award Mini Grants, and Effectiveness in Undergraduate Research Mini Grants. The projects range from AI decision-support tools for early childhood educators and LLM-powered virtual reality science environments to crisis-resilience frameworks for schools and undergraduate studies on exercise addiction and menopause. Many are pilots meant to attract future external funding while building interdisciplinary collaboration and student research experience.

## Core Concepts

- **CREATE 2.0 Awards** ($10,000 each, 4 teams):
  - **Data-to-Decision (D2D) Insight**: Led by [[Qunshan Zheng]], an [[AI Decision Support]] system that helps early childhood educators turn student data into instructional strategies, intervention plans, and individualized support.
  - **Advancing AI Literacy**: [[Arianna Banack]], [[Ruthmae Sears]], and Stephanie Arthur are piloting [[AI Literacy]] and human-flourishing professional development with [[Hillsborough County Public Schools]], the 7th-largest US district, for secondary science and math teachers.
  - **Project INQUIRY**: An [[Large Language Models|LLM]]-powered [[Virtual Reality in Education|virtual reality]] environment where high schoolers investigate hurricanes using changing, sometimes conflicting data. They work with AI virtual agents to build, test, and revise evidence-based explanations. The team plans field tests with up to 60 students.
  - **Strengthening Instructional Resilience**: Led by [[Alexandra Panos]], the team is co-creating a framework for student well-being and instructional continuity after hurricanes, floods, and extreme heat. It starts with a needs assessment and focus groups, to be completed by December 2026.
- **Early Career Award Mini Grants** ($15,000 each, 3 faculty):
  - **Cultivating AI-Literate Educators**: [[Wenyu Guo]] is studying ethical, equitable, culturally responsive AI integration in K-12 literacy instruction through teacher interviews and workshops, running through Spring 2027.
  - **Youth and Tourism Zones**: [[Molly Hamm-Rodriguez]] is studying youth education and employment in two fast-growing tourism regions of the Dominican Republic, Montellano and Pedernales.
  - **Project MicroScape**: [[Bo Pei]]'s VISTA Lab, mentored by [[Dana Zeidler]], is building an immersive VR module in which middle schoolers trace microplastics through the Tampa Bay ecosystem. The goal is to strengthen causal reasoning and evidence-based environmental decision-making.
- **Undergraduate Research Mini Grants** (3 projects):
  - **Teacher Candidates**: [[Janet Outlaw]] will mentor up to 30 elementary teacher candidates doing practitioner research in elementary literacy.
  - **Exercise Addiction**: [[Marcus Kilpatrick]]'s students will compare psychological responses to aerobic exercise of different duration and intensity in people with problematic exercise habits versus people with healthy ones.
  - **Menopause**: [[Bill Campbell]]'s lab studies body composition, weight-loss resistance, fitness, nutrition, and hormone therapy use in resistance-trained women going through the [[Menopause|menopausal transition]].

## Key Takeaways

- **Total Investment**: $125,000 funding 10 faculty-led projects across three grant programs.
- **Award Sizes**: CREATE 2.0 grants $10,000 each; early-career grants $15,000 each.
- **Pilot-to-Scale Model**: Grants are seed funding meant to attract larger external funding later.
- **Teacher Demand**: Teachers want more AI literacy professional development and confidence using AI.
- **Transferable Reasoning**: Hurricane VR scenarios aim to build skills for complex, uncertain situations.
- **Crisis-Responsive Teaching**: Current recovery models impose untenable timelines and ignore lived experience.
- **Hard-to-See Processes**: VR makes microplastic pathways visible when classrooms can't show them.
- **Menopause Knowledge Gap**: Fitness-oriented women enter menopause with little awareness of the physiological changes involved.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the USF College of Education's internal research investment fund?
**A**: It put $125,000 into 10 faculty-led projects through CREATE 2.0 Awards, Early Career Award Mini Grants, and Undergraduate Research Mini Grants. The projects address real-world problems in AI, resilience, and human performance.

**Q2**: How does Project INQUIRY use AI to teach scientific reasoning?
**A**: It places high schoolers in an LLM-powered VR environment where they investigate hurricanes using changing, sometimes conflicting weather data. They work with AI virtual agents to build, test, and revise evidence-based explanations.

**Q3**: What do these projects suggest about how AI is entering K-12 education?
**A**: AI is being added both as a teaching tool (decision support, VR agents) and as a literacy goal. Teachers say they need more professional development and confidence to use AI ethically and well in instruction.
