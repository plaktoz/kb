## Summary

The inaugural [[Dartmouth AI Survey]] of more than 1,500 Dartmouth students and 1,000 US college students finds that students use AI mostly to learn but believe their peers use it far more to cut corners. This perception makes 70% say take-home work has become unfair and 39% feel pressured to use AI. Dartmouth responds with guidelines, free premium AI tools, and an emphasis on foundational skills.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Dartmouth AI Survey]]**: Multi-year survey of AI's effect on learning and career readiness; next wave in August 2027.
- **[[Bruce Sacerdote]] and [[Peter Chin]]**: Researchers who presented the results at the AI at 70 conference.
- **Perception Gap**: 11% report using AI for problem sets versus 49% who think peers do.
- **[[AI Skills]] and careers**: 74% of Dartmouth students and 55% nationally see AI skills as critical to their careers.
- **[[Academic Integrity]]**: 70% say take-home work has become unfair due to AI.
- **[[Anthropic]] and [[AWS]] partnership**: Gives Dartmouth students free premium AI tools.

## Key Takeaways

- **Perception Gap**: 8% copy and paste with little editing, but think 50% of peers do.
- **Fairness Concerns**: 70% say take-home work is unfair; 39% feel pressured to use AI.
- **Learning Use**: 61% use AI to explain difficult concepts; 52% say back-and-forth AI beats solo work.
- **Writing Risk**: 84% nationally (92% at Dartmouth) say AI can hurt writing skills.
- **Self-Taught**: 79% of college students mostly taught themselves their AI skills.
- **Field Differences**: 70% of science students say AI deepens understanding versus 48% in arts and humanities.
- **No Jobpocalypse**: 84% of Dartmouth students say AI changes jobs without necessarily eliminating them.

Misperceived peer norms drive behavior and fairness judgments** — People who wrongly believe others are breaking a norm feel pressure to conform and see the system as unfair, even when most privately behave otherwise. ## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the Dartmouth AI Survey?
**A**: Students say they use AI mostly to learn, not cut corners, but believe their peers use it much more heavily. This shapes their feelings about fairness and pressure.

**Q2**: What numbers illustrate the gap between self-reported and perceived peer use?
**A**: 11% report using AI to solve problem sets while 49% think peers do, and 8% report copy-pasting with little editing while they think 50% of peers do.

**Q3**: How is Dartmouth responding to the findings?
**A**: It is providing clear expectations and guidelines, free premium AI tools via Anthropic and AWS, hands-on AI programs, and continued emphasis on critical reading, writing and analysis.
