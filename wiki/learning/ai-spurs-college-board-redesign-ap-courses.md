## Summary

The [[College Board]] is redesigning [[AP Computer Science Principles]] and [[AP Seminar]] starting in the 2027-28 school year to address generative AI's effect on how students learn and demonstrate skills. Computer Science Principles will embed AI/ML content and a new AI Create Project, while AP Seminar will bar generative AI access during scored essay writing due to unreliable AI-detection tools.

## Core Concepts

- [[College Board]] — redesigning two AP courses in response to internal research (April 2025–June 2026) on how AP teachers are responding to generative AI.
- [[AP Computer Science Principles]] — will embed generative AI across core units (machine learning, algorithm development, data ethics, societal impacts) and add the AI Create Project, an assignment requiring students to build an AI-powered application.
- [[AP Seminar]] — redesigned to restrict generative AI access during drafting and editing of scored essays, addressing concerns about determining authentic student work.
- [[AI Detection Software]] — cited by teachers as unreliable, producing false positives, prompting the shift toward secure, AI-free writing environments instead of detection-based enforcement.
- [[AI Literacy]] — a tension in the research: teachers see AI literacy as essential while worrying it could undermine critical thinking and independent work.

## Key Takeaways

- **Two different approaches**: CS Principles embraces AI content; AP Seminar restricts AI access during scored writing.
- **Strong teacher support**: 80%+ of CS Principles teachers found proposed AI material useful for students.
- **Detection distrust**: Teachers reported false positives from AI-detection software, pushing College Board toward controlled writing environments instead.
- **Teachers use AI more for prep than grading**: 63% used AI for instructional materials vs. 28% for student feedback and 15% for grading.
- **Uneven teacher readiness**: 46% said they understood generative AI but still needed guidance; ~25% work without a school AI policy.
- **New training planned**: College Board will expand AP Summer Institute programming and professional learning tied to the redesigns.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of the article about the College Board's course redesigns?
**A**: AI is reshaping how students learn and demonstrate mastery, prompting the College Board to take opposite approaches — integrating AI into AP Computer Science Principles while restricting it during AP Seminar's scored essay writing.

**Q2**: Why did AP Seminar choose to restrict AI access rather than rely on AI-detection software?
**A**: Teachers found AI-detection tools produced false positives and students could bypass digital monitoring, so the College Board opted for a secure writing environment without generative AI access instead.

**Q3**: What does the gap between teachers' use of AI for prep (63%) versus grading (15-28%) suggest for how schools should roll out AI policy?
**A**: Training and guidance should prioritize helping teachers use AI confidently for instructional material creation first, since that is where adoption is already highest, while treating AI-assisted grading and feedback as areas needing more caution and support.
