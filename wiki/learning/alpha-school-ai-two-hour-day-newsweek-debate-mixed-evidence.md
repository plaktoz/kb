## Summary

[[Alpha School]], a network of 46 US private schools, claims AI tutoring lets students learn twice as fast in two hours a day, but the evidence is self-reported and the wider debate on AI in classrooms is polarized. The article lays out the promise of personalized tutoring at scale against mixed study results, concerns about concentration and screens, and an edtech market that investors expect to grow sharply.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Alpha School]] and [[TimeBack]]**: Two-hour AI-led academic days with "guides" instead of traditional teachers; TimeBack is being used in Unbound Academy in Arizona and piloted in Texas public schools.
- **[[AI Tutoring]]**: Tracks hundreds of data points per student, such as response latency and guessing, over weeks and months.
- **[[Mastery Learning]]**: Students return to material until they master it before moving on.
- **[[EdTech]] market**: Education is estimated at 4-6% of global GDP; edtech about 8-10% of that, with the AI share forecast at $90 billion or more by 2030.
- **[[Selection Bias]]**: Alpha's fees reach up to $75,000, and its results are self-reported and unverified.
- **Critics**: [[Libby Hills]] says the evidence lags the claims; [[Kristen Mansharamani]] says individualization does not require AI.

## Key Takeaways

- **Alpha's Promise**: Learn twice as fast in two hours daily, freeing time for extracurriculars.
- **Data-Rich Tutoring**: AI follows individual performance over months, unlike haphazard testing in classes of 20-30.
- **Claimed Results**: Alpha says it is top 1 percent of US schools; unverified.
- **Self-Report Caveat**: Figures cover part of a school year and adjust only for autumn starting scores.
- **China Study**: Among 27,000+ students, AI use improved homework but sharply lowered monthly test and exam scores.
- **Reliance Penalty**: Largest declines were among students whose homework suggested leaning on AI.
- **Optimal-Use Hint**: About a fifth of students benefited, suggesting better ways to use AI exist.
- **Concentration Concern**: A Montessori founder sees concentration as the biggest loss in this generation.
- **Dissatisfaction Backdrop**: Gallup K-12 satisfaction hit a 27-year low; OECD reading scores fell sharply.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Assisted performance can rise while independent mastery falls** — When a tool does part of the work, homework output can improve even as unaided test results decline, so learning must be judged without the aid. · *model:* [[Cognitive Offloading]]
→ explains: China Study, Reliance Penalty, Optimal-Use Hint

**T2. Self-reported results from a selected population do not establish an effect** — High scores in a school that charges steep fees and reports its own numbers may reflect who enrolls rather than what the method does. · *model:* [[Selection Bias]]
→ explains: Claimed Results, Self-Report Caveat

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Alpha School's central claim, and how does the article treat it?
**A**: Alpha claims students learn twice as fast in two hours a day with AI tutoring. The article says the results are self-reported and unverified, and that the evidence has not caught up with the claims.

**Q2**: What did the study of Chinese high school students find?
**A**: In a study of over 27,000 students, AI users spent less time on homework and scored better on it, but monthly tests and exams fell sharply, most among students who appeared to rely on AI.

**Q3**: How might a school use AI tutoring while avoiding its risks, according to the article's voices?
**A**: Use AI for personalized lesson plans and assessments rather than chatbots, as Alpha's cofounder says, while freeing teachers for individualization, as Mansharamani argues, since about a fifth of students benefited from AI use.
