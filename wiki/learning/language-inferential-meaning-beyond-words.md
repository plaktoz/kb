## Summary

Human language is not a fixed code but a deeply inferential system where listeners interpret far more than the literal meanings of words. Wilson and Sperber's *Meaning and Relevance* shows that word order, tone, and context radically reshape what is communicated. Two cognitive capacities — linguistic ability and [[Theory of Mind]] — power this capacity to decode implication, emotion, and intent.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Inferential Communication]]**: Unlike animal signals with rigid one-to-one meanings, human language relies on listeners actively inferring intent, attitude, and context beyond literal words.
- **[[Relevance Theory]]** (Wilson & Sperber): A framework explaining how humans derive intended meaning by seeking the most relevant interpretation given context and cognitive effort.
- **[[Theory of Mind]]**: The ability to attribute mental states — beliefs, desires, intentions — to others; essential for interpreting irony, sarcasm, and indirect speech.
- **[[Pragmatics]]**: The study of how context shapes meaning; contrasted with semantics (literal word meaning).
- **Word Order and Implication**: "Peter got angry and Mary left" vs. "Mary left and Peter got angry" — identical words, entirely different implied causal chains.
- **[[Irony]] as Compression**: Irony conveys attitude toward a proposition and toward those who hold it, communicating layers of disappointment, social distance, and cultural commentary with minimal words.

## Key Takeaways

- **Inference over code**: Human language is inferential, not a fixed signal system.
- **Context reshapes meaning**: Word order and tone alter implication as much as word choice.
- **Irony is efficient**: Three words like "That was fun" encode disappointment, distance, and critique.
- **Two core abilities**: Linguistic capacity + [[Theory of Mind]] together power rich communication.
- **Resist pedantry**: Taking language purely literally misses most of what is actually communicated.
- **Non-verbal bridge**: Tone and context allow meaning to transfer even without a shared language.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Signal vs. Noise]]**: In coded animal communication, every mutation to the signal breaks it; human inferential language is robust because the receiver reconstructs meaning from context, not just the signal itself — the channel is lossy but the system is fault-tolerant.
- **[[Compression]]**: Irony is an extreme form of meaning compression — a few words carry attitude, social commentary, and emotional register that would take many sentences to state explicitly, illustrating that high-bandwidth communication is not about more words but richer context.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central claim about how human language differs from animal communication?
**A**: Human language is inferential — we reconstruct meaning from context, tone, and implication rather than decoding fixed signal-to-meaning mappings as animals do.

**Q2**: How does word order demonstrate that meaning exceeds literal content?
**A**: "Peter got angry and Mary left" implies Peter's anger caused Mary's departure, while "Mary left and Peter got angry" implies the reverse — the same words in different order carry distinct causal implications.

**Q3**: What practical lesson does the article draw from understanding language as inferential?
**A**: Resist taking language purely at face value; most of what is communicated lies in inference, context, and implied attitude rather than the literal definitions of the words used.
