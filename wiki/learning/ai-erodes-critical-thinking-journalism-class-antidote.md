## Summary
A Brookings Institution report warns that the risks of generative AI in education outweigh the benefits, because overreliance can stunt skills like critical thinking before students learn them. A middle-school journalism teacher argues that journalism and news literacy classes teach AI-proof thinking: verifying facts, spotting bias, and weighing multiple perspectives. The goal is not to raise journalists but to preserve skills that shouldn't be handed to bots.

## Core Concepts
- [[Brookings Institution]] Center for Universal Education: year-long study with 500 students, parents, and educators.
- [[Cognitive Offloading]]: delegating difficult tasks to a chatbot before building the skill.
- [[Critical Thinking]]: analyzing information, verifying facts, considering viewpoints.
- [[News Literacy]]: evaluating source credibility and recognizing bias.
- [[Filter Bubbles]]: separate screen-based realities that limit exposure to other views.
- [[Journalism Ethics]]: fairness and nuance by seeking out all sides.

## Key Takeaways
- **Brookings Warning**: AI risks in education outweigh benefits; overreliance could stunt skills before they form.
- **Order Matters**: AI can enhance learning if students learn to think first.
- **Newsroom Practice**: Students verify facts, weigh viewpoints, and write their own copy.
- **Source Evaluation**: Sixth graders learn why a TikTok post may be unreliable.
- **Bias Detection**: Look for charged words, missing viewpoints, and differing framings.
- **Real Responsibility**: Trusting students with real journalism builds community engagement.
- **Student Example**: A student researched lockdown-drill effectiveness using case studies and statistics.
- **Aim**: Teach asking the right questions and listening thoughtfully, not producing journalists.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Skills must be built before they are offloaded** — Delegating a difficult cognitive task before mastering it prevents the skill from forming; tools help only once the user can think independently. · *model:* [[Cognitive Offloading]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about AI and students?
**A**: Overreliance on generative AI can stunt critical thinking before students develop it, so students should learn to think for themselves first, and journalism class is a good place to do that.

**Q2**: What evidence or mechanism does the author offer for journalism's value?
**A**: Students evaluate source credibility, recognize bias, verify facts, and seek multiple perspectives while writing their own copy, doing the work themselves rather than offloading it to a chatbot.

**Q3**: What does the author conclude schools should do?
**A**: Invest educational resources so every student learns to think critically, using news literacy and journalism to teach skills such as asking good questions and listening thoughtfully.
