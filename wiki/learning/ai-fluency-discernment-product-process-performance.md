## Summary

Lesson 8 of Anthropic's AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations course. Discernment is the quality-control competency in AI collaboration — the ability to critically evaluate what AI produces, how it produces it, and how it behaves during interaction. It operates as the flip side of the description competency: description communicates what you want; discernment determines whether you got it.

## Core Concepts

- [[AI Fluency]] — working with AI effectively, efficiently, ethically, and safely
- [[Discernment Competency]] — evaluating AI outputs, processes, and behaviors
- [[Description Competency]] — the paired competency focused on clearly communicating intent
- [[Hallucination]] — AI factual errors or reasoning mistakes that discernment must catch
- [[Human-AI Collaboration]] — the iterative loop of instruction and evaluation

## Three Dimensions of Discernment

### 1. Product Discernment
Evaluating the quality of what the AI actually produces. Key questions:
- Is this factually accurate?
- Is it appropriate for my audience and purpose?
- Is it coherent and well-structured?
- Does it meet my requirements?
- Does it add value or solve the intended problem?

### 2. Process Discernment
Evaluating how the AI reached its output — especially important for complex tasks where the correct answer isn't immediately obvious. Watch for:
- Logical errors or lapses in attention
- Taking inappropriate steps
- Fixating on a single interpretation and being unable to consider alternatives
- Circular reasoning
- Reintroducing previously rejected ideas (e.g., elements from a discarded outline option creeping back in)

Ensures you and the AI are "thinking in sync" throughout the work.

### 3. Performance Discernment
Evaluating how well the AI interacts with you as it does the work — distinct from the work itself:
- Is the AI communicating in a way that supports productivity?
- Does it respond well to feedback and direction?
- Is the interaction appropriately detailed (not asking unnecessary questions; not being too terse when depth is needed)?
- Is the interaction efficient or unnecessarily complex?

## Discernment → Feedback Loop

Discernment does not end at evaluation. Identifying a problem requires actionable feedback:
1. Specify what the problem is.
2. Explain why it is a problem.
3. Provide concrete suggestions for improvement.
4. Revise instructions or examples accordingly.

When discernment flags a problem, **better description is usually the solution**. However, sometimes the right response is to rethink the delegation decision entirely — using the wrong tool or the wrong approach requires a different fix than a prompting tweak.

## Relationship to Description

Discernment and description form a **continuous loop**:
- Description → communicate needs to AI
- Discernment → evaluate how well needs were met
- Gap → refine description → repeat

This loop keeps AI collaboration guided by human judgment, which the course identifies as a critical element of genuine AI fluency.
