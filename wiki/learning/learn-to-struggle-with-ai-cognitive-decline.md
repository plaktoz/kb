## Summary
Singapore's education sector is confronting a second wave of the AI debate — not academic-integrity bans, but concern that bypassing "productive struggle" via AI shortcuts erodes critical thinking. A 2025 MIT Media Lab EEG study found AI-assisted essay writers showed measurably lower brain engagement and recall than independent writers, and educators are now deliberately designing workflows that force students back into that discomfort.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Cognitive Offloading]]** — the brain delegating planning and reasoning to AI tools without processing it, empirically shown via EEG to reduce brain engagement
- **[[Productive Struggle]]** — the deliberate discomfort of wrestling with a problem before consulting AI, which SMU's Venky Shankararaman calls essential: "Learning happens in that friction"
- **[[MIT Media Lab EEG Study]]** — 2025 study of 54 individuals aged 18-39 found AI-assisted essay writers had lower brain engagement and worse recall than independent writers
- **[[Digital Divide]] Redefinition** — shifting from device access to whether students have been taught to use AI tools critically

## Key Takeaways
- **17% lower exam scores**: a study of 1,000 Turkish high schoolers found AI-tool users scored 17% lower on supervised exams once tools were removed, despite better homework.
- **SMU's approach**: foundational courses require independent work (e.g., writing from a blank page) before AI is introduced within a discipline.
- **NTU's An Bo warns**: "we may be raising a generation that never learns to struggle productively."
- **Novice educators default to giving answers** rather than nudging struggling students, missing prime learning opportunities, per a 2024 NAACL study.
- **Workflow design**: SUTD's Sumbul Khan has students log every decision to use, reject, or bypass AI output to build accountability.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models
- **[[Desirable Difficulty]]**: The article's "productive struggle" concept maps directly onto this learning-science principle — friction that feels inefficient in the moment (wrestling with a problem before consulting AI) produces stronger, more durable understanding than a frictionless path to the answer.

## 🃏 Review Questions
**Q1**: What did the 2025 MIT Media Lab EEG study find about AI-assisted essay writing?
**A**: Individuals who wrote essays with AI assistance showed measurably lower brain engagement and performed worse at recalling what they had written than those writing independently.
**Q2**: What happened to the Turkish high school students who relied heavily on AI for homework?
**A**: They submitted better homework but scored 17% lower on supervised exams once the AI tools were removed, having bypassed the effortful learning phase.
**Q3**: How is SMU restructuring its curriculum to address over-reliance on AI?
**A**: Every program now designates foundational courses where students must demonstrate they can work independently of AI before applying it within their discipline.
