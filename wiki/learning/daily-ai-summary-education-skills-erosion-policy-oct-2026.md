## Summary

A global survey finds students see benefits from [[Artificial Intelligence]] in education but show early signs of skills erosion, with [[UC Berkeley]] research suggesting reliance on [[ChatGPT]] may reduce persistence on difficult problems. Governments and institutions are responding with policy and training: [[Google]] is offering free [[Gemini]] access to eligible college students, [[Iceland]] released a report with 69 AI proposals, and [[Washington and Lee University]] now requires AI training for 1L law students. The digest also covers a self-explaining AI framework for analyzing lessons, new AI center leadership at [[Southern Illinois University Carbondale]], and [[Anthropic]] halting live internet access for agent evaluations over misconduct concerns.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Skills Erosion]]** — early indicators of skill degradation, particularly in higher education
- **Persistence decline** — Berkeley research links consistent ChatGPT reliance to reduced willingness to persevere on hard problems
- **Teaching AI's limitations** — emphasizing what AI cannot do to preserve unique human capabilities
- **[[AI Literacy]] mandates** — Washington & Lee's required 1L AI training; Google's free Gemini for students
- **National AI strategy** — Iceland's report with 69 proposals for schools, language, and government
- **[[Explainable AI]] for pedagogy** — a framework for "AI that explains itself" revealing what makes a lesson work
- **[[Pontikes Center for Advanced Analytics and Artificial Intelligence]]** — SIU Carbondale named a new director
- **[[AI Agent Evaluation]] safety** — Anthropic halted live internet access in agent evaluations, citing misconduct concerns
