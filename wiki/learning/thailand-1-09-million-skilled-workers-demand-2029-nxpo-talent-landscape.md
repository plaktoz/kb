## Summary
Thailand's 10 target industries need 1,087,548 highly skilled workers between 2025 and 2029, with aviation and logistics the largest share, according to the NXPO Talent Landscape survey. The government is responding with flexible university curricula, employer-linked training platforms, tax incentives and a lifelong learning policy.

## Core Concepts
- **[[NXPO]]** and **[[MHESI]]**: The agencies that produced the talent survey and drive the policy response.
- **[[Thailand Talent Landscape 2025–2029]]**: The survey projecting skilled-worker demand across 10 target industries.
- **[[Higher Education Sandbox]]**: Lets universities change programme duration, credits and curriculum standards; [[Chulalongkorn University]]'s CEDT programme is the example.
- **[[STEMPlus Platform]]** and **[[STEM One-Stop Service]]**: Connect educators, employers and learners with demand data and course certification.
- **[[Lifelong Learning]]**: Policy spanning early childhood to older age, with credit accumulation and accessible learning finance.
- **[[Thailand Plus Package]]** and **[[BOI STEM++]]**: Tax incentives for hiring STEM staff and funding training.
- **[[Thailand: Nation of Masters]]**: Strategy with Siam Silica, Siam Medica and Siam Terra deep-tech initiatives.
- **[[Surachai Sathitkunarat]]**: NXPO president who presented the projections.

## Key Takeaways
- **Total demand**: 1,087,548 highly skilled workers needed across 10 industries, 2025–2029.
- **Concentration**: Aviation and logistics (440,573) plus smart electronics and robotics (226,423) make 61.3%.
- **Employer-linked curricula**: CEDT works with 100+ private partners and gives yearly workplace experience.
- **Platform results**: 603,243 completed 2,609 courses; 16,490 new STEM jobs across 260 organisations.
- **Short-course outcomes**: GenNX graduates report 86% employment, with incomes up two to sixfold in three months.
- **Tax incentives**: 1.5x deduction on STEM salaries and 2.5x on certified training, 2023–2025.
- **Structural pressures**: Ageing population, climate change, geopolitical volatility, and international competition for talent.
- **Deep-tech targets**: Eight IC design businesses and one photonics or power-semiconductor fab by 2030.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the Thailand Talent Landscape 2025–2029 survey?
**A**: Thailand's 10 target industries will need 1,087,548 highly skilled workers between 2025 and 2029, led by aviation and logistics at 40.5% of demand.

**Q2**: What evidence does NXPO give that its training platforms work?
**A**: 603,243 people completed 2,609 courses from 219 providers, linked to 16,490 new STEM jobs. GenNX short-course graduates report 86% employment and two to sixfold income gains within three months.

**Q3**: How is Thailand changing education to close the skills gap?
**A**: It lets universities adjust curricula through the Higher Education Sandbox, involves employers in curriculum design and assessment, and offers tax deductions for STEM hiring and training.
