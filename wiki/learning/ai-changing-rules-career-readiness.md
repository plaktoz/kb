## Summary
AI is reshaping what it means to be workforce-ready rather than eliminating entry-level jobs outright. MSU Denver faculty argue distinctly human skills — empathy, ethical reasoning, and knowing which tasks should stay human — are becoming as valuable as technical AI fluency, and the university is launching a no-code "AI Fundamentals" elective this fall for students of any major.

## Core Concepts
- **[[AI Fluency]]** — combines technical proficiency with human judgment about which responsibilities should remain human vs. be offloaded to AI
- **[[Entry-Level Hiring]] Shift** — Strada Education Foundation finds senior talent leaders nearly 3x more likely to say AI increases entry-level hiring than reduces it
- **[[Problem Definition]] Skill** — CS professor Steve Geinitz argues the critical skill is defining a problem and its moving parts before directing AI to solve it, not coding ability itself
- **[[AI Fundamentals]] Course** — MSU Denver's new no-code elective covering machine learning, generative AI, ethics, bias, privacy, and decision-making

## Key Takeaways
- **88% of organizations** use AI in at least one business function, per McKinsey's "State of Organizations 2026."
- **40%+ of employers** report AI has shifted new employees away from routine admin work toward more analytical responsibilities.
- **Student framing shift**: Industrial Design student William Block says AI use changed his self-concept from "worker" to "manager of the AI."
- **No coding required** for the new AI Fundamentals course — it emphasizes problem definition and communicating requirements to developers.
- **Human-skill framing**: humanities-taught empathy, perspective-taking, and ethical reasoning are framed as durable workplace differentiators.

## 🃏 Review Questions
**Q1**: What does the Strada Education Foundation research suggest about AI's effect on entry-level hiring?
**A**: Senior talent leaders are nearly three times more likely to say AI will increase entry-level hiring than reduce it, with AI shifting new hires toward more analytical work.
**Q2**: According to CS professor Steve Geinitz, what skill matters more than coding ability for AI-proficient graduates?
**A**: The ability to define a problem, identify its moving parts, and determine whether AI can help — then communicate that clearly enough for developers to act on it.
**Q3**: What is MSU Denver's new AI Fundamentals course, and who can take it?
**A**: A no-code elective open to students of any major, covering machine learning, generative AI, ethics, bias, privacy, and decision-making through hands-on case studies, launching fall 2026.
