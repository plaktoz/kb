## Summary
An MSU study led by Elizabeth Cloude found that learning success depends less on which cognitive/emotional/metacognitive states learners exhibit and more on the pattern of how they transition between them, a balance the researchers call "[[Optimal Adaptivity]]." Using physiological sensing and verbalizations during a mobile learning game, the study found persistence alone explained only 9% of learning-gain variance, while self-regulation of state transitions explained 31%. The relationship is curvilinear: both too little and too much change in strategy or state is suboptimal — learners need stability with enough flexibility to adapt to feedback and setbacks.

## Core Concepts
- **[[CAMeta States]]** — cognitive, affective, metacognitive, and motivational states tracked in the moment during learning; what mattered was the transition pattern between them, not which states occurred.
- **[[Optimal Adaptivity]]** — a balance of stability (holding a level trajectory toward goals) and flexibility (adjusting strategy on feedback or setbacks); the strongest predictor of successful learning outcomes in the study.
- **[[Self-Regulated Learning]]** — how learners manage their own thoughts, emotions, and strategies over time; explained 31% of variance in learning gains in this study.
- **Rigid vs. chaotic failure modes** — learners who never changed strategy (rigid/stable) and learners who changed constantly (chaotic/flexible) both showed poorer outcomes than the adaptive middle ground.
- Physiological markers (facial expressions of excitement/motivation) were weak predictors compared to behavioral/state-transition patterns.

## Key Takeaways
- More learning strategies or more persistence does not linearly predict better learning gains.
- Persistence alone explained only 9% of learning-gain variance; self-regulation of state transitions explained 31%.
- Learning is non-linear, complex, and contextually based — not a simple frequency-of-behavior model.
- The relationship between behavior/state frequency and learning gain is curvilinear, not linear.
- Study used a mobile game ("Antidote COVID-19") with real-time physiological sensing to quantify learning process, not just outcome.
- Follow-up research will extend this to Minecraft-based STEM education environments.
