## Summary
Completing a task with generative AI is not the same as learning from it, so learners need a deliberate strategy for how they use AI. Evidence from a large maths field experiment and metacognition studies suggests that AI built to tutor, and learners who attempt problems first, preserve learning that answer-giving AI erodes. The author proposes "learning literacy" alongside AI literacy.

## Core Concepts
- [[Generative AI]] and [[ChatGPT]] as tools that can either replace or support learning activity.
- [[Metacognition]]: monitoring and regulating one's own thinking, which shapes how people use AI (copying output vs. using feedback actively).
- [[Retrieval Practice]]: recalling answers from memory before looking them up improves long-term retention; e.g. use with [[Duolingo]].
- [[Learning Literacy]]: knowing enough about learning to decide how a tool should be used, complementing [[AI Literacy]].
- [[AI Tutoring]] design: hints, attempt-before-assistance and explain-your-reasoning prompts rather than direct answers.
- [[Self-Regulated Learning]] and reflective journalling.

## Key Takeaways
- **Done vs. Learned**: Producing a correct answer today doesn't build independent ability tomorrow.
- **Same Tool, Different Activity**: Attempting the problem first, then asking why it failed, drives learning.
- **GPT-4 Field Experiment**: About 1,000 maths students; standard GPT-4 users scored 17% worse once access was removed.
- **Tutor Design Helps**: The tutor-style GPT-4 group largely avoided that decline.
- **Metacognition Matters**: A semester study linked metacognitive awareness to more active ChatGPT use for writing.
- **Meta-analysis**: 32 studies, 3,029 participants showed positive effects of AI on self-regulated learning, varying with design.
- **Shared Responsibility**: Schools, developers and learners all share the job of preserving effortful thinking.
- **Key Question**: Which learning activities does AI let me stop doing, and which does it improve?

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about using AI for learning?
**A**: Finishing a task with AI is not the same as learning, so learners need a strategy that keeps them doing the effortful mental work. What matters is which learning activities AI lets someone stop doing and which it helps them do better.

**Q2**: What did the GPT-4 maths field experiment find?
**A**: Among nearly 1,000 secondary students, those using standard GPT-4 performed 17% worse than students with no AI once access was removed. Students using a tutor-designed GPT-4 largely avoided this decline.

**Q3**: How can a learner apply this when using an AI model?
**A**: Attempt the problem first, then ask for a hint, another example or an explanation of why the answer was wrong, and ask the AI to challenge your reasoning. Asking "what am I actually learning by using AI this way?" keeps use purposeful.
