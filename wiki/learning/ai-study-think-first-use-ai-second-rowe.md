## Summary

Learning-science researcher Luke Rowe argues students should master foundational knowledge before using AI to study, because heavy reliance causes cognitive offloading that inflates practice performance but collapses real exam results. AI is best used after unaided retrieval, to organise notes, generate harder practice questions, and give critical feedback. The guiding rule is "think first, use AI second (or, in some cases, not at all)."

## Core Concepts

- [[Cognitive Offloading]]: students let AI do the thinking and quit studying before mastering content.
- [[Retrieval Practice]]: recalling, explaining, solving, or summarising material yourself before asking AI.
- [[Mock Exam Conditions]]: closed laptop, phone away, timer set to the real exam duration.
- [[AI Feedback Prompting]]: asking AI to critique, not rewrite, an answer against marking criteria.
- [[ChatGPT]] and other AI chatbots as study tools with mixed research evidence.
- Small-group study as a human alternative to AI feedback.

## Key Takeaways

- **Mixed Evidence**: Studies claiming AI boosts learning are questioned for low-quality methods and poor transparency.
- **Offloading Risk**: Regular reliance leads students to stop studying before truly mastering content.
- **Practice Exams**: Recreate real exam conditions; AI use gives a false sense of confidence.
- **Chinese Study**: AI use raised homework scores but monthly exam scores fell about 20% in six months.
- **Retrieve First**: Recall, explain, or write concepts yourself before asking AI anything.
- **AI After Mastery**: Use AI to organise notes, spot gaps, and generate escalating practice questions.
- **Feedback Prompts**: Ask AI to identify strengths, errors, and top three improvements, not rewrite.
- **Healthy Balance**: Schedule tech-free study blocks and meet friends to give and receive feedback.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Assisted performance is not learning** — AI can temporarily boost performance, but real exams reveal true learning, so assisted practice results overstate mastery. · *model:* [[Fluency Illusion]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Rowe's central claim about using AI to study?
**A**: Students should master foundational knowledge and think first before using AI, since over-reliance causes cognitive offloading that undermines long-term learning.

**Q2**: What evidence shows AI can help homework but hurt learning?
**A**: A Chinese study found AI use improved high school students' homework scores but their monthly exam scores dropped about 20% within six months.

**Q3**: How should you use AI when reviewing a practice essay?
**A**: Ask it not to rewrite your answer but to identify what you did well, what is inaccurate or missing, and the three most impactful changes, checked against the marking criteria.
