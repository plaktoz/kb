## Summary

Universities are shifting from negotiating AI access to designing the conditions around it: UCCS became a leading example by requiring students to complete an "AI Literacy Foundations" course before receiving ChatGPT Edu access. The newsletter argues that adoption metrics (accounts, logins, prompts) are easy to measure but say little about actual learning, and surveys a growing wave of assessment-reform research (Cornell-led study, a new AI-resilient assessment framework, and a New South Wales policy review) asking how to make student reasoning visible when AI assistance is widely available.

## Core Concepts

- **AI-literacy gating** — UCCS ties ChatGPT Edu account provisioning to completion of a mandatory Canvas literacy course, treating literacy as part of infrastructure rollout rather than a separate initiative.
- **Adoption vs. learning measurement gap** — dashboards can show seats provisioned and prompts submitted, but not whether students can explain reasoning, check evidence, or perform without the tool.
- **[[AI-Resilient Assessment]]** — a Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence framework organizing redesign around process documentation, oral defense, authentic tasks, and transparent AI-use policies.
- **[[Assessment Validity]] under AI use** — a Science-published study of 95,000+ students across 20 U.S. public research universities found 37% used generative AI at least monthly and an estimated 9% used it to cheat, weakening the inference that submitted work reflects individual learning.
- **Institutional AI provisioning wave** — UCCS joins University of Georgia ($800K pilot for ChatGPT Edu/Gemini Pro licenses) and University of Arkansas (expanded governed access to ChatGPT Edu, Gemini, NotebookLM, Microsoft tools) in a broader pattern of approve-negotiate-train-give-local-authority.

## Key Takeaways

- **UCCS opened ChatGPT Edu to all students** on August 14, gated behind a required AI-literacy course; faculty/staff access is suspended if not completed by September 25.
- **74% of faculty believe** students use AI to write essays/papers; only 21% feel confident guiding classroom AI use (College Board survey of 3,000+ faculty).
- **37% of students use generative AI monthly**; ~9% estimated to use it for cheating, per a 95,000-student, 20-university study.
- **New South Wales is reviewing** whether to pause unsupervised take-home assessments, a K-12 policy question with direct higher-ed assessment implications.
- **The next phase for institutions should be evaluation** of literacy training's effect on judgment and responsible use, not just completion tracking.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this newsletter issue?
**A**: Universities have gotten good at provisioning AI access but not at measuring what students actually learn from it, and assessment design — not access policy — is becoming the central open question in AI and higher education.

**Q2**: What specific mechanism does UCCS use to link AI access to learning?
**A**: UCCS requires all students to complete an "AI Literacy Foundations" course in Canvas before their ChatGPT Edu account is provisioned, treating literacy training as a gate rather than an optional add-on.

**Q3**: How should faculty apply this issue's argument when designing assignments this fall?
**A**: Write down what each assignment is meant to reveal about student understanding, decide which parts can include AI assistance, and build in direct evidence of unaided reasoning (e.g., oral defense, process documentation) where it matters most.
