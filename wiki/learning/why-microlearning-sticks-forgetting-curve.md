## Summary

Microlearning — single-concept lessons of 5-15 minutes with built-in retrieval checks — outperforms traditional long-form study largely because it respects working-memory limits and interrupts the [[Forgetting Curve]] through frequent, spaced retrieval rather than session length; completion rates run around 80% versus roughly 20% for traditional courses. Consistency of short sessions matters more than duration, and the same underlying mechanisms ([[Spacing Effect]], [[Retrieval Practice]]) covered in [[science-of-spaced-repetition]] explain why.

## Core Concepts

- [[Forgetting Curve]] — Ebbinghaus's finding that ~50% of new information is lost within an hour and ~70% within 24 hours without review
- [[Cognitive Load]] — working memory holds roughly 4-7 items at once, which short lessons respect and long-form content risks overwhelming
- [[Microlearning]] — single-objective, 5-15 minute lessons with a built-in retrieval check, contrasted with traditional multi-concept courses
- [[Testing Effect]] — brief post-lesson quizzes cement retention more effectively than rereading

## Key Takeaways

- **Microlearning completion rates ~80%** versus ~20% for traditional long-form courses
- **Frequency beats duration**: how often you return to material predicts retention better than how long each session runs
- **Four failure modes** identified: passive consumption with no retrieval, no learning sequence/structure, skipped retrieval practice, and inconsistent review cadence
- **25% higher retention** reported by companies using microlearning versus other training methods
- **Habit formation framing**: roughly 66 days to build a new habit, but the first microlearning session only takes 5 minutes — lowering the barrier to starting

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Compound Growth (Memory as Infrastructure)]]**: As already noted in [[science-of-spaced-repetition]], this article reinforces the same compounding logic from the consumer-app angle — small, frequent retrieval sessions accumulate into durable knowledge the same way small, frequent deposits compound financially, while cramming is the equivalent of a single large, poorly-timed contribution.
