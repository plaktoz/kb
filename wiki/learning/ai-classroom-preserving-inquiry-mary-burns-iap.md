## Summary
In an interview, Mary Burns argues AI can either erode or support inquiry-based science education depending on whether it is designed to keep students doing the thinking. Teachers need sustained, content-focused AI literacy training, and international frameworks help only if they are relevant in the classroom. The IAP and ISSE conclude that AI must support students' thinking, not replace it.

## Key Takeaways
- **Skipped Effort**: In a 63-student study, many stopped inquiring without asking follow-ups.
- **Knowledge First**: Inquiry and AI augmentation both require literacy and solid domain knowledge.
- **Design Decides**: Reflection-prompting chatbots can help; the key is keeping students thinking.
- **Teacher Literacy**: "No AI-literate students without AI-literate teachers."
- **What Works**: Content focus, active practice, peer communities, sustained coaching; not one-off vendor workshops.
- **Framework Limits**: Global frameworks often lack consensus and may reflect funders' interests.
- **Assessment Shift**: Ask "What intellectual work did the student perform?" not "Did AI do this?"
- **Risks Ahead**: Teacher attrition and a possible AI education bubble.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Skill grows only through the learner's own effortful thinking** — When a tool does the effortful part of a task, the underlying capability is not built and may erode. · *model:* [[Cognitive Offloading]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Burns's central claim about AI and inquiry-based education?
**A**: AI can either undermine or support inquiry, and the deciding factor is whether it is designed to keep students doing the thinking. AI must support students' thinking, not replace it.

**Q2**: What evidence shows how AI can short-circuit inquiry?
**A**: In a study of 63 middle schoolers using ChatGPT for science investigations, students often stopped inquiring without a follow-up question. They also could not distinguish strong from weak prompts and missed vague AI explanations.

**Q3**: How should teachers and assessment adapt?
**A**: Teachers need sustained, content-focused AI literacy training, including knowing when to set AI aside. Assessment should shift from policing AI use to evaluating the intellectual work students actually perform.
