## Summary
[[Alpha School]], which charges up to $75,000 a year for a two-hour AI-driven academic day, saw only 42% of students meet its goal of learning twice as fast in both math and reading, per internal data reported by The New York Times. Students who started behind fared worse, reports describe stress and faulty AI lesson plans, and the model is now expanding into public schools amid research showing an AI learning penalty.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Alpha School]]**: Network of roughly 50 campuses and about 1,500 students with no traditional teachers, only "guides".
- **[[TimeBack]]**: Alpha's AI tutor app, central to the "2x learning in two hours" promise.
- **[[MacKenzie Price]]**: Cofounder who says the app personalizes and accelerates learning.
- **[[AI in Education]]**: Public districts such as Houston ISD are piloting the software.
- **[[Generative AI Learning Penalty]]**: Homework scores rise with AI while closed-book exam scores fall.

## Key Takeaways
- **Goal Missed**: Only 42% of students met the 2x learning goal in math and reading.
- **Math Gap**: About half of K-8 students missed the math target; one in five missed reading.
- **Behind Students**: Only 37% of students at or below the 50th percentile hit growth goals.
- **Hidden Costs**: Parents and guides report late-night work, stress, and anxiety.
- **Content Quality**: Former employees say faulty AI lesson plans sometimes did "more harm than good".
- **Public Expansion**: Over 2,600 public school students in Texas and South Carolina now use it.
- **Research Signal**: In a 26,811-student study, homework rose 18% while exam scores fell 20%.
- **PISA Finding**: Daily AI users scored lower in science than nonusers.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does Alpha's own data show about its central promise?
**A**: Only 42% of students met the network's goal of learning twice as fast in both math and reading, with about half of K-8 students missing the math target.

**Q2**: What evidence suggests AI use can hurt learning?
**A**: A study of 26,811 secondary students in China found homework scores rose 18% after adopting generative AI while closed-book exam scores fell 20%, and PISA found daily AI users scored lower in science than nonusers.

**Q3**: Why is Alpha's move into public schools a tougher test?
**A**: Public schools cannot remove struggling students and lack the prize incentives Alpha uses at private campuses; Houston's superintendent noted students spent more time on the apps than planned.
