## Summary

A grounded-theory qualitative study of eight IT students at Portuguese universities finds that [[face-to-face learning]] more effectively develops non-technical skills (communication, teamwork, collaboration) through synchronous interaction, while [[online learning]] better strengthens technical skills, self-regulation, and adaptability through independence and flexibility. The author suggests a hybrid model could combine both sets of benefits.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Face-to-face learning]]**: Driven by direct interaction with classmates/professors and access to physical resources; develops communication, teamwork, collaboration, idea exchange, and problem-solving, at the cost of time-management challenges (commuting, coordinating group schedules).
- **[[Online learning]]**: Driven by access to online platforms/content and flexibility; develops technical skills (especially programming, via resources like Stack Overflow and GitHub), self-regulation, discipline, and time management, at the cost of isolation, distraction, and reduced interpersonal skill development.
- **[[Grounded theory]]**: The qualitative research method used — open coding, axial coding, and selective coding — to build two separate theoretical models (one per learning environment) from interview data.

## Key Takeaways

- **Skill specialization by modality**: F2F strengthens interpersonal/non-technical skills; online strengthens technical and self-management skills.
- **Small but rich sample**: Only 8 students interviewed (81 pages of transcript data), but researchers report thematic saturation — no new themes emerging by the end.
- **Consistent downside patterns**: F2F's main friction is logistics (commuting, scheduling); online's main friction is isolation and reduced engagement (monotony, camera-off classes).
- **Hybrid model as synthesis**: The study's practical recommendation is a hybrid approach combining online flexibility/technical-skill benefits with F2F's interpersonal/collaborative benefits.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: IT students develop different skill sets depending on learning environment — face-to-face learning builds non-technical/interpersonal skills more effectively, while online learning builds technical skills, autonomy, and self-regulation more effectively.

**Q2**: What research method did the study use, and what specific finding resulted from it?
**A**: A grounded-theory approach analyzing semi-structured interviews with 8 IT students, producing two separate theoretical models — one for F2F and one for online learning — that were then compared.

**Q3**: How might an educator apply this finding when designing an IT curriculum?
**A**: They could deliberately structure a hybrid curriculum that uses F2F sessions for group projects, brainstorming, and communication practice, while relying on online modules for independent technical skill-building like programming exercises.
