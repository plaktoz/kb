## Summary

A meta-analysis by [[Sterre Ruitenburg]] and colleagues, restricted to experimental studies in real primary and secondary classrooms, finds that [[Retrieval Practice]], spaced practice, and [[Interleaving]] all have positive average effects. The effects are smaller than in some earlier reviews and depend on the type of knowledge, the test used, the timing, and feedback. The author concludes that these strategies can work, but they are not a switch one simply turns on.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Retrieval Practice]]**: Effects were clearest for factual knowledge and for retention, not for comprehension or transfer.
- **[[Spaced Repetition]]** (spaced practice): Distributed practice beat massed practice, but different forms of spacing did not differ significantly.
- **[[Interleaving]]**: Positive effect on average, with results varying considerably across studies.

## Key Takeaways

- **Study Scope**: 53 studies, 7,303 students, and 233 effect sizes in real classrooms.
- **Retrieval Effect**: Average g = 0.37 for retrieval practice, clearest for factual knowledge (g = 0.53).
- **Retrieval Limits**: No significant effect on conceptual or procedural knowledge, comprehension, or transfer.
- **Generative Complement**: Understanding and application may need generative activities alongside retrieval practice.
- **Spacing Effect**: Average g = 0.20 for spaced practice; distributed beat massed practice at g = 0.39.
- **Spacing Timing**: Session gap, relative to the final test, matters; more spacing isn't always better.
- **Interleaving Effect**: Average g = 0.40, drawn from only ten studies and 1,902 students.
- **Feedback Interaction**: Interleaving helped with no or delayed feedback, but not with immediate feedback.
- **Speculative Explanation**: Immediate feedback may reduce the need to discriminate problem types.
- **Not a Switch**: Positive average effects hold in authentic classrooms, but outcomes are conditional.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Retrieval consolidates memory; understanding needs more** — Retrieval strengthens memory of learned content, while comprehension and transfer depend on additional generative cognitive processes. · *model:* [[Testing Effect]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Do retrieval practice, spaced practice, and interleaving work in real classrooms?
**A**: Yes, all three show positive average effects in a meta-analysis restricted to real primary and secondary school classrooms, though the effects are modest.

**Q2**: Where was the retrieval practice effect clearest, and where was it absent?
**A**: It was clearest for factual knowledge (g = 0.53) and for retention, with no significant effect on conceptual or procedural knowledge, comprehension, or transfer.

**Q3**: What does this imply for teachers choosing a strategy?
**A**: Strategies should be matched to the outcome, test, and timing rather than applied as a blanket rule, and retrieval may need to be paired with generative activities for understanding and application.
