## Summary
Career-technical education is growing as policymakers, industry, and school leaders tie classroom skills to jobs, with K-12 enrollment up 10% to 8.6 million students. EdWeek Research Center surveys show K-12 and college CTE educators want funding, more teachers, and stronger employer partnerships. The article previews a Dec. 1 Education Week webinar on the fastest-growing CTE and work-based learning areas.

## Core Concepts
- [[Career and Technical Education]] (CTE): programs linking K-12 and college classroom skills to jobs.
- [[Work-Based Learning]]: school-industry arrangements, which college educators say need stronger [[Employer Partnerships]].
- [[EdWeek Research Center]]: source of survey data on educator priorities and expected program growth.
- [[Cybersecurity Education]]: one of the technology-based programs educators expect to add over five years.
- [[Workforce Development]]: the policy driver behind CTE growth.
- Webinar guests: Candice Mott (D.C. Office of the State Superintendent of Education), [[Shaun Dougherty]] (Boston College), and Dan Hinderliter (Advance CTE).

## Key Takeaways
- **Enrollment Growth**: K-12 CTE enrollment rose 10% to 8.6 million students (U.S. Department of Education).
- **Tech Expansion**: Many K-12 and college educators expect to add technology programs, including cybersecurity.
- **K-12 Priorities**: Educators stress adequate funding, more CTE teachers, and stronger employer partnerships.
- **College Priorities**: Over two-thirds cite stronger industry partnerships to improve work-based learning.
- **Funding Need**: College educators also want funding for new courses or pathways.
