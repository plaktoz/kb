## Summary

Eedi and Google DeepMind published the full results of a 10-week randomised controlled trial of AI Tutoring with 1,179 students in 10 UK schools. In the lesson, AI tutoring matched human tutoring and was safe, but the broad Star Maths test showed no clear gain, and exploratory results suggest tutoring helped girls more than boys. Eedi argues that trials need to run a full academic year, and its next year-long trial moves tutors from approving every message to overseeing conversations.

## Core Concepts

- [[Eedi]]: Maths edtech company that led the trial design, school recruitment and implementation, and built the constrained AI Tutor.
- [[Google DeepMind]]: Fine-tunes Gemini for teaching and co-analysed and co-published the findings.
- [[Randomised Controlled Trial]]: Students were randomly assigned for a term to static content, a human tutor, or one of two AI Tutor versions.
- [[AI Tutoring]]: A constrained tutor that diagnoses the misconception behind each wrong answer, not an off-the-shelf chatbot.
- [[Human-in-the-Loop]]: A tutor reviewed every AI-drafted message; the next trial shifts to human-on-the-loop oversight.
- [[Star Maths]]: Standardised test taken before and after the trial to measure broad learning.
- [[Personalised Context]]: One AI arm also received a summary of each student's past performance and misconceptions.
- [[Learning Engineering Virtual Institute]]: Renaissance Philanthropy programme backing the new year-long trial of 15,000+ students.

## Key Takeaways

- **In-Lesson Parity**: Students answered similar questions correctly 56-58% after AI or human tutoring, versus 43% after static content.
- **Broad Test**: Star Maths scores for AI-tutored students were similar to static-content students.
- **Safety**: Tutors approved 94.7% of AI drafts unchanged; no harmful content reached students.
- **Maths Errors**: 7 AI messages with false maths reached students (0.01%), versus 7 human errors (0.02%).
- **Gender Gap**: Tutoring tended to help girls and hurt boys, tentatively and needing validation.
- **Extra Context**: Adding learning history did not clearly improve outcomes over current-question context.
- **Tutor Experience**: Supervising AI drafts felt less rewarding than writing messages directly.
- **Duration Limit**: Earlier trial gains only emerged after a year or more.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Averages can mask opposing subgroup effects** — A neutral overall result can combine gains for one group with losses for another, so subgroup analysis is needed before drawing conclusions. · *model:* [[Flaw of Averages]]
→ explains: Gender Gap, Broad Test

**T2. Short-horizon measures lag long-horizon learning** — Broad learning outcomes take longer to appear than in-lesson results, so a short trial cannot settle whether gains accumulate.
→ explains: Duration Limit, Broad Test, In-Lesson Parity

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the Eedi and Google DeepMind trial conclude about AI tutoring?
**A**: In the lesson, AI tutoring matched human tutoring and did so safely, but ten weeks was not long enough to show broader learning gains on the Star Maths test.

**Q2**: What evidence showed the AI tutor's safety and accuracy?
**A**: Tutors approved 94.7% of AI drafts unchanged, no harmful content reached students, and false maths statements reached students at 0.01% of the corpus versus 0.02% for human tutors.

**Q3**: How does Eedi plan to apply these findings?
**A**: Its year-long trial of over 15,000 students shifts tutors from approving every message to overseeing conversations and stepping in where needed. Eedi also urges edtech firms to publish their own safety benchmarks and error rates.
