## Summary

TCU cognitive psychologist Uma Tauber is applying retrieval practice, spaced repetition, and immediate feedback to build educational tools that help dementia caregivers retain and use critical care information under real-world pressure. The team's newest project extends this into an AI tool that personalizes evidence-based guidance to each caregiver's specific situation on demand. Beyond information retention, the research aims to reduce caregiver stress and guilt by reframing symptoms like apathy as disease-driven rather than intentional.

## Core Concepts

- [[Uma Tauber]] — cognitive psychologist at [[Texas Christian University]] leading the research
- [[Retrieval Practice]] — caregivers repeatedly practice recalling dementia-care concepts rather than reading materials once
- [[Spaced Repetition]] — reviewing information over time to build durable, long-term memory rather than short-term recall
- [[Immediate Feedback]] — a learning-science component paired with retrieval practice and spacing in the intervention design
- [[Caregiver-Centered Design]] — research topics and delivery methods shaped directly by input from caregiver support groups
- [[Texas Christian University]] — interdisciplinary team spanning psychology, medicine, nursing, pharmacy, and computer science

## Key Takeaways

- **Learning science over one-time info**: Repeated recall practice outperforms reading materials once for real-world retention.
- **Goal is durable memory**: Techniques target long-term retention usable in the moment, not short-term recall.
- **Reframing symptoms reduces guilt**: Understanding apathy as disease-driven, not intentional, eases caregiver frustration.
- **Caregiver-shaped research**: Support-group conversations directly determine which topics and formats are built.
- **Next step is personalized AI**: A new AI tool will deliver evidence-based, situation-specific guidance on demand.
- **Multidisciplinary team**: Psychology, medicine, nursing, pharmacy, and computer science collaborate on the tools.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Desirable Difficulty]]**: Retrieval practice and spacing are inherently effortful compared to passive reading, but that friction is precisely what produces memory durable enough for caregivers to use under real-time stress.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core approach Tauber's team uses to help dementia caregivers?
**A**: They apply cognitive-science learning strategies — retrieval practice, spaced repetition, and immediate feedback — to help caregivers durably retain and recall critical care information exactly when they need it, rather than simply providing static resources.

**Q2**: How does the research change caregiver stress, according to Tauber, even without changing the disease symptoms?
**A**: By teaching caregivers that behaviors like apathy are a symptom of the disease rather than intentional, the research changes caregivers' knowledge and perception of them, reducing guilt and increasing confidence.

**Q3**: What is the team's newest direction for delivering this support?
**A**: They are developing an AI tool that personalizes evidence-based caregiving guidance to each caregiver's specific situation, delivering trusted answers on demand rather than requiring caregivers to search through static resources.
