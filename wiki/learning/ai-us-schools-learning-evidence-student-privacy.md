## Summary
US school AI adoption now faces a two-part test: evidence that a specific use actually builds skills students retain once the chatbot is gone, and enforceable contract terms governing the data a specific product collects. Fall 2026 developments — NYC's one-year moratorium on student-facing generative AI for 2-K through 8th grade, the AFT/UFT/Microsoft National AI Safety & Privacy Standard, and a University of Minnesota expert-consensus report — address distinct questions of learning, permission, and data control. Neither a fluent AI answer nor a privacy promise supplies both kinds of evidence.

## Core Concepts
- **[[NYC Generative AI School Moratorium]]**: September 2 pause on student-facing generative AI for 2-K through 8th grade in 2026–27 (~600,000 students); [[AI Companions|companion chatbots]] banned across all grades; teacher use, assistive tech, multilingual learners, and career-readiness programs exempted; high schoolers get [[AI Literacy]] modules and supervised pilots.
- **[[National AI Safety & Privacy Standard]]**: September 9 [[AFT]]/[[UFT]]/[[Microsoft]] standard districts can write into Microsoft customer agreements, making protections contractually enforceable — not a federal law for every vendor.
- **Scope limits of the standard**: 31-page agreement covers authenticated GenAI products "primarily designed and marketed" for education; excludes general productivity, search, and cloud tools; bans covered data for model training (narrow safety exception); treats student-linked prompts and outputs as protected; bars features fostering emotional dependency; requires [[Human-in-the-Loop|meaningful human review]].
- **[[University of Minnesota]] expert consensus**: 30 learning scientists rated 65 learning processes, reaching consensus on 42; AI shows most promise at the [[Retrieval Practice|practice]] stage, while delegating thinking during initial acquisition or later application can undermine [[Skill Development]]. Structured expert judgment, not product trials.
- **[[Bastani et al. PNAS Study]]**: 2025 randomized field experiment with ~1,000 Turkish high school math students; unguarded GPT-4 chat interface boosted assisted practice but cut unassisted exam scores 17% vs. control; a guarded tutor with teacher-designed hints largely removed the harm without beating control.
- **[[Assisted Performance vs Independent Learning]]**: The core measurement distinction — a chatbot-produced answer is not evidence the student acquired the skill.
- **[[RAND]] American Youth Panel**: Student AI homework use rose from 48% (May 2025) to 62% (Dec 2025), while 67% believed more AI use would harm [[Critical Thinking]] — self-reported beliefs, not measured decline.
- **Teacher-facing AI**: [[Gallup]]/Walton Family Foundation survey of 2,232 K–12 teachers — six in ten used AI in 2024–25; weekly users estimated 5.9 hours saved per week. Measures a different outcome than student learning.
- **Existing privacy law**: [[COPPA]] lets schools authorize data collection on parents' behalf only for educational use with no other commercial purpose; [[FERPA]] obligations still apply.
- **Federal and union framing**: July 2025 [[US Department of Education]] guidance allows federal grant funds for AI if compliant — permission to fund is not evidence of efficacy; [[NEA]]'s 2024 framework emphasizes human relationships, evidence, data protection, equity, accessibility, and AI literacy.

## Key Takeaways
- **Two justifications needed**: learning evidence for the use, enforceable terms for the product.
- **NYC scale**: Moratorium covers ~600,000 students in 2-K through 8th grade for 2026–27.
- **Companion ban**: NYC prohibits companion chatbots in all grades.
- **Contract, not law**: Microsoft standard is enforceable only via each district's agreement.
- **Coverage gap**: Education licence doesn't automatically cover general productivity, search, or cloud tools.
- **UMN consensus**: 30 experts, 65 processes, 42 consensus points; practice is the sweet spot.
- **PNAS warning**: Unguarded GPT-4 users scored 17% lower on unassisted exams.
- **Guardrails help**: Hint-based tutor neutralized the harm but showed no gain over control.
- **Use vs. doubt**: Homework AI use 48% → 62%; 67% fear critical-thinking harm.
- **Teacher savings**: Weekly AI users estimate 5.9 hours saved per week — self-reported.
- **Evaluation checklist**: independent learning, purpose, scope, teacher workload, data control, accessibility.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When assisted task output becomes the success metric, students optimize for answers rather than skills — exactly why the PNAS study found better practice scores but 17% worse unassisted exams.
- **[[Desirable Difficulties]]**: The UMN finding that delegating thinking during acquisition undermines skill development reflects that effortful processing, not fluent output, drives durable learning.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central argument about AI adoption in US schools?
**A**: Schools must justify AI purchases twice — with evidence that a specific use supports learning students retain without the tool, and with enforceable contract terms governing the specific product's data use. A privacy agreement and a learning study answer different questions.

**Q2**: What did the 2025 PNAS randomized experiment find about GPT-4 use in math practice?
**A**: Both a general chat interface and a guarded hint-based tutor improved assisted practice, but students using the general interface scored 17% lower than control on an unassisted exam. The guarded tutor largely removed that harm without outperforming control.

**Q3**: What should a district verify before relying on the AFT/UFT/Microsoft privacy standard?
**A**: It should confirm which products and features its own licence and agreement cover, since the standard applies only to authenticated education-designed products and excludes general productivity, search, and cloud tools. Existing COPPA and FERPA obligations also still apply.
