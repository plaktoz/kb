## Summary

Most workplace training fails because nothing in the environment requires employees to change after it ends. Saunderson offers six practices, from diagnosing the real performance problem to making the workplace reinforce new behavior, so training shows up in on-the-job performance. Success is measured by changed behavior, not completion.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Training Transfer]]**: applying learning in the flow of work.
- **[[Performance Problem Diagnosis]]**: separating skill gaps from process, tool and accountability barriers.
- **[[Manager Reinforcement]]**: managers as the biggest influence on whether training sticks.
- **[[Behavior Change Evaluation]]**: checkpoints at 30, 60, 90 and 180 days.
- **[[Workplace Environment]]**: job aids, peer discussion, aligned incentives and [[Leadership Modeling]].
- **[[Learning and Development]]** leaders accountable for results.

## Key Takeaways

- **Diagnose First**: Training cannot fix problems caused by unclear expectations, broken tools or friction.
- **Design for Real Work**: Awareness is not application; use real scenarios and job tools.
- **Limit Targets**: Define no more than five observable behaviors to change.
- **Learner Buy-In**: Rigid scripts feel inauthentic; teach principles with flexibility.
- **Manager Role**: Give managers a one-page guide with behaviors, coaching prompts and follow-ups.
- **Late Measurement**: Check 30/60/90/180 days after training, not just surveys.
- **Environment Wins**: Workplaces can reinforce old habits more strongly than new ones.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Treat the cause, not the symptom** — An intervention only works if it targets the actual cause of the problem; training applied to non-skill problems is wasted. · *model:* [[Root Cause Analysis]]
→ explains: Diagnose First

**T2. Behavior follows the environment** — People revert to old behavior unless surroundings require, reward and support the new one. · *model:* [[Environment Design]]
→ explains: Environment Wins, Manager Role, Learner Buy-In, Design for Real Work

**T3. Completion is not impact** — Measuring delivery or satisfaction does not show whether behavior changed, so evaluation must track on-the-job results over time. · *model:* [[Goodhart's Law]]
→ explains: Late Measurement

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the real problem in workplace learning according to the article?
**A**: Training ends, completion is recorded, and nothing changes on the job. Employees keep their old behavior because nothing in the environment requires change.

**Q2**: How does the article say to evaluate whether training worked?
**A**: Plan checkpoints at 30, 60, 90 and 180 days asking whether employees are trying, improving, behaving consistently, and creating visible impact. Early surveys and tests only show liking and understanding.

**Q3**: What should a leader do this quarter?
**A**: Pick a training program where behavior has not changed, define the expected on-the-job behavior, ask managers how they reinforce it, and remove one barrier to performing it.
