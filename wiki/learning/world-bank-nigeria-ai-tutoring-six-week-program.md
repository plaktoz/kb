## Summary

A six-week, teacher-guided after-school program in [[Edo State]], Nigeria, in which public secondary-school students used [[Microsoft Copilot]] (powered by [[GPT-4]]) as an English tutor produced substantial learning gains in a randomized evaluation by [[World Bank]] researchers. The gains were estimated as comparable to roughly 1.5–2 years of typical learning progress in similar settings — a measure of assessed skills, not two years of curriculum completed. Researchers stress the effect came from the whole program (curriculum alignment, structured prompts, teacher support, peer learning and AI), so the chatbot's isolated contribution cannot be determined.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Tutoring]] with Teacher Guidance**: Teachers introduced lesson topics; students then practiced grammar and writing with the AI, receiving explanations and feedback, while teachers supervised.
- **[[Randomized Controlled Trials in Education]]**: The program was evaluated against students receiving their usual education, giving causal evidence for the program as a bundle.
- **Bundled Intervention Effect**: Gains are attributable to the complete design, not separable to the chatbot alone — a key limit the researchers made explicit.
- **Differentiated Pacing**: A responsive tutor offers another explanation, invites follow-ups and allows repeated practice for students who learn at different speeds.
- **Critical AI Use**: Teachers helped students spot incorrect AI responses and encouraged reasoning over shortcuts.
- **"AI as Assistant Teacher"**: Framing from teacher [[Okhide Eugene]] of Imaguero College, Benin City — human teachers supervise, AI assists.
- **[[Education Equity]]**: Private tutoring is available to families who can afford it; AI tutoring could narrow that gap only if access is deliberately extended to less wealthy communities.

## Key Takeaways

- **Program design**: Twice-weekly sessions for six weeks, June–July 2024, English focus.
- **Effect size**: Gains comparable to ~1.5–2 years of typical learning in similar settings.
- **Important caveat**: Measures assessed-skill improvement, not two years of curriculum covered.
- **Attribution limit**: Study cannot isolate the chatbot's contribution from teachers, prompts and peers.
- **Student-reported gains**: Communication, pronunciation, writing, spelling and vocabulary improved.
- **Scaling prerequisites**: Electricity, reliable internet, computers and trained teachers.
- **Open questions**: Do gains endure, and do they transfer to other subjects and larger populations?
- **Policy recommendation**: Expand through carefully evaluated programs that measure learning and support teachers.
