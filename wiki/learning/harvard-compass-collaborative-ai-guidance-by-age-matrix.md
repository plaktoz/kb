## Summary
Harvard Graduate School of Education released the AI Guidance by Age Matrix, drawing on more than 5,000 research pieces across 81 developmental areas to show how AI may help or hinder learning by age and skill. It is part of the Compass Collaborative, launched Nov. 10, which aims to bridge developmental science and AI decisions in schools. Early reactions are mixed, and the resource is framed as a living tool.

## Core Concepts
- [[AI Guidance by Age Matrix]]: a role, domain, and age-range tool giving research summaries and help/hinder analysis.
- [[Compass Collaborative]]: HGSE initiative linking developmental science to educator, policymaker, and developer AI decisions.
- [[Harvard Graduate School of Education]]: publisher; [[Jeff Curley]] and [[Tessa Forshaw]] co-direct the collaborative.
- [[Developmental Science]]: effects of AI vary with a learner's age and the skill being developed.
- [[Retrieval Practice]]: AI answer tools can short-circuit the retrieval that builds memory.
- [[Critical Thinking]]: evaluating AI output can build it, while passive use bypasses it.
- [[Adaptive Learning Platforms]]: can hold difficulty in a range that supports meaningful practice.

## Key Takeaways
- **Evidence Base**: More than 5,000 research pieces across 81 developmental areas.
- **Age Matters**: AI's effects differ by learner age and skill.
- **Unchanged Truths**: Learning needs human relationships with experienced others and practice.
- **Rule of Thumb**: AI moving toward practice or relationships is generally good territory.
- **Age 9-11 Example**: Adaptive tools help; homework answer tools short-circuit memory-building retrieval.
- **Research Caveat**: Stanford found mixed results; an audit called existing meta-analyses flawed.
- **Mixed Reception**: One teacher called it helpful; one assistant principal found it basic.
- **Living Resource**: Updated as research emerges; decisions can't wait.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the AI Guidance by Age Matrix and what does it claim?
**A**: A Harvard GSE resource built on 5,000+ studies across 81 developmental areas showing that AI's effects on learning depend on a learner's age and the skills being developed.

**Q2**: What mechanism makes some AI uses harmful for learning, per the article's example?
**A**: For ages 9 to 11, AI homework and answer tools can short-circuit the retrieval that builds memory, while evaluating AI output can build critical thinking.

**Q3**: How should educators judge whether an AI use is acceptable?
**A**: Ask whether it moves toward practice or human relationships, which is generally good territory, or away from them, which deserves a second thought.
