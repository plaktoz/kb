## Summary

A PBS NewsHour report finds that while university leaders emphasize job readiness, students say learning to socialize and build relationships is the most important part of college. Because pandemic isolation and screen time have eroded social skills, schools like [[UMass Amherst]] are building structured, often screen-free opportunities to connect. University leaders argue these soft skills may become the work skills of the future in the AI era.

## Core Concepts

- **Job-readiness push**: [[Brendan Kelly]] says higher education is no longer about courses and credit hours; [[Kevin Guskiewicz]] advertises [[Michigan State University]]'s employability ranking.
- **Student priorities**: Students cite meeting diverse people and forming connections as essential and lasting.
- **[[Student Loneliness]]**: At UMass Amherst, reported loneliness fell from about 54% in 2023 to about 42%, below the national average.
- **[[Scaffolding (Learning Theory)]]**: Students want "structured and scaffolded" in-person opportunities; [[Betsy Cracco]] calls them "the playdate generation."
- **Screen-free events**: Bonfires, climbing events and a glow light dance party.
- **Every student known**: [[Christopher Blakely]]'s goal is a campus where each student is known by somebody.
- **[[Soft Skills]]**: Provost [[Laura Lee McIntyre]] says managing schedules, commitments and workload prepares students for work and life.

## Key Takeaways

- **Student view**: Students call social connection the single most important part of college.
- **Loneliness drop**: UMass cut reported loneliness from about 54% to 42% since 2023.
- **Social erosion**: Time in virtual worlds erodes social skills before students arrive on campus.
- **Structured connection**: Phone-free, scaffolded events give students low-barrier ways to meet.
- **Reciprocal engagement**: Campus design aims for every student to be known by somebody.
- **AI-era relevance**: Soft skills may be the work skills of the future.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Social skills need structured practice** — Social competence weakens without in-person practice, and learners rebuild it faster when given structured, supported opportunities rather than left to sort it out alone. · *model:* [[Scaffolding (Learning Theory)]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What do students say is the most important part of college, in contrast to administrators' focus?
**A**: Students emphasize learning to socialize, meeting diverse people and forming relationships, while administrators stress job readiness and employability.

**Q2**: What evidence shows UMass's approach is working?
**A**: Reported student loneliness fell from about 54% in 2023 to about 42%, lower than the national average, using structured, often screen-free events.

**Q3**: Why might social skills matter more in the AI era?
**A**: Leaders argue that soft skills like managing commitments and working with people may be the work skills of the future in a shape-changing workforce.
