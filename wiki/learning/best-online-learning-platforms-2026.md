## Summary

A comparative guide to 15 online learning platforms in 2026, organized into three structural types — MOOCs (Coursera, edX), Learning Management Systems (Moodle), and course marketplaces (Udemy, Skillshare) — each suited to different goals: formal accreditation, affordability and breadth, career-profile visibility, or deep technical upskilling.

## Core Concepts

- **[[MOOC]] (Massive Open Online Course)**: University-developed courses (Coursera, edX) offering structured academic rigor, often with accredited certificates or degree pathways.
- **[[Learning Management System]] (LMS)**: Institution-facing platforms (e.g., Moodle) for organizing course materials, tracking progress, and managing assignments in synchronous or asynchronous formats.
- **[[Course Marketplace]]**: Platforms (Udemy, Skillshare) where independent experts create and sell courses directly, trading formal accreditation for affordability and breadth.
- **[[Platform-Goal Fit]]**: The article's central framework — platform choice should follow the learner's actual goal (accreditation vs. affordability vs. career visibility vs. technical depth) rather than defaulting to the most popular option.

## Key Takeaways

- **Coursera/edX**: best for accredited, university-backed credentials; free audit options with paid certificates ($30–$300).
- **Udemy**: 200,000+ courses, frequent deep discounts, lifetime access — best for affordable, self-paced exploration, though quality varies by independent instructor.
- **LinkedIn Learning**: 16,000+ courses integrated directly into LinkedIn profiles — best when career-visible credentialing matters as much as the content itself.
- **Skillshare/Domestika**: project-based, peer-feedback-driven creative learning with no formal accreditation.
- **Pluralsight/Udacity/Codecademy**: deep technical upskilling with interactive labs, skill assessments, and (for Udacity) strong career support — at a higher price point.
- **Cited statistics**: over 85% of online learners value self-paced scheduling; personalized learning can boost retention by up to 30%; more than 220 million learners took online courses in 2024.
