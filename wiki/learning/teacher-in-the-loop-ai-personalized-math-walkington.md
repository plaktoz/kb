## Summary
A study by SMU's Candace Walkington found seven middle school math teachers could use ChatGPT to personalize problems with pop culture references, but only by applying heavy professional discretion to its output. The AI hallucinated, for example suggesting Minecraft for a lesson on circles. The teachers judged the gains in productivity, cognitive offloading and personalization worth the adaptation effort.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Teacher-in-the-Loop]]**: Teachers work with AI to adapt materials but keep control over what enters their lessons.
- **[[Personalized Learning]]**: Tailoring instruction to student interests, here by inserting pop culture references into math problems.
- **[[Candace Walkington]]**: SMU education professor who led the study in *Educational Technology Research and Development*.
- **[[ChatGPT]]** and [[AI Hallucination]]: The chatbot filled teachers' youth-culture knowledge gaps but missed on context.
- **[[Prompting Moves]]**: The discretion teachers apply to adapt AI output, a major part of the analysis.
- **[[Cognitive Offloading]]**: Teachers delegate repetitive tasks to AI.
- **[[KnowledgeWorks]]** and **[[National Center for Education Statistics]]**: Surveys on how educators and school leaders use AI.

## Key Takeaways
- **Hallucination Example**: AI proposed Minecraft, a blocky world with no true circles, for a circles lesson.
- **Study Scale**: Seven middle school teachers with 521 students used ChatGPT to personalize math problems.
- **Specificity Gap**: Students wanted references to a particular game or character, not just "video games."
- **Context Limits**: AI's lack of context can yield inappropriate or inaccurate instructional advice.
- **Vetted Materials**: Keep balance so AI does not pull teachers far from evidence-based curricular sources.
- **Adoption Data**: 40-46% of school leaders report some teachers use AI for tailored materials (NCES).
- **Net Trade**: Teachers judged productivity and personalization gains worth the adaptation effort.
- **Time Trade-off**: AI swaps one kind of teacher time for another; teacher judgment makes it work.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of Walkington's study on AI-personalized lessons?
**A**: AI can help teachers make lessons more relatable, but teachers' professional discretion, insight and relationships with students are paramount. A "teacher-in-the-loop" approach keeps teachers in control of what reaches the lesson.

**Q2**: What evidence showed AI's limits in personalizing math problems?
**A**: ChatGPT suggested Minecraft for a lesson on circles even though its world is blocky, and students wanted references to specific games or characters rather than generic ones. Teachers spent extra time adapting the output.

**Q3**: How should teachers weigh using AI for personalization?
**A**: Treat it as a trade of one kind of teacher time for another, and scale back if the moves needed to get usable output become arduous. In the study, the gains outweighed the effort.
