## Summary
UNESCO's September 2026 launch of the Skills for the Future Platform addresses a deepening global skills crisis — 450 million youth lack adequate skills, only 22% of AI professionals globally are women, and 40% of today's skillsets no longer match job market needs — with a coordinated platform, Global Skills Tracker, and Global Skills Academy (1.2 million learners in 150 countries) that could unlock $6.5 trillion in economic value if the skills divide is closed.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Global Youth Skills Gap]]**: 450 million young people worldwide lack adequate labour market skills; 1 in 5 individuals aged 15–34 disengaged from formal skill development pathways. 40% of today's skillsets no longer match job market needs. A $6.5 trillion economic opportunity (World Bank) if bridged.
- **[[Gender Gap in AI Professions]]**: Only 22% of AI professionals globally are women; in low-income countries 90% of girls and young women remain offline — structural barriers to AI-era participation are compounding the existing gender employment gap.
- **[[Skills for the Future Platform]]**: UNESCO platform allowing private sector and civil society to showcase skills initiatives; leverages CSR/ESG data for transparency; KPMG among inaugural partners. Designed to coordinate action and reduce duplication.
- **[[Global Skills Academy (UNESCO)]]**: 5 years old in 2026; 1.2 million learners trained in digital and green skills; 230 TVET institutions across 150 countries. Free courses in AI, coding, cybersecurity, entrepreneurship, and career development.
- **[[Global Skills Tracker]]**: UNESCO's interactive dashboard at unevoc.unesco.org; identifies trends in skills demand across countries, industries, and occupations. Currently covers 10 countries including Argentina, Kenya, Malaysia, Nigeria, and the Philippines.
- **[[WEF Job Transformation Forecast]]**: 22% of jobs expected to transform by 2030 due to technological disruption (WEF, cited by UNESCO).

## Key Takeaways
- **450M youth**: lack adequate skills globally.
- **22%**: AI professionals who are women.
- **40%**: current skillsets already mismatched to job market needs.
- **22% of jobs**: expected to transform by 2030 (WEF).
- **$6.5T**: economic value unlocked if skills divide closed (World Bank, 7-year horizon).
- **1.2M learners**: Global Skills Academy reach across 230 TVET institutions in 150 countries.
- **Platform model**: coordinates private sector, civil society, and TVET via shared dashboard.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the scale of the global youth skills crisis according to UNESCO's 2026 data?
**A**: 450 million young people lack adequate skills for the labour market; 1 in 5 individuals aged 15–34 are disengaged from formal skill development pathways; 40% of today's skillsets no longer match job market needs.

**Q2**: What is the UNESCO Global Skills Academy and what has it achieved?
**A**: A 5-year-old initiative that has supported 1.2 million learners in digital and green skills training across 230 TVET institutions in 150 countries, offering free courses in AI, coding, cybersecurity, entrepreneurship, and career development.

**Q3**: Why is the gender gap in AI particularly alarming according to UNESCO's data?
**A**: Only 22% of AI professionals globally are women; in low-income countries 90% of girls and young women remain entirely offline — meaning the transition to an AI-era economy is at risk of leaving women behind at both access and professional participation levels.
