## Summary

[[Handshake]] launched AI Skills Studio, a free platform where job seekers build practical AI skills by completing short, project-based missions with real tools from [[OpenAI]], [[Google]], and [[Salesforce]]. The company pitches it as a way to bring learning and hiring together, because job seekers list AI skills far more often than before while employers are getting more applications per job and have few reliable ways to verify those skills. Completed projects earn verified badges that show up in employer sourcing and review workflows as evidence of finished work, not self-reported claims.

## Core Concepts

- **AI Skills Studio**: Handshake's free learning experience built on project-based missions. The missions are developed with leading employers and AI labs.
- **[[Project-Based Learning]]**: Candidates learn by doing, building something tangible with the AI tools now shaping work.
- **AI Project Builder Badge**: Awarded for completing and publishing an approved AI project through AI Skills Studio.
- **AI Fellow Badge**: Awarded for at least five hours of paid, hands-on AI work through [[Handshake AI]].
- **[[Hiring Signals]]**: Badges appear while employers source candidates, review applicants, and view profiles, alongside an explanation of how each badge was earned.
- **Hiring Intelligence**: When employers add AI-related skills to their criteria, Handshake uses the badge signals to surface stronger matches.
- **[[Skills-Based Hiring]]**: The longer-term vision is for employers to add capability-specific projects early in hiring, so candidates can show what they can do before an interview.
- **[[Workforce Transformation]]**: Handshake describes two sides, helping employers hire for new capabilities and helping people build them. It positions itself at the intersection of AI labs, educators, employers, and job seekers.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI Skills Surge**: AI skills appear 9x more on Class of 2026 than 2022 profiles.
- **Applicant Glut**: Applications per job on Handshake are up 26% year over year.
- **Verification Gap**: Employers have more candidates but fewer clear ways to identify skill.
- **Learn by Doing**: Short missions use real tools from OpenAI, Google, and Salesforce.
- **Two Badges**: AI Project Builder (published project) and AI Fellow (5+ paid AI hours).
- **Evidence Over Claims**: Badges show completed work, not another self-reported skill.
- **Matching Boost**: Badges feed Hiring Intelligence when employers specify AI skills.
- **Not Yet Launched**: Employer-defined projects early in the hiring process are future roadmap.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Signaling Theory]]**: A self-reported AI skill costs nothing to claim, so it tells employers little. A badge tied to a verified, published project is harder to fake, which is why Handshake bets it can separate candidates in a crowded applicant pool.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What problem is Handshake's AI Skills Studio designed to solve?
**A**: Employers are getting more applicants, but they have few clear ways to tell who actually has AI skills. AI Skills Studio lets job seekers build and prove those skills through hands-on projects, which gives employers stronger evidence than self-reported claims.

**Q2**: What data does Handshake cite for the need, and what badges does the platform award?
**A**: AI skills appear 9x more often on Class of 2026 profiles than on Class of 2022 profiles, and applications per job are up 26% year over year. Candidates can earn an AI Project Builder badge (approved, published project) and an AI Fellow badge (5+ hours of paid AI work through Handshake AI).

**Q3**: How could employers use this model beyond today's launch?
**A**: An employer could identify a capability it needs and add a relevant project earlier in hiring, so candidates show what they can do before an interview. The same insight could help employers decide which capabilities to hire for and which to develop in their existing workforce.
