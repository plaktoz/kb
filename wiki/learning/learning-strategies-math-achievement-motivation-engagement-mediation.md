## Summary
A study of 444 middle and high school students found that learning strategies raise mathematics achievement only through motivation and engagement. Once these were added to the model, the direct effect of learning strategies became non-significant. Teaching strategies alone is therefore insufficient without supporting motivation and engagement.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Learning Strategies]]**: cognitive, metacognitive, and motivational strategies (per Boekaerts) learners use to manage their learning.
- **[[Self-Regulated Learning]]**: planning, monitoring, regulating, and evaluating one's own learning.
- **[[Academic Motivation]]**: the strongest mediator between learning strategies and achievement.
- **[[Student Engagement]]**: a second mediator, also linked to achievement.
- **[[Mediation Analysis]]**: bootstrapped serial mediation (PROCESS Model 6) testing indirect effects.
- **[[Mathematics Achievement]]** and **[[Metacognition]]**.

## Key Takeaways
- **Sample**: 444 middle and high school students were studied.
- **Full Mediation**: Learning strategies' direct effect vanished once motivation and engagement were included.
- **Motivation Path**: The indirect effect via motivation was B = 0.23, the largest.
- **Engagement Path**: The indirect effect via engagement was smaller, B = 0.05, but significant.
- **Serial Path**: Strategies to motivation to engagement to achievement was also significant.
- **Motivation Dominates**: Its indirect effect exceeded the engagement and serial effects.
- **Instruction Implication**: Strategy teaching alone has limited value if motivation is low.
- **Caveat**: The design is correlational, so causal claims are unsupported.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Knowing a method does not produce results without willingness to apply it** — Capability influences outcomes through the effort and engagement that put it to use. When those intermediaries are accounted for, the capability's direct effect disappears.
→ explains: Full Mediation, Motivation Path, Instruction Implication

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the study's central finding?
**A**: Learning strategies affect mathematics achievement through motivation and engagement rather than directly. The direct effect became non-significant once both mediators were included.

**Q2**: Which indirect pathway was strongest, and what evidence supports it?
**A**: The pathway through motivation (B = 0.23, 95% CI [0.14, 0.33]) was significantly greater than both the engagement pathway and the serial pathway.

**Q3**: How should teachers apply the findings?
**A**: Pair strategy instruction with practices that build motivation and engagement, such as goal setting, progress monitoring, meaningful tasks, and feedback. The authors caution these are directions for practice, not proven causal effects.
