## Summary

A tutoring-marketplace founder reports that a high-quality AI math app was used by only about 10% of students until their human tutors began assigning and following up on the practice, after which more than half completed it. A Tennessee randomized trial of Khanmigo showed the same thin engagement and no gains beyond ordinary Khan Academy practice. The author argues content was never the bottleneck: motivation comes from a person who notices and cares, so a teacher should stay in charge with AI filling the gaps between lessons.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Tutoring]]**: AI assistants that walk students through steps on demand; high quality but rarely used unprompted.
- **[[Tutorela]]**: The author's hybrid marketplace pairing human tutors with AI for between-session practice; the author discloses it sells this model.
- **[[Limud Naim]]**: Israeli tutoring marketplace (15 years, 500,000+ lessons) whose math app had 10,000+ exercises and a free AI assistant.
- **[[Khanmigo]]**: Khan Academy's AI tutor, studied in a two-year [[National Bureau of Economic Research]] randomized trial in 18 Tennessee middle schools.
- **[[Alpha School]]**: National test case for AI-first education, expanding into Texas and South Carolina public schools.
- **[[Accountability]]**: A person who knows the student and expects the work to get done; the "ingredient" the software lacked.
- **[[Hybrid Tutoring]]**: Tutor sets direction and assigns practice; AI covers the time between lessons.

## Key Takeaways

- **Low Uptake**: Only about 10% of students used the polished AI math app on their own.
- **Khanmigo Trial**: Median student messaged it on a third of practice days; 17% of mistakes.
- **No AI Uplift**: Math gains matched Khan Academy practice without AI; about 15% engage with Khanmigo.
- **Tutor Assignment Effect**: Over half completed tutor-assigned work, up from about 10% self-directed.
- **Same App, New Owner**: Only change was a human managing each student's learning.
- **Content Not Bottleneck**: Free high-quality explanations abound; missing is someone who notices and cares.
- **Where AI Fits**: Answers 9 p.m. questions, re-explains patiently, supplies unlimited practice under tutor direction.
- **Advice**: Leaders should ask who holds students accountable; parents should pair AI with a person.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. The binding constraint determines output** — Improving a non-limiting input (content quality, personalization) yields nothing while the real limiter (student motivation to show up) stays unaddressed. · *model:* [[Theory of Constraints]]

**T2. Follow-through depends on a person who expects it** — Engagement rose when a known human assigned and followed up on the same work, so accountability from a relationship drives effort more than tool quality. · *model:* [[Human-in-the-Loop]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about AI-first education?
**A**: Content was never the bottleneck; students lack someone who notices whether they did the work and cares how it went. AI works best with a teacher or tutor in charge and AI covering the time in between.

**Q2**: What evidence shows the effect of adding a human?
**A**: At Limud Naim, only about 10% used the app alone, but more than half completed practice once their tutor assigned and followed up on it. In the Tennessee Khanmigo trial, engagement was thin and math gains matched practice without AI.

**Q3**: How should schools and parents apply this?
**A**: Schools should ask who will hold each student accountable for using the software, not just whether it is good. Parents should pair AI tools with a tutor, teacher, or parent who sets goals and follows up.
