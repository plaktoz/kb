## Summary

New research finds that stalled innovation efforts often stem not from insufficient effort, but from teams blending incompatible learning activities — like customer interviews, brainstorming, and after-action reviews — without clarity on how they intersect. The highest-performing teams sequence and separate these learning modes rather than running them simultaneously.

## Core Concepts

[[Organizational Learning]] is not a single activity but a portfolio of distinct modes — inquiry, ideation, reflection — that require different mindsets. When teams blend these [[Learning Activities|learning activities]] at the wrong time, confusion undermines [[Innovation]] rather than accelerating it. The research implies that effective [[Team Learning]] requires explicit sequencing and role clarity around which mode a team is in at any given moment.

## Key Takeaways

- **Root cause of stalled innovation**: Teams often blend incompatible learning activities at the wrong times rather than lacking effort.
- **Example activities**: Interviewing customers, brainstorming ideas, and conducting after-action reviews are distinct modes that get conflated.
- **High performers separate modes**: The best teams don't attempt every form of learning simultaneously — they sequence learning activities deliberately.
