## Summary
Spaced repetition exploits two foundational cognitive mechanisms — the spacing effect and retrieval practice — to build durable long-term memory with significantly less total study time than massed practice. Algorithm-driven systems (SRS) like Anki calculate the optimal review interval per card based on recall difficulty, ensuring each review happens at the moment just before forgetting. The result is a compound memory infrastructure: scheduled maintenance prevents decay and consolidation reduces future maintenance cost.

## Core Concepts

- [[Spacing Effect]] — distributing practice over time produces stronger memory traces than massed (same-session) practice
- [[Retrieval Practice]] — the act of recalling information strengthens memory encoding more than re-reading it; "the testing effect"
- [[Forgetting Curve]] — Ebbinghaus's model: ~50% forgotten within an hour, ~70% within 24 hours without review
- [[Spaced Repetition System (SRS)]] — algorithm-driven software (Anki, SM-2) that schedules reviews at optimal intervals
- [[SM-2 Algorithm]] — the SuperMemo algorithm underlying Anki; calculates next review date based on recall difficulty rating
- [[Biological Age of Memory]] — memories have exponential decay rates; spaced repetition resets the decay clock at each review

## Key Takeaways

- **Core mechanism**: Review at the moment just before forgetting — maximizes retrieval effort (strengthens encoding) while minimizing total review time
- **Spacing effect**: Distributed practice beats massed practice for long-term retention even when total study time is equal
- **Retrieval practice**: Active recall is ~2x more effective for retention than passive re-reading
- **Forgetting curve**: Without review, ~70% of new information gone within 24 hours — spaced repetition directly counteracts this
- **Algorithm**: Correct answers push next review further out; wrong answers shorten it — personalizes to individual performance
- **Best applications**: Medical memorization (pharmacology, anatomy), language vocabulary, professional certifications, any factual domain requiring durable recall
- **Compound benefit**: Maintenance cost decreases over time as memories consolidate — a learning flywheel

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Compound Growth (Memory as Infrastructure)]]**: Spaced repetition applies the same logic as compound interest — the earlier you begin systematic review, the lower the total effort cost over time, because memory consolidation reduces future maintenance requirements.
- **[[Interleaving vs Blocking]]**: The spacing effect is related to interleaving (mixing topics) vs blocking (studying one topic at a time) — both work by introducing forgetting-and-retrieval cycles that force reconstruction, strengthening the memory trace.
