## Summary

School principals have moved from using AI for emails and newsletters to more strategic uses, building custom tools to cut absenteeism, draft teacher goals, and track student needs. Their aim is to offload clerical work so staff can spend more time with students, captured in one principal's motto "people over paperwork." They also set limits, guarding student data privacy, keeping communications personal, and keeping students from leaning on AI so much that it dulls their critical thinking.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI in Education]]**: Principals' use of [[Generative AI]] has evolved since [[ChatGPT]]'s November 2022 launch from drafting emails to strategic problem-solving.
- **[[Derek Cantrell]]** (Allegheny High School, Virginia): Built apps and websites by prompting AI, and brainstormed an [[Chronic Absenteeism|absenteeism]] tracking system with [[Google]]'s AI suite.
- **Earn-back attendance system**: Tracks unexcused absences, tardies, and referrals; warns students near limits; lets them recover standing via lunch detentions or tasks; and rewards good records with cafeteria points.
- **[[Sterling Seeman]]** (Springer Middle School, Delaware): Created AI prompts that help teachers draft measurable yearly goals, inspired by a Delaware education department pilot.
- **[[Rachel Jensen]]** (Idaho Home Learning Academy): Uses automated tools to build per-student trackers of needs and accommodations for teachers overseeing 90+ students online.
- **[[Student Data Privacy]]**: Keeping student data localized rather than feeding it into public AI tools.
- **[[AI Overreliance]]**: Concern that student dependence on AI erodes [[Critical Thinking]].
- **[[AI Hallucinations]]**: Students must check AI outputs for errors even in approved uses.

## Key Takeaways

- **Strategic Shift**: Principals now use AI for strategy, not just emails and newsletters.
- **No-Code Tool Building**: A non-programmer principal builds custom apps through AI prompting.
- **Positive Attendance Incentives**: Absence tracking pairs warnings with earn-back options and cafeteria rewards.
- **Goal-Writing Prompts**: AI drafts measurable teacher goals, shifting time to achieving them.
- **Online Student Trackers**: AI consolidates student needs for teachers with 90+ students.
- **People Over Paperwork**: Less clerical work means more time for relationships and presence.
- **Data Caution**: Avoid putting student data into public-domain AI tools.
- **Personal Touch**: One principal won't let AI write emails or newsletters.
- **Quality Concerns**: AI-made lesson plans and assessments may contain errors.
- **Student Guardrails**: Summaries, outlines, and editing are fair use; outputs must be checked.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Offloading clerical work frees scarce time for human work** — When tools absorb routine administrative tasks, a fixed time budget shifts toward the parts of the job only people can do, such as relationships and being present.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: How has principals' use of AI changed since ChatGPT's public launch?
**A**: It has moved from drafting emails and polishing newsletters to strategic uses such as building custom tools to reduce absenteeism, set teacher goals, and track student needs.

**Q2**: How does Derek Cantrell's AI-designed attendance system work?
**A**: It tracks unexcused absences, tardies, and disciplinary referrals, warns students nearing limits, lets them earn back good standing through lunch detentions or tasks, and rewards good records with points for cafeteria treats.

**Q3**: What guardrails do principals recommend when adopting AI in schools?
**A**: Keep student data localized and out of public AI tools, avoid AI for personal communications, check outputs for errors, and limit student use so it doesn't dull critical thinking.
