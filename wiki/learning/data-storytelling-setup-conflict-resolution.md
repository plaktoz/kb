## Summary

[[Scott Berinato]] of HBR demonstrates that effective [[Data Storytelling]] reduces to a universal three-part structure — setup, conflict, resolution — that underlies every narrative ever told. He applies this framework to a complex multi-country home price index chart, deconstructing and rebuilding it as a focused, sequential story. Stripping away non-essential data and replacing generic titles with story-reflecting titles are the two primary levers for improving audience comprehension and emotional resonance.

## Core Concepts

- [[Data Storytelling]]: applying narrative structure to quantitative data presentations
- [[Setup-Conflict-Resolution]]: the universal story framework — setup (baseline reality), conflict (change), resolution (new reality created by the change)
- [[Data Visualization]] design: splitting a single chart into sequential states so each state carries one story beat
- [[Chart Titling]]: descriptive titles that carry the story rather than generic variable labels
- [[Global Real Home Price Index]]: case study chart used to illustrate the framework
- [[Narrative Structure]] as a cognitive tool for creating emotional connection with audiences

## Key Takeaways

- Every story follows setup → conflict → resolution; no exceptions.
- **Setup**: establishes the baseline reality or situation for the audience.
- **Conflict**: introduces change — without change, there is no story.
- **Resolution**: the new reality that the change creates.
- **Understand your index baseline** before interpreting convergence in a chart.
- **Split charts into states** to guide audience focus to one story beat at a time.
- **Use descriptive titles** reflecting the story; generic titles waste the most visible text on the slide.
- **Remove all data irrelevant** to the specific setup, conflict, or resolution being told.
- Narrative creates emotional connection — audiences don't just believe, they feel the data.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Signal vs. Noise]]**: Berinato's method is an applied instance of ruthlessly eliminating noise so the signal (the story) becomes unmissable — each chart state retains only the data points that serve one narrative beat, making the principle operational and concrete.
- **[[Narrative Bias]]**: Humans are evolutionarily wired to process and retain information packaged as stories; structuring data presentations around setup-conflict-resolution exploits this cognitive architecture rather than fighting it, which is why the emotional connection Berinato describes is not rhetorical flourish but a predictable cognitive outcome.
