## Summary
This meta-analysis of 34 studies (91 effect sizes, from 5,741 screened) finds AI-based pedagogical interventions have a positive overall effect on self-regulated learning (Hedges' g = 0.76). Effects are positive across cognitive, metacognitive, and resource management strategies. The effect varies significantly by educational level, AI pedagogical role, and AI application type, but not by discipline, intervention length, control group type, sample size, or SRL measure.

## Core Concepts
- [[Self-Regulated Learning]] (SRL): learners managing their own cognitive, metacognitive, and resource management strategies.
- [[Meta-Analysis]]: pooled effect sizes using [[Hedges' g]] across 34 studies.
- [[Metacognition]]: metacognitive strategies, one SRL subdomain.
- Moderator analysis: research context, intervention design, AI, and SRL factors.
- [[AI Pedagogical Roles]] and AI application types: the design features that significantly moderate impact.

## Key Takeaways
- **Overall Effect**: AI interventions raise self-regulated learning, Hedges' g = 0.76.
- **Cognitive Strategies**: Smallest subdomain effect, g = 0.48.
- **Metacognitive Strategies**: Moderate-to-large effect, g = 0.73.
- **Resource Management**: Largest subdomain effect, g = 0.93.
- **Evidence Base**: 34 studies and 91 effect sizes from 5,741 screened.
- **Significant Moderators**: Educational level, AI pedagogical role, and AI application type.
- **Non-Significant Moderators**: Discipline, intervention length, control group, sample size, SRL measure.

## 🧠 Core Principles

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does the meta-analysis conclude about AI and self-regulated learning?
**A**: AI-based pedagogical interventions have a positive overall effect on self-regulated learning (Hedges' g = 0.76). The effect is positive across all three subdomains.

**Q2**: How do the effects differ across SRL subdomains, and what was the evidence base?
**A**: Effects were g = 0.48 for cognitive strategies, 0.73 for metacognitive, and 0.93 for resource management. They come from 91 effect sizes across 34 studies selected from 5,741.

**Q3**: What does the moderator analysis imply for designing AI interventions?
**A**: Effects vary significantly by educational level, AI pedagogical role, and AI application type, so these design choices matter. Discipline and intervention length did not show significant differences.
