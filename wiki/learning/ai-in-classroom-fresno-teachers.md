## Summary
Central California classrooms are adopting AI as a teaching assistant — Fresno City College professor Todd McLeod uses ChatGPT and Claude to build personalized, interactive learning tutorials — while students and parents remain split on whether it erodes human interaction, effort, and personal voice in schoolwork. Only 18% of U.S. K-12 teachers report formal AI training (Gallup), and Fresno Unified is rolling out phased training reaching ~70% of staff so far.

## Core Concepts
- **[[AI as Interactive Tutorial Layer]]**: McLeod's framing — AI doesn't replace the textbook's content but adds an interactive layer letting students ask "wait, I don't get that, tell me more," ensuring every student gets taught every topic every week.
- **[[AI Training Gap]]**: Gallup finds only 18% of K-12 teachers have received formal AI training, even as adoption accelerates — a capability/infrastructure mismatch.
- **[[Voice Erosion Concern]]**: Student Connor's objection that AI use in essays causes students to "lose your overall voice" — framed as partly the teacher's responsibility to guard against.

## Key Takeaways
- Fresno Unified's rollout is phased: ~70% of district staff completed basic AI training, with separate teacher-specific training ongoing.
- McLeod frames ethical AI use as a core educator responsibility: teaching students to use tools "in an appropriate fashion" without "causing harm to yourself or others," and never abandoning their own judgment to verify AI output.
- Student sentiment is split — some (like Danny) want more real-person interaction; others echo the "safety net" framing McLeod offers for catching up on missed material.

## 🃏 Review Questions
**Q1**: How does Todd McLeod describe AI's role relative to the traditional textbook?
**A**: The textbook still delivers the content, but AI adds a new interactive element on top of it — letting students ask follow-up questions in real time ("I don't quite get that, tell me more") rather than passively reading static material.

**Q2**: What gap does the Gallup poll cited in the article reveal about AI adoption in K-12 education?
**A**: Only 18% of U.S. K-12 teachers report having received formal AI training, even as AI tools are being adopted in classrooms — indicating training is lagging behind adoption.

**Q3**: What responsibility does McLeod assign to educators regarding AI use, beyond just teaching content?
**A**: To teach students to use AI tools ethically and appropriately, and to keep applying their own judgment and analysis to AI output rather than abandoning their own intelligence just because a tool is available.
