## Summary

This systematic review examines how [[Intelligent Tutoring Systems]] (ITS) and [[Robot Tutoring Systems]] (RTS) address global educational challenges, analyzing 86 studies using the PRISMA framework and Latent Class Analysis. ITS leverages AI techniques such as [[Bayesian Knowledge Tracing]] and [[Large Language Models]] to deliver cognitive support, while RTS fosters social and emotional engagement through human-like interaction. The authors find meaningful progress in AI-driven adaptability and learning outcomes but flag persistent challenges around ethics, scalability, and cognitive adaptability gaps.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Intelligent Tutoring Systems]] (ITS)**: Computer-based systems that use AI to personalize instruction and provide real-time cognitive support to learners.
- **[[Robot Tutoring Systems]] (RTS)**: Physical robotic agents that engage learners socially and emotionally through human-like interaction.
- **[[Bayesian Knowledge Tracing]]**: A probabilistic model used by ITS to infer a student's latent knowledge state and adapt content accordingly.
- **[[Large Language Models]] in Education**: LLMs applied within ITS to generate natural-language explanations, feedback, and dialogue.
- **[[PRISMA Framework]]**: Systematic review methodology used to ensure rigorous literature selection and reporting.
- **[[Latent Class Analysis]]**: Statistical technique applied here to cluster the 86 studies into three system archetypes: computer-based ITS, robot-based RTS, and multimodal systems.
- **[[Multimodal Learning Systems]]**: Hybrid approaches combining visual, auditory, and physical interaction channels for richer educational experiences.
- **[[Adaptive Learning]]**: The core value proposition of ITS — dynamically adjusting difficulty, pacing, and content to match individual learner needs.
- **[[Educational Equity]]**: Scalability and accessibility of AI tutoring systems across diverse learner populations and resource-constrained environments.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three archetypes identified**: Computer-based ITS, robot-based RTS, and multimodal systems via Latent Class Analysis.
- **Cognitive vs. social support**: ITS excels at cognitive scaffolding; RTS adds social-emotional engagement.
- **LLMs expanding ITS**: Large language models are increasingly powering more natural tutoring dialogues.
- **Bayesian tracing still central**: Probabilistic knowledge modeling remains foundational to adaptive ITS design.
- **Hybrid integration advocated**: Authors call for ITS/RTS convergence to address both cognitive and affective learner needs.
- **Persistent challenges**: Ethics, scalability, and cognitive adaptability gaps remain unsolved across 86 reviewed studies.
- **Diverse learner support lacking**: Current systems underserve learners with disabilities or non-standard learning profiles.
- **Future research priorities**: Scalability, ethics, and inclusive design flagged as the most critical open problems.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Cognitive Load Theory]]**: Effective tutoring systems must manage intrinsic, extraneous, and germane load — ITS designs that adapt difficulty in real time are operationalizing this principle to prevent overload and keep learners in the zone of proximal development.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: As ITS optimizes for measurable learning outcomes (test scores, completion rates), there is a risk systems learn to game their own metrics rather than deepen genuine understanding — the scalability and ethics challenges flagged in this review are partly manifestations of this tension.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central argument of this systematic review about ITS and RTS?
**A**: AI-driven tutoring systems show meaningful progress in adaptability and learning outcomes, but hybrid ITS/RTS integration is needed to address both cognitive and social-emotional dimensions of learning, alongside unresolved challenges in ethics, scalability, and support for diverse learners.

**Q2**: How did the authors classify the 86 studies, and what methodology did they use?
**A**: Using the PRISMA framework for study selection and Latent Class Analysis for clustering, the authors identified three system types: computer-based ITS, robot-based RTS, and multimodal systems.

**Q3**: What is the practical implication of this review for educational technology design?
**A**: Designers should pursue hybrid ITS/RTS architectures that combine AI-driven cognitive adaptability with the social-emotional engagement of robotic systems, while prioritizing scalability and inclusive design for diverse learner populations.
