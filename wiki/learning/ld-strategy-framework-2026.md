## Summary
An [[L&D Strategy]] is a business case for closing specific skill gaps tied to business outcomes, not a training schedule — most organizational L&D efforts fail to show ROI because they're built on assumed rather than validated skills data, measure course completion instead of actual competency change, and don't connect to real-time operational decisions.

## Core Concepts
- **[[L&D Strategy]] vs. training plan**: A training plan lists what activities run and when; an L&D strategy explains why — which capability gaps it closes, what business objective that serves, and how success is measured.
- **[[Skills Gap Analysis]]**: The evidence base for any L&D strategy; must use validated, manager-assessed competency data rather than self-assessment or top-down assumption to avoid targeting the wrong gaps.
- **Competency re-assessment**: Measuring capability change (baseline → intervention → re-assessment) rather than relying on completion rates or satisfaction scores as a proxy for whether training actually worked.

## Key Takeaways
- **Three failure modes recur**: strategies built on assumed (not validated) skill gaps, completion used as a false proxy for effectiveness, and strategy disconnected from real-time operational decisions.
- **Six-step framework**: skills gap analysis → align to business objectives → prioritize by risk/impact → design targeted interventions matched to gap type → execute and track → measure and adapt.
- **Intervention type should match gap type**: structured learning for knowledge gaps, coaching/stretch assignments for skill gaps, formal certification for compliance gaps, mentoring for behavioral/leadership gaps.
- **ROI is measured by competency change**, not completion — a baseline assessment, a defined target proficiency level, and a post-intervention re-assessment are the minimum measurement infrastructure.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central distinction between an L&D strategy and a training plan?
**A**: A training plan is an operational schedule of activities; an L&D strategy is the evidence-based business case explaining which capability gaps are being closed, why, and how success will be measured.

**Q2**: Why do most L&D strategies fail to deliver measurable ROI?
**A**: They're typically built on assumed skill gaps from manager surveys rather than validated competency data, and they measure training completion rather than re-assessing whether capability actually improved.

**Q3**: How should an organization decide what type of intervention to use for a given skill gap?
**A**: Match the intervention to the gap type — structured learning (eLearning, classroom) for knowledge gaps, coaching and stretch assignments for skill gaps, formal certification for compliance gaps, and mentoring/feedback for behavioral or leadership gaps.
