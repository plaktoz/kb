## Summary

A review of AI tutoring evidence finds generic tools like ChatGPT can harm learning — one RCT found students performed 17% worse on exams despite scoring better in practice sessions while using it — while purpose-built, conversational, curriculum-aligned tutors that scaffold reasoning show large measurable gains, including a physics-course RCT with effect sizes up to 1.3 standard deviations. An independent evaluation of Third Space Learning's AI tutor Skye found students improved from 34% to 92% accuracy within a single session.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Tutoring]]** — must be conversational, curriculum-aligned and scaffold reasoning to be effective
- **[[Cognitive Outsourcing]]** — Oakley and Sejnowski's term for when AI thinking replaces student learning
- **[[Education Endowment Foundation]]** — establishes one-to-one tutoring's five-month average progress benchmark
- **[[Third Space Learning]]** — publisher and maker of the AI maths tutor Skye
- **[[Randomized Controlled Trials]]** — Bastani et al. and Kestin et al. studies cited as key evidence

## Key Takeaways

- **ChatGPT study use**: 50% better practice, 17% worse exam results.
- **Purpose-built AI tutor RCT** showed up to 1.3 SD learning gains.
- **Skye tutor**: students improved from 34% to 92% accuracy in one session.
- **Effective AI tutoring must scaffold reasoning**, not supply direct answers.
- **UK DfE mandates human oversight** and no default direct answers.
