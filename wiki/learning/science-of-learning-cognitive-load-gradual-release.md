## Summary

[[Nathaniel Swain]]'s book *Harnessing the Science of Learning* translates cognitive-load theory, retrieval practice, spaced repetition, and knowledge-rich curriculum design into practical strategies for educators specifically, rather than learners. Its "[[I Do, We Do, You Do]]" gradual-release model — explicit demonstration, guided practice, then independent practice — maps directly onto cognitive science of skill acquisition, and the article argues professional development for teachers rarely follows this same evidence-based structure. A companion explainer from Gordon College frames the [[Science of Learning]] more broadly as an interdisciplinary field (cognitive science, neuroscience, psychology, education) that gives school leaders an evidence-based alternative to trend- or vendor-driven decision-making, citing the [[Science of Reading]] movement and the debunking of "[[Learning Styles]]" as its most visible wins.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Cognitive Load Theory]]** — working memory is small; visual clutter or extraneous demands during new learning impede it, especially for young learners
- **[[I Do, We Do, You Do]]** — gradual release of responsibility: explicit demonstration, guided collective practice, then independent practice
- **[[Distraction-Free Instruction]]** — "no bells and whistles" principle for reducing extraneous cognitive load during early instruction
- **[[Technology Evaluation Questions]]** — does a tool reduce or add cognitive load, support or substitute explicit instruction, extend or duplicate a teacher's role
- **[[Science of Reading]]** — structured literacy approach (oral language, phonemic awareness, phonics, vocabulary, fluency, comprehension) adopted by many states/districts based on reading research
- **[[Learning Styles]]** — the visual/auditory/kinesthetic preference model; research has largely discredited matching instruction to a claimed learning style in favor of explicit, systematic instruction
- **[[Retrieval Practice]]** — low-stakes quizzes, cumulative review, and spaced repetition strengthen long-term retention more than repeated re-reading
- **[[Evidence-Based Leadership]]** — Gordon College framing: school leaders should ask what research supports an initiative, whether it's been replicated, and how success will be measured before adopting it

## Key Takeaways

- **Working memory is the bottleneck**: minimizing extraneous load matters most for young learners and students with learning differences.
- **Skipping "I do, we do" causes confusion**: independent practice without demonstration can build confidence in wrong approaches.
- **PD rarely follows its own advice**: professional development is often delivered as information transfer without gradual release.
- **Three PD design principles**: small increments, modeling before practice, connecting to existing knowledge.
- **Technology isn't inherently good or bad**: its value depends on whether it reduces cognitive load and supports explicit instruction.
- **Science of reading is the field's clearest success story**: structured literacy has replaced balanced-literacy assumptions in many states/districts.
- **"Learning styles" don't hold up**: instruction should follow research-supported practice, not a student's claimed sensory preference.
- **Leadership decisions should be evidence-gated**: evaluate curriculum, PD, and interventions against replicated research rather than trends or vendor claims.
