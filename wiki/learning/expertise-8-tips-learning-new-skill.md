## Summary

UNC professor Jeffrey Greene translates Anders Ericsson's deliberate practice research into eight actionable tips for learning any new skill, emphasizing intentional practice over raw hours, the right kind of motivation, and structured feedback rather than the popular "10,000 hours" myth.

## Core Concepts

Greene's advice is grounded in the [[Deliberate Practice]] model developed by [[Anders Ericsson]], challenging the popularized "[[10,000-Hour Rule]]" as an oversimplification. Central to the framework is [[Feedback Loops|feedback]] that "feeds forward" toward the next goal rather than just assessing the past, and a nuanced treatment of [[Intrinsic vs Extrinsic Motivation]] — both are valid as long as the learner feels in control of the choice.

## Key Takeaways

- **Find a guide**: Explicit step-by-step direction (book, instructor, online guide) beats unstructured trial and error.
- **Distraction-free environment**: It's easier to prevent distractions than resist them once present.
- **Build endurance gradually**: Start with 15-30 minute sessions; even experts rarely practice more than 4-5 hours a day.
- **Practice deliberately**: Consciously attend to specific sub-skills, even "de-automatizing" skills you've plateaued on.
- **Motivation can be extrinsic or intrinsic**: Both work fine as long as the learner feels in control; it only turns harmful when coercive.
- **Feedback must feed forward**: Good coaching asks "what's the next goal?" not just "how did you do?"
- **Be your own coach**: Most people can internalize progress and self-coach even without an expert present.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Mastering a new skill depends on a diligent, deliberate approach — not simply accumulating hours — following the deliberate practice model of Anders Ericsson.

**Q2**: What distinguishes "deliberate" practice from ordinary practice in this framework?
**A**: Deliberate practice means being intentional and mindful while practicing a specific sub-skill, consciously paying attention rather than going through the motions automatically.

**Q3**: How should feedback be structured to best support skill development?
**A**: Feedback should "feed forward" by helping the learner identify the next goal or step, while also affirming that they're in control, competent, and that the coach genuinely cares about their progress.
