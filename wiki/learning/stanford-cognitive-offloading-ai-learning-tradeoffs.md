## Summary

Stanford researchers argue that AI can either expand or short-circuit learning depending on how much of the thinking it offloads. A study of Brazilian middle schoolers found that students who lost AI access performed worse creatively than students who never had it, and a review of 20 causal studies points to general-purpose chatbots as the most detrimental tools. Tools with scaffolding and deliberate friction appear safer, but the researchers say the technology has outpaced the evidence.

## Core Concepts

- [[Cognitive Offloading]]: reducing a task's mental processing demands through tools, from finger counting and calculators to AI that can offload an entire job.
- [[Transfer of Learning]]: applying prior knowledge, skills, and strategies to new contexts; Guilherme Lichand treats it as fundamental to learning.
- [[Mental Representations]]: Hariharan Subramonyam's view that "learning is representing", building good internal representations for later use.
- [[Productive Struggle]]: discomfort, resilience, and self-regulation as essential social-emotional parts of learning.
- [[Constructivist Learning]]: bottom-up learning by creating and iterating, which Subramonyam sees AI supporting.
- [[Scaffolding (Learning Theory)]]: tools that use Socratic questioning or essay feedback without generating the work.
- [[Guilherme Lichand]], [[Lily Fesler]], [[Hariharan Subramonyam]], [[Sachin Allums]]: Stanford researchers quoted or cited.
- [[Stanford SCALE Initiative]]: its AI Hub for Education reviewed over 800 papers on AI in education.

## Key Takeaways

- **Full Delegation**: AI can offload an entire job; "no thinking" means no learning, per Lichand.
- **Which Skills**: Offloading deep processes like creative problem solving can reduce ability once AI access is lost.
- **Brazil Study**: Students who lost AI access scored below students who never had it.
- **Dose Effect**: Negative effects concentrated in kids who used the AI tool most.
- **Evidence Review**: 800+ papers screened; 20 high-quality causal studies; general-purpose chatbots were most detrimental.
- **Scaffolded Tools**: Socratic questioning and essay feedback tools showed less clear offloading harm.
- **Creation-Centered AI**: Students designed simulations in AI, but only after doing design planning themselves.
- **Friction Balance**: Enough friction to learn, not so much that people abandon the tool.
- **Go Slow**: Lichand says there is "a lot of value in waiting"; evidence lags technology.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Learning requires effortful thinking that cannot be fully delegated** — If a learner never spends time thinking about a task, no learning occurs; the more thinking is offloaded, the larger the later loss of ability. · *model:* [[Cognitive Offloading]]
→ explains: Full Delegation, Brazil Study, Dose Effect

**T2. Productive struggle builds transfer** — Discomfort, frustration, and self-regulation during learning are what let learners apply skills to new tasks, so tools must preserve them. · *model:* [[Desirable Difficulty]]
→ explains: Which Skills, Friction Balance, Go Slow

**T3. Scaffolding supports thinking without replacing it** — Tools that guide with questions or feedback, rather than producing answers, avoid fully offloading understanding. · *model:* [[Scaffolding (Learning Theory)]]
→ explains: Scaffolded Tools, Evidence Review, Creation-Centered AI

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central claim about AI and learning?
**A**: AI's effect on learning depends on how much thinking it offloads: it can improve or harm outcomes, and the difference likely comes down to the type of tool used.

**Q2**: What did the Brazilian middle school study find?
**A**: Students who used an AI tool on a first creative task lost the tool's advantage when it was removed, and their creative problem solving fell below that of students who never had it. The effects concentrated in heavier users of the tool.

**Q3**: How should AI learning tools be designed or introduced?
**A**: With enough scaffolding and friction that learners still do the understanding and build resilience, but not so much that they stop using the tool. Implementation should be deliberate with guardrails, not rushed.
