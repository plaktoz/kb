## Summary

The San Francisco Standard reports that students are arriving at college less ready to write, and AI now lets them skip the practice that builds writing and thinking. Teachers like Eliza Russo have moved essays into the classroom, trading long-form practice for integrity, while academics debate whether writing itself is essential to thought. Evidence from a Chinese study suggests AI raises homework scores while lowering closed-book exam results.

## Core Concepts

- [[Eliza Russo]]: Lincoln High English head who ended take-home essays after "Macbeth-gate."
- [[Cognitive Offloading]]: students handing homework thinking to AI and therefore not learning.
- [[Entry Level Writing Requirement]]: UC's writing bar; 1,109 Berkeley freshmen missed it last fall.
- [[Five-Paragraph Essay]]: template teaching criticised by [[Jon Lang]] for replacing thinking.
- [[Dan Willingham]]: cognitive psychologist who says the brain is designed to avoid thinking.
- [[Ben Riley]] and the hot dog analogy for AI as a regular part of the mental diet.
- [[Joe Cruz]]: argues colleges should consider oral exams and discussion to assess thinking.
- [[Bita Nazarian]] and [[826 Valencia]]: writing reflects personal values; AI homogenises application essays.
- [[Phaedrus]]: Plato's early worry that writing weakens memory.

## Key Takeaways

- **Macbeth-gate**: Identical-sounding AI essays led Russo to restrict writing to in-class, handwritten work.
- **Pew Survey**: 54% of teens used AI chatbots for schoolwork; 59% say cheating is common.
- **Shrinking Measurement**: California ended writing tests; NAEP has no writing tests scheduled through 2033.
- **Readiness Gap**: Berkeley freshmen missing the writing standard rose over fivefold since 2016.
- **Inflated Grades**: UC freshmen with 4.0+ weighted GPA rose from 28.6% (1994) to 74.9% (2022).
- **Template Teaching**: Formulaic essays and exam-only writing mean students never draft and revise.
- **China Study**: About 27,000 students: homework up 18%, closed-book exam scores down 20%.
- **Effort Builds Learning**: Willingham says struggle produces learning, yet the brain pulls toward shortcuts.
- **Open Debate**: Cruz questions writing's centrality; Nazarian defends it for self-expression.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Effort produces learning** — Cognitive struggle is what builds skill, so removing the difficulty removes the learning even when output quality rises. · *model:* [[Desirable Difficulty]]
→ explains: Effort Builds Learning, China Study, Macbeth-gate

**T2. What isn't measured isn't taught** — When a skill drops out of testing and policy attention, instruction and student preparation in it erode. · *model:* [[Goodhart's Law]]
→ explains: Shrinking Measurement, Readiness Gap, Inflated Grades

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central concern about AI and student writing?
**A**: Students are already less ready to write, and AI lets them skip the long, messy practice that teaches composition and, some argue, thinking itself.

**Q2**: What did the Chinese study of nearly 27,000 students find?
**A**: After students began using AI, homework scores rose 18% and time per assignment fell from 64 to 45 minutes, but closed-book monthly exam scores fell 20% within six months.

**Q3**: How has one teacher adapted, and what is the trade-off?
**A**: Russo limits essays to handwritten, in-class work to guarantee students do their own writing, but they lose practice building longer, more complex arguments.
