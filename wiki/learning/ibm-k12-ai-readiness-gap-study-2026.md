## Summary
IBM's July 2026 survey of 2,048 respondents (Morning Consult) finds that AI use is already routine in U.S. secondary classrooms — 76% of middle school educators use AI weekly — but only 20% have received extensive training, creating a dangerous preparation gap that IBM is addressing with a K-12 AI Leaders Fellowship launching in fall 2026 with an inaugural cohort of up to 100 New York area educators.

## Core Concepts
- **[[K-12 AI Adoption Gap]]**: 76% of middle school and 73% of high school educators report weekly AI classroom use, yet only 20% have received extensive AI training. The adoption curve has dramatically outpaced the preparation curve — a structural risk to responsible AI education.
- **[[IBM K-12 AI Leaders Fellowship]]**: New fellowship targeting K-12 superintendents and educators; inaugural cohort of up to 100 participants in the greater New York area launching fall 2026, with plans to expand to additional U.S. states. Participants receive IBM SkillsBuild AI credentials, curriculum materials, and expert facilitator access.
- **[[Teacher Training as Top Barrier]]**: 42% of K-12 educators cite lack of AI training as the top barrier to effective AI integration; 34% cite limited AI curriculum and instructional materials. Training gap is the primary bottleneck.
- **[[Parent-Educator AI Literacy Disconnect]]**: 77% of parents want input on classroom AI use but only 20% clearly understand the AI guidance their child receives; 51% prioritize clearer guidelines and 48% want greater transparency.
- **[[AI Dependency vs. Cheating Concerns]]**: Teachers' top concerns are student AI dependency (52%) and cheating/plagiarism (47%); administrators' top concerns are insufficient AI training (26%) and cost of AI tools (22%).

## Key Takeaways
- **Weekly AI use**: 76% of middle school educators; 73% of high school; 45% daily use at high school level.
- **Extensive training received**: only 20% of K-12 educators.
- **Top educator barrier**: lack of training (42%); then limited AI curriculum (34%).
- **Adapting well to AI**: only 24% of educators and 16% of parents say the U.S. education system is adapting "very well."
- **Optimal starting grade**: 41% of educators and parents agree middle school (grades 6–8) is the right time to begin AI learning.
- **College degree signal**: 30% of parents (vs. 24% educators) say AI has made a four-year degree feel less essential.
- **IBM fellowship**: fall 2026 launch, up to 100 participants, New York area; plans for nationwide expansion.
