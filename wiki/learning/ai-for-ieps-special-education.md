## Summary

Special education teachers are using AI to cut [[IEP]] (Individualized Education Plan) drafting time by more than half — from four to six hours down to under two. A 2025 University of Virginia study found no statistically significant quality difference between teacher-written and AI-generated IEP goals, with most teachers rating AI drafts as equal or better. Practitioners and researchers agree AI should assist an iterative process rather than replace teacher judgment and human connection.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Individualized Education Plan]] (IEP)**: A legally required document for students with disabilities that outlines goals, services, and accommodations; notoriously time-consuming to write.
- **[[AI-Assisted Writing]]**: Using tools like [[ChatGPT]] to translate teacher observations ("brain dumps") into formally structured IEP language, cutting drafting time significantly.
- **[[Special Education Teacher Preparedness]]**: Over half of special educators feel unprepared to independently write IEPs after completing training programs — a gap AI can help bridge.
- **[[Human-in-the-Loop]]**: The teacher's direct knowledge, observations, and judgment remain central; AI generates drafts but should not produce finished products independently.
- **[[Face-to-Face Learning Time]]**: Research supports that more direct student-teacher interaction leads to better educational outcomes for students with disabilities — time saved on paperwork means more time with students.
- **[[Danielle Waterfield]]**: Lead researcher at University of Virginia and former special education teacher who studied AI vs. teacher-written IEP goal quality.

## Key Takeaways

- **Time savings**: AI cuts IEP drafting from 4–6 hours to under 2 hours per student.
- **Quality parity**: UVA 2025 study found no significant quality gap across six dimensions.
- **Unpreparedness gap**: 50%+ of special educators feel unprepared to write IEPs independently after training.
- **Brain dump method**: Teacher provides detailed student observations; AI formats into IEP language.
- **Privacy caution**: Identifying information should be stripped before sharing student data with AI.
- **AI as co-author, not author**: AI assists iteration; teacher judgment and relationship remain primary.
- **Strategy benefit**: AI can brainstorm implementation strategies for nonverbal or complex-need students.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Comparative Advantage]]**: Teachers have irreplaceable comparative advantage in knowing individual students; AI has comparative advantage in structuring formal language — division of labor frees each to do what they do best.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: If IEP quality scores become the optimization target, AI-generated goals risk gaming the metric without reflecting true student need — underscoring why teacher oversight remains essential.
