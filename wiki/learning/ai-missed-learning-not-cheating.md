## Summary

Pearson education researcher Morgan Lieske argues schools should shift focus away from policing AI cheating — which research shows hasn't fundamentally changed since AI's arrival — and toward the real risk of "missed learning," where students skip drafting, reasoning, and productive struggle because AI can do it for them.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Cheating Detection Failure]]**: AI detection tools are unreliable and disproportionately flag multilingual writers, meaning enforcement-focused strategies erode trust without solving the underlying problem.
- **[[AI as the New Baseline]]**: College Board data shows 79-84% of U.S. high schoolers already use generative AI for schoolwork, and RAND reports 62% use it for homework — making AI use the norm, not the exception.
- **[[Missed Learning]]**: The true risk isn't rule-breaking but students bypassing drafting, reasoning, and productive struggle — foundational cognitive processes that AI can silently replace.
- **[[Visible Thinking Design]]**: A proposed instructional response requiring drafts, reflections, and reasoning-focused tasks so AI assistance can't substitute for the thinking process itself.

## Key Takeaways

- **Cheating hasn't spiked**: Stanford research finds cheating rates haven't dramatically changed in the AI era — the underlying causes (pressure, workload, low motivation) predate AI.
- **67% of students** believe AI use harms their critical thinking skills, a figure that rose significantly through 2025 (RAND/Schwartz and Diliberti).
- **Detection tools fail**: They struggle to keep pace with evolving models and disproportionately misflag multilingual writers, per Perkins et al. and Zou et al.
- **Policy reframe**: The article proposes replacing "How do I stop/detect AI use?" with "How do I design learning so AI can't replace the thinking?"
- **Brookings warning**: Overreliance on AI "can put children's fundamental learning capacity at risk" and undermine foundational cognitive development.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When "no AI use" becomes the enforced target rather than "genuine learning," students and schools optimize for evading detection rather than for the underlying goal of actual comprehension.
