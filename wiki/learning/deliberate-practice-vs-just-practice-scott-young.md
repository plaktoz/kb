## Summary

Scott Young argues that deliberate practice advice is frequently misapplied: it's the right prescription for people who already practice a lot but have plateaued, but bad advice for beginners whose actual bottleneck is simply whether they show up and practice at all. He illustrates this with a "doughy programmer vs. elite swimmer" framing that applies across fitness, painting, and language learning.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Doughy Programmer vs. Elite Swimmer]]**: Young's framing for why the same goal (get in shape, improve a skill) needs opposite advice depending on whether someone is a beginner with no habit yet, or an already-committed practitioner who has plateaued.
- **[[Volume vs. Efficiency]]**: Any goal has multiple levers (volume, efficiency, intensity); efficiency-focused advice like deliberate practice only pays off once volume becomes hard to increase further.
- **"[[Whatever You Can Stick To]]"**: Young's practical alternative to optimized-but-unsustainable methods (e.g., full language immersion) — for most learners, using whatever engages them (Duolingo, manga, subtitled films) beats a technically superior method they won't sustain.

## Key Takeaways

- For beginners, the correct advice is usually to focus obsessively on just showing up — technique and optimization can come later.
- For people already embedded in a practice (a working developer, a novelist with published-but-unsold books, a practicing doctor), deliberate practice is the right lever because sufficient exposure to the work already exists.
- Applying "elite swimmer" advice (immersion, spaced repetition systems, structured tutoring) to a "doughy programmer" problem can actually reduce follow-through by making a goal feel more complicated and less fun.
- Young explicitly connects this to career development: deliberate practice matters most where daily job exposure already guarantees volume, and the importance of the pursuit justifies the discomfort deliberate practice requires.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Deliberate practice is valuable advice, but only for people who already practice a lot and have plateaued — for beginners, the real bottleneck is usually just consistently practicing at all, not optimizing how they practice.

**Q2**: What is the "doughy programmer vs. elite swimmer" example, and what does it illustrate?
**A**: A beginner trying to get in shape needs simple habit-formation advice (just show up), while an elite swimmer chasing milliseconds needs deliberate, specialized coaching — the same underlying goal requires opposite advice depending on starting point.

**Q3**: How should someone decide whether to prioritize volume or efficiency in a new pursuit?
**A**: Prioritize volume (just doing the thing more) while it's still the easier lever to pull; shift toward efficiency (like deliberate practice) once adding more practice time becomes the harder constraint to move.
