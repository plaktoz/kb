## Summary

[[David Deming]] reviewed more than 75 studies, mostly RCTs, on AI in education and concluded that 49 showed broadly positive impacts, 16 were mixed or null, and 10 were negative. The positive results share one pattern: AI was scaffolded, for example by making students think or write first, using Socratic tutors, or personalizing practice difficulty. Unguided or answer-giving AI undermines learning, so the status quo of unregulated or banned use is the worst option.

## Core Concepts

- **"Think first, ChatGPT later"**: Doing the work yourself before AI help preserves later unaided performance.
- **[[Productive Struggle]]**: Difficulty that is confusing but still allows steady progress; AI can personalize it at scale.
- **[[Scaffolding (Learning Theory)]]**: Guidance on how to use AI (Socratic tutors, write-first rules, structured feedback) separates good outcomes from bad.
- **[[Cognitive Offloading]]**: Letting AI think and write creates an illusion of mastery, but offloading rote work can free capacity for higher-level thinking.
- **[[Publication Bias]]**: Pre-registered studies show positive rates (64%) matching the full sample (65%), suggesting little file-drawer problem.
- **[[Alpha School]]**: AI-driven school network whose self-paced model likely suits only already motivated students.
- **AI feedback on writing**: Structured feedback after independent drafting improves later unaided writing.

## Key Takeaways

- **Study Tally**: 49 of 75 studies positive, 16 mixed or null, 10 negative.
- **Publication Bias Check**: 9 of 14 pre-registered studies were positive, matching the overall 65% rate.
- **Think First**: Think-first groups outperformed unrestricted-AI groups on later unaided tasks.
- **Sierra Leone RCT**: Gemini Guided Learning raised unaided math scores 0.26 standard deviations in eight weeks.
- **Khanmigo**: Tennessee middle schools gained 0.14 standard deviations per year of participation.
- **Scaffolding Test**: Chilean economics students improved only after being taught how to use the AI tutor.
- **Unsupervised Use**: Chinese students' AI adoption raised homework scores but lowered final exam scores.
- **Writing Gains**: AI-assisted professional writing was 18% better and 40% faster; weaker writers gained most.
- **Middlebury Result**: AI users spent more time researching and scored better a week later.
- **Brown Anecdote**: Take-home average 96% fell to 49% on proctored in-person final.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Learning requires productive struggle, not frictionless answers** — Skill gain depends on effort invested in the right difficulty band; removing the effort removes the learning, while wasteful struggle also fails. · *model:* [[Desirable Difficulty]]
→ explains: Think First, Sierra Leone RCT, Unsupervised Use

**T2. Unguided tools don't produce good use; structure does** — Most learners lack the self-control to use a powerful shortcut well, so outcomes depend on the scaffolding around the tool. · *model:* [[Scaffolding (Learning Theory)]]
→ explains: Scaffolding Test, Khanmigo, Study Tally

**T3. Less brain activity isn't evidence of less learning** — Offloading rote mechanics can free capacity for higher-order thinking, so effort must be judged by learning outcomes rather than effort proxies. · *model:* [[Cognitive Offloading]]
→ explains: Middlebury Result, Writing Gains

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does Deming conclude after reviewing 75 studies on AI in education?
**A**: He updated positively: 49 studies showed broadly positive impacts, because most put scaffolding around AI use, such as write-first rules or Socratic tutors.

**Q2**: How does Deming test whether publication bias skews the evidence?
**A**: He compares pre-registered studies to the full sample; 9 of 14 pre-registered studies (64%) were positive versus 49 of 75 (65%) overall, suggesting no serious file-drawer problem.

**Q3**: What should educators do instead of banning or ignoring AI?
**A**: Teach students good uses by scaffolding AI, for example requiring unassisted attempts first, using Socratic tutors, and giving explicit guidance on how to use the tool.
