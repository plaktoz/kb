## Summary

Angela Duckworth argues that grit — passion and perseverance for long-term goals — is best exercised by staying "stubborn at the top" (fixed overarching goals) while remaining "agile at the bottom" (flexible tactics), including how one uses AI. A cover-letter experiment by Benjamin Lira found that AI-assisted writers improved their own unaided writing skill more than those who practiced without AI, because AI raises the quality of the learning environment through demonstration. The key is to "watch and learn" from AI output rather than passively accept it, treating each interaction as a coaching opportunity.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Grit]]** — [[Angela Duckworth]]'s framework: passion and perseverance for long-term goals, distinct from raw talent
- **Stubborn at the top, agile at the bottom** — fixed high-level goals but flexible day-to-day tactics, including AI usage strategies
- **[[Deliberate Practice]]** — [[Anders Ericsson]]'s research on learning curves; staying on a learning curve separates high achievers from the rest
- **AI as learning environment enhancer** — AI reduces effort but can simultaneously raise output quality as a demonstrative model, analogous to a great coach
- **[[John Wooden]]'s four-step model** — explanation, demonstration, imitation, repetition; AI can fulfill the demonstration step
- **Gen Z AI ambivalence** — 75% of Gen Z (18–28) used AI for productivity in the past month but worry it is a crutch eroding their own skills
- **[[Situational Agency]]** — designing your environment (coach, facility, teammates, culture) rather than relying solely on willpower, as illustrated by [[Michael Phelps]] and coach [[Bob Bowman]]

## Key Takeaways

- **Grit framework**: Be stubborn about end goals, agile about daily tactics and tools.
- **Cover-letter experiment**: AI-assisted writers' own skills improved more than unaided-practice group.
- **Mechanism**: AI raises learning-environment quality via demonstration, not just effort reduction.
- **Watch and learn**: Treat AI output as a coaching example, not a finished product to copy.
- **Delegation rule**: Offload tedious, low-learning-curve tasks to AI; protect time for high-level work.
- **Gen Z ambivalence**: 3 in 4 used AI recently; most worry it reduces sharpness over time.
- **Rational ambivalence**: Concerns about AI as a crutch reflect real accountability gaps and hallucination risk.
- **Situational agency**: Elite performance requires deliberately choosing environments and coaches, not just willpower.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Desirable Difficulty]]**: The cover-letter experiment shows that reducing effort via AI does not automatically harm learning — what matters is the quality of the feedback signal, not effort volume alone; harder practice is only better when the difficulty produces useful signal.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Gen Z's worry about AI dependency is a live example — once productivity becomes the measured output, optimizing for AI-generated results risks hollowing out the underlying skill that productivity was meant to proxy.
- **[[Environment Design]]**: Duckworth's point about Michael Phelps reframes grit from a purely internal trait to a systems property — elite outcomes emerge from selecting and sustaining the right environment, not from willpower in a vacuum.
