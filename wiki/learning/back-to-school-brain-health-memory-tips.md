## Summary

UAB neurobiologist Farah Lubin explains how neuroplasticity and the hippocampus underlie learning, how chronic stress disrupts the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus to impair concentration and recall, and offers evidence-based strategies — consistent sleep, retrieval practice, spaced practice, and stress management — for students to strengthen memory this school year.

## Core Concepts

- [[Farah Lubin]] — UAB neurobiology professor and source of the tips
- [[Neuroplasticity]] — the brain's capacity to change the strength and structure of neural connections when learning something new
- [[Hippocampus]] — brain structure that organizes experience and strengthens neural pathways for long-term retention
- [[Prefrontal Cortex]] — responsible for attention, planning, and decision-making; disrupted by chronic stress
- [[Retrieval Practice]] — testing rather than simply re-reading to strengthen memory
- [[Spaced Practice]] — reviewing material over days and weeks rather than cramming

## Key Takeaways

- **Learning is neuroplasticity**: New learning changes the strength and structure of neuronal connections.
- **Hippocampus drives retention**: Repeated use and practice strengthen the neural pathways it organizes.
- **Durable learning needs three things**: Active retrieval, connecting new material to prior knowledge, and revisiting content over time.
- **Chronic stress impairs two key systems**: It disrupts both the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus, hurting concentration and recall.
- **Retrieval and spacing are the top two techniques**: Lubin names testing (not re-reading) and reviewing over days/weeks as most effective.
- **Sleep supports nearly everything**: It aids learning, memory consolidation, attention, emotional regulation, and cognitive performance.
- **Cramming backfires**: It increases anxiety and reduces learning efficiency compared to regular study routines.
- **Simple stress levers work**: Exercise, consistent sleep, mindfulness, social support, and breaking tasks into steps all lower stress and raise cognitive performance.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core relationship between neuroplasticity, the hippocampus, and durable learning?
**A**: Learning triggers neuroplasticity — changes in neural connection strength — and the hippocampus organizes and strengthens these connections through repeated use, with durable learning requiring active retrieval, connection to prior knowledge, and revisiting material over time.

**Q2**: What two evidence-based study techniques does Lubin identify as most effective, and why?
**A**: Retrieval practice (testing rather than re-reading) and spaced practice (reviewing over days/weeks rather than cramming), because they align with how the brain naturally strengthens and consolidates memories.

**Q3**: How does chronic stress specifically impair a student's ability to learn?
**A**: It disrupts the prefrontal cortex (attention, planning, decision-making) and the hippocampus (memory formation), leading to difficulty concentrating, learning new information, or retrieving previously learned material.
