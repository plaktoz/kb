## Summary

The Postsecondary Teaching with Technology Collaborative developed a research-backed Self-Directed Learning (SDL) Instructional Model to help online and hybrid college courses compensate for reduced face-to-face contact. The model — self-directed learning videos, reflective prompts, and student-peer interaction/networking (SPIN) — was refined over five years of research and real-world piloting, and instructors reported it required minimal added workload since it mainly reframes assignments they already use.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Self-Directed Learning]] (SDL)**: A student's capacity to manage their own time, motivation, and study strategies with reduced instructor oversight — the core challenge online/hybrid courses create relative to in-person instruction.
- **[[SDL Instructional Model]]**: Three combinable strategies — self-directed learning videos, reflective prompts, and student-peer interaction and networking (SPIN) — targeting motivational, metacognitive, and applied learning processes.
- **[[Reframing vs. Adding]]**: The model's core implementation principle — instructors get value by reframing existing assignments (e.g., turning a standard reflection into a structured metacognitive prompt) rather than adding new coursework.
- **[[Course Implementation Guide]]**: A supporting resource bundle (pacing guides, slide decks, ready-to-use text) designed to lower the barrier to adopting the SDL model in existing courses.

## Key Takeaways

- **The model was tested and refined over five years of research**, including real-world pilot studies showing high student participation and actionable instructor insight.
- **Instructors can use student responses to adjust courses in real time**, since the model surfaces where students are struggling as the term progresses, not just at its end.
- **One pilot instructor called implementation "extremely easy"** — largely copy-and-paste from provided materials — and continued using it in subsequent semesters.
- **A student returning to college after years away** reported the strategies specifically helped her plan and manage time better in an online environment where she had previously felt disorganized.
- **The model doesn't require new assignments** — its main value is reframing existing course components to explicitly build planning, reflection, help-seeking, and growth-mindset skills.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What problem is the Self-Directed Learning Instructional Model designed to solve?
**A**: It helps online and hybrid college courses provide more deliberate support for the time-management, motivation, and study-strategy demands that reduced face-to-face contact places on students.

**Q2**: What are the three components of the SDL Instructional Model?
**A**: Self-directed learning videos, reflective prompts, and student-peer interaction and networking (SPIN) — used individually or together to reinforce motivational, metacognitive, and applied learning processes.

**Q3**: How does the model minimize added burden on instructors, according to the research?
**A**: Rather than requiring new assignments, it mainly reframes assignments instructors already use (e.g., existing reflections or check-ins), supported by a ready-to-use Course Implementation Guide with pacing guides and slide decks.
