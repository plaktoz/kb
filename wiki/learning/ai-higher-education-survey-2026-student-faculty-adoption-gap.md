## Summary

The [[Digital Education Council]]'s 2026 global survey of 45,398 respondents across 35 countries finds 88% of students now use AI in learning, but institutional readiness trails far behind: only 29% of students believe instructors can effectively guide them on AI, and just 28% feel their assessments reflect skills needed in an AI-enabled workplace. Faculty intent to teach with AI has dropped to its lowest point globally in the US and Canada, creating a widening readiness gap between learners and institutions.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Student AI Adoption]]**: 88% of students use AI in learning (2026); 77% of faculty use it in teaching — an increase of 16 percentage points from 2025, but adoption rates alone mask a guidance and assessment gap.
- **[[Faculty AI Readiness Gap]]**: 64% of faculty claim [[AI Literacy]] training, yet fewer than 3-in-10 students see results in their learning experience — revealing a disconnect between claimed and demonstrated competency.
- **[[Assessment Misalignment]]**: Only 28% of students feel assessments reflect AI-era workplace skills; 80% of employers (DEC 2025 workplace report) say higher education is not keeping up with industry change.
- **[[AI Dependence in Learning]]**: 22% of students find it harder to work without AI; 66% worry AI could make learning too shallow (rising to 81% in the US/Canada), signaling concern about skill atrophy.
- **[[Regional AI Divide in Higher Education]]**: Faculty intent to teach with AI is 92–94% in APAC and Latin America, but has fallen from 76% to 67% in the US and Canada — the lowest globally.

## Key Takeaways

- **88%** of students globally use AI in learning; **77%** of faculty use it in teaching.
- **57%** of students report inadequate AI guidance around assessments.
- Only **29%** of students believe instructors can effectively guide AI use.
- **22%** of students find it harder to work without AI — dependence is rising.
- **66%** worry AI makes learning too shallow; jumps to **81%** in the US/Canada.
- US/Canada faculty teaching-with-AI intent fell from 76% → 67%, lowest globally.
- Employer signal: **80%** say higher education is not keeping pace with industry AI change.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Institutions report AI literacy training completion rates, but student experience shows those metrics don't translate to effective guidance — measuring the credential, not the competency.
- **[[Capability–Expectation Gap]]**: The divergence between faculty self-reported AI literacy and student-perceived guidance quality illustrates how institutional readiness measures consistently lag behind learner reality, a structural lag pattern seen across technology transitions in education.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding of the DEC 2026 global higher education survey?

**Q2**: What specific data point illustrates the gap between faculty self-reported AI literacy and actual student impact?
**A**: 64% of faculty claim AI literacy training, yet fewer than 3-in-10 students see the results in practice — and employer data reinforces this, with 80% saying higher education is not keeping up with industry change.

**Q3**: How should an institution use these findings to prioritize its AI integration strategy?
**A**: Institutions should shift focus from adoption metrics to demonstrated impact — redesigning assessments to reflect AI-enabled workplace skills and investing in faculty development that produces visible student guidance, not just training completion certificates.
