## Summary

A Common Sense Media survey of 1,017 American teens (ages 13-17) finds roughly 7 in 10 use AI for academic support, and while most report it helps them understand schoolwork better, 39% also recognize they're missing out on learning by leaning on it — while 70% say their teachers have never discussed how to use AI safely.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Self-Aware Overreliance]]**: A large share of teens simultaneously use AI heavily and recognize it may be undermining their own learning — awareness of the tradeoff doesn't stop the behavior, similar to patterns seen with other convenience technologies.
- **[[Critical Thinking Sharpening Gap]]**: Common Sense Media's Yvette Renteria argues AI can help students solve problems faster but doesn't sharpen critical thinking and problem-solving skills the way working through ambiguity does.
- **[[Assessment Redesign]]**: Former Education Secretary John King argues schools need to rethink how they assess learning (blue-book exams, oral presentations, cold-calling) given AI's ubiquity, rather than only restricting AI access.

## Key Takeaways

- **Usage scale**: ~70% of teens use AI for academic support; 63% use it to get answers to schoolwork, 25% use those answers as-is.
- **Perceived cost**: 39% of teens feel they're missing out on learning when they use AI for an assignment.
- **Perceived benefit**: Two-thirds said AI helped them better understand what they were learning.
- **Safety education gap**: 70% of teens said their teachers had never discussed how to use AI safely.
- **Tool mix**: Most teens use general-purpose tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok); ~41% use AI writing-support tools like Grammarly.
- **Response**: Common Sense Media introduced a free high-school curriculum with AI lessons and educator professional development, beginning with younger students exploring how technology shapes human interaction before addressing AI chatbot relationships directly.
