## Summary

Whether AI erodes human skills depends on how it is used: when AI simply does the work, skills fade, but when it offers hints, feedback or examples that keep people thinking, learning can match or improve. Studies of doctors, high-school math students and SAT practice show declines once AI is removed, while a hint-based tutor and a cover-letter study show no loss. Researchers advise choosing carefully which skills to protect: "Outsource the crap, not the craft."

## Core Concepts

- **[[Cognitive Offloading]]**: Handing a specific skill to AI means you probably will not retain it well ([[Trent Cash]], University of Waterloo).
- **[[Use It or Lose It]]**: Skills come from practice and are maintained through practice.
- **Skill atrophy evidence**: A 2025 study of doctors using AI to spot colon polyps; a 2025 high-school math study; an SAT practice study presented at the 2026 Conference on Language Modeling.
- **AI as tutor vs. answer machine**: [[Alp Sungu]]'s hint-giving tool, which withheld answers, matched book study.
- **[[Desirable Difficulty]]**: The struggle of working through a new concept is where real learning happens.
- **Learning by example**: [[Benjamin Lira Luttges]] found AI-rewritten cover letters taught as well as human feedback when learners tinkered with them.

## Key Takeaways

- **Doctors**: After three months of AI help, polyp detection worsened when working alone.
- **Math students**: Students using ChatGPT-like tools did worse than non-users once the tool was removed.
- **SAT practice**: Users struggled without AI and were more likely to skip questions.
- **Hint-based tutor**: AI that withheld answers helped students learn as well as book study.
- **Cover letters**: AI and human feedback groups produced equally likely-to-interview letters.
- **Protect your craft**: Do not delegate skills you value or that give you meaning.
- **Resilience**: Cash dismisses fears that AI will turn brains to mush.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Skills persist only through practice** — A skill you stop exercising because a tool does it decays; retention requires engaging with the cognitive work yourself. · *model:* [[Use It or Lose It]]
→ explains: Doctors, Math students, SAT practice, Protect your craft

**T2. Effortful struggle is where learning happens** — Support that preserves the learner's effort (hints, examples) sustains learning, while support that removes the effort does not. · *model:* [[Desirable Difficulty]]
→ explains: Hint-based tutor, Cover letters

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What determines whether AI makes people lose skills?
**A**: How it is used: AI that does the work for you weakens skills, while AI that guides you or keeps you engaged can strengthen them.

**Q2**: What did Alp Sungu's math tutoring study show?
**A**: A ChatGPT-like tool that gave hints and encouragement instead of answers helped students learn as well as those who studied with books, since they still had to think.

**Q3**: How should people decide what to delegate to AI?
**A**: Decide which skills they want to protect, and do not delegate things they enjoy or that give them meaning: "Outsource the crap, not the craft."
