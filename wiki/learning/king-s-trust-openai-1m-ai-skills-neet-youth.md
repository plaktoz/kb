## Summary
The King's Trust and OpenAI launched a £1 million partnership to help up to 25,000 young people in the UK build AI skills for work over 18 months, prioritising those not in education, employment or training (NEET). It is part of a wider programme of nationally recognised AI qualifications The King's Trust will launch in the coming months.

## Core Concepts
- **[[The King's Trust]]**: UK youth charity marking its 50th year, having supported more than 1.3 million young people.
- **[[OpenAI]]**: donating £1m plus AI expertise; also runs [[OpenAI Academy]] with free learning resources.
- **[[NEET]]**: young people not in education, employment or training, the programme's priority group.
- **[[AI Skills Training]]**: practical skills combined with recognised qualifications and practical experience.
- **[[Alan Milburn]]**: welcomed the partnership ahead of the Young People and Work review's final report.
- **[[Jonathan Townsend]]** and **[[Tom Duff Gordon]]**: leaders of The King's Trust UK and OpenAI EMEA Policy respectively.

## Key Takeaways
- **Scale**: Up to 25,000 young people supported over the next 18 months.
- **Funding**: OpenAI donates £1 million plus AI expertise.
- **Demand**: 68% of surveyed NEET 16–24-year-olds would train or upskill (Open University Business Barometer 2026).
- **Qualifications**: Nationally recognised AI qualifications will launch in the coming months.
- **Employer Input**: The Trust is working with employers, AI specialists and young people to define needed skills.
- **Equity Framing**: Milburn: young people should get a fair share of opportunity, not bear disruption's cost.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core of the King's Trust and OpenAI partnership?
**A**: A £1 million OpenAI-backed programme to help up to 25,000 young people, prioritising NEET youth, build AI skills for work over 18 months. It sits within a wider set of nationally recognised AI qualifications.

**Q2**: What evidence of demand does the article cite?
**A**: The Open University's Business Barometer 2026 found 68% of surveyed NEET 16–24-year-olds would be willing to train or upskill to improve their job prospects.

**Q3**: What does the programme aim to ensure for young people in an AI-enabled economy?
**A**: That they gain practical skills, recognised qualifications and confidence to move into education and employment, and get a fair share of AI's opportunity without bearing the cost of disruption.
