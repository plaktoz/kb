## Summary

[[Carnegie Mellon University]] and [[Microsoft]] launched PRAISE (Preceptorship for AI-accelerated Software Engineering), a certificate program that lets early-career Microsoft engineers take CMU courses online while senior engineers mentor them at work. It grew out of [[Mark Russinovich]]'s observation that AI tools help senior engineers more than juniors. Organizers hope it becomes a template for leveling up new developers across the industry.

## Core Concepts

- **[[PRAISE Program]]**: Early-career Microsoft employees ("preceptees") pair with senior "preceptors" and take courses at CMU's Griffin School of Computer Science.
- **Curriculum**: Fall courses are Machine Learning in Production and Preceptorship in AI-Augmented Software Engineering I; spring adds DevOps: Engineering for Secure Development and Deployment and the second preceptorship course, credit toward a master's degree.
- **Junior-engineer gap**: Russinovich noted AI benefits senior engineers more; Michael Hilton adds that juniors once built expertise through tasks AI now absorbs.
- **Judgment about AI use**: Microsoft frames knowing when to use AI as ongoing education, combining formal curriculum with [[Mentorship]].
- **Work-integrated learning**: Participants study while working full-time rather than leaving the job.

## Key Takeaways

- **Apprenticeship Plus Academia**: Mentorship at work is paired with formal coursework.
- **Juniors Still Needed**: Russinovich says AI doesn't remove the need for junior engineers or accountability.
- **Credit Pathway**: Courses can count toward a CMU software engineering master's.
- **Expansion Hope**: CMU may add students and other industry partners.
- **Template Ambition**: Director Aldrich sees a model for leveling up developers industry-wide.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Expertise is built by doing progressively harder tasks, so automating entry-level tasks removes the ladder** — When AI absorbs the work juniors once used to build skill, organizations must replace it deliberately through mentorship and structured learning.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What problem is PRAISE designed to solve?
**A**: AI tools help senior engineers more than juniors, and early-career engineers no longer build expertise through the tasks AI now handles, so they need structured guidance.

**Q2**: How is the program structured?
**A**: Early-career Microsoft engineers work with senior mentors at the company while taking online CMU courses in machine learning and AI-augmented software engineering, then DevOps in spring.

**Q3**: What outcome do the organizers hope for?
**A**: CMU hopes to grow the program and add partners, and director Jonathan Aldrich expects it to become a template for rapidly leveling up new developers across the industry.
