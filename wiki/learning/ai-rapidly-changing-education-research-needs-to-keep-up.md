## Summary

Brookings argues that randomized controlled trials, the gold standard for education research, break down for fast-evolving AI tools, and proposes "implementation R&D" — rapid, iterative testing of specific design choices — as a faster, more relevant evidence path, noting only 1 in 5 edtech products currently have any evidence of improving outcomes. The piece outlines three principles: build evidence in stages, ask how a tool works before whether it works, and let research questions drive the method.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Randomized Controlled Trials]]** — assume stable interventions, a poor fit for fast-evolving AI tools
- **[[Implementation R&D]]** — iterative, real-time testing of specific AI-tool design choices
- **[[Brookings Institution]]** — Brown Center on Education Policy, publisher of this commentary
- **[[Institute of Education Sciences]]** — funds generative-AI R&D centers for iterative pilot testing

## Key Takeaways

- **Only 1 in 5 edtech products** have evidence of improving outcomes.
- **RCTs assume stable interventions**; AI tools change monthly.
- **Implementation R&D tests specific design choices** before full RCTs.
- **IES funds generative-AI R&D centers** for iterative pilot testing.
