## Summary

Brookings argues that [[Randomized Controlled Trials]], the gold standard for education research, break down for fast-evolving AI tools, and proposes "[[Implementation R&D]]" — rapid, iterative testing of specific design choices — as a faster, more relevant evidence path, noting only 1 in 5 edtech products currently have any evidence of improving outcomes. The piece outlines three principles: build evidence in stages, ask how a tool works before whether it works, and let research questions drive the method. States and districts cannot wait years for evidence on tool versions that no longer exist.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Randomized Controlled Trials]]** — assume stable interventions; a poor fit for fast-evolving AI tools
- **[[Implementation R&D]]** — iterative, real-time testing of specific AI-tool design choices before large-scale evaluation
- **[[Institute of Education Sciences]]** — funds generative-AI R&D centers for iterative pilot testing
- **[[A/B Testing]]** — continuous evaluation approach suited to dynamic AI tools; e.g., Teaching Lab compares standard vs. student-centered coaching prompts
- **[[Brookings Institution]]** — Brown Center on Education Policy, publisher of this commentary
- **[[Leanlab Education]]** / **[[Boston University EVAL]]** — partnerships supporting iterative tech testing in real classrooms

## Key Takeaways

- **Only 1 in 5 edtech products** have any evidence of improving outcomes.
- **RCTs assume stable interventions**; AI tools are updated constantly.
- **Implementation R&D** refines design, delivery, and usage before formal trials.
- **Principle 1 — Build evidence in stages**: feasibility first, large RCTs only once stable.
- **Principle 2 — Ask how before whether**: check behavioral changes before measuring achievement gains.
- **Principle 3 — Let questions drive method**: use A/B testing and continuous evaluation.
- **Decisionmakers can't wait**: a state investing in AI today needs evidence before the tool version changes.
- **RPPL Shared Measures Toolkit** — example of implementation R&D identifying what practitioners need to measure.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Chasing RCT evidence as the sole standard causes the field to optimize for study-readiness rather than actual student outcomes — exactly what leaves 4 in 5 edtech products unevaluated.
- **[[Chesterton's Fence]]**: Before discarding RCTs entirely, the authors make the case for understanding why RCTs exist and where they break down, then surgically replacing that role with implementation R&D rather than abandoning rigor altogether.
