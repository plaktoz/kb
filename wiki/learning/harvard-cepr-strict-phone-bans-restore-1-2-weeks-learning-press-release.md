## Summary
Harvard CEPR's press release reports the first national student-level analysis of strict phone bans, following 3.3 million students at 4,600 schools. Lockable-pouch bans raise achievement growth by about 0.01 standard deviations a year, restoring 1-2 weeks of learning, roughly half of the annual decline seen since 2013. The release also recommends using the Meta settlement to study out-of-school social media use.

## Core Concepts
- [[Center for Education Policy Research]] and [[Tom Kane]]: Lead the study with NWEA and Dartmouth researchers, including [[Douglas Staiger]].
- [[Cell Phone Bans in Schools]]: Compared achievement growth before and after strict bans, 2021-22 to 2025-26.
- [[Yondr Pouches]]: Lockable pouches that hold phones for the school day.
- [[NWEA MAP Growth]]: Data covering grades 3 to 10 across 49 states and DC.
- [[Bell-to-Bell Bans]]: State bans in 22 states in 2025-26 showed suggestive gains, with confounding caveats.
- [[Meta Settlement]]: Meta agreed to limit minors' access outside school hours, creating a chance to study social media effects.

## Key Takeaways
- **Historical Loss**: 8th graders lost about a grade equivalent from 2013 to 2024, 2-3 weeks yearly.
- **Pouch Effect**: About 0.01 SD yearly gain, equal to 1-2 weeks or two minutes per hour.
- **Consistency**: Effects did not differ significantly by school level or poverty level.
- **Reading Protection**: Pouches seemed to reduce the share of students with large reading drops.
- **State Bans**: Bell-to-bell bans: +0.016 SD reading; instructional-time bans: +0.019 SD math.
- **Caveat**: State-ban gains may be confounded by pre-existing trends.
- **Next Steps**: Extend restrictions beyond Meta, set baseline measures, track engagement and mental health.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Small per-period effects accumulate into large totals** — A modest annual gain or loss compounds over years; a "slow leak" of 2-3 weeks a year became almost a year behind, and 1-2 weeks a year could add up the same way. · *model:* [[Compounding]]
→ explains: Historical Loss, Pouch Effect

**T2. A raw difference cannot be credited to the intervention when other trends differ** — Researchers cautioned that state-ban gains might reflect pre-existing trends in states that adopted bans, so the evidence is only suggestive. · *model:* [[Confounding Variable]]
→ explains: State Bans, Caveat

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the study's central finding about strict phone bans?
**A**: Lockable-pouch bans raise achievement growth by about 0.01 standard deviations a year, restoring 1-2 weeks of learning, or about half of the annual decline since 2013.

**Q2**: What data and evidence support this finding?
**A**: The study follows 3.3 million students at 4,600 schools using NWEA MAP Growth data, comparing growth before and after bans. Gains begin in year one and appear to continue three years later.

**Q3**: What should states do next, according to the researchers?
**A**: Extend Meta-style restrictions to other platforms, assemble baseline social media use data, and track student engagement and mental health to learn if students benefit.
