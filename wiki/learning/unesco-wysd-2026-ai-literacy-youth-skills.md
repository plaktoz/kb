## Summary
UNESCO's World Youth Skills Day 2026 (WYSD2026) convened global stakeholders in New York around the theme "Skills for a Shared Future." The event emphasized that AI and digital literacy must be combined with creativity, critical thinking, and empathy — AI technical skills alone are insufficient for navigating an AI-driven future. UNESCO is calling for inclusive education systems that integrate youth voices into policy design.

## Core Concepts

- [[World Youth Skills Day 2026]] — UNESCO event marking WYSD2026; theme: "Skills for a Shared Future"
- [[AI Literacy]] — defined as the ability to access, evaluate, and apply information effectively in AI-integrated learning environments
- [[Human-Centred Skills]] — creativity, critical thinking, empathy — skills that complement AI literacy and cannot be automated
- [[Education beyond 2030]] — UNESCO's global consultation on future education frameworks
- [[Skills for Shared Future]] — framework emphasizing inclusive, resilient education systems that respond to change through youth participation

## Key Takeaways

- **AI literacy alone is not enough** — must combine with creativity, critical thinking, empathy for durable career value
- **Youth voice in policy** is a design requirement, not just a virtue — young people must shape education decisions affecting them
- **Global skills gap is widening**: AI is advancing faster than education systems can adapt
- **Inclusive framing**: equity and accessibility are preconditions for AI literacy to benefit all, not just the privileged
- **Connection to SDG 4**: AI solutions that advance Quality Education (SDG 4) while contributing to Innovation (SDG 9) and Reduced Inequalities (SDG 10)

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Complementarity Principle]]**: Human skills and AI capabilities are not substitutes — they are complements. The most valuable workers in an AI economy are those who pair AI fluency with distinctly human capacities, not those who try to out-AI the AI or avoid it entirely.
