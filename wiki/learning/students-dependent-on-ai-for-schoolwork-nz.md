## Summary

New Zealand's education watchdog is calling for a national AI framework after finding nearly 60% of teachers believe AI harms students' critical thinking, while students themselves openly describe becoming "dependent" on tools like ChatGPT for everyday schoolwork.

## Core Concepts

- [[Semantic Network Homogenization]] — Georgetown neuroscientist Adam Green's argument that AI-generated ideas route through a shared common network rather than a person's own life experience, making thinking "much more homogenised" over time.
- [[AI as Dictionary vs AI as Doer]] — the distinction teachers are drawing in practice: using AI like a reference tool (checking definitions) is acceptable, using it to generate the actual work is not.
- [[Soft-Touch AI Regulation]] — New Zealand's current approach of guidelines rather than binding rules, criticized for lacking guardrails, transparency and accountability.

## Key Takeaways

- **Teacher sentiment**: Nearly 60% of teachers say AI is negatively affecting students' critical thinking.
- **Student admission**: Students at Rongotai College directly describe relying on ChatGPT even for everyday, non-academic questions, not just assignments.
- **Hidden cost**: Teachers report spending up to two extra hours per assessment batch checking for AI-generated plagiarism.
- **Mixed policy response**: Some departments allow AI for research; others (e.g. English) require signed waivers banning its use entirely.
- **Regulatory gap flagged**: An AI ethics advisor argues NZ's guideline-only approach fails to provide accountability for AI companies.
