## Summary
Inside Higher Ed rounds up five recent findings on Gen Z college students: improving mental health, AI as a perceived safer source of emotional support, AI's mixed effect on college, economic concerns driving young voters, and early career exploration boosting confidence. Long-standing barriers such as mental health and financial pressure persist.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Healthy Minds Study]]**: 2025-26 survey of 67,477 students; [[Student Mental Health]] is improving.
- **[[The Rithm Project]]**: Research on young people using [[AI Emotional Support]].
- **[[Inside Higher Ed Student Voice Survey]]**: 5,034 undergraduates on [[Generative AI]] and academic barriers.
- **[[CIRCLE]]**: Data on young voters in the 2026 midterms, led by economic concerns.
- **[[Career Discovery]]**: [[Podium Education]]'s for-credit [[Career Exploration]] course.

## Key Takeaways
- **Flourishing Rise**: 43% of students flourishing, up from 38%; depression 34%, down from 37%.
- **AI as Safer Confidant**: Youth turn to AI because its risks feel more manageable than humans'.
- **AI in Coursework**: 34% say AI aids efficiency; 25% changed writing to avoid false AI flags.
- **Persistent Barriers**: Mental health (41%) and financial pressure (37%) top cited barriers.
- **Job Pipelines**: 54% want institutions to prioritize job pipelines and internships.
- **Economy First**: 52% of young voters named economy and jobs a top-three issue.
- **Career Course Results**: 80% reported greater career confidence; persistence intent up 15 points.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. People choose the option whose risk feels most manageable, not the one they trust most** — Confiding in AI persists despite known flaws because its risks (hallucination, flattery) feel lower than a human's judgment and emotional reactivity.
→ explains: AI as Safer Confidant

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the overall trend in college student mental health in the Healthy Minds Study?
**A**: It is improving: 43% of students are flourishing (up from 38%), and depression and anxiety symptoms declined slightly, though suicidal ideation and self-harm were largely unchanged.

**Q2**: Why do young people turn to AI for emotional support, per The Rithm Project?
**A**: They don't need to trust it; they just see its risks (hallucination, glazing) as more manageable than the fear of judgment and emotional reactivity from a human.

**Q3**: How might colleges help students build career confidence and persistence?
**A**: By introducing career exploration early, as in Podium's Career Discovery course, where 80% reported greater career confidence and intent to persist rose 15 percentage points.
