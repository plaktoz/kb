## Summary

Education journalist Holly Korbey compiled her back-to-school roundup of cognitive-science reporting for teachers, organized around four themes: core cognitive-science ideas (working memory, retrieval practice, explicit instruction), the "science of math," the "science of reading," and classroom-tested practices from her Teaching That Succeeds Symposium. The collection frames evidence-based teaching as an accessible, practical translation of cognitive research rather than abstract theory.

## Core Concepts

- [[Working Memory]] — flagged as the single most important cognitive-science concept for teachers to understand when designing instruction
- [[Retrieval Practice]] — the practice of recalling information from memory to strengthen long-term learning, discussed both for classroom and home use
- [[Explicit Instruction]] — direct, structured teaching methods contrasted with more open-ended "productive struggle" approaches that are falling out of favor in some schools
- [[Science of Reading]] — an evidence-based movement covering phonics instruction, reading comprehension, and how much time students actually spend reading whole books

## Key Takeaways

- **Working memory first**: Understanding working memory limits is presented as foundational for effective teaching.
- **Productive struggle debate**: Some teachers, schools, and states are rolling back "productive struggle" math instruction in favor of more explicit methods.
- **Math instructional hierarchy**: An evidence-based sequence for progressing students through new math skills is highlighted as underused.
- **Reading gap**: A cited survey found students read few or no whole books at school, fueling the "phonics 2.0" (linguistic phonics) movement.
- **Practice over theory**: The Teaching That Succeeds Symposium translated all this research into 18 classroom-tested, teacher-shared techniques.
- **Phonics balance**: New research cited finds some classrooms over-teach phonics decoding at the expense of "real reading" time.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core organizing claim of this roundup?
**A**: That a body of cognitive-science and science-of-reading research — covering working memory, retrieval practice, explicit instruction, and reading pedagogy — can be translated into practical, evidence-based classroom techniques for the new school year.

**Q2**: What specific pedagogical debate does the roundup highlight around math instruction?
**A**: It highlights that "productive struggle" (open-ended problem-solving) is falling out of favor with some teachers, schools, and states in favor of more explicit, evidence-based instructional-hierarchy approaches.

**Q3**: What practical event does the author point to for turning this research into classroom practice?
**A**: The Teaching That Succeeds Symposium, where K-12 teachers across subjects shared 18 classroom-tested routines and techniques built on cognitive-science research, all available free on YouTube.
