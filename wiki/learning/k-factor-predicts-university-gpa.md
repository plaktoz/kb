## Summary
A study of 272 undergraduates found that the K-factor — a life-history-theory measure of future-oriented, socially-connected, long-term strategy — predicts university GPA beyond SAT scores, Big Five personality traits, and parent education. Higher Mini-K scores correlated with higher conscientiousness, extraversion, and agreeableness, and lower neuroticism, yet still explained unique variance in GPA after controlling for those traits. The cross-sectional design and single Mini-K measure limit claims about the K-factor's predictive validity over time.

## Core Concepts
- **[[K-factor]]** — psychometric marker of life-history strategy that predicts university GPA beyond established predictors.
- **[[Life History Theory]]** — framework for how people allocate limited time and energy across competing demands like learning, relationships, and immediate rewards.
- **[[Mini-K Scale]]** — 20-item measure used to assess the K-factor.
- **[[Big Five Personality Traits]]** — openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism, measured via the 120-item IPIP-NEO scale.
- **[[SAT]]** — standardized test score used as the established comparison predictor of GPA.

## Key Takeaways
- **Higher Mini-K scores** were weakly but significantly linked to higher GPA.
- **Mini-K predicted GPA** even after controlling for SAT scores and Big Five traits.
- **Parent education** lost significance once SAT and Mini-K were included together.
- **Mini-K correlated** with higher conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, and lower neuroticism.
- **Study design was cross-sectional**, limiting claims about long-term predictive validity.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the K-factor, and what does the study find it predicts?
**A**: The K-factor is a life-history-theory psychometric measure of future-oriented, long-term life strategy; the study found it predicts university GPA beyond SAT scores, personality, and parent education.

**Q2**: How did Mini-K scores relate to the Big Five personality traits?
**A**: Higher Mini-K scores were associated with higher conscientiousness, extraversion, and agreeableness, and lower neuroticism, with no meaningful relationship to openness.

**Q3**: What limitation prevents the study from establishing that the K-factor predicts long-term academic success?
**A**: The design was cross-sectional — personality and Mini-K scores were collected around the same time as GPA — so it cannot establish predictive validity over time.
