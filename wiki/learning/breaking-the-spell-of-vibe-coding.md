## Summary

Vibe coding — generating AI code without intending to review or understand it — triggers an illusion of productivity structurally similar to gambling: intermittent reinforcement, perception-reality gaps, and halted skill development. A METR study found developers using AI believed they were 20% faster but were actually 19% slower — a 40-point perception gap. The deeper risk is that speculative AI forecasts are causing people to stop developing expertise they will still need.

## Core Concepts

- [[Vibe Coding]] — generating large amounts of AI-written code not intended for human review or understanding
- [[Flow State]] (Csikszentmihalyi) — requires skill-matched challenges and clear feedback; vibe coding undermines both
- [[METR Study]] — AI users believed +20% faster; actual result was -19% speed — ~40% perception-reality gap
- [[Losses Disguised as Wins]] — gambling mechanism applied to AI coding: false progress signals
- [[Skill Atrophy]] — halting upskilling based on speculative AI forecasts
- [[Rachel Thomas]] — fast.ai; [[Jeremy Howard]] — fast.ai founder; cited warning about outsourcing thinking to AI
- [[Geoffrey Hinton]] / [[Dario Amodei]] — overconfident predictions used as cautionary examples

## Key Takeaways

- **METR finding**: AI users 19% slower despite feeling 20% faster — confidence and reality inverted.
- **Gambling parallel**: Variable reward schedules make vibe coding feel productive while degrading actual output.
- **Predictions fail**: Hinton's radiology prediction (2021), Amodei's "90% of code" prediction (2025) — neither materialized.
- **Skill halt is the real risk**: People stopping development based on speculative timelines they've miscalibrated.
- **AI handles syntax, not engineering**: Grammar vs. thinking — the gap is judgment, architecture, and creativity.
- **Jeremy Howard**: Those who outsource all thinking risk "stopping upskilling, learning, and becoming more competent."
