## Summary

SP Jain School of Global Management built a proprietary AI platform with an AI Tutor for pre-class learning and an AI Mentor for research projects. By moving information delivery to AI, class time shifts to case discussion, judgment under ambiguity, and decision-making. The author argues schools should design around capability development, not content delivery.

## Core Concepts

- **[[SP Jain School of Global Management]]**: Business school with campuses in Singapore, Dubai, Mumbai, and Sydney that built the platform in-house.
- **[[AI Tutor]]**: Faculty-curated pre-class content that builds foundational understanding before sessions.
- **[[AI Mentor]]**: Thinking partner that helps students frame research questions and surface theories and gaps.
- **[[Futures-Oriented Pedagogy]]**: Teaching for adaptability, since specific technical skills are depreciating.
- **[[Case-Based Learning]]**: Classroom model emphasizing defending positions, testing assumptions, and revising thinking.
- **[[Learning Agility]]**: Capacity to navigate ambiguity, learn continuously, and decide with incomplete information.
- **[[AI Fluency]]**: Directing, interrogating, and critically evaluating AI systems.

## Key Takeaways

- **Flipped Time**: AI Tutor builds foundations beforehand, freeing class time for application and discussion.
- **Active Use**: Students must formulate prompts, evaluate responses, and decide what to pursue.
- **Better Questions**: Students now ask when a concept fails to apply, not just what it means.
- **Research Framing**: AI Mentor maps theories and gaps; students arrive with developed ideas.
- **AI Doesn't Do Research**: It supplies conceptual vocabulary and confidence, not the inquiry itself.
- **Depreciating Skills**: Specific technical skills lose value; the capacity to learn compounds.
- **Four Principles**: Capabilities over competencies, AI for knowledge transfer, research as practice, AI-fluent graduates.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central argument about AI in business education?
**A**: Schools should use AI to handle content delivery so human instructors can build capabilities such as navigating ambiguity, learning continuously, and making sound decisions. It frames AI as making space for a better kind of education, not replacing it.

**Q2**: How do the AI Tutor and AI Mentor work at SP Jain?
**A**: The AI Tutor delivers faculty-curated pre-class content so students arrive with foundational understanding. The AI Mentor acts as a thinking partner in research, mapping relevant theories and gaps so students frame precise questions.

**Q3**: What four principles does the author suggest other institutions adopt?
**A**: Focus on capabilities not just competencies, let AI handle what it does well, make research a capability-building exercise, and graduate AI-fluent professionals. The goal is graduates whose skills grow in value over time.
