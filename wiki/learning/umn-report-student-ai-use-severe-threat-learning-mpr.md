## Summary

MPR News reports on a University of Minnesota expert survey finding little high-quality research on AI and learning, but wide agreement that careless use of general-purpose GenAI could cause "severe and one-sided" disruption to learning, especially in elementary school. Lead author Panayiota Kendeou advises letting students build skills independently first, and introducing AI only once foundational skills exist. The report names cognitive thinking, learning skills, collaboration, social-emotional development, and human connection as at risk.

## Core Concepts

- [[Generative AI]]: general-purpose tools whose casual use is said to threaten memory formation and higher-order thinking.
- [[Panayiota Kendeou]]: University of Minnesota educational psychologist and lead report author.
- [[Higher-Order Thinking]]: needed to understand and use ideas in novel ways; flagged as at risk.
- [[Foundational Skills]]: writing and critical thinking that students should build independently before using AI.
- [[Student Agency]]: preserved by letting students work independently first.
- [[Social-Emotional Development]]: listed with collaboration and human connection among at-risk areas.
- [[Learning Science Evidence Base]]: experts found a dearth of high-quality research on AI's effect on learning.

## Key Takeaways

- **Core Warning**: Experts agreed careless GenAI use could cause "severe and one-sided" learning disruption.
- **Evidence Gap**: High-quality research on AI's effect on learning is scarce.
- **Primary Grades**: Experts urged extra caution for elementary students building basic skills.
- **Sequencing**: Students should write independently before using a tool that writes for them.
- **Helpful Later**: Skilled students can use GenAI to stress-test ideas and find resources.
- **Teacher Stance**: Withhold access if the goal is skill development; grant it after skills form.
- **At-Risk Areas**: Cognitive thinking, learning skills, collaboration, social-emotional growth, human connection.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Skills form only through doing the work yourself** — When a tool performs the task before the learner has practiced it, the learner never builds the skill the task was meant to develop. · *model:* [[Cognitive Offloading]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the University of Minnesota report's central warning about student AI use?
**A**: Casual, careless use of general-purpose GenAI could severely and one-sidedly disrupt the learning processes needed to commit ideas to memory and to think at a higher order, especially for elementary students.

**Q2**: What did the expert survey find about the research base?
**A**: Experts found a dearth of high-quality research on how AI affects learning, yet still reached wide consensus on the risk of disruption in certain contexts.

**Q3**: When should teachers give students access to GenAI?
**A**: Only after students have developed foundational skills such as writing and critical thinking; then AI can help stress-test ideas, expand arguments, and find resources.
