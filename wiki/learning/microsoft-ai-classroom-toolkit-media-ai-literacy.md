## Summary
Microsoft and Cyberlite launched an expanded AI Classroom Toolkit focused on critical thinking, verification, and media and AI literacy (MAIL). Scenario-based lessons teach students to judge AI outputs by claims and sources rather than by whether AI made them. The effort sits alongside product design, policy, and a growing global focus on MAIL.

## Core Concepts
- [[Media and AI Literacy]] (MAIL): the judgment to decide whether and how to use AI and what information to trust.
- [[AI Classroom Toolkit]]: built with [[Cyberlite]], including the Climate Campaign and Helpful Bot lessons and the "What Kind of AI User Are You?" quiz.
- [[Lateral Reading]]: practiced in sorting real and AI-made posts.
- [[OECD]] MAIL assessment in [[PISA]] from 2029, and [[UNESCO]]'s "Think Critically, Click Wisely" AI extension.
- Microsoft's five principles for AI in education, including AI that supports learning rather than replacing thinking.
- Related tools: [[Minecraft Education]], [[Microsoft Teams for Education]] Search Progress, [[Digital Inquiry Group]].

## Key Takeaways
- **Trust Criterion**: AI origin doesn't decide trustworthiness; claims and who is behind them do.
- **Training Gap**: 53% of educators and 77% of students report no AI training.
- **Toolkit Reach**: 10 countries and 11 languages since 2023.
- **South Korea Results**: 93% of participating educators reported improved understanding of AI ethics, privacy, and bias.
- **Learn by Doing**: Young people want games, scenarios, and peer discussion.
- **Societal Stakes**: Eroding authenticity risks "permanent uncertainty" about everything.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Trustworthiness is judged by claims and provenance, not production method** — Whether content is credible depends on what it asserts and who stands behind it, so verification means tracing claims to reliable sources.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central argument of the article?
**A**: As AI makes authenticity harder to judge, media and AI literacy is a foundational skill educators need help teaching, and the expanded toolkit supports that.

**Q2**: What does the Climate Campaign lesson teach?
**A**: Students sort real and AI-made posts and practice lateral reading, learning that AI use doesn't decide trustworthiness; what a post claims and who is behind it does.

**Q3**: Why is the need for such resources urgent?
**A**: 53% of educators and 77% of students say they haven't had AI training, while the OECD will assess MAIL in PISA from 2029.
