## Summary

The University of Pennsylvania Graduate School of Education is launching the Penn GSE Living Laboratory for Human Agency and AI, a network hub for evidence-based innovation in AI and education. Its central argument is that educators should shape how AI develops and gets used in education, not respond to it after the fact. The Living Lab will co-design and test AI tools in real classrooms, build AI literacy, and study safe and responsible use, funded by three years of philanthropic support.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Penn GSE Living Laboratory for Human Agency and AI]]**: Set up as a network rather than a standalone center. It links researchers, educators, school systems, and industry partners.
- **Leadership**: Dean [[Katharine O. Strunk]] and founding co-directors [[Bodong Chen]] and [[Seiji Isotani]].
- **Educators as designers**: Chen argues that educators and students should be the *designers* of [[AI in Education]], not just its users, and should build evidence of what their designs make possible.
- **Five priorities**: People First ([[Human Agency]]); Trust, Safety, and Ethics; Practice-Driven Innovation; [[AI Literacy]] and Pedagogy; Infrastructure for Scale.
- **Existing portfolio**:
  - [[CORAL]], Shiyan Jiang's project in which young children design humanoid robots.
  - [[CraftPad]], Chen's AI-assisted workspace for teachers' lesson design.
  - Yasmin Kafai's [[Algorithm Auditing]] project, which teaches high schoolers to investigate [[AI Bias]].
- **Supporting programs**: The Learning Analytics and Artificial Intelligence master's program, and the three-state PASS initiative on AI governance, equity, and safe deployment in schools.
- **Grounding in learning science**: Isotani stresses that AI-enabled personalization at scale must be grounded in [[Learning Sciences]] and [[Learning Analytics]].
- **Possible projects**: Evaluating [[AI Tutoring]] tools, building responsible-use resources for educators, and testing AI literacy strategies. A speaker series supported by Google Public Sector will also run.

## Key Takeaways

- **Proactive stance**: Educators should shape AI, not react to it after the fact.
- **Network model**: The Living Lab is a collaborative hub, not a standalone research center.
- **Guidance gap**: Educators experiment with AI daily without clear evidence of what works.
- **Designer role**: Students and teachers should design AI in learning, not merely use it.
- **Five priorities**: Human agency, safety/ethics, co-design, AI literacy, and scalable infrastructure.
- **Funding**: Three years of philanthropic support for research and school partnerships.
- **Bias education**: High schoolers learn to audit algorithms for bias.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central premise behind Penn GSE's Living Lab?
**A**: Education systems are too often asked to respond to technological change after the fact. The education community should instead actively shape how AI is developed and used in learning.

**Q2**: What are the Living Lab's five priorities?
**A**: People First (human agency), Trust, Safety, and Ethics, Practice-Driven Innovation, AI Literacy and Pedagogy, and Infrastructure for Scale.

**Q3**: How does the Living Lab plan to make AI in education evidence-based in practice?
**A**: It will co-design and test AI tools in real educational settings, evaluate AI tutoring tools, and study AI's impact on teaching. Educators and students act as designers who build evidence of what their designs make possible.
