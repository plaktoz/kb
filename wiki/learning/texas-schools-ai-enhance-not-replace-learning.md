## Summary
Texas school districts range from restricting AI for younger students to launching AI-focused campuses, but leaders share the goal of having AI enhance rather than replace student thinking. The key test is whether students are improving a skill or just producing a better finished product. A Frisco ISD parent built a study aid that guides students rather than giving answers.

## Core Concepts
- [[Aroutis Foster]] (American University) frames the issue as skill development versus polished output.
- [[Dallas ISD]]: bars AI for elementary and middle school; embeds it in high school curriculum for research and study but not for completing tests.
- [[Houston ISD]] "Future 2" model: expanding to 25 more campuses, including a Foundations of AI course.
- Restrictions in [[New York City Public Schools]] and [[Los Angeles Unified School District]] while impact is studied.
- [[LemonSugar Ai]]: a study companion by Atheeth Sishtla that breaks concepts down and quizzes instead of answering.
- [[Cognitive Offloading]] versus AI as a learning aid.

## Key Takeaways
- **Learning Test**: "Better at the skill, or a better finished product?"
- **Parent Adoption**: Nearly three-quarters of parents say their children use AI occasionally.
- **Parent Concerns**: Academic integrity and lost critical thinking worry most parents; nearly nine in 10 still see reasons for optimism.
- **District Split**: Cautious Dallas ISD versus expansive Houston ISD.
- **Math Example**: AI hints after an attempt teach; AI-generated solutions teach nothing.
- **Guardrails**: Without them, a generation risks only surface-level knowledge.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Learning requires the learner to do the cognitive work** — A tool builds skill only when it supports the learner's own reasoning; when it supplies the answer, the output improves but learning does not. · *model:* [[Cognitive Offloading]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core distinction schools must make about AI use?
**A**: Whether students are getting better at a skill or merely producing a better finished product, since a better product does not mean learning occurred.

**Q2**: How does Foster's math example show AI helping versus hurting learning?
**A**: If a student attempts a problem and AI shows where the reasoning went wrong or offers a hint, they learn to solve it themselves. If AI simply generates the solution, the student turns in a correct answer but learns nothing.

**Q3**: How does LemonSugar Ai put this into practice?
**A**: Instead of answering, it breaks the concept into pieces, asks guiding questions such as naming the three branches of government, and can generate a quiz to reinforce learning.
