## Summary

[[Charla D. Chailland]], a former elementary and reading teacher turned assistant professor, found her lecture-based college teaching drew weak student evaluations. She rebuilt her courses on the [[Gradual Release of Responsibility]] model ("I do, we do, you do") and treated engagement as her job, and her ratings improved from near 4 to near 1 on a five-point scale where 1 is strongest agreement. She argues the human conditions of learning vary little between kindergarten and college, so elementary pedagogy deserves respect in higher education.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Gradual Release of Responsibility]]** — model, practice together, then release to the learner while watching
- **[[Teacher Responsibility for Engagement]]** — engagement belongs to the teacher, not the student's motivation
- **[[Formative Assessment]]** — regular informal checks for understanding instead of waiting for a high-stakes exam
- **[[Lecture Model in Higher Education]]** — information is retrievable in seconds, so the lecture has lost its monopoly
- **[[Elementary Pedagogy in Higher Education]]** — explicit instruction, scaffolding and relationship-building as faculty-development assets
- **[[Student Belonging]]** — the same questions at age 5 and 20: Am I smart? Do I belong?

## Key Takeaways

- **Wrong Assumption**: She expected 2004 students in a 2024 classroom and was wrong in nearly every particular.
- **Measured Change**: Ratings on key items moved from close to 4 to near 1.
- **Workshop Format**: Class time became a workshop with visible expectations and frequent informal checks.
- **Engagement Is Teacher's Job**: When the room goes flat, ask what got in the way.
- **Practice Competence**: She stopped performing competence and practiced it visibly, owning mistakes with humor.
- **Attention Is Earned**: A lecturer now competes for attention rather than commanding it.
- **Transfer**: A chemistry professor adopted a discussion activity from her early literacy course.
- **Faculty Development**: Treat faculty with elementary backgrounds as carriers of pedagogical knowledge.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Attention is earned by the teaching, not granted by credential** — Learners of any age give attention when instruction earns it, so responsibility for engagement sits with the teacher.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Chailland's central claim about kindergarten and college teaching?
**A**: The human conditions of learning vary little across ages, so deliberate, scaffolded pedagogy built for young children serves college students just as well.

**Q2**: What specific changes did she make, and what evidence shows they worked?
**A**: She rebuilt courses on "I do, we do, you do", with visible expectations, regular informal checks, and a workshop format. Ratings on key items moved from close to 4 to near 1 on a scale where 1 is strongest agreement.

**Q3**: What does she say colleges should do with faculty who arrive from elementary classrooms?
**A**: Treat them as carriers of pedagogical knowledge, not professors-in-training. Their explicit instruction, scaffolding, formative checking and relationship-building are what teaching scholarship keeps recommending.
