## Summary

HBR argues that "speed to skill" — how fast individuals and organizations learn, apply, and iterate on new capabilities — has replaced steady persistence as the defining competitive advantage, citing Google's DORA metrics and OpenAI's near-100% A/B-tested launches as examples of institutionalized learning velocity. Unilever's internal talent marketplace shows how integrating learning, doing, and performing compounds skill acquisition at scale.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Speed to Skill]]** — how quickly individuals and organizations learn, adapt, and apply capabilities
- **[[Google]]** — tracks learning speed via its DORA DevOps framework
- **[[OpenAI]]** — treats nearly every launch as an A/B-tested learning opportunity
- **[[Unilever]]** — internal talent marketplace lets employees apply new skills within weeks
- **[[World Economic Forum]]** — predicts 44% of core skills disrupted by 2027

## Key Takeaways

- **WEF predicts 44% of workers' core skills** disrupted by 2027.
- **55% of organizations** rank gen-AI adoption a top 2026 priority.
- **Google tracks learning speed** via its DORA DevOps framework.
- **Unilever's talent marketplace** lets employees test new skills within weeks.
