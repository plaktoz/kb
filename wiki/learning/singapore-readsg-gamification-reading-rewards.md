## Summary

Singapore's [[National Library Board]] launched **ReadSG** on September 6, 2026 — a five-year pilot that converts daily logged reading time into small cash rewards via GovTech's [[CrowdTaskSG]] platform. The program is explicitly designed as a behavioral nudge rather than an income stream, with 50 consecutive days of participation yielding roughly S$1. A parallel communal track targets 7.5 million cumulative reading minutes to unlock up to S$150,000 in charitable donations.

## Core Concepts

- **[[ReadSG]]**: Singapore's national reading incentive pilot; logs 15-minute daily sessions as virtual coins redeemable for small cash payouts
- **[[Gamification]]**: Converting reading time into coins and communal milestones to sustain engagement
- **[[Micropayments]]**: Small monetary rewards (S$0.02/session) used not as income but as commitment devices
- **[[Behavioral Nudge]]**: Policy design that steers behavior through incentives rather than mandates — a concept from [[Nudge Theory]]
- **[[National Reading Movement]]**: Singapore's 2016 predecessor initiative that ReadSG formally extends
- **[[CrowdTaskSG]]**: GovTech platform used to log and reward civic participation

## Key Takeaways

- **Earning rate**: 15 min of reading = 20 coins; 1,000 coins = S$1 (~S$0.02/session)
- **50 consecutive days** of sessions yields approximately S$1
- **Communal goal**: 7.5M cumulative reading minutes → S$150,000 in charitable donations
- **NLB framing**: Describes the payout as "a behavioral nudge rather than an income stream"
- **Five-year pilot**: Program refinements planned based on user feedback
- **Global signal**: Governments watching whether gamification + micropayments can shift screen habits at population scale
- **Policy data upside**: Behavioral data collected may inform future civic design globally

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Commitment Device]]**: By attaching a tiny but real monetary reward, ReadSG exploits loss aversion and status quo bias — users who start a streak feel the cost of breaking it, not just the gain of continuing
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Measuring reading time rather than comprehension or habit durability risks participants optimizing for logged minutes over genuine engagement — a tension the five-year pilot will stress-test
