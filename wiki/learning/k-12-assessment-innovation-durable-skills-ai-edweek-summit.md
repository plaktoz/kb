## Summary

At the EdWeek Market Brief Summit, panelists said K-12 assessment is shifting toward frequent feedback, real-world skills and closer ties to instruction, with AI seen as a way to ease the burden of evaluating student work. Districts in North Carolina and Florida described pilots and tensions around instructional time. Panelists cautioned that not every durable skill should be measured and that quality and comparability must be preserved.

## Core Concepts

- [[K-12 Assessment Innovation]]: Moving beyond the traditional testing model toward more meaningful measures.
- [[Durable Skills]]: Communication, collaboration and problem-solving, measured alongside academic subjects.
- [[AI-Assisted Grading]]: An AI tool does an initial rubric check that teachers can review and override.
- [[Lillian Pace]]: KnowledgeWorks policy leader who sees a shift from polarized debate to seeking alternatives.
- [[James Worthington]]: Iredell-Statesville Schools leader piloting classroom-based durable-skill assessment with ETS.
- [[Omar Riaz]]: Miami-Dade assessment leader balancing testing volume with instructional time.
- [[Interim and Diagnostic Testing]]: Being reconsidered as Florida moves to three state assessments a year.
- [[Assessment Comparability]]: Needed as assessment systems become less centralized.

## Key Takeaways

- **Policy Interest**: 24 states applied for a federal assessment innovation grant funding three or four.
- **Vendor Model**: Grant-funded co-development fits changing needs better than restrictive RFPs.
- **Classroom Pilot**: A Shakespeare puppet show assessed collaboration in a district of 20,000+ students.
- **AI Role**: AI takes the bulk of review work off teachers while they keep final say.
- **Instructional Time**: Miami-Dade, with 300,000+ students, must protect time amid more assessments.
- **Limits**: Empathy is hard to assess consistently across cultures and communities.
- **Standardization Balance**: Neither overly standardized nor so loose it becomes meaningless.

## 🧠 Core Principles

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central shift in K-12 assessment described at the summit?
**A**: Assessment is moving from a traditional model toward frequent feedback, real-world skills and closer links to instruction. The debate has shifted from whether standardized assessment should exist to how it can better serve students and educators.

**Q2**: How does the Iredell-Statesville pilot assess durable skills?
**A**: Teachers redesigned classroom projects, such as a Shakespeare puppet show, to evaluate skills like collaboration. An AI tool does an initial check using teacher-developed rubrics, and teachers can review and override it.

**Q3**: What caution do panelists raise about expanding this work?
**A**: Not every durable skill should be measured, since empathy is hard to assess consistently across cultures. Systems also need to preserve quality and comparability without being overly standardized.
