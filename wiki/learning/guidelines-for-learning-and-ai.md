## Summary

[[Scott H. Young]] argues that AI presents both opportunity and serious risk for learners, particularly through over-dependency that bypasses the productive struggle required for skill development. Drawing on the autopilot analogy, he proposes five tentative guidelines governing when AI assistance helps versus harms learning. His framework centers on a See → Do → Feedback loop where AI can support perception and feedback steps but cannot replace the doing.

## Core Concepts

- **Autopilot analogy** — over-reliance on automation is dangerous for novices who haven't yet built foundational competence; from [[Carl Hendrick]]'s essay
- **[[Productive Struggle]]** — core mechanism of skill acquisition from Young's 2019 book [[Ultralearning]]; AI that removes struggle removes learning
- **[[Cognitive Load Theory]]** — excessive difficulty can slow learning; timely AI help can reduce friction at the right moments (acknowledged in Young's 2024 book [[Get Better at Anything]])
- **See → Do → Feedback loop** — AI can legitimately assist the See and Feedback stages; the Do stage must remain unaided
- **Five AI-learning guidelines** — heuristics for calibrating AI use across novice and expert contexts

## Key Takeaways

- AI completing tasks entirely produces zero skill growth.
- Don't use AI on your first attempt — preserves judgment formation.
- Don't use AI on your last attempt — must follow AI feedback with unaided retry.
- Do use AI to surface overlooked alternatives and breadth.
- Do use AI post-attempt to clarify confusion, like expert review.
- Learning = See → Do → Feedback; AI aids steps 1 and 3 only.
- Optimal AI assistance level depends on learner stage and task type.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Desirable Difficulty]]**: Introducing the right amount of friction strengthens retention and transfer — Young's guidelines are essentially a map of which AI shortcuts destroy desirable difficulty and which preserve it.
- **[[Cognitive Load Theory]]**: Not all friction is productive; excessive load impedes novice learning, which is why AI clarification post-attempt can accelerate progress without bypassing skill formation.
