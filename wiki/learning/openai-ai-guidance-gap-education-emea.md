## Summary
OpenAI argues that as AI grows more capable, teachers matter more, and that education systems must close a "guidance gap" between student AI use and instruction on how to use it. Drawing on ThinkYoung research and partnerships in Estonia, Slovakia, Greece and Italy, it reports early teacher time savings but says the effect on student learning still needs sustained evaluation.

## Core Concepts
- [[OpenAI]]: authors of the report and partner to EMEA governments and education systems.
- [[ThinkYoung]]: research body whose survey exposed the student guidance gap.
- [[AI Literacy]]: jointly designed programmes are one of three partnership priorities.
- [[Educator Preparation]]: investing in teachers' skills and confidence is a core lesson.
- [[Andreas Schleicher]] and the [[OECD]]: PISA and AI debates frame the reassessment of education goals.
- [[OpenAI Learning Lab]]: brings education researchers together to study when AI supports education.
- Country partnerships in [[Estonia]], [[Slovakia]], [[Greece]] and [[Italy]].

## Key Takeaways
- **Guidance Gap**: 79% of surveyed students use AI for homework; only 47% were shown how.
- **Teacher Role**: As AI becomes more capable, teachers' judgement matters more, not less.
- **Partnership Priorities**: Equitable access, joint AI literacy programmes, and ongoing measurement and evaluation.
- **Country Lessons**: Public leadership, teacher investment, and support tailored to local curricula.
- **Early Benefits**: Teacher adoption and reported time savings are encouraging signals.
- **Open Question**: Whether AI strengthens knowledge, independent thinking and retention remains unproven.

## 🧠 Core Principles

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central argument of the report?
**A**: As AI becomes more capable, teachers become more important, so education systems must equip educators and give students clear expectations to close the AI guidance gap.

**Q2**: What data point illustrates the guidance gap?
**A**: ThinkYoung found 79% of surveyed students use AI for homework or assignments, but only 47% reported being shown how.

**Q3**: How should governments apply the lessons from the EMEA partnerships?
**A**: Set clear public direction, invest in teachers' skills and confidence, tailor support to local curricula, and fund sustained evaluation of whether AI improves learning.
