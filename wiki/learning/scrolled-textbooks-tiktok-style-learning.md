## Summary

Palo Alto startup [[ScrollEd]] is pitching at TechCrunch Disrupt 2026 with an app that converts textbooks and PDFs into a scrollable, TikTok-style vertical feed featuring AI-generated video, audio, text, and interactive quizzes. Founders [[Utsav Gupta]] and [[Rebecca Neff]] built it to address the tension between younger users' preference for short-form content and the depth required for genuine learning. The broader vision is a social network that is "better for you."

## Core Concepts

- **[[ScrollEd]]**: EdTech app that transforms uploaded PDFs/text files into swipeable, vertical learning feeds with AI-generated multimedia content and quizzes.
- **[[Short-Form Learning]]**: Pedagogical approach adapting the TikTok-style UX (vertical scroll, swipe gestures) to structured educational material.
- **[[AI-Generated Educational Content]]**: [[ScrollEd]] uses AI to produce video, audio, and text summaries from source documents, lowering the production barrier for interactive lessons.
- **[[Doomscrolling]] as Design Inspiration**: Founders pivoted dissatisfaction with passive doomscrolling into a product that channels the same UX compulsion toward learning.
- **[[Adaptive Learning Infrastructure]]**: Roadmap item — future personalization layer that tailors content to learner progress and behavior.
- **[[TechCrunch Disrupt]] Startup Battlefield 200**: Annual pitch competition; [[ScrollEd]]'s consumer launch is timed to the October 2026 event in San Francisco.

## Key Takeaways

- **UX Mechanic**: Swipe up = new topic; swipe sideways = deeper dive ending in a quiz.
- **Content Input**: Any PDF or text file can be uploaded and converted.
- **Target Markets**: Educational institutions, corporate training, and individual consumers.
- **Business Model**: Free consumer tier, paid Pro subscription, institutional annual licenses with progress reporting.
- **Founder Motivation**: Both founders felt trapped by doomscrolling; product is a direct response to that habit.
- **Core Tension Named**: Short-form feeds "pose challenges for any kind of media that requires depth" — ScrollEd's thesis is that swipe depth mechanics can bridge this.
- **Near-Term Roadmap**: Expanded lesson library, source-checking workflows, institution pilots, personalized adaptive learning.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Habit Loop]]**: ScrollEd borrows the cue-routine-reward loop from social feeds (scroll → content hit → repeat) and substitutes educational content for passive consumption — hijacking an existing neural groove rather than fighting it.
- **[[Depth vs. Breadth Trade-off]]**: The sideways-swipe mechanic externalizes the classic learning tension — up progresses breadth, sideways trades breadth for depth — making the trade-off explicit and user-driven rather than hidden in curriculum design.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is ScrollEd's core product claim?
**A**: It converts textbooks and PDFs into a TikTok-style vertical feed with AI-generated multimedia and quizzes, making structured learning as frictionless as doomscrolling.

**Q2**: How does the swipe mechanic encode pedagogical intent?
**A**: Swiping up advances to a new topic (breadth), while swiping sideways drills deeper into the current topic and ends in a quiz (depth with assessment).

**Q3**: What institutional opportunity does ScrollEd target beyond consumers?
**A**: Educational institutions and corporate training teams can purchase annual licenses that include engagement and progress reporting dashboards.
