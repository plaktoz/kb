## Summary

Applying spaced repetition to skill practice (illustrated through music) means revisiting material right around when you'd naturally forget it rather than mass-repeating it in one sitting, with recommended repetition gaps widening over time (1, 7, 16, 35 days) to convert short-term gains into durable mastery.

## Core Concepts

- [[Forget to Learn Theory]] — the greater the drop in retrieval strength before a review, the greater the learning gain when the memory is accessed again
- [[Interleaved Practice]] — alternating between multiple passages/skills within a single session as a form of spaced repetition on a shorter timescale
- [[Illusion of Competence]] — performing a skill error-free once in a single session doesn't mean it's actually learned; mastery requires spaced re-testing over days

## Key Takeaways

- **Widening repetition schedule**: recommended gaps of 1 day, 7 days, 16 days, then 35 days when committing new material to repertoire.
- **Longer-horizon maintenance schedule**: revisit within 1–2 days for material learned a week ago; after 1 week for a month-old piece; after 2 weeks for 3 months; after 3 weeks for 6 months; after 1 month for a year.
- **Deliberate supervision matters**: actively analyzing what went wrong (via real-time listening or recordings) during initial practice is a prerequisite for spaced repetition to work — it's not just about timing, but about quality of each practice rep.
- **Frustration is expected**: because spaced repetition deliberately revisits material near the point of forgetting, it produces more frequent failure and mental effort than massed practice — this is the mechanism, not a flaw.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does spaced repetition intentionally wait until you're close to forgetting the material?
**A**: Per the "Forget to Learn" theory, the greater the drop in retrieval strength before a review, the greater the learning gain when the memory is successfully reaccessed — reviewing too early or too often produces weaker gains.

**Q2**: What is the recommended repetition schedule for committing new material to memory?
**A**: First repetition after 1 day, second after 7 days, third after 16 days, and fourth after 35 days.

**Q3**: Why can playing something correctly once be misleading about how well it's actually learned?
**A**: This is the "illusion of competence" — error-free performance in a single sitting reflects short-term memory access, not durable long-term mastery, which only spaced re-testing over multiple days can confirm.
