## Summary

Mashable's 2022 roundup lists major platforms where you can learn for free, noting that most let you study without paying but charge for verified certificates. It covers [[edX]], [[FutureLearn]], [[Coursera]], [[Khan Academy]], [[General Assembly]], [[Udemy]], [[Kadenze]], and [[Class Central]] as a review aggregator. Pricing and free-access details are from early 2022 and may be out of date.

## Core Concepts

- **Free vs. certified access**: Most courses can be taken free for fun; verified certificates (useful for a CV or LinkedIn) cost money.
- **Accredited vs. unaccredited**: Usually stated on a platform's About page; General Assembly is not accredited by the US Department of Education.
- **[[edX]]**: Founded by MIT and Harvard, now owned by 2U; MicroMasters from about $1,200, MicroBachelors $166 per credit.
- **[[FutureLearn]]**: UK cultural and academic partners; free access for 14-day periods, unlimited membership $250 per year.
- **[[Coursera]]**: Founded 2012 by Daphne Koller and Andrew Ng; Coursera Plus about $399 per year, most courses free without certificate.
- **[[Khan Academy]]**: Free classes covering programming, chemistry, LSAT prep, and art history.
- **[[General Assembly]]**: Coding, design, data, and marketing courses; some short ones free, immersives up to $15,960.
- **[[Udemy]]**: Founded 2010 by Eren Bali; 150,000 courses, most about $15 to $300, with a Free Resource Center of 150 courses.
- **[[Kadenze]]**: STEAM courses; membership $20 per month, some courses free without certificate.
- **[[Class Central]]**: Search engine and review site for free online courses.

## Key Takeaways

- **Free Access**: Most platforms let you audit courses free, minus the certificate.
- **Certificate Cost**: Paid tiers range from $20 per month to $399 per year.
- **FutureLearn Limit**: Free course content is available only for 14-day periods.
- **Udemy Scale**: 150,000 courses, 57,000 instructors, 65+ languages.
- **Vet Courses**: Check accreditation on each platform's About page.
- **Read Reviews**: Use Class Central to compare classes before committing.
- **Dated Source**: Prices and policies reflect February 2022.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about free online learning?
**A**: Many reputable platforms let you learn for free, usually without a certificate, so you can study for fun or upskilling without paying.

**Q2**: What distinguishes free from paid access on these platforms?
**A**: Free tiers generally give course content, while paid tiers add verified certificates. For example, FutureLearn gives 14 days of free access and Coursera Plus costs about $399 a year.

**Q3**: How would you choose a course using this guide?
**A**: Check whether the course is accredited, decide whether you need a certificate for a CV or LinkedIn, and read reviews on Class Central before picking a platform.
