## Summary

An American College of Education survey of 1,046 US workers who use AI found that 63% say AI teaches them skills their employer never formally trained them on. Many of these skills go unrecognized: 34% say employers do not recognize or reward AI-based learning, and 54% say no structured programs exist. The college argues for credited, formal upskilling through academic partnerships.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Informal Learning]]**: Workers self-teach with AI outside employer-sponsored training.
- **[[AI Upskilling]]**: Gap between worker skill growth and structured employer programs.
- **[[Skill Recognition]]**: Self-taught AI skills often do not appear on resumes or in performance reviews.
- **[[Trust in AI]]**: 65% worry AI information may be inaccurate or incomplete.
- **[[American College of Education]]**: Commissioned the survey, conducted by Fractl, and promotes formal upskilling.

## Key Takeaways

- **Hidden Learning**: 63% say AI teaches skills beyond formal training.
- **Recognition Gap**: 34% report no formal recognition or reward for AI-learned skills.
- **Program Gap**: 54% say employers offer no structured programs yet.
- **Speed Motive**: 46% use AI because it is faster than asking a colleague.
- **Face-Saving**: 29% use AI to avoid admitting they do not know; 32% of managers too.
- **Trust Limits**: Only 7% trust AI more than a colleague or manager.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the survey's central finding?
**A**: 63% of workers who use AI say it teaches them skills their employer never formally trained them on, while many employers neither recognize nor structure that learning.

**Q2**: What motives and concerns shape how workers learn with AI?
**A**: 46% want a faster answer than asking a colleague and 29% avoid admitting ignorance, yet 65% worry AI information may be inaccurate or incomplete.

**Q3**: What does ACE suggest organizations do about it?
**A**: Close skill gaps through formal upskilling, such as academic partnerships, so that learning is credited and recognized rather than staying invisible on resumes and reviews.
