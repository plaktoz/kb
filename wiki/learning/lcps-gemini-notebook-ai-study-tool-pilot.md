## Summary

Loudoun County Public Schools (LCPS) will pilot Gemini Notebook (formerly [[NotebookLM]]) in eight schools starting in November to help students study, engage with course materials, and deepen understanding. The tool grounds its answers in teacher-provided or student-uploaded sources, and the pilot stresses AI as support for critical thinking rather than a substitute for student work. LCPS will collect teacher and student feedback to evaluate the pilot.

## Core Concepts

- [[Loudoun County Public Schools]]: the division running the pilot across middle schools, high schools, and virtual distance learning (grades 6-12).
- [[NotebookLM]] (Gemini Notebook): an AI research and study assistant that works from user-supplied sources rather than as a general-purpose chatbot.
- [[Source-Grounded AI]]: restricting responses to provided materials to keep learning on classroom content and reduce inaccuracy.
- [[AI Study Guides]]: generating practice questions, quizzes, and glossaries from class notes and readings.
- [[Audio Overviews]]: turning uploaded readings into conversational, podcast-style audio for review.
- [[Student Data Privacy]]: access through secure LCPS accounts in Google Workspace for Education; student content is not used to train public AI models.
- [[Critical Thinking]]: the pilot's stated aim is independent thinking and evaluating information.

## Key Takeaways

- **Pilot Scope**: Eight schools begin piloting in November: three middle schools, four high schools, and a virtual program.
- **Source Grounding**: Responses draw on provided materials, reducing inaccurate information versus general chatbots.
- **Verification**: Students are still encouraged to check responses against original sources.
- **Use Cases**: Study guides, quizzes, glossaries, audio overviews, outlining, and organizing research.
- **Privacy**: Student content isn't used to train public models, per Google's education commitments.
- **Pedagogy**: AI positioned as a resource for understanding, not a replacement for student work.
- **Evaluation**: Teacher and student feedback will assess instruction, engagement, and learning impact.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Constraining a generator to a bounded source set reduces fabrication** — Answers anchored to user-supplied materials stay on-topic and are less likely to be inaccurate, though verification against originals is still needed.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is LCPS piloting, and why was this tool chosen over general AI chatbots?
**A**: Eight schools will pilot Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM), which grounds responses in teacher-provided or student-uploaded sources. This keeps learning focused on classroom content and reduces the potential for inaccurate information.

**Q2**: How does the pilot protect student privacy?
**A**: The tool runs inside the division's Google Workspace for Education environment, and students use secure LCPS accounts. Per Google's education privacy commitments, student content and interactions are not used to train public AI models.

**Q3**: How should students and teachers use the tool during the pilot?
**A**: Students can generate study guides, listen to audio overviews, and outline research, while verifying outputs against original sources. Teachers guide responsible use, emphasizing independent thinking over substituting AI for their own work.
