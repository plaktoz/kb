## Summary

A PLS-SEM survey of 962 secondary vocational students in China integrates the [[Technology Acceptance Model]] (TAM) with the [[Community of Inquiry]] (CoI) framework to explain online learning motivation (OLM). [[Self-Efficacy]] was the strongest correlate of OLM, followed by perceived usefulness and ease of use, while teaching presence was the only CoI dimension with a significant (though small) mediating role. The authors conclude that online design should pair accessible technology with structured instructor support and confidence-building, while flagging heavy construct overlap among OLM, usefulness, and self-efficacy.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Technology Acceptance Model]]**: Perceived usefulness (PU), perceived ease of use (EU), and [[Self-Efficacy]] (SE) shape technology adoption and motivation.
- **[[Community of Inquiry]]**: Online learning runs through [[Teaching Presence]], [[Social Presence]], and [[Cognitive Presence]].
- **[[Online Learning Motivation]]**: Internal drivers of willingness, effort, and persistence, distinct from behavioural engagement; linked to [[Self-Determination Theory]].
- **[[PLS-SEM]]**: Partial least squares structural equation modelling (SmartPLS 4) used on 962 respondents from the Chaoxing platform.
- **[[Albert Bandura]]**: Social cognitive theory explains why confidence drives motivation and persistence.
- **[[Vocational Education]]**: Learners seek practical, career-linked competencies and need structured demonstrations and guidance.

## Key Takeaways

- **Self-Efficacy Dominates**: SE had the largest effect on OLM (β = 0.416), above PU (0.242) and EU (0.121).
- **Ease Feeds Usefulness**: EU predicted PU (β = 0.284), consistent with core TAM assumptions.
- **Teaching Presence Matters Most**: TP was the only CoI dimension directly tied to OLM and mediating SE/EU/PU effects.
- **Small Mediation**: Indirect effects through teaching presence were small (β ≈ 0.015–0.030).
- **Usefulness Is Not Depth**: PU and EU did not predict cognitive presence; value perception does not yield deep thinking.
- **Confidence Is Not Sociability**: SE did not predict social presence in teacher-directed vocational courses.
- **Construct Overlap**: HTMT up to 0.964 for OLM-SE; results are associations, not distinct effects.
- **Design Implications**: Combine clear instruction, timely feedback, tech orientation, and deliberate peer and reflective tasks.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Belief in one's capability drives motivation and persistence** — Confidence in succeeding at a task shapes the effort and persistence invested in it, ahead of perceived task value or convenience. · *model:* [[Self-Efficacy]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the study's central finding about what drives online learning motivation among secondary vocational students?
**A**: Self-efficacy was the strongest factor associated with motivation, followed by perceived usefulness and ease of use. Teaching presence was the only Community of Inquiry dimension with a significant direct link and mediating role.

**Q2**: What evidence shows social and cognitive presence did little to explain motivation, and what caveat applies?
**A**: Social and cognitive presence had non-significant direct effects on motivation and no significant mediating effects, while teaching presence's indirect effects were small (β 0.015–0.030). HTMT values up to 0.964 among OLM, PU, and SE mean results should be read as associations.

**Q3**: How should vocational online courses be designed given these results?
**A**: Pair accessible technology with strong instructor guidance, timely feedback, and confidence-building such as orientation and scaffolded tasks. Add structured peer discussion and reflective, authentic problem-solving, since social and cognitive presence do not emerge automatically.
