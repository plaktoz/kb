# AI Fluency: Delegation Competency

**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EljzyfdYkrc (Anthropic / Ringling College / UCC — AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations, Lesson 4)

Delegation is one of the core AI fluency competencies. It focuses on working **effectively and efficiently** with AI by deciding *what* work to do, *who* (human or AI) should do it, and *how* the split should be structured.

## Three Elements of Delegation

### 1. Problem Awareness
Understand your goals and the work involved **before** introducing AI.

Key questions to answer first:
- What exactly are you trying to accomplish?
- What does success look like?
- What kinds of thinking or work are required?
  - Simple but time-consuming tasks → good automation candidates
  - Areas of uncertainty → AI as a thinking partner
  - Areas of ignorance → AI to surface data
  - Areas requiring critical judgment → likely human-only

> "The most effective AI collaborators are experts in their fields first and AI delegators second."

Without problem awareness, you cannot evaluate AI outputs, steer AI effectively, or know when to intervene.

### 2. Platform Awareness
Maintain a working knowledge of available AI systems and their trade-offs.

- Different models prioritise speed vs. depth, accuracy vs. creativity
- The landscape changes almost daily — hands-on experimentation is the only reliable method
- Knowing the right tool for the job is as important as knowing how to use it

### 3. Task Delegation
Strategically distribute work between human and AI based on the above:

| Work type | Approach |
|-----------|----------|
| Routine, repetitive | Automation |
| Creative or nuanced | Human-AI collaboration (augmentation) |
| Critical judgment, ethics | Human-only |
| Ongoing external interactions | AI agents acting on your behalf |

## Key Insight

Effective delegation is not "handing over the wheel." It is a *continuous strategic process* of matching the right kind of intelligence to each part of the work.
