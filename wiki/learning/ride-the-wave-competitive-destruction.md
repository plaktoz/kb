## Summary

Drawing on Charlie Munger's concept of "competitive destruction," this piece argues that major technological and economic waves consistently destroy incumbents who cling to outdated models — Kodak's refusal to embrace digital photography (despite inventing it in 1975) being the canonical example. Success requires three things in combination: vision to spot the wave early, courage to move before consensus forms, and skill to time entry correctly. Missing the wave doesn't just mean falling behind — it means watching others compound structural advantages.

## Core Concepts

- [[Charlie Munger]]'s [[Competitive Destruction]]: industries are repeatedly reset by new technology waves
- [[Kodak]] as the canonical case: invented digital photography in 1975, chose not to deploy it
- [[Early Bird Advantage]]: "when these new businesses come in, there are huge advantages for the early birds"
- [[Wave Timing]]: vision + courage + skill as the three required inputs — any one missing is disqualifying

## Key Takeaways

- **Kodak lesson**: Inventing the disruption and still missing the wave is possible — and common
- **Three requirements**: Vision (spot it early) + Courage (move before others) + Skill (time entry)
- **Compounding disadvantage**: Missing the wave means others compound advantages while you stagnate
- **"Mired in shallows"**: Munger's description of late movers caught between old and new models
- **Implication**: Clinging to present success is the most common form of wave-missing
