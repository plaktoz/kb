## Summary

After 14 years as a practicing anesthesiologist, the author pursued a new board certification in obesity medicine to meet patient demand for evidence-based guidance on topics like GLP-1 medications. Drawing on Malcolm Knowles' adult learning theory, she contrasts memorizing to survive in her 20s with studying out of curiosity in her 40s, and adopts three evidence-based study strategies. She closes by reframing the discomfort of being a beginner again as a source of joy and reinvention, using Carol Dweck's growth mindset research.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Andragogy]]** — [[Malcolm Knowles]]' adult learning theory holding that adults absorb material better when it connects to lived experience.
- **[[Active Recall]]** — self-testing rather than rereading, citing [[Roediger and Karpicke]] research showing recall practice outperforms review.
- **[[Time Boxing / Pomodoro Technique]]** — a modified 50-minutes-on/10-minutes-off schedule paired with prioritizing sleep for memory consolidation.
- **[[Growth Mindset]]** — [[Carol Dweck]]'s framework for treating wrong answers as data rather than identity threats.
- **[[Obesity Medicine]]** and **[[GLP-1 Medications]]** — the clinical motivation driving the author's new certification pursuit.

## Key Takeaways

- **Motivation shifted with age**: 20s learning was survival memorization; 40s learning is curiosity-driven and immediately applied.
- **Self-testing beats rereading**: recall practice cited as more effective than passive review.
- **Structured breaks matter**: 50/10 modified Pomodoro cycle plus sleep prioritization aids consolidation.
- **Studying with a partner helps**: a "study-and-spa weekend" paired accountability with exam preparation.
- **Reframe wrong answers**: growth mindset treats mistakes as data, not threats to identity.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Beginner's Mind]]**: The discomfort of "being a beginner again" after 14 years of expertise is the shoshin problem — deliberately re-entering novice status to learn something genuinely new.
