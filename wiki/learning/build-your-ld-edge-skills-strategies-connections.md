## Summary

L&D professionals strengthen their organizational influence by connecting learning initiatives directly to what leadership already measures — understanding how the organization makes money, its strategic priorities, and its KPIs — rather than framing proposals purely in learning terms.

## Core Concepts

- **Business-aligned L&D**: Framing training proposals around outcomes leadership tracks (productivity, retention, sales performance, customer satisfaction) instead of learning-specific metrics like completion rates.
- **Organizational fluency**: Understanding the business's revenue model and strategic priorities as a prerequisite for making a credible case for any L&D initiative.

## Key Takeaways

- **Reframe the pitch**: Tie any proposed L&D initiative explicitly to metrics leadership already cares about — productivity, retention, sales performance, customer satisfaction.
- **Know the business first**: Understanding how the organization makes money and its strategic priorities is positioned as a prerequisite to influence, not a nice-to-have.
- **Access points cited**: DevLearn (200+ learning activities, Expo pass available for non-travelers) and the Everyday Tools in Learning Development Online Conference as lower-cost ways to build this fluency.
