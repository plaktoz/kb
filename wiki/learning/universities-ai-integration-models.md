## Summary
The Digital Education Council identifies three emerging models of human-AI collaboration in higher education — human-guided tool use, collaborative workflows, and limited-supervision autonomy — and highlights Northeastern University's faculty-led effort to embed [[AI Literacy]] across every discipline rather than isolating it in standalone courses.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Three Models of Human-AI Collaboration]]**: (1) AI as a human-guided tool for summarization/content creation under clear direction — currently most common; (2) collaborative workflows where AI takes a more active execution role with human oversight; (3) AI operating with limited supervision on parts of a workflow, with humans shifting to strategic design and monitoring.
- **[[Discipline-Specific AI Readiness]]**: Northeastern's Chief of AI, Javed Aslam, argues "AI readiness is going to be inherently discipline-specific" — requiring a common overarching framework adapted independently by each college rather than a one-size-fits-all curriculum.
- **[[Bottom-Up Curriculum Mapping]]**: Northeastern implemented its AI literacy framework through faculty representatives across colleges reviewing existing programmes to identify where AI literacy could be developed, rather than a top-down mandate.

## Key Takeaways
- **Fragmentation problem**: Despite widespread student/faculty AI adoption, programme-level integration remains largely uncoordinated across institutions.
- **Co-intelligence framing**: Aslam: "We need to prepare our students for a workplace with co-intelligence, where we'll be working with and alongside AI tools."
- **Implementation approach**: Northeastern combined classroom instruction with applied/project-based learning and industry placements, adapting the DEC AI Literacy Framework to its own context.
- **Trajectory**: Most institutional AI strategies remain early-stage, but pressure to move from experimentation to systematic approaches is expected to grow.
