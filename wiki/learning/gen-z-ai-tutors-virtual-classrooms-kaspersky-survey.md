## Summary

A 2026 [[Kaspersky]] survey of 7,200 respondents across 18 countries finds that [[Gen Z]] is more enthusiastic than other generations about personalised AI tutors and VR/AR classrooms with AI assistants. Gen Z also reports seeing more technology enter their education over the past five years. Kaspersky stresses that adoption must come with [[AI Literacy]], because over-reliance on algorithms threatens [[Critical Thinking]] and independent problem-solving.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Personalised AI Tutors]]**: Gen Z ranks them among the most promising future technologies. They could let students learn at their own pace, get personalised explanations, and practise according to individual needs.
- **[[VR and AR Classrooms]]**: Immersive classrooms that include AI assistants and offer more interactive learning experiences.
- **[[AI Literacy]]**: Understanding what AI systems can and cannot do, checking AI output against reliable sources, and protecting personal data on AI platforms.
- **[[Cognitive Offloading]] risk**: [[Evgeniya Russkikh]], Head of Academic Affairs at Kaspersky, warns that over-reliance on algorithms creates "cognitive and educational dead-ends."
- **International guidance**: The [[OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026]] and [[UNESCO]] guidance on [[Generative AI]] in education highlight human oversight, ethical use, data protection, and continued development of human skills.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI tutors**: 18% of Gen Z rank them most promising, versus 16% overall.
- **VR/AR classrooms with AI**: 14% of Gen Z rank them most promising, versus 12% overall.
- **Rising tech exposure**: 68% of Gen Z saw more tech in education over five years, versus 55% overall.
- **Survey scale**: 7,200 respondents in 18 countries, including Saudi Arabia, India, Brazil, and Germany.
- **Literacy before adoption**: students must verify AI output and understand its limits.
- **Core risk**: over-reliance on algorithms erodes critical thinking and independent problem-solving.
- **Shared responsibility**: both students and educators need cross-checking and responsible-use skills.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the Kaspersky survey's central finding about Gen Z and education technology?
**A**: Gen Z shows more interest than other generations in personalised AI tutors and VR/AR classrooms with AI assistants, and expects AI to play a growing role in education.

**Q2**: What data supports the claim that Gen Z is more tech-forward in education?
**A**: 18% of Gen Z named AI tutors as most promising versus 16% overall, and 14% named VR/AR classrooms versus 12% overall. In addition, 68% had seen technology used more widely in their education over five years, versus 55% overall.

**Q3**: What should schools do alongside rolling out AI tutors?
**A**: Schools should build AI literacy so that students and educators understand AI's limits, cross-check its output against reliable sources, and protect personal data. This guards against over-reliance that weakens critical thinking.
