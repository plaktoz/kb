## Summary
Inside Higher Ed's 2026-27 Student Voice survey (5,034 undergraduates, 310 institutions) finds AI is changing how students study, with benefits and new anxieties, while familiar barriers, mental health (41%) and financial pressure (37%), still limit success most. Students rate their education highly (81% good or excellent) but few are confident in affordability, and they want AI used to help them learn and be used transparently.

## Key Takeaways
- **AI Benefits**: 34% study more efficiently; 33% feel more confident with hard concepts.
- **AI Risks**: 16% rely on AI too heavily; 25% alter writing to dodge detectors.
- **Responsible AI**: 65% want AI to help them learn; 60% want transparency.
- **Top Barriers**: Mental health 41%, financial pressure 37%; 11% report no barriers.
- **Unmet Needs**: 61% went without an essential; only 21% say emergency aid exists.
- **Quality vs Cost**: 81% rate education good or excellent; 24% confident in affordability.
- **Career Prep**: 54% want more job pipelines and internships; 11% want AI literacy.
- **Support Network**: 68% would turn to someone outside the institution.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the survey's central finding?
**A**: AI is changing the student experience, but long-standing barriers, mainly mental health (41%) and financial pressure (37%), still most limit academic success.

**Q2**: What data shows the financial strain on students?
**A**: 61% went without at least one essential in the past year for financial reasons, and only 24% are highly confident their institution is keeping education affordable. Just 21% say their college offers emergency aid, and 62% are unsure.

**Q3**: How should institutions apply the findings on AI?
**A**: Use AI to help students learn rather than do the work for them, be transparent about its use, and include AI-free moments, as Austin suggests. Tools could also connect students to people and campus supports instead of replacing human contact.
