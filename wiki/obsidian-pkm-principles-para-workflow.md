## Summary

Obsidian works best as a lightweight, connected personal knowledge system built around the [[PARA Framework]] (Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives). The core thesis is that knowledge compounds over time when ideas are captured quickly, linked meaningfully, and revisited regularly. The most effective vaults mirror the user's actual thinking workflow rather than imposing rigid categorical structures.

## Core Concepts

- [[Obsidian]] — local markdown vault for networked note-taking
- [[PARA Framework]] — four-bucket organizational system: Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives
- [[Personal Knowledge Management]] — the practice of systematically capturing and connecting ideas
- [[Knowledge Graph]] — the network of interlinked notes that emerges from deliberate linking
- [[Inbox Capture]] — low-friction intake buffer for raw ideas before organization
- [[WikiLink]] — Obsidian's `[[double-bracket]]` syntax for creating connections between notes
- [[Zettelkasten]] — atomic note-taking method that underpins networked PKM systems
- [[Literature Note]] — a note derived from an external source, kept separate from your own ideas

## Key Takeaways

- **Knowledge is a network**: [[Obsidian]] surfaces ideas through exploration, not rigid categories.
- **Capture first, organize later**: [[Inbox Capture]] keeps friction low; process on a schedule.
- **Use [[PARA Framework]]**: Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives — no over-engineering.
- **Link aggressively**: add at least three meaningful [[WikiLink]]s per new note.
- **Separate sources from insights**: anchor raw material in source notes, write synthesis elsewhere.
- **Templates and frontmatter**: standardize note types and tags for reliable retrieval.
- **Treat the vault as living**: review, refine, and prune regularly — not a static archive.
- **Core maxim**: "Writing is thinking." Notes become tools, not archives, when used actively.
