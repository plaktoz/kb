## Summary

A meta-analysis of 18 randomized controlled trials (1,527 participants) found [[Baduanjin]], a traditional low-intensity Chinese mind-body exercise, significantly improved sleep quality, reduced depressive symptoms, and improved quality of life, handgrip strength, and body composition in older adults — though the authors caution that high heterogeneity and methodological limitations across the included trials mean the findings should be interpreted cautiously pending larger, longer-term RCTs.

## Core Concepts

- [[Baduanjin]] — an accessible, low-intensity traditional Chinese mind-body exercise ("Eight Pieces of Brocade")
- [[Meta-Analysis]] — pooled results from 18 RCTs (1,527 participants), registered with PROSPERO
- [[Sleep Quality (Older Adults)]] — significantly improved (MD = −2.81, p < 0.001) across the pooled trials
- [[Depressive Symptoms]] — significantly reduced (SMD = −0.88, p < 0.001)

## Key Takeaways

- **Sleep quality improved significantly**: MD = −2.81 (95% CI −3.38 to −2.24, p < 0.001)
- **Depressive symptoms reduced significantly**: SMD = −0.88 (95% CI −1.31 to −0.45, p < 0.001)
- **Quality of life, handgrip strength, and body composition** (BMI, waist/hip circumference) also improved significantly
- **Evidence caveat**: high heterogeneity and methodological limitations across the 18 trials mean results should be interpreted cautiously
- **Next step called for**: large-scale, rigorous RCTs with diverse cohorts and longer follow-up to confirm long-term benefit and compare Baduanjin against other exercise types

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Heterogeneity in Meta-Analysis]]**: The authors' own caution — that "high heterogeneity" limits confidence despite statistically significant pooled effects — is a core epidemiological principle: a meta-analysis can produce a precise-looking number while masking wide variation in trial quality, population, and dosing that a single point estimate doesn't reveal.
