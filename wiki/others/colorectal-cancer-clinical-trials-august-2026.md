## Summary

Fight Colorectal Cancer's August 2026 clinical-trials roundup highlights new response-rate and expanded-access data for BRAF V600E-targeted triplet therapy in metastatic colorectal cancer, alongside a Phase 3 adjuvant-immunotherapy trial and an RNA-virus therapy for immunotherapy-resistant tumors.

## Core Concepts

- **[[BRAF V600E mutation]]**: A biomarker in metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) targeted by the combination of [[encorafenib]], [[cetuximab]], and FOLFIRI chemotherapy.
- **[[BEACON trial]] expanded-access cohort**: Evaluates the same drug combination plus chemotherapy in older patients and those with comorbidities who weren't eligible for the original trial.
- **[[BREAKWATER trial]]**: Phase 3 trial testing mFOLFOX6 with or without atezolizumab after surgery in stage III colon cancer with mismatch repair deficiency (dMMR/MSI-H).
- **[[IVX037]] + sintilimab**: An RNA virus therapy trial targeting microsatellite-stable (MSS) tumors that don't typically respond to checkpoint inhibitors.

## Key Takeaways

- The encorafenib + cetuximab + FOLFIRI combination showed a significant increase in overall response rate and progression-free survival versus historical controls at ASCO GI 2026, for previously treated BRAF V600E-mutated mCRC.
- Preliminary real-world data from the expanded-access cohort shows efficacy in older patients and those with comorbidities, supporting more inclusive eligibility for BRAF-targeted triplet regimens.
- In the BREAKWATER trial, adding atezolizumab to adjuvant chemotherapy improved 3-year disease-free survival from 76.6% to 86.4% in dMMR/MSI-H stage III colon cancer.
- IVX037 + sintilimab represents an emerging strategy for MSS tumors, the majority of colorectal cancers, which are typically resistant to immunotherapy alone.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this roundup?
**A**: Several colorectal cancer clinical trials in 2026 are expanding targeted and immunotherapy treatment options, particularly for BRAF-mutated and mismatch-repair-deficient subtypes, while also exploring new approaches for immunotherapy-resistant tumors.

**Q2**: What specific survival benefit did the BREAKWATER trial demonstrate?
**A**: Adding atezolizumab to standard adjuvant chemotherapy (mFOLFOX6) improved 3-year disease-free survival from 76.6% to 86.4% in patients with dMMR/MSI-H stage III colon cancer.

**Q3**: Why does the IVX037 + sintilimab trial matter for the broader colorectal cancer population?
**A**: Most colorectal cancers are microsatellite stable (MSS) and don't respond well to checkpoint inhibitors alone; an RNA virus therapy approach targets exactly this immunotherapy-resistant majority rather than the smaller MSI-H/dMMR subgroup that already benefits from immunotherapy.
