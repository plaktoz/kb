## Summary
A Newcastle University randomized controlled trial found that adults who added a daily glass of 100% fruit juice or smoothie to a 5-a-day intervention showed statistically significant lower depression scores than a control group after four weeks, without adverse metabolic effects.

## Core Concepts
- **[[5-a-Day Barrier]]**: Fewer than 1 in 5 UK adults (17%) meet fruit-and-vegetable recommendations; the study tested whether juice/smoothies, often seen as a lower-cost, lower-effort option, could help close that gap without displacing other fiber intake.
- **[[Dietary Intervention for Mood]]**: The trial adds to a body of research linking simple, practical dietary changes to modest but measurable mental-health benefits, distinct from pharmacological interventions.
- **[[Modest but Significant Effect Size]]**: The 2.52-point difference on a 27-point depression scale (PHQ-9) was described by researchers as modest but statistically significant — an important calibration for how much impact to expect from a single dietary change.

## Key Takeaways
- **Trial design**: 42 adults (three groups of 14: control, whole-produce-only, whole-produce-plus-juice) over 4 weeks, each receiving £10/week.
- **Result**: The juice/smoothie group scored 2.52 points lower on the 27-point PHQ-9 depression scale than controls (2.93 vs. 5.45 adjusted mean).
- **No fiber tradeoff**: Both intervention groups increased daily fiber intake by an estimated 8-10g, indicating juice did not crowd out other produce.
- **Safety**: No adverse effects on metabolic health markers were found despite concerns about juice sugar content.
- **Funding note**: The study was funded by the Fruit Juice Science Centre.
