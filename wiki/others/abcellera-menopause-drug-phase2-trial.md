## Summary

AbCellera's non-hormonal antibody ABCL635 cut hot-flash frequency by 83% (vs. 33% for placebo) after a single dose in a Phase 2 trial, with no dropouts from adverse events, sending AbCellera's stock up 34% and positioning the drug as a potential once-monthly alternative to daily oral menopause treatments.

## Core Concepts

- **[[ABCL635]]**: A non-hormonal antibody that blocks the neurokinin 3 receptor (NK3R), a signaling pathway involved in the brain's control of body temperature, being developed as a once-monthly subcutaneous injection.
- **[[Vasomotor Symptoms]]**: Hot flashes and night sweats affect up to 80% of menopausal women, with about 12 million U.S. women experiencing moderate-to-severe symptoms and over 6 million seeking treatment.
- **[[Phase 2 Trial Design]]**: A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study of 92 postmenopausal women averaging ~10 moderate/severe hot flashes/day, with 100% trial completion.
- **[[Long-Acting Dosing Advantage]]**: Positioned against daily oral treatments (e.g., Veozah, Lynkuet), a once-monthly injection could improve adherence for chronic symptom management.

## Key Takeaways

- **Efficacy**: 83% reduction in hot flash frequency vs. 33% for placebo after 4 weeks; 58% reduction in severity vs. 12% for placebo.
- **Safety**: No patient dropouts due to adverse events among all 92 enrollees; most common side effects were headache, fatigue, and injection-site reaction.
- **Market reaction**: AbCellera stock rose 34% on the announcement.
- **Secondary benefits**: Statistically significant improvements in sleep quality and overall patient-assessed condition.
- **Financial position**: AbCellera holds over $565 million in cash/marketable securities, with total liquidity topping $675 million heading into H2 2026.
