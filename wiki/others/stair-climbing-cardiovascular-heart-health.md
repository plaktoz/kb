## Summary

A meta-analysis of over 480,000 people across nine studies found regular stair climbers were 39% less likely to die of cardiovascular disease and 24% less likely to die of any cause than infrequent stair climbers, positioning stair climbing as a low-effort, highly accessible way to build meaningful physical activity into daily life.

## Core Concepts

- [[Stair Climbing as Incidental Exercise]] — unlike gym workouts, stair climbing requires no dedicated time or equipment, making it a realistic activity target for people with limited access to structured exercise.
- [[Cardiovascular Mortality Reduction]] — the study's core metric: a 39% lower risk of dying specifically from cardiovascular disease among frequent stair climbers.
- [[Dose-Response Relationship]] — one cohort study within the analysis suggested climbing roughly six flights of stairs daily may provide the greatest benefit, though researchers say the ideal "dose" isn't yet established.

## Key Takeaways

- **Core statistics**: 39% lower cardiovascular mortality risk and 24% lower all-cause mortality risk among frequent versus infrequent stair climbers.
- **Study scale**: researchers reviewed nearly 1,900 studies before analyzing pooled data from more than 480,000 participants across nine high-quality studies, with a median follow-up of 14 years.
- **Broader cardiac benefit**: regular stair climbing was also linked to lower rates of heart attack, stroke and heart failure, not just mortality.
- **Practical framing**: unlike structured workouts, stairs are something people can "easily fit into your day at home, at work or when you're out" — a low-barrier intervention.
- **Caveat**: stair climbing isn't appropriate for everyone, particularly people with mobility limitations or joint conditions; researchers call for wearable-tracked studies to nail down the optimal dose.
