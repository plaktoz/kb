## Summary

A preclinical study suggests [[sildenafil]] (Viagra) may inhibit cancer metastasis by blocking a cholesterol uptake pathway that tumor cells exploit to spread. The mechanism involves [[PDE5]] inhibition affecting cholesterol acquisition in cancer cells, not the drug's primary cardiovascular pathway. While early results across multiple cancer types are promising, the research is preclinical — human clinical trials would be necessary before any clinical recommendation could follow.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Sildenafil]] (Viagra)** — PDE5 inhibitor originally developed for cardiovascular conditions; widely available and well-characterized safety profile
- **[[Cancer Metastasis]]** — the spread of cancer cells from primary tumor to secondary sites; leading cause of cancer mortality
- **[[Drug Repurposing]]** — finding new clinical applications for existing approved drugs; lower development barrier than new compounds
- **[[Cholesterol-Metastasis Link]]** — proposed mechanism: cancer cells need cholesterol to spread; sildenafil interferes with this acquisition pathway
- **[[PDE5 Inhibition]]** — sildenafil's molecular mechanism; affects cholesterol pathways in tumor cells as a secondary effect

## Key Takeaways

- **Mechanism**: sildenafil may block cholesterol acquisition pathway that cancer cells use for metastasis
- **Multiple cancer types**: inhibitory effect observed across several cancer models in the lab
- **Preclinical only**: results from cell/animal studies — not ready for clinical use
- **Drug repurposing appeal**: sildenafil has long safety record and wide availability — low barrier if mechanism confirmed
- **Not a recommendation**: patients should not self-administer sildenafil as cancer treatment based on this study
