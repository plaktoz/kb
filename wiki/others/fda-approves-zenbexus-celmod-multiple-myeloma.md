## Summary

The FDA granted accelerated approval to Bristol Myers Squibb's ZENBEXUS (iberdomide), the first-ever approved CELMoD (cereblon-modulating protein degrader), for multiple myeloma patients as early as first relapse — based on Phase 3 EXCALIBER-RRMM results showing it doubled minimal residual disease-negative complete response rates versus the standard triplet regimen (41% vs. 21%).

## Core Concepts

- **[[CELMoD]]**: Cereblon-modulating protein degraders are a new drug class; ZENBEXUS is the first FDA-approved therapy in this category, building on BMS's two-decade targeted protein degradation platform.
- **[[Minimal Residual Disease (MRD) Negativity]]**: A highly sensitive measure of treatment response (detecting 1 malignant cell among up to 1,000,000 normal cells) increasingly used as a surrogate endpoint for progression-free survival and to accelerate regulatory approvals.
- **[[EXCALIBER-RRMM Trial]]**: Phase 3 study (n=939 total, primary MRD population n=420) comparing ZENBEXUS+daratumumab+dexamethasone (ZDd) against daratumumab+bortezomib+dexamethasone (DVd) in relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma.
- **[[Accelerated Approval Pathway]]**: Granted Breakthrough Therapy designation and reviewed under the FDA's Project Orbis initiative, enabling concurrent review by multiple countries' health authorities; continued approval is contingent on confirmatory trial verification of clinical benefit.

## Key Takeaways

- **Efficacy**: 41% MRD-negative complete response with ZDd vs. 21% with DVd (p < 0.0001) — first approval based on MRD-negative CR in this indication.
- **Indication**: Approved for adults with multiple myeloma who received at least one prior line of therapy including a proteasome inhibitor and immunomodulatory agent — as early as first relapse.
- **Safety**: Boxed warnings for embryo-fetal toxicity and serious venous/arterial thromboembolism; available only through the ZENBEXUS REMS restricted distribution program.
- **Adverse events**: 90.2% experienced neutropenia, 78.9% experienced infections; 7.8% discontinued due to adverse reactions; 4.9% fatal adverse reaction rate.
- **Pipeline**: A second CELMoD (mezigdomide) is under FDA review with a target decision date of May 13, 2027.
