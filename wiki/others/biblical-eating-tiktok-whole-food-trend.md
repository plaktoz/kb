## Summary

"Biblical eating," a TikTok-driven trend where Christian influencers encourage diets based on scripture-mentioned foods, has gained traction among MAHA (Make America Healthy Again) proponents. Nutrition experts say many promoted foods are genuinely healthy, but caution that the diet isn't formally studied and can become problematic when nutrition advice comes from unqualified influencers rather than professionals.

## Core Concepts

[[Biblical Eating]] emphasizes whole, minimally processed foods like olive oil, fish, and sourdough bread, resembling the [[Mediterranean Diet]] in composition but rooted in scripture rather than nutrition research. The trend illustrates a broader pattern of [[Diet Culture|social-media-driven nutrition trends]], where faith-based or lifestyle framing can improve adherence and mindfulness, but also risks channeling health authority away from qualified professionals toward influencers, per Emory researcher Josh Howard.

## Key Takeaways

- **Promoted foods**: Raw milk, lamb, beef, pomegranate, sourdough bread, goat meat, figs, fish, raw honey, butter, salt, olive oil.
- **Expert view**: Many of the foods can be part of a healthy diet — the pattern avoids common restrictive-diet pitfalls like eliminating entire food groups.
- **Key risk**: The diet is not formally studied, and influencer-driven health advice can undermine trust in qualified nutrition professionals.
- **Adherence benefit**: The spiritual framing may genuinely improve mindfulness and sustainability of healthy eating habits for some people.
