## Summary

A secondary analysis of the [[COSMOS Trial]], presented at NUTRITION 2026, found that adults 60+ taking a daily multivitamin for three years reported significantly better functional health than those on placebo — driven mainly by reduced symptom burden (fatigue, shortness of breath, swelling) rather than physical-ability gains alone. Cocoa extract supplementation showed no overall benefit but did help participants with congestive heart failure specifically; the findings are preliminary and not yet peer-reviewed.

## Core Concepts

- [[COSMOS Trial]] — large randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 16,000+ older adults testing a commercial multivitamin (Centrum Silver) and cocoa flavanol supplement
- [[Functional Health]] — a person's ability to independently perform daily activities (walking, climbing stairs, dressing, bathing); considered a core marker of healthy aging
- [[Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire]] — validated tool measuring cardiovascular symptoms, physical limitations, and quality of life, used to score the trial's functional health outcomes
- [[FGF21]] is unrelated here, but the same "complement, not substitute" framing applies: multivitamins are positioned as an adjunct to exercise and diet, not a replacement (see [[Protein Restriction]] for a contrasting supplement-vs-lifestyle nutrition debate)

## Key Takeaways

- **Multivitamin group showed better functional health scores** than placebo after 3 years, mainly via reduced fatigue, shortness of breath, and swelling
- **Cocoa extract showed no overall benefit** but a signal of improvement specifically in participants with congestive heart failure
- **Findings are preliminary** — not yet peer-reviewed, so effect sizes and generalizability remain uncertain
- **Experts stress multivitamins are a complement, not a substitute**, for exercise, sleep, a heart-healthy diet, and medical care in healthy aging
- **Potential mechanisms** include better blood pressure regulation, vascular function, reduced inflammation, and prevention of deficiencies like anemia

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Symptom Burden vs Physical Capacity]]**: the trial's benefit showed up mostly in reduced symptoms (fatigue, breathlessness) rather than measured physical performance — a useful reminder that "functional health" outcomes can improve through reduced suffering even when underlying physical capacity hasn't measurably changed.
