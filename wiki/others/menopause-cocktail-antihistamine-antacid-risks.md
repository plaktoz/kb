## Summary

A viral social media trend combines over-the-counter antihistamines (cetirizine/[[Zyrtec]]) and antacids (famotidine/[[Pepcid]]) as a DIY "menopause cocktail" for hot flashes and night sweats. Medical experts warn against it: no clinical trials support this combination for menopause, long-term antihistamine use increases dementia risk in older adults, and the two drugs combined may cause additive sedation. The article illustrates how health misinformation spreads when conventional care feels inaccessible.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Menopause Symptom Management]]** — hot flashes, night sweats, sleep disruption; evidence-based options include hormone therapy and low-dose antidepressants
- **[[Anticholinergic Effects]]** — class of drug side effects from antihistamines; linked to dementia risk in older adults with long-term use
- **[[Health Misinformation Spread]]** — mechanism by which unproven OTC combinations go viral when medical care feels expensive or inaccessible
- **[[Off-Label Drug Use]]** — using medications for unapproved indications; high risk when driven by social media rather than clinical evidence

## Key Takeaways

- **No clinical evidence**: no trials have tested cetirizine + famotidine specifically for menopause symptoms
- **Dementia risk**: long-term antihistamine use in older adults associated with increased dementia risk
- **Additive sedation**: combining antihistamine and famotidine may cause excessive sedation
- **FDA-approved alternatives exist**: hormone therapy and SSRIs/SNRIs have established safety profiles for menopause
- **Misinformation vector**: social media health trends thrive when conventional care feels inaccessible or unresponsive
