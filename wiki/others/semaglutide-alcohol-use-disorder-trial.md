## Summary

A University of Colorado Anschutz trial found that oral [[Semaglutide]] reduced heavy drinking days, cravings, and alcohol-related problems in 50 adults with moderate-to-severe alcohol use disorder, marking a potential first new treatment avenue since 2006. Only three medications are currently FDA-approved for alcohol use disorder, and researchers chose the oral pill form specifically because it may be more acceptable to patients than the injectable formulations tested previously. Larger, longer-term trials are planned to confirm the findings and establish optimal dosing.

## Core Concepts

- [[Semaglutide]] — GLP-1 drug behind Ozempic and Wegovy, tested here for alcohol use disorder
- [[Alcohol Use Disorder]] — chronic, undertreated condition affecting an estimated 27.9 million Americans annually
- [[University of Colorado Anschutz]] — institution running the trial
- [[GLP-1 Receptor Agonist]] — drug class mechanism previously associated with appetite suppression

## Key Takeaways

- Oral semaglutide reduced heavy drinking days and cravings in a 50-person trial
- No new alcohol use disorder medication has been approved since 2006
- Only 3 FDA-approved medications currently exist for alcohol use disorder
- Oral form was chosen over injectable for better patient acceptability
- Side effects were mostly mild; participants also used cannabis less
- Larger trials are planned to confirm results and establish dosing
