## Summary

A Kaiser Permanente study of 13,585 uterine cancer patients found the Black-White survival gap is 43% wider in women under 65 than in older women, and that this disparity is driven by younger Black women being three times more likely to be diagnosed with aggressive tumor types — not by insurance access, since all patients studied had equal coverage.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Aggressive Tumor Type Disparity]]**: Younger Black women (under 65) were 3x more likely to be diagnosed with aggressive uterine tumor types than younger White women, compared to a 2x disparity among older patients — explaining most of the age-related survival gap.
- **[[Insurance Access Ruled Out]]**: Because all Kaiser Permanente members have equal insurance coverage, the study could isolate tumor biology/type as the driver of the survival gap, refuting the prior hypothesis that pre-Medicare insurance gaps explained the disparity.
- **[[Racial Disparity in Uterine Cancer Mortality]]**: Black women in the U.S. are nearly twice as likely as White women to die from uterine cancer overall.
- **[[Unknown Etiology]]**: Researchers found no significant difference in known cancer-predisposing genes between groups, leaving environmental exposures (e.g., pesticides, toxins) as an unconfirmed hypothesis.

## Key Takeaways

- **Study scale**: 13,585 women (2,055 Black, 11,530 White) diagnosed with uterine cancer between 2005-2022 at Kaiser Permanente Northern/Southern California.
- **Gap magnitude**: Survival disparity was 43% wider among patients under 65 vs. older patients.
- **Tumor-type disparity by age**: 3x higher aggressive-tumor risk for younger Black women vs. 2x for older Black women, relative to White counterparts.
- **Ruled out factors**: Insurance, demographics, diagnostic speed, treatment, and socioeconomic status did not significantly explain the age-related gap difference.
- **Trend improving**: The overall Black-White survival gap has narrowed in recent years, likely due to advances in chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: The survival gap between Black and White uterine cancer patients is significantly wider in women under 65, and this is driven by tumor aggressiveness rather than insurance access.

**Q2**: What specific finding ruled out insurance as the explanation?
**A**: Because all study participants were Kaiser Permanente members with equal insurance coverage, researchers could rule out pre-Medicare insurance gaps and isolate tumor type as the main driver.

**Q3**: What is the practical implication of this research?
**A**: It highlights the need for research into why younger Black women develop more aggressive uterine tumors, and for care tailored to this younger population rather than assuming insurance-focused interventions will close the gap.
