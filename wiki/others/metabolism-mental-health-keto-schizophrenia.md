## Summary

Stanford researcher Shebani Sethi's field of "metabolic psychiatry" grew out of a 2009 case where a ketogenic dietary intervention alleviated a patient's schizophrenia symptoms after years of failed medication. The emerging field studies ketogenic metabolic therapy as a potential clinical treatment for schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and depression, distinct from keto's popular weight-loss use.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Metabolic Psychiatry]]**: An emerging clinical field studying how metabolic processes — not just neurochemistry — underlie psychiatric conditions and how dietary interventions might treat them.
- **[[Ketogenic Metabolic Therapy]]**: Clinical, measured use of a ketogenic diet targeted specifically at treating mental health disorders, distinct from popular weight-loss keto diets.
- **[[Shebani Sethi]]**: Stanford physician whose 2009 observation of a patient's schizophrenia remission during a weight-loss program's dietary intervention launched her research into diet-based psychiatric treatment.

## Key Takeaways

- A 2009 case showed ketogenic dietary change alleviating schizophrenia symptoms after medication failed.
- Metabolic psychiatry studies diet's role in schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and depression.
- Growing popular interest in keto diets has prompted more clinical, rather than lifestyle, research.
- Sethi has since published further peer-reviewed research on metabolic processes in psychiatric conditions.
