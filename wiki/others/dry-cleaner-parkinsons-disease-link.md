## Summary

A nationwide Medicare case-control study of over 19 million older adults found a clear distance-response relationship between living near a dry cleaner and Parkinson's disease risk, with those within 500 feet showing 14% higher odds than those four to five miles away. Researchers suggest chlorinated solvents like trichloroethylene, historically used in dry cleaning, may be a remediable environmental risk factor.

## Core Concepts

- [[Trichloroethylene]] (TCE) — a neurotoxic, environmentally persistent chlorinated solvent historically used in dry cleaning, implicated as a possible contributor to Parkinson's disease
- [[Distance-Response Relationship]] — an epidemiological pattern where risk decreases progressively as residential distance from a hazard source increases
- [[Parkinson's Disease]] — a neurodegenerative disease where genetics explain less than 10% of cases, driving interest in environmental causes
- [[Case-Control Study]] — the nationwide Medicare-based design (180,982 cases vs. 18.8 million controls) used to detect the association

## Key Takeaways

- **Core finding**: Living within 0.10 miles of a dry cleaner was associated with 14% higher odds of Parkinson's disease versus living 4-5 miles away.
- **Strongest subgroup**: Parkinson's with gait disturbance showed a 23% higher odds within 0.10 miles; dementia/cognitive-decline cases showed 25% higher odds.
- **No tremor link**: The claims-defined tremor group showed no significant association, possibly due to incomplete coding.
- **Sex difference**: The association was present in both sexes but stronger and extended farther in men.
- **Geographic spread**: Stronger associations clustered in Dallas-Fort Worth, Detroit, southern Illinois, and the NYC-NJ region, among others.
- **Key limitation**: Dry-cleaner proximity is only a proxy for actual solvent exposure, not a direct measurement.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this study?
**A**: Residential proximity to a dry cleaner is associated with higher odds of developing Parkinson's disease, following a distance-response pattern where risk is highest closest to the dry cleaner and weakens with distance.

**Q2**: What was the key data point demonstrating this relationship?
**A**: People living within 500 feet (0.10 miles) of a dry cleaner had 14% higher odds of Parkinson's disease than those living four to five miles away, based on a nationwide Medicare case-control study of over 19 million people.

**Q3**: Why do researchers suspect dry cleaners specifically, and what is the key limitation of the finding?
**A**: Dry cleaners historically used chlorinated solvents like trichloroethylene, which are neurotoxic and persist in soil and groundwater; however, proximity to a dry cleaner is only a proxy for actual solvent exposure, since individual exposure levels were not measured.
