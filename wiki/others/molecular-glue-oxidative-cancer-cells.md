## Summary

Dana-Farber Cancer Institute researchers built a systematic screening platform for discovering molecular glue degraders and used it to find the first molecular glue metabolically activated by glutathionylation — meaning it only works inside cells with elevated oxidative stress, a hallmark more common in cancer cells than normal ones. The discovery, published in Nature, suggests molecular glues can be more context-dependent and tunable than previously understood.

## Core Concepts

- [[Molecular Glue Degrader]] — a small molecule that binds an E3 ligase (a protein-tagging enzyme) and redirects it to mark a disease-related protein for cellular disposal
- [[Protein Degradation Therapy]] — a drug approach that eliminates disease-causing proteins via the cell's own recycling machinery, rather than merely inhibiting them
- [[Glutathionylation]] — a metabolic modification, discovered here for the first time to activate a molecular glue, that only occurs under oxidative stress conditions common in cancer cells
- [[E3 Ligase]] — one of ~600 human enzymes that tag proteins for destruction; only a small handful have been exploited by degrader drugs so far

## Key Takeaways

- **Platform innovation**: A magnetic-bead screening system tests E3 ligases against drug-compound libraries to systematically find new molecular glue candidates.
- **Key discovery**: The compound M12 only functions as a molecular glue after being altered by glutathionylation, a byproduct of oxidative stress.
- **Cancer relevance**: Oxidative stress is more common in cancer cells than normal cells, suggesting context-selective, potentially safer targeting.
- **Versatility**: Activated M12 could be tuned to degrade multiple cancer-related proteins, including SMARCA2, WEE1, and CDK7.
- **Scale of opportunity**: Existing degrader drugs use only a small fraction of the ~600 E3 ligases in the human genome; this platform could expand that range significantly.
- **Historical context**: Builds on the 2014 discovery that lenalidomide works as a molecular glue degrader, which first showed "undruggable" transcription factors could be targeted this way.
