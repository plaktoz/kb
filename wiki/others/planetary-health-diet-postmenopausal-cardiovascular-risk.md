## Summary

An observational study of nearly 67,000 postmenopausal women (Women's Health Initiative, presented at NUTRITION 2026) found the highest-adherence quintile to the [[Planetary Health Diet]] had a 28% lower cardiovascular disease risk, 34% lower coronary heart disease risk, and 22% lower stroke risk than the lowest-adherence quintile, with benefits appearing even at moderate adherence. Findings are preliminary (conference abstract, not yet peer-reviewed) and observational, so causation cannot be established.

## Core Concepts

- [[Planetary Health Diet]] — a plant-forward eating pattern (whole grains, fruit, vegetables, nuts, legumes; limited fish/dairy/meat) designed for both human and environmental health, similar in composition to the [[Mediterranean Diet]] and [[DASH Diet]]
- [[Women's Health Initiative]] — the long-running observational cohort (67,000 postmenopausal women, 8.2-year median follow-up) this analysis draws from
- [[Postmenopausal Cardiovascular Risk]] — rises after menopause as estrogen decline alters cholesterol, blood pressure, and fat distribution
- [[Dose-Response Relationship]] — each 10-point increase in diet-adherence score was associated with reduced cardiovascular and coronary heart disease risk

## Key Takeaways

- **28% lower cardiovascular disease risk** and **34% lower coronary heart disease risk** in the highest- vs lowest-adherence quintile
- **22% lower stroke risk, 31% lower heart failure risk** also observed at highest adherence
- **Benefits scale with adherence**: even moderate diet changes showed statistically significant risk reduction — no need for an all-or-nothing overhaul
- **Practical starting steps**: fill half the plate with vegetables, swap processed meats for oily fish or legumes, choose whole grains, replace 1-2 meat meals/week with beans or lentils
- **Caveats flagged by independent experts**: findings are an unreviewed abstract, and the observational design cannot prove the diet itself caused the risk reduction

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Dose-Response Reasoning]]**: The fact that risk reduction scales continuously with adherence (rather than only appearing at an extreme threshold) is one of the classic Bradford Hill criteria epidemiologists use to judge whether an association is more likely causal than coincidental — a stronger signal than a simple high-vs-low group comparison alone.
