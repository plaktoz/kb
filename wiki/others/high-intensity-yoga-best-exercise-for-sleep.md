## Summary
A meta-analysis of 30 randomized controlled trials (2,500+ participants) found that high-intensity yoga — two 30-minute sessions per week for 8-10 weeks — produced significantly better sleep improvements than walking, aerobic exercise, resistance training, or traditional Chinese exercises like tai chi and qi gong. Researchers speculate the benefit comes from yoga's combination of breath control and resistance-based movement.

## Core Concepts
Researchers at [[Harbin Sport University]] ranked exercise types by their effect on [[Sleep Quality]] using a network meta-analysis. [[High-Intensity Yoga]] — distinct from slow-flow yoga, involving faster pose transitions using body weight for resistance — ranked first, ahead of walking, [[Resistance Training]], and [[Aerobic Exercise]]. Possible mechanisms include breath control activating the [[Parasympathetic Nervous System]] via the [[Vagus Nerve]], and yoga's effect on regulating brainwave activity to promote deeper sleep.

## Key Takeaways
- **Optimal prescription**: Two 30-minute high-intensity yoga sessions per week for 8-10 weeks was the most effective regimen studied.
- **Ranking**: Yoga > walking > resistance training > aerobic exercise > traditional Chinese exercises, for sleep improvement.
- **Contrasting evidence**: A separate 2025 study found tai chi matched cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) at 15-month follow-up, suggesting different exercises may suit different people or timeframes.
- **Caveat**: Researchers caution the evidence base remains limited and call for further high-quality research.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core finding of this meta-analysis?
**A**: High-intensity yoga, done twice weekly for 30 minutes over 8-10 weeks, was the most effective exercise type for improving sleep among 30 studies compared.

**Q2**: What evidence supports this ranking?
**A**: A network meta-analysis combining 30 randomized controlled trials with over 2,500 participants found yoga had a "significantly superior" effect on sleep versus aerobic exercise specifically, and outranked walking, resistance training, and traditional Chinese exercises.

**Q3**: How should this finding be applied, given its limitations?
**A**: It suggests high-intensity yoga is a reasonable first choice for improving sleep, but given the limited study count and population specificity, it shouldn't replace consulting a doctor for persistent sleep disturbances.
