## Summary

A preliminary Rutgers study found that women aged 50-79 who restricted eating to an 8-9 hour daily window, alongside a calorie deficit, showed greater improvements in spatial planning and problem-solving than those eating over a typical 12-hour window, despite similar weight loss. The trial was small (47 participants) and has not yet undergone peer review.

## Core Concepts

- [[Time-Restricted Eating]] — limiting food intake to an 8-9 hour daily window, ending ~4 hours before sleep
- [[Circadian Rhythm]] — proposed mechanism linking meal timing to cognitive outcomes
- [[Sue Shapses]] — Rutgers professor and lead investigator
- [[Cognitive Decline]] — aging-related decline the intervention aims to mitigate
- [[Calorie Restriction]] — controlled variable shared by both study groups

## Key Takeaways

- Time-restricted group scored better on spatial planning and problem-solving tests
- Both groups lost ~15 lbs; weight loss alone didn't explain the cognitive gains
- Restricted eating window ended about 4 hours before bedtime
- Study was small (47 women) and not yet peer-reviewed
- No significant group differences in reaction time or multitasking
