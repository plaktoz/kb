## Summary
Researchers at Emory University and Georgia Tech built [[CapuchinAI]], an AI system combining facial recognition and touchscreen testing to automate individualized cognitive testing of wild capuchin monkeys in their natural environment. Field tests in Costa Rica's Taboga Forest Reserve showed the system identifies individual capuchins with 97% accuracy and that wild monkeys rapidly learned the touchscreen-reward association, providing the first scalable, systematic method for studying primate cognition outside the lab. The proof-of-concept opens a path for other researchers to adapt the low-cost, open-source platform to study cognition across different wild primate species.

## Core Concepts
- **[[CapuchinAI]]** — a battery-powered field platform combining a webcam, touchscreen, [[Raspberry Pi]], and food dispenser to run individualized cognitive tasks on wild capuchins.
- **[[YOLO]] (You Only Look Once)** — the open-source computer vision model used to build facial recognition that distinguishes individual capuchins with 97% accuracy.
- Individual-tailored testing — the system recognizes a specific monkey and presents a task matched to that individual's progress level, rather than a one-size-fits-all stimulus.
- Field validation — 16 wild capuchins engaged with the prototype during the pilot, showing significant individual differences in learning speed and interaction style.
- Legacy of **[[Frans de Waal]]** — the project builds on his pioneering work in animal cognition at Emory's Living Links Center.
- Planned cognitive domains for future testing: learning ability, impulse control, cognitive flexibility, short- and long-term memory.

## Key Takeaways
- CapuchinAI is the first scalable, systematic method to evaluate wild (not lab-housed) primate cognition.
- The system limits per-individual rewards per session to prevent a dominant monkey from monopolizing access.
- Facial recognition also filters out non-target wildlife (e.g., coatis) from triggering the system.
- The entire rig runs 8 hours on a lightweight battery pack and withstood field conditions and animal aggression.
- Researchers published an open-source guide and blueprint so other labs can replicate the platform for other primate species.
