## Summary

deVere Group argues that [[Anthropic]]'s planned $2 trillion IPO, pitched on a $30 trillion+ total addressable market, is reviving [[AI Bubble]] fears. The bear case rests on 435x trailing revenue, heavy customer concentration, billions in compute burn, regulatory and government-relations risk, and Treasury yields above 5% that make risk-free returns competitive. Bulls point to 14-fold revenue growth and a $120 billion run-rate that cuts the multiple to about 17x, so the IPO will test how much investors will pay for AI's promise when bonds pay them to wait.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Anthropic]] IPO**: Targeting a $2T valuation, possibly the biggest IPO ever. Marketing could start the week of 9 November, with trading before Thanksgiving (per [[Bloomberg]]).
- **[[Total Addressable Market]]**: Anthropic will reportedly claim a TAM above $30 trillion, higher than [[SpaceX]]'s, which has drawn Wall Street scepticism.
- **[[Concentration Risk]]**: Leaked financials seen by [[Morningstar]] show two customers made up nearly 25% of 2025 revenue.
- **[[Price-to-Sales Ratio]]**: 435x trailing 2025 revenue vs ~17x the expected $120B annualized run-rate ([[SemiAnalysis]]'s Joey Brookhart).
- **Prospectus risks**: $42B 2025 net loss (mostly non-cash), $8B+ operating loss, and $518B in future compute and infrastructure commitments. It also flags revenue risk from [[Donald Trump]]'s February order barring the DOD from using Anthropic models after it was designated a supply-chain risk.
- **[[Michael Burry]]**: Has moved his AI-bubble timeline forward and is adding leverage to shorts through put options, which are cheap because the [[VIX]] is low.
- **[[Andrew Bailey]]** / [[Bank of England]]: "Everybody is currently priced to be a winner... not everybody is a winner." The bank's Financial Policy Committee warns of a "sharper correction" than July's.
- **[[Nigel Green]]**: deVere CEO. He says compute spend is "wiping out revenue" and that record IPOs often precede market falls.
- **Rate backdrop**: The [[Federal Reserve]] hiked for the first time since 2023, and the 10-year Treasury yield hit its highest level since 2002. Invesco's Paul Jackson is shifting money into bonds.
- **Regulatory risk**: The EU's heavy regulation has pushed AI labs to the US, but US policy can shift with elections and incumbents.

## Key Takeaways

- **Valuation multiple**: $2T equals 435x 2025 revenue but ~17x the projected $120B run-rate.
- **Customer concentration**: Two customers made up nearly 25% of 2025 revenue.
- **Growth case**: Q2 revenue up 14-fold to $11.5B, with an adjusted operating profit.
- **Loss and commitments**: $42B 2025 net loss; $518B in future compute commitments.
- **Government risk**: The DOD ban after the supply-chain-risk label is flagged in the prospectus.
- **Yield competition**: Treasuries above 5% pull capital away from speculative IPOs.
- **Record-IPO signal**: Green says record IPOs often precede market declines.
- **Burry's shorts**: Burry is using cheap puts, as he expects the bubble to burst sooner.
- **Not everyone wins**: Bailey says the technology can succeed while many companies fail.
- **SpaceX precedent**: Fell 50%+ from its post-IPO high within seven weeks, then recovered above its offer price.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Risk-free yields set the hurdle for speculative assets** — When safe government debt pays more, every speculative investment has to clear a higher bar, so capital moves toward guaranteed returns and demand for risky listings falls. · *model:* [[Opportunity Cost]]
→ explains: Yield competition, Record-IPO signal

**T2. A transformative technology does not guarantee winning firms** — A technology can prove revolutionary while many of its early companies fail. Pricing every player as a winner leaves asset prices open to correction. · *model:* [[Mean Reversion]]
→ explains: Not everyone wins, Burry's shorts, SpaceX precedent

**T3. Revenue concentrated in a few buyers makes valuation fragile** — When a few customers or one key revenue stream drive results, a single disappointing spending decision can undercut the growth projections a valuation depends on. · *model:* [[Concentration Risk]]
→ explains: Customer concentration, Government risk, Burry's shorts

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central argument about Anthropic's $2 trillion IPO?
**A**: AI as a technology is likely here to stay, but Anthropic's valuation may outrun its fundamentals. Its high revenue multiple, customer concentration, compute burn, and a high-yield bond market revive bubble fears.

**Q2**: Why do Anthropic's revenue multiples vary so widely, from 435x to 17x?
**A**: A $2T valuation is 435x trailing 2025 revenue but only about 17x the $120B annualized run-rate investors expect by year-end. The gap depends on whether you value past results or projected growth.

**Q3**: How should an investor think about the IPO given current interest rates?
**A**: With Treasuries yielding above 5%, the best since 2007, the IPO has to beat a guaranteed return. Investors should ask whether the extra risk is worth it, as some fund managers are already rotating into bonds.
