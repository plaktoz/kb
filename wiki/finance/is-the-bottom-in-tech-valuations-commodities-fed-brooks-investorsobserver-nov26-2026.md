## Summary
The InvestorsObserver newsletter asks whether the AI selloff has found a bottom, noting tech has given back its AI premium and trades at 2019 forward valuations, though earnings estimates may still be too high. It also covers falling commodity prices, a spending-over-income gap, Anthropic's return to its IPO price, foreign central bank Treasury outflows, and Robin Brooks's claim that the Fed is causing the meltdown.

- [[Forward P/E]] valuation: tech is back to its 2019 level, and chipmakers are the cheapest ever on this measure.
- [[Earnings Estimates]] risk: analysts have never entered a bear market with estimates that were too low.
- [[Commodity Disinflation]]: the Bloomberg Commodity Index is down 14% from its September peak.

- [[Dean Baker]] on one-off investment gains inflating Amazon and Alphabet profits.

## Key Takeaways
- **Valuation Reset**: Tech forward earnings multiples are back to 2019 levels.
- **Estimate Risk**: Analysts have never gone into a bear market with estimates too low.
- **Commodities Falling**: Bloomberg Commodity Index is down 14% from September, six weekly declines.
- **Consumer Strain**: October spending rose 6.4% versus income growth of 4.8%.
- **Credit Card Debt**: Balances hit a record $1.29 trillion in Q3.
- **Anthropic IPO**: Shares returned to the $83 IPO price after a three-day slide.
- **Inflated Profits**: Baker says half of Amazon's 2026 profit came from Anthropic gains.
- **Treasury Outflows**: Foreign official Treasury holdings fell from $4.01 trillion to $3.67 trillion.
- **Brooks on Fed**: Falling inflation expectations plus expected hikes raise real rates.
- **Citi Path**: One December hike, a pause, then cuts in the second half of 2027.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Multiples are only as good as the earnings estimate** — A forward P/E depends on the "E", which is a forecast; a cheap multiple can mislead if estimates are too high. Bigger crashes have followed bigger estimate misses.
→ explains: Valuation Reset, Estimate Risk

**T2. Real rates tighten when inflation expectations fall** — Real rates equal nominal rates minus expected inflation, so if expectations drop while the expected policy path stays high, policy tightens even with no new hike.
→ explains: Brooks on Fed, Citi Path

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the newsletter's central question and its key observation about valuations?
**A**: It asks whether the bottom is in. Tech has already given back its AI premium and trades at 2019 forward valuations, but those multiples rest on earnings estimates that may still be too high.

**Q2**: How does Robin Brooks argue the Fed is worsening the selloff?
**A**: Brent crude and two-year inflation expectations have fallen, yet markets still expect higher rates, so real interest rates rise. Brooks says this makes policy more restrictive even before the next hike.

**Q3**: What does Citigroup's forecast imply for investors?
**A**: Citi expects one more quarter-point hike in December, a pause in early 2027, and cuts starting in the second half of 2027. Investors may have to wait until then for rate relief.
