## Summary

U.S. headline CPI accelerated to 3.7% year over year in September 2026, up from 3.4% in August and above the 3.6% forecast, while [[Core CPI]] rose 2.6% against a 2.5% forecast. A surge in gasoline prices drove the jump, and with both headline and core beating expectations, the [[Federal Reserve]] has less room to cut rates without risking another inflation setback. Markets responded with lower stock futures and higher [[Treasury Yields]], adding pressure on growth stocks and the [[Nasdaq 100]].

## Core Concepts

- [[Consumer Price Index]] — September headline +3.7% y/y and +0.6% m/m (matching the monthly forecast); core +2.6% y/y and +0.3% m/m.
- [[Headline vs Core Inflation]] — both measures exceeded expectations, weakening the case that the overshoot is energy-only.
- [[Energy Price Shock]] — gasoline +9.1% m/m and +34.2% y/y; the energy index rose 5.2% in the month while food was unchanged.
- [[Second-Round Effects]] — higher transport and production costs may be passed on to consumers, keeping inflation elevated beyond energy.
- [[Restrictive Monetary Policy]] — cutting too soon could stimulate spending and investment, making inflation harder to control.
- [[Personal Consumption Expenditures Price Index]] — the Fed's preferred gauge, weighed alongside employment conditions.
- [[Yield Curve]] — the 2-year yield (~4.91%) tracks near-term Fed expectations; the 10-year reached ~5.29%.
- [[Discounted Cash Flow]] — higher yields raise the discount rate on future earnings, hitting long-dated growth valuations.
- [[Growth Stocks]] vs. cash-generative businesses — strong balance sheets and resilient demand fare better; energy producers may benefit.
- Related: [[september-cpi-preview-energy-headline-vs-core-fed-oct2026]], [[august-cpi-2026-rate-hike-odds-85-stock-rally]].

## Key Takeaways

- **Headline Beat**: CPI 3.7% y/y vs. 3.6% forecast, up from 3.4%.
- **Core Beat**: Core CPI 2.6% y/y vs. 2.5% forecast; +0.3% m/m.
- **Gasoline Surge**: Gas +9.1% m/m, +34.2% y/y; energy index +5.2%.
- **Food Flat**: Food prices unchanged in September.
- **Pass-Through Risk**: Fuel costs may spread into shipping, logistics and consumer prices.
- **Rate Cuts Harder**: Hot readings make an early Fed cut harder to justify.
- **Yields Climb**: 2-year ~4.91%, 10-year ~5.29% after the report.
- **Growth Stock Headwind**: Higher discount rates pressure Nasdaq 100 and high-multiple stocks.
- **Defensive Quality**: Strong cash flow and resilient earnings best withstand higher-for-longer.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Higher discount rates shrink the value of distant cash flows most** — When risk-free yields rise, bonds offer a better alternative return and future earnings are discounted more heavily, so assets whose value depends on profits far in the future lose the most. · *model:* [[Discounted Cash Flow]]
→ explains: Growth Stock Headwind, Yields Climb, Defensive Quality

**T2. Input-cost shocks propagate through pass-through** — A rise in a broadly used input such as energy raises costs across transport and production, and firms passing those costs on spreads inflation beyond the original sector.
→ explains: Gasoline Surge, Pass-Through Risk

**T3. Easing into unresolved inflation stokes demand and prolongs it** — Lowering borrowing costs before inflation is clearly falling stimulates spending and investment, so a central bank needs sustained evidence of disinflation before cutting.
→ explains: Rate Cuts Harder, Headline Beat, Core Beat
