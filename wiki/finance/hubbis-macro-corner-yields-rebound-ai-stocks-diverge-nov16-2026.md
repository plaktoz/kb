## Summary
Hubbis's weekly macro review says firm US hiring and a hotter-than-expected CPI pushed Treasury yields higher after the prior week's flight to safety. Nvidia's CEO departure, TSMC and Applied Materials sales, and CoreWeave's financing strain show the AI trade can no longer be read through a single index. It frames the week for Asian wealth managers around which risks a portfolio is taking and whether it is paid for them.

## Core Concepts
- [[US Treasury Yields]]: the two-year rose from 4.51% (6 Nov) to 4.86% (12 Nov); 10-year auction cleared at 5.020%, 30-year at 5.441%.
- [[Federal Reserve]]: Governor [[Christopher Waller]] said core CPI near 0.3% or higher would warrant considering a 50-basis-point hike in December.
- [[Nvidia]]: [[Jensen Huang]] resigned as CEO; [[Colette Kress]] is interim CEO; shares fell as much as 14% intraday.
- [[CoreWeave]]: Q3 revenue US$3.66 billion, net interest expense US$846 million above operating income US$207 million, US$132.5 billion backlog.
- [[TSMC]] and [[Applied Materials]]: October revenue up 59.7% and fiscal Q4 revenue up 53% year on year.
- **Asia inflation**: China producer prices +5.7% versus consumer +0.7%; India headline 6.09%, above the RBI tolerance band.
- **Oil and UK**: Brent settled at US$95.82 on Saudi and Hormuz disruption; UK Q3 GDP +0.7%, services +0.9%, construction -0.7%.
- [[Concentration Risk]] and [[Diversification]]: AI exposures sharing customers or delivery dates overstate diversification.

## Key Takeaways
- **Jobs and CPI**: October payrolls +165,000; headline CPI +0.4% month on month, core +0.3%.
- **Rates Repricing**: US two-year yield rose 17 basis points on Tuesday after CPI.
- **Auction Concession**: The 30-year cleared slightly above market yield, implying modest buyer concession.
- **Hedge Test**: Treasuries rallied on the equity shock, then fell on the inflation shock.
- **AI Divergence**: Chip and equipment sales surged while CoreWeave's interest cost exceeded operating income.
- **Cash Timing**: About US$9 billion of expected CoreWeave prepayments matters more near term than the backlog.
- **Index Moves**: S&P 500 and Nasdaq each closed down 0.4% Thursday, still 2.9% above prior Friday.
- **Currency**: Dollar strength cushions unhedged US holdings but raises the cost of dollar liabilities.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. A hedge is judged against the source of the shock** — A bond meant to offset equity losses must be assessed against what caused the sell-off, while a bond funding a known payment works through both moves.
→ explains: Hedge Test, Rates Repricing

**T2. Shared drivers overstate diversification** — Holdings that share customers or delivery dates carry correlated risk, so holding many names can overstate how much risk is diversified. · *model:* [[Diversification]]
→ explains: AI Divergence, Cash Timing

**T3. Near-term cash timing can outweigh long-dated commitments** — A prepayment due next quarter can matter more to financing than a much larger multi-year backlog.
→ explains: Cash Timing, AI Divergence

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core argument about this week's markets?
**A**: A firm jobs report and hotter CPI drove yields higher after the prior week's flight to Treasuries, while diverging AI company results show the trade can't be judged through one index. The practical question is which risks a portfolio takes and whether it is paid for them.

**Q2**: What data shows CoreWeave's financing strain?
**A**: Q3 net interest expense of US$846 million exceeded operating income of US$207 million, producing a US$709 million net loss, while it spent US$9.44 billion on capital items. It expects about US$9 billion in customer prepayments over the next three months.

**Q3**: How should advisers apply this to client portfolios?
**A**: Test bond holdings against both the equity sell-off and the inflation surprise, and compare cash received with cash committed over the same period for AI holdings. Check whether an operator, its suppliers and its lenders share customers or delivery dates, and treat currency exposure as a separate decision.
