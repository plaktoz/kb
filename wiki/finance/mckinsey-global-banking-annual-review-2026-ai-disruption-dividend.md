## Summary
Banking earned a record $1.3 trillion in 2025, but the technology "grace period" that let banks follow new tech slowly is ending because customers of all ages are adopting AI faster than any prior technology. McKinsey argues the AI dividend goes to banks that move at speed: three-speed innovation, institutional intelligence (data, context, encoded judgment), and machine-speed controls. Individual AI productivity gains rarely reach the P&L unless end-to-end processes are redesigned.

## Core Concepts
- [[Global Banking Annual Review]]: McKinsey's yearly report on industry performance, with 2025 net income up 7 percent to $1.3 trillion.
- [[Technology Grace Period]]: banks' "smart followership" worked because high-value customers aged 55+ adopted digital slowly.
- [[Neobanks]] such as [[Revolut]], [[Nubank]], and [[WeBank]]: leaner models with ROEs of roughly 20 to 35 percent or more.
- [[Agentic Commerce]]: payment networks ([[Visa Intelligent Commerce]], [[Mastercard Agent Pay]]) let verified software agents transact.
- [[Large Language Models]] as a new distribution channel: 74 percent of AI-for-banking users go through ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.
- [[Institutional Intelligence]]: three layers (data, context, encoded judgment) so every agent works from what the whole bank knows.
- [[Three-Speed Innovation]]: fast business-as-usual, faster incubators, fastest seed/R&D, with success rates of >90%, ~50%, and ~20%.
- [[Machine-Speed Controls]]: control moves from sampled process checks to approved policies and continuous monitoring of all agent actions.

## Key Takeaways
- **Record Profits**: 2025 net income hit $1.3 trillion; 2026 profit growth seen at 9-11 percent.
- **Grace Period Ends**: AI adoption is the fastest in history across young and old.
- **Customer Behavior**: 23 percent use gen AI for banking; 46,000 consumers surveyed.
- **Trust Advantage**: 62 percent of AI users prefer their primary bank, but half may leave.
- **Productivity Gap**: 80 percent report individual gains; only 6 percent see significant EBIT impact.
- **Task vs Process**: Agents cut task time ~60 percent, yet committees still gate decisions.
- **Redesign Rare**: Only one-quarter of gen AI companies have redesigned work anywhere.
- **Data Obstacle**: 48 percent of bank executives rank data readiness as the biggest AI barrier.
- **Controls Shift**: Banks approve policies and limits, then monitor all agent actions continuously.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Speeding up a non-bottleneck step doesn't speed up the system** — Throughput is set by the slowest stage, so faster tasks inside an unchanged process deliver little system-level gain until the constraint is redesigned. · *model:* [[Theory of Constraints]]
→ explains: Productivity Gap, Task vs Process, Redesign Rare

**T2. Durable advantage comes from assets competitors can't simply buy** — When every firm can buy the same models, the edge lies in accumulated, proprietary assets (data, context, encoded judgment) that compound with use. · *model:* [[Economic Moat]]
→ explains: Data Obstacle, Trust Advantage

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central argument of McKinsey's 2026 banking review?
**A**: The AI disruption dividend will go to banks that execute with both precision and speed, because the technology grace period that let banks follow slowly no longer exists.

**Q2**: Why do individual AI productivity gains fail to show up in bank earnings?
**A**: Technology was designed at the task level, so savings are fractional (an hour or two) and can't be monetized, while processes such as committee approvals remain unchanged. 80 percent report individual gains but only 6 percent attribute significant EBIT to AI.

**Q3**: How should banks organize innovation and controls to move faster?
**A**: Run innovation at three speeds (business-as-usual, incubator, seed/R&D) with different success-rate expectations, and shift controls from process checks to approved policies with continuous monitoring of every agent action and a named human accountable for exceptions.
