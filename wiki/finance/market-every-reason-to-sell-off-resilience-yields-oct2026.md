## Summary

The [[S&P 500]] sits less than 1% below its record and the [[Nasdaq Composite]] is near an all-time high, even though the [[10-Year Treasury Yield]] is at its highest level since 2002. A weak jobs report has also lowered expectations for a [[Federal Reserve Rate Hike]] this year. Strategists argue that the market has shrugged off every reason to sell off, which points to further gains: seasonality and the presidential cycle are bullish, and the AI build-out keeps growth strong enough to absorb higher rates. Valuations may not compress until yields pass about 5.5%.

## Core Concepts

- **Market resilience**: High oil prices, AI-related risks and weakening [[Market Breadth]] have all suggested a pullback, but none has happened yet.
- **[[Sean McLaughlin]]** (All Star Charts): Says the market "is running out of time" to sell off and "the path of least resistance appears to be higher."
- **[[Seasonality]]**: October is traditionally volatile, but it has posted a 63% win rate over the past 20 years.
- **[[Presidential Cycle]]**: Over the past 50 years, the S&P 500 has averaged an 18.3% return in the comparable seven-month window.
- **Yield threshold for valuations**: [[Hardika Singh]] (Fundstrat) says valuations historically start compressing once yields pass 5.5%.
- **Growth offsets rates**: [[UBS]] says yields matter more for stocks when growth falters, which the [[AI Infrastructure]] boom is preventing.
- **[[Ulrike Hoffmann-Burchardi]]** (UBS CIO Americas): Forecasts strong earnings growth and recommends global diversification plus exposure to growth themes.

## Key Takeaways

- **Near record**: S&P 500 sits less than 1% below its record high.
- **Yield extreme**: The 10-year Treasury yield is at its highest level since 2002.
- **October base rate**: October has posted a 63% win rate over 20 years.
- **Cycle tailwind**: The presidential cycle has averaged an 18.3% return over the next seven months.
- **Earnings catalyst**: McLaughlin says good earnings news could spark a significant 3–6 month rally.
- **5.5% line**: Fundstrat says valuations compress once yields pass 5.5%.
- **UBS earnings forecast**: 25% S&P 500 earnings growth this year, 14% in 2027.
- **Allocation advice**: Diversify across the US, Europe and Asia, and tilt toward AI, power, resources and healthcare.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why do strategists argue the stock market's resilience points to further upside?
**A**: The market has ignored high oil prices, AI risks, weak breadth and 20-plus-year-high yields without selling off. McLaughlin says this means "the path of least resistance appears to be higher."

**Q2**: At what level do strategists expect Treasury yields to start hurting stock valuations, and why haven't they hurt yet?
**A**: Fundstrat's Hardika Singh says valuations historically compress once yields pass 5.5%. UBS adds that yields mainly hurt stocks when growth falters, and the US AI build-out is keeping growth strong, with forecast S&P 500 earnings growth of 25% this year.

**Q3**: How does UBS suggest investors position themselves given higher rates and strong AI-driven growth?
**A**: Hoffmann-Burchardi recommends spreading risk across the US, Europe and Asia. She also suggests targeting major growth trends: AI, power and resources, and healthcare.
