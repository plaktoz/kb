## Summary

Microsoft stock rose 28% in a month to $490.39, far outpacing the XLK tech sector ETF's 3% gain, driven by a strong July 29 earnings beat, 43% Azure growth, and $678 billion in commercial backlog. Reaching $600 would require forward EPS of $19.97 to hold up, Azure sustaining 45% constant-currency growth, and free cash flow recovering as capital expenditures peak.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Azure Growth]]**: Grew 43% in the quarter, crossed $100 billion in annual revenue for the first time, with Q1 FY2027 guidance calling for 45% constant-currency growth.
- **[[Commercial RPO]]**: Remaining performance obligations grew 84% to $678 billion, contracted revenue not yet recognized, signaling forward demand visibility.
- **[[Capex Cycle Risk]]**: Full-year capex reached $115.95 billion in FY2026 with Q4 capex more than doubling year over year, while free cash flow fell 23.19% despite operating cash flow rising 30%.
- **[[Hyperscaler Competition]]**: Azure runs neck-and-neck with [[Amazon Web Services]]; [[Alphabet]]'s Google Cloud has closed ground, and [[Oracle]] runs a disruptive multi-cloud database strategy across all three rivals' infrastructure.
- **[[Microsoft 365 Copilot]]**: Passed 30 million paid seats, a key growth lever cited in the bull case.

## Key Takeaways

- MSFT rose 28% in a month to $490.39 versus consensus target of $569.45.
- Analyst positioning is heavily bullish: 14 strong buy, 40 buy, 3 hold, zero sell.
- Q2 non-GAAP EPS of $4.74 beat consensus by 11.81%; revenue up 17.8% to $90.01B.
- Net income rose 31.33%, aided by a $3.2B gain from Microsoft's Anthropic investment.
- Hitting $600 requires forward EPS to hold, Azure sustaining 45% growth, and FCF turning up as capex peaks.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of the article?
**A**: Microsoft's 28% one-month rally is a fundamentals-driven re-rating rather than a speculative melt-up, but reaching $600 depends on Azure sustaining its guided 45% growth and free cash flow recovering as capex peaks.

**Q2**: What specific backlog metric supports the case that Microsoft's growth is not merely speculative?
**A**: Commercial remaining performance obligations grew 84% to $678 billion — contracted revenue not yet recognized — indicating forward growth is already visible in signed contracts rather than projected.

**Q3**: What would have to go wrong for the bear case to materialize?
**A**: If Azure growth decelerates before Microsoft's elevated capital expenditures (which more than doubled in Q4) normalize, the return-on-invested-capital math would deteriorate quickly, especially with More Personal Computing revenue already declining.
