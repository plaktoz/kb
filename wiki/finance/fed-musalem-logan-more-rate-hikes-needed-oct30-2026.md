## Summary

Fed presidents [[Alberto Musalem]] and [[Lorie Logan]] said days after the Fed held rates that more hikes are needed, with Logan calling for at least two more 25-basis-point increases. Futures price roughly a 76% chance of a December hike, and former Fed officials criticized the lack of urgency and Chair [[Kevin Warsh]]'s plan for strategy-only meetings.

## Core Concepts

- [[Federal Reserve]] and the [[FOMC]]: held rates Wednesday with two dissents (Cook, Hammack) favoring a hike.
- [[Monetary Tightening]]: Musalem wants "additional firming... soon"; Logan wants two more quarter-point hikes, then a pause.
- [[Inflation Expectations]]: kept anchored by acting early, per Musalem; PCE inflation runs above 3%.
- [[CME FedWatch]]: pricing about 76% odds of a December quarter-point hike.
- [[Kevin Warsh]]'s plan: one 2027 and two 2028 meetings focused on strategy, not rate decisions.
- Former officials [[Jeffrey Lacker]] and [[James Bullard]]: Lacker sees too little urgency; Bullard opposes skipped meetings.

## Key Takeaways

- **Hawkish Signal**: Musalem says more firming is needed "and soon".
- **Hike Path**: Logan sees at least two more 25bp hikes, then a pause.
- **Market Pricing**: Fed funds futures show about 76% odds of a December hike.
- **Economy**: GDP grew 3.4%, unemployment is 4.2%, and PCE inflation exceeds 3%.
- **Dissents**: Cook and Hammack voted for a hike at this week's hold.
- **Skipping Meetings**: Logan says a skip does not change the destination.
- **Governance Debate**: Bullard opposes Warsh's strategy-only meetings; Lacker suggests more meetings instead.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Early, small adjustments beat late, large ones** — Acting before inflation becomes entrenched costs less than acting abruptly afterward, because expectations are still anchored. Delay raises the eventual cost of correction.
→ explains: Hawkish Signal, Hike Path, Skipping Meetings

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did Musalem and Logan say about future Fed policy?
**A**: Musalem said additional firming will be necessary "and soon," while Logan said the FOMC needs at least two more 25-basis-point hikes. Both see inflation as too high.

**Q2**: What evidence supports the expectation of a December hike?
**A**: CME's FedWatch shows roughly 76% odds of a quarter-point hike in December. Two FOMC voters, Cook and Hammack, already dissented in favor of a hike this week.

**Q3**: Why does Musalem favor moving now rather than later?
**A**: He argues incremental moves while inflation expectations are anchored are less disruptive than larger, abrupt action later. The short-run costs of cutting inflation are lower before it becomes entrenched.
