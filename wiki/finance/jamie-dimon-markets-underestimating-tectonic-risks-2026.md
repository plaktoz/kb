## Summary

JPMorgan Chase posted record Q2 2026 earnings of $7.70/share (+47% YoY), but CEO Jamie Dimon cautioned that structural risks — geopolitical tensions, sticky inflation, large fiscal deficits, elevated asset prices — are building like tectonic plates beneath apparent market stability. Dimon's message is tempered optimism: conditions are favorable now, but investors may be significantly underpricing compounding global risks. The warning mirrors his prior pattern of sounding alarms while continuing to execute well.

## Core Concepts

- [[Jamie Dimon]]'s "[[Tectonic Plates]]" metaphor for slow-building structural macro risks
- [[JPMorgan Chase]] Q2 2026 record earnings vs. one-time Visa gain (adjusted EPS $6.43 vs. reported $7.70)
- [[Sticky Inflation]] + [[Fiscal Deficits]] + [[Geopolitical Risk]] as the compounding risk cluster
- [[Elevated Asset Prices]] as the market's blind spot to underlying structural fragility

## Key Takeaways

- **Record quarter**: $7.70/share, +47% YoY; adjusted $6.43 excluding one-time Visa gain
- **Tectonic metaphor**: Risks shift slowly but "cause meaningful disruptions when they shift or collide"
- **Four risk pillars**: Geopolitical tensions, sticky inflation, large fiscal deficits, elevated asset prices
- **Not a crash call**: Dimon's tone is watchful optimism, not alarm — conditions remain favorable
- **Relevance to ETF holders**: Broad equity exposure (VWRA, CSPX, SPMO, IWMO) faces repricing risk if tectonic shift materializes
