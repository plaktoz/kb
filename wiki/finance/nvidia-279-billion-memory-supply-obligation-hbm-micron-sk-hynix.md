## Summary
Nvidia disclosed $279 billion in supply obligations over the next several years — more than double the $119 billion figure from three months prior — driven primarily by memory procurement for Blackwell GPUs and Vera CPUs. Gross margins of 75% are expected to dip to the "low 70%" range due to rising HBM and DRAM costs, making memory suppliers the primary beneficiaries of Nvidia's AI hardware buildout. The author argues that SK Hynix and Micron, trading at ~6x forward P/E, are significantly undervalued given Nvidia's multi-year commitment reducing boom-bust cycle risk.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nvidia]]** committed $279B in supply obligations — roughly $92B in fiscal 2027, ~$87B in FY2028, and ~$88B in FY2029 — cementing a multi-year memory procurement backlog.
- **[[High Bandwidth Memory]] (HBM)** and **[[DRAM]]** procurement costs are the primary driver of Nvidia's gross margin pressure, with margins guiding down to the "low 70%" range from 75%.
- **[[SK Hynix]]** and **[[Micron Technology]]** are the leading qualified HBM suppliers for Nvidia platforms and the direct beneficiaries of this $279B commitment.
- **[[Blackwell GPU]]** and **[[Vera CPU]]** are the Nvidia products driving the surge in memory procurement needs.
- **[[AI Capex Bubble]]** concerns and memory's historical boom-bust cycles have kept Micron and SK Hynix at depressed valuations (~6x forward P/E) despite structural demand tailwinds.

## Key Takeaways

- **Supply Obligation Doubling**: Nvidia's supply commitments jumped from $119B to $279B in one quarter.
- **Revenue Context**: Nvidia data center revenue hit $89B in Q2 FY2027, up 117% YoY; Q3 guidance is $108B.
- **Margin Headwind**: Gross margins to dip to "low 70s%" by fiscal year-end from current 75%.
- **Memory Supplier Valuations**: SK Hynix and Micron trade near 6x forward P/E — historically cheap.
- **Undervaluation Thesis**: Multi-year Nvidia commitment reduces boom-bust cycle risk that has historically weighed on memory stocks.
- **Qualified Suppliers Only**: Nvidia's HBM supply is concentrated among a small set of qualified partners, limiting competition risk for incumbents.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Mean Reversion]]**: Memory stocks trade at depressed cyclical multiples because investors price in historical boom-bust patterns — but Nvidia's locked-in multi-year obligations structurally reduce the magnitude of the next bust, making mean-reversion to higher multiples plausible.
- **[[Second-Order Thinking]]**: The obvious trade on Nvidia's AI dominance is buying Nvidia; the less-obvious second-order play is buying the constrained memory suppliers who capture margin from every Nvidia chip shipped.
