## Summary

On September 28, [[Nvidia]]'s board added $150 billion to its buyback program, lifting total remaining authorization to $235 billion — the largest single increase to a share-repurchase plan in history — with the full amount targeted for deployment by fiscal year 2028. Despite the stock's ~25% year-to-date gain and shares rising ~3% after-hours on the news, investor sentiment stays guarded given Nvidia's $5.6 trillion valuation, AI capex durability questions, and margin pressure from rising memory costs. The author judges the stock reasonably valued against ~70% projected FY2028 revenue growth and a forward P/E near 24, while flagging real uncertainty around AI capital-spending pace and technology maturity.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nvidia]] Buyback Authorization**: Board approved $150B increase, pushing total remaining authorization to $235B — ~4.3% of market cap — to be deployed by FY2028.
- **[[Jensen Huang]]**: Framed the buyback as evidence Nvidia's financial strength lets it fund innovation while rewarding shareholders, signaling confidence in future results.
- **[[Colette Kress]]**: CFO guided [[Gross Margin]] could compress from ~75% toward 71%–72% by fiscal Q4 2027, largely due to rising memory costs.
- **[[Stock Buyback]] as Capital Allocation**: Recently completed ~$20B in repurchases in a single quarter, implying a theoretical pace of ~$39B/quarter to exhaust the new authorization by the FY2028 deadline.
- **[[S&P 500]] Concentration**: Nvidia already represents ~7.9% of the index, offered as an alternative for investors wanting diversified AI exposure.

## Key Takeaways

- **Record authorization**: $150B addition brings total buyback capacity to $235B, largest ever increase.
- **Valuation context**: $235B is ~4.3% of Nvidia's $5.6 trillion market cap.
- **Deployment pace**: Roughly $39B/quarter needed over six quarters to hit FY2028 target.
- **Share reaction**: Stock rose ~3% after-hours; up ~25% year-to-date.
- **Margin risk**: Gross margin guided down from ~75% to 71%–72% by fiscal Q4 2027.
- **Growth thesis**: ~70% projected FY2028 revenue growth and ~24x forward earnings framed as reasonable valuation.
- **Bear case cited**: Circular financing arrangements with suppliers/customers draw skepticism.
- **Conservative alternative**: S&P 500 exposure suggested, given Nvidia's ~7.9% index weight.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Signaling Theory]]**: Jensen Huang explicitly ties the record buyback to management's confidence in future results — a textbook case of using a costly, hard-to-reverse capital commitment to signal information the market can't directly observe.
