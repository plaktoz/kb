## Summary
J.P. Morgan's chief global strategist argues that beneath a strong AI-driven boom, the U.S. economy shows real weakness — soft job growth, sliding real wages, stagnant homebuilding — while equity valuations are elevated and dependent on continued mega-cap tech capex. He forecasts moderate GDP growth, easing inflation, a Fed on hold, and recommends diversifying into international stocks and alternatives given U.S. equity concentration.

## Core Concepts
The [[AI Boom]] is described as "pure momentum" — driving profit gains, investment spending, and a wealth effect — while underlying [[Labor Market]] data (flat-to-negative job growth, wages below CPI) signals weakness. Key [[Macroeconomic]] headwinds and tailwinds include the [[Iran-U.S. Conflict]] and its effect on oil prices, new [[Tariffs]] replacing IEEPA levies, restrictive [[Immigration Policy]] shrinking the labor force, and a nearly $2 trillion federal deficit limiting further fiscal stimulus. On [[Fed Policy]], the Fed is expected to hold rates in September. The piece closes with [[Valuation]] concerns: the S&P 500 forward P/E of 20.2x is elevated and reliant on continued AI capex, with total U.S. equity value now exceeding 400% of GDP.

## Key Takeaways
- **GDP forecast**: Growth expected to rise to 2.8% in Q3 2026 (from 1.5% in Q2), decelerating to ~2.0% in 2027.
- **Labor market**: Payroll growth anemic (50k-100k/month); unemployment still expected to fall toward 4.0% by Q4 2026 due to shrinking labor force.
- **Inflation path**: July CPI expected at 3.4% y/y, easing toward the Fed's 2.0% target by spring 2027.
- **Earnings quality**: S&P 500 proforma EPS up 50% y/y overshoots true growth (~20%) once one-time tech capital gains are excluded.
- **Valuation warning**: U.S. corporate equity value is now >400% of GDP, versus 244% pre-pandemic and 74% before the 1987 crash.
- **Diversification call**: International stocks look cheaper than U.S. peers; fixed income and alternatives (infrastructure, real estate) recommended to diversify away from a concentrated U.S. AI bet.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Base Rate Neglect]]**: Elevated headline earnings growth (50% y/y) obscures that roughly half is a one-time accounting effect from unrealized capital gains at two large tech firms — the "true" underlying base rate is closer to 20%, a distinction easy to miss when extrapolating from the vivid recent trend.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this outlook?
**A**: The AI capex boom is currently masking real underlying economic weakness in jobs, wages, and housing, and current equity valuations depend heavily on that boom continuing uninterrupted.

**Q2**: What specific data point illustrates the earnings distortion the author flags?
**A**: Reported S&P 500 proforma EPS grew 50% y/y, but excluding over $150 billion in unrealized capital gains at two large tech companies, underlying growth was closer to 20%.

**Q3**: What investment implication follows from this analysis?
**A**: Investors should diversify away from a concentrated U.S. AI bet into cheaper international stocks, fairly priced U.S. fixed income, and alternatives like infrastructure and real estate.
