## Summary

[[Anthropic]]'s IPO gives its supplier-investors, notably [[Amazon]] and [[Google]], stakes worth a combined ~$307.9 billion at $83 per share, putting the AI boom's circular financing under scrutiny. Analysts argue the listing may make the financing self-sustaining, since public-market investors and bonds, not suppliers, will now fund losses, leaving Anthropic with roughly $220 billion in cash. The main risk is losing the AI race, while compute suppliers are expected to be fine either way.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Circular Financing]]**: Supplier-investors such as Amazon, Google, [[Nvidia]], AMD, Microsoft, Broadcom, Micron, and TSMC invest in Anthropic, which then buys their compute and products.
- **IPO Capital Structure**: ~$141 billion raised, ~$91.7 billion net proceeds at $83 per share, plus a $26 billion private placement from suppliers.
- **Compute Commitments**: $900 billion committed, primarily for compute and infrastructure; ~$280 billion cumulative revenue needed over four years per [[SemiAnalysis]]'s Joey Brookhart.
- **[[CoreWeave]] Contrast**: Gil Luria sees neoclouds like CoreWeave as more purely circular than Anthropic.
- **Frontier Competition Risk**: Anthropic's value depends on staying ahead of OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and open-weight models.

## Key Takeaways

- **Supplier Stakes**: Amazon's 14.7% (~$159B) and Google's 13.7% (~$149B) stakes total ~$307.9B.
- **Revenue Pace**: $17B in Q3 2026 revenue, a $68B annualized rate.
- **Cash Position**: About $220B in cash and short-term investments after the IPO.
- **Funding Shift**: Public investors and corporate bonds replace suppliers in covering quarterly losses.
- **Brookhart's View**: Revenue now comes from other customers; Amazon need not prop Anthropic up.
- **Supplier Safety**: Compute suppliers profit regardless of which lab wins the frontier.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Circularity ends when outside capital and customers fund the loop** — Financing that returns to the investor as supplier revenue is only self-dealing while the investor-supplier is the marginal funder; independent public capital and third-party customers make the demand self-sustaining.
→ explains: Funding Shift, Brookhart's View

**T2. Infrastructure suppliers are insulated from which application wins** — Demand for the underlying resource persists whichever competitor captures the end market, so suppliers carry less risk than the platform they supply.
→ explains: Supplier Safety

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What makes Anthropic's financing "circular," and what does the article say the IPO changes?
**A**: Suppliers like Amazon and Google invest in Anthropic and get the money back when it buys compute. The IPO may make this self-sustaining because public-market investors and bonds, not suppliers, will now fund its losses.

**Q2**: What figures support the claim that Anthropic can pay for its compute bill?
**A**: It expects about $220B in cash, generated $17B in Q3 2026 revenue ($68B annualized), and needs roughly $280B in cumulative revenue over four years against $900B of commitments, per Brookhart.

**Q3**: Who bears the most risk if Anthropic loses the AI race?
**A**: Anthropic itself; Luria notes that Amazon, Google, and Microsoft still benefit because AI use runs in their data centers regardless of whose models are used.
