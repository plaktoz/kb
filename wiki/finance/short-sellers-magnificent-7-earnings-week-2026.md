## Summary

Short interest in [[Meta]] and [[Amazon]] has reached its highest level since 2022 ahead of this week's earnings, even as prediction markets assign both stocks over 90% odds of beating estimates — a setup that echoes prior short-squeeze cycles in 2022 and 2023-2024. The bearish positioning is a direct bet that all three hyperscalers ([[Meta]], [[Amazon]], [[Microsoft]]) will again raise AI capex guidance the way [[Alphabet]] did, triggering the same kind of post-earnings selloff.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Short Squeeze Pattern]]** — Meta shorts peaked in fall 2022 right before a historic rally, and Amazon shorts loaded up in late 2023 ahead of a 2024 run; the current setup rhymes with both precedents
- **[[Put/Call Ratio]]** — despite heavy short interest, Meta's 0.58 and Amazon's 0.32 full-chain put/call ratios show calls still outnumbering puts, complicating the bearish thesis
- **[[Capex Guidance as Catalyst]]** — Alphabet's capex-guide-driven selloff has become the template investors are pricing into this week's Meta, Amazon, and Microsoft reports

## Key Takeaways

- **Beat odds priced in**: META 90.2%, AMZN 95.5%, MSFT 91.5% — a beat is nearly assumed; capex commentary is the real swing factor
- **Microsoft** watch item: Azure constant-currency growth, with 53.5% odds on a 40–42% growth band
- **Amazon** watch item: AWS holding ~28% growth pace, the fastest in 15 quarters
- **Reminder**: Meta's Q1 2026 beat still produced an 8.55% earnings-day decline — beating estimates is no longer sufficient to move the stock
