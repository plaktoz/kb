## Summary

Wednesday's PCE inflation report is expected to show core and headline prices both up 0.3% month-over-month, with annual rates stuck at 3.7% and 3.3%, well above the Fed's 2% target, while consumer spending remains resilient despite weak sentiment. Fed officials are split on urgency but broadly expect at least one more rate hike in 2026, and a retroactive BEA methodology revision could push July's reported annual PCE down to around 3%, muddying the read on the path ahead.

## Core Concepts

- **[[PCE Inflation]]**: The Fed's preferred inflation gauge; consensus expects +0.3% at both headline and core levels for the month, with annual rates unchanged at 3.7% (all-items) and 3.3% (core) — both well above the [[Federal Reserve]]'s 2% target.
- **[[Federal Open Market Committee]] outlook**: September's meeting delivered a quarter-point hike and put the benchmark range at 3.75%-4%; 16 of 18 FOMC officials who submitted forecasts expect at least one more 2026 rate move, alongside a raised consensus PCE outlook.
- **[[Kevin Warsh]]**: Fed Chairman says financial conditions are not "restrictive," pointing to strong hiring, business investment, and private-sector earnings as evidence the economy can handle further tightening.
- **[[Michael Barr]]**: Fed Governor says tariffs and the prolonged Iran war have "knocked off course" progress toward the 2% goal, sees no clear trend back to target, and expects further rate hikes will likely be needed.
- **[[John Williams]] (New York Fed)**: Cites AI buildout demand as a third inflation driver, but strikes a more dovish tone than Barr — "no need for urgency" — while still expecting one further rate increase this year; notes housing services disinflation and no labor-market inflation pressure.
- **[[Bureau of Economic Analysis]] methodology revision**: Retroactive changes (back to 2021) to how legal services, software/computer accessories, and portfolio management prices are measured could revise July's annual PCE reading down two-to-three tenths, potentially to around 3%.
- **Consumer spending resilience**: August spending is expected up 0.8% (vs. 0.2% in July), driven partly by a gas-price surge; Bank of America data shows debit/credit card spending up 6.9% year-over-year for the week ended Sept. 19, and still up 5.7% excluding gasoline.

## Key Takeaways

- **No relief signal expected**: Both headline and core PCE forecast to rise 0.3% month-over-month with annual rates unchanged from July.
- **Fed consensus leans toward more hikes**: All but two of 18 FOMC officials expect at least one more 2026 increase.
- **Officials split on urgency**: Barr sees continued need for hikes; Williams sees room to wait despite still expecting one more this year.
- **AI buildout flagged as inflation driver**: Williams explicitly names AI-related demand for goods as a contributor to persistent inflation.
- **BEA revision could lower the rearview**: July's annual PCE may be revised down to ~3% without changing the forward outlook.
- **Spending outpaces sentiment**: Consumers keep spending (BofA: +6.9% YoY card spend) even as confidence readings falter.
- **Market pricing**: Traders see a strong probability of an October hike, with another possible in December or January.
