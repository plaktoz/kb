## Summary

Microsoft (MSFT) has quietly become a strong dividend growth stock, raising its quarterly payout from $0.39 to $0.98 per share over the past decade — a 151% increase — including a recent 8% hike. Its current ~0.8% yield appears modest but is suppressed by the stock's ~65% five-year price surge; at prior price levels the yield would exceed the S&P 500 average. The combination of dividend growth, reasonable valuation at 27x earnings, and AI-driven growth prospects makes MSFT a compelling holding.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Microsoft (MSFT)]]** — primary subject; both a tech giant and a growing [[Dividend Growth Stock]] with a decade-long track record of consistent payout increases
- **[[Dividend Growth Investing]]** — strategy focused on stocks that consistently raise dividends; MSFT's 151% 10-year growth is the core evidence cited
- **[[Yield on Cost]]** — the concept that a low current yield can mask strong income growth for long-term holders; MSFT's yield at the ~$300 historical price would be ~1.3%, above the [[S&P 500]] average of ~1.1%
- **[[AI-Driven Growth]]** — MSFT's positioning in AI is cited as the forward earnings catalyst that supports continued dividend growth and capital appreciation
- **[[Price-to-Earnings Ratio]]** — MSFT trades at 27x trailing earnings, described as not excessive given AI upside

## Key Takeaways

- **Dividend growth**: $0.39 → $0.98 quarterly, ~151% increase over 10 years.
- **Recent hike**: 8% increase brings quarterly dividend to $0.98/share.
- **Yield context**: Current ~0.8% yield is suppressed by stock's ~65% five-year price gain.
- **Adjusted yield**: At ~$300 prior price, yield would be ~1.3% — above S&P 500's ~1.1% average.
- **Valuation**: 27x trailing earnings described as not excessive for an AI-driven compounder.
- **Investor trade-off**: Low current yield is offset by strong capital appreciation alongside dividend growth.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Yield on Cost]]**: MSFT's low current yield is an optical illusion — investors who bought at lower prices enjoy a yield well above market average, illustrating why absolute yield is a poor metric for dividend growth stocks.
- **[[Total Return Investing]]**: The article implicitly applies a total-return lens — combining modest yield with strong price appreciation and dividend growth produces better outcomes than chasing high-yield, low-growth alternatives.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core investment thesis for Microsoft as a dividend growth stock?
**A**: MSFT has raised its quarterly dividend ~151% over 10 years (from $0.39 to $0.98), and its AI positioning supports continued earnings and payout growth alongside capital appreciation.

**Q2**: Why does Microsoft's current ~0.8% dividend yield understate its income potential for long-term holders?

**Q3**: How should an investor weigh MSFT's low yield against its valuation and growth prospects?
**A**: At 27x trailing earnings with AI-driven growth ahead, the low yield is an acceptable trade-off — dividend growth and capital appreciation together can produce strong total returns even without a high starting yield.
