## Summary

[[Alphabet]] lost just over $500bn in market value after admitting that its own [[AI Mode]] is eating into traditional [[Google Search]] ad revenue, with shares down over 12% to a six-month low. [[Amazon]] and [[Microsoft]] also reported while continuing huge AI spending, with investors reacting sharply to Amazon's negative $42bn cash flow and mildly to Microsoft's results. Analysts say the market is now asking where AI value will ultimately be captured.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Alphabet]] Q3 2026 results**: Revenue up 25% to $127.6bn but below analyst expectations, especially in advertising.
- **[[AI Mode]]**: Google's conversational search answers, which cannibalize ads shown beside traditional search results.
- **[[Philipp Schindler]]**: Google's chief business officer, who said AI Mode was eating into ad money yet felt "very good about our trajectory".
- **[[Kim Scott]]**: Former Google ad leader who framed the hit as a deliberate long-term bet on AI.
- **[[Amazon]] and [[Andy Jassy]]**: Sales up 11% to $201bn with negative $42bn cash flow; Jassy sees two potential $1 trillion businesses in cloud and retail.
- **[[Microsoft]] and [[Satya Nadella]]**: Revenue up 19% to $92.3bn, cloud up 30% to $63bn, Copilot daily users up 500%.
- **[[AI Capital Expenditure]]**: Big Tech's heavy spending is straining growth of core business lines.

## Key Takeaways

- **Market Value Loss**: Alphabet fell from $3.97 trillion to $3.46 trillion in one day.
- **Search Cannibalization**: Google's own AI answers reduce revenue from traditional search ads.
- **Long-Term Bet**: Kim Scott says Google would rather eat its own lunch than let others.
- **Amazon Cash Burn**: Negative $42bn cash flow; spending up six-fold in three months; shares fell 6% after hours.
- **Microsoft Muted Reaction**: Shares fell under 1% after spending about $5bn less on AI.
- **Copilot Growth**: Automated tasks nearly doubled since July, per Nadella.
- **Open Question**: Analysts ask where AI value will ultimately be captured.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. A product that answers the same need with less ad monetization cannibalizes the incumbent's revenue** — When a firm's own substitute replaces its higher-earning product, it accepts short-term revenue loss to avoid losing the customer to a rival. The article shows this with Google's AI Mode and Kim Scott's "eat its own lunch" framing.
→ explains: Search Cannibalization, Long-Term Bet, Market Value Loss

**T2. Heavy investment must be funded while the core business stays strong** — Investors penalize spending that outpaces the core business's ability to keep growing and generating cash. Amazon's negative cash flow was punished, while Microsoft's lower AI spend drew a muted reaction.
→ explains: Amazon Cash Burn, Microsoft Muted Reaction, Open Question

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did Alphabet lose over $500bn in value in a day?
**A**: Its results missed analyst expectations, especially in advertising, after it revealed its own AI Mode was hitting search ad revenue. Shares fell over 12% to a six-month low.

**Q2**: What cash flow figure worried investors about Amazon?
**A**: Amazon reported negative $42bn cash flow, with spending up six-fold over three months, alongside 11% sales growth to $201bn. Its shares fell 6% after hours.

**Q3**: What question does the article say Wall Street is now asking about AI?
**A**: Analyst Tracy Woo said the question is where AI value will ultimately be captured, and how to fund massive AI investment while keeping established businesses strong.
