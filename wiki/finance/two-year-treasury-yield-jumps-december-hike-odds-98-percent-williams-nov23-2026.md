## Summary
Short-end Treasury yields rose on Monday as stocks rebounded, with the 2-year up more than 7 basis points to 4.69%. Traders now price a roughly 98% chance of a quarter-point Fed hike in December, up from about 88% on Friday, ahead of a speech by New York Fed President John Williams and Wednesday's GDP and PCE data.

## Core Concepts
- [[Treasury Yields]]: 2-year at 4.69%, 10-year at 4.932%, 30-year at 5.299%.
- [[Federal Reserve]] rate hikes: a 25bp hike on December 9 is now the base case.
- [[CME FedWatch]]: tool showing the roughly 98% December hike probability.
- [[John Williams]]: New York Fed president scheduled to speak Monday.
- [[Thierry Wizman]]: Macquarie strategist who sees the 50bp hike as an impossibility.
- [[PCE Inflation]]: September annual rate 3.6%; October reading due Wednesday.
- [[Safe-Haven Assets]]: safety assets retreated as stocks rebounded.

## Key Takeaways
- **2-Year Yield**: Up more than 7 basis points to 4.69%, the most rate-sensitive tenor.
- **10-Year and 30-Year**: 10-year up 2bp to 4.932%; 30-year slightly down at 5.299%.
- **Hike Odds**: December quarter-point hike priced near 98%, up from 88% Friday.
- **Strategist View**: Stocks ending their slide makes a 25bp hike likelier; 50bp unlikely.
- **Data Ahead**: Revised Q3 GDP expected 3.4% and October PCE expected up 0.3% Wednesday.
- **Inflation Backdrop**: September PCE rose 0.5% monthly, largest gain since March.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What drove short-term Treasury yields higher on Monday?
**A**: Stocks rebounded strongly, partly unwinding last week's safety bid, while hawkish Fed commentary and data reinforced expectations of more hikes.

**Q2**: What do markets price for the Fed's December meeting?
**A**: About a 98% chance of a quarter-point hike, up from about 88% on Friday, per CME FedWatch. Macquarie's Wizman calls a 50bp hike an impossibility.

**Q3**: Which upcoming events could move rate expectations further?
**A**: Williams's speech Monday, and Wednesday's revised Q3 GDP (expected 3.4%) and October PCE (expected 0.3% monthly rise).
