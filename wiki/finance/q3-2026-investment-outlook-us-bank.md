## Summary
U.S. Bank's Q3 2026 investment outlook finds solid growth enduring — supported by continued business investment in AI infrastructure, strong earnings revisions, and treasury yields at a decade-high starting point — while flagging three key risks: residual Iran-Strait of Hormuz fragility, a potential Fed rate hike under new chair Kevin Warsh, and elevated valuations in large-cap US equities versus more attractively valued foreign developed and emerging markets.

## Core Concepts
- **[[AI Infrastructure Investment Cycle]]**: Capital spending by large US companies expected to grow ~40% over the next 12 months, broadening beyond Big Tech into energy, industrial, and systems sectors supporting AI infrastructure. This is a durable multi-year tailwind, not a short-term trade.
- **[[Strait of Hormuz Risk]]**: Iran conflict remains the most fluid geopolitical risk in Q3 2026; shipping activity through the Strait has started recovering but the reopening is fragile. Directly relevant to oil prices and broader risk sentiment.
- **[[Kevin Warsh Fed Policy]]**: At Warsh's first meeting as Fed chair, rates held steady with a renewed focus on price stability. Markets price at least one 0.25% hike by year-end. Contrast with previous Powell-era dovish pivot expectations.
- **[[Foreign Developed Markets Earnings Growth]]**: Analysts forecast 11% EPS growth for foreign developed stocks in 2026, and 50%+ for emerging markets — both offering better entry valuations than elevated large-cap US multiples.
- **[[IPO Pipeline 2026]]**: Anthropic and OpenAI expected to list publicly by year-end; public listing market reopening after SpaceX's debut. Private credit funds managing withdrawal requests; default rates ~6% (in line with long-term average).
- **[[Treasury Yield Starting Point]]**: Yields between 4–5% provide the strongest income base for bond investors since the pre-2010 era; corporate bonds yield 5–7%.

## Key Takeaways
- **AI capex**: 40% expected growth in large-company capital spending over next 12 months.
- **Labor**: 114,000 jobs/month avg through May 2026; wage growth +4.1% YoY.
- **Foreign developed EPS**: 11% forecast; emerging markets 50%+ (higher bar but also higher risk).
- **Fed**: Warsh held at first meeting; at least one 0.25% hike priced by year-end.
- **Bonds**: 4–5% Treasury yields; corporate credit 5–7%; strongest bond income starting point in over a decade.
- **Infrastructure/Utilities**: Rising electricity demand from AI data centers supports utility earnings growth.
- **Private markets**: SpaceX debut opened IPO window; Anthropic/OpenAI expected to list by year-end.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Mean Reversion in Valuations]]**: US large-cap multiples are "above average" while foreign developed and EM offer better entry points — the classic mean-reversion case for international diversification when domestic valuations are stretched.
- **[[Duration Risk vs. Income Tradeoff]]**: Treasury yields at 4–5% are a materially better starting point for bonds vs. the 2010–2021 environment — the starting yield is the single best predictor of 10-year bond returns, so this is a structural argument for fixed income exposure.
