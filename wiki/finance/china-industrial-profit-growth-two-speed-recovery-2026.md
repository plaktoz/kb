## Summary
China's industrial profit growth slowed to 15.1% in June from 21.1% in May, revealing a two-speed economy where AI-fueled chip and equipment manufacturers thrive on exports while domestic demand sectors (autos, property) continue to contract. H1 profits rose 18.7% YoY. Auto manufacturing profits fell 19.5% H1 YoY, with car sales declining for a ninth consecutive month in June.

## Core Concepts

- [[China Industrial Profits]] — 15.1% June growth (down from 21.1% in May); H1 at 18.7% YoY
- [[Two-Speed Recovery]] — export manufacturers (especially chip/equipment) thriving; domestic consumption sectors struggling
- [[China Automobile Sector]] — profits -19.5% H1; car sales down 9 consecutive months
- [[Politburo Meeting July 2026]] — key policy-setting gathering; investors watching for targeted stimulus signals
- [[NBS Statistics]] — covers firms with annual revenue ≥20M yuan ($2.95M) from main operations

## Key Takeaways

- **AI-fueled chip boom is masking domestic weakness**: strip out tech manufacturing and China's recovery picture darkens significantly
- **Deflation ending but domestic demand weak**: factory-gate deflation ended after 3 years, but consumer spending hasn't followed
- **Politburo meeting expected to disappoint broad stimulus hopes**: export resilience gives Beijing cover to avoid big stimulus packages
- **ING economist**: sustained profits growth could allow companies to resume wage growth — a precondition for domestic demand recovery
- **CXMT IPO at +470%** signals investor appetite for China tech even as industrial headline numbers slow

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Composition Fallacy]]**: China's aggregate profit growth looks healthy at 15–18%, but the composition reveals fragility — growth is concentrated in a narrow AI/export sector while most of the domestic economy remains depressed.
