## Summary
[[Microsoft]]'s fiscal Q4 2026 results beat Wall Street estimates with 43% Azure revenue growth and a 45% growth forecast for the next quarter, sending shares up more than 8%, while CEO [[Satya Nadella]] described a strategic pivot toward Microsoft's own AI models and custom silicon alongside — rather than primarily through — OpenAI.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Azure]]**: Microsoft's cloud platform, which grew 43% in fiscal Q4 and is guided to 45% constant-currency growth next quarter, comfortably beating the ~41% analysts expected.
- **AI technological pluralism**: Nadella's stated enterprise architecture strategy of giving customers freedom to choose AI technologies by cost/performance rather than being locked into one provider — reflecting Microsoft's shift from OpenAI-dependence toward proprietary models and custom silicon delivering up to 40% efficiency gains.
- **Cloud contracted backlog**: Reached $678B, up from $627B, with the full ~$50B sequential increase coming from companies outside the leading AI model makers — a signal of broad enterprise adoption rather than hyperscaler concentration.

## Key Takeaways
- **Azure grew 43%** in Q4 FY2026, guided to 45% next quarter versus ~41% expected; overall revenue guidance beat consensus ($90.4B vs. $89.66B).
- **Microsoft 365 Copilot paid seats surpassed 30 million**, up from 20 million last quarter and ahead of the 26.9 million analysts forecast.
- **$329.1B in not-yet-commenced data center leases** (spanning FY2027-2033) signal multi-year conviction in AI demand.
- **Capex accounting changed**: data center leases now spread over 25 years instead of 15, mechanically lowering reported capex ($175B guided for calendar 2026) without changing underlying spending; free cash flow was $19.6B, beating estimates but down 23% YoY.

**A**: Microsoft's massive AI infrastructure investments are beginning to yield tangible returns, evidenced by Azure growth beating estimates and an optimistic forward guide.

**A**: Microsoft 365 Copilot paid seats surpassed 30 million, up from 20 million the prior quarter and ahead of the 26.9 million analysts had forecast.

**Q3**: How should an investor interpret Microsoft's changed capex accounting (25-year vs. 15-year lease depreciation)?
**A**: The lower reported capex figure doesn't reflect reduced actual spending — it's an accounting change — so investors should look at underlying commitments like the $329.1 billion in not-yet-commenced leases and the growing cloud backlog to gauge real investment scale.
