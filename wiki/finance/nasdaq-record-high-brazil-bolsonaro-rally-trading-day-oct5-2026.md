## Summary
The [[Nasdaq Composite]] rose about 1% to a fresh record on October 5, 2026, as Q3 earnings optimism and lower oil prices offset rising US bond yields. The day's biggest moves came in Brazil, where stocks jumped 8% and the real 4% after right-wing candidate [[Flavio Bolsonaro]]'s unexpectedly strong first-round showing made him the favorite in the presidential runoff. Across G10 currencies, political and bond-market turmoil in Europe, the UK and Japan pushed the dollar index to an 18-month high, the "cleanest dirty shirt" in the laundry.

## Core Concepts
- **US equities at records**: The [[Nasdaq Composite]] gained 1%, the [[S&P 500]] 0.7% and the Dow 0.2%; every S&P sector rose except real estate. [[Nvidia]] gained 2% to a record, with its market cap nearing $6 trillion.
- **"S&P 10,000" thesis**: [[Jamie McGeever]] notes the milestone is only 30% above current levels, and would feel close if Q3 earnings resemble Q2's.
- **Brazil election shock**: [[Flavio Bolsonaro]], son of former president [[Jair Bolsonaro]], beat expectations in the first round. Online betting markets now give him an 83% chance of winning the runoff in three weeks.
- **Lula's fiscal-credibility bid**: Incumbent [[Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva]], seeking a fourth non-consecutive term, is weighing Vice President [[Geraldo Alckmin]] as finance minister to win over centrist voters.
- **Spain joins the European political risk list**: PM [[Pedro Sanchez]] called a snap election after nationwide housing protests, even though his Socialist Party trails in nearly every poll. Spain's fiscal position is sound (deficit around 2-2.5% of GDP), but Spanish yields still rose 5 bps.
- **Dollar strength**: The [[US Dollar]] benefits from political and debt stress in Europe, the UK and Japan. [[Donald Trump]] and [[Scott Bessent]] would prefer a weaker dollar to help exports, but a strong dollar does hold down imported inflation.
- **Bond yields**: US 10-year and 30-year [[Treasury Yields]] posted their highest closes in over two decades (see [[10-Year Treasury Yield]]).

## Key Takeaways
- **Nasdaq record**: +1% to new high on earnings optimism and 2% oil drop.
- **Brazil +8%**: Biggest rise this century outside the GFC and pandemic.
- **Brazilian real +4%**: Its biggest one-day gain in four years.
- **Betting odds**: Markets put Bolsonaro's runoff victory probability at 83%.
- **Euro weakness**: Fell 0.3% to a 17-month low near $1.12.
- **Dollar index**: Hit an 18-month high as the "cleanest dirty shirt."
- **Long-end yields**: US 10- and 30-year closes highest in over 20 years.
- **Gold steady**: Little changed, holding above $4,100/oz.
- **Spain snap vote**: Called for November 29 after housing protests (per MarketScreener).
- **Next catalysts**: US trade data, a $58B 3-year auction, and speeches by [[John Williams]], [[Lorie Logan]] and [[Michelle Bowman]].

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Cleanest Dirty Shirt]]**: Currencies trade on relative merit, not absolute health. The US has its own political and debt problems, but the dollar wins because rivals in Europe, the UK and Japan look worse.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What drove the biggest market moves on October 5, 2026?
**A**: Flavio Bolsonaro's surprisingly strong first-round result in Brazil's presidential election. Brazilian stocks rose 8% and the real 4%, while the Nasdaq hit a record on earnings optimism and lower oil.

**Q2**: Why is the dollar strengthening even though the US has its own political and debt troubles?
**A**: Deepening political and bond-market uncertainty in Europe, the UK and Japan makes the dollar the relatively safest option, the "cleanest dirty shirt in the laundry." That pushed the dollar index to an 18-month high.

**Q3**: Why is a strong dollar a mixed outcome for the Trump administration?
**A**: Trump and Bessent want a weaker dollar to boost exports and narrow the trade deficit. A stronger dollar, however, suppresses imported inflation and reduces the need for tighter monetary policy.
