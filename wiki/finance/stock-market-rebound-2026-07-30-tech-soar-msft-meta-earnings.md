## Summary

Major US indexes rebounded sharply on July 30, 2026, with the Nasdaq up 2.8%, S&P 500 up 1.7%, and Dow up 1.2%, driven almost entirely by Information Technology after Microsoft's blowout Q4 earnings sent shares surging 16%. Meta shares sank 8% after profits missed on surging AI infrastructure costs, while PCE inflation data and below-consensus GDP growth provided a mixed macro backdrop. Traders repriced September rate hike odds down from 82% to 63% after Fed Chair Kevin Warsh held rates steady the prior day.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nasdaq Composite]]** / **[[S&P 500]]** / **[[Dow Jones Industrial Average]]**: all closed sharply higher after Wednesday's 1,150-point Dow selloff
- **[[Microsoft]] Q4 2026 Earnings**: adjusted EPS $4.74 vs $4.25 expected; revenue $90.01B vs $87.7B; [[Azure]] cloud revenue +43%; shares +16%, pushing market cap above $3.5T

- **[[PCE Inflation]]**: June PCE +3.7% YoY (down from 4.1%); core PCE +3.3% (down from 3.4%) — matched expectations

- **[[Federal Reserve]] Rate Decision**: 9-3 vote to hold; Chair [[Kevin Warsh]] signaled resolve on inflation; September rate hike probability 63% (down from 82%)
- **Semiconductor stocks**: [[Roundhill Memory ETF]] (DRAM) +16%; [[iShares Semiconductor ETF]] (SOXX) +8.5%; [[Micron]], [[Sandisk]], [[Seagate]], [[Western Digital]] +12–26%

## Key Takeaways

- Nasdaq +2.8%, S&P 500 +1.7%, Dow +1.2% (600 points) on July 30.
- Microsoft shares +16%; market cap crossed $3.5 trillion post-earnings.
- Azure cloud revenue grew 43%, beating estimates by ~$1 billion.
- Meta EPS missed by $1.01; costs surged 55% on AI infrastructure.
- Meta laid off 8,000 employees in May; booked $1.18B severance charge.
- PCE inflation: 3.7% annual, down from 4.1% — matched forecasts.
- Q2 GDP 1.5%, below 1.8% forecast; AI import spending a drag on GDP math.
- September rate hike probability fell to 63% from 82% a week prior.
- Memory/semiconductor ETFs surged 8–16% reversing prior-day losses.
- Six of 11 S&P 500 sectors remained negative; IT sector alone drove gains (+5.1%).

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Mean Reversion]]**: The sharp one-day rebound following Wednesday's 1,150-point Dow drop illustrates how extreme single-session selloffs often overshoot fair value, creating mechanical buying pressure the next session regardless of new information.
- **[[Signal vs. Noise]]**: Meta's record revenue was drowned out by cost-structure noise (AI capex, legal charges, severance), demonstrating how earnings reactions hinge on surprises relative to expectations, not absolute performance.
