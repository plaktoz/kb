## Summary

Longer-dated US Treasury yields hit their highest levels in more than two decades on Monday, October 5, 2026, as investors absorbed new service-sector data and looked ahead to Wednesday's release of the [[Federal Reserve]]'s September meeting minutes. The [[10-Year Treasury Yield]] touched 5.349% intraday, its highest since April 3, 2002, before settling near 5.307%, while the [[30-Year Treasury Yield]] briefly hit 5.703%, a level not seen since late May 2002. A weak September jobs report on Friday had eased fears of an October rate hike, and traders now price a nearly 82% chance the Fed holds rates.

## Core Concepts

- **[[10-Year Treasury Yield]]**: Last traded at 5.307%, up 3 bps; jumped about 7 bps to 5.349% intraday, highest since April 3, 2002.
- **[[30-Year Treasury Yield]]**: Rose about 3 bps to 5.661%; earlier up more than 7 bps to 5.703%, highest since late May 2002.
- **[[Bond Market Sell-off]]**: Investors have grappled with a Treasury sell-off over the past six weeks.
- **[[Momentum Selloff]]**: [[Jay Hatfield]] of [[Infrastructure Capital Advisors]] framed the move as momentum-driven, noting the 10-year usually trades 100 bps over the terminal [[Fed Funds Rate]].
- **[[ISM Services PMI]]**: The [[Institute for Supply Management]] services index came in at 54.9 for September, roughly in line with expectations and slightly below August's pace.
- **Services price index**: Rose 1.4 points to 74, putting the 12-month average at its highest since March 2023.
- **[[FOMC Minutes]]**: Minutes from the Fed's September policy meeting are due Wednesday.
- **[[CME FedWatch Tool]]**: Shows a nearly 82% probability the Fed keeps rates unchanged at its October meeting.
- **[[September 2026 Jobs Report]]**: Friday's lackluster payrolls data helped bring yields down and eased concern about another hike.

## Key Takeaways

- **New intraday high**: 10-year hit 5.349%, highest since April 3, 2002.
- **Long end**: 30-year touched 5.703%, highest since late May 2002.
- **Close-ish level**: 10-year last traded at 5.307%, up 3 bps.
- **Momentum framing**: Hatfield calls it "a momentum selloff" with room to go higher.
- **Rule of thumb**: 10-year usually trades 100 bps over terminal Fed funds.
- **Services growth**: ISM services PMI at 54.9, near expectations, below August.
- **Inflation signal**: Services price index up 1.4 to 74; 12-month average highest since March 2023.
- **Fed odds**: Nearly 82% chance of no change at the October meeting.
- **Next catalyst**: September FOMC minutes due Wednesday.
- **Six-week trend**: Weak jobs data only briefly paused the broader bond selloff.
