## Summary

A Motley Fool column syndicated via The Globe and Mail argues that [[Alphabet]], down 32% from its summer all-time high in an AI-stock sell-off, is a buy because Search and [[Google Cloud]] are both delivering strong results. The main risk is negative free cash flow from capital expenditures of $195-205 billion in 2026, but profitable core businesses can carry it.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Alphabet]] sell-off**: Stock down 32% from its high as investors doubt whether AI investment will pay off.
- **[[Google Search]]**: Q3 revenue $63.3 billion, up 12%; [[Sundar Pichai]] said query growth hit a five-year high despite chatbot worries.
- **[[Google Cloud]]**: Q3 revenue up 103% to $30.7 billion; operating income $11.1 billion; margin 36.2% versus 23.7%.
- **Cloud backlog**: $561 billion at end of September; just over 55% expected to be recognized within 24 months.
- **[[Thomas Kurian]]**: Says 18 Cloud product lines each bring over $1 billion in annual revenue.
- **[[Free Cash Flow]] risk**: Negative $9.1 billion in Q3 on $51.4 billion of capex.
- **[[AI Capital Expenditure]]**: $195-205 billion guided for 2026, with more planned for 2027.

## Key Takeaways

- **Group Revenue**: Q3 revenue $127.6 billion, up 25% year over year.
- **Advertising**: $82 billion quarterly, up 11%, still highly profitable.
- **Cloud Margin**: Operating margin expanded to 36.2% from 23.7%.
- **Cloud Backlog**: $561 billion backlog supports future revenue.
- **Main Risk**: Negative free cash flow of $9.1 billion in Q3.
- **Funding Capacity**: Google services earned $39.2 billion operating income to fund AI spend.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about Alphabet?
**A**: After a 32% drop from its high, Alphabet is appealing because its two biggest businesses, advertising and Google Cloud, are performing strongly. The sell-off reflects doubts about AI payoff, not weak operations.

**Q2**: What data supports the Google Cloud bull case?
**A**: Q3 Cloud revenue rose 103% to $30.7 billion, operating margin expanded to 36.2% from 23.7%, and the backlog reached $561 billion.

**Q3**: What is the main risk, and why does the author think Alphabet can bear it?
**A**: Negative free cash flow of $9.1 billion in Q3 on $51.4 billion of capex is the shadow over the stock. Alphabet's profitable advertising operations, with $39.2 billion of Google services operating income, can help carry the spending.
