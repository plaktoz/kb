## Summary

Novo Nordisk shares sank 11% premarket after its heart drug ziltivekimab failed to reduce major adverse cardiovascular events in a Phase 3 trial, while Microsoft's prior-day earnings rally added $450 billion to its market capitalization — the largest single-day market-cap gain for any US company, surpassing Nvidia's April 2026 record. Separately, Fed Chair [[Kevin Warsh]]'s "resolute" but action-free press conference left bond markets testing his inflation-fighting credibility.

## Core Concepts

- [[Novo Nordisk]] — ziltivekimab showed target engagement (reduced IL-6 and hsCRP) but no reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events versus placebo; will trigger a non-cash impairment charge in Q3 2026
- [[Microsoft Market Cap Record]] — $450B single-day gain after a 15.5% stock jump, driven by earnings beating estimates with lighter-than-expected capex
- [[Kevin Warsh]] — Fed Chair whose second press conference was called "all hat, no cattle" by Bank of America analysts for reaffirming inflation resolve without raising rates
- [[Bond Vigilantes]] — bond markets pushing yields higher specifically because they don't believe Warsh's rhetoric will translate into action

## Key Takeaways

- **Novo Nordisk's ziltivekimab trial failure** doesn't affect 2026 adjusted operating profit guidance but will trigger a Q3 impairment charge
- **Microsoft's $450B one-day market-cap gain** surpassed Nvidia's prior record of $440B (set after the April 2026 tariff-pause announcement)
- **Memory-related ETFs** (Roundhill Memory ETF, iShares Semiconductor ETF) jumped 16% and 8.5% the prior day on memory-stock strength (Sandisk, Micron, Seagate, Western Digital)
- **Warsh's press conference disappointed hawks**: no rate hike and no clear forward guidance, despite "remains resolute" rhetoric on the 2% inflation target
- **10-year Treasury yield held above 4.68%**, continuing to climb as bond markets question the Fed's inflation-fighting resolve

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Cheap Talk]]**: Warsh's rhetorical commitment to fighting inflation without an accompanying rate action is a textbook case of "cheap talk" in economics — markets discount statements that cost the speaker nothing to make and instead price in the credible action (or inaction) that follows.
