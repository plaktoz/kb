## Summary

Almost a quarter of IA Global sector funds (141 of 567) completely flipped between top and bottom performance quartiles when the [[Momentum Factor]] trade reversed sharply after June 19, 2026 — the global momentum factor swung from a 42.5% gain to an 11.8% loss in just five weeks as the AI-infrastructure-driven rally unwound. The episode illustrates how concentrated a "diversified" index can become when returns are dominated by a narrow group of correlated momentum names.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Momentum Factor]]** — an investing style of buying recently outperforming stocks, which ran ~45% ahead of quality investing since May 2025 before reversing violently
- **[[Style Rotation Risk]]** — the finding that 75 funds fell from first to fourth quartile (mostly AI/momentum/growth funds) while 66 rose from fourth to first (mostly value, quality, and minimum-volatility funds) shows how factor exposure, not stock-picking skill, drove most of the dispersion
- **[[Concentration Risk in Index-Level Diversification]]** — St. James's Place CIO Justin Onuekwusi's warning that markets can look diversified at the index level while returns are actually driven by a narrow group of companies

## Key Takeaways

- **Reversal magnitude**: Korea's chip-heavy market fell 26% from its June peak; global momentum went from +42.5% to −11.8% in five weeks
- **Consistent winners were rare**: only 14 of 567 IA Global funds stayed first-quartile across both the rally and the reversal — mostly energy, value, and value-tilted small-cap strategies
- **AI/momentum funds got hit hardest**: dedicated AI and momentum-factor ETFs (Polar Capital AI, iShares MSCI World Momentum Factor, Xtrackers World Momentum) fell from top to bottom quartile
- **Defensive styles benefited**: minimum-volatility trackers and healthcare-sector funds were comparatively insulated, with healthcare swinging from losses to a 7% gain post-reversal
