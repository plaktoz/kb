## Summary

Futures markets price only a 15% chance of an October 28 rate hike, but that consensus rests on old assumptions about how the Fed communicates that may not hold under Chair [[Kevin Warsh]]. Warsh has rejected [[Forward Guidance]], disclaimed the [[Summary of Economic Projections]], and called the 2% PCE goal "a firm, fixed target" while CPI runs at 3.7% and PPI at 6.3%. Investors may therefore be dangerously complacent about the odds of a surprise hike.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Kevin Warsh]]'s communication style**: Rejects telling markets his plans before the [[FOMC]] meets; at an [[International Monetary Fund]] event in Bangkok he said he wasn't signaling a decision.
- **The troika**: Warsh, Vice Chair [[Philip Jefferson]], and New York Fed President [[John Williams]] form the Fed's informal leadership team. Jefferson and Williams signaled little urgency to hike, and markets repriced toward a hold.
- **Broken conventions**: Traditionally the chair's lieutenants spoke for him; after the leadership transition, outsiders can't be sure that still holds.
- **Warsh's hawkish framework**: His [[Jackson Hole]] speech described strong growth, inflation as a bigger worry than jobs, and loose credit conditions — setting up a unanimous September hike.
- **Financial conditions**: The [[Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index]] has risen ~0.05 points since Jackson Hole but remains negative (still loose).
- **Inflation data**: [[Consumer Price Index]] up 3.7% y/y through September; [[Producer Price Index]] up 6.3%; implied [[Core PCE]] of 3.1% y/y.
- **Possible drivers of a hike**: Booming [[AI Capex]] demand and warm inflation readings could let Warsh push wavering committee members.

## Key Takeaways

- **Market Odds**: Futures assign just 15% probability to an October hike.
- **Lieutenant Signals**: Jefferson and Williams indicated comfort holding; markets repriced toward pause.
- **Disclaimed SEP**: Warsh said of the September projections, "Those aren't my forecasts."
- **SEP Path**: Median official saw one more 2026 hike — room to pause October, hike December.
- **Hot Inflation**: CPI 3.7%, PPI 6.3%, implied core PCE 3.1% year over year.
- **Firm Target**: Warsh called the 2% PCE goal "a firm, fixed target."
- **Loose Conditions**: Financial conditions tightened slightly since Jackson Hole but remain loose.
- **Volatility Cost**: A hike against strong market expectations risks volatility and dents lieutenants' credibility.
- **Complacency Risk**: Assuming Warsh won't surprise is risky until the FOMC press conference.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Expectations inherit their assumptions** — Market pricing of a policy decision is only as reliable as the communication conventions it assumes; when the decision-maker changes the rules, expectations built on past experience become prone to complacency.
→ explains: Market Odds, Lieutenant Signals, Disclaimed SEP, Complacency Risk

**T2. Surprises are costly against strong consensus** — Acting contrary to firmly held market expectations produces volatility and erodes the credibility of whoever was seen to guide those expectations, making future decisions harder to predict.
→ explains: Volatility Cost, Lieutenant Signals

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does the article argue markets may be underpricing an October Fed hike?
**A**: The 15% odds rest on conventions — lieutenants speaking for the chair, the chair accepting the FOMC view — that Warsh, who rejects forward guidance and disclaimed the SEP, may not follow.

**Q2**: What inflation data could Warsh invoke to justify a hike?
**A**: September CPI rose 3.7% and PPI 6.3% year over year, implying core PCE of 3.1%, well above a 2% target Warsh called "firm, fixed."

**Q3**: What is the trade-off Warsh faces if he hikes in October?
**A**: Hiking against strong market expectations would cause volatility and could dent the credibility of Jefferson and Williams, who were seen to guide markets toward a hold.
