## Summary
[[Microsoft]] posted fiscal 2026 full-year results with revenue up 18% to $331.8 billion and net income up 31% to $133.7 billion, yet its market cap sits roughly 4.5% lower than a year prior — a paradox explained by multiple compression from ~37x to ~27x earnings as heavy AI capex spending ($115.9 billion on property/equipment, up 80%) depressed free cash flow and raised concerns about capital intensity. The author concludes shares are more of a hold than a buy.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Multiple Compression]]**: Investors once paid ~37x earnings for [[Microsoft]] (MSFT); they now pay ~27x. The business grew ~31% in earnings, but each earnings dollar is being valued roughly a third less, leaving the stock down ~4.5% year-over-year despite exceptional fundamentals.
- **[[AI Capex Intensity]]**: Microsoft spent $115.9 billion on property and equipment in fiscal 2026 (up 80% YoY). CFO [[Amy Hood]] guided ~$175 billion in capex for calendar 2026 — a figure that exceeds Microsoft's entire fiscal 2026 operating income of $155.2 billion.
- **[[Free Cash Flow Trade-off]]**: Despite a 31% jump in net income, full-year FCF fell from ~$72 billion to ~$67 billion, illustrating that heavy infrastructure spend can depress cash generation even during a strong earnings growth cycle.
- **[[Asset-Light vs. Infrastructure-Heavy Valuation]]**: The market currently rewards asset-light businesses over infrastructure builders. [[Apple]] spent under $7 billion in capex over nine months while its market cap rose ~33% to $4.51 trillion at ~35x earnings; MSFT trades at ~27x despite stronger earnings growth.
- **[[Azure]]**: Surpassed $100 billion in annual revenue (+41% YoY); commercial backlog hit $678 billion (up 84% YoY).

## Key Takeaways

- **Earnings growth ≠ stock return**: MSFT net income +31% to $133.7B yet stock ~4.5% lower YoY.
- **Multiple compressed from ~37x to ~27x**: valuation per earnings dollar shrank proportionally to business growth.
- **Capex exceeds operating income**: $175B calendar 2026 capex guide > $155.2B fiscal 2026 operating income.
- **FCF declined**: ~$67B vs. ~$72B prior year, despite surging earnings.
- **Azure crossed $100B annual revenue** for the first time (+41% YoY).
- **Commercial backlog: $678B**, up 84% YoY — contracted future revenue.
- **EPS ex-OpenAI gains still +22%**: strong underlying business, not reliant on investment windfalls.
- **Apple comparison**: <$7B capex, market cap +33%, 35x P/E — contrasting market preference for capital efficiency.
- **Analyst stance**: author rates MSFT "more of a hold than a buy."

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Mr. Market]]**: The stock's decline despite exceptional fundamentals is a reminder that market price reflects investor expectations and sentiment about future cash flows, not just trailing earnings — Mr. Market is pricing in capex risk and AI disruption fears that the income statement alone does not capture.
- **[[Opportunity Cost]]**: The Apple contrast illustrates opportunity cost in capital allocation thinking: investors comparing MSFT's capital-intensive AI bet against Apple's asset-light returns are rationally demanding a valuation discount until the capex translates into proportional free cash flow.
