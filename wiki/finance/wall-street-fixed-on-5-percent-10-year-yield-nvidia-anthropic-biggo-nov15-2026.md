## Summary
A Korean analyst's outlook, "Many Events, But Ultimately Rates", argues that Nvidia's earnings and Anthropic's IPO matter less than the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield, now back above 5%. The Huang resignation is not expected to hit earnings, valuations have eased and IPO supply is small, but expected Fed hikes could erode stocks' relative appeal. Institutional views on the AI trade remain split.

## Core Concepts
- [[10-Year Treasury Yield]]: back above 5%, jumping from 4.85% to 5.15% in a week.
- [[Federal Reserve]] hikes: futures price a near-90% chance of a December hike; [[Deutsche Bank]] sees three 25bp hikes.
- [[Nvidia]] and [[Jensen Huang]]: shares down over 13% on the week, with a forward P/E of 11x versus a 22x high.
- [[Anthropic IPO]]: estimated 0.3% of S&P 500 market cap for the year's IPO volume, low versus past booms.
- [[Earnings Yield]]: the S&P 500's roughly 5.5% (18x forward P/E) is close to the 5% bond yield.
- [[Yield Curve]] shape: flattening from 2-year rises favors growth; steepening from 10-year rises favors value.
- [[Inflation]]: October CPI beat expectations and PPI posted its largest annual rise in about four years.

## Key Takeaways
- **Rates Dominate**: Treasury yields outweigh Nvidia's earnings and Anthropic's listing as the market driver.
- **Nvidia Slump**: Six-week losing streak, the longest since October 2012.
- **Valuation Relief**: Nvidia forward P/E at 11x, versus 22x this year's high.
- **No Supply Shock**: IPO volume is 0.3% of S&P 500 cap, versus 0.8-0.9% in past booms.
- **Stocks Versus Bonds**: 5.5% earnings yield offers little cushion over a 5% Treasury.
- **Curve Matters**: Flattening favors growth stocks; steepening favors value stocks.
- **Hawkish Fed**: Daly and Collins back tightening; Deutsche Bank projects 4.5-4.75% by March.
- **Mixed Views**: BlackRock cut U.S. equities to neutral; JPMorgan Asset Management suggests a tech-cyclical barbell.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the report's core claim about the week's market?
**A**: Despite Nvidia's CEO exit, Nvidia earnings and Anthropic's IPO, the deciding variable is the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield above 5%, because rising rates could offset AI-related tailwinds.

**Q2**: Why does a 5% yield threaten stocks here?
**A**: The S&P 500's earnings yield is about 5.5% at an 18x forward P/E, so further yield increases narrow stocks' advantage over bonds. Futures also price a near-90% chance of a December Fed hike.

**Q3**: How should investors adjust, according to the analyst?
**A**: Watch whether next-year earnings estimates are revised up and whether foreign investors turn net buyers. The analyst also suggests favoring firms with faster earnings growth next year, and growth stocks in a flattening curve versus value stocks in a steepening one.
