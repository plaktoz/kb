## Summary

[[MSCI]] scrapped its "Extreme Price Increase" cooling-off rule for fast-rising stocks with sufficient free-float, a methodology change confirmed August 5 that could accelerate AI and semiconductor names into the [[iShares MSCI World ETF]] (IWMO's benchmark family) at its August 12 index review, implemented August 31.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Extreme Price Increase (EPI) mechanism]]**: MSCI's prior rule forcing a mandatory cooling-off/waiting period before admitting stocks with outsized recent gains; now lifted for companies with a free-float factor of at least 0.75.
- **[[iShares MSCI World ETF]]**: ~$8.18 billion in assets across 1,284 holdings; roughly 72.45% allocated to US equities and 30.27% to information technology — a concentration the rule change could deepen further.
- **August 2026 index review**: MSCI confirmed results publish August 12, with rebalancing implemented at the close of trading August 31.

## Key Takeaways

- The rule relaxation lands right after an earnings season where Palantir surged ~30% on 93% revenue growth, AMD beat expectations, and Microsoft's Azure division topped $100B in annual sales — moves that might have tripped the old EPI cooling-off threshold.
- The ETF closed around $208.63–$208.86 on Thursday, up 12.31% since January and just 1.63% below its 52-week high ($212.08, set in June); RSI of 64.4 is approaching but not yet in overbought territory.
- The change doesn't reduce the fund's underlying concentration risk in US technology — it simply makes the index faster to reflect that concentration as momentum stocks qualify for inclusion sooner.
- Market participants are watching the August review specifically for semiconductor and AI names that previously tripped the old waiting-period rule.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]] (index-methodology variant)**: Once a rule designed to dampen index chasing of "hot" stocks is relaxed specifically because those stocks are now driving fund performance, the index's construction starts optimizing for capturing momentum rather than for stability — a shift in what the benchmark is actually measuring.
