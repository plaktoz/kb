## Summary

Wall Street fell sharply on October 16, with the Nasdaq down 2.19% and on track for its worst weekly loss since June, driven by a three-way combination of AI infrastructure debt concerns (CoreWeave -12%), oil prices jumping 3%+ on Middle East supply worries, and hotter-than-expected inflation reports. The S&P 500 was set for its steepest weekly loss since January despite remaining up ~12% for 2026.

## Core Concepts

- **[[CoreWeave]] selloff** — Down 12% on Oct 16, -25% over five sessions; AI infrastructure debt concerns are the primary catalyst
- **[[PHLX chip index]]** — Down 2.5%; 8th straight day of declines; Broadcom -3.6%, AMD -1.5%
- **Oil price spike** — +3%+ on Middle East supply worries; "tends to weigh on P/E multiples" per US Bank strategist
- **[[Anthropic]] IPO valuation concerns** — Reuters: some current and prospective investors weighing a lower valuation for Anthropic's upcoming IPO
- **Inflation persistence** — Hotter-than-expected CPI and PPI this week; Barclays now expects 25bp hikes in December and January
- **Fed Chair [[Kevin Warsh]]** — Voiced optimism about U.S. economic strength; acknowledged global economies "hanging in there" despite energy shock
- **AI concentration risk** — S&P 500 up ~12% YTD, but median component only +6%; ~185 S&P 500 stocks are negative for 2026

## Key Takeaways

- **Nasdaq -2.19%**: On track for worst weekly loss since June; ~4% for the week.
- **CoreWeave -25% in five sessions**: AI debt concerns driving aggressive de-risking.
- **Oil +3%**: Compresses equity P/E multiples via inflation and higher-for-longer rate expectations.
- **AI return concentration**: Most of S&P 500's 12% gain is from AI megacaps; median stock up only 6%.
- **Earnings catalyst ahead**: Q3 earnings from Tesla, Meta, P&G due next week; analysts project 30%+ EPS growth.
- **Anthropic IPO headwind**: Valuation concerns add another layer of AI sector uncertainty.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Concentrated market gains from a single theme create fragile indices.** When most of the S&P 500's return comes from AI megacaps, any shock to AI sentiment produces outsized index drawdowns even if the broader economy is healthy. · *model:* [[Concentration Risk]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What three factors drove the Wall Street selloff on October 16?
**A**: AI infrastructure debt concerns (led by CoreWeave's -12% decline), oil prices jumping over 3% on Middle East supply worries, and hotter-than-expected inflation data raising rate-hike expectations.

**Q2**: What does the S&P 500's return distribution reveal about the 2026 rally?
**A**: The S&P 500 is up ~12% for 2026, but the median component has only gained 6% and about 185 stocks are negative — virtually all the index-level gain is concentrated in AI-related megacaps.

**Q3**: How does rising oil weigh on equity valuations specifically?
**A**: Higher oil prices boost inflation expectations, which pushes bond yields higher and compresses price-to-earnings multiples — investors discount future earnings more heavily when risk-free rates rise.
