## Summary

HSBC initiated coverage of [[SpaceX]] (NASDAQ: SPCX) with a Hold rating and a $115 price target — below the $135 IPO price — even after applying a 2x premium for Elon Musk's track record with disruptive technologies. Shares have fallen roughly 50% from their post-IPO peak of $225.64, trading at approximately 75x trailing revenue despite the company remaining unprofitable. Wall Street consensus sits near $237, though targets range widely from $62 to $800, reflecting deep uncertainty ahead of SpaceX's first quarterly earnings report due August 4, 2026.

## Core Concepts

- **[[SpaceX]] (NASDAQ: SPCX)**: Recently public aerospace company led by [[Elon Musk]], trading at a significant premium to revenue despite being unprofitable.
- **[[HSBC]] Initiation**: Coverage started at Hold with a $115 price target derived from a sum-of-parts analysis augmented by a 2x innovation premium.
- **[[Sum-of-Parts Valuation]]**: Method used to justify the $115 base case; bull case at $293 hinges on [[Starship]] commercial viability by 2027 and doubled launch capacity.
- **[[Starship]]**: SpaceX's next-generation rocket whose commercial readiness is a key swing factor in upside scenarios.
- **[[Post-IPO Correction]]**: Shares down ~50% from peak of $225.64, reflecting valuation reset after initial enthusiasm.
- **[[Analyst Divergence]]**: Targets ranging from $62 to $800 indicate unusually wide disagreement on intrinsic value for a newly public company.

## Key Takeaways

- HSBC set a $115 target — essentially at-market — with a Hold rating.
- Even a 2x Musk premium in the model only justified the current share price.
- Bull case of $293 requires [[Starship]] operational by 2027 with 2x launch capacity.
- Consensus target ~$237; widest analyst range on record ($62–$800).
- SpaceX trades at ~75x trailing revenue (~$19B) and is unprofitable.
- First earnings as a public company expected August 4, 2026.
- Shares fell ~6% Friday, closing at $115.07, ~50% off post-IPO peak.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Wide Moat vs. Wide Uncertainty]]**: The extreme analyst target spread ($62–$800) reflects a company where competitive advantages are acknowledged but the path to monetization is genuinely unknowable — classic deep-uncertainty valuation, not just disagreement on inputs.
- **[[Anchoring Bias]]**: The $135 IPO price and $225 peak act as reference anchors; HSBC's at-market Hold at $115 is notable precisely because it resists anchoring upward to the consensus $237 target.
