## Summary

On 8 October 2026 a Financial Times report, confirmed by Reuters, put [[OpenAI]]'s run rate at about $50bn at end-September, roughly $18–20bn below the $68–70bn figure that had circulated. The gap appears to be mainly an accounting story (gross vs. net partner sales), but it hit AI stocks hard because so much of the supply chain, much of it debt-funded or financed by suppliers, depends on OpenAI continuing to grow and pay for compute. The article argues that hyperscaler capex in late-October earnings matters more for the AI trade than any private-company run rate.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Annualized Revenue Run Rate]]**: One month of revenue times 12. It is unaudited, sensitive to accounting choices, and analysts call it sometimes misleading.
- **Gross vs. net booking ([[Revenue Recognition]])**: [[OpenAI]] books its net share of cloud-partner sales (about 20% historically shared with [[Microsoft]]). [[Anthropic]] books the gross amount of certain sales via AWS, Google and Microsoft.
- **Market reaction (8 Oct)**: [[Nasdaq Composite]] −1.25% to 27,193.34, its worst session since mid-August. [[CoreWeave]] −7.77%, [[Oracle]] −5.48%, [[Broadcom]] −4.35%, [[AMD]] −3.90%, [[Nvidia]] −2.94%. The Dow rose 0.10%, and all three indices recovered on 9 Oct.
- **Year-end target**: Bloomberg reported on 9 Oct that OpenAI still expects a $70bn+ run rate by year-end, which needs about +40% in Q4. Q3 growth was +77% total and +107% enterprise. CFO [[Sarah Friar]] cited 70% growth.
- **OpenAI vs. Anthropic**: Anthropic's run rate was above $65bn at end-July, and its Q2 revenue ($11.5bn) overtook OpenAI's ($6.7bn) on each company's own basis.
- **Funding needs**: $278bn projected cash burn for 2026–2030. OpenAI is seeking $30bn+ at a $1.4tn pre-money valuation, and it has ruled out a 2026 listing.
- **[[Vendor Financing]] / circular financing**: [[Nvidia]] holds a $30bn equity stake in OpenAI and made more than $40bn of AI equity bets in 2026. [[AMD]] supplies 6 GW of GPUs with a warrant for up to 160m shares. Broadcom offers [[Anthropic]] a loan facility of up to $42bn. [[Elon Musk]] called these flows an "infinite money glitch."
- **Leveraged end of the chain**: [[Oracle]] has $664bn of RPO, of which about $300bn is OpenAI, alongside −$5.4bn FCF and about $132bn net debt. [[CoreWeave]] has about $46bn net debt (8.2x EBITDA) and 8.5–9.75% note coupons.
- **[[Hyperscaler Capex]]**: 2026 guidance totals about $720–745bn (Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta). Moody's sees nearly $1tn in 2027.
- **Macro backdrop**: 10-year yield about 5.23% and 30-year about 5.60%, both near 24-year highs. Brent was above $100. The Fed under [[Kevin Warsh]] hiked in September, with a 69.2% chance of a December hike.

## Key Takeaways

- **Revenue gap**: About $50bn actual vs. $68–70bn circulated, a gap of roughly $20bn.
- **Accounting, not collapse**: The higher figure counted gross cloud-partner sales.
- **Run-rate caveat**: Unaudited, one-month snapshot, and not comparable across booking methods.
- **Leverage hit hardest**: CoreWeave and Oracle fell most, while chip designers fell less.
- **Circularity spreads doubt**: Suppliers funding customers tie the chain to a few labs.
- **Concentration risk**: Several big contracts rest on one loss-making customer.
- **Q4 hurdle**: Reaching $70bn needs about 40% growth in one quarter.
- **Cash burn**: $278bn projected for 2026–2030, with a $30bn+ raise sought.
- **Next test**: Late-October hyperscaler capex outweighs private run-rate headlines.
- **Macro pressure**: Yields near 24-year highs weigh on growth-stock valuations.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Shared dependence transmits shocks** — When many suppliers' revenue depends on the same few customers, especially customers the suppliers themselves fund, doubt about one customer's income spreads across the whole chain at once. · *model:* [[Concentration Risk]]
→ explains: Circularity spreads doubt, Concentration risk

**T2. Leverage amplifies sensitivity to customer doubt** — Firms that borrow heavily to build capacity for a few clients fall furthest when those clients' ability to pay is questioned. · *model:* [[Leverage]]
→ explains: Leverage hit hardest

**T3. A metric is only as comparable as its definition** — Unaudited, extrapolated figures built on different accounting choices can diverge by billions without any change in the underlying business. · *model:* [[Measurement Invariance]]
→ explains: Accounting, not collapse, Run-rate caveat, Revenue gap

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does the article conclude caused the 8 October 2026 AI selloff?
**A**: It calls the OpenAI headline mainly an accounting story: a gross-based ~$70bn figure met a net-based ~$50bn figure. Markets reacted because much of the AI chain depends on OpenAI's ability to keep growing and paying for compute.

**Q2**: Why did CoreWeave and Oracle fall more than Nvidia?
**A**: Both borrow heavily to build data centres for AI clients. Oracle has about $132bn net debt and ~$300bn of its RPO tied to OpenAI, and CoreWeave has ~$46bn net debt at 8.2x EBITDA. Chip designers have stronger balance sheets and a wider customer base.

**Q3**: What should investors watch next, according to the article?
**A**: Late-October Big Tech earnings and capex plans (Microsoft and Alphabet on 28 October) plus the 27–28 October FOMC meeting. The article argues these hyperscaler spending plans anchor demand for the whole chain more than any private run rate does.
