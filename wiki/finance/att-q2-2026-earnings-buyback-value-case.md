## Summary

AT&T beat Q2 2026 estimates with 20% adjusted EPS growth and raised its buyback plan to $10B, while trading at ~8x earnings with a 4.6% yield — a classic value investing setup. The catch: revenue is growing only 2.3% annually, close to the threshold where it could turn negative, and net debt of $126B is set to rise further with the EchoStar acquisition. The author recommends owning it for income investors but warns to keep positions modest.

## Core Concepts

- [[AT&T]] Q2 2026 as a [[Value Investing]] case study: low multiple, high yield, strong cash flow
- [[Free Cash Flow]] discipline: $4.7B FCF funds both $7.6B dividends and $10B buyback — minimal cushion
- [[Fiber Expansion]] as AT&T's growth engine (38.6M locations passed)
- [[Debt Risk]]: $126.4B net debt rising to ~3.2x EBITDA post-EchoStar before declining

## Key Takeaways

- **EPS growth**: Adjusted EPS $0.65 vs. $0.54 prior year (~20% growth)
- **Buyback raised**: $10B in 2026, up from $8B
- **3-year plan**: $45B total shareholder returns, $21B+ FCF by 2028
- **Revenue risk**: 2.3% annual growth — "just a few percentage points from flipping to a decline"
- **Verdict**: Income investors — yes; growth investors — look elsewhere
