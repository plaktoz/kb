## Summary

The [[FOMC]] unanimously raised the [[Federal Funds Rate]] by 25 basis points to 3.75%–4.00% on September 16, 2026, but it was [[Kevin Warsh]]'s phrase "timelier return" to the 2% inflation goal that rattled markets, sending the Dow down 631 points. The updated [[Dot Plot]] projects another 50 basis points of hikes before year-end, no cuts in 2027, and core inflation not returning to 2% until 2029. Warsh's remark that he would be "hard-pressed to describe broad financial conditions as restrictive" signaled the Fed sees more tightening headroom, raising concerns that rising borrowing costs could undermine Wall Street's AI-driven bull market.

## Core Concepts

- [[Kevin Warsh]] — [[Fed Chair]] whose phrase "timelier return" to the 2% inflation goal spooked markets; also referenced his Jackson Hole comment that inflation must fall at "sufficient speed."
- [[FOMC]] — voted unanimously to raise rates; hawkish dot plot and Warsh's language together put Wall Street on notice for extended tightening.
- [[Dot Plot]] — September 2026 update projects 50 additional basis points of hikes before year-end, no rate cuts in 2027, and core inflation not at 2% until 2029.
- [[Federal Funds Rate]] — raised to 3.75%–4.00%; Warsh's view that financial conditions are not "restrictive" implies more room to hike.
- [[Disinflation]] — the pace of falling inflation is the Fed's central concern; "timelier return" signals dissatisfaction with the current disinflation speed.
- [[AI Infrastructure Investment]] — rising borrowing costs threaten capital-intensive AI infrastructure buildout, potentially ending the four-year AI-driven bull market.
- [[Dow Jones Industrial Average]] — dropped 631 points on FOMC day, a direct market verdict on Warsh's hawkish language.

## Key Takeaways

- **Market shock**: Dow fell 631 points; S&P 500 and Nasdaq also closed lower on FOMC day.
- **"Timelier return"**: Two words from Warsh's statement drove the sell-off — signaling impatience with disinflation pace.
- **Dot plot hawkishness**: 50 more bps of hikes expected before year-end; no cuts in 2027; 2% inflation not until 2029.
- **Conditions "not restrictive"**: Warsh's phrasing implies the Fed doesn't yet see financial conditions as tight enough.
- **Jackson Hole callback**: Warsh's earlier call for inflation to fall at "sufficient speed" reinforced the hawkish read.
- **AI bull market at risk**: Higher rates could slow AI infrastructure spending, threatening a four-year market rally.
- **Policy uncertainty elevated**: Extended timeline to 2% target (2029) prolongs rate uncertainty for businesses and investors.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Signaling Theory]]**: "Timelier return" is a low-cost signal with high-impact consequences — two words that shift market expectations about the entire rate path without any formal policy change, illustrating how central bank communication functions as a monetary policy instrument in its own right.
- **[[Second-Order Thinking]]**: The immediate read (one 25-bp hike) masked the second-order implication (50 more bps ahead, no 2027 cuts, 2029 inflation target) embedded in the dot plot — investors who only processed the headline missed the real signal.
