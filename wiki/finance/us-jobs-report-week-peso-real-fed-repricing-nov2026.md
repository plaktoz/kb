## Summary

Three US labor releases between November 3 and 6, 2026, led by the October jobs report on November 6, can reprice the dollar and Latin American currencies and bonds by changing Fed expectations and Treasury yields. The Fed held at 3.75%-4.00% with two dissents for a hike, and Polymarket put another 2026 hike at 70.5%. The same data can move the Mexican peso and Brazilian real differently depending on whether Fed repricing or growth optimism dominates.

## Core Concepts

- **[[October Jobs Report]]**: The Employment Situation, due November 6, combines the establishment and household surveys; [[JOLTS]] comes first on November 3.
- **[[Federal Reserve]]**: Held its rate on October 28 by 10 votes to 2; [[Lisa Cook]] and [[Beth Hammack]] voted for a hike.
- **[[Prediction Markets]]**: Polymarket priced another 2026 hike at 70.5% and "zero cuts" at 96.9%.
- **[[Mexican Peso]]**: Exposed through [[Carry Trade]] positioning and [[Remittances]]; the dollar is up about 11% against it since early September.
- **[[Brazilian Real]]**: Driven by dollar liquidity, Treasury yields, commodities, and domestic risk premiums.
- **[[Dollar Strength and Emerging-Market Risk]]**: Higher US yields raise the cost of holding peso and real assets and tighten global financial conditions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Transmission Chain**: Jobs data move Fed expectations, then Treasury yields, the dollar, flows, and commodities.
- **Revisions Matter**: Payroll revisions can move markets as much as the headline gain.
- **Peso Carry Risk**: Sharply higher US yields narrow carry and can force peso positions to unwind.
- **Divergent Outcomes**: Strong US data can lift Brazil's exporters yet weaken the real if rates dominate.
- **Worst Case**: Sharply weak payrolls could still hit Latin assets as markets seek dollar liquidity.
- **Gauges**: Two-year Treasury yield and the dollar are the cleanest reads on Fed repricing.
- **Surprise Over Level**: Watch the result relative to consensus, not just the headline number.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Higher yields in the reserve currency raise the opportunity cost of holding riskier assets** — When US yields and the dollar rise, capital leaves emerging-market currencies and bonds that now offer a relatively smaller reward. · *model:* [[Opportunity Cost]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why do US jobs numbers matter for the Mexican peso and Brazilian real?
**A**: They change Fed rate expectations, which move Treasury yields and the dollar. A stronger dollar and higher US yields pressure peso and real assets and local bonds.

**Q2**: Why can one strong US report affect Mexico and Brazil differently?
**A**: Peso moves lean on US yields through carry trades, while the real also depends on commodities and global growth. A strong report can support Brazilian exporters unless wage-driven Fed repricing dominates.

**Q3**: What should an investor watch during jobs week?
**A**: The surprise relative to consensus, wages, unemployment, participation, and payroll revisions. The two-year Treasury yield and the dollar are the cleanest gauges of Fed repricing.
