## Summary

Major U.S. banks — [[JPMorgan Chase]], [[Bank of America]], [[Wells Fargo]], [[Citigroup]], [[Goldman Sachs]], and [[Morgan Stanley]] — are heading into Q2 2026 earnings with analyst expectations of year-over-year profit growth. Consumer card spending rose 6.3% year-over-year in June, the strongest in four years, while tailwinds from AI deals, loan growth, and [[SpaceX]]'s IPO bolster the outlook. Despite the positive backdrop, analysts caution the quarter may not materially reset expectations higher.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Big Bank Earnings]]**: Q2 2026 results expected from all six major Wall Street banks, with unanimous consensus for profit growth.
- **[[Consumer Spending Resilience]]**: [[Bank of America]]'s Institute data shows card spending up 6.3% YoY in June, driven by discretionary purchases — strongest in four years.
- **[[AI Capital Markets Supercycle]]**: Wall Street operations described as "firing on all cylinders," with hopes of a multiyear AI-driven capital markets boom.
- **[[SpaceX IPO]]**: Generated a $500 million windfall for participating banks, serving as a one-time earnings tailwind.
- **[[Credit Market Health]]**: No stress signals; overall credit picture described as "benign" by [[HSBC]] analyst Saul Martinez.
- **[[Jamie Dimon]]**: JPMorgan CEO acknowledged the bank is "overearning" while noting uncertainty ahead.

## Key Takeaways

- All six major banks report Q2 2026 results; analysts expect year-over-year profit growth across all.
- [[Bank of America]] card spending: +6.3% YoY in June, best four-year reading.
- SpaceX IPO delivered ~$500M windfall to participating banks.
- AI deals and loan growth cited as key fundamental tailwinds.
- Credit conditions remain benign — no stress visible in markets.
- Analysts warn the quarter may not "recalibrate" expectations materially higher.
- [[Jamie Dimon]] retention award now exceeds $280M, reflecting bank resurgence.
- Market has shifted toward scrutinizing sustainability, not just headline profit beats.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Mean Reversion]]**: Dimon's own "overearning" admission implies awareness that current conditions are above-cycle norms — a first-principles read that outsized profits carry embedded reversion risk investors should price in.
- **[[Availability Bias]]**: The vividness of AI deal flow and a blockbuster IPO can mask whether the underlying earnings quality is structurally elevated or cyclically inflated.
