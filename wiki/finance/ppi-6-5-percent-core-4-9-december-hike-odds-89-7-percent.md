## Summary

October's Producer Price Index rose 0.3% on the month, slightly below the 0.4% forecast, but 6.5% year over year, the largest annual jump since November 2022. Goods drove the gain and core PPI ran 4.9% above last year, lifting CME FedWatch odds of a quarter-point December hike to 89.7%, a particular headwind for long-duration AI stocks.

## Core Concepts

- [[Producer Price Index]]: measures what businesses are paid for what they sell, before consumers see prices.
- [[Bureau of Labor Statistics]]: reported final demand goods up 0.5% and services up 0.2%.
- [[CME FedWatch]]: December 25-basis-point hike odds rose from 85.4% to 89.7%.
- [[Federal Reserve]] rate hikes and [[Long-Duration Stocks]]: higher rates shrink the present value of distant earnings.
- [[Nvidia]] and [[Jensen Huang]]: resigned as CEO, with [[Colette Kress]] interim, adding pressure to the AI trade.
- [[Core Inflation]]: PPI excluding food, energy and trade services rose 0.5% on the month and 4.9% on the year.

## Key Takeaways

- **Headline PPI**: Up 6.5% year over year, versus 7.4% in November 2022.
- **Goods-Led**: More than 60% of the October advance came from non-food, non-energy goods.
- **Input Costs**: Steel mill products rose 2.7%; industrial chemicals rose 3.1%.
- **Hike Odds**: 25-basis-point December hike at 89.7%; 50-basis-point at 10.3%.
- **Odds Ratchet**: Jobs, CPI and PPI data each pushed hike odds higher, never lower.
- **Multiple Drivers**: Nvidia leadership turmoil also weighed on tech, not PPI alone.
- **Next Test**: BLS publishes November PPI on December 15.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Higher discount rates reduce the present value of distant earnings** — The further in the future a company's profits lie, the more a rate increase cuts their value today. · *model:* [[Duration Risk]]
→ explains: Hike Odds, Multiple Drivers

**T2. Upstream prices lead downstream prices and margins** — When input costs rise, producers either absorb them in margin or pass them along, so producer prices give an early read on consumer prices and profits. · *model:* [[Leading vs. Lagging Indicators]]
→ explains: Input Costs, Goods-Led, Headline PPI

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about the October PPI?
**A**: Although the print came in slightly cooler than forecast at 0.3% monthly, the 6.5% annual level and 4.9% core reading remain high enough to leave a December Fed hike nearly certain.

**Q2**: What drove October's producer price increase?
**A**: Goods, which rose 0.5%, did most of the work, with steel mill products up 2.7% and industrial chemicals up 3.1%, while crude petroleum fell and made the headline look tamer.

**Q3**: Why does the rate outlook matter for AI and growth stocks?
**A**: A higher policy rate shrinks the present value of earnings expected years from now, and much of the AI trade is priced on that kind of future profit.
