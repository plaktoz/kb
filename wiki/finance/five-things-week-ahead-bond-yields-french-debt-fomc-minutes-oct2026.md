## Summary
Investing.com's preview of the first full trading week of Q4 2026 (October 5-9) puts the recent jump in government bond yields at the center, a rise that is spreading globally as the French-German 10-year spread hits its widest level since 1990. The other things to watch are the [[FOMC Minutes]] explaining September's first rate hike since 2023, the [[ISM Services PMI]], and earnings from [[Levi Strauss]] and [[PepsiCo]] as a read on the U.S. consumer. Resilient growth plus inflation from energy and AI-infrastructure costs is feeding expectations that the [[Federal Reserve]] will keep rates [[Higher for Longer]].

## Core Concepts
- **Q4 starting point**: The [[S&P 500]] is up more than 12% year to date and sits close to its August record high. [[Earnings Season]] starts in earnest later in October, and a Fed decision and the November 3 [[Midterm Elections]] are coming up.
- **[[Bond Yields]] spike**: Yields at multi-decade highs weighed on sentiment Monday. They reflect worries about energy-driven inflation and the soaring cost of [[AI Infrastructure]]. See [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]] and [[bond-yields-higher-for-longer-structural-forces-oct2026]].
- **[[French Government Bonds]]**: The spread between French and German 10-year yields (the [[Bund]] is the de facto Eurozone benchmark) recently touched its widest since 1990. That has raised fears of an emerging [[Euro Sovereign Debt Crisis]].
- **[[Deutsche Bank]] view**: With bond markets under "high stress and high alert," the week's main focus is central banks: the FOMC minutes on Wednesday and the [[European Central Bank]] account of its last meeting on Thursday.
- **September hike**: The [[Federal Open Market Committee]] raised rates 25bps to 3.75%-4%, its first hike since 2023. The aim was to rein in inflation, which has been above the 2% target for more than half a decade. See [[fed-12-0-rate-hike-3-75-percent-housing-stocks-sep2026]].
- **More hikes?**: Fed projections showed some officials see another hike before year-end, in October or December. Bets on October cooled after [[John Williams]] said there was no urgency and September jobs data came in soft. See [[october-rate-hike-odds-below-50-williams-barr-cook-sep2026]].
- **[[ISM Services PMI]]**: Expected at 55.1 versus 55.4 in August. Services make up about two-thirds of U.S. output. In August, new orders hit a 3.5-year peak while input prices rose, a possible sign of lingering inflation.
- **[[Levi Strauss]]**: Reports Wednesday after the close. It is betting on premium denim with wealthier customers. CEO [[Michelle Gass]] has pushed baggier Gen Z styles, the higher-margin [[Direct-to-Consumer]] business, and a "sizable premium segment."
- **[[PepsiCo]]**: Reports this week, days after announcing low-to-mid single-digit price increases on some chip brands to keep up with inflation. It is dealing with consumers shifting to healthier snacks, high input costs and wary shoppers. Back in February it cut prices on [[Lay's]] and [[Doritos]] after shoppers pushed back on earlier increases.

## Key Takeaways
- **S&P 500 YTD**: Up more than 12%, near August's all-time high.
- **Yield drivers**: Energy-driven inflation and soaring AI infrastructure costs.
- **France stress**: French-German 10-year spread at its widest since 1990.
- **Fed hike**: September's 25bp hike to 3.75%-4% was the first since 2023.
- **Inflation overshoot**: Above the 2% target for more than half a decade.
- **October hike bets**: Cooled after Williams' comments and soft September jobs data.
- **ISM services**: 55.1 expected vs. 55.4 prior; above 50 means expansion.
- **Levi's stock**: Down over 4% YTD despite July's raised profit outlook.
- **PepsiCo pricing**: Chip prices up low-to-mid single digits, after February's cuts.
- **Central bank week**: FOMC minutes Wednesday, ECB account Thursday.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the dominant market theme heading into the October 5-9, 2026 week, according to Investing.com?
**A**: A jump in government bond yields to multi-decade highs, driven by worries about energy-driven inflation and AI infrastructure costs, which is now spreading globally. Resilient growth is adding to expectations that the Fed will keep rates higher for longer.

**Q2**: What signals are pulling Fed rate expectations in different directions?
**A**: September projections showed some officials favor another hike in October or December. But NY Fed President John Williams said there was no urgency, and soft September jobs data cooled bets on an October move.

**Q3**: Why might Levi Strauss and PepsiCo earnings matter beyond the two companies?
**A**: They offer a read on the U.S. consumer before the main earnings season starts. Levi's is betting on premium buyers, while PepsiCo is going back and forth on pricing, cutting prices in February after backlash and now raising chip prices again to keep up with inflation.
