## Summary

[[Novo Nordisk]] (NVO) fell ~9.4% after its experimental heart drug [[Ziltivekimab]] failed Phase 3 ("Zeus" trial), missing its primary endpoint of reducing major adverse cardiovascular events despite lowering inflammatory markers. The failure adds to a series of 2026 setbacks for Novo Nordisk, though its core [[Semaglutide]] franchise (Ozempic, Wegovy) remains intact and the GLP-1 market is projected to reach $120 billion by 2030. At a P/E of ~11, the author views the stock as a potential buying opportunity.

## Core Concepts

- [[Novo Nordisk]] (NVO) — Danish pharma, leading GLP-1 developer
- [[Ziltivekimab]] — monthly [[IL-6 Inhibitor]] targeting cardiovascular/kidney/inflammation patients
- [[Zeus Trial]] — Phase 3; ~6,300 patients; failed MACE primary endpoint
- [[MACE (Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events)]] — heart attacks and strokes; primary trial endpoint
- [[IL-6]] / [[C-Reactive Protein]] — inflammation markers reduced by ziltivekimab, but MACE risk unchanged
- [[Semaglutide]] — Novo Nordisk's GLP-1 franchise (Ozempic, Wegovy); unaffected by trial
- [[GLP-1 Market]] — projected $120 billion by 2030
- [[CagriSema]] — Novo Nordisk combo drug; previously lost head-to-head vs [[Tirzepatide]] (Eli Lilly)
- [[Eli Lilly]] — key GLP-1 competitor
- Hermes and Artemis trials — ongoing; results expected early 2027

## Key Takeaways

- **Ziltivekimab** reduced IL-6 and CRP but failed to lower MACE risk.
- **Serious infection rates** were higher in the treated group vs placebo.
- **2026 operating profit guidance** unchanged; Q3 non-cash impairment charge expected.
- **NVO trades at ~11x P/E**, near historic lows after multiple 2026 setbacks.
- **GLP-1 franchise** (Ozempic, Wegovy) remains Novo Nordisk's core growth driver.
- **Two related trials** (Hermes, Artemis) ongoing; results expected early 2027.
- **$120B GLP-1 market** projected by 2030 — long-term thesis intact.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Surrogate Endpoint Fallacy]]**: Ziltivekimab improved biomarkers (IL-6, CRP) but not the clinical outcome that matters (MACE), illustrating how optimizing a measurable proxy does not guarantee improvement in the underlying outcome — a recurring trap in drug development and investing alike.
- **[[Mr. Market]]**: The ~9.4% single-day drop on one failed trial of a non-core drug, while the $120B GLP-1 franchise is unaffected, reflects market overreaction — the kind of short-term pessimism [[Benjamin Graham]]'s Mr. Market is known for creating.
- **[[Pipeline Discount]]**: Pharma valuation implicitly prices in probability-weighted pipeline optionality; a Phase 3 failure reprices that option to zero, causing a step-down disproportionate to the drug's contribution to intrinsic value if the base business is healthy.
