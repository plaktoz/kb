## Summary

Real US GDP grew 3.4% annualized in Q3, while private domestic demand grew 4.9%, the fastest pace since Q2 2021. Surging imports of AI data-center equipment widened the trade deficit and cut 3.32 percentage points from headline growth. Treasury debt-to-GDP ticked up to 121.7% because debt grew faster than the economy.

## Core Concepts

- [[Gross Domestic Product]]: real growth of 3.4% annualized; nominal GDP of $33.0 trillion grew 5.6%.
- [[Real Final Sales to Private Domestic Purchasers]]: excludes government, trade, and inventories; covers 88% of GDP; up 4.9%.
- [[Consumer Spending]]: up 3.8% for a second straight quarter; 69% of the economy; added 2.52 points.
- [[AI Investment Boom]]: drove equipment investment up 20.9% and a large inventory build.
- [[Trade Deficit]]: net exports subtracted 3.32 points as goods imports jumped 26.4%.
- [[GDP Deflator]]: 2.2% inflation in Q3, helped by lower average energy prices.
- [[Debt-to-GDP Ratio]]: 121.7%, up as Treasury debt reached $40.2 trillion.
- [[Residential Fixed Investment]]: down 3.3% amid a housing glut.

## Key Takeaways

- **Private Demand**: Real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 4.9%, after 4.6% in Q2.
- **Headline GDP**: 3.4% growth versus a 2.5% post-recession pre-pandemic average.
- **Contributions**: Investment +3.27, consumers +2.52, federal +0.91, net exports -3.32 points.
- **AI Equipment**: Equipment investment rose 20.9%; goods imports rose 26.4%.
- **Inventories**: Inventory change added 1.61 points, partly unsold AI equipment.
- **Housing Drag**: Residential investment fell 3.3% as builders face a glut.
- **Federal Spending**: Rose 15.8% annualized, with defense up 11.7%.
- **Debt Ratio**: Debt grew 1.8% versus GDP's 1.37% quarterly; ratio at 121.7%.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Headline aggregates can hide underlying demand** — A summary figure nets together components that move for unrelated reasons, so a single offsetting component can mask the strength or weakness of the rest.
→ explains: Private Demand, Contributions, AI Equipment

**T2. A debt ratio rises whenever debt outgrows the economy** — The ratio of debt to output worsens when the numerator grows faster than the denominator, regardless of the level of either.
→ explains: Debt Ratio

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does the article call the economy "red hot" when GDP grew 3.4%?
**A**: Real final sales to private domestic purchasers, which strips out government, trade, and inventories, rose 4.9%, the fastest since Q2 2021. Headline GDP was dragged down by a surge in imports.

**Q2**: How did AI investment affect the GDP components?
**A**: It lifted equipment investment 20.9% and inventories, but high-dollar AI-related imports pushed goods imports up 26.4%, so net exports subtracted 3.32 points from growth.

**Q3**: Why did the debt-to-GDP ratio worsen in Q3?
**A**: Treasury debt rose 1.8% quarter to quarter while nominal GDP rose 1.37%, lifting the ratio to 121.7%, though it remains below 122.4% a year earlier.
