## Summary

The [[Straits Times Index]] rose 1% (57.62 points) to 5,638.99 on August 6, 2026, led by blue-chip gains including all three local banks, even as broader-market breadth was negative (losers outnumbered gainers) and most regional Asian indices fell. An OCBC wealth advisor framed the move as a broadening market rotating into financials and healthcare rather than a loss of confidence.

## Core Concepts

- [[Straits Times Index]] — Singapore's benchmark blue-chip index, directly relevant to the [[ES3]] ETF holding
- [[Sector Rotation]] — capital moving from semiconductors/AI-theme stocks (which "took a breather") into financials and healthcare
- [[Market Breadth Divergence]] — the index rising 1% even as decliners (338) outnumbered advancers (239), reflecting concentration in large-cap gainers

## Key Takeaways

- **STI**: +1% to 5,638.99; DFI Retail Group led gainers (+3.7%), Mapletree Pan-Asia Commercial Trust the worst performer (-3%).
- **Local banks all higher**: DBS +2.1%, OCBC +2.4%, UOB +1.2%.
- **Regional divergence**: Hong Kong's Hang Seng -1.5%, Japan's Nikkei -0.9%, South Korea's Kospi -4.6%, Malaysia's KLCI -0.6% — Singapore was a regional outlier to the upside.
- **Analyst read**: OCBC's Afdhal Rahman called it "a broadening market rather than a loss of confidence," with capital rotating into "less-loved sectors such as financials" as the AI/semiconductor theme paused.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: How did the STI perform relative to other regional Asian markets on August 6, 2026?
**A**: The STI rose 1% while Hong Kong, Japan, South Korea, and Malaysia's benchmarks all fell — Singapore was a notable outlier.

**Q2**: What explains the STI's gain despite more decliners than advancers in the broader market?
**A**: Gains were concentrated in large-cap blue chips (especially the three local banks and DFI Retail Group), which carry more index weight than the broader field of smaller decliners.

**Q3**: What sector rotation does the OCBC analyst say is underway?
**A**: Capital rotating out of the AI/semiconductor theme, which had rallied hard in the first half, and into "less-loved" sectors like financials and healthcare.
