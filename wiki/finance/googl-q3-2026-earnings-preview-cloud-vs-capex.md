## Summary
Alphabet reports Q3 2026 results on October 28, having posted double-digit revenue growth for 12 straight quarters. A tougher Search comparison and heavy AI infrastructure spending may pressure growth and margins, but Google Cloud, advertising and subscriptions should support the quarter. Analysts expect EPS of $2.94 and rate the stock "Strong Buy" at about 17 times forward earnings.

## Core Concepts
- [[Alphabet]]: parent of [[Google Search]], [[YouTube]] and [[Google Cloud]]; reports Q3 on October 28.
- [[Google Cloud]]: Q2 revenue up 82% to $24.8 billion with a 35.6% operating margin.
- [[Cloud Backlog]]: rose over $50 billion sequentially to $514 billion.
- [[Tensor Processing Unit]]: TPU systems in customer data centers began generating revenue in Q2.
- [[AI Infrastructure Capex]]: elevated spending that may weigh on earnings and free cash flow.
- [[Google Search]]: faces a tougher year-over-year comparison because growth accelerated in Q3 last year.
- [[Subscription Revenue]]: YouTube Music, YouTube Premium and Google One add recurring income.

## Key Takeaways
- **Growth Streak**: Double-digit revenue growth for 12 consecutive quarters.
- **Search Comp**: Prior-year acceleration makes Q3 growth harder to match.
- **Cloud Growth**: Revenue growth accelerated from 63% in Q1 to 82% in Q2.
- **Cloud Profit**: Operating income of $8.8 billion; margin up from 20.7% to 35.6%.
- **Backlog**: $514 billion supports continued Cloud demand.
- **EPS Estimate**: $2.94 versus $2.87 a year earlier, implying modest growth.
- **Beat Record**: Alphabet beat earnings expectations in each of the past four quarters.
- **Valuation**: About 17 times forward earnings with a "Strong Buy" consensus.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Fixed-cost scale lifts margins faster than revenue** — When revenue grows against a largely fixed cost base, incremental revenue drops through to profit, expanding operating margin. · *model:* [[Operating Leverage]]
→ explains: Cloud Profit, Cloud Growth

**T2. Growth investment trades off against near-term profit and cash flow** — Spending to build capacity ahead of demand lowers current margins and free cash flow even while revenue growth stays strong.
→ explains: EPS Estimate, Search Comp
