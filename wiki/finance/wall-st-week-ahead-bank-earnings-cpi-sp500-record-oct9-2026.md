## Summary

Reuters previews a busy week ahead for U.S. markets: quarterly bank earnings and inflation (CPI) data will test the strength of the [[S&P 500]]'s rally, which hit an all-time closing high on Tuesday — its first since mid-August — and is up more than 13% year-to-date. Rising [[Treasury Yields]], with the 10-year near 5.23% after a 24-year high, are flagged as a growing headwind even though stocks have so far shrugged off a breach of the once-feared 5% threshold.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Bank Earnings]]**: Major banks' quarterly reports next week will signal corporate-profit strength alongside the inflation data.
- **[[Consumer Price Index]] (CPI)**: Upcoming CPI release is a second key data point investors are watching for the path of interest rates.
- **[[S&P 500]] record**: Hit an all-time closing high on Tuesday, its first since mid-August, despite declines on Wednesday and Thursday; YTD gain over 13%.
- **[[10-year Treasury Yield]]**: Around 5.23%, after recently touching its highest level in 24 years, driven partly by the [[Federal Reserve]]'s rate-hiking path.
- **[[Equity Valuation Pressure]]**: Higher yields make bonds more competitive with stocks and pressure valuations; the market has so far pushed through the 5% threshold once seen as a danger line.

## Key Takeaways

- **Catalysts**: Bank earnings and CPI data headline the week ahead.
- **New high**: S&P 500's Tuesday close was its first record since mid-August.
- **YTD gain**: More than 13% for the year, despite a Wednesday-Thursday pullback.
- **Yields**: 10-year Treasury near 5.23%, after a recent 24-year high.
- **Resilience**: Stocks have avoided major downside even as yields breached the once-critical 5% level.
- **Quote**: "At some point, major indices are going to cry uncle on higher rates" — Reynolds.
