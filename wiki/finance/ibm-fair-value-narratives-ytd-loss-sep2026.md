## Summary

[[IBM]] shares fell 23.0% (including dividends) from $296 at the start of 2026 to $220, despite a year full of quantum-computing headlines, an [[OpenAI]] partnership, and GenAI bookings growth. [[Simply Wall St]] frames this as a test of two competing "Fair Value" narratives — a bull case ($350) betting on margin expansion and a bear case ($198) betting on hyperscaler erosion — and finds Q2 2026 results (revenue $17,162M, net income $2,166M, 12.6% net margin) landed closer to the bearish scenario because margins failed to expand as the bull case required.

## Core Concepts

- **[[IBM]]** — enterprise technology company undergoing a multi-year pivot to hybrid cloud and AI-software revenue, anchored by the [[Red Hat]] acquisition
- **[[Fair Value Narrative]]** — Simply Wall St's framework of paired bull/bear valuation stories, each with explicit revenue-growth and margin assumptions, used to retroactively judge what the market "should" have priced
- **[[Net Margin]]** — the key metric that discriminated between the bull ($350 Fair Value, 15.9% margin target) and bear ($198 Fair Value) cases; actual Q2 2026 net margin came in at 12.6%
- **[[Hybrid Cloud]]** — IBM's core growth thesis, combined with GenAI platform revenue and recurring software/services
- **[[Hyperscalers]]** — [[AWS]]/[[Microsoft Azure]]-style rivals cited in the bear case as a long-term structural threat to IBM's legacy software and mainframe lines

## Key Takeaways

- **YTD loss**: IBM fell 23.0% including dividends, from $296 to $220.
- **Bull Fair Value**: $350, assuming 6.1% annual software growth and margins reaching 15.9%.
- **Bear Fair Value**: $198, assuming 4.6% annual growth with hyperscaler-driven erosion.
- **Q2 2026 actuals**: revenue $17,162M, net income $2,166M, net margin 12.6% — below the bull case's margin target.
- **Diagnosis**: headline AI/quantum news and growth kept flowing, but margin expansion — the variable the bull case actually needed — did not materialize.
- **Practical lesson**: track net margin and earnings alongside AI/cloud narratives rather than trusting headline growth or product announcements alone.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Narrative Fallacy]]**: A steady stream of quantum and AI-partnership headlines created an appearance of progress, but the market ultimately priced the underlying margin trajectory rather than the story — a reminder that vivid narrative and fundamental driver can diverge.
- **[[Base Rate vs. Story]]**: Both bull and bear cases agreed on positive revenue growth; the split hinged entirely on margin assumptions, illustrating that in valuation disputes the disagreement is often not about direction but about a single quantifiable driver.
