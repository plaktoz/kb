## Summary

AMD reached a $1 trillion market cap for the first time on September 21, 2026, capping a 187% year-to-date surge, while Nvidia is up only 22% in the same period. Despite AMD's explosive run, the author argues Nvidia is the better bet over the next 12 months because its ~15x forward earnings multiple leaves more margin of safety compared to AMD's ~40x. AMD's lofty valuation demands flawless execution and sustained investor enthusiasm to justify.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AMD]]** milestone: first $1 trillion market cap, driven by data center GPU momentum and CEO [[Lisa Su]]'s guidance that data center revenue could more than double YoY in 2027.
- **[[Nvidia]]** financials: Q2 FY2027 revenue of $96.2B (+106% YoY), net income $59.7B (+126% YoY), Q3 guidance of $108B, and ~70% revenue growth projected for FY2028.
- **[[Valuation Multiple]]** divergence: AMD trades at ~40x projected 2027 earnings; Nvidia trades at ~15x projected FY2028 earnings — a striking compression opportunity.
- **Supply-constrained growth**: Nvidia management described the outlook as "supply-constrained," signalling that demand is not the limiting factor.
- **[[Semiconductor Stocks]]** divergence: two AI chip leaders with vastly different YTD performances and forward multiples in the same macro environment.

## Key Takeaways

- **AMD milestone**: Reached $1T market cap Sept. 21, 2026, up 187% YTD.
- **Nvidia by contrast**: Up only 22% YTD despite superior earnings growth.
- **Nvidia Q2 FY2027**: Revenue $96.2B (+106% YoY); net income $59.7B (+126% YoY).
- **Nvidia Q3 guidance**: $108B revenue; ~70% growth projected for FY2028.
- **AMD Q2 2026**: Record revenue $11.5B (+50% YoY); data center more than doubled.
- **AMD valuation risk**: ~40x 2027 earnings after a 22% gain in four trading days.
- **Nvidia valuation cushion**: ~15x FY2028 earnings at ~$227/share.
- **Author's call**: Nvidia wins the next 12 months due to lower multiple and less execution risk.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Margin of Safety]]**: Nvidia's ~15x forward multiple versus AMD's ~40x means Nvidia can tolerate more disappointment before the thesis breaks — the classic value investing cushion applied to growth stocks.
- **[[Mean Reversion]]**: AMD's 187% YTD gain compresses future expected returns; a stock that has already priced in perfection leaves little room for compounding from here.
