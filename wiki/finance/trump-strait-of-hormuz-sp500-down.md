## Summary

Trump's renewed blockade of Iranian ships through the [[Strait of Hormuz]] in mid-July 2026 pushed the [[S&P 500]] down roughly 0.8%, with the [[VIX]] rising to 18.7, above June's average but well below March's war-onset levels. Compounding market pressure, the administration announced a 50% tariff on certain Canadian goods and 10–25% tariffs on 60 trading partners after prior attempts were struck down by the Supreme Court. The author argues long-term investors should view the turbulence as a potential buying opportunity rather than a panic trigger, citing strong historical returns through past crises.

## Core Concepts

- [[Strait of Hormuz]] — key shipping chokepoint; its blockade disrupts global oil supply and triggers risk-off sentiment in equity markets
- [[S&P 500]] — benchmark U.S. equity index; fell ~0.8% on renewed Iran conflict news
- [[VIX]] (CBOE Volatility Index) — fear gauge at 18.7, elevated but below the ~25.6 seen during March's war onset
- [[Iran Conflict 2026]] — ongoing geopolitical risk factor driving oil price uncertainty and equity volatility
- [[Tariff Policy]] — 50% levy on Canadian goods and broad 10–25% tariffs on 60 trading partners adding to market uncertainty
- [[Long-Term Investing]] — thesis that staying invested through volatility produces superior outcomes vs. panic selling
- [[Short-Term Fixed-Income ETFs]] — suggested allocation for investors with shorter time horizons

## Key Takeaways

- **S&P 500 drop**: Index fell ~0.79% after Trump renewed the [[Strait of Hormuz]] blockade.
- **VIX at 18.7**: Above June average (17.91), well below March war-onset average (~25.6).
- **Tariff escalation**: 50% on some Canadian goods; 10–25% on 60 trading partners after court defeats.
- **Historical resilience**: Pre-2008-recession S&P 500 investment returned 659% (with dividends) through 2026.
- **Pre-pandemic entry**: A 2020 S&P 500 investment returned 153% through 2026.
- **Buying opportunity framing**: Author positions volatility as an entry point for patient investors.
- **Short-horizon alternative**: [[Short-Term Fixed-Income ETFs]] recommended for those needing capital soon.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Time in Market vs. Timing the Market]]**: The 659% and 153% historical returns cited directly illustrate why staying invested through geopolitical shocks beats attempting to sidestep drawdowns — the cost of missing recoveries dwarfs the losses avoided.
- **[[Availability Bias]]**: The vividness of an Iran blockade headline makes a ~0.8% drawdown feel catastrophic; historical base rates (wars, recessions, pandemics all recovered) provide the corrective anchor the author supplies.
