## Summary

Global equities climbed on August 25, 2026, as investors positioned ahead of Nvidia's earnings, with the S&P 500 rising 0.32% to 7,677.28 and the Nasdaq gaining 0.66%. Treasury yields fell for a second consecutive day, partly tied to Treasury Secretary Bessent's expanded buyback program, while oil dropped about 3% as traders judged economic pressure less disruptive to supply than military action. Bitcoin briefly crossed $80,000 for the first time since mid-May.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nvidia Earnings Catalyst]]**: Anticipation of Nvidia's quarterly report drove technology outperformance; Tim Ghriskey noted that Nvidia's recent valuation decline attracted buyers seeking a strong print.
- **[[Treasury Buyback Program]]**: Secretary Bessent's expanded buyback initiative contributed to the second consecutive drop in 10-year [[Treasury Yields]], which fell ~8 basis points to 4.625%.
- **[[Oil Price Dynamics]]**: [[WTI Crude]] fell ~3% to $82.36/barrel despite U.S. sanctions threats against Iran; traders assessed economic headwinds as less disruptive to supply than military conflict.
- **[[Bitcoin]]**: Briefly touched $80,000 for the first time since mid-May before retreating.
- **[[Gold]]**: Spot gold edged up 0.31% to $4,665.86/oz but stalled near a key resistance level ahead of Wednesday's Fed inflation data.

## Key Takeaways

- **Equities up**: [[MSCI]] global gauge +0.42%; S&P 500 +0.32% to 7,677.28; [[Nasdaq]] +0.66%.
- **Tech outperformance**: Nasdaq led on Nvidia anticipation following recent sector weakness.
- **Yields down**: 10-year Treasury fell ~8 bps to 4.625%, second consecutive decline.
- **Buyback driver**: Bessent's expanded Treasury buyback program pressured yields lower.
- **Oil drops 3%**: WTI at $82.36 despite Iran sanctions rhetoric; Brent at $88.58.
- **Bitcoin milestone**: First touch of $80,000 since mid-May.
- **Gold stalls**: $4,665.86/oz; rally capped near resistance ahead of Fed PCE data.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Risk-On / Risk-Off Framework]]**: The simultaneous rise in equities, decline in yields, and oil sell-off reflects a "soft landing" risk-on posture — growth assets bid up while rate-sensitive and commodity assets price in a less inflationary environment.
- **[[Expectations Pricing]]**: Bitcoin and tech stocks moved primarily on anticipated events (Nvidia earnings, Fed data) rather than confirmed outcomes — illustrating how markets price the expected path, not the current state.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What was the primary driver of technology sector outperformance on August 25?
**A**: Investor anticipation ahead of Nvidia's earnings report, with strategist Tim Ghriskey noting that Nvidia's prior valuation decline had attracted buyers looking for a strong quarterly print.

**Q2**: What two forces drove Treasury yields lower on the day?
**A**: Continued demand for Treasuries pushed yields down for a second consecutive session, with Treasury Secretary Bessent's expanded buyback program cited as a contributing factor; the 10-year fell approximately 8 basis points to 4.625%.

**Q3**: Why did oil fall despite U.S. sanctions threats against Iran?
**A**: Traders assessed that economic pressure on Iran would be less disruptive to global oil supply than military action, leading them to sell despite the geopolitical headline risk.
