## Summary
Brent crude fell 5.4% to $92.97 on Monday after Trump downplayed renewed strikes on Iran, even though ships were hit near the Strait of Hormuz. Analysts say traders are pricing in a less disruptive outcome while physical-market data points to a return to excess supply as high prices cut demand.

## Core Concepts
- [[Brent Crude]] and [[West Texas Intermediate]]: the benchmark prices that fell $5.34 and $3.68 respectively.
- [[Donald Trump]]: said he was highly unlikely to order fresh attacks on Iran while talks progress.
- [[Strait of Hormuz]]: shipping attacks continue, but oil volumes remain strong, and Iraq reached an understanding to export via a lane near Oman.
- [[Iran Conflict]]: Tehran says it will not negotiate unless Washington meets seven conditions from a June interim agreement.
- [[OPEC]]: October output rose 1.38 million bpd to 22.87 million bpd, per a Reuters survey.
- [[Strategic Petroleum Reserve]]: US stocks fell to 279.5 million barrels, the lowest since October 1982.
- [[Dated Brent]]: now at a discount to nearby futures, per Morgan Stanley, signaling physical-market weakness.

## Key Takeaways
- **Price Move**: Brent fell 5.4% to $92.97; WTI fell 4.1% to $87.15.
- **Weekly Slide**: Brent dropped 16% last week, the steepest weekly fall since Covid began.
- **Sentiment vs. Reality**: Sellers priced in peace while attacks on ships and a Saudi pipeline continued.
- **Surplus Signal**: JPMorgan estimates a 1.4 million bpd October surplus as demand drops on high prices.
- **Supply Growth**: OPEC output rose 1.38 million bpd in October.
- **Low Buffer**: The US SPR is at its lowest level since 1982.
- **Diplomacy Doubt**: Senior US and Gulf officials doubt an imminent deal despite the Qatar meeting.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. High prices suppress demand and restore surplus** — Elevated prices reduce consumption while supply keeps growing, pushing a tight market back toward oversupply. · *model:* [[Supply and Demand]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did oil fall on Monday despite continued attacks near the Strait of Hormuz?
**A**: Trump signaled he was unlikely to order fresh strikes on Iran while talks progress, which encouraged sellers on prospects for peace. Analysts noted traders were pricing in a less disruptive outcome even though fighting was not slowing.

**Q2**: What evidence points to a well-supplied physical oil market?
**A**: Dated Brent weakened to a discount to futures, JPMorgan estimated a 1.4 million bpd October surplus, and OPEC output rose 1.38 million bpd to 22.87 million bpd. Some analysts say demand is dropping because of high prices.

**Q3**: What risk remains for oil prices despite the selloff?
**A**: Shipping and regional security risks remain unresolved, with tanker incidents, a Saudi pipeline strike, and the Houthi attack on Aramco's Riyadh office. The US SPR is also at its lowest since 1982, leaving a thinner buffer against disruption.
