## Summary

Goldman Sachs analysts warn that the [[S&P 500]] historically averages a negative 2% three-month return at the start of [[Federal Reserve]] tightening cycles, driven by growth drag, valuation compression, and higher borrowing sensitivity. Despite near-term headwinds, the 12-month average return following a tightening cycle start is +9%, and Goldman's base case remains that the bull market continues with earnings growth as the key driver. Markets have already priced in roughly 75 basis points of tightening through mid-2027, potentially softening the initial impact.

## Core Concepts

- [[Federal Reserve]] tightening cycle — the Fed's shift from holding or cutting rates to raising them; the first hike in three years is expected at the September 2026 FOMC meeting.
- [[S&P 500]] historical returns — Goldman Sachs data shows average 3-month return of -2% at cycle starts, but +9% over the following 12 months.
- [[Goldman Sachs]] — Ben Snider-led equity research team providing the historical analysis and current cycle assessment.
- [[AI Capital Expenditure Sensitivity]] — the AI-driven investment boom makes this tightening cycle unusually sensitive to higher borrowing costs compared to prior cycles.
- [[Earnings Growth]] — Goldman's primary framework for equity performance; viewed as a more important driver than rate levels alone.
- [[Fed Funds Futures]] — markets pricing ~90% probability of a quarter-point hike; roughly 75 bps of total tightening priced through mid-2027.

## Key Takeaways

- **3-month return**: S&P 500 averages -2% at the start of Fed tightening cycles.
- **12-month return**: Average +9% following tightening cycle initiation — medium-term outlook remains positive.
- **Growth drag**: Tightening weighs on economic growth, which matters more than rate levels for equities.
- **Valuation compression**: Hiking cycles historically mark peaks of "high-valuation, high-concentration bull markets."
- **AI sensitivity**: Current cycle is "particularly capital intensive," amplifying rate sensitivity.
- **Pre-priced tightening**: ~75 bps already priced in through mid-2027, potentially muting the shock.
- **Goldman's bull case**: Earnings growth remains the key driver; bull market expected to continue.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Discounted Cash Flow]]**: Higher rates increase the discount rate applied to future earnings, compressing present valuations — especially severe for capital-intensive AI infrastructure with long payback periods.
- **[[Reflexivity]]**: Markets partially price in tightening before it happens; the ~75 bps already embedded in futures dampens the negative signal of each individual hike, making the actual move less impactful than the announcement cycle.
