## Summary

[[Arm Holdings]] (ARM) fell ~10% in a broader AI stock selloff triggered by [[Dario Amodei]]'s essay advocating slower AI model development, putting the stock roughly 40% below its 52-week high of $452.70. The author argues the selloff is disconnected from Arm's fundamentals — revenue flows from chip licensing and royalties tied to physical shipments, not AI training timelines. Despite healthy business metrics, the stock's ~79x forward earnings valuation makes it difficult to justify buying at current prices.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Arm Holdings]] Business Model**: Earns revenue through [[IP Licensing]] fees and per-chip royalties; revenue follows physical chip shipments rather than AI training schedules.
- **[[AI Stock Selloff]] (Sept 2026)**: Triggered by [[Dario Amodei]] (Anthropic CEO) essay urging slower AI model development, with [[Sam Altman]] (OpenAI) echoing support; drove broad semiconductor declines.
- **[[Royalty Revenue]] Segments**: Smartphones (~43% of fiscal 2026 royalty revenue); data center (growing fastest, more than doubling YoY) via [[Neoverse]] server chips.
- **[[Valuation Risk]]**: Forward P/E of ~79x embeds years of AI-driven growth, creating a high bar even for a fundamentally strong business.
- **[[Smartphone Market]] Headwinds**: Higher memory prices dampening smartphone sales trimmed royalty growth guidance to "high teens."

## Key Takeaways

- ARM fell ~40% from its 52-week high of $452.70 amid AI sentiment shift.
- Fiscal Q1 2027 revenue: $1.29B (+22% YoY); non-GAAP EPS: $0.45 (+29% YoY).
- Data center royalties more than doubled YoY but come from Neoverse chips, not AI accelerators.
- Smartphones remain largest royalty segment at ~43% of fiscal 2026 royalty revenue.
- Guidance trimmed to "high teens" royalty growth due to memory prices, not AI slowdown concerns.
- **Valuation at ~79x forward earnings** is the primary reason author stays on the sidelines.
- Selloff cause (AI training pace) is largely irrelevant to Arm's actual royalty revenue mechanics.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Mr. Market]]**: The 10% single-day drop reflects sentiment contagion from an AI narrative shift rather than a deterioration in Arm's underlying cash flows — illustrating how Mr. Market prices mood, not just mechanics.
- **[[Price vs. Value]]**: Arm's business is healthy by fundamental metrics, but price paid matters; at 79x forward earnings, even a good business can be a poor investment.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why is Arm Holdings' stock down more than 40% from its 52-week high, and does the reason reflect actual business deterioration?
**A**: The decline was driven largely by AI sentiment — specifically Dario Amodei's essay urging slower AI model development — but Arm's revenue comes from per-chip royalties tied to physical shipments, not AI training timelines, so the selloff is somewhat disconnected from fundamentals.

**Q2**: What are Arm's two largest royalty revenue segments, and which is growing fastest?
**A**: Smartphones are the largest segment (~43% of fiscal 2026 royalty revenue), while data center royalties are growing fastest (more than doubling YoY) via Neoverse server chips.

**Q3**: Given the healthy fundamentals, why does the author still recommend staying on the sidelines?
**A**: The stock trades at roughly 79x forward earnings, pricing in years of AI-driven growth; that valuation leaves little margin of safety even if the business continues performing well.
