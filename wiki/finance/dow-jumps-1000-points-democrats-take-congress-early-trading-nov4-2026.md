## Summary

Early Wednesday trading rallied after Democrats won control of Congress, with the Dow up 1,178 points (2.3%) and the S&P 500 up 1.2% by 9:35 a.m. ET. Investors sorted winners (solar, wind) from losers (Trump Media, Musk companies, AI), but strategists say earnings and interest rates matter more than elections. Treasury yields eased on hopes that a Democratic Congress would check deficit spending.

## Core Concepts

- [[2026 US Midterm Elections]]: Stunning losses for Trump's party; Democrats set to control both the Senate and the House.
- [[Split Government and Markets]]: Wall Street generally prefers divided control because it lessens the risk of transformative economic change.
- [[Sector Rotation]]: Solar and wind companies rose; Trump Media, Musk businesses, and AI names fell.
- [[AI Stock Regulation Risk]]: A Democratic counterweight could mean more AI regulation; Texas wins may slow data center buildout.
- [[Treasury Yields]]: The 10-year yield eased to 4.92% from 4.95% after surging through the summer to decades-high levels.
- [[Brian Jacobsen]] and [[Carsten Brzeski]]: Strategists behind "earnings trump elections" and the "angry duck" vs. "tame duck" views.
- [[Trump Media & Technology Group]], [[Tesla]], [[SpaceX]], [[Nvidia]], [[Advanced Micro Devices]], [[First Solar]], [[Enphase Energy]]: Company moves in the early rally.
- [[Elon Musk]]: Former top Trump adviser and heavy Republican donor, a possible target for investigations by a Democratic Congress.

## Key Takeaways

- **Index Moves**: Dow +1,178 points (2.3%), S&P 500 +1.2%, Nasdaq +1.1% at 9:35 a.m. ET.
- **Possible Best Day**: S&P 500 potentially on track for its best day in six weeks.
- **Volatile Futures**: Futures swung from losses to gains before the market opened.
- **Post-Midterm Pattern**: US stocks historically do well in the six months after any midterm.
- **Trump Media Slide**: Down 9.1% after a 55.1% year-to-date loss; near $5 from $175 peak.
- **Musk Businesses**: Tesla fell 5.2% and SpaceX sank 4.8%.
- **AI Pressure**: Nvidia -1.5% was the heaviest S&P 500 weight; AMD rose 0.3% despite strong profit.
- **Green Power Surge**: First Solar jumped 15.1% and Enphase Energy climbed 12.1%.
- **Bond Yields**: 10-year Treasury fell to 4.92% from 4.95%, signaling less debt-pileup risk.
- **Policy Uncertainty**: Trump retains executive authority over defense, foreign policy, and some trade.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Fundamentals outweigh politics** — Stock prices depend more on where company profits and interest rates are heading than on election outcomes; early moves may not last.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about the market's reaction to the midterms?
**A**: Stocks rallied as Democrats took Congress, and Wall Street likes split control of Washington. Strategists say "earnings trump elections" because profits and interest rates matter more in the long term.

**Q2**: Which specific moves show how investors sorted winners from losers?
**A**: First Solar rose 15.1% and Enphase 12.1%, while Trump Media fell 9.1%, Tesla 5.2%, SpaceX 4.8%, and Nvidia 1.5%. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.92%.

**Q3**: What are the optimistic and downbeat views of Trump's position after the election?
**A**: Carsten Brzeski's downbeat case is an "angry duck" who restarts tariff and NATO spending tensions. His optimistic case is a "tame duck" who slows disruption and focuses on ending the two big wars and winning the Nobel Peace Prize.
