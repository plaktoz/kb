## Summary

AI infrastructure spending is compounding rapidly across the stack in 2026, with [[Nvidia]] reporting $89B in data center revenue for Q2 alone — up 117% YoY — and the four largest hyperscalers collectively guiding to ~$745B in combined 2026 capex. Cloud operators are also surging, led by [[Google Cloud]] at 82% YoY growth, while Goldman Sachs projects hyperscaler capex could reach $1.1–1.4 trillion by 2027. Risks include efficiency gains moderating demand and capex running at historically high levels relative to operating cash flows.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nvidia]]** — dominant chip supplier; Q2 2026 data center revenue $89B (+117% YoY); CEO Jensen Huang guided to $1T in [[Blackwell]] and [[Vera Rubin]] sales through 2027
- **[[Broadcom]]** — $10.8B AI semiconductor revenue in Q2 2026 (+143% YoY); Q3 guidance $16B (>200% growth); full-year raised to $56B
- **[[AMD]]** — $6.7B data center revenue Q2 2026 (+107% YoY)
- **[[Intel]]** — $6.3B DCAI revenue Q2 2026 (+59% YoY); strongest growth in years
- **[[IBM]]** — Infrastructure revenue $3.84B Q2 2026 (-7% overall); mainframe declines offset 37% AI-server growth
- **[[AWS]]** — $42.2B Q2 2026 (+37% YoY); full-year 2025 ~$128.7B
- **[[Microsoft Azure]]** — $39.3B Intelligent Cloud Q2 2026 (+32%); Azure alone crossed $100B annual run rate for first time
- **[[Google Cloud]]** — $24.8B Q2 2026 (+82% YoY); fastest-growing major provider
- **[[Hyperscaler Capex]]** — Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Meta collectively guide to ~$745B combined 2026 capex
- **[[Goldman Sachs]]** 2027 estimate: ~$1.1T base case, $1.4T bullish scenario

## Key Takeaways

- **Nvidia dominance**: $89B data center revenue in one quarter; +117% YoY growth
- **Broadcom surge**: Q3 AI chip guidance >200% YoY; full-year raised to $56B
- **Google Cloud fastest**: +82% YoY, outpacing AWS (+37%) and Azure (+32%)
- **Azure milestone**: First cloud segment to cross $100B annual run rate
- **Capex trajectory**: $745B collective hyperscaler capex in 2026; Goldman sees $1.1T+ by 2027
- **Risk factor**: Efficiency gains (e.g. model compression) could moderate infrastructure demand
- **Nvidia Q3 guide**: $108B total revenue for quarter ending Oct 2026

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Jevons Paradox]]**: As AI inference becomes cheaper and more efficient, total demand for compute may rise rather than fall — the same dynamic that made cheaper electricity increase total electricity consumption; efficiency gains historically expand adoption faster than they reduce unit consumption.
- **[[Winner-Take-Most Markets]]**: Nvidia's 117% growth while AMD lags at 107% and Intel at 59% illustrates how in platform markets, early architectural leads (CUDA ecosystem) compound into durable revenue concentration.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central finding about the pace of AI data center revenue growth in 2026?
**A**: Revenue is compounding across the full stack — chip suppliers like Nvidia (+117%) and cloud operators like Google Cloud (+82%) are all posting triple-digit or near-triple-digit YoY growth, with hyperscalers collectively targeting ~$745B in 2026 capex.

**Q2**: What specific revenue milestone did Microsoft Azure cross, and what did Goldman Sachs project for 2027 hyperscaler capex?
**A**: Azure surpassed $100B in annual revenue for the first time; Goldman Sachs projected 2027 hyperscaler capex at roughly $1.1T in its base case and up to $1.4T in a bullish scenario.

**Q3**: What risks could moderate this infrastructure buildout, and how does Nvidia's outlook reflect current momentum?
**A**: Efficiency gains from model compression or architectural improvements could reduce per-workload infrastructure needs; despite this, Nvidia guided to $108B in total revenue for Q3 2026, reflecting continued near-term demand strength.
