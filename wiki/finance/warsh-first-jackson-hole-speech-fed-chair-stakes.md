## Summary

Fed Chair [[Kevin Warsh]] delivers his first major speech as chair at [[Jackson Hole]] with unusually high stakes: investors want concrete clarity on inflation and rate policy after months of deliberately vague, "high-altitude" rhetoric, while the Fed's preferred inflation gauge sits at 3.7%, well above its 2% target.

## Core Concepts

- [[Jackson Hole Symposium]] — the Kansas City Fed's annual economic policy gathering, traditionally used by Fed chairs to deliver long-range, agenda-setting remarks rather than tactical near-term signals.
- [[Forward Guidance Reluctance]] — Warsh's deliberate avoidance of specifying future policy moves, instead telling markets to watch the economy rather than Fed communications.
- [[AI and Inflation Dynamics]] — the tension between AI's near-term inflationary effect (raising costs for construction crews and memory chips) and its longer-term disinflationary potential through productivity gains.

## Key Takeaways

- **Inflation gauge**: The Fed's preferred yardstick shows prices up 3.7% over the 12 months ending in July, down from 4.1% in May but still well above the 2% target.
- **Market odds**: Investors see about a one-in-three chance the Fed raises its benchmark rate at the mid-September meeting.
- **Investor frustration**: Warsh promised to curb inflation last month but offered no specifics, disappointing investors seeking "some understanding of how he views the inflation dynamics right now" (Kathy Bostjancic, Nationwide).
- **Warsh's own framing**: he wants to address "big questions" about productivity, demographics and the global economy rather than a "sea-level forecast" of near-term Fed action.
- **AI's dual effect**: Deutsche Bank's Matthew Luzzetti says AI is "clear evidence" of near-term inflationary pressure today but could become disinflationary over the medium term by lifting productivity growth.
