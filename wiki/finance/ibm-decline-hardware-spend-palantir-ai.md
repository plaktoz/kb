## Summary

IBM shares fell 25% after CEO Arvind Krishna revealed that enterprise customers shifted spending toward AI hardware — servers, storage, and memory — delaying large software deals in IBM's mainframe and transaction-processing business. Krishna argued that long-term value will shift toward orchestration and data layers, where IBM is building governance tools, but the company remains a multi-segment incumbent with uneven cycle exposure. Palantir, a pure play on enterprise AI decision-making, posted 85% revenue growth and expanding margins, making it a sharper bet on the same theme despite a richer valuation.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Infrastructure Spending Cycle]]**: Hardware costs (servers, storage, memory) are crowding out enterprise software budgets in the near term.
- **[[IBM]]**: Multi-segment incumbent offering AI governance tools including Sovereign Core, Confluent (data streaming), and Lightwell open-source security initiative.
- **[[Palantir Technologies]]**: Pure-play enterprise AI platform (Foundry, AIP) turning organizational data into operational decisions.
- **[[Orchestration and Data Layer]]**: Krishna's thesis that durable AI value accrues above the hardware layer, in data management and model governance.
- **[[Net Dollar Retention]]**: Palantir at 150% NDR signals existing customers are rapidly expanding usage, a leading indicator of platform stickiness.
- **[[Free Cash Flow Valuation]]**: Palantir trades at ~67x FCF guidance; IBM at ~17x forward earnings — reflecting growth premium vs. value discount.

## Key Takeaways

- IBM shares fell 25% due to hardware-driven AI budget shifts delaying software deals.
- CEO Krishna: value will migrate to orchestration and data layers over time.
- IBM's AI tools include Sovereign Core, Confluent, and Lightwell ($5B commitment).
- Palantir: 85% revenue growth, 11 consecutive quarters of acceleration.
- Palantir margins expanded from 44% to 60% YoY, strong operating leverage.
- Net dollar retention at 150%; remaining deal value up 98% to ~$12B.
- Palantir trades at ~39x sales and ~67x FCF — rich but growth justifies premium.
- IBM cheaper at 17x forward earnings but faces segment cycle risk.
- Palantir down 30% YTD, making valuation more reasonable entry point.

## First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Innovator's Dilemma]]**: IBM's multi-segment legacy structure creates conflicting cycle pressures — each division optimizes separately, making coherent AI-era pivots harder than for a focused pure-play like Palantir.
- **[[Winner-Take-Most Dynamics]]**: Enterprise AI platforms with deep data integration (Palantir's Foundry/AIP) exhibit strong lock-in once embedded; NDR of 150% reflects this compounding flywheel.
