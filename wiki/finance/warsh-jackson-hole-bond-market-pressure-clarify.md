## Summary

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh faces pressure to clarify his stance at Jackson Hole after a volatile month in which the bond market sold off following the July FOMC meeting and the Treasury twice intervened directly in currency and bond markets — raising unresolved questions about how far the Fed will cooperate with the Treasury on managing yields.

## Core Concepts

- [[Treasury-Fed Accord]] — the 1951 agreement that clarified the division of responsibility between the Treasury and the Fed on bond issuance and interest rates; Warsh has previously spoken of crafting an updated version.
- [[Bond Market Intervention]] — the Treasury's two direct interventions since the July FOMC meeting: using a Fed facility to prop up the yen, and separately acting to support prices of long-term Treasury bonds.
- [[Fed-Treasury Cooperation Ambiguity]] — the open question of how much the Fed will coordinate with an increasingly interventionist Treasury on managing bond and currency markets, and where the line falls.

## Key Takeaways

- **Post-FOMC selloff**: after the July meeting, the bond market sold off and long-term rates soared, with critics saying Warsh offered promises of price stability without concrete steps.
- **Two Treasury interventions**: the Treasury used a Fed facility to support the yen, then separately acted to support long-term Treasury bond prices — both since the July meeting.
- **Analyst demand for clarity**: Standard Chartered's Steve Englander and John Davies wrote that Warsh will need to "bluntly state" the FOMC will raise rates if core PCE inflation doesn't steadily decline in order to regain market confidence.
- **Unanswered structural question**: whether Warsh addresses long-term borrowing costs (treating it as a Fed concern) or stays silent on the long end (treating it as "the Treasury's problem").
- **Historical callback**: Warsh has previously floated updating the 1951 Treasury-Fed Accord that originally separated the two institutions' roles on debt and rates — Jackson Hole is seen as an opportunity to elaborate.
