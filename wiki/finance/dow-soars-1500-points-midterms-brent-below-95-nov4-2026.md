## Summary

US stocks surged on Wednesday after Republicans lost sole control of Washington in the midterm elections, with the Dow up 1,519 points (2.9%) and the S&P 500 up 1.8% by mid-afternoon. Investors sorted winners (solar, healthcare, dollar stores) from losers (defense, oil, AI, Trump Media, Musk companies), while Brent crude plunged 8.6% to $94.70. Strategists caution that earnings and interest rates, not election outcomes, ultimately drive stock prices.

## Core Concepts

- [[2026 US Midterm Elections]]: Democrats set to control both the Senate and the House, ending one-party control.
- [[Split Government and Markets]]: Wall Street generally prefers divided control because it lessens the risk of transformative economic change.
- [[Brent Crude]]: Fell 8.6% to $94.70; has veered between roughly $70 and $120 since just before the [[Iran War]].
- [[Sector Rotation]]: Solar, wind, healthcare, and dollar stores rallied; defense, oil majors, and AI names fell.
- [[AI Stock Regulation Risk]]: Democratic wins, notably in Texas, may slow AI data center build-outs and add regulation.
- [[Treasury Yields]]: The 10-year yield eased to 4.89% from 4.95% on hopes of a check on deficit spending.
- [[Brian Jacobsen]] and [[Carsten Brzeski]]: Strategists offering the "earnings trump elections" and "angry duck vs. tame duck" views.
- [[Trump Media & Technology Group]], [[Nvidia]], [[Alphabet]], [[Lockheed Martin]], [[First Solar]], [[HCA Healthcare]], [[Dollar Tree]]: Company moves in the day's rally.

## Key Takeaways

- **Index Moves**: Dow +1,519 points (2.9%), S&P 500 +1.8%, Nasdaq +1.9% at 2:06 p.m. ET.
- **Post-Midterm Pattern**: Stocks generally do well in months after midterms, regardless of winner.
- **Losers**: Trump Media -16.6%, CoreWeave -10.2%, Tesla -2.4%, SpaceX -4.8%, Nvidia -1.8%.
- **Defense Slide**: Lockheed Martin -7%, Northrop Grumman -4.2%, General Dynamics -2.4% on Iran war pullback hopes.
- **Energy Shift**: Brent fell 8.6% to $94.70; Exxon -4.9%, ConocoPhillips -5.6%; First Solar +5.8%.
- **Healthcare and Retail**: HCA +9.6%, Dollar Tree +8.9% on hopes of reversing summer cuts.
- **Bond Yields**: 10-year Treasury fell to 4.89%, seen as less risk of added debt.
- **Global Rally**: Paris +2%, Shanghai +2.2%, London +1.8%; Nikkei -1.4% was the outlier.
- **Policy Uncertainty**: Trump retains executive authority over defense, foreign policy, and some trade.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Fundamentals outweigh politics** — Over the long term, stock prices depend more on where company profits and interest rates are heading than on who controls Washington.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central claim about how markets relate to the midterm election results?
**A**: Stocks surged as Republicans lost sole control, and Wall Street favors split control for lowering the risk of transformative change. Still, strategists say "earnings trump elections" because profits and interest rates matter more long term.

**Q2**: Which specific market moves showed how investors sorted winners from losers?
**A**: Solar (First Solar +5.8%), healthcare (HCA +9.6%), and Dollar Tree (+8.9%) rose, while defense (Lockheed Martin -7%), oil majors (Exxon -4.9%), and AI names (CoreWeave -10.2%) fell. Brent crude dropped 8.6% to $94.70.

**Q3**: What uncertainties remain for investors after the election?
**A**: Trump keeps executive authority over defense, foreign policy, and some trade, so tariff and NATO tensions could restart ("angry duck") or ease ("tame duck"). Investors also face risks to AI, Trump Media, and Musk businesses from a Democratic-led Congress.
