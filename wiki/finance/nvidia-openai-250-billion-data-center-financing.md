## Summary

Nvidia is in talks to provide $250 billion in financial guarantees backing OpenAI's lease of a 10-gigawatt data center campus in southern Ohio, which would be the largest vendor-financing commitment in tech history. The arrangement exploits Nvidia's investment-grade balance sheet to secure debt that OpenAI could not raise independently, while a parallel deal could see Nvidia finance up to $350 billion in GPU purchases — totaling $600 billion in exposure. Critics have flagged the structure as a circular financing scheme where a chip supplier underwrites its own customer's ability to buy its products.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nvidia]]** backstopping [[OpenAI]]'s lease obligations via vendor financing, leveraging Nvidia's creditworthiness as collateral
- **[[AI Data Center]]** buildout: a 10 GW campus on a decommissioned uranium-enrichment site near Columbus, Ohio, developed by [[SB Energy]] ([[SoftBank]])
- **[[Vendor Financing]]**: Nvidia's balance sheet stands in for OpenAI's lacking investment-grade credit rating
- **[[Circular Financing]]**: chip supplier guarantees customer spending on its own products — flagged by [[Michael Burry]] as structurally bearish
- **[[Rubin Ultra]]** and **[[Feynman]]** GPU architectures (2027–2028) are the expected hardware for the campus
- **[[Off-Balance-Sheet AI Commitments]]**: Big Tech aggregate estimated at $1.65 trillion; [[Meta]] alone carries ~$420 billion
- **[[SoftBank]]** separately backing a $40 billion OpenAI loan through an eight-bank syndication

## Key Takeaways

- Nvidia's potential $250B guarantee is nearly 6x Google's $44B data center rent guarantee.
- Full project cost including chips expected to exceed $500 billion.
- Nvidia has already committed $40B+ to AI equity investments, including ~$30B in OpenAI.
- OpenAI lacks investment-grade credit; Nvidia's balance sheet serves as collateral.
- Ohio site power is federally controlled; Anthropic, Microsoft, and Google are also seeking access.
- AI infrastructure spend expected to exceed $700B in 2026; $3 trillion cumulative through 2028.
- OpenAI gains dedicated compute independence from Microsoft, Amazon, and Oracle.
- First phase (800 MW) targeted for completion in 2028; SB Energy IPO planned at $50B+ valuation.
- Nvidia's fiscal Q2 earnings (August) will be the first chance to question management on commitments.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Circular Dependency]]**: Nvidia financing OpenAI's GPU purchases creates a closed loop where the supplier's revenue depends on its own credit backstop — systemic fragility that amplifies rather than distributes risk.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: As AI infrastructure commitments become the proxy for competitive positioning, firms race to announce scale rather than demonstrate returns, inflating off-balance-sheet obligations disconnected from monetization.
