## Summary
The S&P 500 has fallen 10% from its October high on worries about the AI industry's profitability, financing costs, regulation and Nvidia's leadership upheaval, officially a "correction". Historically corrections occur every couple of years and most recover without becoming a bear market, though analysts split on whether this is a popping bubble or a healthy reset.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Stock Market Correction]]**: A 10% fall from a high; a [[Bear Market]] is a drop of at least 20%.
- **[[AI Stock Sell-Off]]**: Triggered by doubts over AI [[Valuation]], profitability and data center demand.
- **[[AI Data Center Spending]]**: Rising bond-funded capex, a [[Federal Reserve]] rate hike expected next month, and a new Congress plus Texas and Georgia governors hostile to data centers.
- **[[Nvidia]] and [[Jensen Huang]]**: The CEO's sudden departure was the catalyst for the sell-off.
- **[[Anthropic IPO]]**: Its blockbuster debut this week failed to stem the selling.
- **Analyst views**: [[David Trainer]] sees a bubble popping; [[Dan Ives]] sees panic; [[Karan Ramchandani]] sees a reevaluation; [[Mark Hackett]] sees a possible constructive reset.
- **[[CFRA]] history data**: Statistics on corrections since 1946.

## Key Takeaways
- **Frequency**: Corrections occur every couple of years on average, even in long bull markets.
- **Bull-market precedent**: Five corrections occurred during the March 2009 to February 2020 bull run.
- **Recoverable corrections**: 25 corrections since 1946 averaged 14.1% loss, 130 days to bottom.
- **Recovery time**: Those 25 corrections took an average 112 days to recoup losses.
- **Bear markets**: 14 declines became bear markets, averaging a 32% S&P 500 loss.
- **Last bear market**: The 2022 bear market cut the S&P 500 by 25.4%.
- **Debate**: Bubble burst versus panic versus selective reevaluation of AI companies.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about the 10% S&P 500 drop?
**A**: A correction is unnerving but not unusual; such drops have happened regularly for over a century and are often seen as healthy washouts of euphoria. The fear is that it could signal a bear market, a drop of at least 20%.

**Q2**: What does CFRA data show about past corrections that did not become bear markets?
**A**: Across 25 corrections since 1946, the S&P 500 lost an average of nearly 14.1% over about 130 days to bottom, then took an average 112 days to recoup losses. The 14 declines that became bear markets averaged a 32% loss.

**Q3**: How do the quoted analysts differ on what to do with this sell-off?
**A**: Trainer says the bubble is popping with further to go, while Ives calls it panic-driven and Ramchandani sees investors becoming more selective about which AI companies can deliver. Hackett says a true flush could reset sentiment for a more sustainable rally into year-end.
