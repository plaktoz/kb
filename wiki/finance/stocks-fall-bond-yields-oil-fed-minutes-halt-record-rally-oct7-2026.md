## Summary

On Wednesday, October 7, 2026, U.S. stocks pulled back from record highs. The Dow fell about 1%, the S&P 500 lost 0.5–0.6%, and the Nasdaq dropped 0.7%. The article names four causes: Treasury yields at 24-year highs, Brent crude above $101, nerves ahead of the Fed's September meeting minutes, and ordinary profit-taking after the S&P 500 closed above 7,800 for the first time the day before.

## Core Concepts

- **Record-rally pullback**: One day earlier, strong earnings estimates and gains in [[Nvidia]], [[Apple]], and [[Microsoft]] pushed the [[S&P 500]] to its first close above 7,800.
- **[[10-Year Treasury Yield]]**: Rose past 5.35%, the highest since April 2002. The [[30-Year Treasury Yield]] gained more than 8 bps to 5.725%, the highest since May 2002.
- **Yield competition**: When government bonds pay high, guaranteed returns, riskier assets like stocks look less attractive. Higher yields also make borrowing more expensive for companies and consumers.
- **[[Oil Price Shock]]**: [[Brent Crude]] rose above $101 a barrel and [[WTI Crude]] neared $90, reviving fears that [[Inflation]] will stay sticky.
- **Supply risks**: Renewed [[Houthi]] attacks on Saudi airports and the [[Strait of Hormuz]] keep the risk of supply disruption high. A tropical storm heading for the U.S. Gulf Coast led [[Chevron]] to evacuate nonessential offshore staff.
- **[[FOMC Minutes]]**: The minutes of the September meeting, where the [[Federal Reserve]] raised rates, were due at 2 p.m. ET. Traders want to know whether that hike was a one-off or the start of more.
- **[[Profit-Taking]]**: After the S&P 500 and [[Nasdaq Composite]] closed at all-time highs, traders sold shares to lock in gains from the tech-driven rally.

## Key Takeaways

- **Dow**: Fell about 1%, more than 500 points.
- **S&P 500**: Down 0.5–0.6%, a day after its first close above 7,800.
- **Nasdaq Composite**: Down 0.7%.
- **10-year yield**: Above 5.35%, the highest since April 2002.
- **30-year yield**: 5.725%, up more than 8 bps, the highest since May 2002.
- **Brent above $101**: WTI near $90 a barrel.
- **Supply threats**: Houthi attacks, Hormuz risk, and a possible Gulf hurricane.
- **Fed focus**: Was the September hike one-off or the start of more?
- **Market mechanics**: Profit-taking after record closes added to the selling.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Opportunity Cost]]**: A guaranteed Treasury return above 5.35% raises the bar stocks must clear. As the risk-free alternative pays more, equities become relatively less attractive.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did U.S. stocks fall on October 7, 2026, after hitting records?
**A**: A mix of 24-year-high Treasury yields, Brent crude above $101, anxiety ahead of the Fed's September minutes, and profit-taking after record closes pushed the major indexes lower.

**Q2**: How high did Treasury yields go, and why does that hurt stocks?
**A**: The 10-year topped 5.35% (highest since April 2002) and the 30-year hit 5.725%. Higher yields raise borrowing costs and make guaranteed bond returns more attractive than risky stocks.

**Q3**: What were traders looking for in the Fed's September minutes?
**A**: Clues to whether the September rate hike was a one-off adjustment or a signal of more hikes to come.
