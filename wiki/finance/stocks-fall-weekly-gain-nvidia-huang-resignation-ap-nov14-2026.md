## Summary

US stocks closed lower Friday as AI-focused companies fell, yet the S&P 500 still gained 2.5% for the week, its first winning week since October. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's resignation cost the company just under $600 billion in market cap this week, while hot wholesale inflation and 5.15% 10-year yields kept a December Fed hike expected.

## Core Concepts

- [[S&P 500]]: fell 0.4% to 7,561.67 on the day, up 2.5% for the week and 10.5% for the year.
- [[Nvidia]] and [[Jensen Huang]]: Huang resigned after an affair with a Chinese tech executive raised national security concerns; [[Colette Kress]] is interim CEO.
- [[AI Stocks]]: [[Broadcom]] fell 4.8% and [[AMD]] 8.8% after surging earlier in the week.
- [[Concentration Risk]]: a few mega-cap technology stocks drive an outsized share of market returns.
- [[Federal Reserve]]: expected to raise rates in December, with another hike likely in January.
- [[Producer Price Index]]: wholesale inflation cooled slightly but remains very high.
- [[Oil Prices]] and the [[Iran War]]: Brent settled at $95.84 versus about $72 in late February.
- [[US Treasury Yields]]: the 10-year rose to 5.15% and the two-year to 4.87%.

## Key Takeaways

- **Weekly Gain**: S&P 500 up 2.5%, its biggest weekly gain since August.
- **Index Closes**: Dow +0.1% to 52,347.55; Nasdaq -0.9% to 25,136.27.
- **Breadth Mismatch**: Over 60% of S&P 500 stocks rose, yet the index fell.
- **Nvidia Fallout**: Huang's exit cost Nvidia just under $600 billion in market value.
- **Rate Outlook**: Economists overwhelmingly expect hikes in December and January.
- **Inflation Spread**: Food and other costs rose even as energy costs eased.
- **Energy Driver**: War-driven energy costs raise gasoline and shipping costs across goods.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Concentrated indexes transmit single-company shocks to the whole market** — When a few mega-cap stocks hold an outsized share of index weight, their moves can override the direction of most constituents. · *model:* [[Concentration Risk]]
→ explains: Breadth Mismatch, Nvidia Fallout

**T2. Broadening inflation is harder for central banks to look through** — Once price pressure spreads from its original source into the wider economy, it can no longer be dismissed as temporary. · *model:* [[Second-Order Effects]]
→ explains: Inflation Spread, Rate Outlook, Energy Driver

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did the S&P 500 fall on Friday even though most of its stocks rose?
**A**: Slumping AI-focused technology stocks with large index weights, including Nvidia, Broadcom and AMD, outweighed gains in more than 60% of the index's constituents.

**Q2**: What did the latest wholesale inflation report show, and why does it matter?
**A**: It showed inflation cooling a bit but still very high, with food and other costs gaining ground despite easing energy costs. That suggests inflation is spreading into the broader economy, making it harder for the Fed to treat it as temporary.

**Q3**: What does the article imply for borrowers and growth?
**A**: A Fed hike in December, with another likely in January, can cool inflation but makes borrowing more expensive and can slow economic growth. The 10-year yield, which influences mortgage rates, already rose to 5.15%.
