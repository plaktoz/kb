## Summary

Adobe reported Q3 2026 earnings prioritizing user acquisition over paid conversion, with revenue of $6.76B (+13% YoY) but Q4 guidance that missed analyst expectations, sending shares down ~5%. The company's response to generative AI competition is [[Adobe Firefly]], integrated across Creative Cloud, paired with a freemium tier that reached 100M+ monthly active users. New CEO Anil Chakravarthy is executing a funnel strategy: acquire users for free, build habit, then convert to paid ARR over time.

## Core Concepts

- [[Adobe]] ([[ADBE]]) — Creative Cloud suite (Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere Pro) facing AI-driven disruption
- [[Adobe Firefly]] — Adobe's generative AI model integrated via text prompts into existing products
- [[Freemium Model]] — free tier offered to acquire users at scale before converting to paid subscriptions
- [[Annual Recurring Revenue]] (ARR) — $27.50B total; AI-first ARR grew +150% YoY
- [[Generative AI Competition]] — ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, and Gemini increasingly compete with Creative Cloud
- [[Anil Chakravarthy]] — incoming Adobe CEO driving the freemium-to-paid funnel strategy

## Key Takeaways

- **Revenue**: $6.76B in Q3 (+13% YoY); diluted EPS $4.62
- **ARR**: Total ARR $27.50B; AI-first ARR up +150% YoY
- **Freemium MAUs**: 100M+ monthly active users, up +70% YoY
- **Q4 Guidance**: $6.8B–$6.85B revenue midpoint — below consensus, triggering ~5% after-hours decline
- **AI Competition**: ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, Gemini all pressure Creative Cloud's core user base
- **Strategy**: Funnel model — free acquisition → habit formation → paid conversion; compared to early Facebook
- **Market Sentiment**: Stock likely range-bound until freemium-to-paid conversion rates improve

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Freemium Flywheel]]**: Adobe is betting that free users build habitual workflows inside Firefly, making paid upgrade the path of least resistance — the same lock-in dynamic that made Adobe's desktop tools sticky for decades.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Optimizing for MAU growth (now 100M+) risks becoming a vanity metric if conversion rates remain opaque; investors are already signaling they want ARR evidence, not user counts.
