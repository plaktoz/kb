## Summary

On October 6, 2026, the S&P 500 rallied for a fourth straight day to a new all-time high, its first since August 13, even as the 10-year Treasury yield topped 5.35%. [[Eddy Elfenbein]] argues that September's weak jobs report (+29,000) gives the [[Federal Reserve]] cover to hold rates this month without changing its plan, and a December hike is still likely. He then previews the Q3 big-bank earnings kickoff on October 13, where capital-markets revenue is the swing factor: trading is the upside risk and investment-banking fees are the downside risk.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Wall of Worry]]**: The market keeps rising through scary headlines. The latest worry is the bond market: the 10-year yield is up 70 bp in a month to above 5.35%. See [[10-year-treasury-yield-highest-since-2002-bond-selloff-oct2026]].
- **Market backdrop**: The S&P 500 is up 109% over four years. The [[S&P 500 Growth ETF]] (SPYG) is up 13.4% since July 30. High rates are keeping homeowners from moving or tapping record [[Home Equity]].
- **September [[Nonfarm Payrolls]]**: +29,000 versus an 84,000 forecast. Unemployment was 4.2%, and revisions to July and August totaled −60,000. See [[september-jobs-report-29000-payrolls-unemployment-4-2-percent]].
- **Unemployment streak**: The jobless rate has stayed below 5% for 61 straight months, the longest run since the 64 months from March 1965 to June 1970.
- **Job-growth slowdown**: The economy added 667,000 jobs over the last 18 months, versus 1.96 million over the 18 months before that.
- **Sector detail**: Healthcare +17k, construction +11k and manufacturing +9k. Government −17k, temp help −11k and information −10k (amid [[AI Job Displacement]] worries).
- **Fed path**: Traders put an 80% chance on a hold at the [[FOMC]] meeting in three weeks. Elfenbein reads the report as delaying the Fed, not changing its gameplan, and still expects a likely December hike.
- **[[JPMorgan Chase]] (JPM)**: Consensus is EPS of $5.90 on $51B revenue. Watch loan growth, consumer credit, [[Net Interest Income]] and [[Jamie Dimon]]'s commentary. [[Oppenheimer]] expects JPM to be the only large bank to beat.
- **[[Goldman Sachs]] (GS)**: Consensus is EPS of $13.50–$14.30 on $17B revenue. GS is "the purest read on capital markets" and has beaten estimates for four straight quarters. It is more exposed than universal banks to an advisory or underwriting miss.
- **[[Citigroup]] (C)**: The long-term cleanup continues. Guidance calls for a 2026 [[Return on Tangible Common Equity]] a bit above 11%, an efficiency ratio of about 60% or better, and NII ex-markets growth of 5–6% or more. Consensus is EPS of $2.65 on $23.7B revenue.
- **[[Wells Fargo]] (WFC)**: A core-banking story (NII, commercial loans, provisions). Consensus is EPS of $1.84 on $22.3B revenue, only modestly above the year-ago quarter.
- **[[Bank of America]] (BAC) and [[Morgan Stanley]] (MS)**: Both report Wednesday, along with [[BlackRock]]. BAC (EPS $1.11–$1.17, revenue $30.7–$31.2B) is the cleanest read on NII and credit. MS is Goldman's wealth-and-markets counterpart, and advisory is its swing factor.
- **Oppenheimer's cautious take**: Plain-vanilla banking is fine, but capital-markets revenue is tracking toward the low end. It cut its investment-banking revenue view 9.5% and raised trading about 6%, lowering EPS estimates for GS, MS, BAC and C.

## Key Takeaways

- **New high**: S&P 500 record on Oct 6, the first since Aug 13.
- **Rates**: 10-year yield above 5.35%, up 70 bp in one month.
- **Jobs miss**: +29k payrolls, 4.2% unemployment, wages +3% y/y (five-year low).
- **Fed**: 80% odds of an October hold, with a December hike still likely.
- **Bank kickoff**: JPM, GS, C and WFC on Oct 13, then BAC, MS and BLK on Oct 14.
- **Swing factor**: Trading is the upside risk and IB fees are the downside risk.
- **Oppenheimer**: Only JPM is favored to beat, and WFC is expected to match.
- **Credit watch**: Card, CRE or non-bank delinquencies would draw scrutiny.
- **Secondary focus**: Buyback pace at Citi and JPM, and 2026 NII or expense guidance.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Wall of Worry]]**: Elfenbein uses it directly. Stocks reached a record while bond yields surged and jobs data disappointed, because the worries were already priced in and fell short of the worst fears.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Elfenbein's read on the September jobs report's effect on Fed policy?
**A**: The weak +29,000 print gives the Fed political cover to hold rates this month. It only delays a hike that is still likely in December, without changing the Fed's gameplan.

**Q2**: According to the preview, what is the key upside and downside risk for Q3 big-bank earnings?
**A**: Markets/trading revenue is the upside risk, supported by AI-related financing and hedge-fund borrowing. Investment-banking fees are the downside risk, which is why Oppenheimer cut its IB revenue view 9.5%.

**Q3**: Why would a weak advisory quarter hurt Goldman Sachs more than JPMorgan?
**A**: Goldman is the purest read on capital markets, so a miss on advisory or equity underwriting hits it harder. Universal banks like JPMorgan are buffered by core lending and net interest income.
