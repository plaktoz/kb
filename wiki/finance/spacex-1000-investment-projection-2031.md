## Summary

[[SpaceX]] (NASDAQ: SPCX) debuted at $135, surged to $225, then fell over 50% to ~$150, with insider lock-up expirations still ahead. Management projects $1 trillion in annual revenue by 2030 — driven primarily by its AI compute-rental business — while Wall Street consensus is more conservative at ~$530 billion in AI-related revenue by 2031. Using CoreWeave and Oracle as valuation comparables (~6x sales), a $600–650 billion revenue scenario implies a $3.6 trillion valuation, translating a $1,000 investment today into roughly $1,770 by 2031 (~12% annually).

## Core Concepts

- **[[SpaceX]] (NASDAQ: SPCX)**: Newly public aerospace and AI compute company; shares down ~50% from post-IPO peak of $225, trading around $150.
- **[[AI Compute Rental]]**: SpaceX's core growth driver — renting data center capacity to firms like [[Anthropic]] and [[Alphabet]]; CFO Bret Johnsen reports a sub-one-year payback on new compute capital deployments.
- **[[Revenue Projection]]**: Management targets $1 trillion in annual revenue by 2030; Wall Street consensus is ~$530 billion in AI-related revenue by 2031, rising to $600–650 billion including [[Starlink]] and launch services.
- **[[Comparable Company Valuation]]**: [[CoreWeave]] and Oracle both trade ~6x trailing sales, providing the multiple used to project SpaceX's terminal valuation.
- **[[Insider Lock-Up]]**: Post-IPO lock-up expirations still pending; [[Elon Musk]] has pledged not to sell his stake, partially mitigating overhang risk.
- **[[Orbital Data Centers]]**: Emerging segment with significant uncertainty; potential upside but unproven at scale.

## Key Takeaways

- SpaceX IPO'd at $135, peaked at $225, now trades ~$150 post-selloff.
- Management projects $1T revenue by 2030; Wall Street sees $530B AI revenue by 2031.
- Sub-one-year payback on compute capital is exceptional vs. Alphabet's two-year average.
- 6x revenue multiple on $600B implies a $3.6T valuation — 77% above current levels.
- $1,000 invested today could reach ~$1,770 by 2031, or ~12% annualized.
- Author flags better risk-adjusted opportunities exist elsewhere.
- Key risk: whether AI compute supply shortage (which justifies premium pricing) persists.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Regression to the Mean]]**: SpaceX's management forecast of $1T revenue by 2030 (50x growth from $20B) requires sustained exponential expansion — base rates on such projections historically overestimate; the Wall Street consensus at roughly half that figure reflects mean-reversion skepticism.
- **[[Comparable Company Analysis]]**: Using CoreWeave and Oracle as valuation anchors assumes SpaceX's revenue quality and growth durability match peers — a critical assumption when the orbital data center segment remains unproven.
