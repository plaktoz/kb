## Summary

Singapore stocks edged lower on Monday, October 26, 2026, with the [[Straits Times Index]] (STI) down 0.1% to 5,415.91 amid mixed regional markets. [[Hongkong Land]] led gainers while [[Sembcorp Industries]] was the worst performer, and all three local banks ended lower. A deVere Group CEO said Brazil's election result is a preview of how much fiscal looseness bond markets will tolerate and urged diversification across regions and currencies.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Straits Times Index]] (STI)**: Lost 6.25 points (0.1%) to 5,415.91.
- **[[Hongkong Land]]**: Top STI gainer, up 2.2% to US$8.38.
- **[[Sembcorp Industries]]**: Worst STI performer, down 2.8% to S$5.84.
- **Singapore Banks**: [[DBS Group]] -0.3% to S$74.21, [[OCBC]] -0.2% to S$28.95, [[UOB]] -0.7% to S$41.30.
- **Market Breadth and Activity**: Gainers equalled losers at 252 each; 932.6 million securities worth S$1.4 billion changed hands. [[CapAllianz]] led by volume and DBS by value (S$174.8 million).
- **Regional Markets**: [[Hang Seng Index]] +0.5%, [[Nikkei 225]] -0.8%, [[Kospi]] -1.5%, [[FTSE Bursa Malaysia KLCI]] -0.5%.
- **[[Brazil]] election**: Incumbent [[Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva]] won a surprise victory; [[Nigel Green]] of [[deVere Group]] called it a live test of bond-market tolerance for fiscal looseness.

## Key Takeaways

- **STI Level**: Down 0.1% to 5,415.91 on Monday.
- **Top Gainer**: Hongkong Land rose 2.2% to US$8.38.
- **Laggard**: Sembcorp Industries fell 2.8% to S$5.84.
- **Banks Lower**: UOB fell most among local lenders at 0.7%.
- **Flat Breadth**: 252 gainers matched 252 losers.
- **Regional Split**: Hang Seng rose 0.5% while Kospi fell 1.5%.
- **Fiscal Test**: Brazil shows how much fiscal looseness bond markets will tolerate.
- **Currency Risk**: Currency swings can quietly erase gains made abroad.
- **Spread Exposure**: Diversifying across regions and currencies cushions repricing shocks.
