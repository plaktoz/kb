## Summary
Standard Chartered raised its global AI capex forecast after meetings with AI industry leaders — expecting 85% y/y growth to $750B in 2026, reaching $1tn in 2027 with a 33% CAGR through 2030 — while framing Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole speech as the key near-term signal for whether the Fed will help cap a bond market under fiscal-driven yield pressure.

## Core Concepts

- [[Global AI Capex Forecast]] — Standard Chartered's revised estimate: $750B in 2026 (+85% y/y), $1tn in 2027, 33% CAGR through 2030, based on stellar earnings and direct meetings with AI leaders in the US and Asia
- [[Bond Market Pain Threshold]] — the 30-year Treasury yield's rise to its highest since 2007, prompting the Treasury's surprise plan to at least double bond buybacks (from $2bn to $4bn per operation)
- [[Fed Reaction Function Uncertainty]] — the Warsh-led Fed's abandonment of forward guidance on rates as a driver of elevated yields, alongside rising US debt (just past $40tn) and heavy AI-hyperscaler bond issuance
- [[Financial Repression Dynamic]] — the bank's framing of Treasury buybacks plus a softer dollar as tools to manage debt costs, benefiting gold as the "pressure release valve"

## Key Takeaways

- **AI capex raised**: 2026 estimate lifted to $750B (from prior estimates), 2027 to $1tn, long-term CAGR nudged from 32% to 33% through 2030 — reinforcing a bullish stance on Big Tech and semiconductors.
- **Rate view**: expects the 10-year Treasury yield to stay rangebound at 4.50–4.75%, with any move above 5% likely temporary; prefers 3–5 year maturity bonds over reaching for long-dated debt.
- **Gold target**: reiterated bullish call with a target of $4,900/oz, benefiting from a softer USD as the Treasury manages yield pressure.
- **Macro backdrop**: softer payrolls, cooling inflation, and falling retail sales support the view that inflation peaked in Q2, favoring Fed rate holds through year-end and cuts starting 2027.
- **Weekly balance sheet**: net near-term risk-asset read was negative — positives (Treasury buybacks, US-Canada trade deal) offset by negatives (slowing consumer sentiment, weak China data, hawkish Fed minutes).
