## Summary

[[SpaceX]] (NASDAQ: SPCX) faces a cascading series of post-IPO lockup expirations from late September through December 2026 and a major one in June 2027, with each tranche releasing hundreds of millions of shares that could pressure the stock. Q2 2026 results beat expectations — 92% YoY revenue growth and a narrowing net loss — providing fundamental support against selling pressure. Historical context shows lockup expirations don't guarantee declines when business momentum is strong.

## Core Concepts

- [[Lockup Expiration]] — post-IPO restrictions preventing early investors from selling; SpaceX has staggered tranches releasing through 2027
- [[Share Dilution Pressure]] — large share unlocks increase the tradable float, introducing potential selling supply that can weigh on price
- [[IPO Price Anchor]] — SpaceX currently trades near its $150 IPO opening price (~$143), down ~34% from all-time high, making this a psychologically significant reference level
- [[Elon Musk]] — CEO whose ~48.4% ownership stake unlocks on June 12, 2027 (one year post-IPO), the single most consequential lockup event
- [[Starlink]] — SpaceX's satellite internet service, a core revenue driver alongside rocket operations and AI initiatives

## Key Takeaways

- **Sept. 24 unlock**: ~328.4 million shares eligible to trade; identical tranches follow on Oct. 9 and Oct. 24.
- **Post-Q3 earnings**: ~1.3 billion shares unlock, the largest near-term tranche.
- **Dec. 8**: ~800 million additional shares become tradable.
- **June 12, 2027**: Elon Musk's ~48.4% stake unlocks — the most consequential event by far.
- **Prior precedent**: Sept. 9 lockup coincided with a 3.9% single-day decline; stock remains ~34% off all-time high.
- **Q2 beat**: Revenue $7.81B vs. $6.93B estimate (+92% YoY); net loss narrowed to $541M from ~$1B prior year.
- **Key counterweight**: Strong fundamentals and demand can absorb unlock supply — not all expirations lead to sustained declines.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Supply and Demand]]**: Lockup expirations mechanically increase share supply; whether price falls depends on whether buyer demand expands proportionally — the outcome is not predetermined by the unlock itself.
- **[[Anchoring Bias]]**: Investors and analysts using the $150 IPO price or the all-time high as reference points may over- or under-weight the significance of current price levels when assessing unlock risk.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the most consequential single lockup event on SpaceX's horizon, and when does it occur?
**A**: Elon Musk's ~48.4% ownership stake becomes eligible for sale on June 12, 2027 — one year after the IPO — dwarfing all other unlock tranches in scale.

**Q2**: What were SpaceX's Q2 2026 financial results, and how did they compare to estimates?
**A**: Revenue came in at $7.81B versus an estimated $6.93B, representing 92% year-over-year growth, while net loss narrowed to $541M from approximately $1B in the prior year.

**Q3**: Do lockup expirations reliably cause stock price declines?
**A**: Not necessarily — the article notes that strong business results and market demand can offset selling pressure from newly eligible shares, though the Sept. 9 expiration did coincide with a 3.9% single-day drop.
