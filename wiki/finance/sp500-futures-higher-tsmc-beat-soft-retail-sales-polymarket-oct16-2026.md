## Summary

U.S. stock futures pointed to a moderately higher open on Oct. 16, 2026, with [[Polymarket]] traders assigning a 65% chance the [[S&P 500]] would open up, as investors weighed a blockbuster [[TSMC]] earnings beat, softer underlying consumer spending, and Treasury pressure on Iran. Analyst [[Louis Navellier]] argued Treasury yields fell despite a 1.3% September [[Producer Price Index|PPI]] surge because retail sales were inflated by gas-station prices, masking a slowing consumer. He expects third-quarter S&P 500 earnings to rise at least 33%, justifying current valuations.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Prediction Markets]]**: Polymarket's daily "S&P 500 Up or Down" contract priced a 65% chance of a higher open; the Oct. 15 contract resolved "Up" on $37,723 volume.
- **Index futures**: [[Nasdaq 100]] futures led (+0.20%), followed by [[Russell 2000]] (+0.22%), S&P 500 (+0.15%), and Dow (+0.11%).
- **Operation Economic Outcast**: Treasury Secretary [[Scott Bessent]] targeted an Iraqi bank accused of helping Iran access global finance, amid a U.S. naval blockade that reportedly drove Iran's seaborne crude exports to zero.
- **Oil prices**: [[Brent Crude]] dipped to $105.38 and [[WTI Crude]] to $97.39.
- **Retail sales composition**: A 6.4% gas-station jump inflated the headline; ex-vehicles and gas sales rose only 0.2%, and the GDP control group just 0.1%.
- **[[TSMC]] Q3 results**: Revenue up 51%, earnings up 69%, and raised 2026 revenue guidance.
- **Bank earnings day**: [[Travelers]], [[Truist Financial]], [[M&T Bank]], [[Citizens Financial Group]], and [[Regions Financial]] reported.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Headline figures can mask the underlying trend** — When a volatile component (such as gasoline prices) inflates an aggregate, markets read the stripped-down core measure as the true signal, so a hot headline can coexist with easing pressure. · *model:* [[Signal vs. Noise]]
→ explains: PPI Shock Ignored, Gas-Inflated Sales, Control Group Weak
