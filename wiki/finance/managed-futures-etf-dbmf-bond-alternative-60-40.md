## Summary

DBi's Andrew Beer argues bonds have been "dead money" for the decade and that [[Managed Futures]] ETFs, which can go long and short across global markets, hedge stocks better than bonds in volatile periods. His iMGP DBi Managed Futures Strategy ETF (DBMF) is up almost 19% this year. NovaDius's Nate Geraci counters that managed futures are a complement, not a replacement, and that investors should not abandon bonds.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Managed Futures]]**: A hedge fund strategy, historically unavailable to retail investors, now packaged in low-cost, liquid ETFs that replicate leading managed-futures funds.
- **[[60/40 Portfolio]]**: Beer sees an "existential crisis" in strategic asset allocation; he won't hold 40% in an asset that adds nothing to the rest of the portfolio.
- **Low correlation**: The goal is an asset with low correlation to both stocks and bonds that does well in crises.
- **Long and short flexibility**: ETFs can position early against turbulent markets, relevant as volatility rises on debt and AI-trade worries.
- **Counterview**: Geraci calls fixed income "the most challenging area in investing right now" but says alternatives belong in a diversified sleeve alongside stocks and bonds.
- **Underperformance risk**: These funds may lag for stretches and need a long horizon.

## Key Takeaways

- **Bond Skepticism**: Beer says bonds have delivered "dead money" this decade.
- **DBMF Performance**: Up almost 19% year to date as of Thursday's close.
- **Crisis Diversifier**: Managed futures aim to profit when stocks and bonds both struggle.
- **Not a Replacement**: Geraci says bonds still have a role.
- **Know the Interaction**: Investors should understand how alternatives behave alongside the rest of the portfolio.
- **Patience Required**: Outperformance in volatility comes with periods of lag.
