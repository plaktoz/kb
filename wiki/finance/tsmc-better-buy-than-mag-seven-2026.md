## Summary

The author argues that [[TSMC]] (TSM) is a superior investment to all seven [[Magnificent Seven]] stocks, having outperformed them with ~36% YTD gains and 78% over the past 12 months. TSMC's unique position as the foundry partner for every Magnificent Seven company — combined with record financials and technology leadership — makes it a compounding beneficiary of the AI boom rather than a single-company bet. The article highlights TSMC's $100B additional U.S. investment in Arizona and its early production of 2-nanometer chips as structural competitive advantages.

## Core Concepts

- **[[TSMC]] (Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company)**: The world's dominant contract chip manufacturer, producing chips for virtually every major AI and consumer tech company.
- **[[Magnificent Seven]]**: Apple, Nvidia, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, and Tesla — all of which rely on TSMC for chip manufacturing or foundry services.
- **[[2-Nanometer Chip Technology]]**: TSMC began producing 2nm chips in Q2 2026, offering higher transistor density and improved energy efficiency vs. prior generations.
- **[[Agentic AI]]**: CEO C.C. Wei cited agentic AI as a driver of surging CPU demand in data centers — benefiting TSMC regardless of which chip architecture (x86, Arm, RISC-V) wins.
- **[[Arizona Fab Expansion]]**: TSMC's total Arizona commitment reached $265B following an additional $100B investment, representing a major U.S. onshoring of semiconductor production.
- **[[Jensen Huang]]**: Nvidia CEO quoted describing TSMC's manufacturing as something that "you can't overstate the magic" of.

## Key Takeaways

- **Universal Supplier**: Every Mag Seven company depends on TSMC — it profits from all of them.
- **Q2 2026 Revenue**: $40.2B, up 33.7% YoY.
- **Gross Margin**: 67.7%; net profit margin of 55.6% — elite for a manufacturer.
- **2nm Production**: Started Q2 2026, ahead of rivals.
- **U.S. Expansion**: Total $265B Arizona commitment signals regulatory goodwill and supply chain security.
- **AI Architecture Agnostic**: TSMC benefits regardless of which chip design (x86, Arm, RISC-V) dominates AI data centers.
- **YTD Performance**: ~36% gain, outperforming all Magnificent Seven stocks over the same period.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Picks and Shovels Investing]]**: Rather than betting on which AI application or hyperscaler wins, owning TSMC is owning the essential infrastructure — every winner must go through them, so TSMC captures upside across the entire ecosystem without picking a single winner.
- **[[Monopoly Economics]]**: TSMC's manufacturing process lead (2nm now, with rivals 1–2 years behind) creates structural pricing power — customers have no alternative at the frontier, compressing price sensitivity and sustaining margins above 55%.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core argument for TSMC being a better buy than any Magnificent Seven stock?
**A**: TSMC is the chip manufacturer for all seven companies — meaning it captures upside from the entire group's AI-driven growth rather than being a single-company bet, while also outperforming each of them individually on a 12-month basis.

**Q2**: What specific financial metrics support TSMC's strength as of Q2 2026?
**A**: Revenue reached $40.2B (up 33.7% YoY), with a gross margin of 67.7% and a net profit margin of 55.6%, alongside the commencement of 2-nanometer chip production.

**Q3**: How does the agentic AI trend benefit TSMC regardless of which chip architecture wins?
**A**: CEO C.C. Wei noted that agentic AI drives higher CPU demand in data centers whether customers use x86, Arm, or RISC-V architectures — and since TSMC manufactures chips across all of these, it benefits from the overall compute buildout rather than any one platform's success.
