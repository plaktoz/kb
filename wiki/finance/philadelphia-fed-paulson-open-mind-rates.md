## Summary

Philadelphia Fed president [[Anna Paulson]] said she supported the Fed's decision to hold rates steady last week and is keeping an "open mind" about next steps, estimating underlying inflation at 2.4%–2.8% and stressing that persistence — not a single month's improvement — is what will determine future policy.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Federal Funds Rate]]**: Currently held at 3.5%–3.75% following a 9-3 FOMC vote, with three regional presidents (Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari, Dallas's Lorie Logan) dissenting in favor of a hike.
- **[[Underlying Inflation]]**: Paulson's preferred measure, stripping out temporary effects of tariffs and energy shocks, currently estimated at 2.4%–2.8% versus the Fed's 2% target.
- **Two-Scenario Framing**: Either current policy is mildly restrictive and will bring inflation to target in an acceptable time frame, or it isn't restrictive enough given inflation has been elevated for over five years.
- **[[Core PCE]]**: The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, at 3.3% year-over-year in June, down a tenth of a point from May.

## Key Takeaways

- Paulson: "The recent improvement in some inflation data is welcome... it is a step in the right direction, but it is only one step."
- Anecdotal evidence cited: a large consumer-goods CEO is holding prices fixed despite rising costs because shoppers are price-sensitive; small businesses, especially those serving real estate, report growing challenges.
- AI build-out is creating some price pressure, which Paulson expects to stay contained "if policy is appropriately calibrated."
- The Fed's 9-3 vote to hold, with three dissents favoring a hike, shows real internal disagreement about whether policy is currently restrictive enough.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Paulson's core claim about the Fed's current stance?
**A**: She supports the recent decision to hold rates but hasn't committed to a future direction, saying she's watching whether inflation improvement is a sustained trend or a one-off before deciding on next steps.

**Q2**: What specific inflation figure is Paulson using to judge policy, and what does it show?
**A**: Underlying inflation (stripping out tariffs and energy shocks), which she estimates at 2.4%–2.8% — still above the Fed's 2% target despite recent improvement.

**Q3**: What would it mean for markets if Paulson's "not restrictive enough" scenario turns out to be correct?
**A**: It would suggest the Fed needs to hold rates higher for longer or even hike further to bring inflation to target, which would be a more hawkish outcome than markets pricing in near-term cuts might expect.
