## Summary

The Oct. 7 [[FOMC Minutes]] show Chair [[Kevin Warsh]]'s Fed planning another hike in 2026, with meetings left on Oct. 28 and Dec. 9, as [[Consumer Price Index|CPI]] runs at 3.7% year over year. The author argues higher rates are usually a headwind for stocks, but Vanguard research suggests short-run damage is limited unless rates rise about a percentage point more. Higher [[Treasury Yields]] and lower bond prices may make bonds a more attractive "buy the dip" alternative.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Kevin Warsh]] and the [[FOMC]]**: Planning another quarter- or half-point hike after the September increase.
- **[[Federal Funds Rate]]**: The Fed controls only the short-term rate, but longer-term bond yields tend to rise with it.
- **[[Consumer Price Index]]**: September 2026 CPI rose 3.7% year over year.
- **Rate-hike transmission**: Costlier borrowing deters car purchases, mortgages, credit cards, factories, and hiring.
- **2022 precedent**: S&P 500 fell about 19% and Nasdaq-100 about 33% during aggressive hiking.
- **Bond yield-price relationship**: When [[Treasury Yields]] rise, bond prices fall, creating an entry point for buyers.
- **Index funds**: [[Vanguard S&P 500 ETF]] (VOO) for stocks and [[Vanguard Total Bond Market ETF]] (BND) for bonds.

## Key Takeaways

- **Next Hikes**: Minutes point to another 0.25 or 0.50 point hike by year-end.
- **Inflation**: September CPI hit 3.7% year over year.
- **Stock Headwind**: Higher borrowing costs can slow spending, hiring, and investment, pressuring stock prices.
- **2022 Benchmark**: Last aggressive hiking cycle cut S&P 500 about 19%.
- **Vanguard View**: Stocks may not suffer until rates rise roughly a point more.
- **Bond Case**: Yields at 5% or higher make locking in safe returns appealing.
- **Advice**: Long-term investors can keep buying low-cost index funds, cautiously.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Tightening transmits through borrowing costs** — Raising the policy rate makes credit costlier, which suppresses household and business spending and thereby inflation, at the short-run expense of asset prices. → explains: Stock Headwind, 2022 Benchmark

**T2. Yields and bond prices move inversely** — When yields rise, existing bond prices fall, so the return on new purchases rises. → explains: Bond Case

**T3. Safe yields compete with risk assets** — When risk-free yields reach 5% or more, investors weigh locking in that yield against holding stocks. · *model:* [[Opportunity Cost]]
→ explains: Bond Case

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about Fed hikes and markets?
**A**: Another hike is likely this year, and while higher rates are usually bad for stocks, the likely small adjustments should not trigger a 2022-style crash. Bonds may become more attractive as yields rise.

**Q2**: What evidence does the article give on how hikes affect stocks?
**A**: In 2022's aggressive hiking, the S&P 500 fell about 19% and Nasdaq-100 about 33%. Vanguard research suggests rates may need to rise at least a percentage point more before stocks suffer.

**Q3**: How might an investor apply this?
**A**: Long-term investors can keep buying a low-cost index fund like VOO, while those drawn to 5%+ safe yields can use a bond fund like BND.
