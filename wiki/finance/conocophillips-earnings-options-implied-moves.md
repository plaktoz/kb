## Summary
Ahead of August 6 earnings, the options market priced implied post-earnings moves for four companies — [[ConocoPhillips]], Howmet Aerospace, [[Cloudflare]], and [[Datadog]] — ranging from 3.47% to 13.31%, illustrating how options pricing signals expected volatility independent of a stock's fundamentals.

## Core Concepts
- **Implied move**: The size of price swing options markets are pricing in around an earnings report, derived from options premiums rather than analyst estimates.
- **[[ConocoPhillips]]**: $142B energy company with the smallest implied move (3.47%) on this list but still $4.94B of market value at stake given its size.
- **Options-market risk sizing**: Comparing dollar value at stake (market cap × implied move %) shows how the same percentage move means very different absolute risk across companies.

## Key Takeaways
- **ConocoPhillips**: 3.47% implied move, $4.94B at stake; shares up 22% YTD heading into the print.
- **Howmet Aerospace**: 6.74% implied move, $7.79B at stake; shares up 36.1% YTD, near 52-week highs.
- **Cloudflare**: 11.78% implied move, $12.7B at stake; shares up 53.7% YTD.
- **Datadog**: widest implied move at 13.31%, $13.9B at stake, after a 115.4% YTD run — the biggest run-up correlating with the largest expected swing.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is an options-market "implied move" and what does it measure?
**A**: The size of price swing the options market is pricing in for a stock around an event like earnings, derived from options premiums.

**Q2**: Which stock had the largest implied move and why might that be?
**A**: Datadog, at 13.31%, following a 115.4% year-to-date run — stocks with the biggest recent rallies tend to carry the highest expectations (and risk) into earnings.

**Q3**: Why is dollar value at stake a more useful comparison than percentage move alone?
**A**: A smaller percentage move on a larger company (like ConocoPhillips' 3.47%) can represent a similar or larger absolute dollar swing than a bigger percentage move on a smaller company, so market cap needs to be factored in to compare real risk.
