## Summary

The benchmark 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.304% on Wednesday, its highest intraday level since May 2002, extending a bond selloff despite cooler-than-expected PCE core inflation. The 30-year yield climbed to 5.651%, also near 24-year highs, with Friday's employment report seen as the next catalyst for an October Fed rate decision.

## Core Concepts

- **[[10-Year Treasury Yield]]**: Reached 5.304% intraday Wednesday, the highest level since May 2002, extending the ongoing bond selloff.
- **[[30-Year Treasury Yield]]**: Climbed to 5.651%, hovering near its own 24-year high alongside the 10-year.
- **[[PCE Inflation]]**: Core PCE rose 3% over the 12 months through August, down from 3.3% the prior month and below the 3.3% economists expected — yields fell briefly on the data before recovering.
- **[[Federal Reserve Rate Hike]]**: Markets are weighing the odds of an October rate increase, with Friday's employment data expected to be a key input.
- **[[Commerce Department]]**: Published the PCE inflation data that briefly moved yields lower.

## Key Takeaways

- **Fresh milestone**: 10-year yield hit 5.304%, highest since May 2002.
- **30-year also elevated**: Reached 5.651%, near a 24-year high.
- **Inflation surprise**: Core PCE cooled to 3% from 3.3%, below the 3.3% forecast.
- **Reversal pattern**: Yields dipped on the cooler data, then recovered and extended the longer-end rise.
- **Next catalyst**: Friday's employment report is likely to sway October rate-hike odds.
