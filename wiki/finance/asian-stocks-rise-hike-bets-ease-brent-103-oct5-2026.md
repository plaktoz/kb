## Summary

Asian stocks were set to open higher on Monday, October 5, after softer US September jobs data eased pressure on the [[Federal Reserve]] to keep raising rates. Money markets now price less than a 25% chance of an October hike. [[Brent Crude]] rose above $103 a barrel as Yemen moved to recapture Houthi-held areas, even though [[OPEC+]] kept production quotas unchanged. Bonds stayed under pressure because the months-long [[Treasury]] rout, which pushed 10-year yields to their highest since 2002, has not reversed.

## Core Concepts

- **Asia open**: Equity-index futures for [[Japan]], [[South Korea]] and [[Taiwan]] pointed to gains. [[Nasdaq 100]] futures rose 0.2% after the cash index closed at a record on Friday, and [[S&P 500]] futures edged higher.
- **[[Nonfarm Payrolls]]**: Rose 29,000 in September after downward revisions to the prior two months, missing every estimate in Bloomberg's survey. Unemployment rose to 4.2%, partly because the workforce grew.
- **[[Goldilocks Economic Backdrop]]**: [[Shane Oliver]] of [[AMP Ltd]] called the report "not too hot, not too cold" data that supports the view that the Fed won't rush into another hike.
- **Oil**: December Brent rose above $103 after gaining almost 5% the previous week. The move was driven by Yemen's offensive against [[Houthi]]-controlled areas, despite OPEC+ leaving quotas unchanged.
- **[[Gold]]**: Little changed after its biggest weekly loss since June.
- **[[Bond Market]] rout**: Worries about persistent inflation, government spending and corporate borrowing for the [[AI Buildout]] keep yields high. The jobs data brought only a brief Treasury rally.
- **Week ahead**: Auctions of 10- and 30-year Treasuries will test demand for long bonds, and [[FOMC]] minutes from September are due Wednesday.
- **Global risks**: Investors are watching for contagion in Europe's government bond market, where a selloff revived memories of the [[European Debt Crisis]]. Brazilian assets are in focus as votes from Sunday's presidential election are counted.

## Key Takeaways

- **Hike odds**: Markets price less than a 25% chance of an October Fed hike.
- **Payrolls miss**: +29,000 in September, below every Bloomberg survey estimate.
- **Unemployment**: Rose to 4.2%, partly because the workforce grew.
- **Brent**: Above $103 despite OPEC+ leaving quotas unchanged.
- **Nasdaq 100**: Closed at a record Friday, and futures were up 0.2%.
- **Yields**: 10-year Treasury yields hit their highest since 2002 last week.
- **Catalysts**: Long-bond auctions, Fed minutes on Wednesday, European bond contagion, Brazil's election.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Bad News Is Good News]]**: A weak jobs report lifted Wall Street and Asian futures because it cuts the odds of another Fed hike. While policy tightening is the main constraint on markets, softer economic data can push equities up.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why were Asian stocks set to rise on October 5, 2026?
**A**: Softer US September jobs data eased pressure on the Fed to keep raising rates. Money markets priced less than a 25% chance of an October hike, building on Friday's Wall Street gains.

**Q2**: What did the September US jobs report show?
**A**: Nonfarm payrolls rose only 29,000 after downward revisions to the prior two months, missing all Bloomberg survey estimates. Unemployment rose to 4.2%, partly because the workforce grew, and wage growth slowed.

**Q3**: Why did bonds stay under pressure despite the dovish jobs data?
**A**: The Treasury market is still in a months-long rout driven by persistent inflation, government spending and AI-related corporate borrowing, which pushed 10-year yields to their highest since 2002. This week's 10- and 30-year auctions will test demand for long-maturity debt.
