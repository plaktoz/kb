## Summary
While Anthropic's IPO drew investors in, the rest of tech was demolished, with Nvidia down 23% over five days for its worst week since October 2008. Sozzi calls it a perfect storm of political, leadership, China, technical and macro pressures, and former Bridgewater strategist Rebecca Patterson calls it a rational repricing on new facts.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Anthropic IPO]]**: Stormed onto public markets, becoming a nearly $2 trillion company even as other AI names fell.
- **[[Nvidia]] and [[Jensen Huang]]**: Loss of the founder; interim CEO [[Colette Kress]] is not seen as the answer.
- **[[AI Stock Sell-Off]]**: Nasdaq fell more than 2% Thursday; AMD and chip stocks were hit.
- **[[AI Data Center Spending]]** and regulation: A Democratic Congress may slow data center construction.
- **[[Semiconductor Export Controls]]**: China regulatory fears and a short-seller report on [[AMD]].
- **[[Federal Reserve]] and inflation**: Sticky inflation and hawkish rhetoric tighten financial conditions.
- **[[Rebecca Patterson]]**: Argues for a rational repricing and prefers infrastructure over buying the dip.

## Key Takeaways
- **Nvidia slide**: Down 23% in five days, fell to fourth-largest company.
- **Perfect storm**: Politics, CEO exit, China, technical selling, sticky inflation, hawkish Fed.
- **Technical selling**: Market-clearing events are forcing investors to raise cash.
- **Disconnect**: Anthropic is near $2 trillion while the rest of AI is repriced.
- **Willingness to pay**: Investors still want AI but will not pay any price.
- **Patterson's view**: Lower valuations make sense given new facts.
- **Positioning**: She prefers infrastructure and inflation-protected sectors; AI buyers should average in later.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Prices adjust when new facts change expected outcomes** — When new information lowers expected returns or raises risk, asset prices reset to a lower valuation, even if the underlying theme stays attractive.
