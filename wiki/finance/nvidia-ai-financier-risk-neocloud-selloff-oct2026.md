## Summary

[[Nvidia]] has evolved from chipmaker to AI financier, with $36 billion in neocloud backstops, a guarantee of up to $105 billion on OpenAI's Ohio data center, and over $500 billion in off-balance-sheet commitments. Neocloud stocks ([[CoreWeave]], [[Nebius]], IREN) are down at least 30% this month and CoreWeave's CDS spread is above 11%, delivering the first major stress test of that strategy. Nvidia's $70 billion half-year free cash flow and credit rating are its edge, but analysts and Moody's are closely watching the exposure.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nvidia]]**: Moved from selling chips to funding model developers, data centers, and neoclouds; Jensen Huang calls its platform "an investable asset."
- **[[Neoclouds]]**: Cash-burning GPU cloud providers (CoreWeave, Nebius, IREN, Firmus, Nscale) that depend on Nvidia backing.
- **[[Backstop Guarantees]]**: Resale-price floors, offtake commitments, and lease guarantees that pay out only on default.
- **[[OpenAI]]**: Pike County, Ohio 10 GW data center; Nvidia guarantees up to $105 billion over a 20-year hardware requirement.
- **[[Off-Balance-Sheet Risk]]**: Over $153 billion NPV of Nvidia off-balance-sheet liabilities per New Constructs; Moody's does not count guarantees as debt.
- **[[Credit Default Swaps]]**: CoreWeave spreads above 11% show insurance against default getting costlier.
- **[[Jensen Huang]]**: Says neocloud demand is "off the charts" and capital is scarce.

## Key Takeaways

- **Backstop Scale**: $36B in neocloud backstops plus up to $105B for OpenAI's Ohio site.
- **Market Stress**: CoreWeave, Nebius, IREN down 30%+ in October; CoreWeave CDS above 11%.
- **Nvidia Stock**: Third straight down week; down 7.6% in October.
- **Cash Edge**: $70B free cash flow in H1 versus AMD's roughly $4B.
- **Commitments**: Over $500B off-balance-sheet commitments, mostly maturing through fiscal 2029.
- **Asset-Class Push**: $500B financing effort with six asset managers, including $125B residual value guarantees.
- **Rating Watch**: Moody's calls guarantees the fastest-growing off-balance-sheet type and is closely monitoring exposure.
- **Insurers**: Nvidia seeks insurers for GPU value risk, but they are "not yet a complete panacea."

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Guarantees hide contingent liabilities until the trigger event** — A backstop costs nothing while borrowers perform, so exposure can grow large off the balance sheet and only surface when partners default. · *model:* [[Off-Balance-Sheet Risk]]
→ explains: Backstop Scale, Commitments, Rating Watch

**T2. A supplier that finances its customers ties its own risk to theirs** — When the vendor funds demand for its own product, customer distress and the vendor's losses become correlated, so strength in the vendor's balance sheet is tested exactly when customers stumble. · *model:* [[Vendor Financing]]
→ explains: Market Stress, Cash Edge, Nvidia Stock

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central risk the article identifies in Nvidia's strategy?
**A**: By financing and backstopping AI customers, Nvidia takes on risk tied to the AI buildout, and neocloud stock drops and CDS spikes are testing it. If demand for compute disappoints, those guarantees could be called.

**Q2**: What concrete figures show the size of Nvidia's exposure?
**A**: Nvidia had $36B in neocloud backstops, a guarantee of up to $105B for OpenAI's Ohio data center, and over $500B in off-balance-sheet commitments. New Constructs puts the NPV of off-balance-sheet liabilities at over $153B.

**Q3**: What offsets the risk, and who is watching it?
**A**: Nvidia has $70B in H1 free cash flow, an investment-grade rating, and is courting insurers. Moody's says it monitors exposure constantly but expects chips to hold values above contractual residual amounts near-term.
