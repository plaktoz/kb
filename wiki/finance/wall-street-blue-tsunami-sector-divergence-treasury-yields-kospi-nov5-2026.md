## Summary

After Democrats' sweep of Congress in the U.S. midterms, U.S. stocks rallied sharply (Dow +3.03%) while Wall Street banks projected medium- to long-term gains but divergent sector outcomes, with AI data center regulation the main risk. The KOSPI fell and triggered a sell-sidecar on fears of slower AI infrastructure investment. Treasury yield direction stays uncertain, and the Iran war is the key oil variable.

## Core Concepts

- [[2026 US Midterm Elections]]: Democrats ("blue tsunami") take both chambers of Congress.
- [[AI Infrastructure Investment Cycle]]: Wall Street expects regulation to delay some projects but not reverse the cycle.
- [[Data Center Regulation]]: concerns over electricity and water burdens; Texas governor-elect [[Gina Hinojosa]] pledged to suspend new approvals.
- [[Morgan Stanley]]: overweight global equities; cites post-midterm gains since 1982.
- [[Standard Chartered]]: forecasts 8-10% annual returns and a 12-month DXY target of 98.5.
- [[JPMorgan Asset Management]]: expects AI boom to continue with higher costs and more concentration.
- [[KOSPI]]: opened down 1.82% at 5,580.09, triggering a sell-sidecar.
- [[Sector Divergence]]: healthcare and consumer sectors may benefit from delayed Medicaid and SNAP cuts.
- [[Treasury Yields]]: divided government limits fiscal stimulus; slower growth and easing inflation cap yields.
- [[Iran War]] and [[Oil Prices]]: the biggest commodity variable.

## Key Takeaways

- **Index Moves**: Dow +3.03% (best in about 19 months), S&P 500 +1.58%, Nasdaq +1.44%.
- **Historical Pattern**: Large caps rose 15-16% in six months after midterms since 1982.
- **KOSPI Reaction**: Sell-sidecar triggered on fears of slower AI data center buildout.
- **AI Outlook**: Boom continues, but costs rise, growth slows, concentration intensifies.
- **Beneficiaries**: Healthcare and consumer sectors may gain if Medicaid and SNAP cuts ease.
- **Yield Uncertainty**: Divided government makes large stimulus unlikely; long-term yield decline unclear.
- **Inflation Path**: JPMorgan sees inflation peaking this year-end, near 2% by May.
- **Policy Risks**: Budget passage and the debt ceiling are key uncertainties over two years.
- **Trump Veto**: Policy changes remain uncertain because Trump could veto them.
