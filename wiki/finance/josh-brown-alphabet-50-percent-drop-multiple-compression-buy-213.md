## Summary

[[Josh Brown]] argued on The Compound and Friends that [[Alphabet]] stock "could go down 50%" if [[Google Search]] share slips 2%, and that he would buy at $213 to $215. The article shows a 2% Search hit costs only about 1% of revenue, so any 50% drop would have to come from [[Multiple Compression]]. The author finds $215, about 15x forward earnings, too low to wait for, but would take Brown more seriously if Search growth falls to single digits.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Josh Brown]]** — Ritholtz Wealth Management CEO who made the 50% downside warning
- **[[Alphabet]]** — shares fell 13% after the Oct. 28 Q3 report; Search & other was 55.7% of 2025 revenue
- **[[Google Search]]** — Search & other growth slowed from 19% in Q1 to 12% in Q3 as [[AI Mode]] earns less per query
- **[[ChatGPT]]** — handles roughly a fifth as many search-like queries as Google, by OpenAI's own count
- **[[Multiple Compression]]** — the mechanism by which a 50% fall would occur, from about 20x to 10x forward earnings
- **[[Google Cloud]]** — revenue grew 103% in Q3, with a $560.8 billion backlog, a second growth engine
- **[[Forward P/E]]** — about 20x now, vs. a 22x five-year average and a 16x low

## Key Takeaways

- **Brown's Claim**: A 2% Search share slip could send the stock down 50%.
- **The Math**: A 2% Search hit is about $4.5 billion, roughly 1% of revenue.
- **Multiple Mechanism**: A 50% drop needs the forward P/E to fall from 20x to 10x.
- **Growth Slowdown**: Search & other growth fell from 19% to 12% across three quarters.
- **Second Engine**: Google Cloud grew 103% with a $560.8 billion backlog.
- **Cash Strain**: Q3 capex was $51.4 billion and free cash flow was negative $9.1 billion.
- **Buy Level**: Brown's $213-$215 equals about 15x, below the 16x tariff-selloff low.
- **Author's Trigger**: Single-digit Search growth in February would make the bear case credible.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Price equals earnings times a multiple, and the multiple tracks perceived growth** — Investors pay up for a franchise they believe keeps growing and stop paying up once they think it is shrinking, even if it stays dominant. A large price drop can therefore occur without a matching earnings loss.
→ explains: The Math, Multiple Mechanism, Growth Slowdown

**T2. Diversified earnings sources reduce dependence on any single threatened franchise** — Revenue from a second engine does not depend on the outcome of the threat to the core business. · *model:* [[Diversification]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Josh Brown's claim about Alphabet, and how does the article assess it?
**A**: Brown says a 2% slip in Google's search share could cut the stock 50%. The article finds the revenue impact is only about 1%, so the drop would have to come from investors paying a much lower multiple.

**Q2**: What numbers show how large a multiple change a 50% drop requires?
**A**: Forward earnings multiple would have to fall from about 20x to about 10x, far below the five-year low of 16x. Brown's $215 buy price implies about 15x.

**Q3**: What would change the author's mind about the risk?
**A**: If Search & other growth drops into single digits when Alphabet reports in early February, it would look like more than a trade of revenue per query for usage, and the author would take the 50% warning far more seriously.
