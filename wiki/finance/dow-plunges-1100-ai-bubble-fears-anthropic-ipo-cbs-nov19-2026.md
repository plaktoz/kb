## Summary
The Dow fell 1,101 points (2.2%) to 49,097 on Thursday as fears grew that the AI stock boom is deflating, with no single catalyst identified. More than 90% of S&P 500 stocks declined, and AI names reversed morning gains into afternoon losses. Anthropic still had a strong market debut after the largest IPO in history.

## Core Concepts
- [[Dow Jones Industrial Average]]: closed down 1,101 points, or 2.2%, at 49,097.
- [[S&P 500]] and [[Nasdaq Composite]]: fell 2% and 2.3%, with more than 90% of S&P 500 stocks lower.
- [[AI Bubble Concerns]]: investors question whether data-center spending will deliver productivity and profit growth.
- [[Nvidia]]: stock flipped from a 4.7% gain to a 2.7% loss after [[Jensen Huang]]'s resignation and tighter chip [[Export Controls]].
- [[Anthropic IPO]]: shares rose as much as 34% in the biggest IPO ever.
- [[Market Sentiment]]: analysts say rallies bring out sellers rather than buyers.

## Key Takeaways
- **Broad Selloff**: Over 90% of S&P 500 stocks fell; no single catalyst cited.
- **AI Reversals**: Micron swung from +7.8% to -1.8%; CoreWeave from +10.5% to -5.5%.
- **Nvidia Overhang**: Huang's resignation and tighter export controls on AI chips weigh on sentiment.
- **Anthropic Debut**: Shares jumped up to 34% despite the weak market.
- **Non-Tech Weakness**: Williams-Sonoma fell 15.3% on a slight revenue miss; Walmart fell 1.2% despite a beat.
- **Sentiment**: Analyst notes hesitation on AI picks-and-shovels and stretched defensive stocks.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What drove Thursday's market plunge, according to analysts?
**A**: Analysts said no single catalyst accounted for it. They pointed to ongoing concerns about how durable markets are that lean heavily on AI's financial prospects.

**Q2**: What evidence showed the selloff was broad and that AI names reversed?
**A**: More than 90% of S&P 500 stocks fell. Micron, CoreWeave and Nvidia all flipped from morning gains to afternoon losses.

**Q3**: What does the Anthropic IPO debut imply against this backdrop?
**A**: Demand for AI exposure persisted in the largest IPO ever, with shares up as much as 34%, even as broader AI-linked stocks sold off.
