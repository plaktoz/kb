## Summary

[[IBM]] completed a ~CAD 997M fixed-income offering of 4.750% notes due 2034, giving fresh insight into its funding costs. The most-followed valuation narrative pegs IBM as ~7.7% undervalued ($256.08 fair value vs. $236.31 close), framing it as a defensive-growth AI/hybrid-cloud platform company, though its P/E already trades above IT-industry and peer averages.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Bond Offering]]**: A CAD ~$997M issuance of 4.750% notes due August 15, 2034 — a funding-cost signal distinct from equity price action.
- **[[Valuation Narrative]]**: A crowd-sourced fair-value framework (via Simply Wall St) anchoring IBM's fair value at $256.08 using a 9.55% discount rate, based on improving software/AI mix and durable cash flows.
- **[[P/E Ratio vs. Peers]]**: IBM's 20.8x P/E sits above the US IT industry average (18.7x) and peer average (13.8x), though still below a modeled "fair" ratio of 35.3x for its business mix.

## Key Takeaways

- IBM shares traded at $236.31, up 4.42% over 7 days but down 17.82% over 30 days — a sharp recent pullback.
- 5-year total shareholder return stands at 108.63%, reflecting stronger long-term compounding despite short-term volatility.
- The bullish narrative depends on continued AI/hybrid-cloud adoption and higher-margin software growth; it would be undermined if either slows.
- Market-multiple pricing (P/E) already reflects a fair amount of "quality" premium versus peers, leaving less obvious multiple-expansion upside than the fair-value narrative alone suggests.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: IBM's recent ~$1B bond offering provides a funding-cost data point, and the stock is framed by a popular valuation narrative as roughly 7.7% undervalued based on its transition into a software- and AI-led hybrid cloud platform business.

**Q2**: What would invalidate the "undervalued" narrative discussed?
**A**: If AI and hybrid cloud adoption slows, or if IBM's higher-margin software growth falls short of the narrative's assumptions, the $256.08 fair-value anchor would no longer be supported.

**Q3**: How should the P/E-multiple comparison temper the fair-value narrative?
**A**: Since IBM's current P/E (20.8x) already trades above both its industry and peer averages, much of the "AI transition" story may already be priced in — meaning the stock isn't cheap on a relative basis even if a discounted-cash-flow-style narrative sees room to a higher fair value.
