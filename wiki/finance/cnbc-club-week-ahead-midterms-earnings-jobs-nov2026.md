## Summary

CNBC Investing Club previews the week of Nov. 2, 2026 after the busiest week of Q3 earnings season ended on a sour note. Three things matter: Tuesday's midterm elections (AI buildout and Iran implications), earnings from six Club holdings (Apple, Eaton, DuPont, Qnity, Cardinal Health, Kimberly-Clark), and Friday's October jobs report, which shapes expectations for a possible Fed rate hike.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Midterm Elections 2026]]**: Control of the House and Senate; big wins for anti-data-center politicians would threaten the [[AI Buildout Trade]], while the opposite outcome could spark a relief rally.
- **[[Iran]] tensions**: [[Donald Trump]] is waiting until after the midterms before ordering new military action; escalation could hurt oil prices, bond yields, and inflation expectations.
- **[[Apple]]**: First call under CEO [[John Ternus]]; focus on demand versus a PC-market slowdown flagged by [[Intel]], gross margins under rising memory costs, and [[Services Business]] resilience. Expected: revenue $113.4B, EPS $1.99.
- **[[Eaton]]**: Data center demand is intact per Google Cloud, Microsoft, and Amazon results; watch manufacturing capacity, [[Boyd Thermal]] integration, mobility divestiture, and orders. Expected: revenue $8.5B, EPS $3.54.
- **[[DuPont]]**: Organic growth, Middle East water-business disruption from the Iran war, and a 30-basis-point oil and gas inflation margin headwind. Expected: revenue $1.84B, EPS $1.87.
- **[[Qnity Electronics]]**: Shares down almost 20% since August; Intel's PC slowdown and organic substrate shortage raise questions about demand. Expected: revenue $1.49B, EPS $1.20.
- **[[Cardinal Health]]**: Near all-time highs; watch the [[CVS Health]] distribution deal economics (renewed through 2032), generics, and higher-margin growth areas. Expected: revenue $66.6B, EPS $2.94.
- **[[Kimberly-Clark]]**: Already preannounced results Oct. 15; watch the pending [[Kenvue]] acquisition, cost inflation, China diaper sales, and professional business. Expected: revenue $4.13B, EPS $1.64.
- **[[Nonfarm Payrolls]] and the [[Federal Reserve]]**: Friday's report is expected to show 75,000 jobs and 4.2% unemployment; inflation is well above the 2% target and the [[CME FedWatch Tool]] puts a December quarter-point hike at just under 75%.
- **Other data**: [[JOLTS]], ADP private payrolls, ISM manufacturing and services PMIs, and factory orders.

## Key Takeaways

- **Earnings Week Mood**: Busiest earnings week ended sour; the market needs wins.
- **Election Risk**: Anti-data-center victories threaten the AI buildout trade; the opposite brings relief.
- **Iran Overhang**: Trump delaying military action until after midterms; escalation risks oil, yields, inflation.
- **Apple Outlook**: Forward guidance, margins, and Services matter most for the stock reaction.
- **Memory Cost Squeeze**: Apple raised prices on hardware; margins still at risk if not fully passed through.
- **Orders vs Sales**: Eaton orders reflect current demand; sales reflect past demand.
- **Jobs Data**: Payrolls forecast at 75,000; unemployment held at 4.2%.
- **Fed Hike Odds**: December quarter-point hike priced just under 75%.
- **Least Important Data**: September JOLTS is a month old, so least consequential.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What are the three big things the article says to watch in the week of Nov. 2, 2026?
**A**: The Tuesday midterm elections, earnings from six Club holdings (Apple, Eaton, DuPont, Qnity, Cardinal Health, Kimberly-Clark), and the labor-market data culminating in Friday's October nonfarm payrolls report.

**Q2**: Why does the jobs report matter so much for the Fed, and what are the expectations?
**A**: Inflation is well above the Fed's 2% target, so unemployment must stay low to give the Fed room to curtail inflation. Economists expect 75,000 payroll additions and 4.2% unemployment, with just under 75% odds of a December quarter-point hike.

**Q3**: How should an investor read Eaton's results beyond reported sales?
**A**: Focus on order growth, since orders reflect current demand while sales reflect past demand that has already been fulfilled, and investors look toward the future.
