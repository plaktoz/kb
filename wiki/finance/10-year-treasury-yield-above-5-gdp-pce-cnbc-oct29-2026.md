## Summary

The 10-year Treasury yield hovered just above 5% on Thursday after Q3 GDP grew 3.4% annualized, beating the 3.1% forecast, and September PCE inflation accelerated to 3.6% year over year. The data followed the Fed's October hold at 3.75%-4%, and markets price an 83% chance of a December quarter-point hike. Attention turns to Friday, when Fed speakers' post-meeting blackout lifts.

## Core Concepts

- [[10-Year Treasury Yield]]: down 3 basis points at 5.011%; a benchmark for mortgages and credit cards.
- [[2-Year Treasury Yield]]: 4.693%, tracking short-term Fed policy; [[30-Year Treasury Yield]] at 5.334%.
- [[Basis Points]]: one basis point equals 0.01%; yields and prices move inversely.
- [[Gross Domestic Product]]: Q3 annualized growth of 3.4% versus 3.1% expected.
- [[Personal Consumption Expenditures Price Index]]: up 0.5% month over month and 3.6% year over year, above the Fed's 2% target.
- [[Federal Open Market Committee]]: held the rate at 3.75%-4% while acknowledging elevated inflation.
- [[Kevin Warsh]]: Fed chair who said policymakers are not on a set path.
- [[CME FedWatch]]: prices an 83% chance of a December hike.
- [[Ali Hassan]]: Thornburg portfolio manager who reads the Fed update as a softer policy path, not an inflation scare.

## Key Takeaways

- **Yield Level**: 10-year at 5.011%, 3 basis points lower on the day.
- **Growth Beat**: Q3 GDP rose 3.4% annualized versus 3.1% expected.
- **Inflation**: September PCE rose 3.6% year over year, slightly below the 3.7% forecast.
- **Monthly Pace**: PCE up 0.5% from August, in line with consensus.
- **Fed Hold**: Rate held at 3.75%-4% with inflation described as elevated.
- **Hike Odds**: Markets price an 83% chance of a December quarter-point hike.
- **Next Test**: Friday's end of the Fed blackout could revive hike pricing.
