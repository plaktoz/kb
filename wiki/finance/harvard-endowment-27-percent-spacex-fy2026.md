## Summary

[[Harvard University]]'s endowment returned 27.1% in fiscal 2026, its best year since 2021 and 4.8 points ahead of the S&P 500's 22.3%, lifting it to nearly $68 billion. A $2.2 billion [[SpaceX]] stake, its largest holding, powered the gain. Federal pressure offsets the good news: research revenue fell 19.5% and the excise tax on net investment income rises from 1.4% to 8%, costing roughly $300 million a year.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Endowment Returns]]**: 27.1% in fiscal 2026 (year ending June 30), beating the S&P 500 by 4.8 percentage points.
- **[[SpaceX]] position**: A $2.2 billion stake revealed in Harvard's second-quarter 13F filing, the largest single holding; TSMC is next at just under $350 million.
- **Distribution smoothing**: Harvard deliberately smooths payouts so operating support doesn't track markets.
- **Federal pressure**: The Trump administration seeks penalties and funding withholding; federally sponsored research revenue fell 19.5% to $509 million.
- **Excise tax**: Rising nearly six-fold from 1.4% to 8% this fiscal year, an estimated $300 million annual cost, and "several times larger" in a year like this one.

## Key Takeaways

- **Best Since 2021**: The 27.1% return is Harvard's strongest in five years.
- **Concentrated Winner**: SpaceX is the top holding at $2.2 billion.
- **Headwinds**: Research funding down 19.5%; tax rising to 8%.
- **Smoothed Payouts**: Strong returns feed distributions gradually.
- **Market Caveat**: Leaders warn markets can take back what they give.
