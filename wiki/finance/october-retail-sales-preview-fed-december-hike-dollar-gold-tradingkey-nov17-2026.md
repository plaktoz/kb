## Summary
TradingKey previews the October 2026 US retail sales release (consensus +0.2% m/m, down from +0.8% in September) as a test of consumer resilience and of December Fed rate-hike expectations. It argues that because retail sales are nominal and October CPI rose 0.4%, the market should focus on control group and ex-autos/gasoline spending, and it maps strong and weak outcomes onto equities, the dollar and gold.

## Core Concepts
- [[Retail Sales]]: headline expected +0.2%, ex-autos +0.4%; RBC expects +0.1% headline and +0.3% ex-autos and gasoline.
- [[Control Group Sales]]: the measure the market may watch more closely than the headline.
- [[Federal Reserve]] December rate hike: expectations have risen significantly amid renewed inflation.
- [[Nominal vs. Real Spending]]: retail sales are not price-adjusted, and October CPI rose 0.4% (core 0.3%).
- [[US Dollar Index]] near 103.5 and [[Gold]] near $3,850 (briefly $3,832.70).
- [[Treasury Yields]] and high-valuation equities (Nasdaq, AI, semiconductors).

## Key Takeaways
- **Consensus**: Headline retail sales +0.2% m/m versus +0.8% in September.
- **Inflation Caveat**: Sales are nominal; CPI up 0.4%, so growth may be price-driven.
- **Watch Metric**: Control group and ex-autos/gasoline spending matter more than headline.
- **Strong Data**: Boosts dollar and yields, pressures gold and high-valuation stocks.
- **Weak Data**: Moderates hike expectations, supports gold rebound and dollar correction.
- **Levels**: Dollar Index about 103.5, highest since April 2025; gold toward $3,800 or $4,000.
- **Resilient Categories**: Online, dining, healthcare and personal care held up; groceries lagged.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim about the October retail sales release?
**A**: It is a key test of consumer resilience against inflation and high rates, and it directly influences expectations for a December Fed rate hike.

**Q2**: Why might headline retail sales mislead?
**A**: Retail sales are nominal and October CPI rose 0.4%, so part of the growth may reflect higher prices rather than real volume. The market may therefore focus on control group and ex-autos/gasoline spending.

**Q3**: How should different outcomes shift positioning?
**A**: A much stronger print could lift the dollar and yields and pressure gold and high-valuation stocks. A clearly weak print could cool hike expectations, helping gold and the dollar correction, but hurt cyclical earnings forecasts.
