## Summary

The Dow rose 614 points (1.2%) on July 30 in what Carson Group's Ryan Detrick called a "typical snap back day after an exaggerated Fed Day move" — but the rally was narrower than the headline suggests, with the equal-weight S&P 500 in the red and the S&P 500 ex-Information Technology actually down for the day.

## Core Concepts

- [[Snapback Rally]] — a sharp reversal following an overextended prior-day selloff, distinct from a durable trend change
- [[Equal-Weight S&P 500]] — finished negative even as the market-cap-weighted index rallied, revealing the rally's narrowness
- [[Ryan Detrick]] — Carson Group chief strategist framing the move as a mean-reversion bounce, not new bullish information

## Key Takeaways

- **Dow +614 points (1.2%)**, Nasdaq +2.8%, S&P 500 +1.7%
- **"Exaggerated Fed Day move" reversal**: the prior day's selloff is framed as having overshot, making Thursday's bounce mechanical rather than fundamentally driven
- **Breadth warning**: S&P 500 Ex-Information Technology finished down; equal-weight S&P 500 also in the red
- **Session highlights**: memory stocks surged (Sandisk led S&P gainers), Microsoft had its best day since 2008, and the Japanese yen spiked sharply enough to stoke currency-intervention worries

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Mean Reversion]]**: Detrick's framing is a direct mean-reversion read — extreme one-day moves (the prior day's Fed selloff) tend to partially self-correct the next session regardless of new fundamental information, which is exactly what the narrow, tech-only rebound suggests happened here.
