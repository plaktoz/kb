## Summary
Motley Fool's Daniel Sparks picks [[Micron]], [[Amazon]], and [[IBM]] as a buy-and-hold-through-2030 trio because their differing valuations and cash profiles cover each other's weak spots. Micron looks cheap and is increasingly under contract, AWS has a $578 billion backlog, and IBM offers steady dividends plus a new AI agent platform with Microsoft. He would keep Micron the smallest position because its profits have collapsed before.

## Core Concepts
- [[Micron]]: fiscal 2026 revenue more than tripled to $133.2B and net income rose about tenfold to $85.0B, yet the stock trades under 5 times expected fiscal 2027 earnings.
- Strategic customer agreements: Micron's 26 agreements should exceed a third of revenue through 2030, often with floor and ceiling prices.
- [[Amazon Web Services]]: revenue up 39% to $46.0B in Q3, about 63% of Amazon's operating income, with a $578B backlog.
- [[Andy Jassy]]: says AWS can grow north of 40% in 2027 and 2028; capex of about $220B this year pushes free cash flow negative.
- [[IBM]] and [[Microsoft]]: Imagine Enterprise, launched Nov 10, offers over 6,000 pre-built AI agents and assets.
- [[Diversification]]: the three stocks differ in valuation, growth, and cash generation, so each offsets the others' weaknesses.
- [[Memory Cycle]]: memory prices can drop fast when supply catches up, making Micron's profits the most volatile.

## Key Takeaways
- **Micron Valuation**: Under 5 times forward earnings, implying investors price in a steep profit fall.
- **Cyclical Scar**: Micron lost $5.8B in fiscal 2023 on revenue about half the prior year's.
- **Contracted Revenue**: Micron's customer agreements, often with price floors and ceilings, add visibility.
- **AWS Backlog**: $578B is over three years of its $184B annual run rate.
- **Amazon Cash Burn**: Trailing free cash flow was negative $42.4B versus positive $14.8B a year earlier.
- **IBM Profile**: Revenue growth of 4-5%, software up 9%, dividends paid every year since 1916.
- **Shared Risk**: A sharp slowdown in AI spending could hurt all three.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Combine holdings whose weaknesses are offset by the others' strengths** — A portfolio is sturdier when one holding's volatility, cash burn, or slow growth is covered by another's, rather than relying on one business for years. · *model:* [[Diversification]]
→ explains: Micron Valuation, Amazon Cash Burn, IBM Profile

**T2. Contracted revenue reduces earnings volatility** — Revenue locked in by agreements or backlog is less exposed to swings in spot demand and prices, which supports holding through a cycle.
→ explains: Contracted Revenue, AWS Backlog, Cyclical Scar

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does the author pair Micron, Amazon, and IBM?
**A**: Each covers a weak spot in the others: Micron's volatile profits, Amazon's cash burn, and IBM's slow growth. Their differing valuations are a reason they fit well together.

**Q2**: What supports confidence in Micron despite its history of profit collapse?
**A**: Its 26 strategic customer agreements should make up over a third of revenue through 2030, mostly with set pricing terms including floors and ceilings.

**Q3**: How should an investor size these positions?
**A**: The author would keep Micron as the smallest position because its profits have collapsed before and memory prices can fall fast. All three could stumble if AI spending slows sharply.
