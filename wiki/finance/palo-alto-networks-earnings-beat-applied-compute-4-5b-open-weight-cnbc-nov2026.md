## Summary
Palo Alto Networks beat fiscal first-quarter estimates, raised its annual outlook, and saw shares rise about 6% as it announced a $4.5 billion acquisition of AI startup Applied Compute. The deal pushes the company beyond cybersecurity into securing and training customized open-weight models. CEO Nikesh Arora attributes growth to customers consolidating security platforms and rushing to patch against AI-assisted attacks.

## Core Concepts
- [[Palo Alto Networks]] fiscal Q1 beat: revenue up 39% year over year, guidance raised.
- [[Applied Compute]] acquisition ($4.5B): a startup that helps train customized [[Open-Weight Models]].
- [[Nikesh Arora]]'s strategy: be the one player delivering "secure compute and security capability"; part of an aggressive acquisition spree.
- [[Security Platform Consolidation]] driving firewall and AI-security growth.
- [[AI-Assisted Cyberattacks]] and recent outages at Microsoft, banks and the FAA raising patching urgency.
- Analyst views from [[Bank of America]] ("bold move") and [[Citigroup]] ("long-game" beyond cyber across the AI value chain).

## Key Takeaways
- **Earnings Beat**: Fiscal Q1 revenue rose 39% year over year; guidance raised.
- **Stock Reaction**: Shares rose about 6% on Thursday.
- **Big Acquisition**: $4.5 billion for Applied Compute, a shift beyond cybersecurity.
- **ARR Target**: Deal expected to add over $1B annual recurring revenue by fiscal 2029.
- **Demand Driver**: Businesses consolidating platforms and patching faster against AI-powered attacks.
- **Strategic Positioning**: Aim to deliver secure compute and security together.
- **Analyst Verdict**: BofA calls it bold; Citi sees an AI-value-chain long game.
