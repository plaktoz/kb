## Summary

Wall Street is entering earnings season with heightened scrutiny on the AI trade, having shifted from rewarding all AI spending to demanding proof of margins, revenue, and free cash flow. The [[PHLX Semiconductor Index]] fell 12% since late June while the [[Roundhill Magnificent Seven ETF]] rebounded ~8%, illustrating divergence within the AI theme. Investors are beginning to look beyond 2027 and rotating toward semiconductor equipment, foundries, memory, payment networks, and data center REITs as more durable AI beneficiaries.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Trade Rotation]]**: Market moving from broad AI enthusiasm to "more discerning" evaluation — margins, revenue, and [[Free Cash Flow]] now under scrutiny.
- **[[PHLX Semiconductor Index]]**: Fell ~12% since late June, signaling volatility at the AI hardware layer.
- **[[SK Hynix]]**: Nasdaq debut with shares surging 14%; positioned as a beneficiary when [[Micron]] signals unmet demand — memory seen as a continued opportunity.
- **[[Magnificent Seven ETF]]**: Roundhill ETF rebounded ~8% since late June, decoupling from semiconductor weakness.
- **[[AI Capex Sustainability]]**: [[UBS]] strategists say investor focus is shifting toward whether AI spending is sustainable beyond 2027.
- **[[Data Center REITs]]**: Cited alongside semiconductor equipment, foundries, memory, and payment networks as preferred AI-adjacent plays.
- **[[Yardeni Research]]**: Characterized recent semiconductor weakness as "a buying opportunity."

## Key Takeaways

- S&P 500 gained under 1% last week; Nasdaq whipsawed with semiconductors as the epicenter.
- [[SK Hynix]] surged 14% on Nasdaq debut — memory seen as a strong AI-adjacent opportunity.
- Semiconductor index -12% vs. Mag 7 ETF +8% since late June — divergence within AI theme.
- Market has shifted: all AI spending no longer rewarded; margins and FCF now required.
- UBS strategists rotating toward equipment, foundries, memory, payment networks, data center REITs.
- Sustainability of AI capex beyond 2027 is the emerging investor question.
- Earnings season expected to clarify the outlook for coming quarters.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Second-Order Thinking]]**: The market's shift from celebrating AI investment to questioning its ROI reflects second-order thinking — after the first wave of hype, investors now ask what the *returns* on that spending actually look like.
- **[[Margin of Safety]]**: Rotating toward durable sub-sectors (memory, equipment, REITs) over pure-play AI names reflects a preference for assets where the thesis doesn't require perfect execution to hold.
