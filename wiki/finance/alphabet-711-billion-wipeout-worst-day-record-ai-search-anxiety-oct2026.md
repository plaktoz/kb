## Summary

[[Alphabet]] fell 13% in its worst day since listing, erasing US$500 billion (A$711 billion) after Q3 search and advertising sales missed and free cash flow turned negative on more than US$50 billion of quarterly AI spending. Investors fear [[AI Mode]] and chatbots like [[ChatGPT]] and [[Claude]] are eroding [[Google Search]] monetisation. [[Microsoft]] barely moved because it funds its AI expansion with positive free cash flow.

## Core Concepts

- [[Alphabet]] Q3 2026 earnings: Google Services revenue of US$96.3 billion missed expectations by just over US$2 billion.
- [[Google Search]] and [[AI Mode]]: AI queries carry "lower monetisation", according to [[Philipp Schindler]].
- [[Google Cloud]]: revenue up 103% to US$30.7 billion, with operating margin of 36.2%; the [[Gemini]] app reached one billion users.
- [[Free Cash Flow]]: Alphabet's was negative for a second quarter (minus US$9.1 billion) versus [[Microsoft]]'s positive.
- [[AI Capital Expenditure]]: Alphabet's AI spending exceeded US$50 billion in the quarter; [[Amazon]] capex hit US$77.3 billion against US$58.67 billion expected.
- Market reaction: [[Nasdaq]] fell 1.4% against 0.3% for the S&P 500 and Dow; [[Intel]] and [[Caterpillar]] also slid.

## Key Takeaways

- **Record Wipeout**: Alphabet shares fell 13%, its biggest one-day drop since the 2004 listing.
- **Search Threat**: Investors worry [[AI Chatbots]] are eating into the search advertising business.
- **Cash Flow Split**: Alphabet's negative free cash flow contrasted with Microsoft's positive cash generation.
- **Cloud Strength**: Google Cloud revenue rose 103% year-on-year with expanding margins.
- **Lower Monetisation**: AI queries earn less than classic search; Google plans to add more ads.
- **Financing Scrutiny**: Markets shift from rewarding all AI spending to examining how it is financed.
- **Contrary View**: VanEck says it is premature to treat weak results as proof the core is threatened.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Cannibalization of the core** — A new product that serves the same user need at lower monetisation erodes the incumbent's profit pool, even when it is the incumbent's own offering. · *model:* [[Cannibalization]]
→ explains: Search Threat, Lower Monetisation

**T2. Funding determines how spending is judged** — Investment backed by visible cash generation is rewarded, while spending that outruns cash flow is punished. · *model:* [[Capital Allocation]]
→ explains: Cash Flow Split, Financing Scrutiny

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did Alphabet suffer its worst-ever one-day fall?
**A**: Disappointing Q3 search and advertising sales, plus negative free cash flow on over US$50 billion of AI spending, fuelled fear that AI is eroding its core search business.

**Q2**: What data points show the split between Alphabet and Microsoft?
**A**: Alphabet's free cash flow was minus US$9.1 billion for a second straight quarter, while Microsoft funds AI expansion with positive free cash flow and its shares fell only 0.2%.

**Q3**: What does the reaction imply for big tech AI investment?
**A**: Markets will reward AI spending backed by visible cash generation and punish spending that outruns it, so the key test is whether capex converts into Cloud revenue fast enough.
