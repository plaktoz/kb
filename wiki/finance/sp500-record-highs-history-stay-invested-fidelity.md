## Summary
Despite headwinds including a war in Iran and elevated Treasury yields, the [[S&P 500]] has climbed ~13.6% year-to-date through early September 2026. Historical data — including Fidelity research going back to 1950 — shows that record highs are not sell signals; the index has averaged a 12.7% gain in the 12 months following a record close, and upcoming calendar and earnings tailwinds could fuel further highs.

## Core Concepts
- **[[S&P 500]] Record High Momentum**: The index logged 461 record highs between 2013 and June 2026, delivering roughly 400% cumulative returns over that period — evidence that record closes precede further gains far more often than selloffs.
- **[[Fidelity]] 1950–2026 Research**: In the 12 months after a new record close, the S&P 500 averaged a 12.7% gain — a data point that directly refutes the "sell the high" instinct.
- **[[Vanguard S&P 500 ETF]] (VOO)**: Used as the performance proxy for S&P 500 tracking; 13.6% YTD gain through early September 2026.
- **[[Buy-and-Hold Compounding]]**: $10,000 invested on January 1, 1988 would have grown to nearly $523,000 by end of 2024 — but only if the investor never exited the market; missing even a few of the best days significantly erodes long-term returns.
- **[[Presidential Cycle Year 3]]**: The third year of the presidential cycle (2027 in the current cycle) has historically been the strongest of the four — an additional tailwind for equity investors staying the course.

## Key Takeaways
- **Record highs ≠ sell signals**: 461 S&P 500 all-time highs from 2013–June 2026 were followed by ~400% cumulative gains.
- **Post-record average return**: Fidelity data since 1950 shows 12.7% average gain over the 12 months after a record close.
- **Compounding demands patience**: $10K in 1988 → ~$523K by end of 2024 requires never leaving the market.
- **Best-day risk**: Missing a handful of the best trading days materially erodes long-run returns.
- **Seasonal tailwind**: October ends the historically weaker six-month period; the seasonal setup turns favorable.
- **Presidential cycle**: 2027 (year 3 of the cycle) has historically been the strongest for equities.
- **Earnings catalyst**: Analysts project S&P 500 Q3 2026 earnings growth of 28.5%, which could push the index to new highs.
- **YTD performance**: S&P 500 up ~13.6% through early September 2026 despite Iran war and elevated Treasury yields.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Availability Bias]]**: The visceral memory of past market crashes makes record highs feel dangerous — yet the data shows the opposite is true; investors systematically overweight the probability of a selloff from record levels because crashes are more memorable than steady advances.
- **[[Opportunity Cost]]**: Exiting the market to "protect gains" at record highs has an invisible cost — the compounding you don't receive — which the $10K→$523K example makes concrete: the cost of sitting on the sidelines is not zero, it compounds against you.
