## Summary

[[Simply Wall St]] argues [[IBM]] at US$232.14 may be about 15% undervalued on a [[Discounted Cash Flow]] basis, anchored on roughly $13.1 billion of trailing free cash flow assumed to grow gradually. Quantum and semiconductor research, including nanostack transistors with Siemens, supports long-term cash expectations but adds near-term investment and timing risk. Community narratives split between roughly fair value and 39% overvalued.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Discounted Cash Flow]] view**: Values IBM on free cash flow of about $13.1B over the last twelve months, with a moderate step-up in annual cash over the coming decade; the output sits above the $232.14 share price.
- **Valuation context**: 5-year total return of 130.1% and a 20.4x P/E.
- **Quantum and chips**: Nanostack 3D transistor work with Siemens and fault-tolerant quantum systems may support future cash flows but raise investment needs and timing uncertainty.
- **Bull narrative**: Roughly fairly valued; [[Hybrid Cloud]] and AI strategy, with software near 45% of revenue and about 80% of it recurring.
- **Bear narrative**: 39% overvalued; the risk is execution in converting partnerships and AI investment into sustained growth and cash generation.
- **Insider selling**: Flagged by the source as a factor to check.

## Key Takeaways

- **15% Discount**: DCF output exceeds the $232.14 price.
- **FCF Base**: About $13.1B trailing free cash flow underlies the model.
- **Split Community**: Narratives range from fair value to 39% overvalued.
- **Recurring Software**: About 80% of software revenue recurs.
- **Execution Risk**: Converting AI and partnerships into cash is the bear case.
