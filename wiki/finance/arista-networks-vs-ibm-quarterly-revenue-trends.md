## Summary

Arista Networks and IBM represent contrasting AI-era investment profiles: Arista has posted eight consecutive quarters of sequential revenue growth driven by AI data center buildouts, while IBM's revenue has been volatile due to its mixed hybrid-cloud, consulting, and infrastructure business model. Arista achieved a 45% operating margin in Q2 2026 versus IBM's 15%, illustrating the profitability gap between pure-play AI networking and diversified enterprise IT. IBM reduced its 2026 full-year sales forecast as customer spending shifted toward AI infrastructure priorities — the very tailwind powering Arista.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Arista Networks]]** — cloud networking and high-performance switching hardware vendor serving major internet and financial firms; benefiting from AI data center expansion
- **[[IBM]]** — hybrid cloud software, enterprise servers, and consulting conglomerate; pursuing quantum computing via HRL Laboratories acquisition
- **[[AI Data Center Buildout]]** — secular demand wave for high-bandwidth switching and networking, directly driving Arista's growth
- **[[Operating Margin]]** — measure of operational efficiency; Arista at 45% vs IBM at 15% in Q2 2026
- **[[Hybrid Cloud]]** — IBM's core platform strategy combining on-premise and public cloud infrastructure
- **[[Quantum Computing]]** — IBM's long-term bet via HRL Laboratories acquisition

## Key Takeaways

- **Arista revenue growth**: Q3 2024 $1.8B → Q2 2026 $3.0B, consistent sequential increases each quarter.
- **IBM revenue volatile**: ranged $14.5B–$19.7B over the same period with no clear upward trend.
- **Operating margin gap**: Arista 45% vs IBM 15% in Q2 2026 — a 30-point spread.
- **Arista Q3 2026 forecast**: ~$3.3B, continuing the growth trajectory.
- **IBM headwinds**: infrastructure segment down 7% YoY; consulting flat; full-year guidance cut.
- **IBM securities fraud investigations** ongoing as of Q2 2026 reporting period.
- **Spending shift**: enterprise budgets moving toward AI infrastructure, helping Arista, hurting IBM consulting.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Winner-Takes-Most Markets]]**: AI data center networking concentrates spending toward specialized vendors like Arista that own the high-performance switching layer, leaving diversified incumbents like IBM competing across too many fronts to capture the same margin.
- **[[Innovator's Dilemma]]**: IBM's consulting and infrastructure revenue base creates inertia that slows reorientation toward AI infrastructure — the same legacy revenue that once provided stability now signals displacement risk.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What explains the divergence in revenue trajectory between Arista and IBM over Q3 2024–Q2 2026?
**A**: Arista grew consistently by riding AI data center demand for cloud networking hardware, while IBM's mixed business model — heavy in consulting and legacy infrastructure — produced volatile results with no structural growth.

**Q2**: What specific financial metrics illustrate the performance gap between the two companies in Q2 2026?
**A**: Arista posted a 45% operating margin versus IBM's 15%, and Arista's quarterly revenue reached $3.0B while IBM's infrastructure segment declined 7% year-over-year and consulting was flat.

**Q3**: How should an investor interpret IBM's 2026 full-year guidance cut in the context of AI spending trends?
**A**: The guidance cut reflects a structural budget shift by enterprise customers toward AI infrastructure — exactly what Arista supplies — suggesting IBM's traditional revenue streams face ongoing pressure rather than a temporary cyclical dip.
