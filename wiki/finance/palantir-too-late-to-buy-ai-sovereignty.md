## Summary

Despite a 1,000%+ gain over three years, the author argues Palantir's growth story is far from over because it solves the gap between having access to AI and actually putting it to work. Its [[Palantir AIP]] turns data from thousands of sources into real-time decisions, with rapid deal flow ($3.37B across 220 $1M+ deals in Q2) and a new push into AI sovereignty. For long-term holders, the author concludes it is not too late to buy.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Palantir Technologies (PLTR)]]** — AI software firm serving governments and enterprises; stock up 1,000%+ in three years
- **[[Palantir AIP]]** — Artificial Intelligence Platform converting multi-source data (incl. satellites) into real-time operational insights
- **[[AI Sovereignty]]** — a nation's or organization's ability to build and control AI without relying on foreign governments or external providers
- **[[Sovereignty Bootcamps]]** — two 2026 events with ~200 organizations, accelerating onboarding of net-new customers
- **[[Strategic Partnerships]]** — Fujitsu (Takane LLM), Nvidia, PwC, and Nebius Group extend distribution and sovereign-AI reach
- **[[AI Deployment Gap]]** — the difference between having AI access and operationalizing it, which Palantir monetizes

## Key Takeaways

- **Stock run**: Gained over 1,000% in the last three years.
- **Q2 deal flow**: 220 deals over $1M each, totaling $3.37B+ contract value.
- **Fujitsu case**: Linked 3,000 suppliers, 18 factories; $10M+ annual cost savings.
- **Sovereignty pitch**: Karp says firms should own AI value, not surrender data.
- **Bootcamps**: ~200 organizations attended; expected to fuel triple-digit U.S. commercial growth.
- **Partnerships**: Nvidia, PwC, Nebius, Fujitsu broaden supply-chain and sovereign-AI use cases.
- **Moat claim**: Author calls platforms nearly impossible for competitors to replicate.
- **Verdict**: Not too late for long-term investors; "just getting started."

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Value accrues to whoever closes the deployment gap** — Access to AI models is commoditizing; the scarce, monetizable capability is integrating AI into real operations and decisions. · *model:* [[Last-Mile Problem]]
→ explains: Palantir AIP, Fujitsu cost savings, AI Deployment Gap

**T2. Control of data determines who captures AI's value** — Organizations that hand data to external AI providers create value others capture; sovereignty keeps that value in-house. · *model:* [[Value Creation vs. Value Capture]]
→ explains: AI Sovereignty, Sovereignty Bootcamps, Karp's data-ownership argument

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the author's core argument for why it isn't too late to buy Palantir?
**A**: Palantir solves the gap between AI access and practical deployment, a position the author views as unique and hard to replicate, so growth should continue despite the 1,000%+ run.

**Q2**: What data points does the article cite for Palantir's business momentum?
**A**: 220 Q2 deals worth $1M+ each totaling over $3.37B; a Fujitsu-enabled manufacturer saving $10M+ annually across 3,000 suppliers and 18 factories; ~200 organizations at Sovereignty Bootcamps.

**Q3**: How would you apply the AI sovereignty thesis when evaluating other AI software stocks?
**A**: Ask whether the vendor lets customers retain control of their data and AI-generated value; vendors enabling sovereignty may win regulated and government buyers wary of external dependence.
