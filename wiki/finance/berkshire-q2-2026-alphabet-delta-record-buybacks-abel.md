## Summary

In the second quarter, [[Berkshire Hathaway]] under CEO [[Greg Abel]] added to [[Alphabet]], bought 17.5 million more [[Delta Air Lines]] shares (up 44% to over 57 million), and repurchased about $4.5 billion of its own stock, up from $235 million in Q1. Berkshire may buy back shares only when the CEO judges the price below conservatively estimated intrinsic value, so the buybacks signal management's view of value. Berkshire ended June with about $359.2 billion in cash and short-term investments.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Greg Abel]]'s approach**: Follows [[Warren Buffett]]'s philosophy of buying below [[Intrinsic Value]] but deploys cash more aggressively.
- **Buyback rule**: Repurchases are allowed only when the CEO determines the price is below conservatively estimated intrinsic value.
- **Buyback prices**: Class B shares bought at an average of $476.01 in May and $487.98 in June.
- **[[Delta Air Lines]] stake**: Opened in Q1, Abel's first quarter; now more than 57 million shares.
- **Cash reserve**: About $359.2 billion remains available after the purchases.
- **Conglomerate exposure**: Insurance, Duracell and Dairy Queen alongside stakes in Apple and Bank of America.

## Key Takeaways

- **Buybacks Surge**: About $4.5 billion in Q2 versus $235 million in Q1.
- **Value Signal**: Rule-bound buybacks imply management sees shares below intrinsic value.
- **Alphabet Added**: Berkshire increased its stake substantially in Q2.
- **Delta Up 44%**: Position grew by 17.5 million shares.
- **Dry Powder**: More than $359 billion in cash remains.
