## Summary

Microsoft (MSFT) closed at $481.15 on August 21, 2026, up 30% from a July low, with analysts setting a $569.56 consensus target contingent on two conditions: Azure growth holding above 40% (seen as high-certainty, given management guidance of ~45% next quarter) and a confirmed technical breakout above the $500 resistance level (the larger uncertainty).

## Core Concepts

- **[[Growth-Trend Sensitivity]]**: For a growth giant like Microsoft, the market reacts more to the *direction* of a metric like Azure growth than to absolute quarterly profit — the same beat-and-raise report produced an 11.7% single-day plunge in December (Azure "only" 39%, framed as slowdown) versus a 9% after-hours jump in July (Azure accelerated to 43%).
- **[[Copilot Penetration Rate]]**: M365 Copilot's paid-seat count (30M+, roughly 7% of ~430M commercial M365 users) is the specific metric analysts watch to verify whether Azure demand is being driven by genuine AI monetization rather than infrastructure spend alone.
- **[[Technical Breakout Confirmation]]**: A price level (here, $500) becomes meaningful only when tested repeatedly and then held on high volume — MSFT touched $500 multiple times in early August without a confirmed breakout, making the next attempt a key signal.

## Key Takeaways

- Azure growth accelerated to 43% in Q4 FY2026 (vs. 39% the prior quarter), beating the 40% consensus; JPMorgan and Citi both raised price targets ($625 and $600 respectively) on ~45% guidance for the next quarter.
- Short-term trading range is expected to be $472–$494, with $500 as key resistance and the 20-day moving average (~$472) as key support.
- Bear signals to watch: Azure growth dropping below 35% for two consecutive quarters, repeated failure to hold $500, a break below $472, or rising capex-to-revenue without management signaling spending is "adjustable."
- The $570 target is considered reasonable but conditional — Azure's growth trajectory is the higher-confidence variable; the technical breakout above $500 is the larger open question.
