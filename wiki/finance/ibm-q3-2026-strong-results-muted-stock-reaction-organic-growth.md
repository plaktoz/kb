## Summary
[[IBM]]'s Q3 2026 report (Oct. 21) was strong, with revenue up 5% to $17.1B and operating EPS up 11% to $2.95, largely confirming that the July miss was a matter of deal timing. The stock isn't rebounding because part of the growth was acquired, management is guiding to a slower Q4, and acquisitions have weakened the balance sheet. The author nonetheless remains bullish long term on enterprise AI, hybrid cloud and quantum.

## Core Concepts
- [[IBM]]: Fell about 25% in July, its worst day in 115 years, on slipped contract closings.
- [[Deal Timing Slippage]]: About two-thirds of large software deals that slipped in Q2 closed in Q3, per CFO Jim Kavanaugh.
- [[Transaction Processing Software]]: Mainframe-linked; swung from a 9% decline to 10% growth, but expected flat for the full year.
- [[Confluent Acquisition]]: Roughly $11B deal closed in March, adding about $314.8M quarterly revenue.
- [[Organic vs Reported Growth]]: Software growth of 9% reported falls to about 4% organic once Confluent is excluded.
- [[Free Cash Flow]]: $3B in Q3; full-year FCF growth outlook raised to more than $1B.
- [[Balance Sheet Leverage]]: Cash down $3.4B to $11.1B; debt up $2.6B to $63.8B.
- [[Dividend Coverage]]: $4.8B dividends against $7.8B FCF through nine months; yield about 2.7%.

## Key Takeaways
- **Strong Q3**: Revenue +5% to $17.1B; operating EPS +11% to $2.95.
- **Delayed, Not Lost**: Most slipped Q2 software deals closed in Q3.
- **Bought Growth**: Confluent lifts software growth to 9%; organic is only about 4%.
- **Q4 Slowdown**: About $32B full-year software revenue implies roughly 4% Q4 growth versus 8%.
- **Mainframe Dip**: Mainframe hardware sales fell 20% against a strong launch a year ago.
- **Mgmt Tempers**: Krishna doesn't expect transaction processing to keep double-digit growth.
- **Thinner Cushion**: $11.4B in acquisitions cut cash and raised debt to $63.8B.
- **Long-Term Bull Case**: Established customers, steady cash flow, reliable dividend.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why isn't IBM's stock rebounding despite strong Q3 results?
**A**: Details temper the headline: part of the growth was acquired, management guides to slower Q4 growth, and acquisitions have reduced cash and raised debt.

**Q2**: How does the article show software growth is less impressive than 9%?
**A**: The Confluent acquisition closed in March, adding about $314.8M of quarterly revenue against a $7.2B year-ago base, which puts organic software growth near 4%.

**Q3**: What does the author conclude for investors?
**A**: The Q2 miss looks like delayed spending rather than lost customers, and the dividend remains well covered. The author stays bullish long term but notes less cushion for another big deal.
