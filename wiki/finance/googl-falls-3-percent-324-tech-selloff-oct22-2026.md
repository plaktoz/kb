## Summary
[[Alphabet]] (GOOGL) fell 3.15% to $324.22 on October 22, 2026, more than the Nasdaq Composite's 2.60% decline, in a broad tech sell-off tied to resurfaced worries over AI spending and the cost of capital. No Alphabet-specific announcement drove the move. Same-day coverage centered on [[Google Cloud]] capacity, including Pichai's compute-prioritization comments and a reported $11 billion-a-year SpaceX compute lease.

## Core Concepts
- [[Alphabet]]: Closed at $324.22, 20.65% below its 52-week high of $408.61.
- [[Tech Sell-Off]]: Meta fell 6.98%, Amazon 4.57%, Microsoft 2.23%; Dow edged up 0.02%.
- [[AI Spending Worries]]: Concerns over AI spending and the cost of capital hit chips and hyperscalers.
- [[Sundar Pichai]]: Said [[Google DeepMind]]'s compute needs are "non-negotiable"; Cloud gives up short-term revenue.
- [[Google Cloud]]: Grew 82% in Q2 including TPU system sales; 15% global market share.
- [[SpaceX Compute Lease]]: Reported $920 million a month through 2029 for xAI cloud infrastructure, about 110,000 GPUs.
- [[Concentration Risk]]: Analyst warns a power failure at xAI's centralized facilities could hit many tenants.
- [[Meta Platforms]]: Fell after quarterly results and a 2-for-1 stock split announcement.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Allocating scarce capacity to one use forgoes revenue from another** — Giving frontier research first claim on compute means Cloud customers get less, so the cost of the choice is the revenue given up. · *model:* [[Opportunity Cost]]
→ explains: Compute Priority, Revenue Unquantified

**T2. Concentrating many dependents on one facility creates correlated failure risk** — A shared site means one outage can hit a large share of tenants at once. · *model:* [[Concentration Risk]]
→ explains: SpaceX Deal Risk
