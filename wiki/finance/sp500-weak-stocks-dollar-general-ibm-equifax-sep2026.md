## Summary
StockStory (via Yahoo Finance) flags three S&P 500 constituents — Dollar General, IBM, and Equifax — as stocks to "think twice about" despite their index membership, citing weak revenue growth, underperforming returns on capital, and margin pressure. The article closes with a promotional pitch for StockStory's alternative "market-beating" stock picks.

## Core Concepts

- [[S&P 500 Index Membership vs Quality]] — the article's framing that inclusion in the index is not itself a signal of investment quality, since constituents can still face "stagnating growth, heavy debt, or disruptive new competitors"
- [[Dollar General]] — discount retailer selling household essentials, groceries, and seasonal goods; flagged for limited scale-driven growth and weak returns on capital
- [[IBM]] — legacy tech giant now focused on hybrid cloud, AI, and consulting; flagged for below-sector revenue growth and underperforming EPS growth despite the broader AI narrative
- [[Equifax]] — global data analytics firm with credit records on over 800 million consumers; flagged for margin decline from rising costs and weak EPS growth
- [[Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) as Red/Green Flag]] — used again here (as in StockStory's other index screens) to diagnose Dollar General's "underwhelming" 8.8% return on capital

## Key Takeaways

- **Dollar General (DG)**: $27.54B market cap, 4% annual revenue growth (3-yr avg), 8.8% ROIC, below-competitor gross margin; trades at $124.27 (15.3x forward P/E).
- **IBM (IBM)**: $212.5B market cap, 5-yr revenue growth called "below standards" for business services, 8.6% annual EPS growth called sector-underperforming; trades at $225.61 (17.8x forward P/E).
- **Equifax (EFX)**: $17.4B market cap, operating margin declining on rising costs, just 1.1% annual EPS growth over 5 years; trades at $148.44 (16.3x forward P/E).
- **Framing**: All three critiques center on growth-vs-scale limits, weak capital returns, or margin erosion rather than balance-sheet distress.
- **Disclosure note**: Piece ends with StockStory promoting its own "market-beating" stock list, citing Nvidia and Tecnoglass as past picks — a promotional angle to weigh against the critique's objectivity.
