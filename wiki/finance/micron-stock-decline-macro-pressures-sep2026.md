## Summary

Micron (MU) fell ~4.7% on September 11, 2026, significantly underperforming the broader market as macro headwinds, AI regulation uncertainty, and employee compensation concerns weighed on the stock. Rising 10-year Treasury yields hitting a 2023 high of 4.84%, combined with climbing oil prices and upcoming CPI data, pushed investors toward risk-off positioning. Secondary pressures included AI governance discussion and Micron's bonus announcement for Taiwan workers raising modest margin concerns.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Micron Technology]] (MU)**: Semiconductor company facing a single-session ~4.7% decline, recovering from an intraday low of -5.3%.
- **[[10-Year Treasury Yield]]**: Reached 4.84%, the highest since 2023, despite Treasury buyback efforts — the primary driver of risk-off sentiment.
- **[[Risk-Off Positioning]]**: Investors rotated away from growth/tech equities in response to macro signals including rising yields and oil prices.
- **[[AI Regulation]]**: Growing discussion of guardrails for artificial intelligence created uncertainty, though near-term chip demand impact is considered unlikely.
- **[[Employee Compensation / Margin Pressure]]**: Micron announced substantial bonuses at its Taiwan facilities to preempt labor disputes — viewed as appropriate but adding modest margin uncertainty.

## Key Takeaways

- **Macro Driver**: 10-year Treasury yield hit 4.84% — highest since 2023 — triggering risk-off selling.
- **MU Decline**: Stock dropped ~4.7%, vs. S&P 500 and Nasdaq each down ~0.6%.
- **AI Regulation**: Uncertainty from AI guardrail discussions; no material near-term demand shift expected.
- **Labor Cost**: Taiwan worker bonuses announced to avoid disputes; modest margin concern flagged.
- **Intraday Recovery**: Off as much as 5.3% before partial recovery by session close.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Risk-Off Sentiment]]**: When discount rates rise (higher yields), the present value of future growth cash flows falls — directly explaining why high-multiple semiconductor stocks like Micron are disproportionately hit versus the broader index.
