## Summary

[[Microsoft]] priced $25 billion of senior unsecured notes the day after its Azure beat, its first U.S. investment-grade bond sale since 2017, while capex pressure cut free cash flow to $18.6 billion. A TIKR model projects a mid-case target near $1,290 by 2031 (about 143% total return, roughly 21% annual IRR) from a $530.14 price. The Azure guide depends on efficiency gains not yet found, so the fiscal Q2 report is the test.

## Core Concepts

- [[Microsoft]]: issuer of ten tranches maturing 2028 to 2066, with coupons from 4.85% to 6.35%.
- [[Amy Hood]]: CFO who guided Q2 capex above $50 billion and Azure growth of 49-50% in constant currency.
- [[Microsoft Azure]]: 48% growth reported, with the guide leaning on fleet efficiency gains.
- [[Lease Financing]]: a financing "vehicle" that the notes now supplement.
- [[Free Cash Flow]]: $18.6 billion in fiscal Q1 versus $25.66 billion a year earlier.
- [[TIKR]]: source of the advanced valuation model and the Street target mean of about $606.
- [[Token Pricing]]: named as a swing factor after higher-than-expected pricing helped Q1.
- [[CoreWeave]] and [[Nebius]]: third-party capacity suppliers whose shares fell about 5% on October 29.

## Key Takeaways

- **Bond Deal**: $25 billion in ten tranches, 2028-2066, the first IG sale since 2017.
- **Cash Squeeze**: $39.4 billion of capex cut free cash flow to $18.6 billion.
- **Capex Guide**: Q2 capex above $50 billion; calendar 2026 estimate lowered to about $170 billion.
- **Efficiency Dependence**: Hood said "we need to find some more, which we're counting on."
- **Softer Signals**: Commercial bookings fell 12%; Hold ratings rose to five from two.
- **Margin Pressure**: Microsoft Cloud gross margin guided to roughly 65% in Q2.
- **Valuation Case**: Mid target of about $1,290 assumes 19% revenue compounding and 40% net margins.
- **Key Test**: Fiscal Q2 Azure growth of 49% or better with capex over $50 billion.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central argument about Microsoft's bond sale?
**A**: The $25 billion raise adds a second financing channel next to leasing as free cash flow tightens under heavy capex. Whether it is a bridge to a slower payoff depends on whether Azure growth keeps pace with spending.

**Q2**: What data point shows the cash-flow pressure?
**A**: Operating cash flow rose 29% to $58 billion, but $39.4 billion in property and equipment spending left free cash flow at $18.6 billion, down from $25.66 billion a year earlier.

**Q3**: What would confirm or undermine the bull case?
**A**: Azure growth of 49% or better with capex above $50 billion would show the buildout is meeting demand. A miss while capex keeps climbing would make the bonds look like a bridge to a slower payoff.
