## Summary
A quality/momentum stock screen flags Lennox International as a buy candidate on strong margins and returns on capital, while naming Delta Air Lines and HP Inc. as avoids due to weak revenue trends, softening returns on capital, or eroding profitability of incremental sales.

## Core Concepts

- [[Quality Factor Screening]] — the article's evaluation lens: operating margin trend, free-cash-flow margin trend, and return on invested capital (ROIC) as the core signals separating "buy" from "avoid" within the same index
- [[Momentum + Fundamentals Combination]] — the piece's framing that the best entries combine elite underlying fundamentals with the market only just beginning to notice them, rather than momentum or quality alone
- [[Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) as Red/Green Flag]] — used directly to diagnose both stocks: Delta's "eroding returns on capital from an already low base" signals value-destroying investment, while Lennox's 37.3% ROIC signals disciplined capital allocation

## Key Takeaways

- **Sell/avoid — Delta Air Lines (DAL)**: sluggish revenue-passenger-mile growth, flat projected free cash flow margin, and eroding ROIC; trades at $81.26/share, 10x forward P/E.
- **Sell/avoid — HP Inc. (HPQ)**: sales fell 1.2% annually over five years, projected sales flat next 12 months, and flat EPS despite revenue growth (less-profitable incremental sales); trades at $29.52/share, 10.9x forward P/E.
- **Buy/watch — Lennox International (LII)**: 17.7% operating margin with five-year profit growth as it scaled, free cash flow margin up 8.6 points over five years, and 37.3% ROIC; trades at $413.10/share, 16.8x forward P/E.
- **Historical framing**: the article cites prior momentum-screen picks like Nvidia (+1,460% June 2020–June 2025) and smaller names like Kadant (+214% over the same period) as evidence for the screening approach.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Within the same S&P 500 index, quality/momentum screening separates strong compounders (Lennox) from deteriorating businesses (Delta, HP) — large-cap status alone doesn't guarantee similar forward performance.

**Q2**: What specific metric does the article use to flag both Delta and Lennox, in opposite directions?
**A**: Return on invested capital (ROIC) — Delta's "eroding returns on capital from an already low base" signals destructive recent investment, while Lennox's high 37.3% ROIC signals management's skill at identifying profitable investments.

**Q3**: What is the article's stated philosophy for when to buy a "great" stock?
**A**: "The best time to own a great stock is when the market is finally noticing it" — combining already-strong fundamentals with early-stage market recognition, rather than chasing momentum alone or ignoring stocks the market hasn't yet re-rated.
