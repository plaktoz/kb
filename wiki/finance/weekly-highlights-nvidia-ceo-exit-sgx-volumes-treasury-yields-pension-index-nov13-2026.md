## Summary

A Singapore-focused weekly recap covering Nvidia's CEO resignation and share plunge, a six-month high in SGX derivatives volume, US 10-year yields back above 5% on rate-hike bets, and Singapore's top-in-Asia pension ranking. Rising rates and market volatility are the connecting thread across the week's stories.

## Core Concepts

- [[Nvidia]] co-founder and CEO [[Jensen Huang]] resigned after reports of two affairs; CFO [[Colette Kress]] is interim CEO and Harvey Jones becomes the first-ever chair.
- [[Singapore Exchange]] (SGX) derivatives volume hit a six-month high as volatility drove [[Hedging]] demand.
- [[US Treasury Yields]] rose with the 10-year at 5.068%, driven by hot [[CPI]] data and expectations of [[Federal Reserve]] rate hikes.
- [[Marsh CFA Institute Global Pension Index]] ranked Singapore 4th globally and first in Asia, scoring 82.0 across adequacy, sustainability and integrity.
- [[Oil Prices]] and the Middle East war added to inflation worries, with Brent near US$93.60.

## Key Takeaways

- **Nvidia Shock**: Shares fell as much as 14%, market cap dipped below US$4 trillion.
- **Analyst Reaction**: At least seven Wall Street firms downgraded Nvidia in a week.
- **Next Catalyst**: Nvidia's 3QFY2027 results are due on 17 November 2026.
- **SGX Volumes**: Daily derivatives volume rose 17% month on month to 1.6 million contracts.
- **STI Slide**: The Straits Times Index fell about 5% in October but stayed 21% up year on year.
- **Yield Spike**: The two-year yield jumped over 11 basis points to 4.843%.
- **Hike Pricing**: Traders fully price a December hike; Goldman adds a March 2027 hike.
- **Housing Strain**: US existing home sales hit their lowest in over 16 years.
- **Pension Ranking**: Netherlands led at 85.8; US placed 33rd, Hong Kong 17th.
