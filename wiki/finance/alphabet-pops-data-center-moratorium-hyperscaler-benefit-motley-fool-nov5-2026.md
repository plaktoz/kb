## Summary

Alphabet rose as much as 5.9% (closing up 3.2%) after Democrats won Congress and key governorships on platforms of stricter data center oversight. The author argues a data center moratorium would hurt competitors and benefit incumbent hyperscalers like Google while slowing capex and lifting free cash flow. At about 15 times earnings, he calls the stock a compelling opportunity.

## Core Concepts

- [[Alphabet]]: stock up 3.2% on the day after being down 13% post-earnings, its largest one-day drop.
- [[2026 US Midterm Elections]]: Democrats took the House and Senate plus several governorships.
- [[Data Center Moratorium]]: about 50 ballot measures nationally; [[Gina Hinojosa]] pledged a Texas moratorium.
- [[Hyperscalers]]: incumbents with existing data center networks that would face less competition.
- [[Google Cloud]]: Q3 revenue up 103% year over year to $30.7 billion.
- [[Capital Expenditures]]: roughly $200 billion planned for 2026 versus $90 billion last year.
- [[Free Cash Flow]]: negative $9 billion, which drove the post-earnings selloff.

## Key Takeaways

- **Stock Move**: Up as much as 5.9% intraday, closing up 3.2%.
- **Texas Exposure**: 41% of capacity coming online in 2028 is slated for Texas.
- **Other States**: Ohio, Georgia, and Arizona also elected Democratic governors.
- **Cloud Growth**: Google Cloud revenue rose 103% to $30.7 billion in Q3.
- **Capex Concern**: About $200 billion 2026 capex and negative $9 billion free cash flow.
- **Moratorium Upside**: Restrictions hurt competitors and could slow Alphabet's own capex.
- **Valuation**: Trades near 15 times earnings, close to last week's all-time low.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Restrictions favor incumbents** — Rules that limit new capacity raise the cost of entry for challengers and protect firms that already hold the scarce assets. · *model:* [[Barriers to Entry]]
→ explains: Moratorium Upside, Capex Concern

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did Alphabet stock rise on Wednesday?
**A**: Democratic election wins raised the prospect of data center moratoriums, which the author argues would hurt competition and benefit hyperscalers like Google.

**Q2**: What data explains the earlier selloff and the current valuation case?
**A**: Investors balked at negative $9 billion free cash flow and about $200 billion in 2026 capex, dropping the stock 13%. At roughly 15 times earnings, near an all-time low, the author sees an opportunity.

**Q3**: How could a moratorium actually help Alphabet?
**A**: It would limit competitors while Google already has its data center network, and it would slow Alphabet's capex, boosting profits and cash flow.
