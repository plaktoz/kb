## Summary

[[ASML Holding]] (NASDAQ: ASML), the sole supplier of EUV lithography machines, may be the biggest beneficiary of the AI-driven memory boom — ahead of memory makers [[Sandisk]] and [[Micron]]. Memory represented 49% of ASML's system sales in Q2 2026, with management projecting a 75% increase in memory-related net system sales for the full year. Because ASML holds a monopoly on EUV equipment, its margins have significant room to expand while the memory makers' margins may be near peak.

## Core Concepts

- **[[ASML Holding]]**: Dutch semiconductor equipment company; sole producer of [[EUV Lithography]] machines required for advanced chip manufacturing.
- **[[EUV Lithography]]**: Extreme ultraviolet lithography — the only commercially available technology for printing the most advanced semiconductor nodes; ASML holds a monopoly.
- **[[HBM (High Bandwidth Memory)]]**: High-capacity stacked memory architecture powering AI accelerators and data centers; primary growth driver for DRAM manufacturers in 2026.
- **[[Micron Technology]]**: Major US memory maker; reportedly sold out its entire 2026 [[HBM (High Bandwidth Memory)|HBM]] capacity, driving customers into multi-year supply agreements.
- **Memory Capex Cycle**: [[Deloitte]] estimates memory manufacturer capex rising ~67% to $97B in 2026, then ~50% further to $146B in 2027 — a sustained multi-year tailwind for equipment suppliers.
- **Margin Expansion Asymmetry**: ASML's current profit margins sit below those of memory makers, leaving more upside; Sandisk and Micron margins may already be near cyclical peaks.

## Key Takeaways

- **Monopoly Moat**: ASML is the only company that can supply EUV machines to chip fabs.
- **Memory Mix Surge**: Memory hit 49% of ASML system sales in Q2 2026, up sharply.
- **75% Revenue Growth**: Management projects 75% increase in memory net system sales for full-year 2026.
- **HBM Sold Out**: Micron's 2026 HBM capacity fully committed; multi-year agreements locked in.
- **Capex Runway**: $97B memory capex in 2026 → $146B in 2027 per Deloitte estimates.
- **Margin Upside**: ASML margins below memory peers today; monopoly pricing enables expansion.
- **Analyst Bullishness**: Analysts upgraded long-term earnings growth outlook after Q2 2026 results.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Picks and Shovels Investing]]**: Rather than betting on which memory maker wins the AI boom, owning the indispensable toolmaker (ASML) captures upside regardless of which producer leads — the equipment supplier profits from all customers' capex.
- **[[Monopoly Pricing Power]]**: A legal monopoly on EUV machines means ASML can expand margins as demand grows, unlike commodity memory makers who compete on price during downturns.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why might ASML outperform Sandisk and Micron as a play on the AI memory boom?
**A**: ASML holds a monopoly on EUV lithography machines, meaning every memory manufacturer must buy from it — it captures industry-wide capex growth regardless of which producer leads, and its margins have more room to expand.

**Q2**: What specific data points support the AI memory demand thesis for ASML in 2026?
**A**: Memory represented 49% of ASML's Q2 2026 system sales, management projects a 75% increase in memory-related net system sales for the full year, and Deloitte estimates memory capex rising ~67% to $97B in 2026 then ~50% to $146B in 2027.

**Q3**: How should an investor use this insight when building exposure to the AI memory theme?
**A**: Instead of (or in addition to) buying memory makers whose margins may be peaking, consider ASML as an upstream picks-and-shovels play that benefits from all producers' capex spending and has greater margin expansion potential from its monopoly position.
