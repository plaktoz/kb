## Summary

By midday on October 6, 2026, the [[S&P 500]] had climbed 0.85% to a fresh all-time high of 7,840. Falling oil prices eased inflation worries, and enthusiasm for AI picked up again. Nuclear power stocks led the day's movers after [[Constellation Energy]] announced a deal with [[Alphabet]]'s [[Google]], and [[Marvell Technology]]'s investor-day targets lifted semiconductor stocks.

## Core Concepts

- **Index levels (11:38 AM ET)**: The [[S&P 500]] was up 0.85% at 7,840, the [[Nasdaq Composite]] up 0.88% at 27,718, and the [[Dow Jones Industrial Average]] up 0.69% at 51,622.
- **Macro backdrop**: Falling [[Oil Prices]] eased [[Inflation]] concerns. The [[10-Year Treasury Yield]] fell 5 bp to 5.27%, and [[Gold]] rose 0.64%.
- **Sector rotation**: Healthcare was the only sector down. Real estate and industrials were notably strong.
- **Nuclear rally**: The [[Constellation Energy]]–[[Google]] deal lifted the whole [[Nuclear Energy]] group, including [[Vistra]] and [[Talen Energy]].
- **Semiconductor sentiment**: [[Marvell Technology]] jumped more than 7% on its investor-day revenue plans. [[Citi]] raised its price target on [[Advanced Micro Devices]], arguing that [[Agentic AI]] will drive major CPU demand.
- **AI infrastructure**: [[Dell Technologies]] rose after expanding its AI platform. [[CrowdStrike]] gained 3.78% on bullish analyst sentiment.
- **Memory weakness**: [[Sandisk]], [[Seagate Technology]] and [[Western Digital]] fell on reports that [[Toshiba]] plans to take a bigger share of the memory market.

## Key Takeaways

- **New record**: S&P 500 set intraday all-time high at 7,840.
- **Oil relief**: Falling oil prices eased inflation fears; 10-year yield fell to 5.27%.
- **Nuclear surge**: Constellation–Google deal lifted Vistra and Talen Energy.
- **Marvell pop**: Up 7%+ after investor-day revenue targets impressed analysts.
- **AMD call**: Citi raised its target on an agentic-AI CPU demand thesis.
- **Memory slip**: Sandisk, Seagate and Western Digital fell on Toshiba share-grab reports.
- **Lone laggard**: Healthcare was the only sector down that morning.
