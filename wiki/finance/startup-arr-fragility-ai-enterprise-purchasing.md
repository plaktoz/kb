## Summary

AI has disrupted traditional enterprise IT purchasing behavior, making startup ARR far less stable than in the classic SaaS era. Madrona VC research finds 77% of enterprises reevaluate AI vendors every six months or on a rolling basis — a "fast in, fast out" dynamic that erodes the switching-cost moats multi-year contracts once provided. Andreessen Horowitz research points to outcome-based pricing as a more economically defensible model for both vendors and buyers.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Annual Recurring Revenue]] (ARR)** — the core revenue metric for SaaS and AI startups, now increasingly fragile due to shortened vendor evaluation cycles
- **[[Enterprise AI Adoption]]** — IDC projects ~$4.25 trillion in enterprise tech spending for 2026, almost entirely AI-driven; yet fewer than half of AI pilots reach full production
- **[[Switching Costs]]** — traditionally created by multi-year SaaS contracts; AI era has substantially lowered these, accelerating churn risk
- **[[Outcome-Based Pricing]]** — [[Andreessen Horowitz]] research advocates pricing tied to measurable business outputs (tickets closed, reports processed, leads generated) rather than usage metrics like tokens
- **[[Madrona Venture Capital]]** — surveyed 150 enterprise IT professionals; coined "fast in, fast out" to describe the new vendor evaluation pattern
- **[[AI Pilot-to-Production Gap]]** — fewer than half of enterprise AI pilots reach full deployment; prior [[MIT]] research put failure rate at ~95% on ROI

## Key Takeaways

- **Vendor Re-evaluation Acceleration**: 77% of enterprises reassess AI vendors every 6 months or continuously.
- **ARR at Risk**: Startup ARR milestones (e.g. $10M in 3 months) obscure underlying contract fragility.
- **Moat Erosion**: AI's lower switching costs dismantle the multi-year contract moats that powered SaaS growth.
- **Outcome Pricing**: A16z finds most technical buyers prefer pricing tied to outcomes, not token usage.
- **Pilot Failure Rate**: <50% of AI pilots reach production — improvement from prior ~95% ROI failure rate.
- **Budget Growth vs. Execution Gap**: 74% plan to expand AI budgets, yet production deployment lags significantly.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Switching Cost Moat]]**: Classic SaaS compounded value through lock-in; AI commoditizes integration, collapsing the moat and exposing ARR to rapid churn — the structural advantage disappears when the underlying technology lowers friction.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: ARR growth became a celebrated benchmark metric; startups optimized for it via short-cycle pilot wins, masking the fragility of revenue that was never truly sticky.
