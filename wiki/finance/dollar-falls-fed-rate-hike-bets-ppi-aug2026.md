## Summary
The [[US Dollar Index]] slipped 0.1% to 99.94 after July [[Producer Price Index]] data came in softer than expected, pushing the market-implied odds of a September Fed rate hike down to about 34% and lifting rate-sensitive tech stocks and Treasury bonds.

## Core Concepts
- **[[PPI vs. CPI]]**: Producer prices, a leading indicator that feeds into future consumer prices, moderated alongside an already in-line CPI report, reinforcing a broader disinflation narrative across both wholesale and retail price gauges.
- **[[Fed Rate Hike Odds]]**: Via the CME FedWatch tool, odds of a September hike fell from a higher prior level to ~34%, with odds of a hold rising to ~66% — showing how real-time futures pricing translates single data releases into shifting policy expectations.
- **[[Currency-Bond-Equity Linkage]]**: Softer PPI simultaneously weakened the dollar, pulled Treasury yields down (10-year -6.1bps to 4.631%), and lifted tech stocks (+1.1% for S&P 500 tech sector) — illustrating how one data point ripples across asset classes via shared rate expectations.

## Key Takeaways
- **Headline PPI**: Flat M/M, +4.7% Y/Y in July, below expectations.
- **Core PPI**: +0.2% M/M, +4.2% Y/Y, below the +0.3% forecast.
- **Rate odds shift**: September hike probability fell to ~34%; hold probability rose to ~66%.
- **Cross-asset reaction**: Dollar index -0.1% to 99.94; 10-year yield -6.1bps; S&P 500 tech sector +1.1%.
- **Other data**: Initial jobless claims rose 9,000 to 209,000, above the 202,000 estimate.
