## Summary

[[SpaceX]] (SPCX) cannot join the [[S&P 500]] before June 2027 due to a mandatory 12-month seasoning requirement, and ongoing GAAP losses driven by massive AI data center capital expenditure may delay inclusion well beyond that date. S&P Dow Jones Indices rejected proposals in June 2026 to fast-track large IPOs, preserving three existing gatekeeping criteria: seasoning, GAAP profitability, and a minimum public float. The article argues that quarterly GAAP net income — not the calendar date — is the key metric investors should watch, and cautions against buying the stock purely in anticipation of index-forced buying.

## Core Concepts

- **[[S&P 500]] inclusion criteria**: Three hurdles — 12 months of exchange trading (seasoning), positive GAAP net income in the most recent quarter *and* across the trailing four quarters combined, and at least 10% public float.
- **Seasoning rule**: SpaceX's IPO locks it out until June 2027 at the earliest; S&P Dow Jones rejected attempts to waive this for megacap IPOs.
- **GAAP profitability problem**: SpaceX posted a $541 million net loss in Q2 2026; the trailing four-quarter combined loss exceeds $8 billion, inflated by a ~$4.3 billion Q1 loss (including ~$1.5 billion in one-time debt-retirement charges).
- **Depreciation & interest overhang**: $28.5 billion in H1 2026 capex (mainly [[AI]] data centers) is converting into rising D&A ($2.8 billion in Q2, up from $1.5 billion YoY); a $25 billion bond issuance adds $629 million in quarterly interest expense.
- **Float resolution**: ~328 million locked-up shares were set to become eligible around September 24, with more following by December — this criterion is largely resolving on schedule.
- **[[Forced index buying]]**: The anticipation that index funds must buy when a stock is added, often cited as a near-term price catalyst.

## Key Takeaways

- **June 2027 is the earliest possible date** SpaceX can qualify for the S&P 500.
- **The profitability bar is harder than the calendar bar**: trailing four-quarter GAAP income must turn positive.
- **Q2 2026 net loss: $541 million**; trailing four-quarter combined loss: >$8 billion.
- **Operating losses are improving**: Q2 operating loss was $143 million vs. $970 million a year prior.
- **Revenue growth is strong**: 92% YoY in Q2 2026; SpaceX was GAAP profitable in full-year 2024 ($791 million).
- **D&A doubled YoY** to $2.8 billion in Q2 — a structural drag from AI capex investments.
- **$25 billion bond issuance** adds $629 million/quarter in interest charges on top of capex-driven depreciation.
- **Author advises against buying for index-inclusion catalyst** — GAAP profitability is the real gating factor.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When "S&P 500 inclusion" becomes the investment thesis, traders optimize for that proxy rather than underlying business fundamentals — the article explicitly warns this is the wrong frame and that GAAP profitability is what actually matters.
- **[[Mean Reversion]]**: SpaceX was GAAP profitable in 2024; the current loss period is partly driven by lumpy, front-loaded capex that will eventually amortize, suggesting the business could return to profitability — but the timeline is uncertain, not guaranteed.
