## Summary

[[Anthropic]]'s anticipated October IPO could rival [[SpaceX]]'s June offering as one of the largest ever, with a potential valuation around $2 trillion. New "fast-track" index-inclusion rules — adopted by major index providers after SpaceX's IPO — could let Anthropic enter certain ETFs within days or weeks of going public rather than the typical months-long wait. Three ETFs are highlighted as likely near-term holders: Invesco QQQ, iShares Russell 1000 Growth (IWF), and First Trust U.S. Equity Opportunities (FPX).

## Core Concepts

- **[[Anthropic]] IPO**: Expected October debut, potentially valued around $2 trillion, comparable in scale to SpaceX's record-breaking June IPO.
- **Index Fast-Track Rules**: Major index providers (Nasdaq, FTSE Russell) introduced expedited inclusion rules after SpaceX's IPO to let sufficiently large newly public companies join key indexes quickly instead of waiting for the standard quarterly/annual reconstitution cycle.
- **Invesco QQQ ETF (QQQ)**: Tracks the Nasdaq-100. Under a new Nasdaq rule, a newly public company that would rank among the 40 largest Nasdaq-100 components can join the index after just 15 trading days.
- **iShares Russell 1000 Growth ETF (IWF)**: Tracks a Russell growth index. FTSE Russell's updated policy lets a qualifying new IPO that would land within the Russell Top 500 enter Russell U.S. Indexes after its fifth trading day, provided it's classified as a growth stock.
- **First Trust U.S. Equity Opportunities ETF (FPX)**: Tracks an index of roughly 100 large, liquid newly public U.S. companies, with eligibility lasting for a company's first 1,000 trading days — making it a more direct, sustained vehicle for IPO exposure than the fast-track index funds.

## Key Takeaways

- Anthropic's ~$2T IPO valuation could make it large enough to qualify for fast-track inclusion in major indexes almost immediately after listing.
- QQQ could add Anthropic within 15 trading days if it ranks among the Nasdaq-100's 40 largest components.
- IWF could add Anthropic within 5 trading days if it lands in the Russell Top 500 and is classified as a growth stock.
- FPX offers the longest-running IPO exposure (first 1,000 trading days) rather than a one-time fast-track entry.
- These fast-track rules exist largely because of precedent set by SpaceX's own record IPO earlier in 2026.
- Author's caveat: don't buy these funds solely to chase Anthropic exposure — they're a more measured, diversified way to gain indirect access without buying IPO-day shares directly.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Picks and Shovels Investing]]**: Rather than trying to secure IPO-day shares directly, broad-market and growth ETFs offer diversified, lower-risk indirect exposure to a single blockbuster listing.
- **Index Mechanics as Market Structure**: Passive index-inclusion rules are not neutral plumbing — they actively shape near-term demand and price action for a stock the moment fast-track eligibility is triggered, since index funds must buy to track their benchmark.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why were index providers' "fast-track" inclusion rules created?
**A**: They were adopted after SpaceX's record-breaking June IPO to let sufficiently large newly public companies enter major indexes within days or weeks rather than waiting for a standard reconstitution cycle months later.

**Q2**: What is the fastest path for Anthropic to enter a major index post-IPO, and via which ETF?
**A**: iShares Russell 1000 Growth ETF (IWF) — FTSE Russell's rule allows entry after just the fifth trading day if Anthropic lands in the Russell Top 500 and is classified as a growth stock.

**Q3**: How does FPX's approach to IPO exposure differ from QQQ and IWF?
**A**: FPX tracks an index of newly public companies with eligibility lasting their first 1,000 trading days, offering sustained IPO-stage exposure, whereas QQQ and IWF are one-time fast-track entries into broader indexes triggered by market-cap thresholds.
