## Summary

The [[Buffett Indicator]] — total US stock market cap divided by GDP — hit an all-time high of 236% in mid-2026, well above the dot-com peak. Combined with the [[Shiller CAPE Ratio]] at 41 (versus a historical average of ~17), multiple valuation metrics simultaneously signal extreme overvaluation. The article warns this doesn't predict an imminent crash but historically precedes periods of below-average returns.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Buffett Indicator]]** — market cap-to-GDP ratio; [[Warren Buffett]]'s preferred broad market valuation gauge
- **[[Shiller CAPE Ratio]]** — cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings ratio; smooths earnings over 10 years to reduce cycle noise
- **[[Market Overvaluation]]** — multiple simultaneous signals suggesting stocks are priced far above historical norms
- **[[Dot-com Parallel]]** — current valuations compared to the 1999-2000 peak before the 56% S&P 500 drawdown

## Key Takeaways

- **Buffett Indicator at 236%**: all-time high; previous peak was dot-com era ~200%
- **Shiller CAPE at 41**: roughly 2.4× the historical average of ~17
- **Not a timing tool**: high valuations can persist for years; signal is about long-term return expectations
- **Historical consequence**: S&P 500 fell 56% after dot-com peak when similar overvaluation signals appeared
- **Implication for [[CSPX]] / [[VWRA]]**: broad market ETF holders face elevated valuation risk
