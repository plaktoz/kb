## Summary

[[Alphabet]] has entered [[Berkshire Hathaway]]'s top five holdings, displacing [[Chevron]], after new CEO [[Greg Abel]] added 24.5 million shares in Q2 2026 — a 45% stake increase to ~79 million shares valued at ~$28.2 billion (9.41% of the portfolio). Despite Alphabet's recent ~7% monthly decline and its first-ever negative free cash flow quarter, Berkshire's continued accumulation signals conviction in Alphabet's long-term value at 16x earnings.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Greg Abel]]**: New Berkshire CEO continuing — and accelerating — Warren Buffett's original Alphabet bet; added 36M shares in Q1 and 24.5M in Q2 2026.
- **[[Berkshire Hathaway]] portfolio composition**: Top holdings ranked — [[Apple]] (22%), [[American Express]] (17%), [[Coca-Cola]] (11%), Alphabet (9.41%), with Chevron displaced.
- **[[AI Capex Cycle]]**: Alphabet raised CapEx guidance to $195–$205B and posted its first-ever negative FCF (-$5.8B); Berkshire's buy treats this as a temporary trough, not a structural problem.
- **Value investing at 16x earnings**: Alphabet's valuation is consistent with Berkshire's discipline; the article draws a parallel to [[Amazon]]'s earlier heavy-spending phase that ultimately paid off handsomely.
- **[[FCF Compression]]**: CFO [[Anat Ashkenazi]] acknowledged "free cash flow will remain under pressure" due to AI infrastructure investment — Berkshire appears to be betting this resolves as it did for Amazon.

## Key Takeaways

- Berkshire holds ~79M Alphabet shares worth ~$28.2B — now its fourth-largest position.
- Abel added 36M shares in Q1 and 24.5M in Q2; stake up 45% in two quarters.
- Alphabet Q2: revenue +24% YoY to $120B, operating income +30% to ~$41B.
- First-ever negative FCF quarter (-$5.8B) driven by massive AI infrastructure spend.
- CapEx guidance raised to $195–$205B; the Amazon parallel suggests payoff ahead.
- Stock trades at just 16x earnings after a ~7% monthly decline — cheap by big-tech standards.
- Apple remains far and away the largest holding at 22% of the portfolio.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[J-Curve Investing]]**: Berkshire's accumulation during Alphabet's negative-FCF quarter mirrors the classic J-curve bet — absorb the trough, capture the inflection. The Amazon analogy makes the thesis explicit.
- **[[Mr. Market]]**: Alphabet's ~7% monthly drop created the entry price Berkshire needed; the 16x earnings multiple reflects short-term market anxiety, not the underlying business trajectory.
