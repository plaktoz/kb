## Summary

[[Microsoft]] (MSFT) has returned only ~2% year-to-date in 2026 despite delivering strong fundamentals, because the stock's 27% spring sell-off and subsequent 30%+ recovery effectively reset gains for full-year holders. With shares now trading at 25x forward earnings — deemed "fairly priced" — the author argues the exceptional buying window has closed, though the stock should still outperform the broader market over the next year.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Microsoft]] (MSFT)** — current price ~$495.63; YTD gain ~2%; 52-week range $349.20–$553.72; shares fell 27% in April–June 2026 then recovered 30%+ from the lows
- **[[Azure]]** — surpassed $100 billion in annual revenue for the first time; grew 43% year-over-year in fiscal Q4 2026 (ended June 30)
- **[[Microsoft 365 Copilot]]** — reached 30 million paid seats, a key AI monetization milestone
- **[[Forward P/E Ratio]]** — stock trades at 25x forward earnings, characterized as average for large-cap tech; re-rating to 30x "wouldn't be unreasonable" but is not a near-term certainty
- **[[Valuation Timing]]** — the discount P/E during the spring sell-off represented the real entry point; that window has closed

## Key Takeaways

- **YTD flat despite growth**: MSFT up only ~2% YTD; spring 27% drawdown cancelled full-year gains.
- **Azure milestone**: $100B annual run-rate crossed; +43% YoY growth in Q4 FY2026.
- **Copilot scale**: 30M paid seats signals enterprise AI monetization is real.
- **Fair valuation now**: 25x forward earnings = average for large-cap tech; no longer discounted.
- **Buying window closed**: "The time to buy was a few months ago."
- **Still a market-beater**: >10% growth rate supports outperforming the S&P 500 over the next year.
- **Author and Motley Fool hold MSFT positions** (conflict of interest disclosure).

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Mr. Market]]**: The spring sell-off created a temporary mispricing — Mr. Market offered MSFT at a discounted P/E that did not reflect the underlying business trajectory. Those who recognized the disconnect and bought captured the excess return; latecomers paid fair value.
- **[[Margin of Safety]]**: The 27% drawdown briefly provided the margin of safety that value investors require. At 25x forward earnings, that cushion is gone — expected returns revert to roughly in-line with earnings growth.
