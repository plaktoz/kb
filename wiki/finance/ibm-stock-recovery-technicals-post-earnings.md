## Summary

[[IBM]] shares rose 5.62% on July 28 to $226.90, recovering from a post-earnings low of $201.60 after the worst single-day decline in company history (-25.2% on July 14) and a further Q2 confirmation drop. The July 22 full Q2 report confirmed revenue of $17.16 billion (+1% YoY), EPS of $2.93, 5% software growth offsetting a 42% mainframe decline, and FY2026 guidance of 4-5% constant-currency growth, but an ongoing securities fraud investigation into IBM's pre-warning disclosures remains an overhang limiting how far the stock can recover. The stock's next technical test is the 50-period EMA at $233.20, with RSI near 65 approaching overbought territory.

## Core Concepts

- [[IBM]] stock technical recovery — bounced from $201.60 to $226.90 with higher highs and higher lows
- [[Exponential Moving Average]] (50 EMA) — resistance at $233.20, the key level needed to confirm the rally is more than a dead cat bounce; 200 EMA resistance at $249.70
- [[Relative Strength Index]] (RSI) — near 65, approaching overbought after a deep oversold recovery
- [[Securities Fraud Investigation]] — ongoing probe into whether IBM misled the market about deal-pipeline strength ahead of the July 14 warning; no charges filed yet
- [[IBM Q2 2026 Results]] — Revenue $17.16B (+1% YoY), Adj. EPS $2.93, Software +5% to $7.76B, Mainframe -42%, Hybrid Cloud (Red Hat OpenShift) +11%
- [[Quantum Chip Foundry]] — IBM signed a letter of intent during Q2 to build a US quantum chip foundry, building on $10B+ of cumulative quantum investment

## Key Takeaways

- IBM rose 5.62% Monday to $226.90, up from a post-earnings low of $201.60, still -26% from July's $306 peak
- July 14 drop of 25.2% was IBM's worst single-day decline since 1968, worse than Black Monday 1987
- Full Q2 confirmed preliminary numbers: revenue $17.16B, EPS $2.93, software +5%, mainframe -42%
- FY2026 guidance: 4-5% constant-currency revenue growth, +$1B FCF YoY, +100bps operating margin
- Immediate resistance is the 50 EMA at $233.20; a close above it would signal more than a dead-cat bounce
- Securities fraud investigation remains unresolved and caps recovery until concluded
- Quantum chip foundry letter of intent is a non-binding commitment with negligible near-term revenue impact

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Dead Cat Bounce]]**: The article explicitly frames the 50 EMA test as the line between a genuine recovery and a dead-cat bounce — a sharp snapback after a crash that fails to hold and rolls over again.
