## Summary

Investing.com says [[Nvidia]] is down about 29% in a month on a sector rout, capex worries and the CEO leadership shock, while fundamentals remain strong with revenue growth accelerating and a forward P/E near 17.8x. Technicals still read Strong Sell and oversold can stay oversold. Earnings on Nov 17 are a binary event, so entry timing is a risk-tolerance call.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nvidia]]**: $161.76, market cap about $3.91T, 52-week range $148.80-$243.37, daily RSI 24.6.
- **[[Jensen Huang]]**: His exit triggered downgrades from Oppenheimer and Bernstein; Stifel cut its target to $205.
- **[[Multiple Compression]]**: Consensus estimates barely moved, so the sell-off is a valuation de-rating, not an earnings downgrade.
- **[[Technical Analysis]]**: All 12 moving averages point down; ADX 41.5 confirms a strong downtrend.
- **[[Governance Risk]]**: Leadership shock is framed as governance risk rather than earnings risk.
- **[[Earnings Catalyst]]**: Report due Nov 17 after hours; consensus EPS $2.47, revenue $108.8B.

## Key Takeaways

- **Drawdown**: Down about 29% over the past month, losing roughly $630B without negative company news.
- **Fundamentals**: Revenue growth accelerated from 62.5% to 105.9%; gross margin steady near 75%.
- **Valuation**: Forward P/E 17.8x; InvestingPro fair value $251.74 implies 55.7% upside.
- **Technicals**: Strong Sell; support at $154.34 then $148.80, resistance at $172.58.
- **Catalyst**: A beat plus calm leadership commentary could spark a sharp rebound.
- **Bear Risks**: Unresolved CEO transition, regulation, capex doubts and leveraged single-stock ETFs.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. A price drop without estimate cuts is a change in multiple, not in business value** — When consensus earnings barely move while the price falls, the market is repricing sentiment, which separates the business from the headlines.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Is Nvidia a falling knife or a buy-the-dip opportunity, according to the article?
**A**: It is a fundamentally strong company in a technically broken trend with a binary event four days away. The valuation case is real, but staged entry versus waiting for confirmation is a risk-tolerance call.

**Q2**: What evidence shows the sell-off is not driven by earnings?
**A**: Consensus estimates barely moved over 30 days, revenue growth accelerated to 105.9%, and the last four quarters all beat EPS estimates. The result is multiple compression to about 17.8x forward earnings.

**Q3**: How should an investor think about the Nov 17 earnings?
**A**: A beat with calm leadership commentary could spark a sharp rebound, while a guidance wobble in a broken trend could add pressure. Waiting for a reclaim of the pivot levels is the confirmation alternative.
