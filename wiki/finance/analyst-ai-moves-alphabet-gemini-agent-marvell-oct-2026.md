## Summary

[[BMO Capital Markets]] reiterated [[Alphabet]] as a Top Pick after the launch of [[Gemini Agent]], arguing full-stack AI ownership and a 98% drop in cost per token since 2024 position it to scale enterprise agents. [[Citi]] grew "increasingly selective" on chips after the [[SOX Index]] rebound, naming AMD, Texas Instruments, Lam Research, Teradyne and Synopsys as top picks while downgrading [[NXP Semiconductors]] on limited data center exposure. Wall Street raised [[Marvell Technology]] targets after a fiscal 2031 revenue framework of $70-90 billion, and [[Truist Securities]] initiated [[IBM]] at Hold, citing near-term execution risk.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Gemini Agent]]** — Alphabet's unified enterprise AI agent, competing with Meta's [[Muse]] and OpenAI's [[Dots]]; integrates with Microsoft Office, Salesforce and ServiceNow
- **[[Full-Stack AI]]** — BMO argues Alphabet's ownership across the AI stack lets it capture optimizations at every layer
- **[[Cost per Token]]** — down 98% since 2024; expert calls suggest token prices may bottom around 2030, after which closed-source providers may raise prices
- **Citi "Phase 2" semiconductor playbook** — [[Atif Malik]] cites data center demand (~40% of the chip market) and auto/industrial recovery (~20%), with PC and smartphone weak
- **[[Marvell Technology]] analyst day** — fiscal 2031 framework at $80B midpoint (~10x fiscal 2026), EPS above $30, connectivity-led growth
- **[[NXP Semiconductors]] downgrade** — just 3-4% data center exposure versus 56% automotive revenue
- **[[IBM]] initiation** — Truist's [[Arvind Ramnani]] asks when enterprise AI infrastructure spending will translate into IBM demand

## Key Takeaways

- **Google Cloud AI adoption**: ~80% of Google Cloud customers already use Google's AI products.
- **Token scale**: ~500 enterprise customers each processed over 1 trillion tokens last year.
- **Cost per token**: Fell 98% since 2024 while AI revenue soared.
- **Citi chip picks**: AMD, Texas Instruments, Lam Research, Teradyne, Synopsys ahead of Q3.
- **Marvell outlook raised**: Fiscal 2028 revenue raised to ~$20B from $18B.
- **Marvell de-risking**: Custom accelerators ~19% of 2030 target versus 40%+ previously.
- **Marvell targets**: Jefferies $450, Stifel $370, B. Riley $365, TD Cowen $350.
- **NXP cut to Neutral**: Target lowered to $260 from $370 at 14x 2028 P/E.
- **Other Citi downgrades**: Universal Display to Sell ($71); Nova to Neutral ($415).
- **IBM at Hold**: $240 target; Q2 miss called execution and timing, not structural.
- **IBM software mix**: 45% of revenue, 61% of segment pre-tax income, ~80% recurring.
- **IBM multiple compression**: Shares fell 20%+; forward P/E dropped from 22x to 16x.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Owning every layer captures every layer's gains** — A firm that controls the full stack benefits from optimizations wherever they occur, rather than ceding them to suppliers or partners. · *model:* [[Vertical Integration]]
→ explains: Cost per token, Google Cloud AI adoption

**T2. Unit costs fall as infrastructure scales** — Cost-to-serve declines as volumes grow, hardware improves, and optimizations accumulate, letting revenue rise even as per-unit prices drop. · *model:* [[Economies of Scale]]
→ explains: Cost per token, Token scale

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did BMO reiterate Alphabet as a Top Pick?
**A**: BMO sees Alphabet as well-positioned to scale AI agents across the enterprise after the Gemini Agent launch, given workflow integrations and its full-stack AI leadership, which helps offset fears it is losing its edge.

**Q2**: Why did Citi downgrade NXP Semiconductors?
**A**: Citi believes the analog upturn is more than halfway through and further upside depends on data center buildouts, where NXP has just 3-4% of sales, while 56% of revenue is tied to subdued automotive demand.

**Q3**: What would make Truist more constructive on IBM?
**A**: Deferred contracts closing without meaningful discounts, sustained improvement in Transaction Processing growth, and stronger organic (not acquisition-driven) software growth.
