## Summary

The [[Russell 2000]] index hit a record high above 3,000 on August 5, 2026, with small caps' 20.7% year-to-date return quietly beating both the [[S&P 500]]'s 12.8% gain and the "Magnificent Seven." Analysts attribute the rotation to small caps' lower valuations and improving fundamentals, though the trade remains fragile to rate and growth shocks.

## Core Concepts

- [[Russell 2000]] — the small-cap benchmark that touched a record high, up 20.7% year-to-date, outperforming large caps
- [[Valuation Multiple Compression]] — the iShares Russell 2000 ETF (IWM) trades at 18x earnings versus the SPDR S&P 500 ETF's (SPY) 27x, framing small caps as a cheaper entry to the AI boom
- [[Market Rotation]] — capital moving from top-heavy, AI-dominated large caps into a broader, previously overlooked part of the market
- [[Sector Rotation Risk]] — small caps' greater sensitivity to interest rates and economic growth could reverse the trade quickly if conditions shift

## Key Takeaways

- **Record high**: Russell 2000 surpassed 3,000 for the first time on August 5, 2026.
- **Outperformance**: Small caps returned 20.7% YTD vs. 12.8% for the S&P 500, also beating the Magnificent Seven.
- **Valuation gap**: IWM trades at 18x earnings vs. SPY's 27x, per Yahoo Finance AlphaSpace data.
- **Bull case**: RBC's Lori Calvasina cites below-average valuations, a stronger bottom-up earnings outlook, and tailwinds like reaccelerating job growth and manufacturing activity.
- **Risk factors**: Greater rate sensitivity and potential drag from the Iran conflict on growth could interrupt the trade; recent months already show choppy performance versus the S&P 500.
- **Watch item**: A pickup in M&A activity could be the next catalyst for renewed small-cap excitement, per Calvasina.
