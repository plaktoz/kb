## Summary

After adjusting for capex, Microsoft trades at ~59× trailing free cash flow while Alphabet trades at ~80× — making Microsoft cheaper on the metric that matters most for capital-heavy AI cloud businesses. Alphabet's Google Cloud grew 82% YoY with a 35.6% operating margin, but a $98B unrealized investment gain in its net income inflates reported earnings and flatters its apparent cheapness. For Alphabet to justify its premium, it must grow consolidated free cash flow ~37% above its trailing level without proportionally higher capex or ad revenue damage.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Microsoft Azure]]**: Azure grew 43% in fiscal Q4 2026; Microsoft's total FY2026 FCF was ~$66.99B after $115.95B in property and equipment additions.
- **[[Google Cloud]]**: Revenue of $24.77B in the June quarter (+82% YoY) with $8.81B operating income (35.6% margin); Alphabet's trailing FCF ~$53.27B.
- **[[Free Cash Flow Yield]]**: Operating cash flow minus cash capex — the article's preferred valuation metric; excludes finance-lease payments and depreciation timing distortions.
- **[[Price-to-Free-Cash-Flow]]**: The key relative multiple used: MSFT at ~59×, GOOGL at ~80×; higher multiple requires higher future FCF growth to justify.
- **[[AI Capital Expenditure Cycle]]**: Both companies face sustained heavy investment; Microsoft paid $115.95B in property/equipment plus $3.10B in finance-lease principal in FY2026, leaving meaningful downside risk if capex rises while FCF stalls.

## Key Takeaways

- **FCF multiple gap**: Microsoft ~59× vs. Alphabet ~80× trailing FCF — a meaningful valuation discount for MSFT.
- **Alphabet's earnings mirage**: $98B net gain in Q2 from unrealized investment gains inflates reported earnings; FCF is the cleaner measure.
- **Google Cloud growth**: 82% YoY revenue growth and 35.6% operating margin support the bull case for operating leverage at scale.
- **Microsoft FY2026 FCF**: $182.94B operating cash flow less $115.95B capex = $66.99B; plus $3.10B finance-lease payments excluded.
- **Alphabet FCF hurdle**: Needs ~$73B annual FCF (+37% above trailing) to match Microsoft's current cash multiple at unchanged equity values.
- **Bear cases**: MSFT loses edge if capex keeps rising while FCF stalls; GOOGL loses edge if cloud expansion damages advertising economics.
- **Institutional sentiment**: Alphabet saw holder count rise to 275 (from 265); Microsoft's fell to 273 (from 282) in Q2 2026.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about the relative valuation of Microsoft vs. Alphabet?
**A**: On a trailing free-cash-flow multiple basis, Microsoft (~59×) is materially cheaper than Alphabet (~80×), making it the more attractive purchase even though Alphabet's cloud segment is growing faster.

**Q2**: Why does the article dismiss Alphabet's apparent cheapness on reported earnings?
**A**: Alphabet's June-quarter net income included a $98B unrealized investment gain — a non-cash figure unrelated to cloud or advertising operations — which artificially depresses its P/E ratio and misrepresents its true cash profitability.

**Q3**: How can an investor use the $73B FCF hurdle to evaluate Alphabet?
**A**: The ~$73B figure (~37% above Alphabet's trailing $53.27B FCF) is the level Alphabet must reach to trade at the same cash multiple as Microsoft today; if an investor believes that growth is achievable without proportional capex increases or ad revenue damage, Alphabet becomes the better buy.
