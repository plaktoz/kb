## Summary

The financial sector broke out to record highs in July 2026, driven by strong earnings, fading private-credit concerns, and improving capital-markets activity — attracting investors rotating out of previously dominant chip and AI-hyperscaler stocks. Despite the rally, financials still trade at a more than 30% discount to the broader market and rank among the cheapest S&P 500 sectors on forward earnings, suggesting room for the outperformance to continue.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Sector Rotation]]**: Capital moving from previously dominant sectors (semiconductors, AI hyperscalers) into undervalued sectors (financials) as relative performance and risk perception shift.
- **[[Financial Select Sector SPDR ETF]] (XLF)**: Gained 6.2% in July 2026 alone — its best month since January 2025 — reaching a record closing high on July 28, even though its 2026 year-to-date gain (4%) still lagged the S&P 500's 9.4%.
- **[[Valuation Discount Despite Rally]]**: Financials remain the ninth-cheapest of 11 S&P 500 sectors on forward P/E (FactSet) and trade at a 30%+ discount to the broader market, even after the breakout.
- **[[Relative Strength Breakout]]**: XLF broke out of a 15-month downtrend relative to the S&P 500, and separately broke a long downtrend against the semiconductor index (SOX) — both technical signals cited as evidence the rotation into financials may persist.

## Key Takeaways

- **XLF hit a record closing high on July 28, 2026**, following a 6.2% July rally, its best month in over a year.
- **The bank subsector (KBE) rallied 9.7% over two months** to a record high, easing fears that prior bank underperformance signaled broader systemic risk.
- **Financials saw the largest year-over-year revenue increase of any sector in July** earnings (per FactSet analyst John Butters), reinforcing the rally is earnings-driven, not just multiple expansion.
- **The sector still trades at a 30%+ discount to the broader market**, according to State Street's Michael Arone, suggesting the rally hasn't been fully priced in yet.
- **Upcoming catalysts**: the July jobs report and ongoing manufacturing PMI data (expected to show a seventh straight month of expansion) could reinforce or challenge the rotation thesis.
