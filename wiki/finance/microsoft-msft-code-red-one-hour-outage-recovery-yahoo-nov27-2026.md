## Summary
[[Microsoft]] shares rose 0.26% to $491.18 on November 27 as investors weighed an internal "Code Red" push to resolve future global incidents within an hour. The goal follows a nearly 24-hour [[Microsoft 365 Outage]] on November 12-13 that Microsoft does not yet meet. The article sees limited market damage so far but real commercial risk to [[Copilot]] pricing.

## Core Concepts
- [[Microsoft]] ([[MSFT]]): Shares at $491.18, up 0.26% in the shortened Friday session.
- [[Code Red]]: Memos reviewed by Reuters set a one-hour resolution goal for global incidents.
- [[Jason Henderson]]: Microsoft 365 Core president, acknowledged the goal is not yet met.
- [[Microsoft 365 Outage]]: Hit Exchange Online, Outlook, Teams and Copilot; traced to a July networking gateway fix and lock contention.
- [[Satya Nadella]]: Apologized for the outage.
- [[GuruFocus GF Value]]: $606.32 fair value implies a 19.0% discount.
- [[Copilot]]: Sold on top of Microsoft 365, so base reliability affects its pricing case.

## Key Takeaways
- **Outage Scale**: Nearly 24 hours; more than 80% of servers unresponsive at worst.
- **Outage Reports**: About 130,000 reports across 74 countries at the peak.
- **Root Cause**: A July gateway server fix plus lock contention in a Windows process.
- **Recovery Goal**: Resolve future global incidents within one hour, not yet achieved.
- **Valuation Signal**: GF Score 100/100; GF Value implies a 19.0% discount.
- **Commercial Risk**: Downtime weakens the case for charging enterprises more per seat.
- **Next Test**: Final post-incident report and next quarter's commercial bookings.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Add-on pricing power depends on base-product reliability** — A premium layer sold on top of a core product can only command higher prices if the core stays dependable. Each outage erodes the justification for charging more.
→ explains: Commercial Risk, Recovery Goal

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the "Code Red" effort at Microsoft?
**A**: An internal push, per memos reviewed by Reuters, to resolve future global Microsoft 365 incidents within an hour, a goal the company does not yet meet.

**Q2**: What caused the November 12-13 outage and how bad was it?
**A**: A July fix for networking gateway servers and lock contention in an internal Windows process left over 80% of servers unresponsive at the worst point. Reports peaked near 130,000 across 74 countries.

**Q3**: Why does the outage matter commercially even though the stock barely moved?
**A**: Copilot is sold on top of Microsoft 365, so every hour of downtime weakens the case for charging enterprises more per seat. The next tests are the final incident report and commercial bookings next quarter.
