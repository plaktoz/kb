## Summary

On October 30, 2026 the Nasdaq-100 fell 3.46% and touched 10.0% below its October 6 record intraday, but closed 9.1% below it and so never finished a day in correction. Intel's 28.6% plunge on weak guidance led a 7.2% drop in semiconductors, and investors are now questioning whether hundreds of billions in AI data center spending will pay off. Earlier 2026 breaks of this kind reversed into new records, but a close below 28,093.24 would end that pattern.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nasdaq-100]] and [[Invesco QQQ Trust]]**: Index fell to 28,384.85 and QQQ closed at $690.49; the Composite hit its lowest close since July 31.
- **[[Market Correction]]**: Defined as a 10% drop from a record, measured on closing prices rather than intraday lows.
- **[[Intel]]**: Fell 28.6% after guiding Q4 revenue to $15-16 billion and gross margin to 38% versus 44.1%.
- **[[Philadelphia Semiconductor Index]]**: Fell 7.2%, its worst day since June 23, and 15.4% for October.
- **[[VIX]]**: Hit 23.93 intraday but closed at 19.41, signaling forced selling eased.
- **[[AI Capex]]**: [[Amazon]] expects roughly $220 billion of 2026 capital spending; [[NVIDIA]] and [[Apple]] swapped places as the most valuable company.
- **[[Equal-Weight Index Funds]]**: Offered as a way to spread semiconductor concentration risk.

## Key Takeaways

- **Close vs Intraday**: Correction is a closing-price definition; the index recovered before the bell.
- **Intel Guidance Shock**: Q4 revenue guide below the quarter just reported triggered a 28.6% drop.
- **Chip Damage**: Semiconductor index fell 7.2%; QQQ holders absorb it through NVIDIA and peers.
- **Show-Me Phase**: Market moving from AI "hopes and dreams" to demanding proof of payback.
- **Megacap Rotation**: Apple overtook NVIDIA at $5.03 trillion, hinting at flows toward less data-center-dependent names.
- **Prior Recoveries**: Two earlier 2026 drops over 10% both ended in new records.
- **Next Catalysts**: Apple earnings, midterms, November 6 jobs report, and NVIDIA's mid-November report.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Prices reward proven payback, not hopes** — When investors shift from expecting returns on capital to requiring evidence, any guidance that undershoots expectations is punished disproportionately. · *model:* [[Expectations Investing]]
→ explains: Intel Guidance Shock, Show-Me Phase

**T2. Concentrated exposure transmits a sector shock directly to holders** — A fund heavy in one sector takes that sector's losses in full, while equal weighting dilutes the damage. · *model:* [[Concentration Risk]]
→ explains: Chip Damage, Megacap Rotation

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Did the Nasdaq-100 enter a correction on October 30, 2026?
**A**: Only intraday: it hit 10.0% below its record but closed 9.1% below, so it never finished a day in correction. The label is based on closing prices.

**Q2**: What evidence showed semiconductors drove the selloff?
**A**: Intel fell 28.6% on weak Q4 guidance and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 7.2%, its worst day since June 23. QQQ owners feel this directly because NVIDIA and other chipmakers are top holdings.

**Q3**: What level should investors watch next, and why?
**A**: A close below the October 30 intraday low of 28,093.24 would put the index in correction on a closing basis. It would also break the pattern of quick recoveries seen earlier in 2026.
