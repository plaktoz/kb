## Summary

[[BigBear.ai]] (BBAI) and [[IonQ]] (IONQ) represent two distinct bets in emerging tech — AI/ML decision support for government versus cloud-accessible quantum computing. IonQ's revenue surged from a lower baseline to more than double BigBear.ai's quarterly revenue by Q2 2026, driven by landmark deals and platform launches. BigBear.ai suffered from federal budget cuts but showed modest recovery through its Ask Sage acquisition.

## Core Concepts

- **[[BigBear.ai]] (BBAI)**: AI/ML decision-support tools focused on U.S. government clients; gained Dutch regulatory approval for airport security software and launched a generative AI platform for classified defense environments.
- **[[IonQ]] (IONQ)**: Builds and sells access to quantum computers via direct and cloud channels; finalized [[SkyWater Technology]] acquisition, launched a sixth-generation quantum platform, and partnered with [[Nvidia]] to build large-scale quantum supercomputers.
- **[[Ask Sage]] acquisition**: BigBear.ai's strategic buy credited with contributing to its ~13% year-over-year revenue growth in Q2 2026.
- **[[Quantum Computing]] commercialization**: IonQ's trajectory illustrates accelerating revenue as quantum hardware and cloud access matures toward enterprise adoption.
- **Federal budget exposure**: BigBear.ai's government-dependent revenue base made it vulnerable to U.S. federal spending cuts.

## Key Takeaways

- **Revenue crossover**: IONQ overtook BBAI in quarterly revenue by Q3 2025 and reached $80M vs. $36.7M in Q2 2026.
- **BBAI decline**: Revenue fell from $43.8M (Q4 2024) to $27.3M (Q4 2025) before recovering modestly.
- **IONQ acceleration**: Revenue grew from $7.6M (Q1 2025) to $80M (Q2 2026) — roughly 10x in five quarters.
- **IONQ catalysts**: SkyWater acquisition, sixth-gen platform, and Nvidia partnership drove rapid scaling.
- **BBAI recovery lever**: Ask Sage acquisition helped reverse the decline with ~13% YoY growth in Q2 2026.
- **Business model contrast**: BBAI is government-contract-heavy; IONQ sells hardware and cloud access across sectors.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Asymmetric Growth]]**: IonQ's base effect — starting from near-zero commercial revenue — allowed exponential-looking growth, while BigBear.ai's established but stagnant government contracts created a ceiling rather than a launchpad.
- **[[Single Customer Concentration Risk]]**: BigBear.ai's heavy reliance on U.S. federal clients exposed it directly to discretionary budget cuts, illustrating why customer diversification is a structural moat for revenue stability.
