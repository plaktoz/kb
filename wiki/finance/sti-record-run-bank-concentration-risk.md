## Summary

The Straits Times Index's 24% year-to-date return (outpacing Asia-Pacific and global equities) masks that about 57% of the index's weight sits in just three local banks — DBS, OCBC, and UOB — far more concentrated than the S&P 500, where the top three names carry barely 20% of the weight. The commentary argues this concentration gives investors an incomplete picture of Singapore's broader market of property, tech, manufacturing, and biotech companies, and questions whether current index weightings should be reconsidered.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Straits Times Index]] (STI)** concentration — the flagship 30-company SGX benchmark, with banks carrying disproportionate influence over headline moves.
- **[[DBS Group]], [[OCBC]], [[UOB]]** — the three banks driving ~57% of STI weight; DBS market cap passed S$200B with shares up over a third this year, OCBC up nearly 60%, UOB up about 17%.
- **[[MAS Equity Market Development Programme]]** — a S$5B (later expanded to S$6.5B) initiative launched in 2025 to broaden investor participation beyond blue chips and revive the SGX listings pipeline.
- **Benchmarked-fund flows** — STI ETF AUM exceeded S$5B in June with 15 consecutive months of net inflows, mechanically funneling capital into the three dominant banks by construction.
- **Index depth vs. surface momentum** — the argument that a "better scoreboard" requires real representation from non-bank sectors, not just a rising headline number.

## Key Takeaways

- **STI generated a 24% YTD total return**, beating Asia-Pacific (19.4%) and global equities (13.8%).
- **~57% of STI weight is in three banks**, versus ~20% for the top three S&P 500 companies.
- **DBS crossed S$200B market cap**; OCBC and UOB also posted strong 2026 gains.
- **STI ETF inflows extended to a 15th straight month**, reinforcing bank concentration by mechanically directing new capital.
- **IPO market has "stuttered and stumbled"** despite MAS's market-development efforts, limiting depth beyond the incumbent blue chips.
- **The author calls for revisiting index weightings** so the STI better reflects Singapore's broader economy (property, tech, manufacturing, biotech).

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Survivorship Bias]] / composition bias**: A rising headline index number can conceal that the gain is concentrated in a handful of constituents, so the index's "story" (a booming market) diverges from the underlying reality (a booming banking sector alone).

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this commentary?
**A**: The STI's strong 2026 headline performance is misleading because roughly 57% of its weight sits in just three banks, so the index overstates the health and breadth of Singapore's broader stock market.

**Q2**: What specific data shows the concentration problem?
**A**: DBS, OCBC, and UOB together make up about 57% of the STI's weight, compared to roughly 20% for the top three companies in the S&P 500, and STI ETF inflows have extended for 15 straight months, further reinforcing that concentration.

**Q3**: What does the author suggest should change as a result?
**A**: Index architects should reconsider current STI weightings to give real representation to non-bank sectors like property, tech, manufacturing, and biotech, so the benchmark reflects the market's actual depth rather than just bank-driven momentum.
