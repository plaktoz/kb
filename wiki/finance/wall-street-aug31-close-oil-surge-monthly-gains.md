## Summary

U.S. stocks fell on August 31 as surging crude oil prices — driven by Strait of Hormuz hostilities — reignited inflation fears and reinforced expectations of a September rate hike. Despite the day's sell-off, all three major indexes posted August gains, with the Nasdaq leading monthly percentage growth and the Dow recording its fifth consecutive monthly advance. Markets are now pricing a greater-than-65% probability of a 25-basis-point hike, following a hawkish Jackson Hole address by Fed Chair [[Kevin Warsh]].

- **[[S&P 500]]** daily decline and monthly performance context
- **[[Strait of Hormuz]]** closure driving oil-price spike and inflation anxiety
- **[[Kevin Warsh]]** / [[Federal Reserve]] hawkish forward guidance at [[Jackson Hole Symposium]]
- **CME FedWatch** rate-hike probability as a real-time market sentiment gauge
- **[[Energy sector]]** outperformance vs. [[Utilities sector]] underperformance
- **PG&E (PCG)** wildfire liability amendment triggering largest single-day drop in six years

## Key Takeaways

- **Dow**: -374 pts (-0.70%) to 53,185; **S&P 500**: -25.6 pts (-0.33%); **Nasdaq**: -31.5 pts (-0.12%)
- All three indexes logged positive August returns; Dow up five consecutive months.
- Oil surge tied to [[Strait of Hormuz]] closure and ongoing Middle East hostilities.
- **>65% probability** of 25 bp September rate hike priced in via CME FedWatch.
- Warsh delivered hawkish tone at Jackson Hole the prior Friday.
- [[Iran]]'s President Pezeshkian signaled openness to a negotiated resolution.
- **PG&E** fell 20.1% on wildfire liability amendment — largest drop in six-plus years.
- **Halliburton** and **Valero Energy** each rose ~1.9%; **GameStop** up 2.9% on debt exchange update.
- NYSE breadth: decliners outpaced advancers ~1.95-to-1.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Fear and Greed Cycle]]**: A single macro shock (oil spike) reversed intraday sentiment even as underlying monthly data remained constructive — illustrating how short-term fear dominates rational trend assessment.
- **[[Reflexivity]]**: Higher oil prices → inflation fears → rate-hike bets → equity sell-off → which itself becomes a signal amplifying those same fears, a self-reinforcing loop [[George Soros]] described in financial markets.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What was the central tension in U.S. markets on August 31, 2026?
**A**: Stocks fell on inflation fears from surging oil prices even as all three major indexes were on track to finish August with gains — a day-end sell-off against a positive monthly backdrop.

**Q2**: Why did markets price in a >65% probability of a September rate hike?
**A**: Fed Chair Kevin Warsh struck a hawkish tone at Jackson Hole the prior Friday, and ongoing Strait of Hormuz hostilities kept oil prices elevated, reinforcing concerns that inflation was not yet contained.

**Q3**: How should an investor interpret the coexistence of daily losses and monthly gains?
**A**: The monthly gains signal durable underlying momentum, while the day's sell-off reflects a near-term macro shock; distinguishing short-term noise from longer trend is key to avoiding reactive decision-making.
