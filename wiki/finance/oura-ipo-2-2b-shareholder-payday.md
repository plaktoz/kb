## Summary

Oura is going public with an IPO targeting up to $2.2B, offering 50 million shares priced between $40–$44, but roughly two-thirds of those shares are being sold by existing shareholders rather than the company itself. At the midpoint price of $42, Oura collects only ~$567M while shareholders — led by Forerunner Ventures liquidating its entire 9.3% stake — pocket ~$1.53B. The bulk of Oura's own proceeds (~$526.4M of ~$532.6M) will go toward employee share-grant tax obligations, leaving just ~$6.2M for general corporate purposes.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Oura Ring]]**: Smart-ring wearable company going public; hardware-first with a fast-growing subscription membership segment.
- **[[Secondary Offering]]**: Most IPO shares are sold by existing investors, not the company — diluting the "raising capital" narrative of the offering.
- **[[Forerunner Ventures]]**: Lead VC exiting its entire ~9.3% stake (~28.7M shares) for ~$1.2B; originally invested in the 2020 Series B.
- **[[IPO Proceeds Allocation]]**: Oura's proceeds are almost entirely consumed by [[Restricted Stock Unit]] (RSU) tax withholding obligations, a common but underappreciated structural drag at IPO.
- **[[Wearables Market]]**: Hardware ($974M revenue) is dominant, but the subscription membership segment (89% gross margin, $240.5M, ~20% of sales) is the high-value growth vector.
- **[[Venture Capital Exit]]**: Forerunner's liquidation illustrates how late-stage VCs use IPOs as structured exit events regardless of the company's own capital needs.

## Key Takeaways

- Oura targets up to $2.2B IPO; shares priced $40–$44.
- ~36.5M of 50M shares sold by existing shareholders, not Oura.
- Forerunner Ventures exits full 9.3% stake for ~$1.2B.
- Oura nets only ~$567M; ~$526.4M earmarked for RSU tax withholding.
- Just ~$6.2M of proceeds available for general corporate use.
- Membership segment: 89% gross margin, revenue doubled to $240.5M.
- Hardware revenue: $974M — still ~80% of total sales.
- Projected ~5.7M paying members by fiscal year-end (~2x YoY).
- Market cap at top of range: $14.1B (up from $11B private valuation, Oct 2025).
- Company not taking on debt; $372M cash reserves left untouched.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Principal-Agent Problem]]**: Existing shareholders (agents) structure the IPO primarily to extract value for themselves, while new public investors (principals) may assume the company is raising growth capital — interests are not aligned.
- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: The IPO headline figure ($2.2B) becomes the metric that drives perception, obscuring the fact that most of that sum never reaches the company's operating budget.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central claim of this article about Oura's IPO?
**A**: Oura's IPO is structured primarily as a liquidity event for existing shareholders — especially Forerunner Ventures — rather than a capital raise for the company itself, which nets under $600M after RSU tax obligations consume almost all of its share of proceeds.

**Q2**: How does Oura intend to use its ~$532.6M in IPO proceeds?
**A**: Approximately $526.4M (~99%) goes toward satisfying employee RSU tax withholding obligations, leaving just ~$6.2M for general corporate purposes; the company is not using the IPO to fund operations or growth.

**Q3**: What does the membership segment's performance imply for Oura's long-term business model?
**A**: With an 89% gross margin and revenue doubling to $240.5M, the membership segment — though only ~20% of total sales — signals a shift toward a high-margin recurring-revenue model that could over time reduce dependence on lower-margin hardware sales.
