## Summary
New York Fed President [[John Williams]] signaled the [[Federal Reserve]] doesn't need to rush its next interest-rate increase, days after officials approved their first rate hike in three years. He said inflation still runs too high and another increase "late this year" may be warranted, but the Fed can take time to review more data first.

## Core Concepts
- **[[John Williams]]**: New York Fed President and key voting member of the Fed's policy committee (FOMC), whose remarks can shift market expectations for the next move.
- **[[Federal Reserve]]**: Central bank weighing the pace of further tightening after its first rate hike in three years.
- **[[Monetary Policy Tightening]]**: The Fed's process of raising rates to restrain inflation, currently paced by incoming data rather than a fixed schedule.
- **[[FOMC Meeting Calendar]]**: Two remaining 2026 meetings — October and December — are the focal points for whether and when the next hike lands.

## Key Takeaways
- **No urgency**: Williams said "there is no need for urgency" after September's rate increase.
- **Data-dependent**: The Fed wants "time to gather more information" before its next move.
- **Inflation still elevated**: Williams noted inflation remains too high, keeping a late-2026 hike on the table.
- **Two meetings left**: October and December are the remaining 2026 decision points; markets are debating one, the other, or both.
