## Summary
[[Microsoft]] is winding down several neocloud data center capacity deals, including non-renewal of its [[Oracle]] arrangement and exits from operators such as [[Nscale]] and [[IREN]]. Management is shifting toward more flexible in-house data centers to make AI capex more predictable. The article says investors should watch net debt and free cash flow against AI infrastructure spend as neocloud contracts roll off.

## Core Concepts
- [[Microsoft]] (MSFT): about US$4.0 trillion in scale; 2026 capex of US$175b to US$190b must turn into durable revenue and cash flow.
- [[Neocloud]] operators: third-party AI capacity providers whose delays, power constraints, and older chips make capex less predictable.
- [[Oracle]], [[Nscale]], [[IREN]]: counterparties in the winding-down or non-renewed capacity arrangements.
- [[In-House Data Centers]]: owned capacity that lets management match buildout to its bond-funded AI program and internal return hurdles.
- [[AI Capex]]: heavy spending whose payoff depends on execution rather than on demand.
- [[Free Cash Flow]] and net debt: the metrics that show whether the pivot preserves financial flexibility.
- [[Simply Wall St]]: source of the AI-assisted analysis and the "Narrative" framework.

## Key Takeaways
- **Deal Wind-Down**: Microsoft is exiting Nscale and IREN and not renewing Oracle.
- **Drivers**: Regulators, cost pressures, sustainability rules, and anti-AI protests reshape project economics.
- **Execution Risk**: The pullback speaks to execution risk, not weaker AI demand.
- **Control**: Owned capacity matches buildout to return hurdles and the bond-funded program.
- **Capex Scale**: 2026 capex of US$175b to US$190b must yield durable revenue and cash flow.
- **Signal to Watch**: Net debt and free cash flow versus AI spend over coming quarters.
