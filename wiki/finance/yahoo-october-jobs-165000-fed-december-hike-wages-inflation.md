## Summary

October payrolls rose 165,000 against roughly 70,000 expected, with unemployment at 4.1%, solidifying expectations that the [[Federal Reserve]] will hike 25 basis points in December. Wage growth of 3.1% still trails inflation, which acts as a regressive tax and keeps the Fed focused on price stability. Economists split on whether one strong report signals real labor-market heat or just noisy data.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nonfarm Payrolls]]**: 165,000 jobs versus ~70,000 expected; August and September revised up by 84,000.
- **[[Federal Reserve]] and [[FOMC]]**: Held rates at the last meeting; focus is on the price-stability side of the dual mandate.
- **[[Kevin Warsh]]**: Fed chairman who called employment conditions "quite positive" but inflation too high for close to 6 years.
- **[[Wage Growth vs. Inflation]]**: Wages up 3.1% but average hourly earnings remain below inflation.
- **[[Diane Swonk]]**: KPMG economist who calls inflation a regressive tax on lower- and middle-income households.
- **[[Greg Daco]]** and **[[Chris Hodge]]**: EY and Natixis economists who disagree on whether the report signals tightening or only stability.
- **[[Dual Mandate]]**: Employment and price stability, the framework behind the Fed's December path.

## Key Takeaways

- **Jobs Beat**: 165,000 payrolls versus ~70,000 expected; unemployment 4.1% versus 4.2% expected.
- **Trend Context**: Three-month average is 137,000, versus 48,000 over twelve months.
- **Sector Mix**: Gains concentrated in business services, healthcare, and transportation; manufacturing added 19,000.
- **Wages Lag**: Wages grew 3.1% versus 2.9% expected, still below inflation.
- **Regressive Inflation**: Price rises hit lower- and middle-income households harder than affluent ones.
- **Incremental Tightening**: Fed hopes to cool inflation via smaller hikes than in 2022.
- **Split Reading**: Daco expects a December hike; Hodge sees stability, "not heat".

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did the October jobs report change about Fed expectations?
**A**: Payrolls of 165,000 against ~70,000 expected, with unemployment at 4.1%, solidified expectations of a 25 basis point December hike. Employment looks strong enough that the Fed can focus on lingering inflation.

**Q2**: Why does Diane Swonk call wage-versus-inflation dynamics a sticking point for the Fed?
**A**: Average hourly earnings remain below the pace of inflation, so inflation acts as a regressive tax hitting lower- and middle-income households harder. The Fed hopes to cool it with incremental hikes rather than the aggressive approach of 2022.

**Q3**: How should a reader weigh one strong jobs report?
**A**: The article cautions that one report does not make a trend and that jobs data is volatile. Natixis's Hodge prefers wages as a tightness gauge and sees stability, not heat, while EY's Daco still expects another hike.
