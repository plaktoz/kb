## Summary

The Fed's October 27-28 meeting, Chairman [[Kevin Warsh]]'s fourth, is expected to hold rates unchanged after officials downplayed a hike, but investors will read the statement for clues on inflation and the next move. The AP highlights five watch points: hike odds, forward guidance, Warsh's independence from [[Donald Trump]], his characterization of inflation, and possible dissents.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Federal Reserve]] / [[FOMC]]**: Policy rate at about 3.9%; some had expected a quarter-point hike to about 4.1%, but futures now price almost no chance.
- **[[Kevin Warsh]]**: Fed chair who dislikes [[Forward Guidance]] and raised rates in September against Trump's preferences.
- **[[Forward Guidance]]**: Speeches by [[Philip Jefferson]] and others looked like guidance from officials rather than the chair.
- **[[Central Bank Independence]]**: Trump's renewed push to fire governor [[Lisa Cook]] and his claimed call to Warsh keep the question open.
- **[[Inflation]] and Energy Costs**: Two hot readings in a row; diesel hit a record $6.53 and sits at $6.35.
- **[[Mortgage Rates]]**: 30-year rate peaked at 7.4% and was 7.32% last week, versus just under 6.2% a year ago.
- **Dissent**: [[Beth Hammack]] and [[Lorie Logan]] are the likeliest to vote for a hike.
- **Midterm Elections**: Meeting falls six days before the vote amid affordability concerns.

## Key Takeaways

- **Hold Expected**: Futures show almost no chance of a hike this week.
- **Election Timing**: LaVorgna says the Fed won't "poke the White House in the eye."
- **Guidance Paradox**: Warsh dislikes forward guidance, yet officials' speeches resembled it.
- **Surprise Hike Risk**: Bhave says a surprise hike could end guidance by showing markets can't rely on it.
- **Independence Question**: September's hike eased concerns, but Trump-Warsh contacts remain unclear.
- **Diesel Pass-Through**: High diesel raises shipping costs, which businesses pass on to consumers.
- **Inflation Streak**: Inflation hasn't hit 2% for a full quarter since Q4 2020.
- **December Signal**: "Timelier" return-to-2% language would point to a December hike.
- **Dissent Likely**: Two or three of 12 voters may dissent in favor of a hike.
- **Housing Squeeze**: Oxford Economics says high rates put homes out of reach for 5 million more households.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Cost shocks to a shared input propagate across the economy** — A price spike in a widely used input such as diesel raises shipping costs for nearly every good, and firms pass those costs on, spreading inflation beyond the original sector. · *model:* [[Second-Order Effects]]
→ explains: Diesel Pass-Through, Inflation Streak

**T2. Forward guidance trades flexibility for predictability** — Committing to a signaled path makes policy legible but limits the ability to respond to new data, and a signal that is abandoned destroys the credibility of future signals.
→ explains: Guidance Paradox, Surprise Hike Risk

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What do economists expect from the Fed's October 27-28 meeting, and why does it still matter?
**A**: They expect rates to be held, since futures show almost no hike chance, but the statement and Warsh's remarks may signal where inflation and the next move (possibly a December hike) are heading.

**Q2**: Why does Warsh's stance on forward guidance create tension at this meeting?
**A**: Warsh says guidance commits the Fed to a path, yet officials' speeches this month looked like guidance, so either he approved them or he didn't; a surprise hike could show markets they can't rely on guidance.

**Q3**: How would you interpret Warsh's inflation comments at the press conference?
**A**: If he repeats that inflation is too high and must return to 2% in a "timelier" fashion, economists and investors will read that as a sign of a hike in December.
