## Summary

[[Microsoft]] stock neared its first record close since October 2025 on October 13, 2026, rising as high as $551.13 after a 50%+ rally from its June low that restored roughly $1.5 trillion in market value. The recovery began with the July 29 earnings report, where [[Azure]] grew 43% and passed $100 billion in annualized revenue, reversing a June selloff driven by doubts over returns on [[AI Infrastructure Spending]]. Even so, the stock still trails the Nasdaq 100 in 2026, and a shareholder lawsuit from the downturn remains unresolved.

## Core Concepts

- **June trough**: A 17% monthly drop, the steepest since 2000, erased close to $600 billion. Investors questioned whether heavy [[AI Capex]] would pay off.
- **Earnings-driven reversal**: Azure grew 43% against a 40% consensus, its fastest pace in four years. Shares jumped 16% the next day, and Microsoft went on to post its best quarter since 1998.
- **Analyst sentiment**: 68 of the 72 analysts Bloomberg tracks rate the stock a buy, and none rate it a sell. The consensus price target is $584, about 8% above Monday's $541.15 close.
- **Relative performance**: The stock is up about 14% in 2026, against 21% for the [[Nasdaq 100]]. Among the [[Magnificent Seven]], it is roughly level with [[Meta Platforms]], [[Alphabet]] and [[Amazon]], and behind [[Apple]] and [[Nvidia]].
- **[[Maia 300]]**: Microsoft's third-generation in-house AI accelerator, unveiled October 12. It will become the primary architecture for running AI models, shifting away from Nvidia GPUs. It is built for agentic workloads at cloud scale and will run [[Microsoft 365 Copilot]] and [[GitHub Copilot]]. Production begins in 2027.
- **Legal overhang**: A Michigan police and fire pension fund sued in June. It alleges Microsoft hid that Azure growth was slowing while large AI spending was ahead. The case is unresolved.

## Key Takeaways

- **Record Watch**: Intraday high of $551.13 (+1.8%), near the October 2025 peak.
- **Value Restored**: About $1.5T in market cap regained since the June low.
- **June Crash**: A 17% monthly drop, the worst since 2000, erased about $600B.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What drove Microsoft's rally toward its first record close in nearly a year?
**A**: The July 29 earnings report, where Azure grew 43% (above the 40% estimate) and topped $100 billion in annualized revenue. This reversed June's selloff over doubts about AI spending returns.

**Q2**: How severe was Microsoft's June decline, and how much has it recovered?
**A**: A 17% monthly drop, the steepest since 2000, wiped out close to $600 billion. The stock has since gained more than 50% from that low, restoring roughly $1.5 trillion in market value.

**Q3**: Why does the Maia 300 announcement matter for Microsoft's AI strategy?
**A**: Microsoft will use its homegrown chip as the primary architecture for running AI models, including Microsoft 365 Copilot and GitHub Copilot. This shifts it away from its longstanding reliance on Nvidia GPUs, with production starting in 2027.
