## Summary

The Nasdaq 100 suffered its worst single-day drop in four months on October 22, 2026, falling 2.9%, as Meta reported negative free cash flow and raised its 2026 capex guidance to $135–$145 billion, triggering a wave of investor concern about hyperscaler spending discipline. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 7%, while geopolitical escalation pushed Brent crude up 4% to $113.17/barrel, compounding the selloff across tech-heavy indices.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What was the primary catalyst for the Nasdaq 100's worst day in four months?
**A**: Meta reported negative free cash flow of −$2.12 billion and raised its full-year capex guidance to $135–$145 billion, triggering widespread investor anxiety over AI infrastructure spending without visible returns.

**Q2**: How severe was the semiconductor sector's reaction on October 22?
**A**: The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 7%, with Nvidia, Micron, and Broadcom all declining, and semiconductor ETFs falling 6–7%.

**Q3**: What does Blackstone President Jon Gray's framing suggest about the AI capex debate?
**A**: Gray argued AI capacity is being built to keep up with actual demand, contrasting with a bubble where capacity is built hoping demand arrives — suggesting the selloff may be sentiment-driven rather than fundamentals-driven.
