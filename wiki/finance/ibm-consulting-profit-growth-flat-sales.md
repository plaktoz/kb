## Summary

IBM Consulting increased Q2 segment profit by 15.1% to $647 million on nearly flat revenue growth of 0.2%, driven primarily by delivery productivity actions and gross margin expansion. While improved delivery economics and consultant utilization can boost earnings in the short term, sustaining double-digit profit growth will require stronger revenue conversion as cost cuts encounter tougher year-over-year comparisons. Despite a positive book-to-bill ratio of 1.05 and increased institutional investor interest, repeating margin expansion on stagnant revenue presents significant operational hurdles.

## Core Concepts

- [[IBM Consulting]] — IBM's consulting business segment delivering enterprise client services
- [[Operating Margin Expansion]] — driving profit growth via cost reduction and delivery efficiency without top-line revenue growth
- [[Book-to-Bill Ratio]] — ratio of signed contracts to recognized revenue indicating forward demand pipeline
- [[Delivery Economics]] — improving consultant utilization and lowering cost-of-delivery to protect profitability
- [[Signings Conversion]] — the lag and uncertainty between booking consulting contracts and realizing GAAP revenue
- [[Earnings Quality]] — evaluating whether profit growth from cost restructuring is durable without underlying sales expansion

## Key Takeaways

- **Profit Surges on Flat Sales**: Segment profit rose 15.1% to $647 million despite revenue rising only 0.2%.
- **Margin Sensitivity**: Each margin percentage point represents $53.3 million in quarterly segment profit.
- **Gross Margin Improvement**: Delivery gross margin expanded to 28.9% from 27.5% year-over-year.
- **Modest Book-to-Bill**: Trailing book-to-bill reached 1.05 with signings up 5% as reported.
- **Growth Dilemma**: Sustaining 15% profit growth requires 15% revenue growth or margins reaching 14%.
- **Institutional Accumulation**: Hedge fund holders increased from 59 to 74 in Q2 2026.
