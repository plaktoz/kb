## Summary
Wolfe Research reports that Q2 2026 S&P 500 earnings are running well ahead of consensus (69% revenue beats) and forward Q3 guidance is unusually strong (64% of guiders above consensus, the highest share since COVID), supporting 2026 operating EPS growth estimates of 31%.

## Core Concepts

- [[S&P 500 Earnings Momentum]] — the current stretch of unusually strong beat rates and forward guidance across the index, described by Wolfe Research as the strongest fundamental outlook outside a post-recession recovery in over 50 years
- [[Guidance Beat Rate]] — the share of companies issuing forward guidance above consensus; 64% here is a specific, unusually high signal Wolfe Research is tracking
- [[Mega-Cap Earnings Distortion]] — the gap between headline 31% EPS growth and Wolfe's adjusted ~27% estimate after excluding large one-time gains concentrated in mega-cap tech

## Key Takeaways

- **Beat rate**: 465 of S&P 500 companies reported through the article's date, 69% beating revenue consensus by a 3.8% dollar-weighted surprise.
- **Forward guidance**: 86 companies gave Q3 2026 guidance; 64% guided above consensus at the midpoint — highest since the COVID period.
- **2026 EPS growth**: consensus 31%, Wolfe's adjusted estimate ~27% after excluding mega-cap one-time gains.
- **Open question flagged**: whether this growth rate is sustainable into 2027, particularly given rising AI capital-spending commitments across the index's largest constituents.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: S&P 500 companies are beating and guiding well above consensus at rates not seen since the COVID recovery period, pointing to unusually strong underlying earnings momentum.

**Q2**: What data point does Wolfe Research use to describe this as historically strong, and what caveat does it attach?
**A**: 64% of guiding companies came in above consensus (highest since COVID), but Wolfe's adjusted ~27% EPS growth estimate — after stripping out mega-cap one-time gains — is more conservative than the 31% headline figure.

**Q3**: What is identified as the key risk to sustaining this momentum into 2027?
**A**: Whether earnings growth can hold up as AI capital spending continues rising, since much of the current growth narrative is concentrated in mega-cap technology companies making large AI-related investments.
