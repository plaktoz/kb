## Summary
The CEO of [[HSBC]] cautions financial markets that the aggressive surge in [[Data Center Financing]] carries significant underwriting and debt risks despite the broad [[Artificial Intelligence]] boom. Not all data center facilities possess the power access, cooling infrastructure, or anchor tenant contracts necessary to justify their substantial leverage. Lenders and investors must exercise selective discipline to avoid over-collateralized stranded assets as AI capacity scales unevenly.

## Core Concepts
- [[HSBC]]: Global financial institution scrutinizing debt exposures and capital risk in hyper-scale AI build-outs.
- [[Data Center Financing]]: Large-scale debt and equity mechanisms funding compute infrastructure, power grids, and cooling facilities.
- [[Capex Cycle]]: Cyclical high-intensity capital expenditure periods driven by technological expansion.
- [[Stranded Assets]]: Infrastructure investments that become economically obsolete or unprofitable prior to their expected depreciation.
- [[Underwriting Risk]]: The risk that lenders miscalculate cash flows and asset durability in speculative technology assets.

## Key Takeaways
- **Selective Viability**: The broader AI boom will not automatically guarantee profitability for every financed data center.
- **Debt Scrutiny**: Rapid expansion funded by debt increases financial vulnerability if demand falls short of capacity forecasts.
- **Power and Location Bottlenecks**: Access to reliable power grids and cooling represents the primary constraint separating successful sites from failures.
- **Underwriting Discipline**: Tier-1 banks are becoming increasingly cautious regarding customer concentration and speculative build-outs.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What warning did HSBC's CEO issue regarding data center investments?
**A**: HSBC warned that the AI boom will not make every data center facility profitable, highlighting rising debt and underwriting risks.

**Q2**: What physical and economic factors distinguish viable data centers from failing projects?
**A**: Access to high-capacity power, advanced cooling infrastructure, and diversified, solvent anchor tenants determine long-term project solvency.

**Q3**: How should financial institutions approach the current surge in AI infrastructure debt?
**A**: Lenders must apply rigorous credit underwriting, accounting for power constraints and avoiding over-reliance on speculative capacity projections.
