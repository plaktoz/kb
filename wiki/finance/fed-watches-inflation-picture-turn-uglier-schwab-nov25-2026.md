## Summary
October CPI cooled to 0.4% month over month, but multi-month trends and the breadth of price increases show inflation worsening. Fed Governor Waller floated a half-point hike in December, and markets price a 100% chance of a hike at the December 8–9 meeting. Energy, especially diesel, may keep feeding goods and services prices well into 2027.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Consumer Price Index]]**: October rose 0.4% (about 4.3% annualized); three-month annualized CPI was 5.7% and core CPI 3.7%.
- **[[Producer Price Index]]**: Final-demand PPI rose an annualized 9% over three months through October.
- **[[Federal Reserve]] leadership**: Chair [[Kevin Warsh]] wanted more data before raising rates again; Governor [[Christopher Waller]] urged considering a 50 bp hike.
- **[[PCE Price Index]]**: The Fed's preferred gauge, with October data due November 25.
- **[[Inflation Breadth]]**: 105 of 177 CPI categories (59%) rose faster than 3% annualized, the highest since August 2022.
- **[[Energy Look-Through]]**: The Fed's habit of ignoring volatile energy prices, challenged by staff research cited by Waller.
- **[[Diesel Prices]]**: Diesel fell only 9% from its September record versus 22% for WTI crude, and feeds trucking and farming costs.
- **[[CME FedWatch Tool]]**: Shows a 100% chance of a December hike, 9.6% of a 50 bp move.

## Key Takeaways
- **Headline Cooling**: CPI slowed from 0.6% to 0.4%, but still annualizes near 4.3%.
- **Core Acceleration**: Core CPI at 3.7% annualized, more than double the 1.6% pace through July.
- **Broadening Inflation**: 59% of CPI categories above 3% annualized, up from 46% in August.
- **Underlying Gauges**: Atlanta Fed core sticky-price index rose 4.7% annualized in October.
- **Energy Pass-Through**: Waller cites staff research that energy may lift prices into 2027.
- **Freight and Food**: Truck freight up 25% year over year; wholesale food up 1.3% monthly.
- **Market Pricing**: A hot PCE surprise could push the Fed toward a 50 bp hike.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Breadth distinguishes persistent inflation from a one-off shock** — When price increases spread across many categories rather than a few, they reflect underlying trend pressure and are less likely to reverse on their own.
→ explains: Broadening Inflation, Underlying Gauges, Core Acceleration

**T2. Cost shocks propagate through input channels beyond their initial sector** — A price rise in a widely used input, such as diesel, feeds into many downstream prices, extending the effect past the first-order move. · *model:* [[Second-Order Effects]]
→ explains: Energy Pass-Through, Freight and Food

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does the article say the inflation picture is turning uglier despite slower October CPI?
**A**: The monthly CPI gain slowed, but three-month annualized CPI, PPI and core CPI all accelerated, and price increases are spreading across categories.

**Q2**: What evidence shows inflation is broadening?
**A**: In October, 105 of 177 CPI categories (59%) rose faster than 3% annualized, the highest share since August 2022, up from 46% in August.

**Q3**: Why might the Fed not simply "look through" energy prices this time?
**A**: Fed staff research cited by Waller suggests energy may drive goods and services prices longer than before the pandemic, and diesel feeds into trucking and farming costs. That could keep inflation elevated into 2027.
