## Summary
Bill Ackman warns the Fed would make a mistake by hiking rates in December, arguing financial conditions have already tightened sharply and that chasing 2% inflation risks a recession. He contends a 3% target is more realistic given geopolitical risk, military spending, tariffs and AI infrastructure investment. Fed Chair Warsh maintains that the target is price stability at 2% PCE.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Bill Ackman]]**: Pershing Square founder who criticizes the planned hike on X.
- **[[Federal Reserve]] December meeting**: December 8–9, with a 96.1% market probability of a 25 bp hike to 4%–4.25%.
- **[[Tightening Financial Conditions]]**: Ackman cites SpaceX raising secured debt at 8.259% versus below 6% unsecured at its IPO.
- **[[Cost of Equity Capital]]**: Raised, per Ackman, by the recent stock market decline.
- **[[Inflation Target]]**: Fed's 2% PCE goal; PCE was 3.6% in September.
- **[[Kevin Warsh]]**: Chair who says there is no soft or implicit target.
- **[[Recession Risk]]**: Ackman argues continued hikes will cause a recession.

## Key Takeaways
- **Hike Expectations**: CME FedWatch shows 96.1% odds of a 25 bp December hike.
- **Financing Squeeze**: Even investment-grade SpaceX faces much costlier secured debt.
- **Equity Cost**: Stock declines have raised companies' cost of equity capital.
- **Target Critique**: Ackman calls the 2% ambition misguided in today's world.
- **Structural Shifts**: Military spending, de-globalization, tariffs and AI investment are new inflation features.
- **Fed Stance**: Warsh says there is no soft target; getting inflation down is the Fed's responsibility.
- **Stubborn Inflation**: Inflation has stayed above 2% for nearly six years.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Policy targets calibrated to one structural regime can misfit a new one** — When the forces driving a variable change, a target set under the old conditions may demand costs that exceed its benefits.
→ explains: Target Critique, Structural Shifts

**T2. Tighter financing raises the hurdle rate for investment** — Higher debt and equity costs reduce the investment projects that clear the return threshold, slowing growth and raising recession risk.
→ explains: Financing Squeeze, Equity Cost

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Ackman's central argument against a December hike?
**A**: He says financial conditions have already tightened massively, so hiking would squeeze corporate financing and risk a recession in a failed attempt to reach 2% inflation.

**Q2**: What evidence does Ackman give for tight financial conditions?
**A**: SpaceX, an investment-grade company, raised secured debt at 8.259% versus below 6% for its unsecured bonds at IPO, and the cost of equity has risen with the stock market decline.

**Q3**: What alternative to the 2% target does Ackman propose, and why?
**A**: He suggests 3% inflation is more realistic because geopolitical risk, military spending, de-globalization, tariffs and AI infrastructure investment did not exist when 2% was set.
