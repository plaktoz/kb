## Summary
Fed Funds futures price roughly 90% odds that the FOMC holds rates at its October 28 meeting, with over 80% odds of a hike by year-end. Because Chairman Warsh limits forward-looking statements, the dissent count may be the most actionable signal. A "true hawkish hold" would likely push EUR/USD to a fresh 17-month low.

## Core Concepts
- [[Federal Open Market Committee]] meeting on October 28 at 2:00 ET, followed by a 2:30 ET press conference from [[Kevin Warsh]].
- [[Hawkish Hold]]: holding rates while signaling further tightening; graded by vote count (neutral, mildly hawkish, true hawkish).
- Hawkish dissenters: [[Beth Hammack]] (Cleveland), [[Lorie Logan]] (Dallas), with [[Neel Kashkari]] (Minneapolis) as a wildcard.
- [[CME FedWatch]] and a Reuters economist poll (94 of 100 expect a hold at 3.75-4.00%).
- [[EUR/USD]] technicals: range between 1.1130 and 1.1270 since early October.
- Data and event risk: Q3 GDP and September [[PCE Inflation]] the day after, Middle East conflict, US midterms.

## Key Takeaways
- **Hold Odds**: ~90% priced for a hold; 94 of 100 economists agree.
- **December Hike**: Traders price over 80% odds of a hike by year-end.
- **Vote Reading**: No dissents is neutral; one is mildly hawkish; two or more is true hawkish.
- **Terse Guidance**: Warsh's short statements and limited forward guidance make dissents the key signal.
- **Press Conference Framing**: Citing tighter financial conditions hints at a pause; anchoring inflation expectations hints at hikes.
- **EUR/USD Scenarios**: True hawkish hold targets below 1.1130, then 1.1075 and 1.10.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central argument about this week's FOMC meeting?
**A**: A rate hold is nearly certain, so the market-moving question is how hawkish the hold is, especially given Warsh's reluctance to offer forward guidance.

**Q2**: How does the article say traders should interpret the dissent count?
**A**: No dissents is a "neutral hold" casting doubt on a December hike, one is a "mildly hawkish hold," and two or more is a "true hawkish hold" signaling a temporary pause before a likely December hike.

**Q3**: How would a true hawkish hold affect positioning in EUR/USD?
**A**: It would likely push EUR/USD below 1.1130 to a fresh 17-month low, exposing 1.1075 and possibly 1.10, while a cautious outcome could pull it back toward 1.12.
