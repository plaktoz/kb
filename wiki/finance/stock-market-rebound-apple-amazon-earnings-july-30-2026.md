## Summary

The Nasdaq, S&P 500, and Dow closed sharply higher on July 30 (2.8%, 1.7%, 1.2%) in a broad snap-back from the prior day's Fed-driven selloff, led almost entirely by an Information Technology sector up 5.1% — while a majority of S&P 500 sectors and nearly two-thirds of individual stocks actually finished lower, underscoring narrow rally breadth. [[Microsoft]] surged 16% on strong earnings while [[Meta Platforms]] sank 8% on a miss, with Apple and Amazon reporting after the close.

## Core Concepts

- [[Market Breadth]] — six of eleven S&P 500 sectors finished negative even as the index rose 1.7%, driven almost entirely by mega-cap tech
- [[Federal Reserve]] rate decision — held rates steady 9-3 on July 29, with Chair Kevin Warsh signaling resolve on inflation
- [[PCE Inflation]] — June core PCE rose 3.3% YoY, in line with expectations and down from 3.4% in May
- [[GDP Growth]] — Q2 2026 growth of 1.5%, below the 1.8% consensus and down from 2.1% in Q1

## Key Takeaways

- **Narrow rally**: Nasdaq +2.8%, S&P +1.7%, Dow +1.2%, but driven almost entirely by a 5.1% jump in Information Technology
- **Microsoft vs Meta split**: MSFT +16% on Azure beat; META −8% on earnings miss and rising AI costs
- **Fed backdrop**: rates held steady 9-3; traders pricing a 63% chance of a September hike, down from 82% a week earlier
- **Mixed macro data**: inflation cooling (PCE 3.7% YoY, down from 4.1%) but growth slowing (GDP +1.5% vs 1.8% expected)
- **Oil and gold**: WTI crude fell 1.1% to $83.50/barrel on Iran-strike uncertainty; gold rose 3.4% to $4,170/oz

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Market Breadth as Leading Indicator]]**: A market cap-weighted index rising 1.7% while two-thirds of its components fall is a classic breadth-divergence pattern that technicians treat as a fragility signal — the rally's durability depends on whether participation broadens beyond a handful of mega-cap names.
