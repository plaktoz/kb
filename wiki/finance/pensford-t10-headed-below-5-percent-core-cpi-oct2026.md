## Summary
Hotter-than-expected core CPI first pushed rates up, but an equity selloff in leveraged AI plays pulled the 10-year Treasury yield back down from above 5%. Pensford argues higher rates self-limit by restricting growth, and that the inflation picture under the hood is less alarming than the headline. With an 86% probability of a Fed hold, the 10-year may be headed back below 5%.

## Core Concepts
- [[10-Year Treasury Yield]]: fell for the first time in a long while to 5.08%; Bloomberg's rates team sees the peak as behind us.
- [[Core CPI]]: 0.3% m/m vs 0.2% expected and 2.6% y/y vs 2.5%, even with oil excluded.
- [[Owners' Equivalent Rent]]: more than half of the monthly Core CPI increase.
- [[Federal Reserve]] communication: speakers [[Kevin Warsh]], Hammack, Waller, Collins and Bowman avoid forward guidance.
- [[AI Stock Selloff]]: leveraged AI plays fell on higher-rate fears, which pulled yields lower.
- [[Anna Wong]]'s CPI breadth analysis: share of 170 components rising above 3%.

## Key Takeaways
- **CPI Surprise**: Core CPI beat forecasts on both monthly and annual readings, and rates jumped.
- **Equity Feedback**: The equity selloff in leveraged AI plays pulled rates back down.
- **Self-Limiting Yields**: Higher Treasury yields restrict growth and do some of the FOMC's work.
- **Rent Distortion**: Owners' equivalent rent drove over half of monthly Core CPI.
- **Breadth Easing**: 48% of components rise above 3%, vs 51% last month and 58% earlier.
- **Hot Categories**: Fuel, gas and airfare lead inflation; tobacco is fourth.
- **Retail Sales**: Headline 0.8% vs 0.3% expected, driven by gas; core retail was negative.
- **Fed Outlook**: Markets price an 86% chance of a hold; consensus is 50bps more by March 2027.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Rates restrain growth** — Higher interest rates restrict economic activity, so rising yields eventually act against their own rise; they cannot climb indefinitely. · *model:* [[Feedback Loops]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Pensford's central argument about the 10-year yield?
**A**: Higher rates self-limit because they restrict growth, and the equity selloff already pulled yields down. Bloomberg's technical analysis implies a move back below 5%.

**Q2**: What evidence suggests inflation is less concerning than the core CPI print implied?
**A**: Owners' equivalent rent was over half of the monthly Core CPI rise. The share of components rising above 3% fell to 48% from 51% last month and 58% earlier this year.

**Q3**: What should you expect from the Fed over the coming weeks?
**A**: The Fed is quiet ahead of its meeting, and markets give an 86% chance of a hold. Consensus expects 50bps more hikes by end of March 2027, though the author takes the under.
