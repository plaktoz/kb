## Summary
During a sharp tech-led stock selloff on Thursday, traders bought short-term Treasurys, pushing the 2-year yield down 10.4 basis points to 4.48%. Worries that the post-midterm political shift could stall the AI buildout drove the Nasdaq down more than 5% and lifted money-market assets to a record $8.016 trillion. The selloff also cut the odds of a December Fed rate hike to 66.1% from 82.7%.

## Core Concepts
- [[Treasury Yields]]: 2-year fell to 4.48%, lowest since Sept. 9; 10-year down 5 basis points to 4.48% after hitting 5.3% in October.
- [[Flight to Safety]]: buying front-end Treasurys as a hedge against equity tail risk.
- [[AI Buildout]]: the economy and household stock wealth rely on it, and fears centre on voter opposition to data centers.
- [[2026 US Midterm Elections]]: Democrats take control of Congress in January.
- [[Money-Market Funds]]: record $8.016 trillion in cash on the sidelines per ICI.
- [[Federal Reserve]] and [[Kevin Warsh]]: December hike odds fell per CME FedWatch.
- [[Semiconductor Stocks]]: the SOX index fell sharply.

## Key Takeaways
- **Index Moves**: Nasdaq Composite fell over 5%, S&P 500 4.2%, Dow 3.4%.
- **Yield Drop**: 2-year yield fell 10.4 basis points to 4.48%.
- **Cash Pile**: Money-market funds swelled to a record $8.016 trillion.
- **Fed Odds**: December hike chances fell to 66.1% from 82.7%.
- **Muted 10-Year**: Only 5 basis points lower, possibly hesitation before Friday's jobs report.
- **Strategist View**: UBS still expects hikes in December and March, with a cap on restrictiveness.
- **Jobs Risk**: A strong report might hurt stocks more than bonds.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. In equity stress, capital flows to the risk-free asset** — When risk assets reprice sharply, investors buy government debt, which raises prices and lowers yields. · *model:* [[Safe Haven Assets]]
→ explains: Yield Drop, Index Moves, Muted 10-Year

**T2. Selling can beget more selling** — Falling prices prompt further selling as participants cut losses and reprice, amplifying the move beyond initial news. · *model:* [[Reflexivity]]
→ explains: Index Moves, Cash Pile

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did Treasury yields fall during Thursday's stock selloff?
**A**: Traders cut equity losses by buying short-term Treasurys, pricing in tail risk of a large stock decline. That pushed the 2-year yield down 10.4 basis points to 4.48%.

**Q2**: What data points show the scale of the move and the cash on the sidelines?
**A**: The Nasdaq Composite fell more than 5% and the S&P 500 4.2%, while money-market funds hit a record $8.016 trillion. Odds of a December Fed hike fell to 66.1% from 82.7%.

**Q3**: What does this imply for the Fed and upcoming data?
**A**: Equity weakness reduces pressure for aggressive tightening, though UBS still expects hikes in December and March. Friday's jobs report could matter, since a strong one may hurt stocks more than bonds.
