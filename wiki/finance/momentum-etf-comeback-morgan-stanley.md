## Summary
After a Goldman Sachs momentum-stock basket plunged 35% from its June peak as AI/semiconductor positions unwound, Morgan Stanley strategist Michael Wilson argues the "worst momentum selloff in history" is giving way to a healthier rotation toward stable-earnings companies — a reset, not a death of the [[Momentum Factor]], with relevance for holdings like [[SPMO]] and [[IWMO]].

## Core Concepts
- **[[Momentum Factor Investing]]**: Momentum ETFs (e.g., MTUM, SPMO, PDP) rebalance periodically to hold stocks with the strongest recent price performance, which mechanically overweights whatever sector is currently leading — AI/semiconductors during 2025-26's rally.
- **[[Crowded Trade Unwind]]**: The 35% basket plunge is characterized as a rapid deleveraging of overcrowded positions rather than a fundamental repudiation of the momentum strategy, echoing prior momentum pullback patterns per Goldman's Ben Snider.
- **[[Sector Rotation Within a Factor]]**: Wilson expects momentum rankings to gradually shift from high-beta AI/chip names toward companies with stable earnings, stronger margins, and better operational efficiency — a rotation *within* the momentum factor rather than abandonment of it.

## Key Takeaways
- **Basket drawdown**: Goldman's momentum basket fell 35% from its June peak but remains up 9.4% YTD, roughly in line with the S&P 500.
- **Fund performance**: MTUM (largest momentum ETF, $25B AUM) down ~9% in the past month; PDP down more than 7%.
- **Earnings backdrop**: 86% of S&P 500 companies beat estimates this earnings season per Bloomberg Intelligence — a potential broadening catalyst.
- **Mechanism**: Momentum ETFs rebalance automatically, so if earnings-driven leadership broadens beyond AI/semis, funds like MTUM, SPMO, XMMO, and XSMO would rotate into new leaders without investor action.
