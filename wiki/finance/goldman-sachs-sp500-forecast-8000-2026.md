## Summary

Goldman Sachs Research raised its year-end 2026 S&P 500 target to 8000 (from 7600), arguing the 2026 rally has been powered entirely by corporate earnings growth rather than rising valuations — with AI-infrastructure spending expected to drive roughly half of total earnings growth this year and next.

## Core Concepts

- [[Earnings-Driven Rally]] — Goldman's framing that 2026's stock gains reflect genuine profit growth (18% y/y in Q1) rather than valuation expansion, distinguishing a durable rally from a speculative one.
- [[AI Capex Multiplier]] — hyperscale tech companies are projected to spend $754B on capex in 2026 (+83% y/y) and $905B in 2027, with semiconductor, hardware, industrials and utilities firms capturing the downstream earnings benefit.
- [[Narrow Market Breadth]] — a cautionary signal Goldman flags: only about 17% of S&P 500 stocks have outperformed the index over the past month, one of the lowest readings in a decade, even as the momentum factor surges.
- [[Valuation Multiple Stability]] — Goldman expects the ~21x forward P/E (88th percentile over 40 years) to hold roughly flat through year-end rather than expand further.

## Key Takeaways

- **New target**: S&P 500 year-end 2026 forecast raised to 8000 from 7600, implying a 6% gain from late-May levels.
- **EPS forecasts**: $340 for 2026 (24% growth) and $385 for 2027 (13% growth), with AI-infrastructure beneficiaries driving about half of total earnings growth in both years.
- **Capex scale**: hyperscalers' 2026 capex estimated at $754B, an 83% increase over 2025.
- **Warning signs**: narrow market breadth and a sharp rise in the momentum factor have historically signaled elevated market risk even during otherwise-healthy bull markets.
- **Near-term headwinds**: seasonal weakness ahead of midterm elections and a high bar for further upward capex revisions could moderate returns in the second half.
