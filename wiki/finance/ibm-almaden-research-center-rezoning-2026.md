## Summary
San Jose's City Council is set to vote (Aug 18, 2026, on the consent calendar) on rezoning IBM's 687-acre Almaden Research Center — split into ~35 acres of Industrial Park zoning and ~652 acres of Open Space zoning — after IBM closed the research hub and relocated staff to its Silicon Valley Lab. The rezoning doesn't approve redevelopment itself but sets up the property, still IBM-owned, for a future sale or repositioning.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Planned Development Zoning District]]**: The site's current, custom 1980-era zoning designation tailored specifically for IBM's original research campus use — described by a land-use consultant as "antiquated."
- **[[Industrial Park vs. Open Space Zoning]]**: The proposed split — Industrial Park permits R&D, manufacturing, assembly, testing, and offices (i.e., commercial redevelopment potential); Open Space is restricted to parks, trails, habitat buffers, and preserves.
- **[[Consent Calendar Controversy]]**: Placing a complex rezoning on a council's consent calendar bundles it for a fast, limited-debate vote — residents Jessica Vernon and Abby Simons both objected that this specific rezoning is too nuanced for that fast-track process.

## Key Takeaways
- IBM still owns the entire 687-acre property; the rezoning is a precursor step, not an approval of any specific redevelopment project.
- Cushman & Wakefield is marketing the 544,500-sq-ft property as suited for data centers, industrial, R&D, advanced manufacturing, or general office use.
- The move follows IBM's shift of Almaden's research staff to its Silicon Valley Lab at 555 Bailey Road near Coyote Valley.
- Any actual redevelopment proposal would still require separate public review beyond this zoning update.

## 🃏 Review Questions
**Q1**: What is the practical effect of the proposed zoning split at IBM's Almaden campus?
**A**: About 35 acres (including the research buildings) would become Industrial Park zoning, permitting commercial/R&D/office redevelopment, while roughly 652 acres would become Open Space zoning, restricted largely to parks and preserves.

**Q2**: Why did some San Jose residents object to how the rezoning was being processed?
**A**: It was placed on the City Council's consent calendar, which allows for a fast vote with limited public debate; residents argued the rezoning was too complicated and nuanced for that abbreviated process.

**Q3**: Does the rezoning itself authorize any redevelopment of the IBM site?
**A**: No — the rezoning only updates the site's zoning designation; any specific redevelopment proposal would still need to go through its own separate public review and approval process.
