## Summary

IBM shares recovered to $226.90 on July 28 after bottoming at $201.60 following the worst single-day drop in company history (-25.2% on July 14) triggered by a preliminary Q2 warning. Q2 results confirmed $17.16B revenue (+1% YoY) and $2.93 adj. EPS, with software growing 5% but mainframe infrastructure falling 42%; full-year guidance of 4–5% constant-currency revenue growth provides a floor. Technical recovery is constructive, but the $233.20 50-period EMA remains key resistance, and an ongoing securities fraud investigation continues to cap upside.

## Core Concepts

- **[[IBM]] Q2 2026 Results**: Revenue $17.16B vs. $17.58B consensus; adj. EPS $2.93 vs. $2.97 consensus; Software +5% to $7.76B; Infrastructure -7%, Z [[Mainframe]] -42%.
- **[[Software Division]] Highlights**: [[Red Hat OpenShift]] (Hybrid Cloud) +11% YoY; OpenShift ARR $2.2B; Data revenue +19% on AI-driven analytics; [[Generative AI]] ~50% of consulting signings, >30% of backlog.
- **Securities Fraud Investigation**: Regulators examining whether IBM misled investors about pipeline strength prior to the July 14 preliminary warning; no formal charges filed yet.
- **[[Quantum Computing]] Foundry**: IBM signed a letter of intent to build a US quantum chip foundry, extending $10B+ quantum investment into manufacturing.
- **[[Technical Analysis]] — Key Levels**: 50 EMA $233.20 (resistance), 200 EMA $249.70; supports at $213.30, $201.60, $188.80; RSI ~65 approaching overbought.
- **[[Dead Cat Bounce]] Risk**: No rally since the July 14 crash has reclaimed pre-crash support levels; a confirmed close above 50 EMA is required to signal genuine trend reversal.

## Key Takeaways

- **Historic Decline**: July 14 drop of -25.2% was IBM's worst single-day loss since 1968.
- **Q2 Miss**: Revenue $17.16B vs. $17.58B consensus; EPS $2.93 vs. $2.97.

- **Mainframe Trough**: Z mainframe revenue -42%, reflecting product-cycle timing.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Dead Cat Bounce]]**: A severe oversold decline followed by a partial recovery that fails to reclaim prior support is the textbook pattern — the 50 EMA test is precisely the moment that separates a genuine trend reversal from a temporary relief rally.
- **[[Overhang Effect]]**: Unresolved binary risks (the fraud investigation) act as a structural ceiling on price recovery regardless of fundamental improvement, because rational buyers discount the probability of adverse outcomes — IBM's 35% gap to its July peak reflects this discount in real time.
