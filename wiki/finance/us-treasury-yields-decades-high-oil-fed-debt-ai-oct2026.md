## Summary
US Treasury yields hit multi-decade highs in October 2026, with the 10-year at 5.34% on October 7 and the 30-year at 5.69%. The article attributes the rise to oil-driven inflation after the US-Iran war, Fed hikes, a $40 trillion debt pile, and AI-related borrowing. A WSJ survey of economists expects the 10-year to end 2026 at 5.1%, so the latest dip looks like a pause rather than a turn.

## Core Concepts
- [[10-Year Treasury Yield]]: touched 5.34% on October 7, its highest since 2002; 5.12% on Friday after an intraday low of 5.038%.
- [[30-Year Treasury Yield]]: spiked to 5.69%, a 24-year high; 5.46% on October 16.
- [[Federal Reserve]]: raised rates 25 bps on September 16 to 3.75%–4.00%; markets price a 92% chance of a December hike.
- [[Kevin Warsh]]: Fed Chair; said "inflation is too high and has been for too long".
- [[Strait of Hormuz]] and oil: fighting pushed Brent above $107 in mid-September; September CPI at 3.7%.
- [[Term Premium]]: the extra pay investors demand for holding long-term debt; the 10s-30s gap reached about 37 bps.
- [[US National Debt]]: above $40 trillion, with net interest above $1 trillion a year.
- [[Crowding Out]]: [[Merrill Lynch]] sees big tech's nearly $280 billion of debt competing with Treasuries; [[ING]] attributes about one-fifth of the rise in long-dated yields to AI.
- [[Global Bond Selloff]]: Germany's Bund above 3.5%, Japan's 10-year at a 1996 high, France's 10-year at 4.993%.

## Key Takeaways
- **Record Levels**: 10-year yield hit 5.34%, highest since 2002; up about 135 bps since the war began.
- **Latest Dip**: 10-year fell to 5.038% on Thursday, then edged up to 5.12%.
- **Hot Inflation**: September PPI rose 1.3% month on month; CPI at 3.7%.
- **Fed Outlook**: About 72% odds of a hold this month; 92% odds of a December hike.
- **Oil Shock**: Brent above $107, up over 20% in a month, feeds inflation expectations.
- **Debt Supply**: $40 trillion debt and heavy issuance mean fewer eager buyers; buybacks failed to calm yields.
- **AI Borrowing**: ING credits AI with about one-fifth of the rise; PIMCO's Stracke calls it the main driver.
- **Market Impact**: Morgan Stanley sees up to 7% S&P 500 downside; mortgage rates near 7.4%.
- **Outlook**: WSJ survey expects 10-year at 5.1% by end-2026, 4.78% by end-2027.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Rate expectations set yields** — When investors expect policy rates to stay higher for longer, they demand higher yields on bonds. Inflation expectations, driven here by oil, feed that path.
→ explains: Oil Shock, Fed Outlook, Hot Inflation

**T2. Heavy supply raises the price of capital** — When borrowers, public or corporate, issue large volumes of debt, they compete for a finite pool of money and push yields up. · *model:* [[Crowding Out]]
→ explains: Debt Supply, AI Borrowing

**T3. Long-term lenders demand compensation for uncertainty** — Investors holding longer-dated debt require extra yield, shown by the widening 10-year to 30-year gap. · *model:* [[Term Premium]]
→ explains: Record Levels, Debt Supply
