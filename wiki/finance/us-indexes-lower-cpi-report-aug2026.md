## Summary

Major U.S. indexes closed lower for a second straight day on August 11, 2026 as investors awaited the July [[CPI]] report; oil extended gains on [[Strait of Hormuz]] uncertainty and [[Alphabet]] led Big Tech losses. Analysts flagged a broadening market-leadership shift away from pure AI-momentum names toward higher-quality, durable-earnings stocks.

## Core Concepts

- **[[CPI Report]]**: July inflation data due the next morning, expected to show 3.4% headline / 2.5% core YoY, a deceleration that would mark a second straight month of easing — a key input to September [[Federal Reserve]] rate decisions.
- **[[Magnificent Seven]]**: Ended mostly lower, with [[Alphabet]] down nearly 4%; [[Nvidia]] closed fractionally lower despite signing MOUs for up to $500B in AI data-center financing.
- **[[Equal-Weight S&P 500]]**: Outperformed the market-weight index by the widest margin since 2009 (per Goldman Sachs) over June-July, reflecting broadening profit growth beyond mega-cap tech.
- **[[Market Regime Shift]]**: Morgan Stanley's Michael Wilson frames the market as moving from "early- to mid-cycle," with leadership shifting to durable-earnings, stable-margin companies.

## Key Takeaways

- **Big movers**: [[Riot Platforms]] up 4%+ on a ~$9B, 20-year compute deal with [[Anthropic]]; [[Sea Limited]] jumped 14% on Shopee revenue beating estimates by ~$630M; [[On Holding]] sank 20% on weak guidance.
- **Labor market paradox**: July unemployment fell to 4.1%, a 57-month streak at/below 4.5% (a record), even as job creation has slowed — driven by fewer people actively searching for work.
- **Chip financing scrutiny**: [[Intel]] boosted its stock offering to $20B from $15B to fund AI-driven manufacturing capacity expansion.
- **Sector concentration risk**: Alphabet's ~3.5-4% decline alone dragged the Communication Services sector down 1.9%, the S&P 500's worst performer for the day.
