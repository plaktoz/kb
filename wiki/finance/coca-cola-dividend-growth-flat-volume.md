## Summary

[[Coca-Cola]] has grown its dividend 61% over the past decade — from $1.32/share (2015) to $2.12 today — despite unit case volume growing only ~16% over the same period. The gains were funded primarily by refranchising low-margin bottling operations and aggressive pricing, not volume. The streak now stands at 64 consecutive years of annual dividend increases, though the author cautions the stock trades at a demanding valuation (~25x earnings, 2.4% yield).

## Core Concepts

- **[[Dividend Growth Investing]]**: Sustained annual dividend increases even in the absence of meaningful volume growth, funded by margin expansion and pricing power.
- **[[Refranchising]]**: Coca-Cola shed its low-margin bottling operations between 2016–2018, dropping revenue from $44.3B to $31.9B but expanding [[Operating Margin]] from ~20% to ~29%.
- **[[Pricing Power]]**: In 2025, organic revenue grew 5% while volume was flat — four of those five points came from price and mix, illustrating pricing as the primary growth lever.
- **[[Free Cash Flow]]**: Management projects ~$12.4B in FCF for 2026; the dividend costs just over $9B (~75% of projected cash flow), providing a comfortable coverage ratio.
- **[[Dividend Aristocrats]]**: Companies with 25+ consecutive years of dividend increases; Coca-Cola's 64-year streak makes it a [[Dividend King]].
- **[[Valuation Risk]]**: At ~$88/share and ~25x earnings, the premium price may not adequately compensate for modest growth prospects.

## Key Takeaways

- **Volume growth was minimal**: ~33.8B unit cases in 2025, only ~16% more than a decade earlier (~1.5%/yr).
- **Dividend grew 61%**: From $1.32/share (2015) to $2.12 today; 64th consecutive annual increase in February 2026.
- **Refranchising drove margin expansion**: Operating income rose from $8.7B to $13.8B; margin from ~20% to ~29%.
- **Pricing carried 2025**: 5% organic revenue growth with flat volume — 4pts from price/mix, 1pt from concentrate.
- **FCF covers dividend comfortably**: ~$12.4B projected FCF vs. ~$9B dividend cost in 2026.
- **Recent volume acceleration**: Q2 unit case volume +5% YoY; 2025 organic revenue guidance raised to ~5%.
- **Valuation concern**: ~25x earnings and 2.4% yield — "safety and value aren't the same thing."

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Asset-Light Business Model]]**: Refranchising transformed Coca-Cola from a capital-heavy manufacturer into a brand licensor and concentrate seller — a first-principles shift that explains how margins and cash flow improved even as top-line revenue temporarily shrank.
- **[[Pricing Power as a Moat]]**: A durable brand allows price increases that outpace volume stagnation; this is the core mechanism by which Coca-Cola compounds shareholder returns without needing to sell more units.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: How has Coca-Cola grown its dividend 61% over a decade despite nearly flat unit case volume?
**A**: The gains were funded by refranchising low-margin bottling operations (expanding operating margin from ~20% to ~29%) and sustained pricing power, not volume growth.

**Q2**: What does Coca-Cola's 2025 organic revenue breakdown reveal about its growth drivers?
**A**: Organic revenue grew 5% with flat volume — four of those five points came from price and mix, and only one point from concentrate sales, confirming pricing as the dominant lever.

**Q3**: Why does the author refrain from recommending KO despite its impressive dividend streak?
**A**: At ~$88/share, KO trades at ~25x earnings with a 2.4% yield — the author argues that dividend safety and stock value are not the same thing, and the current premium does not offer an attractive entry point.
