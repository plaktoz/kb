## Summary

Roblox shares plunged 26.9% on July 31 — its biggest one-day decline ever — after missing Q2 bookings and guiding Q3 bookings below expectations without offering a full-year forecast, even as Amazon surged 15.3% on a cloud-driven earnings beat the same day. Two dissenting Fed officials, Cleveland's Beth Hammack and Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari, both publicly pushed for a rate hike this week, arguing "now is the time" to act on inflation.

## Core Concepts

- [[Roblox]] — worst single-day stock decline in company history after a Q2 bookings miss and weak Q3 guidance, though 19 of 34 covering analysts still rate it a Buy
- [[Amazon Q2 2026 Earnings]] — 15.3% surge on strong cloud-segment revenue growth, despite soft Q3 guidance (Prime Day timing) and a capex increase to $220B from $200B
- [[Federal Reserve Dissent]] — Hammack and Kashkari both dissented from the Fed's rate-hold decision in favor of a quarter-point hike
- [[Interactive Brokers]] — economist José Torres described bond markets as "bond vigilantes" protesting Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's patient posture

## Key Takeaways

- **Roblox's 26.9% single-day drop** is the largest in the company's history, driven by a bookings miss and no full-year forecast
- **Amazon's "exceptional" quarter** showed evidence custom silicon and AI commercialization are gaining traction, per REX Financial's Bill Birmingham
- **Two Fed presidents publicly dissented** this week, both preferring an immediate quarter-point hike over holding rates steady
- **10-year and 30-year Treasury yields hit their highest levels since 2007**, capping the stock market's advance despite the earnings-driven rally
- **Analyst sentiment on Roblox remains net-positive** (19 Buy, 13 Hold, 2 Sell) despite the historic single-day crash, betting on stable retention even as monetization lags

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Divergent Reaction to Shared Conditions]]**: Amazon and Roblox reported in the same macro environment (same rates, same AI-spending backdrop) yet moved in opposite extremes — a reminder that company-specific execution (guidance credibility, monetization proof) dominates stock reaction far more than the shared macro backdrop.
