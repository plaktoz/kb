## Summary

The [[Momentum Trade]] — buying recent winners — has unwound sharply in July 2026, with [[MTUM]] down 12% while the [[S&P 500]] held roughly flat, as volatility in AI infrastructure stocks hit a 45-year high excluding recessions. [[Morgan Stanley]] strategist [[Michael Wilson]] argues this is a healthy "[[Market Broadening]]," with consumer discretionary and transports outpacing the S&P 500 by 12% over two months as capital rotates into names outside the AI trade, supporting a year-end S&P target of 8000.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Momentum Trade]]** — strategy of buying recent stock-market winners; MTUM ETF tracks it, down 12% since July 2026 began
- **[[Market Broadening]]** — Morgan Stanley's thesis that S&P 500 gains will increasingly come from outside mega-cap AI names
- **[[Equal Weight S&P 500]]** — RSP ETF up 11% YTD vs. S&P's 9%, evidencing the broadening trend
- **[[Goldman Sachs Momentum Factor]]** — volatility surged to highest level in 45-year history excluding recession years

## Key Takeaways

- **MTUM down 12%** since the start of July 2026 while the S&P 500 stayed roughly flat.
- **Consumer discretionary and transports outpaced S&P 500 by 12%** over the past two months.
- **Morgan Stanley's downside floor is 7000** for the S&P 500 if the AI-stock decline spreads further.
- **Year-end S&P 500 target of 8000** remains "very achievable" per Morgan Stanley, implying ~7% upside.
- **Equal-weight ETF (RSP) outperforming**: 11% YTD versus the cap-weighted S&P's 9%.
