## Summary

The S&P 500 closed at an all-time high (7,798.99) on August 13, 2026, but Bank of America strategist Michael Hartnett warns that surging U.S. national debt (set to top $40 trillion) and rising 30-year Treasury yields (near 5.24%, a decade high) are the two forces that could derail the market's strong run.

## Core Concepts

- **[[National Debt Trajectory]]**: BofA's Hartnett notes U.S. national debt is "set to surpass $40tn in coming days, on course for $50tn by '29," with July's budget deficit ($432.3B) the highest monthly level in five years, driven partly by Medicare costs.
- **[[Treasury Yield Risk]]**: The 30-year Treasury yield hovered near 5.24%, levels not seen in over a decade; a 30-year bond auction this week produced the highest yield since 2001.
- **[[ABB Asset Allocation Framework]]**: Hartnett's shorthand for 2020s positioning — "Anything but Bonds," "Anywhere but China," "Anything but the US Dollar," "all-in on AI" — reflecting policymaker conviction that a nominal GDP boom will resolve indebtedness.
- **[[Sector Rotation]]**: Energy led gains this week (+~6%), with healthcare and financials also outperforming, while technology dragged on relative underperformance.

## Key Takeaways

- **New record**: S&P 500 topped 7,800 intraday and closed at an all-time high of 7,798.99 on Thursday, August 13.
- **Debt risk**: National debt approaching $40 trillion, projected to reach $50 trillion by 2029.
- **Yield risk**: 30-year Treasury yield near 5.24%; a 30-year bond auction saw the highest yield since 2001.
- **Deficit spike**: July's federal budget shortfall hit $432.3 billion, the largest monthly deficit in over five years.
- **Two named risks**: BofA identifies rising national debt and rising bond yields as the specific pair of factors that could end the bull run.
