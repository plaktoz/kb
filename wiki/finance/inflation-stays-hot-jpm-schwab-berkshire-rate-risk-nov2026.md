## Summary

The October CPI report showed sticky inflation (headline +0.4% m/m, core +0.3% for a third month), raising expectations of a 25 basis point Fed hike in December. The article argues higher rates help financials only when asset yields reprice faster than funding costs, and picks JPMorgan, Charles Schwab, and Berkshire Hathaway for balance sheet strength. Rankings are Zacks Rank #3 for JPM and Schwab, #2 for Berkshire.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Consumer Price Index]]**: October CPI released Nov. 10, 2026; energy rose 0.7% after September's 5.2%.
- **[[Federal Reserve Rate Hikes]]**: A Reuters poll of 101 economists expects 25 bps in December; Governor [[Christopher Waller]] supports another increase.
- **[[Net Interest Margin]]**: Bank profit depends on asset repricing versus deposit and wholesale funding costs.
- **[[JPMorgan Chase]]**: Q3 net income $15.3 billion, deposits up 8%, CET1 ratio 13.9%.
- **[[Charles Schwab]]**: Q3 net interest revenue up 14%, partly offset by wholesale borrowings and floating-rate yields.
- **[[Berkshire Hathaway]]**: About $352.3 billion in cash and T-bills as of Sept. 30, 2026.
- **[[Zacks Rank]]**: Rating system cited for each pick.

## Key Takeaways

- **Sticky Inflation**: Headline CPI +0.4% in October after +0.6% in September.
- **Hike Odds**: Most surveyed economists expect a December 25 bps hike to 4.00%-4.25%.
- **Not Automatic**: Higher rates do not automatically lift financial profitability.
- **Schwab Evidence**: Net interest revenue rose 14% yet was dented by funding costs.
- **Selection Criteria**: Favor strong balance sheets, diversified revenue, disciplined funding.
- **Berkshire Liquidity**: Insurance businesses alone hold $230.9 billion in cash and T-bills.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Rate benefit depends on repricing speed** — A rate rise lifts a lender's profit only to the extent asset yields reprice faster than funding costs; otherwise the gain is offset or reversed.
→ explains: Not Automatic, Schwab Evidence

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does the article say higher rates create both opportunities and challenges for financials?
**A**: They can widen net interest margins, but they also raise borrowing costs, weaken loan demand, and increase credit losses. Benefit depends on how fast asset yields reprice versus funding costs.

**Q2**: What Schwab data illustrates that higher rates do not guarantee stronger profits?
**A**: Q3 2026 net interest revenue rose 14%, but higher wholesale borrowings and lower floating-rate asset yields partially offset the gains as lending demand outpaced deposit growth.

**Q3**: How should investors choose financial stocks in a higher-for-longer environment?
**A**: Prioritize strong balance sheets, diversified revenue, disciplined funding, and the ability to sustain earnings across rate cycles, as with JPMorgan, Schwab, and Berkshire.
