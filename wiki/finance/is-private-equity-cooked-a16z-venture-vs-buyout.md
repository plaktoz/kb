## Summary

[[Jen Kha]] of [[a16z]] argues that tech buyouts, long the "safe" private-markets bet, are under structural stress from high [[Leverage]], 2021-22 vintages, owner-set valuations and the end of the conditions that powered the 2010s. By contrast, she says top-tier [[Venture Capital]] benefits from the start of a new technology wave and has outperformed buyout. Her advice is to check 2021-22 vintages for PIK interest, find an exit route, and be in the top 5% of venture if investing there.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Private Equity]] buyout evolution**: from lumpy, outlier-driven deals (Wesray's 1982 Gibson Greetings buyout) to professionalizing family businesses, to buying from other sponsors or taking public companies private.
- **2010s tailwinds**: falling rates and rising multiples "powered over 50% of all buyout returns" (Bain); global buyout AUM grew from $1.6T (2015) to $3.9T (2021). [[SaaS]] was ideal: recurring revenue that was easy to lend against.
- **Debt burden**: PE-backed middle-market companies carry 4.7-6.0x net debt/EBITDA versus about 2x for the median S&P 500 company (J.P. Morgan).
- **2021-22 vintage stress**: many companies add interest to the loan (PIK); loan-to-value rose from 44% to 75% in a year; 70% of debt foreclosed in 2025-26 came from 2021-22 deals. [[Thoma Bravo]]'s roughly $6.4B Medallia buyout was handed to lenders led by [[Blackstone]], wiping out about $5.1B of equity.
- **Owner-set marks**: PE owners appraise their own holdings, while venture marks rise only when outside investors price a new round. Workarounds include continuation vehicles (14% of PE exit value in H1 2026) and NAV loans.
- **[[Power Law]] in venture**: venture invests in out-of-the-money options and seeks fund-returning outliers; a top 5% venture fund (2007-16 vintages) returned 5.3x DPI versus 3.5x for a top 5% buyout fund.
- **Cyclical or structural**: PE-backed tech exit value is down 77% year over year; buyers are buying new technology; buyout fundraising fell 16% in 2025 to $395B.
- **"12 is the new 5"**: a deal needing about 5% annual EBITDA growth in 2015 now needs 10-12% (Bain), while Golub's software borrowers slowed from 9% to under 3% revenue growth.

## Key Takeaways

- **Late-Cycle Asset**: Tech buyout works once a technology wave settles; new waves favor venture.
- **Returns Gap**: Ten-year US buyout returned 14.5% a year versus 17.6% for venture, unlevered.
- **Debt Load**: PE-backed companies carry roughly 2.5-3x the net debt of the median S&P 500 company.
- **2021 Vintage Trouble**: 70% of 2025-26 foreclosed debt came from 2021-22 deals.
- **Marks Lag Reality**: PE owners set their own valuations; venture marks need outside investors' money.
- **Hidden Stress**: Continuation vehicles reached 14% of exit value; NAV loans add a second debt layer.
- **Growth Hurdle**: Deals now need 10-12% EBITDA growth versus about 5% in 2015.
- **Top-5% Matters**: Top venture funds beat top buyout funds on DPI (5.3x vs 3.5x) and TVPI.
- **Not Overnight**: Smaller non-software buyouts can still do fine; giants and 2021 software weigh on benchmarks.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Leverage magnifies the damage when asset values stagnate and financing costs rise** — Debt-financed returns depend on rising prices and cheap credit; when both reverse, the lender's claim can outgrow the asset and wipe out equity. · *model:* [[Leverage]]
→ explains: Debt Load, 2021 Vintage Trouble, Growth Hurdle

**T2. Returns concentrate in a few outliers, so manager selection dominates** — In a power-law business the median is poor and value sits in the top few percent, so exposure without access to the top tier does not earn the asset class's returns. · *model:* [[Power Law]]
→ explains: Top-5% Matters, Returns Gap

**T3. Valuations set by the holder are weak evidence of value** — A mark decided by the party that owns the asset can stay flat despite stress, while one set by outside buyers reflects what the market will pay. · *model:* [[Incentive-Caused Bias]]
→ explains: Marks Lag Reality, Hidden Stress

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Kha's central claim about private equity versus venture?
**A**: Tech buyouts, once the safe bet, face structural stress from heavy debt, 2021-22 vintages and the end of 2010s tailwinds, while top-tier venture benefits from a new technology wave and has outperformed buyout.

**Q2**: What evidence does she give that buyout valuations may overstate reality?
**A**: PE owners appraise their own holdings, and exits increasingly route through continuation vehicles (14% of exit value in H1 2026) and NAV loans. Publicly traded BDCs heavy in software trade well below NAV.

**Q3**: What should an investor with buyout exposure do, according to the article?
**A**: Ask managers how much PIK interest is accruing on 2021-22 vintages and find an exit route. Shifting to private credit or secondaries leaves exposure to the same buyout companies, and venture only pays off if you are in the top 5%.
