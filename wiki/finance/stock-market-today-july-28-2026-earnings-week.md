## Summary

Major U.S. indexes closed mostly higher on July 28, 2026 as strong blue-chip earnings (Sherwin-Williams, Coca-Cola, Boeing) offset a continued chip-stock selloff tied to [[AI Bubble]] fears, with the Dow up ~550 points while the Nasdaq slipped on losses in Micron, Sandisk, and SK Hynix. The session opened the "busiest week of earnings season," with Microsoft, Meta, Apple, and Amazon all reporting, alongside a two-day [[Federal Reserve]] meeting where traders priced a rising probability of a surprise rate hike.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Spending Skepticism]]** — despite ~90% of reporting S&P 500 companies beating estimates, tech-sector post-earnings stock reactions have lagged the historical average, reflecting investor unease about the payoff on AI capex
- **[[Credit Risk from AI Correction]]** — Fitch warned an AI-driven market correction combined with Middle East geopolitical risk could become a "major credit risk"; a Bank of America fund manager survey found nearly half see AI spending as the top systemic risk
- **[[GDP and AI Capex]]** — hyperscaler capex (~$700B projected for 2026) is estimated to account for a full percentage point of U.S. GDP growth this year

## Key Takeaways

- **Dow** +1.03% (~550 pts), **S&P 500** +0.2%, **Nasdaq** −0.2%
- Chip stocks (Micron, Sandisk, Western Digital, Seagate) fell 7.5%–14% amid continued AI-trade rotation
- Oil fell sharply (WTI −4.3%) on a U.S.-Iran pause, supporting the broader market
- Fed funds futures priced a 31% chance of a surprise hike, up from 26% a week earlier
- Apple briefly topped $5 trillion market cap, becoming only the second company to do so after Nvidia
