## Summary

US stocks rose on Friday, October 2, 2026, and the [[Nasdaq Composite]] hit a record intraday high after a weak September [[Jobs Report]] showed only 29,000 jobs added. The soft data cut odds of a late-October [[Federal Reserve Rate Hike]] to 23%. Even with the rally, the Dow and [[S&P 500]] still lost ground for the week. AI financing stories also moved markets: [[Amazon]] reportedly wants to move $8 billion of [[Nvidia]] chips off its balance sheet, and [[Broadcom]] is reportedly raising $60 billion to lend to [[Anthropic]] and other AI firms.

## Core Concepts

- **Index performance**: The Nasdaq rose 1.2%, the S&P 500 0.7% and the [[Dow Jones Industrial Average]] 0.5% on Friday. Every sector except [[Health Care Sector|Health Care]] finished higher.
- **Weekly scorecard**: The Nasdaq gained 0.5%, its third straight weekly gain. The Dow fell 1.3% and the S&P 500 fell 0.3%. The Dow has now dropped in four of the last five weeks.
- **September [[Jobs Report]]**: The [[Bureau of Labor Statistics]] reported 29,000 jobs added, against forecasts of roughly 84,000–89,000. August was revised down to 133,000, and revisions to July and August removed 60,000 jobs combined. Unemployment rose to 4.2%.
- **[[10-Year Treasury Yield]]**: The yield fell to 5.16% right after the jobs report, then climbed back toward 5.30%. It hit a 24-year high of nearly 5.35% on Thursday.
- **[[CME FedWatch]]**: Traders put the odds of an October rate hike at 23%, down from 28% before the report and 64% a week earlier.
- **[[Jeffrey Roach]]** (LPL Financial): He described a split between goods-producing sectors that support the [[AI Boom]] and services sectors hurt by technological change. In his view, two Fed hikes are becoming less likely.
- **[[Off-Balance-Sheet Financing]]**: According to the FT, Amazon may move thousands of installed [[Nvidia Blackwell]] chips into a [[Special Purpose Vehicle]] and lease them back. Nine big US tech firms had about $3 trillion in off-balance-sheet commitments as of August, according to the WSJ.
- **[[Circular Financing]]**: Bloomberg reported that Broadcom is assembling up to $60 billion to lend to AI customers such as Anthropic. This is another case of a chipmaker funding its own buyers.
- **Nominal growth vs. yields**: Nominal [[GDP]] grew 6.3% annualized in Q2, which is faster than the 5.3% 10-year yield. That keeps the $40 trillion [[US National Debt]] stable relative to GDP for now.
- **Single-stock movers**: [[Tesla]] beat Q3 delivery estimates. [[Nike]] guided to a bigger sales decline. [[Onsemi]] switched its [[Synaptics]] deal to all cash. [[Seagate Technology]] and [[Western Digital]] fell sharply after a report on [[Toshiba]]'s hard-drive expansion. The FAA cleared a [[Boeing 737 MAX]] software glitch.
- **Crypto**: [[Bitcoin]] is up about 35% over three months, and [[Strategy (MSTR)]] is up more than 70% over the same period. [[Citi]]'s Alex Saunders raised his October 2027 base-case bitcoin target to $113,000.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Bad News Is Good News]]**: A weak jobs report sent stocks higher because it lowered the odds of further Fed hikes. Markets were trading on the rate path more than on growth.
- **[[Debt Dynamics (r vs g)]]**: When nominal growth (6.3%) beats the borrowing rate (5.3%), debt-to-GDP can stay flat or even shrink if deficits are small. That is why record yields haven't yet made the debt burden spiral.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did stocks rise on October 2, 2026, despite a weak jobs report?
**A**: The September report showed only 29,000 jobs added and unemployment at 4.2%. That cut the odds of an October Fed rate hike to 23%, which lifted stocks and sent the Nasdaq to a record intraday high.

**Q2**: How does Amazon's reported SPV plan for Nvidia chips work?
**A**: Amazon would move thousands of installed Nvidia Blackwell chips, worth about $8 billion, into a special purpose vehicle and lease them back. It keeps using the chips without carrying the depreciating assets on its balance sheet.

**Q3**: What does the Amazon SPV and Broadcom financing news suggest about AI spending?
**A**: Big tech is using creative off-balance-sheet and vendor financing to fund AI buildouts. Nine large firms held about $3 trillion in off-balance-sheet commitments, so real AI spending may be larger than it looks, and circular financing risks are growing.
