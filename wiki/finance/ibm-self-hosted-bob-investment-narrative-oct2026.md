## Summary

[[IBM]] has expanded into regulated workloads with self-hosted deployment for [[IBM Bob]], on-premises [[IBM Digital Asset Haven]] options, and a [[Hedera]]-powered identity partner (IDTrust) listed on the IBM Cloud Catalog. Simply Wall St argues these moves, and the addition of Frank Baker as a fourteenth director, don't materially change IBM's near-term investment story. That story still depends on z17, AI consulting, and consumption-based software execution, while client IT budgets remain the main risk.

## Core Concepts

- **Regulated-workload push**: Banks, governments, and critical-infrastructure clients want AI, digital assets, and identity tools running on their own infrastructure. Self-hosted Bob fits the same buyers as Digital Asset Haven and the IDTrust listing.
- **[[Hybrid Cloud]] thesis**: Owning IBM means believing hybrid cloud and AI can keep driving recurring software, [[Red Hat]], and consulting demand, even with revenue growth expected in the low single digits.
- **Near-term swing factors**: Execution on the [[IBM z17]] mainframe, AI consulting, and consumption-based software. Weak spots are macro-sensitive consulting and transactional workloads.
- **Key risks**: Client IT budgets, scrutiny of mainframe spending, and how both feed into Software and Consulting utilization.
- **Conversion test**: How fast Bob's tooling turns into consulting signings, a changed backlog mix, and recurring software revenue, measured against US$24.6b of existing ARR.
- **Governance**: Frank Baker joins as a fourteenth director, which the article treats as background "plumbing."

## Key Takeaways

- **New Products**: Self-hosted Bob, on-prem Digital Asset Haven, Hedera-based IDTrust identity listing.
- **Narrative Impact**: The moves are not material to the near-term thesis; they are background plumbing.
- **Bull Case for Bob**: Adoption by regulated clients could support the gen AI services book.
- **Bear Case**: Pilots that don't scale leave mainframe and macro trends in charge.
- **ARR Baseline**: US$24.6b of annual recurring revenue already booked.
- **Consensus 2029**: US$78.2b revenue and US$11.9b earnings.
- **Growth Rate**: 4.2% projected annual revenue growth; earnings up ~US$1.2b from US$10.7b.
- **Fair Value**: Narrative fair value implies ~8% upside.
- **Bull Analysts**: About US$81.5b revenue and US$14.4b earnings by 2029.
- **Community Range**: 7 other fair value estimates, up to 33% upside.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: According to Simply Wall St, does IBM's self-hosted AI rollout change the near-term investment case?
**A**: Not in a material way. The near-term case still depends on z17, AI consulting, and consumption-based software execution, and the new AI, digital asset, and identity moves sit in the background.

**Q2**: What forecasts anchor IBM's current investment narrative?
**A**: Analysts expect US$78.2b revenue and US$11.9b earnings by 2029, which implies 4.2% annual revenue growth and earnings rising about US$1.2b from US$10.7b. The narrative fair value implies roughly 8% upside.

**Q3**: What should an IBM investor watch to judge whether self-hosted Bob matters?
**A**: Whether Bob moves from pilots to broad rollouts at banks, governments, and infrastructure clients, which would show up in consulting signings, backlog mix, and eventually recurring software revenue on top of the US$24.6b ARR.
