## Summary

At $543.45, after an 83.7% five-year total return, [[Microsoft]] trades broadly in line with its [[Discounted Cash Flow]] intrinsic value. That means investors are already paying for its expected cash-flow ramp. The DCF starts from about $96.0B in trailing free cash flow and assumes it grows through the early 2030s, even as Microsoft keeps funding heavy AI and data center spending. Community narratives split between a 6% undervalued bull case built on AI capacity and a 37% overvalued bear case built on falling free cash flow and $743.8B in contractual obligations.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Discounted Cash Flow]] valuation**: Projects the cash Microsoft can return to shareholders and discounts it to today. Trailing twelve-month FCF is about $96.0B, with a sizeable ramp built in through the early 2030s.
- **Fair-value verdict**: Intrinsic value sits broadly in line with the $543.45 share price, so the market is already pricing in the cash-flow profile (see [[Expectations Investing]]).
- **Infrastructure cost levers**: Microsoft plans to roll out the [[Maia 300]] AI chip at multi-gigawatt scale to control AI infrastructure costs. Recent [[Data Center]] lease commitments reportedly exceed $130B. Both could reshape future cash generation by changing costs and capital needs.
- **Bull narrative (6% undervalued)**: Calendar 2026 capex of roughly $175B–$190B, plus first-party Maia and [[Cobalt]] chips, addresses capacity constraints where AI demand exceeds supply.
- **Bear narrative (37% overvalued)**: Free cash flow fell, capital spending "has stopped behaving like software," and total contractual obligations stand at $743.8B (see [[AI Capex]]).
- **Related**: An earlier Simply Wall St DCF reached a similar fair-value view ([[msft-stock-1-percent-undervalued-ai-revenue]]). See also [[microsoft-msft-near-first-record-close-maia-300-oct2026]].

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Price equals priced-in cash flows** — A stock's value is the present value of the cash it can return to owners. When the price matches that value, future gains depend on beating the cash flows already priced in. · *model:* [[Discounted Cash Flow]]
→ explains: DCF verdict, Priced in, FCF base

**T2. Capital intensity changes how a business converts profit to cash** — When a business must keep spending heavily to grow, less of its profit turns into free cash flow. Its cost and capital structure then drive its valuation as much as its revenue growth.
→ explains: Maia 300, Lease commitments, Bear case
