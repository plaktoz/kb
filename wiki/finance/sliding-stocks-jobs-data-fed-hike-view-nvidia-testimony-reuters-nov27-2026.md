## Summary
Volatile AI-related stocks leave investors anxious ahead of next week's [[Nonfarm Payrolls]] report and the [[Federal Reserve]]'s December 8-9 meeting, where a quarter-point hike is widely expected. Strong data could make the Fed more hawkish, so weak data may paradoxically help stocks. [[Nvidia]] stays in the spotlight with [[Jensen Huang]] set to testify before the Senate Banking Committee.

## Core Concepts
- [[Federal Reserve]]: Expected to hike 25 basis points in December; Governor [[Christopher Waller]] urged a half-point hike or a faster 2027 pace.
- [[Nonfarm Payrolls]]: November report expected at +90,000 jobs and 4.1% unemployment, after October's +165,000.
- [[Nvidia]] and [[Jensen Huang]]: Reinstated as CEO; S&P Global placed the credit rating on CreditWatch Negative; Senate testimony due Thursday.
- [[AI Capex]] and [[Circular Financing]]: Lingering doubts about AI buildout financing and data center returns.
- [[VIX]]: Around 28 versus a long-term median of 17.6.
- [[Stock Market Correction]]: S&P 500 down 8% and Nasdaq down more than 14% from October 6 highs.
- [[Fear of Missing Out]]: Strategist expects stabilization could flip downside fear into FOMO.

## Key Takeaways
- **Double-Edged Data**: Strong growth gives the Fed reason to hike; weakness may ease pressure.
- **Drawdown**: S&P 500 down 8% and Nasdaq down 14%+ since October 6.
- **Fear Gauge**: VIX near 28, well above its 17.6 median.
- **Nvidia Overhang**: Huang's return reassured markets, but credit watch and testimony remain.
- **Skeptical Investors**: Circular financing and AI capex returns were doubted before the Nvidia crisis.
- **Earnings Catalysts**: Salesforce and Marvell results could sway the flagging AI trade.
- **Stabilization Needed**: Portfolio managers avoid assets that keep falling, however cheap.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Rate expectations transmit economic news to valuations** — Higher interest rates raise borrowing costs and pressure equity valuations, so the market's reaction to data depends on its implied Fed response, not only the data's direction. Strong data can be bad for stocks and weak data good.
→ explains: Double-Edged Data

**T2. Buyers wait for price stabilization before stepping in** — Investors avoid buying falling assets regardless of valuation; sentiment turns only once declines pause and fear of loss gives way to fear of missing out.
→ explains: Stabilization Needed, Skeptical Investors

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why might weak economic data be good for stocks right now?
**A**: The Fed is expected to hike, and higher rates pressure equity valuations. Weak data could give it room to be less hawkish, which would help stocks.

**Q2**: What do the figures show about the recent market stress?
**A**: Since October 6 highs the S&P 500 is down about 8% and the Nasdaq more than 14%, with the VIX near 28 against a long-term median of 17.6.

**Q3**: What would signal that the market is ready to turn, according to Keith Lerner?
**A**: A bit of stabilization and back-and-forth trading, since portfolio managers don't like buying things that keep falling. That could shift fear of the downside to fear of missing out.
