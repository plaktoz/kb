## Summary

[[Oracle]] issued a force majeure notice to [[Blue Owl Capital]]'s subsidiary — the developer of its "Project Jupiter" AI data center in New Mexico — citing natural gas pipeline delays that could push the facility past its 2028 deadline. The notice seeks to defer rent payments on a late delivery, though Oracle remains liable for certain fees. Despite Oracle's public insistence that the project remains on schedule, $18 billion in project financing has traded down to 89–91 cents on the dollar, signaling growing lender stress.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Project Jupiter]]**: Oracle's large-scale AI data center under development in New Mexico, financed by ~$18B in project debt arranged through [[Blue Owl Capital]]'s subsidiary; targeted for completion by 2028.
- **[[Force Majeure Notice]]**: A contractual mechanism Oracle invoked to seek rent-payment deferral if construction is delayed, citing blocked natural gas pipeline approvals by New Mexico state regulators — not a cancellation but a risk-transfer attempt.
- **[[Oracle Cloud Infrastructure]] (OCI)**: Posted 121% revenue growth to $7.4B last quarter; remaining performance obligations reached $664B, representing contracted future demand locked in but not yet delivered.
- **[[Project Financing Stress]]**: The $18B in Project Jupiter debt has fallen to 89–91 cents on the dollar, a meaningful discount that signals lenders are pricing in meaningful completion risk.
- **[[Local Opposition and Regulatory Risk]]**: 65% of respondents in an Albuquerque Journal poll opposed data center construction in New Mexico; two separate natural gas pipeline plans have been blocked by state regulators — the proximate cause of the force majeure claim.
- **[[Oracle Balance Sheet Risk]]**: Oracle carries $125B in long-term debt, negative free cash flow of $5.4B last quarter, and $288B in uncommenced lease commitments — all before Project Jupiter's additional financing.

## Key Takeaways

- **Force majeure filed**: Oracle notified Blue Owl's subsidiary it may invoke force majeure if Project Jupiter misses its 2028 deadline.
- **Root cause**: Two separate natural gas pipeline plans blocked by New Mexico regulators.
- **Oracle's public stance**: Insists "Project Jupiter remains on our planned schedule" despite the notice.
- **Lender stress signal**: $18B project financing trading at 89–91 cents on the dollar — meaningful discount from par.
- **Local opposition**: 65% of New Mexico respondents oppose data center construction in the state.
- **OCI growth**: 121% revenue growth, $664B remaining performance obligations last quarter.
- **FCF negative**: Free cash flow ran negative $5.4B last quarter; long-term debt stands at $125B.
- **Uncommenced commitments**: $288B in lease commitments not yet started — off-balance-sheet risk pile-up.
- **Analyst framing**: Oracle's strategy described as a "bet-the-farm approach" to AI infrastructure.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Signaling vs. Reality]]**: Oracle publicly claiming Project Jupiter is on schedule while simultaneously filing a force majeure notice is a classic case of divergent public and private signals — the legal action reveals the company's internal risk assessment more faithfully than its press statements.
- **[[Leverage Amplification]]**: Oracle's combination of $125B long-term debt, negative FCF, $288B in uncommenced leases, and $18B in additional project financing means any execution slip on Project Jupiter is amplified across an already stressed balance sheet — a highly leveraged position where the downside is not linear.
