## Summary
IBM shares fell 12.2% in a week — their steepest weekly decline in nearly six years — as a broad tech selloff driven by rising Treasury yields wiped out post-earnings gains. Retail sentiment turned bullish on the dip while professional analysts remained divided, including a public bear call from short-seller Jim Chanos.

## Key Takeaways
- **Steep Drop**: IBM fell 12.2% in a week, a 5-month low and the worst weekly decline in nearly six years.
- **Sector-Wide**: Nasdaq 100 fell 2.1% (fifth straight losing week); peer Cognizant dropped 16.7%; Workday and Intuit fell double digits.
- **Retail Bullish**: Stocktwits sentiment for IBM climbed to "bullish" as investors framed the drop as a buying opportunity.
- **Bear Case**: Jim Chanos (predicted Enron's collapse) argues AI is a threat, not an opportunity, for IBM.
- **Analyst Split**: 11 of 21 analysts rate IBM Buy or higher; average price target $324.95 implies 24% upside.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What triggered IBM's worst weekly drop in six years?
**A**: A broad tech-sector selloff driven by rising Treasury yields and renewed valuation concerns, which erased IBM's post-earnings gains.

**Q2**: How did retail and professional sentiment diverge?
**A**: Retail sentiment turned bullish, framing the drop as a buying opportunity, while professional analysts remained split — including a bearish call from short-seller Jim Chanos on AI as a threat to IBM.

**Q3**: What's the implication for an IBM holder evaluating this dip?
**A**: The drop reflects sector-wide rotation rather than an IBM-specific problem, but the bull/bear disagreement (11 Buy vs. 3 Sell-rated analysts) means conviction should hinge on one's own view of IBM's AI-driven growth durability.
