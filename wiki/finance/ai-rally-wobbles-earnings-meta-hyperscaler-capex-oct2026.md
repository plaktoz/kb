## Summary
After AI-linked stocks stumbled and the S&P 500 posted its biggest weekly drop since June, investors look to the coming week's earnings, led by Meta, for proof that AI spending pays off. Financing worries around CoreWeave, strategist downgrades of tech, and rate-hike bets add to the jitters. Strong expected Q3 earnings growth is seen as a cushion against higher rates.

## Core Concepts
- [[AI Trade]]: the market-leading rally in AI, semiconductor and hardware stocks.
- [[Hyperscaler Capex]]: five hyperscalers expected to spend about $1.2 trillion next year, per Goldman Sachs.
- [[Meta Platforms]]: first hyperscaler to report; its [[Muse AI Assistant]] lifted shares.
- [[CoreWeave]]: AI cloud firm with about $50 billion of debt whose shares tumbled.
- [[Philadelphia Semiconductor Index]]: down about 12% this month, up 57% on the year.
- [[Federal Reserve]] rate hikes: hot inflation data raised bets on further increases.
- [[Earnings Season]]: S&P 500 Q3 earnings growth expected above 30% per LSEG IBES.
- [[Cost of Capital]]: rising rates make the capex boom costlier.

## Key Takeaways
- **Weekly drop**: S&P 500 had its biggest weekly fall since June, about 2% below record.
- **Rotation**: Healthcare and consumer staples gained while chips and tech hardware sold off.
- **Debt worry**: CoreWeave's roughly $50 billion debt load sparked AI-financing concerns.
- **Analyst caution**: Deutsche Bank and RBC pulled back on tech recommendations.
- **Capex scale**: Five hyperscalers expected to spend about $1.2 trillion next year.
- **Earnings backdrop**: Q3 S&P 500 earnings expected to grow over 30%.
- **Rates**: Most investors expect a second Fed hike in December, not October.
- **Reports due**: Meta, Lam Research, Texas Instruments, SpaceX, Tesla, Netflix.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is testing investor confidence in the AI rally?
**A**: Stumbles in AI-linked stocks, CoreWeave debt worries and strategist pullbacks make investors look to earnings, starting with Meta, for evidence that AI spending is paying off.

**Q2**: Why does the cost of capital matter for the hyperscaler capex boom?
**A**: With the cost of capital rising, the capex blowout becomes more expensive, which raises the bar for returns, per Horizon's Chuck Carlson.

**Q3**: What should investors watch in this earnings season?
**A**: Whether hyperscaler profitability grows faster than capex, and chip-industry demand signals from Lam Research and Texas Instruments.
