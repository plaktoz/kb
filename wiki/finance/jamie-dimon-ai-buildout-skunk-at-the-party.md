## Summary

JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon warned that surging capital demand from the AI data-center buildout could keep inflation elevated and interest rates higher for longer, while separately flagging that leverage across prime brokerage, hedge funds, and Treasury arbitrage is "pretty high."

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Capex Buildout]]**: Hyperscaler capital spending is projected to rise from 1.4% of US GDP in 2025 to 3.1% in 2027 — roughly twice the pace of the US housing boom at its peak, per Apollo economist Torsten Sløk.
- **[[Capital Demand Inflation]]**: Dimon's framing — that inflation reflects not just expectations but the sheer scale of demand for capital — echoes dissenting Fed officials like Cleveland Fed's Beth Hammack.
- **[[Skunk at the Party]]**: Dimon's metaphor for a scenario where surging long-term bond yields (demanded to fund AI infrastructure, deficits, and remilitarization) disrupt an otherwise favorable market environment.
- **[[Situational Awareness Hedge Fund Unwind]]**: A recent real-world stress event — JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, and others demanded more collateral from a hedge fund facing losses on concentrated AI stock bets, which ultimately sold its portfolio to Citadel.

## Key Takeaways

- **Fed held rates at 3.5%–3.75%** last week, with three officials dissenting in favor of a hike — Dimon's warning echoes that dissenting view.
- **Alphabet is raising a fresh $25 billion** as part of the broader AI capex wave putting pressure on capital markets.
- Dimon: "I don't know if these things will push the rate up, but if they do, that could be the skunk at the party."
- **Leverage warning**: across prime brokerage, hedge funds, ETPs, and Treasury arbitrage, leverage is "pretty high," raising the odds of a disruptive market event.
