## Summary

The [[S&P 500]] closed at a record 7,757.64 on Friday, August 7, 2026 — its 26th record close of the year — capping a 3.6% weekly gain after a surprisingly weak July jobs report reduced expectations of an imminent [[Federal Reserve]] rate hike. Technology and semiconductor stocks, led by [[Nvidia]], drove much of the rebound, while strong Q2 earnings growth (~50.4%) reinforced the bullish case.

## Core Concepts

- **[[S&P 500]]**: Rose 0.6% Friday to a record 7,757.64; up 3.6% for the week, adding roughly $2.5 trillion in combined market capitalization.
- **[[Federal Reserve]] rate expectations**: A surprise July jobs loss (-23,000 vs. +83,000 expected) and downward revisions to prior months reduced the odds of near-term tightening, pulling Treasury yields lower (2-year ~4.203%, 10-year ~4.657%).
- **[[Nvidia]]**: Gained ~12% for the week, adding about $562 billion in market cap — reportedly its largest single-week gain on record — illustrating how concentrated mega-cap moves can drive a market-cap-weighted index.
- **Q2 2026 earnings season**: S&P 500 earnings growth running at ~50.4%, the strongest since Q2 2021, per FactSet/WSJ data cited in the piece.

## Key Takeaways

- **Weak jobs data ≠ bad for stocks**: A softer labor market reduced Fed-hike odds, which investors read as bullish for equities and growth stocks in particular.
- **Concentration risk**: Nvidia alone added ~$562B in market cap in a week, showing how a handful of mega-caps can dominate index-level moves.
- **Broad participation**: Nasdaq +5.2%, Dow +3%, Russell 2000 +3.5% — gains extended beyond just a few AI-trade names.
- **Elevated expectations, not proof of overvaluation**: The article cautions that record highs reflect high expectations rather than a verdict on fair value; a hot inflation print could reverse the rally.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Reflexivity]]**: Falling rate expectations lower the discount rate applied to future cash flows, which mechanically raises the present value of growth stocks — explaining why a "bad" jobs number produced a "good" market reaction.
