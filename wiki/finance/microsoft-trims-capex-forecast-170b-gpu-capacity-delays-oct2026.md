## Summary

[[Microsoft]] lowered its calendar-2026 capital expenditure forecast to $170 billion from $175 billion, citing delays in third-party "GPU-as-a-Service" capacity until the second half of its fiscal year. The cut came alongside strong fiscal Q1 results, with revenue of $92.3 billion (up 19%) and [[Azure]] up 48%. CFO [[Amy Hood]] expects capex to accelerate as the delayed capacity arrives.

## Core Concepts

- [[Capital Expenditure]]: forecast cut from $175 billion to $170 billion, rare among cloud giants that keep raising AI spending.
- [[GPU-as-a-Service]]: third-party AI cloud providers using [[Nvidia]] GPUs, partnered with Microsoft to meet AI demand.
- [[AI Data Center Delays]]: mostly caused by electricity shortages and local opposition.
- [[Azure]]: revenue up 48% to more than $33 billion, beating expectations.
- [[Microsoft Voluntary Buyout]]: offer to more than a quarter of the workforce; at most 5% of eligible employees expected to accept.
- [[Amy Hood]]: Microsoft CFO who gave the forecast on the earnings call.

## Key Takeaways

- **Capex Cut**: Calendar-2026 capex forecast trimmed to $170B from $175B.
- **Cause**: Third-party GPU-as-a-Service contracts delayed to second half of fiscal year.
- **Revenue**: $92.3 billion for fiscal Q1, up 19%.
- **Azure Growth**: Up 48% to more than $33 billion, beating Wall Street expectations.
- **Market Reaction**: Shares rose about 3% in after-hours trading.
- **Capex Rebound**: Hood expects spending to accelerate once delayed capacity arrives.
- **Headcount**: Fell 2% in the quarter; buyout uptake expected at 5% at most.
