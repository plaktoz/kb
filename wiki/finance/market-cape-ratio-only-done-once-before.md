## Summary
The S&P 500's cyclically-adjusted P/E (CAPE) ratio has surpassed 40 for only the second time ever — the first being January 1999, ahead of the dot-com bust. Today's rally, driven by roughly $700B in planned AI infrastructure spending from Amazon and Alphabet alone, draws direct parallels to that era, though the outcome remains uncertain.

## Core Concepts
- The [[CAPE Ratio]] (cyclically-adjusted price-to-earnings) as a long-run valuation gauge.
- The [[Dot-Com Bubble]] parallel — extraordinary capital spending on a new business category preceding a market top.
- [[AI Infrastructure Spending]] as the current cycle's growth driver.

## Key Takeaways
- **Second Time Ever**: CAPE ratio near 41 (crossed 40 in May 2026) — only the second time in history, after January 1999 (peaked at 44 in Jan 2000).
- **Four Straight Up Years**: S&P 500 up 14% YTD; a fourth consecutive double-digit gain year hasn't happened since the late 1990s.
- **Massive Capex**: Amazon and Alphabet alone plan ~$700B in AI infrastructure spending this year; JPMorgan's Dimon expects hyperscaler spend to reach $1T next year.
- **Historical Recovery**: Even after the 2000-2002 slide, the S&P 500 gained 782% from 2003 onward.
- **Recommended Hedge**: Maintain a diversified mix of growth and defensive/dividend stocks.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models
- **[[Base Rate Thinking]]**: The article explicitly compares today's valuation extreme to its only historical precedent (1999-2000) rather than assuming "this time is different," anchoring judgment in the rare base rate of CAPE > 40 events.
