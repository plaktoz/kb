## Summary

[[Microsoft]] priced a $25 billion, ten-tranche senior unsecured bond offering on October 29, 2026, with closing expected November 4. The deal adds debt markets to cash flow and leasing as funding for a $170 billion 2026 capex target, but proceeds are not earmarked for AI and may also fund buybacks, acquisitions, working capital and debt repayment.

## Core Concepts

- [[Microsoft]] bond offering: ten tranches of senior unsecured notes, two floating-rate ($1 billion each, due 2028 and 2029) and eight fixed-rate.
- [[AI Capital Expenditure]]: a $170 billion 2026 target to serve customers including [[Anthropic]] and [[OpenAI]].
- [[Amy Hood]]: said Microsoft would supplement cash generation with other funding sources when conditions are attractive.
- [[AAA Credit Rating]]: Microsoft holds S&P's top-tier rating.
- [[Free Cash Flow]]: positive, as reported on October 28.
- [[Debt Maturity Structure]]: repayment dates run from 2028 to 2066.

## Key Takeaways

- **Deal Size**: $25 billion priced October 29; closing expected November 4.
- **Not Earmarked**: Proceeds may fund buybacks, acquisitions, working capital, capex, or debt repayment.
- **Funding Mix**: Debt joins operating cash flow and leasing as financing channels.
- **Rate Range**: Fixed rates climb from 4.85% (2028) to 6.35% (2066).
- **Largest Tranches**: $4 billion each due 2036 at 5.55% and 2056 at 6.25%.
- **Strong Position**: AAA rating and positive free cash flow accompany the borrowing.
- **Underwriters**: J.P. Morgan, UBS and Wells Fargo are among the book-runners.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did Microsoft announce, and what is its stated purpose?
**A**: It priced a $25 billion ten-tranche bond offering for general corporate purposes, not earmarked for AI, adding financing alongside its $170 billion capex push.

**Q2**: How is the offering structured?
**A**: Two $1 billion floating-rate tranches due 2028 and 2029 plus eight fixed-rate tranches from 4.85% due 2028 to 6.35% due 2066, spreading repayment over decades.

**Q3**: What does the deal imply about how Microsoft funds its buildout?
**A**: Microsoft combines positive free cash flow, leasing and now debt markets, so it borrows from a position of strength rather than after turning cash-flow negative.
