## Summary

A New Mexico court ordered [[Meta Platforms]] to pay more than $900 million into a fund addressing harm to young users of Facebook and Instagram, bringing Meta's total financial liability in the case to $942 million after a March jury verdict found the company breached state consumer-protection law by misrepresenting platform safety for children.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Meta Platforms]] child safety liability**: Judge Bryan Biedscheid's ruling requires both the $900M+ fund payment and additional child-safety measures, on top of a previously imposed civil penalty.
- **New Mexico consumer protection law**: The legal basis for the March jury verdict finding Meta misrepresented the safety of its platforms for children.
- **Regulatory pattern**: Part of a broader wave of state-level legal action against major tech platforms over child-safety practices.

## Key Takeaways

- Total Meta liability in this case now stands at $942 million, combining the new fund payment and a prior civil penalty.
- The ruling includes non-monetary requirements: Meta must introduce additional measures to improve child safety on its platforms.
- The case stems from a March 2026 jury verdict, with this ruling determining the financial and operational remedy.
