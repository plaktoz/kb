## Summary

The Vanguard U.S. Momentum Factor ETF (VFMO) has outperformed the S&P 500 in four of the last seven years by systematically buying stocks with the strongest 12-month price momentum, though its near-100% annual turnover makes it better suited for tax-advantaged accounts than taxable ones — relevant context for holders of momentum-factor funds like SPMO and IWMO.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Momentum Factor Investing]]**: A rules-based strategy that selects stocks with strong trailing 12-month total returns (confirmed by a 6-month check to avoid stale trends), rather than picking stocks discretionarily.
- **[[Vanguard U.S. Momentum Factor ETF (VFMO)]]**: Holds 670 stocks (98.1% US-based), with a $16.8B median market cap portfolio — far smaller-cap-tilted than the $455.6B median of the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO).
- **[[Turnover and Tax Efficiency Tradeoff]]**: VFMO's 99.9% annual turnover (fiscal year ending Nov 2025) generates capital gains distributions that are more efficiently sheltered in IRAs/401(k)s than taxable brokerage accounts.
- **[[Small/Mid-Cap Momentum Exposure]]**: Because the model isn't limited to large-caps, it can capture momentum in smaller companies that large-cap indexes systematically underweight.

## Key Takeaways

- **Track record**: Outperformed the S&P 500 in 4 of the last 7 years, roughly tied in another.
- **Fund stats**: $2.0B AUM, 0.60% dividend yield, 0.13% expense ratio.
- **Top holdings**: KLAC (1.24%), AMAT (1.22%), MU (1.22%) — semiconductor-equipment heavy as of August 2026.
- **Risk**: No guarantee of annual outperformance; the fund can decline more steeply than the S&P 500 in downturns.
- **Account fit**: High turnover (99.9%) makes tax-advantaged accounts (IRA, 401k) preferable to taxable accounts.
