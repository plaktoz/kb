## Summary

The [[VanEck Semiconductor ETF]] (SMH) has outperformed the S&P 500 in 8 of 10 calendar years from 2016–2025, with a $1,000 investment at end-2015 growing to ~$15,000 by end-2025 versus ~$4,000 in a broad index fund. The fund carries significant concentration risk — [[Nvidia]] alone represents ~23% of its ~$72B in assets — and in its two losing years (2018, 2022) it fell roughly twice as deep as the market. The recommended approach is to hold SMH alongside a broad index fund, sized for the possibility of a ~33% drawdown.

## Core Concepts

- [[VanEck Semiconductor ETF]] (SMH) — a thematic ETF tracking the semiconductor sector with ~$72B in assets
- [[S&P 500]] — broad U.S. equity benchmark used as the performance comparison
- [[Concentration Risk]] — top five holdings comprise roughly half the fund's assets; [[Nvidia]] alone ~23%
- [[Drawdown]] — SMH fell ~33% in 2022 (its worst year of the decade), about twice the index's loss
- [[Portfolio Sizing]] — position sizing so a worst-case drawdown does not trigger panic selling

## Key Takeaways

- **Track record**: Beat the S&P 500 in 8 of 10 years from 2016–2025.
- **Margin of outperformance**: Won by at least ~13 percentage points in every winning year.
- **Long-run wealth effect**: 10x vs. 4x growth on a $1,000 investment over the decade.
- **Downside severity**: Lost ~2x more than the index in its two losing years (2018, 2022).
- **Recovery pattern**: SMH's two best years (2019, 2023) came directly after its two worst.
- **Concentration**: [[Nvidia]] at ~23%; top five holdings ~50% of assets.
- **Recommendation**: Pair with a broad index fund; size position to survive a ~33% drop.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Volatility Drag]]**: SMH's larger drawdowns mean the fund must recover more ground than a broad index just to break even — the price of outsized upside is outsized downside, which compounding amplifies in both directions.
- **[[Mean Reversion]]**: The consistent pattern of SMH's worst years being immediately followed by its best years suggests cyclical over- and under-shooting around an underlying trend, a classic reversion dynamic in high-beta sectors.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core performance claim made about SMH?
**A**: SMH outperformed the S&P 500 in 8 of 10 calendar years from 2016–2025 and is on pace for a ninth win in 2026, turning a $1,000 investment into ~$15,000 versus ~$4,000 in an S&P 500 index fund.

**Q2**: How deep were SMH's losses in its two underperforming years, and what happened immediately after?
**A**: In 2018 and 2022, SMH fell roughly twice as deep as the broader market (about a third of its value in 2022); in both cases, the following year (2019 and 2023) became SMH's best years of the decade.

**Q3**: How should an investor size a position in SMH according to the article?
**A**: Hold it alongside a broad index fund rather than as a replacement, and size the position so that a ~33% drawdown would not trigger a panic sell.
