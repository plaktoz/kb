## Summary
Most of the "Magnificent Seven" stocks have fallen sharply from their 52-week highs, with Meta and Alphabet effectively in bear markets. Post-earnings pressure on free cash flow from aggressive AI spending drove the declines at Meta, Alphabet, Amazon and Tesla, while Nvidia fell on fears of hyperscaler overspending. Only Microsoft and Apple are holding up, for company-specific reasons.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Magnificent Seven]]**: [[Apple]], [[Microsoft]], [[Nvidia]], [[Amazon]], [[Tesla]], [[Meta]] and [[Alphabet]], nicknamed the "Unflattering Seven" here.
- **[[Free Cash Flow]] pressure**: aggressive [[AI Capex]] weighed on post-earnings cash generation.
- **[[Hyperscaler]] spending risk**: Nvidia is hit by fears that customers will pull back in 2027.
- **[[Bear Market]] territory**: declines of 20%+ from [[52-Week High]]s.
- **[[Microsoft Azure]]**: strong growth plus positive free cash flow supported Microsoft.

## Key Takeaways
- **Meta and Alphabet**: Both down more than 20% from 52-week highs.
- **Other Drawdowns**: Tesla -19.2%, Amazon -15.1%, Nvidia -12.2%.
- **Earnings Trigger**: Meta, Alphabet, Amazon and Tesla fell on pressured free cash flow.
- **Nvidia Fear**: Hyperscaler overspending may force a pullback in 2027.
- **Resilient Names**: Microsoft showed positive free cash flow; Apple seen as cash-rich hardware play.
- **Strategist View**: BTIG's Jonathan Krinsky says tech weakness is too large to ignore.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about the Magnificent Seven?
**A**: Most of them are falling sharply from their 52-week highs, with Meta and Alphabet effectively in bear markets, making tech weakness too large for the market to ignore.

**Q2**: Why did Meta, Alphabet, Amazon and Tesla fall, and why is Nvidia down?
**A**: The four fell after earnings showed pressured free cash flow amid aggressive AI spending. Nvidia is down on fears that overspending hyperscalers will be forced to pull back in 2027.

**Q3**: Why are Microsoft and Apple holding up, and what does that imply for investors?
**A**: Microsoft showed positive free cash flow alongside strong Azure growth, and Apple is viewed as a cash-rich hardware play rather than an AI play. Cash generation and AI-spend exposure are the differentiators in this selloff.
