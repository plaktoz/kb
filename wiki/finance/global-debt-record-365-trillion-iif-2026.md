## Summary

Global debt across government, household, financial, and non-financial corporate sectors hit a record US$365 trillion in the first half of 2026, marking the sixth consecutive quarterly increase, with [[China]] and the [[United States]] driving the bulk of the growth, according to the [[Institute of International Finance]]'s latest Global Debt Monitor.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Institute of International Finance]] (IIF)**: Banking trade group whose Global Debt Monitor report tracks debt across more than 100 mature and emerging market economies.
- **[[Global Debt Monitor]]**: The IIF's quarterly report tracking total debt across the four major borrowing sectors — government, household, financial, and non-financial corporate.
- **[[China]] and [[United States]]**: The two economies accounting for the bulk of the latest debt increase.
- **[[Fiscal Sustainability]]**: The overarching concern cited amid the sixth consecutive quarterly rise in global debt.

## Key Takeaways

- Global debt reached a record **US$365 trillion** in H1 2026.
- This marks the **sixth consecutive quarterly increase** in global debt.
- **China and the US** account for the bulk of the increase.
- Data spans **more than 100** mature and emerging market economies.
- Per IIF director Emre Tiftik, this debt wave differs from earlier ones (e.g. the 2008 financial crisis, Covid-19) that were triggered by discrete shocks.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core finding of the IIF's latest Global Debt Monitor report?
**A**: Global debt across government, household, financial, and corporate sectors hit a record US$365 trillion in H1 2026 — the sixth consecutive quarterly increase — led by borrowing in China and the US.

**Q2**: What makes the current global debt wave different from previous ones, according to the IIF?
**A**: IIF director Emre Tiftik notes this debt wave, unlike earlier ones, was not triggered by a discrete shock such as the 2008 global financial crisis or the Covid-19 pandemic.

**Q3**: Why does this matter for anyone tracking macro or market risk?
**A**: A record and still-rising global debt load, occurring without a shock as the trigger, points to a structural rather than cyclical build-up — reinforcing mounting concerns over global fiscal sustainability that investors and policymakers should monitor.
