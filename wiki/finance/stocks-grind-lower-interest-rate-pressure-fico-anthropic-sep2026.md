## Summary

US stocks slipped again Tuesday as oil prices pulled back but bond yields kept climbing on inflation, energy-cost, and government-debt concerns, even as Consumer Confidence hit its lowest level since 2014. Fair Isaac (FICO) extended a multi-month collapse after federal housing officials moved to break its mortgage-scoring monopoly, while Anthropic's IPO prospectus revealed plans to spend $518 billion on AI infrastructure alongside a $42 billion 2025 net loss.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Treasury Yields]]**: The 30-year yield rose for a sixth straight session to 5.587%, crossing 5.61% for the first time since 2002; the 2-year and 10-year yields also touched new 52-week highs before pulling back.
- **[[Oil Prices]]**: WTI crude fell 3.7% to $89.21/barrel and Brent fell 1.9% to $95.95, easing one source of inflation pressure even as yields kept rising.
- **[[Consumer Confidence]]**: The Conference Board index fell for a third straight month to 81.9 in September, its lowest since April 2014, well below the 89.0 consensus forecast.
- **[[FICO]] mortgage monopoly fight**: [[Federal Housing Finance Agency]] Director [[Bill Pulte]] moved Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to a single mortgage pricing grid that adds [[VantageScore]] alongside FICO Classic, ending FICO's exclusive scoring position; FICO fell another 26.6%, the S&P 500's worst performer, after Pulte said FICO had raised its per-score price 1,800% since 2020.
- **[[Anthropic]] IPO prospectus**: Plans to spend $518 billion on AI infrastructure buildout, per Reuters; 2025 net loss of $42 billion and operating loss of $8 billion against revenue that grew 12x to ~$4.6 billion, with $20.3 billion cash on hand as of December 31.
- **Seasonal market pattern**: [[Louis Navellier]] notes Q4 is seasonally strong and the third year of a presidential term is historically the strongest of the four-year cycle, with growth stocks outperforming value stocks for 12 consecutive years despite rate hikes.
- **Global debt backdrop**: Navellier points to $365 trillion in global government debt and argues weaker-growth countries like Japan, the UK, and France face rate pressure without the US's relatively strong GDP growth to offset it.

## Key Takeaways

- **Index moves**: Nasdaq -0.09% to 26,797; S&P 500 -0.8% to 7,683; Dow -0.7% to 51,481.
- **Yield vs. oil divergence**: Oil fell sharply while Treasury yields rose to multi-decade highs, signaling debt and inflation concerns are outweighing energy-cost relief.
- **Consumer sentiment lowest since 2014**: Inflation, gas prices, and job scarcity cited as the main drags.
- **FICO's monopoly ends**: One pricing grid now includes both VantageScore and FICO Classic, following a 1,800% price hike since 2020.
- **Anthropic's scale of spending**: $518 billion planned AI infrastructure spend dwarfs its ~$4.6 billion revenue and $20.3 billion cash position.
- **Dario Amodei's caution**: Even as Anthropic keeps shipping model updates, its CEO is urging other AI developers toward more caution in deployment.
- **IPO timing and valuation**: Anthropic's IPO is not expected until after the November midterms, targeting a $2 trillion valuation.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Regulatory Capture]] and its reversal**: FICO's 1,800% price hike since 2020 reflects the behavior of an entrenched, government-adjacent monopoly extracting rents absent competitive pressure — Pulte's single-pricing-grid mandate is a direct institutional correction once the lack of substitutes became politically visible.
