## Summary

The [[Federal Reserve]] will hold its Oct. 28, 2026 meeting a day before the September [[Personal Consumption Expenditures|PCE]] index is released, a recurring mismatch that leaves the [[FOMC]] deciding on relatively stale data. Deutsche Bank's [[Matt Luzzetti]] argues that moving to six meetings a year — a cadence Fed Chair [[Kevin Warsh]] floated to the committee in July — would give officials data roughly 30% timelier at the cost of two extra weeks between meetings. Meanwhile, hot September inflation has officials signaling patience, with markets pricing a 17% chance of an October hike but nearly 75% for December.

## Core Concepts

- **FOMC calendar mismatch**: The current eight-meeting schedule ignores key data release dates; PCE also landed days after the April and July meetings, and the December meeting precedes the November [[Consumer Price Index|CPI]].
- **Six-meeting proposal**: [[Kevin Warsh]] suggested meeting roughly every two months so more information accumulates and policymakers have more deliberation time; no decision was made.
- **Luzzetti's reform package**: Schedule meetings to capture payroll and inflation releases with time to digest them, publish [[Summary of Economic Projections|economic projections]] at every meeting, and shorten the pre-meeting [[Fed Blackout Period|blackout period]].
- **Political optics**: An eight-week cadence would shift the next meeting to mid-November, after the [[US Midterm Elections]].
- **Fed patience on hikes**: [[Austan Goolsbee]], [[Philip Jefferson]], and [[John Williams]] all want more data before another hike, while September [[FOMC Minutes|minutes]] showed most participants expect another increase by year-end.
- **Backing into PCE**: Officials can estimate PCE from CPI and [[Producer Price Index|wholesale price]] data; [[Capital Economics]] and [[RSM]] published estimates.

## Key Takeaways

- **Stale Data Problem**: Oct. 28 meeting precedes Oct. 29 PCE release.
- **Recurring Pattern**: PCE also arrived days after the April and July meetings.
- **Core Trade-off**: Two extra weeks between meetings for data ~30% timelier.
- **Warsh Proposal**: Six meetings yearly floated in July; no decision made.
- **Blackout Reform**: Shorter blackout lets officials comment after pivotal data releases.
- **Election Optics**: Mid-November meeting would fall after the midterms.
- **Hot CPI**: Headline 3.7% in September; core 0.3% m/m, 2.6% y/y.
- **Wholesale Prices**: Rose over 6%, highest in nearly four years, energy-driven.
- **PCE Estimates**: RSM sees headline 3.6% y/y, core 3.1% y/y.
- **Market Pricing**: 17% odds of October hike; nearly 75% for December.
- **Warsh Neutral**: "I don't prejudge meetings," he said at IMF/World Bank meetings.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Decision cadence should follow information arrival** — The quality of a periodic decision depends on how much relevant information is available at the moment it is made, so a schedule set without regard to when data lands trades decision frequency against decision timeliness.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core argument for the Fed moving from eight to six meetings a year?
**A**: The current calendar ignores data release dates, leaving the Fed with stale data; meeting every eight weeks would give officials a fuller, roughly 30% timelier data set at each meeting.

**Q2**: What concrete example shows the calendar mismatch in October 2026?
**A**: The FOMC meets Oct. 28, but the September PCE index — a key inflation gauge — is released Oct. 29, so officials must estimate it from CPI and wholesale price data.

**Q3**: Besides changing meeting frequency, what other reforms does Luzzetti suggest?
**A**: Publishing economic projections at every meeting rather than quarterly, and shortening the blackout period so officials can comment publicly between pivotal data releases and the meeting.
