## Summary
Singapore's Straits Times Index fell 2.2% to 5,179.17 on Nov 6 as global tech stocks slid on fears of tighter AI regulation after the US Democrats' midterm win. Regional indices also fell sharply, with the Nikkei 225 and Kospi each down 6%. Julius Baer analysts argued the market may be misplacing the regulatory risk and stay constructive on AI.

## Core Concepts
- [[Straits Times Index]]: lost 117.08 points; losers beat gainers 472 to 202 across the broader market.
- [[Singapore Banks]]: DBS fell 2.1%, OCBC 0.7% and UOB 2.5%; DBS earnings disappointed.
- [[AI Regulation Risk]]: Democrats' "blue tsunami" midterm win raised fears of stricter [[Artificial Intelligence]] rules.
- [[Nasdaq Composite]]: down 5.5% on Thursday, driving the global [[Tech Stock Selloff]].
- [[Data Center Development]]: [[Julius Baer]] sees state-level curbs, particularly in Texas, as the more tangible risk.
- [[US Nonfarm Payrolls]]: the next test of whether the economy beyond AI is holding up.

## Key Takeaways
- **STI Decline**: Index lost 2.2% (117.08 points) to close at 5,179.17.
- **Regional Rout**: Nikkei 225 and Kospi each fell 6%; Hang Seng lost 1.3%.
- **Bank Weakness**: DBS down 2.1%, UOB down 2.5%, OCBC down 0.7%.
- **Breadth**: Losers outnumbered gainers 472 to 202 on S$3.2 billion turnover.
- **Trigger**: Tech slid on AI regulation fears after the Democratic midterm win.
- **Analyst View**: Julius Baer says markets may be misplacing the risk; federal rules face a veto.
- **Real Risk**: State curbs on data-centre builds could slow, not halt, AI developments.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did Singapore and regional stocks fall on Nov 6?
**A**: Global tech stocks slid on fears of increased AI regulation after the US Democrats' midterm win, with the Nasdaq Composite down 5.5% on Thursday. The STI lost 2.2% and the Nikkei 225 and Kospi each fell 6%.

**Q2**: What evidence did Julius Baer give that the market may be misplacing the AI regulation risk?
**A**: Bipartisan support for AI as a national strategic topic continues, and federal AI rules face a presidential veto. The more tangible risk is states curbing new data-centre developments, particularly in Texas, which would slow but not halt development.

**Q3**: What should investors watch next, according to the article?
**A**: Julius Baer's head of research said the US payrolls report may reveal whether the economy beyond AI is holding up as well as markets hope, after earnings were overshadowed by politics.
