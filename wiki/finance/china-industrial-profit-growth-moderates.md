## Summary

China's industrial profit growth slowed to 15.1% in June from 21.1% in May, with first-half profits rising 18.7% year-on-year. Resilient exports — particularly in AI-driven chip and equipment manufacturing — are cushioning sluggish domestic demand, producing a two-speed recovery. Policymakers face pressure to deliver targeted stimulus as consumption and the property sector remain weak.

## Core Concepts

- **[[China Industrial Profits]]**: The key gauge of manufacturing-sector health, reflecting pricing power, demand, and export competitiveness.
- **[[Two-Speed Recovery]]**: Export-oriented manufacturers outperform while domestic consumption and property sectors lag, widening the divergence within the economy.
- **[[China Export Economy]]**: Overseas demand, especially from [[AI Chip Manufacturing]] customers, is the primary growth engine sustaining Chinese industry.
- **[[China Domestic Demand]]**: Persistent weakness in consumption and the [[China Property Market]] is dragging overall growth and limiting wage recovery.
- **[[China Politburo Stimulus]]**: The Politburo meeting at end-July is a key policy watch event; broad stimulus is tempered by export resilience and Beijing's preference for targeted easing.
- **[[Automobile Manufacturing China]]**: Profits fell 19.5% in H1 2026; car sales declined for a ninth consecutive month, a stark indicator of consumer weakness.

## Key Takeaways

- **Profit deceleration**: Industrial profit growth eased to 15.1% in June from 21.1% in May.
- **H1 growth**: Full first-half profits rose 18.7% YoY, nearly flat vs. Jan-May's 18.8%.
- **Auto sector pain**: Auto profits down 19.5% H1; nine straight months of declining car sales.
- **Export anchor**: AI-fueled chip and equipment exports are propping up manufacturing.
- **Domestic drag**: Consumption and property weakness slowed Q2 GDP to a three-year low.
- **Wage risk**: Sustained profit recovery is needed before companies resume wage growth.
- **Policy watch**: Broad stimulus unlikely; Politburo expected to deliver targeted easing only.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Dual-Track Economy]]**: When external demand and internal demand diverge sharply, aggregate headline numbers mask sector-level crises — the same mechanism that made Japan's "lost decade" hard to diagnose in real time. China's export-led bright spots are masking a consumer-sector contraction that policy tools have yet to resolve.
