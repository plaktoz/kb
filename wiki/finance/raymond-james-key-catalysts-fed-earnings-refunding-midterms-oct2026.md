## Summary
Raymond James CIO Larry Adam sees an unusually crowded two-week catalyst calendar: the Fed meeting, the busiest Q3 earnings week, the Treasury refunding and the midterms. Markets price only a 10% chance of a hike, but the Warsh-led Fed could surprise hawkishly. Hyperscaler results will test whether AI monetization justifies the investment pace behind the 8,450 S&P 500 target.

## Core Concepts
- **[[FOMC]] / [[Kevin Warsh]]**: Markets price a 10% hike probability, down from about 70% after last month's hike; base case is one more hike in December, then a prolonged pause.
- **[[S&P 500]] Q3 earnings**: 172 companies (37% of market cap) report next week; EPS is up about 29% year over year.
- **[[Hyperscalers]]**: Alphabet, Amazon and Microsoft are judged on cloud revenue, capex guidance and free cash flow.
- **[[Quarterly Refunding Announcement]]**: The November 4 update could trim long-end issuance and possibly eliminate the 20-year bond.
- **[[Midterm Elections]]**: Democrats are favored to win the House; the Senate is a toss-up.
- **[[Inflation]] and [[Oil Prices]]**: Oil near $100 and inflation above target for five-plus years keep risks tilted hawkish.

## Key Takeaways
- **Fed Odds**: Hike probability fell to 10% from about 70% a month ago.
- **Hawkish Risk**: Four FOMC members projected two more hikes by year-end.
- **Earnings Streak**: Fifth straight quarter of double-digit EPS growth, longest since 2018.
- **Beat Rate**: 82% of companies beat EPS estimates versus a 77% ten-year average.
- **AI Capex**: Hyperscaler AI infrastructure spending is expected to top $1 trillion in 2027.
- **Refunding Effect**: Less long-end supply could lower yields temporarily but not cut borrowing.
- **Midterm History**: S&P 500 gained about 15% on average in the 12 months after midterms since 1942.
- **Target**: The firm's 12-month S&P 500 target is 8,450.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Supply shifts move yields briefly; fundamentals set them** — Changing the maturity mix of issuance can nudge yields in the short run, but it doesn't change total borrowing needs, which shift to bills and add refinancing risk. Growth and inflation remain the durable drivers of rates.
