## Summary

US stocks sold off sharply on Wednesday July 29, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average shedding roughly 1,100–1,150 points (-2.2%, its worst day in 15 months) after the [[Federal Reserve]] held interest rates steady on a 9-3 vote and Iran launched a surprise ballistic-missile attack on US forces, sending oil prices surging. The S&P 500 closed at 7,316.15 (-1.5%) and the Nasdaq fell 1.7% as a chip-stock selloff deepened and retail investors recorded their largest net selling of individual stocks since the COVID crash. Microsoft and Meta reported earnings after the bell in a pivotal test for the AI-capex trade, with Apple and Amazon due Thursday.

## Core Concepts

- [[Federal Reserve]] — held rates steady 9-3, with three members dissenting in favor of a hike; Chair [[Kevin Warsh]] reaffirmed resolve on the 2% inflation target
- [[Iran]] conflict escalation — Iran launched an "attempted surprise attack" with ballistic missiles on US forces per US Central Command; Trump threatened retaliation ("we'll be hitting them hard")
- [[Oil Prices]] — WTI jumped 7.4% to $85.10/barrel; Brent crude surged 8.2% to $91/barrel
- [[Chip Stock Selloff]] — iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) fell more than 5%, Roundhill Memory ETF (DRAM) fell more than 6%, following a weaker-than-expected SK Hynix profit beat that fueled AI-bubble worries
- [[Magnificent Seven Earnings]] — Microsoft and Meta reported after the bell; Apple and Amazon due Thursday; Alphabet's capex guidance last week had already spooked markets
- [[CME FedWatch]] — traders priced a 54% chance of a September rate hike, down from 79% before the meeting
- [[Vanda Research]] — retail investors logged their largest net selling of individual stocks since the COVID crash, concentrated in Micron, Sandisk, Seagate, and Western Digital

## Key Takeaways

- Dow fell ~1,100–1,150 points (-2.2%), worst single-day point decline since April 2025 tariff selloff
- S&P 500 closed 7,316.15 (-1.5%); Nasdaq -1.7%, its sixth straight losing session
- 10-year Treasury yield rose to ~4.67% after the Fed decision
- Oil surged 7-8% on renewed US-Iran hostilities after Friday's brief pause
- Retail investors dumped MU, SNDK, STX, WDC most heavily, though Seagate and Western Digital rose on earnings
- Sector damage was uneven: Industrials worst sector (-2.2%, Lennox International -20%); GE HealthCare +12% on strong earnings; VF Corp -17% on weak Vans sales and CFO departure; Teradyne +8%; Ford +2%; Procter & Gamble -2% on a sales miss
- Bitcoin roughly flat near $63,800; gold rose 0.8% to $4,070/oz; US dollar index down 0.5%

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Reflexivity]]**: Renewed Iran hostilities and hawkish Fed messaging compounded existing AI-capex anxiety, showing how unrelated shocks (geopolitics, monetary policy, sector-specific earnings fear) can reinforce a single selloff when sentiment is already fragile.
