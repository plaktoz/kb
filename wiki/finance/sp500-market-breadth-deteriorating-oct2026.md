## Summary

Although the S&P 500 sits not far from its highs, [[Market Breadth]] is rapidly deteriorating, with only 25% of constituents above their 50-day moving average — the lowest since April and down from 70% in mid-August. The narrowing reflects investors crowding back into [[Magnificent Seven]] names while abandoning multinationals hurt by rising rates and commodity prices. Strong earnings and a lack of investor euphoria suggest near-term trouble rather than a major long-term market top.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Market Breadth]]**: Measures how many stocks participate in an index move; weakening breadth means fewer stocks validate the index's level.
- **[[Moving Average]] participation**: Share of S&P 500 stocks above their 50-day and 200-day moving averages, both at lows since April.
- **New highs vs. new lows**: NYSE 52-week lows outnumbered highs for 10 straight sessions and 14 of the last 15.
- **[[Magnificent Seven]] rotation**: Q3 flows went back into [[Nvidia]] and [[Meta Platforms]], away from multinationals pressured by [[Interest Rates]] and commodity prices.
- **Earnings support**: Corporate earnings remain strong, highlighted by [[Micron Technology]]'s latest report.
- **[[Investor Sentiment]] as top signal**: [[Jay Kaeppel]] of [[SentimentTrader]] argues major tops rarely form before most investors board the "euphoria train."
- **Sources**: [[The Kobeissi Letter]] supplied the breadth data; [[Brian Sozzi]] authored the piece.

## Key Takeaways

- **25% of S&P 500 stocks** above 50-day moving average, lowest since April.
- **Sharp drop** from 70% above the 50-day in mid-August.
- **Only 47%** of stocks above their 200-day moving average.
- **NYSE new lows beat new highs** for 10 consecutive trading days.
- **Not a sell signal**, per Kobeissi, but warrants attention in coming weeks.
- **Leadership narrowing**: Investors hunkered in mega-cap tech like Nvidia and Meta.
- **Bright spot**: Earnings remain strong, as Micron's report showed.
- **Sentiment check**: No euphoria yet, so a major long-term top is unlikely.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Contrarian Investing]]**: Kaeppel's argument rests on the contrarian premise that market tops require widespread euphoria — while investors remain anxious about rates, valuations, and geopolitics, there are still buyers left to come in, limiting long-term downside.
