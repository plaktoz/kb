## Summary

[[Bitcoin]] has rallied from roughly $65,000 in mid-August to nearly $90,000 last week, though it has since pulled back to $83,460 (down 1.80% on the day), against a 52-week range of $57,945–$126,079 and a $1.7 trillion market cap. The rally is notable because it occurred despite the [[Clarity Act]] — digital-asset regulatory framework legislation — failing to advance, removing what could have been a major catalyst. The author is skeptical that Bitcoin reaches $100,000 by the end of 2026, arguing that a rally without a clear catalyst can reverse just as quickly, and that Bitcoin remains an unreliable [[Safe Haven Assets|safe-haven]] hedge against equity risk.

## Core Concepts

- **Catalyst-free rallies**: Price moves that lack a clear fundamental driver (here, the failed [[Clarity Act]]) are framed as more fragile and prone to reversal than catalyst-driven moves.
- **[[Bitcoin]] as stock alternative**: One explanation for the rally is rotation into Bitcoin as historically high equity valuations push investors toward alternatives — Bitcoin looks "cheap" relative to its own history, still down ~26% over the trailing 12 months and well below its ~$126,000 all-time high.
- **Safe-haven narrative challenged**: The author disputes the idea of Bitcoin as an equity-risk hedge, citing 2022 as a counterexample — the S&P 500 fell 19% while Bitcoin plummeted 65%, showing Bitcoin tends to amplify broad risk-off moves rather than offset them.

## Key Takeaways

- Bitcoin traded at $83,460 as of writing, down from a near-$90,000 peak last week and up sharply from ~$65,000 in mid-August.
- 52-week range: $57,945.16–$126,079.89; market cap ~$1.7 trillion.
- The rally happened without [[Clarity Act]] passage — a potential regulatory catalyst that did not materialize.
- Bitcoin is still down ~26% over the trailing 12 months, making it look comparatively cheap versus historically high stock valuations.
- Even a move to $100,000 would remain well below Bitcoin's prior all-time high of ~$126,000.
- 2022 counterexample: S&P 500 -19% vs. Bitcoin -65%, undercutting the "Bitcoin as safe haven" thesis.
- Author's conclusion: Bitcoin is unlikely to hit $100,000 by end of 2026, and even if it does, broader market concerns could push it back down.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Despite a rally from ~$65,000 to nearly $90,000 since mid-August, the author is skeptical Bitcoin reaches $100,000 by end of 2026, since the move lacks a clear catalyst and Bitcoin has a history of amplifying (not hedging) broad market risk.

**Q2**: Why is the absence of Clarity Act passage significant to the author's argument?
**A**: Regulatory clarity for digital assets was seen as a potential major catalyst for Bitcoin; the rally happening without it suggests the current move is not backed by a durable fundamental driver, making it more vulnerable to reversal.

**Q3**: What historical evidence does the author use to challenge Bitcoin's "safe haven" reputation?
**A**: In 2022, the S&P 500 fell 19% while Bitcoin fell 65% — a much larger drawdown — indicating Bitcoin tends to move with, and amplify, broader risk-off sentiment rather than hedge against it.
