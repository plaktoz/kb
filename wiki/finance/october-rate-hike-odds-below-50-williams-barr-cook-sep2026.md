## Summary

A single "no urgency" sentence from [[John Williams]] on Sept. 29 pushed CME FedWatch odds of an October rate hike from 70% to below 50%, even as Governors [[Michael Barr]] and [[Lisa Cook]] struck more hawkish tones the same week. The 10-year [[Treasury Yield]] closed near 5.26% — its highest since 2007 — with the 30-year near 5.6%, pressuring rate-sensitive stocks ahead of the Oct. 28 FOMC decision.

## Core Concepts

- **[[John Williams]]**: NY Fed President and FOMC vice chair whose permanent vote and market-operations role make his remarks move odds more than a rotating regional president's.
- **[[CME FedWatch]]**: Converts fed funds futures prices into implied rate-hike probabilities; a "chance" is a price that moves on trader positioning, not only on changed outlooks.
- **[[Michael Barr]]**: Fed Governor who said at the Detroit Economic Club that "further policy adjustments are likely to be needed," noting only 2 of the last 20 months had core PCE at the Fed's 2% target.
- **[[Lisa Cook]]**: Fed Governor who flagged "continued pressure on inflation from the AI build-out... and from the pass-through of higher oil prices" — a supply-side risk that rate hikes don't directly address.
- **[[10-Year Treasury Yield]]**: Closed near 5.26%, the highest since 2007, lowering the market value of existing bonds while offering new buyers the best lock-in rates in nearly two decades.

## Key Takeaways

- **Odds nearly halved**: October hike odds fell from 70% to below 50% on one Williams sentence.
- **Hike still "may be appropriate"**: Williams called inflation at 3.7% "unquestionably too high."
- **Hawkish dissent**: Barr and Cook both signaled more tightening may be needed.
- **Yields at multi-decade highs**: 10-year near 5.26%, 30-year near 5.6%.
- **Weak labor/confidence data**: August job openings fell to 7.079 million; consumer confidence hit a 2014 low.
- **Key dates ahead**: August PCE (Sept. 30), September jobs report (Oct. 2), FOMC decision (Oct. 28).

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Signaling Theory]]**: Williams' single sentence outweighed Barr's and Cook's more hawkish quotes because his permanent FOMC vote and the NY Fed's market-operations role make his words a higher-credibility signal, independent of content.
