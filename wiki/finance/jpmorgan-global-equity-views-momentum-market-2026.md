## Summary

J.P. Morgan's Q3 2026 equity outlook attributes most of this year's earnings growth to an AI investment spending boom (projected at $1.6 trillion in 2026, potentially $3 trillion by 2028), warns that current market momentum is among the strongest in 40 years with rising fragility, and recommends diversifying by style and geography ahead of an eventual momentum unwind.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Capex Boom]]**: Data center investment spending projected at $1.6 trillion in 2026 (more than 3x two years ago), driving roughly 75% of expected U.S. profit growth this year when combined with higher oil prices.
- **[[Momentum Factor Risk]]**: J.P. Morgan's systematic research places the current momentum market among the top four such booms in 40 years by size, and second only to the 1999 internet boom by speed — with falling correlation to quality/valuation factors as a warning sign.
- **[[AI Losers Cohort]]**: Companies facing disruption risk from AI capacity growth (especially in software) whose depressed valuations J.P. Morgan flags as attractive long-term diversification opportunities against AI-winner concentration.
- **[[Style and Geography Diversification]]**: J.P. Morgan's recommended hedge against increasingly concentrated, momentum-driven indices — directly relevant to momentum-factor ETF exposure such as [[SPMO]] and [[IWMO]].

## Key Takeaways

- U.S. corporate profits are now expected to grow 27% in 2026 and 22% in 2027 — up sharply from a 14% forecast made in January.
- About 75% of expected U.S. profit growth this year comes from AI spending plus the oil price jump; growth excluding those factors is a more modest ~8%.
- Risks to the AI capex boom: rising reliance on debt financing over cash flow, power-supply constraints, growing opposition to data centers, and uncertainty over future AI token pricing.
- Internally, JPM investors with decades of market experience mostly don't believe markets are in a bubble yet, since the boom is in earnings rather than valuations — but they stress that valuation "matters, eventually" and that market timing is nearly impossible (the NASDAQ was seen as dangerously overpriced in mid-1999, then doubled again over the next nine months).
- Current team positioning favors industrials, quality stocks, and select "AI losers," while avoiding consumer staples and speculative growth names.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Regression to the Mean]]**: J.P. Morgan's warning that today's momentum boom is among the strongest in 40 years implicitly invokes regression to the mean — factor booms of this size and speed have historically been followed by sharp unwinds, which is why diversification is recommended now, before any reversal is visible.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this outlook?
**A**: The 2026 equity rally is real and earnings-driven (not just valuation expansion), but it's also historically concentrated in AI-momentum stocks, which J.P. Morgan sees as an increasing source of portfolio risk worth actively diversifying against.

**Q2**: What specific evidence does J.P. Morgan cite that the momentum trade is becoming riskier?
**A**: Falling correlation between momentum and other factors like valuation and quality (meaning gains increasingly come from just chasing higher prices) and sharply rising volatility among the most fashionable stocks.

**Q3**: How does this outlook apply directly to someone holding momentum-factor ETFs like SPMO or IWMO?
**A**: It suggests such holdings currently sit inside one of the largest momentum booms in 40 years, and J.P. Morgan's own risk-management lesson — that valuation matters eventually and that timing an unwind is nearly impossible — argues for balancing momentum exposure with value and geographic diversification now rather than after a reversal begins.
