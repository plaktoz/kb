## Summary

Gold prices jumped to a seven-week high on Friday, Aug. 7, 2026, with spot gold gaining 2.4% to $4,340.06/oz after a surprise July nonfarm payrolls decline of 23,000 reduced expectations of a near-term Fed rate hike. Bullion was on pace for its best week in seven months, with prices testing key resistance near $4,397.79. The rally was reinforced by a labor-force participation rate near a 50-year low and ongoing Strait of Hormuz-related geopolitical uncertainty tied to the U.S.-Iran conflict.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Gold]]**: Spot gold rose 2.4% to $4,340.06/oz; gold futures gained 2.3% to $4,395.72/oz, a seven-week high.
- **Nonfarm Payrolls**: July report showed a 23,000 job loss vs. +85,000 expected, with June revised down to 20,000 (from 57,000) and May cut to 63,000 (from 129,000) — a combined 103,000 downward revision.
- **[[Federal Reserve]]**: Weak labor data reduced market expectations for a near-term rate hike, supporting gold as a rate-sensitive asset.
- **Labor Force Participation Rate**: Eased to 61.4%, near a 50-year low outside the COVID-19 pandemic, partly attributed to an immigration crackdown reducing the workforce.
- **[[Strait of Hormuz]]**: Ongoing U.S.-Iran negotiations over control of the waterway added geopolitical uncertainty that the analyst linked to inflation and gold demand.

## Key Takeaways

- **Gold Spike**: Spot gold +2.4% to $4,340.06/oz; futures +2.3% to $4,395.72/oz.
- **Jobs Shock**: Nonfarm payrolls fell 23,000 in July vs. an expected +85,000.
- **Downward Revisions**: May and June payrolls cut by a combined 103,000.
- **Participation Slump**: Labor force participation fell to 61.4%, near a 50-year low.
- **Rate-Hike Odds Fall**: Weak jobs data reduced expectations of a Fed rate increase.
- **Technical Resistance**: Gold faces key resistance at $4,397.79, tested previously on June 17, 2026.
- **Geopolitical Overhang**: Unresolved Strait of Hormuz negotiations continue to add a risk premium to gold.
