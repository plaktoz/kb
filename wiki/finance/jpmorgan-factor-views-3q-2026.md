## Summary

J.P. Morgan's Q3 2026 Factor Views report finds [[Equity Momentum]] extending its best run since the dot-com bubble (fueled by AI-capex "momentum winners" rotating from hyperscalers to memory-chip makers), while [[Equity Value]] and [[Equity Quality]] are historically cheap but underperforming — the firm recommends trimming momentum overweights and favoring diversification across factors.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Equity Momentum]]**: The top-performing factor globally, up across every region — but factor volatility hit six-year highs and dispersion is elevated, raising reversal risk; J.P. Morgan is building a proprietary "Momentum Fragility Index" to track crash risk.
- **[[Equity Value]]**: Nearly one standard deviation cheap globally (more than one SD in the U.S., near record lows outside the dot-com/post-COVID bubbles), yet posting negative year-to-date returns outside the U.S.
- **[[Equity Quality]]**: About one standard deviation cheap globally, in its worst stretch since the post-COVID bubble — elevated profitability dispersion may be making high-quality firms targets for AI disruption, pressuring their prices.
- **[[Macro Carry]] and [[Macro Momentum]]**: FX and commodity carry led macro-factor gains (Australian dollar, British pound, Colombian/Mexican peso strength); macro momentum rebuilt equity exposure after a rough March tied to Middle East tensions.

## Key Takeaways

- Global equities rose ~15% in Q2 2026 (strongest since the 2020 COVID recovery), with roughly two-thirds of gains from tech; some chipmakers gained 100-200%.
- Momentum leadership has rotated from 2024's hyperscalers to 2026's memory-chip makers as the AI capex story matures.
- J.P. Morgan's recommended posture: trim (don't abandon) momentum overweights; maintain value and quality exposure given attractive valuations, while acknowledging "this time is different" arguments around AI disruption.
- Macro factors are rated broadly neutral, with the firm favoring diversification across a wide range of factors rather than concentrated bets.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Mean Reversion]]**: The report's core tension — momentum's extreme multi-year run versus value/quality's historic cheapness — is a live test of whether extended factor performance reverts to trend or whether structural (AI-driven) change justifies a persistent regime shift.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: J.P. Morgan sees equity momentum extending its strongest run since the dot-com era on the back of AI-capex winners, while equity value and quality remain historically cheap despite recent underperformance — and recommends diversifying across factors given elevated momentum risk.

**Q2**: What specific evidence signals elevated risk in the momentum factor right now?
**A**: Momentum factor volatility has risen to six-year highs, and factor dispersion (the gap between winning and losing stocks) is high relative to history — both flagged as raising the risk of a momentum reversal.

**Q3**: What's the direct implication for a portfolio holding momentum-factor ETFs (like SPMO or IWMO) alongside broad-market funds (CSPX, VWRA)?
**A**: The report supports trimming outsized momentum overweights rather than exiting entirely, while maintaining or even leaning into value/quality exposure given historically wide valuation discounts — arguing for rebalancing toward diversification rather than chasing the current momentum trend further.
