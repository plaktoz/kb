## Summary

Meta stock slipped to $595.19 — about 25% off its 52-week high — ahead of its Q2 2026 earnings report, trading at roughly 22x earnings, a discount to the S&P 500's ~28.5x multiple. The drawdown is not driven by advertising weakness; Q1 revenue grew 33% YoY and ad metrics remained strong. Investor anxiety centers on Meta's massive and potentially expanding capex guidance of $125–$145B for 2026, and uncertainty over when AI infrastructure spending will generate returns.

## Core Concepts

- [[Meta Platforms]] stock valuation relative to [[S&P 500]] earnings multiples
- [[Capital Expenditure]] expansion driven by [[AI Infrastructure]] buildout
- [[Free Cash Flow]] generation despite heavy investment cycle
- Q2 2026 [[Earnings Report]] expectations and revenue guidance
- [[Ad Revenue]] resilience: impressions up 19%, average ad price up 12% in Q1

## Key Takeaways

- **Stock Price**: Meta at $595.19, ~25% below 52-week high pre-earnings.
- **Valuation**: ~22x earnings vs. S&P 500's ~28.5x — relative discount.
- **Q1 Revenue**: $56.31B, up 33% YoY; advertising remains robust.
- **Capex Concern**: 2026 guidance raised to $125–$145B; ~$20B spent in Q1 alone.
- **Financial Cushion**: $12.39B Q1 free cash flow; $81.18B cash and securities.
- **Q2 Guidance**: Revenue of $58–$61B (22–28% growth), decelerating from Q1.
- **Key Watch**: Whether capex guidance expands further at Wednesday's report.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Mr. Market]]**: Meta's 25% drawdown reflects market mood swings on capex fear rather than deteriorating fundamentals — the business is growing, but sentiment has decoupled from earnings power.
- **[[Delayed Gratification]]**: The core tension is that AI infrastructure spending demands large upfront capital with uncertain payoff timing — investors are discounting the stock because returns lie beyond their comfort horizon.
