## Summary
[[IBM]] shares fell after a Q3 2026 earnings beat because the full-year revenue outlook was unchanged and CEO [[Arvind Krishna]] warned that enterprise customers are scrutinizing the true ROI of AI spending. Krishna framed that scrutiny as a tailwind for IBM and urged firms to scale a few AI initiatives rather than run "100 little experiments." The stock is down about 14.9% in 2026 while the [[S&P 500]] is up 12%.

## Core Concepts
- [[Arvind Krishna]]: Says clients see "some of the AI spending completely out of control with no real return."
- [[Enterprise AI Spending]]: Customers are redirecting budgets from software and mainframes toward AI infrastructure, memory, and [[GPU]]s.
- [[AI ROI Scrutiny]]: Krishna sees workflow redesign and token-spend control as IBM's opportunity.
- [[IBM Q3 2026 Earnings]]: Revenue $17.1B (+5%), adjusted EPS $2.95 versus $2.87 expected.
- [[IBM Software Segment]]: Revenue up 8%; infrastructure up 4%; consulting flat reported, +2% constant currency.
- [[Free Cash Flow]]: Rose $0.7B to $3B; full-year outlook raised to more than $1B growth.
- [[Balance Sheet Leverage]]: Total debt $63.8B; cash $11.1B after $11.4B of acquisitions.
- [[Analyst Price Targets]]: "Moderate Buy" consensus; average target $261.46, Street-high $365.

## Key Takeaways
- **CEO Warning**: Customers increasingly question the true ROI of their AI spend.
- **Focus Advice**: Krishna urges "pick a few" initiatives over 100 little experiments.
- **Stock Reaction**: Shares slid despite beating revenue and EPS estimates.
- **Outlook**: Full-year constant-currency revenue growth still guided to 4% to 5%.
- **Relative Performance**: IBM down 12.3% over a year; S&P 500 up 14.4%.
- **Profitability**: Net income up 25.6%; operating cash flow $4B.
- **Analysts**: 11 Strong Buy, 11 Hold, 1 Strong Sell; target implies 3.7% upside.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Concentrated investment beats scattered experiments** — Resources split across many small initiatives rarely reach the scale needed to produce measurable returns; fewer, larger bets get funded enough to pay off.
→ explains: Focus Advice, CEO Warning

**T2. Spending needs a measured return to survive scrutiny** — As AI budgets grow, buyers demand evidence of ROI, which shifts demand toward vendors that help deploy AI deliberately.
→ explains: CEO Warning, Stock Reaction

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What was the core message of IBM CEO Arvind Krishna's AI spending warning?
**A**: Enterprise customers are becoming more aware of the true ROI of AI spend and see some spending as out of control with no return. Krishna views this scrutiny as a tailwind for IBM.

**Q2**: Why did IBM shares fall despite a strong Q3 report?
**A**: Investors weighed the unchanged full-year revenue outlook against Krishna's cautious AI-spending message, even though revenue ($17.1B) and adjusted EPS ($2.95) beat estimates.

**Q3**: What advice does Krishna give businesses pursuing AI?
**A**: Pick a few AI initiatives and scale them meaningfully instead of spreading resources over "100 little experiments" that never get enough investment to deliver results.
