## Summary

Corning shares rallied on Friday, Aug. 7, 2026, after the Trump administration announced a 15% tariff and minimum import price on polysilicon (effective Dec. 4), directly benefiting Corning's Hemlock Semiconductor joint venture, one of only two U.S. polysilicon plants. Corning was the CNBC Investing Club portfolio's biggest gainer for the week (~18%), riding a rebound in the AI infrastructure trade, while the S&P 500 was on pace for a record close after a weaker-than-expected July jobs report reduced odds of a near-term Fed rate hike.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Corning]]**: Materials science company; biggest single-day and single-week gainer in the CNBC Investing Club portfolio on tariff news and an AI-infrastructure rebound.
- **[[Hemlock Semiconductor]]**: Corning's joint venture with Japan's [[Shin-Etsu Handotai]]; one of only two U.S. polysilicon plants, directly positioned to benefit from the new import tariffs.
- **Polysilicon Tariff**: A Trump administration executive order imposing a 15% tariff and a minimum import price on polysilicon, effective Dec. 4, 2026, meant to protect domestic industry from Chinese competition.
- **[[S&P 500]]**: On pace for a record close Friday, up roughly 3.5% for the week.
- **[[Federal Reserve]]**: The July jobs miss shifted September rate-hold odds to 55% (from 33% a week earlier), per CME FedWatch.

## Key Takeaways

- **Tariff Catalyst**: 15% tariff plus minimum import price on polysilicon takes effect Dec. 4, 2026.
- **Corning's Edge**: Hemlock JV is one of only two U.S. polysilicon plants, insulating it from Chinese import competition.
- **Weekly Surge**: Corning stock rallied roughly 18% for the week, about 13% above its pre-earnings level.
- **Jobs Miss**: July nonfarm payrolls fell 23,000 versus an expected 83,000 increase.
- **Revisions**: The prior two months' job gains were revised down by a combined 103,000.
- **Rate Odds Shift**: September rate-hold probability rose to 55% from 33% a week prior.
- **"Bad News Is Good News"**: Weak labor data boosted equities via lower rate-hike odds.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Second-Order Thinking]]**: The market's "bad news is good news" reaction reflects investors pricing the second-order effect (a more dovish Fed) rather than the first-order effect (a weaker labor market) of the jobs report.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core catalyst behind Corning's rally on Aug. 7, 2026?
**A**: A new Trump administration tariff (15%) and minimum import price on polysilicon, which benefits Corning's Hemlock Semiconductor joint venture — one of only two U.S. polysilicon plants.

**Q2**: What labor-market data accompanied the market's move that day, and how did investors interpret it?
**A**: July nonfarm payrolls fell by 23,000 versus an expected 83,000 increase, with 103,000 in downward revisions to prior months; investors read this as reducing the odds of a Fed rate hike, boosting stocks.

**Q3**: What does the Corning/tariff situation suggest about the investing implications of trade policy?
**A**: Sector-specific tariffs can create outsized winners among the few domestic suppliers positioned to fill the resulting import gap, as with Corning's Hemlock JV being one of only two U.S. polysilicon producers.
