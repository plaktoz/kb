## Summary

US stocks closed a volatile July higher on July 31 — Nasdaq +1%, Dow +0.5%, S&P 500 +0.7% — as the four hyperscalers guided a combined $720–745B in 2026 capital spending, reassuring investors who had feared an AI slowdown, even as the 10-year Treasury yield jumped to its highest level since January 2025.

## Core Concepts

- [[Magnificent Seven]] — Amazon (+15% on earnings beat and chip business growth), Apple (-7% on soft Services/China revenue), Microsoft (+15% the prior day) all reported this week
- [[10-Year Treasury Yield]] — rose to 4.73%, highest in over a year, as markets digested the Fed's rate-hold with no forward guidance
- [[Strait of Hormuz]] — renewed hostilities pushed WTI crude to ~$85/barrel and Brent to ~$90/barrel
- [[Consumer Sentiment]] — University of Michigan reading showed a broad pickup despite elevated energy and gas costs

## Key Takeaways

- **All three major indexes gained for the week**, with the Dow up 0.7% for the month of July despite the mid-month volatility
- **Combined hyperscaler capex guidance of $720–745B for 2026** was the key reassurance point for tech investors
- **Amazon (+15%) and Apple (-7%) moved in opposite directions** on the same earnings day, both tied to AI/chip supply dynamics
- **Oil prices turned higher** on Strait of Hormuz tensions even as broader energy costs weighed on consumer budgets

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Priced-In Expectations]]**: identical capex guidance reassured markets this week specifically because it matched or slightly beat what was already feared — the market was pricing a slowdown, and merely confirming continuity was enough to rally stocks.
