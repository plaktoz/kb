## Summary
[[Jim Lebenthal]] bought more [[Alphabet]] after a 14% monthly slide, calling the drop "a correction, not something worse" rather than the AI bubble popping. The filings back his "shots on goal" case, led by [[Google Cloud]] revenue up 103%. The weak spot is negative free cash flow funded by borrowing and stock sales.

## Core Concepts
- [[Jim Lebenthal]] — CNBC Halftime Report regular who added to Alphabet on the dip
- [[Alphabet]] (GOOGL) — closed at $273.10 on Nov. 25, down 13.79% in a month and about 21.5% below its July 22 price
- [[Market Correction]] — a drop of 10% or more; QQQ fell 10.99% and SPY 6.36% in a month
- [[Google Cloud]] — Q3 revenue up 103% to $30.71B, helped by [[TPU]] system sales; operating income $11.12B
- [[Free Cash Flow]] — negative $9.07B in Q3 as capex rose 114% to $51.36B
- [[At-the-Market Offering]] — gradual new-share sales that raised $4.3B (Class A) and $8.1B (Class C), diluting holders
- [[Gemini]], [[Waymo]], [[YouTube]] — other "shots on goal" cited by Lebenthal

## Key Takeaways
- **Index vs. stock**: The correction label fits QQQ and SPY better than Alphabet's deeper drawdown.
- **Cloud engine**: Cloud growth accelerated from 63% to 82% to 103% this year.
- **Cash burn**: Free cash flow of negative $9.07B, worse than Q2's negative $5.86B.
- **Funding gap**: $28.7B of notes plus ATM stock sales; no buyback activity in latest results.
- **Search slowing**: Search growth slid from 19% to 17% to 12% this year.
- **Misleading P/E**: Trailing P/E of 12 is flattered by $36.9B and $99.0B equity gains.
- **Next test**: Q4 report in early February shows whether spending produces cash.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Earnings quality beats headline multiples** — A valuation ratio built on non-operating gains overstates cheapness; cash generation is the check on whether earnings are real.
→ explains: Misleading P/E, Cash burn

**T2. Growth funded externally is fragile** — Expansion paid for by debt and new share issuance depends on lenders and markets staying willing, and dilutes existing owners. · *model:* [[Leverage]]
→ explains: Funding gap, Cash burn

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Jim Lebenthal's central call on Alphabet, and does the data support it?
**A**: He calls the drop a correction, not the AI bubble popping, and a buying opportunity. The article says the index data and Alphabet's filings support him, though Alphabet carries more balance-sheet risk than the indexes.

**Q2**: What evidence shows the cash-flow risk?
**A**: Q3 capex rose 114% to $51.36B, leaving free cash flow at negative $9.07B. Alphabet sold $28.7B in notes and raised $12.4B through at-the-market share sales.

**Q3**: What would turn the correction into something worse?
**A**: Cloud growth slowing while capex stays near $175B-$185B guidance, search growth continuing to slip, or lenders pulling back from AI borrowers. Retirement investors should watch whether free cash flow turns positive.
