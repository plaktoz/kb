## Summary

Google Cloud previewed a crypto custody service called Multi-Party Compute (MPC) for financial institutions on Oct. 28. The article argues it should not factor into an Alphabet buy decision, because the market is crowded and Alphabet's revenue base is too large for the product to move it. Even $1 billion of MPC revenue in 2027 would be about 0.8% of annualized Google Cloud revenue.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Alphabet]]** — Parent of [[Google Cloud]]; Q3 2026 total revenue of $127.6 billion.
- **[[Google Cloud]]** — Q3 2026 revenue of $30.7 billion, 24.1% of Alphabet's total.
- **[[Multi-Party Compute]]** — Splits each private key into fragments held on separate, isolated systems, which can include rival clouds.
- **[[Crypto Custody]]** — Secure storage of cryptoassets for institutions; Binance reported $6.3 billion stolen on Oct. 30.
- **[[Ethereum]] and [[Solana]]** — Transaction signing methods supported at launch.
- **[[Google Cloud Universal Ledger]]** — Private blockchain launched in 2025; earlier Google crypto work.
- **[[Competitive Landscape]]** — Many existing custody providers offer similar security schemes.

## Key Takeaways

- **Product design**: Key fragments sit on isolated systems; Google approves spends only if owner rules pass.
- **Launch scope**: Supports Ethereum and Solana signing; Alphabet already protects its own assets this way.
- **Crowded market**: Many custodians and blockchains compete; Google's brand adds little in crypto.
- **Scale problem**: Google Cloud's $30.7B quarterly revenue dwarfs any plausible custody income.
- **Sizing example**: $1B MPC revenue in 2027 equals only 0.8% of Cloud's annualized revenue.
- **Verdict**: Alphabet's case rests on other businesses; crypto should not drive the decision.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. A large base dilutes new revenue** — The bigger the existing revenue base, the larger a new product must be to change total growth noticeably. · *model:* [[Law of Large Numbers]]

**T2. Crowded markets limit a late entrant's advantage** — With many competitors offering similar features, a new entrant needs real differentiation to win share, and brand alone does not supply it. · *model:* [[Commoditization]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about Google's crypto push and Alphabet stock?
**A**: The crypto custody launch is unlikely to matter to shareholders. The market is crowded, and the revenue is too small relative to Alphabet's existing base.

**Q2**: What data point shows how small the opportunity is?
**A**: Google Cloud earned $30.7 billion in Q3 2026, 24.1% of Alphabet's $127.6 billion. MPC revenue of $1 billion in 2027 would be about 0.8% of Cloud's annualized revenue.

**Q3**: How does Multi-Party Compute protect assets, and why does the article say it is not a reason to buy?
**A**: It splits each private key into fragments on separate systems, and Google approves a transaction only if it meets the owner's rules. Many rivals offer similar schemes, so it does not obviously set Google apart.
