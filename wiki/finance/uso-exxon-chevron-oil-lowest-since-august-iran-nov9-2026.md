## Summary
Brent and WTI fell to their lowest settlements since August 31 on November 9, 2026, with USO dropping about 4% while Exxon and Chevron lost under 1% thanks to refining cushions. The article argues Trump's "effectively over" remark came after settlement and with no signed ceasefire, so a supply surplus and rising OPEC output are driving prices more than diplomacy.

## Core Concepts
- [[Brent Crude]] and [[West Texas Intermediate]]: settled at $92.57 and $86.85.
- [[United States Oil Fund]] (USO): holds near-month WTI futures and suffers roll costs.
- [[Exxon Mobil]] and [[Chevron]]: integrated majors with refining businesses that cushion crude declines.
- [[Donald Trump]]: said the Iran war is "effectively over" militarily; naval blockade and [[Task Force Iron Gate]] remain.
- [[OPEC]]: output rose 1.38 million barrels a day to 22.87 million.
- [[Iran Conflict]]: Iran disputes that talks exist; no ceasefire signed.

## Key Takeaways
- **Price Move**: Brent fell 5.85% to $92.57; WTI fell 4.38% to $86.85.
- **Timing**: Trump's remark came after the 2:30 p.m. ET settlement, so it did not cause the drop.
- **USO Exposure**: USO is down 15.5% since October 30, with no refining or dividend.
- **Refining Cushion**: Chevron's US refining unit earned $3.58 billion in Q3 versus $638 million.
- **Supply Pressure**: One bank estimates a 1.4 million bpd October surplus.
- **Diplomacy Doubt**: Iran and Rubio both denied an imminent breakthrough.
- **Key Level**: WTI settling above $104.12 before a signed deal would change the outlook.
