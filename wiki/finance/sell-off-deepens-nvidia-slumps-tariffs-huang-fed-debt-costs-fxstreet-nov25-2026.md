## Summary
US stocks fell sharply on Wednesday, with the Nasdaq down over 4% and the S&P 500 down 2.5%, as Monday's Jensen Huang rally unwound. The author argues AI fears, not new tariffs, are the main driver: governance and circular-financing worries, plus high inflation and growth data that favour prolonged high rates and make the AI build-out costlier to fund. Thanksgiving-thin liquidity is amplifying the moves.

## Core Concepts
- [[Nvidia]] and [[Jensen Huang]]: his return as CEO, seemingly at the President's behest, raises corporate-governance and scrutiny fears.
- [[Circular Financing]] concerns around AI, amplified by Huang's return and possible Chinese revenue conduits exposed to export controls.
- [[Tariffs]]: a 20% levy on China (up to 40% without a deal), with Vietnam, the EU, Japan and South Korea expected; effective January.
- [[Federal Reserve]] and [[PCE Inflation]]: core PCE rose to 3.3% from 3.2%; Q3 [[GDP]] revised up to 4.3% from 3.4%.
- [[Anthropic]] IPO: down 5% on the day and well below its IPO price.
- [[Credit Default Swaps]]: Nvidia's CDS spreads are near record, though default is deemed very unlikely given over $40bn quarterly free cash flow.
- [[Market Liquidity]]: holiday-shortened week thins liquidity and exaggerates moves.

## Key Takeaways
- **Market Moves**: Nasdaq fell over 4%, S&P 500 2.5%, Nvidia as much as 8%.
- **Main Driver**: AI fears outweigh tariffs as the cause of the sell-off.
- **Tariffs**: New 20% China levy, rising to 40% without a deal, starts January.
- **Rates Pressure**: Hot data favours prolonged high rates, making AI build-out costlier to fund.
- **Credit Signal**: Nvidia CDS near record, yet $40bn+ quarterly free cash flow limits default risk.
- **Rally Failure**: No dip buying; Nasdaq 100 back at last week's lows.
- **Thin Liquidity**: Thanksgiving week likely exacerbates volatility; only Treasuries and sovereign bonds are surging.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Thin liquidity amplifies price moves** — When few participants trade, the same selling pressure moves prices further, so holiday-week volatility overstates the underlying news. · *model:* [[Liquidity Risk]]
→ explains: Thin Liquidity, Market Moves

**T2. A rally that fails to attract follow-through buying is a relief bounce, not a reversal** — Absent dip buying and a return to prior lows, a sharp rebound is better read as temporary. · *model:* [[Dead Cat Bounce]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does the author say is the main reason for the November 25 sell-off?
**A**: AI fears, especially Nvidia governance and circular-financing worries after Jensen Huang's return, rather than the new tariffs.

**Q2**: What evidence suggests Nvidia is unlikely to default despite near-record CDS spreads?
**A**: The company reported more than $40bn of free cash flow last quarter, and firms with that much cash do not just collapse.

**Q3**: What does the failure of Monday's rally imply for traders?
**A**: With no dip buying and the Nasdaq 100 back at last week's lows, the rally looks like a dead cat bounce, not a durable recovery.
