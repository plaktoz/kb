## Summary

U.S. stock futures slid early on Nov. 25, 2026 ahead of a pre-Thanksgiving data dump led by PCE inflation, while scrutiny of [[Nvidia]] CEO [[Jensen Huang]]'s reinstatement weighed on tech. A [[Polymarket]] contract priced only a 38% chance of a higher S&P 500 open, and [[Jeremy Siegel]] argued falling oil prices are an overlooked tailwind.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Polymarket]] contract**: "S&P 500 (SPX) Up or Down on November 25?" showed a 38% chance of a higher open. The Nov. 24 contract resolved "Down" on $33,317 volume.
- **Index futures**: [[Nasdaq 100]] -1.20%, S&P 500 -0.73%, [[Russell 2000]] -0.50%, Dow -0.44%.
- **Nvidia scrutiny**: Shares fell 5.86% Tuesday and 2.21% after hours. [[Chuck Schumer]] demanded investigations, [[Donald Trump]] said a pardon for Huang is "always an option", and the SEC is reportedly scrutinizing disclosures.
- **Oil**: Brent fell 1.57% to $87.39 and WTI 1.76% to $80.61.
- **Data slate**: Q3 second-estimate GDP, jobless claims, October durable goods, personal income, spending, and headline and core [[PCE Inflation]].
- **[[Jeremy Siegel]] view**: Falling energy prices boost consumer purchasing power and cool headline inflation, so he doesn't expect the Fed to follow [[Christopher Waller]]'s floated 50-basis-point December hike. He likens the AI-spending reassessment as token prices fall to the early internet fiber-optic boom.
- **Tuesday close**: SPY -0.49% to $718.78, QQQ -1.99% to $638.10, DIA +0.79% to $499.74.

## Key Takeaways

- **Prediction Odds**: Polymarket gave 38% odds of a higher S&P 500 open.
- **Futures Slide**: Nasdaq 100 futures led losses at -1.20%.
- **Nvidia Pressure**: Political and regulatory scrutiny over Huang's return deepened the stock's drop.
- **Oil Retreat**: Brent at $87.39 and WTI at $80.61.
- **Data Risk**: PCE, GDP and jobless claims all land at 8:30 a.m. ET.
- **Bull Case**: Siegel sees falling oil easing inflation and Fed pressure.
- **AI Valuations**: Investors question whether AI infrastructure spend justifies valuations as token prices fall.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Falling input costs raise real purchasing power and ease inflation** — Cheaper energy leaves consumers with more spending power and lowers headline inflation, which reduces the pressure on central banks to tighten.
→ explains: Oil Retreat, Bull Case
