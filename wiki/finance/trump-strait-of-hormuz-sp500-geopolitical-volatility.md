## Summary

Trump's renewed Strait of Hormuz blockade pushed the S&P 500 down 0.79% in mid-July 2026, with the VIX rising above its June average but remaining well below the March war-onset spike of ~25.6. The author argues this geopolitical volatility is background noise for long-term investors, citing that S&P 500 positions held through the 2008 recession have returned 659% through today.

## Core Concepts

- [[Strait of Hormuz]] — Critical oil chokepoint; Iranian shipping blockade renewed July 2026
- [[VIX]] — Volatility Index at 18.7; above June average (17.91), below March war peak (~25.6)
- [[S&P 500 Volatility]] — Geopolitical shocks historically temporary; long-term returns remain intact
- [[Trump Tariffs]] — 50% on certain Canadian goods; 10–25% on 60 trading partners; Supreme Court struck prior attempts
- [[Iran Conflict]] — Reignited July 2026; oil and equity market sensitivity
- [[Long-Term Investing]] — Pre-2008 S&P 500 position: +659% including dividends; pre-COVID 2020: +153%

## Key Takeaways

- **0.79% S&P drop**: Strait of Hormuz announcement transmitted directly to equity markets.
- **VIX at 18.7**: Elevated but not panic-level; war-onset March spike was ~25.6.
- **Tariff compounding**: Supreme Court-blocked tariffs being reissued — adding policy uncertainty on top of geopolitics.
- **Long-term frame**: Pre-recession or pre-crash S&P entries still deliver massive returns decades out.
- **For short horizons**: Fixed-income ETFs more appropriate than equity exposure in this environment.
