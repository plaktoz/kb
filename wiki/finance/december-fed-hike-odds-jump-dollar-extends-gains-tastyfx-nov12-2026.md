## Summary
Strong October jobs data and a hotter-than-expected October CPI lifted the probability of at least a quarter-point December Fed hike from 75% to 100% on CME FedWatch. The dollar extended a recovery that began in early September, with the Dollar Index above 102.50, USD/CHF near its 52-week high, and EUR/USD at a fresh 52-week low. Equity-market volatility could still complicate the Fed's decision while also supporting safe-haven demand for the dollar.

## Core Concepts
- [[Federal Reserve]]: [[FOMC]] meets December 8-9 with a rate hike widely expected.
- [[Consumer Price Index]]: October CPI up 0.4% versus 0.3% expected; core up 0.3% versus 0.2%.
- [[CME FedWatch]]: About 86% odds of a quarter-point hike and 14% of a half-point hike.
- [[US Dollar Index]]: Rose from around 98.50 in early September to above 102.50.
- [[USD/CHF]]: Near 0.8328, close to the 52-week high of 0.8357, with the Swiss National Bank rate at 0%.
- [[EUR/USD]]: Fell to about 1.1085, a fresh 52-week low and weakest since spring 2025.
- [[Carry Trade]]: Borrowing a lower-yielding currency to hold a higher-yielding one.
- [[Safe Haven Assets]]: Dollar demand can rise in uncertainty, as at the onset of the Iran war.

## Key Takeaways
- **Jobs Beat**: 165,000 October jobs versus roughly 65,000 expected; unemployment 4.1%.
- **CPI Surprise**: Headline 0.4% and core 0.3%, both above forecasts.
- **Hike Odds**: At-least-quarter-point probability jumped from 75% to 100%.
- **Dollar Strength**: DXY gained 0.57% on November 10 and 0.29% on November 11.
- **Rate Differential**: Another Fed hike widens the gap against the 0% Swiss policy rate.
- **Next Data**: October PPI due November 13, unlikely to change expectations.
- **Risk to Outlook**: A sustained tech and AI selloff could make the Fed hold.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Rate differentials drive currency flows** — Money tends to move toward the currency offering higher interest rates when funding currencies yield less, so widening differentials support the higher-yielding currency. · *model:* [[Carry Trade]]
→ explains: Rate Differential, Dollar Strength

**T2. Uncertainty pulls capital toward safe, liquid assets** — In periods of heightened uncertainty, investors seek relative safety and liquidity, which can sustain dollar demand even if tightening expectations fade. · *model:* [[Safe Haven Assets]]
→ explains: Risk to Outlook, Dollar Strength

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did December Fed hike odds jump?
**A**: A strong October jobs report and an October CPI above forecasts convinced traders the Fed has little reason to ease its inflation focus. The probability of at least a quarter-point hike rose from 75% to 100%.

**Q2**: How does the rate outlook affect USD/CHF?
**A**: With the Swiss National Bank rate at 0%, another Fed hike widens the interest-rate differential and makes holding dollars against francs more attractive through carry trades. USD/CHF trades near 0.8328, close to its 52-week high.

**Q3**: What could derail the expected December hike?
**A**: A sustained selloff in tech and AI stocks could tighten financial conditions and lead the Fed to hold. The same volatility could raise safe-haven demand for the dollar.
