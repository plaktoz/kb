## Summary
The [[S&P 500]] closed Friday, November 6, 2026 at 7,376.86, down 2.68% on the week, its worst Friday-to-Friday week since the April 2025 tariff crash and its fourth straight weekly loss. The pain was concentrated in AI hardware, with the semiconductor index down 12.15% while the Dow fell under 1%. Hot October jobs data lifted December rate-hike odds to 78%, putting CPI and NVIDIA earnings in focus.

## Core Concepts
- [[S&P 500]] and [[SPY]]: index fell from 7,580.07 to 7,376.86; the fund fell from $755.72 to $735.29 and is still up 7.83% for the year.
- [[PHLX Semiconductor Index]]: fell 12.15%; [[XLK]] fell 7% and the energy sector fund 11%.
- [[NVIDIA]]: made up 7.58% of SPY as of its March 2026 fact sheet.
- [[Market-Cap Weighting]]: lets the largest AI stocks dominate a "diversified" index.
- [[Federal Reserve]] rate path: strong jobs data raised December hike odds to 78% from 70%.
- [[2026 Midterm Elections]]: results triggered Thursday's heavy AI selling.
- [[Equal-Weight Index]]: an alternative that gives NVIDIA the same weight as every other company.
- [[Consumer Price Index]]: October report due Tuesday, November 10.

## Key Takeaways
- **Record Mark**: The 2.68% weekly drop was the worst since April 2025, but only narrowly.
- **Losing Streak**: Four straight weekly declines; 5.65% below the October 6 record close.
- **Thursday Damage**: The S&P 500 fell 4.36% on November 5 after midterm results.
- **Chip Concentration**: Semiconductor index fell 12.15% while the Dow fell 0.94%.
- **Hidden Exposure**: NVIDIA alone was about 7.58% of SPY, so "diversified" funds carry chip risk.
- **Jobs Shock**: 165,000 jobs added versus 65,000 expected; unemployment fell to 4.1%.
- **Catalysts**: CPI on November 10 and NVIDIA earnings on November 17.
- **Bear Trigger**: A close below 7,376.86 on November 13 would mean a fifth losing week.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Cap-weighting concentrates risk in the largest holdings** — When an index weights by market value, a few dominant stocks drive its returns, so a fund called diversified can carry a single theme's risk. · *model:* [[Concentration Risk]]
→ explains: Hidden Exposure, Chip Concentration

**T2. Higher rates cut the value of distant profits** — Higher rates lower what investors pay today for profits expected years away, so richly valued growth stocks that rely on those profits fall most. · *model:* [[Discounted Cash Flow]]
→ explains: Jobs Shock, Catalysts

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why was this week notable for the S&P 500?
**A**: The index fell 2.68% to 7,376.86, its worst Friday-to-Friday week since the April 2025 tariff crash, and its fourth straight weekly loss.

**Q2**: Why did the S&P 500 fall while the Dow barely moved?
**A**: Losses were concentrated in AI hardware and energy. The semiconductor index fell 12.15% and XLK fell 7%, while the Dow slipped 0.94%.

**Q3**: How should an investor think about their S&P 500 fund after this week?
**A**: Because the index is market-cap weighted and NVIDIA was about 7.58% of SPY, the fund carries heavy AI chip risk. An equal-weight S&P 500 fund is one way to reduce that concentration.
