## Summary
Nvidia's Q2 fiscal year 2027 results (quarter ended July 26) beat expectations with $96.2B in revenue, up 106% year-over-year, alongside strong guidance and reaffirmed hyperscaler demand. The company's growth is underpinned by surging hyperscale capex, a growing CPU business, and continued capital returns to shareholders. The article argues the stock's valuation remains reasonable relative to its growth rate, making it a continued buy.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nvidia]]** Q2 FY2027 earnings beat, with revenue, margins, and operating income all posting triple-digit or near-triple-digit year-over-year growth.
- **[[Hyperscale Computing]]** demand from cloud giants remains robust, countering fears of a shift toward [[Custom AI Chips]].
- **[[AWS]]**, led by [[Andy Jassy]], and [[Alphabet]], led by [[Sundar Pichai]], both reaffirmed continued Nvidia GPU purchases.
- **[[Capital Expenditure]]** among hyperscalers is projected to approach $800B this year and potentially $1.3T next year, a key growth driver for Nvidia.
- **[[Stock Valuation]]** at roughly 23.9x forward earnings is framed as reasonable given Nvidia's growth trajectory relative to the broader market.

## Key Takeaways

- **Revenue Growth**: Q2 FY2027 revenue hit $96.2B, up 106% year-over-year.
- **Margin Expansion**: Gross margin rose to 75%, from 72.4% a year earlier.
- **Operating Income**: Grew 124% YoY to $63.7B.
- **EPS Growth**: Adjusted EPS reached $2.22, up 120% YoY.
- **Guidance**: Next quarter revenue guidance is ~$108B midpoint, implying ~89.5% YoY growth.
- **Hyperscale Demand**: Hyperscale revenue reached $48.7B, up 102% YoY; AWS added 2 million more Nvidia GPUs.
- **CPU Expansion**: CPU segment targets $20B in standalone revenue this fiscal year, doubling next year.
- **Shareholder Returns**: Nvidia commits to returning at least 50% of free cash flow to shareholders.
- **Valuation**: Trades at ~23.9x forward earnings vs. sector average of 20.9x, seen as reasonable given growth.
