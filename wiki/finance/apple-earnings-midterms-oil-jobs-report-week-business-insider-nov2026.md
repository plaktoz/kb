## Summary

Business Insider lists four catalysts for the week of Nov. 2, 2026: Apple's earnings, oil moves tied to US-Iran diplomacy, the midterm elections, and the October jobs report. They arrive as the AI trade is fragile after a Friday sell-off, with the [[Nasdaq 100]] down 9% from its early-October high and near an official correction.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Apple]] earnings**: Monday after the close, the first call led by new CEO [[John Ternus]]. Apple's expected capex of $11-14 billion is small next to $100+ billion from AI hyperscalers, but investors want its AI plans. Apple became the world's largest company by market cap after [[Nvidia]]'s Friday plunge.
- **Hyperscaler capex anxiety**: Meta and Tesla fell on hints of spending-outlook changes, while Amazon and Alphabet were punished for rising capex despite negative cash flow. [[Northern Trust]]'s Eric Freedman cites capex plans, financing needs, cannibalization and evidence of returns.
- **[[Oil Prices]] and [[Iran]]**: Brent and WTI each fell 6% Monday on hopes of renewed diplomacy, but a New York Times report of US-Israel strike plans after the midterms could revive volatility.
- **Midterm elections**: Per [[BlackRock]], the S&P 500 has averaged a 14% gain in the six months after midterms since 1970. [[JPMorgan Private Bank]] raised its year-end S&P 500 target to 8,000 (4% upside) and expects a near-term range of about -25 to +50 basis points. [[Charles Schwab]]'s Joe Mazzola notes 2018 as an exception.
- **October jobs report**: Friday; economists expect 55,000 payrolls versus 29,000 in September. [[CME FedWatch Tool]] shows a 78% chance of a 25 basis-point [[Federal Reserve]] hike at the next meeting.

## Key Takeaways

- **Fragile AI Trade**: Nasdaq 100 had its worst month in over a year, down 9%.
- **Apple Test**: First Ternus-led call; market wants AI ambitions, not capex.
- **Capex Punished**: Investors penalize both spending changes and spending growth without returns.
- **Oil Swing**: Crude fell 6% on diplomacy hopes; strike-plan reports could reverse it.
- **Midterm Pattern**: S&P 500 averaged 14% gains in six months after midterms since 1970.
- **JPMorgan Target**: S&P 500 year-end target raised to 8,000, implying 4% upside.
- **Hike Odds**: 78% probability of a 25 basis-point Fed hike.
- **Jobs Paradox**: Strong payrolls could trigger stock selling by supporting higher rates.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Good news can be bad news when it raises the cost of capital** — A strong labor market shows the economy can absorb higher rates, so it can pressure risk assets even though it is good for the economy.
→ explains: Jobs Paradox, Hike Odds

**T2. Divided government tends to favor markets** — The article argues that midterm losses by the incumbent party produce gridlock in Washington, which tends to be good for Wall Street, helping explain post-midterm rallies.
→ explains: Midterm Pattern

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What four catalysts does Business Insider say could reshape the market narrative this week?
**A**: Apple's earnings, oil moves tied to US-Iran diplomacy, the midterm elections, and the October jobs report. All come while the AI trade is fragile after a sell-off.

**Q2**: Why could a strong jobs report hurt stocks?
**A**: A stronger job market shows the economy can absorb higher interest rates, a possible drag on risk assets. With a 78% chance of a Fed hike priced in, strong payrolls could give investors a reason to sell.

**Q3**: How might investors use the midterm history in positioning?
**A**: Stocks have historically struggled into midterms but gained strongly afterward, with the S&P 500 averaging 14% over six months since 1970. Strategists suggested any election-driven AI pullback could be a buy-the-dip opportunity, though 2018 was an exception.
