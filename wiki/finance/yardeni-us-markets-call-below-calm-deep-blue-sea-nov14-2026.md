## Summary
Major US stock indexes have gone nowhere since early August, but heavy turbulence beneath the surface is offsetting itself across sectors and industries. Yardeni sees falling valuation multiples nearing a bottom, record earnings estimates, and no broadening credit stress, and he targets 7,900 for the S&P 500 by year end.

## Core Concepts
- [[S&P 500]]: closed at 7,561.67, fluctuating around 7,600 since Aug. 3; year-end target 7,900 implies a forward P/E of 18.6.
- [[Sector Rotation]]: Health Care up 16.4% and Consumer Staples up 10.8% since Aug. 3, while Communication Services fell 11.3%.
- [[Information Technology Sector]]: semiconductor equipment down 23.9% and semiconductors down 13.6%, offset by IT Consulting up 22.3%.
- [[Magnificent 7]]: diverging, with [[Nvidia]] at $3.92 trillion after [[Jensen Huang]]'s resignation news and [[Apple]] at a record $5.18 trillion.
- [[Forward P/E]]: S&P 500 at 18.0; Growth fell 27% to 19.0 while Value stayed at 18.0.
- [[Earnings Estimates]]: 2027 consensus at $427.01 and 2028 at $496.24, both new highs.
- [[10-Year Treasury Yield]]: jumped to 5.10% after CPI and PPI, with the TIPS yield driving the rise.
- [[Credit Default Swaps]]: spreads rose on AI borrower concerns such as CoreWeave, then narrowed.

## Key Takeaways
- **Calm Surface**: S&P 500 down only 0.5% since Aug. 3; Russell 2000 down 6.4%.
- **Hidden Turbulence**: Sector moves range from +16.4% (Health Care) to -11.3% (Communication Services).
- **Rising Estimates**: Analyst earnings estimates are rising, unusual versus their typical fall.
- **Multiple Compression**: Only 36.1% of S&P 500 stocks trade above 20x forward earnings, down from nearly 50%.
- **Growth Premium Gone**: Growth-Value forward P/E spread is 1.0 point, narrowest since 2010.
- **Inflation Signals**: Breakeven spread is 2.21%; energy, diesel, agriculture, and copper prices eased.
- **Yardeni View**: AI buildout slowdown is "speed bumps, not roadblocks"; no recession expected.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Aggregate stability can mask large offsetting moves underneath** — An index near flat can contain big gains and losses across components that largely cancel each other out.
→ explains: Calm Surface, Hidden Turbulence

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Yardeni's central claim about the market?
**A**: The major indexes have gone nowhere since early August, but calm on the surface hides sizable crosscurrents within sectors. He still expects a rally to 7,900 by year end.

**Q2**: What data show the turbulence under the surface?
**A**: Since Aug. 3, Health Care gained 16.4% while Communication Services lost 11.3%, and within tech, semiconductor equipment fell 23.9% while IT Consulting rose 22.3%.

**Q3**: What does the valuation data imply for investors?
**A**: Growth no longer commands a premium, with the Growth-Value P/E spread at 1.0 point, the narrowest since 2010. Yardeni thinks multiple contraction may be near a bottom because he sees no recession.
