## Summary

[[Nebius Group]] (NBIS) is an AI computing infrastructure company renting GPU-powered data centers, which delivered 454% year-over-year revenue growth to $582 million in Q2 2026. Management projects 2026 full-year revenue of $3–$3.4 billion and year-end ARR of $7–$9 billion, backed by over $40 billion in customer commitments. Despite strong fundamentals, the stock's ~18x forward sales valuation and recent 20%+ drop from dilution concerns warrant caution for new investors.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nebius Group]] (NBIS)**: AI cloud infrastructure provider renting GPU compute to enterprises and AI model builders.
- **[[GPU Cloud Infrastructure]]**: The business model of leasing GPU-powered data centers as a service, competing in the neocloud segment alongside players like [[Lambda Labs]] and [[CoreWeave]].
- **[[Annual Recurring Revenue]] (ARR)**: A key SaaS-adjacent metric Nebius uses; projected at $7–$9 billion by year-end 2026.
- **[[Infrastructure Payback Period]]**: Time for capital invested in hardware to be recovered through revenue; Nebius shortened this to ~22 months from the historical 2–3 year range.
- **[[Convertible Notes]]**: Nebius issued $5 billion in convertible notes, raising dilution concerns that triggered a 20%+ share price drop.
- **[[Forward Revenue Multiple]]**: The stock trades at ~18x forward sales on a ~$60 billion market cap — a high-growth valuation that prices in sustained outperformance.

## Key Takeaways

- **Revenue surge**: Q2 revenue +454% YoY to $582M; adjusted EBITDA of $236M.
- **Scale of demand**: Four contracts exceeding $1 billion each; $40B+ in total customer commitments.
- **2026 guidance**: Full-year revenue of $3–$3.4B; year-end ARR of $7–$9B.
- **Capital efficiency win**: Infrastructure payback periods shortened to ~22 months.
- **Valuation risk**: ~18x forward sales is a premium multiple requiring continued execution.
- **Dilution risk**: $5B convertible notes offering caused 20%+ stock decline.
- **Industry risk**: Overcapacity across AI cloud sector could compress pricing.
- **Author verdict**: Strong business, not necessarily a great stock at any price — new buyers should size positions small.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Price vs. Value]]**: The author explicitly separates business quality from stock attractiveness — a reminder that valuation multiples matter independently of operational excellence; paying 18x forward sales embeds optimistic assumptions that must be realized to earn a return.
- **[[Margin of Safety]]**: Recommending a smaller initial position for new investors rather than full commitment reflects the classic principle of leaving room for error when uncertainty is high and the price reflects near-perfection.
