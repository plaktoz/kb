## Summary

U.S. equities rallied toward record highs the week of October 9, 2026 (S&P 500 +0.6%, Dow +0.8%) even as 10-year Treasury yields hit 5.31% — the highest since 2002 — on oil prices, sticky inflation, a hawkish [[Federal Reserve]], and high federal debt. The dollar extended a fourth straight weekly gain, while French bond yields climbed on debt-sustainability worries, and markets now turn to CPI data, retail sales, and the start of Q3 earnings season.

## Core Concepts

- [[10-Year Treasury Yield]] — peaked at 5.31% this week, highest since 2002, tightening domestic financial conditions.
- [[Federal Reserve]] — hawkish policy stance cited as a driver of bond market volatility alongside oil prices and federal debt.
- [[Core Inflation]] — the September CPI print, especially core (ex-energy/food), is the key data point markets await next week.
- [[Q3 Earnings Season]] — large banks report first; robust earnings growth has been a key support for 2026 equity returns.
- [[French Government Bonds]] — yields rising sharply on investor concern over debt sustainability outside the U.S.

## Key Takeaways

- **Equity rally**: S&P 500 +0.6%, Dow Jones +0.8% on the day, closing the week at/near record highs.
- **Yield spike**: 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.31%, the highest level since 2002, before settling at 5.24%.
- **Dollar strength**: Fourth consecutive weekly gain against a trade-weighted currency basket.
- **European stress**: French bond yields rising sharply on deficit and debt-sustainability concerns.
- **Next week's catalysts**: September CPI, core inflation, retail sales, and Q3 bank earnings all in focus.
- **Resilience despite risk**: Strong corporate fundamentals and narrow credit spreads have cushioned the impact of rising yields so far.
