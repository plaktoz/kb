## Summary
A hot October CPI pushed the 10-year Treasury yield back above 5% and made a December Fed hike a certainty in traders' eyes, with a smaller slice betting on 50 basis points. Fed officials and economists are split on size, but several argue that stopping short would damage Fed credibility. Rising yields threaten to close the brief window of lower mortgage rates and hit variable-rate borrowers quickly.

## Core Concepts
- [[Federal Reserve]]: under [[Kevin Warsh]], it has pulled back from rigid forward guidance, leaving traders to interpret each meeting.
- [[10-Year Treasury Yield]]: held above 5% after jumping more than 13 basis points; hit about 5.34% in October, a level not seen since 2002.
- [[CME FedWatch]]: showed full odds of a hike at the Dec. 9 meeting.
- [[Christopher Waller]]: says a 50 basis point hike should be on the table and that stopping at 25 "would put our credibility at risk."
- [[Lorie Logan]] and [[Beth Hammack]]: Logan wants at least two more quarter-point hikes; Hammack favors smaller, more frequent hikes.
- [[Consumer Price Index]] and [[Producer Price Index]]: October CPI rose 0.4% (a tenth above forecast); September wholesale prices rose 6.3% year over year.
- [[Mortgage Rates]]: the 30-year rate fell to 7.11%, but Fannie Mae projects 7.4% in Q4 and 7.5% through 2027.
- [[Iran Oil Prices]]: oil fell about 13% the prior week, though tanker and pipeline attacks leave the picture unsettled.

## Key Takeaways
- **December Hike**: Traders priced a December hike as certain by Nov. 13.
- **Hot Inflation**: October CPI of 0.4% beat forecasts; seventh straight month prices outran wages.
- **Yield Jump**: The 2-year jumped 19 basis points; the 10-year reached 5%.
- **Credibility Debate**: Waller, EY's Daco and KPMG's Swonk say skipping hikes risks Fed credibility.
- **Dissenting View**: Macquarie's Wizman sees a single quarter-point hike as base case.
- **Wholesale Pressure**: Producer prices up 6.3% year over year, sharpest since 2022.
- **Borrower Impact**: Variable-rate credit cards and HELOCs reprice almost immediately after a hike.
- **Housing Weakness**: Pending home sales hit the index's lowest reading in September.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. A central bank's commitment to its target is only credible if it acts when data demand it** — Stopping short of what inflation warrants signals weak resolve, which raises the cost of restoring price stability later.
→ explains: Credibility Debate, December Hike

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central argument about the Fed and Treasury yields?
**A**: After a hot October CPI, the 10-year yield returned above 5% and traders priced a December hike as certain, with debate shifting to whether the Fed goes 25 or 50 basis points.

**Q2**: What data pushed yields up, and where does pressure come from next?
**A**: October CPI rose 0.4%, a tenth above forecast, sending the 10-year up 14 basis points and the 2-year up 19. Oil and wholesale prices are next: producer prices rose 6.3% year over year in September.

**Q3**: How do rising yields affect borrowers?
**A**: A Fed hike reprices variable-rate debt such as credit cards and home equity lines almost immediately. Mortgage rates track the 10-year yield, so the brief dip to 7.11% could reverse.
