## Summary

Crude oil prices rose again this week as tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz slowed to roughly 11 ships/day (from over 100/day pre-war) amid continued Iranian attacks and a US blockade, while the IEA forecasts a Q3 2026 global supply deficit of 1.8 million barrels/day even as Gulf petrostates concede Iran may control the Strait for years to come.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Strait of Hormuz Chokepoint]]**: Tanker traffic has collapsed to ~11 ships/day from over 100/day pre-war as Iran increases attacks and the US maintains its blockade of Iranian vessels, with both sides disputing who controls the waterway.
- **[[Oil Supply Deficit Forecasting]]**: The IEA projects a Q3 2026 deficit of 1.8 million b/d (vs. OPEC's more moderate 580,000 b/d demand-decline estimate), driven by an average 4.3 million b/d production drop.
- **[[Alternative Route Diversification]]**: Saudi Arabia has only been able to move an incremental +200,000 b/d of its +1.0 million b/d output increase due to new disruptions at the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, forcing record-high domestic inventory storage (highest since 2016).
- **[[Strategic Petroleum Reserve Drawdown]]**: The US SPR fell to 299 million bbl, its lowest level since 1983, even as commercial crude inventories rose substantially.

## Key Takeaways

- **Traffic collapse**: Tanker throughput through Hormuz is down roughly 90% (11/day vs. 100+/day pre-war).
- **Price levels**: WTI ranged $76.80–$83.35/bbl; Brent hit $90.05/bbl high, with the WTI/Brent spread widening to $6.10.
- **Supply forecast**: IEA sees a 4.3 million b/d production drop and 1.8 million b/d Q3 deficit; OPEC's demand-decline estimate is more modest (~580,000 b/d).
- **Long-run outlook**: Gulf petrostates increasingly expect Iran to retain effective control of the Strait, with transit fees a live possibility three to five years out.
- **Infrastructure response**: Phillips66, Kinder Morgan, and HF Sinclair announced a $5 billion pipeline to move refined product from West Texas to California, which has lost two refineries this year.
