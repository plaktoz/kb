## Summary
[[Apple]] beat Q3 revenue and earnings estimates, with [[iPhone]] sales up 22% to $54.25B, but its stock fell nearly 6% after-hours on weak forward guidance blamed on a "hundred-year flood" memory chip crunch and foreign exchange headwinds. The report was [[Tim Cook]]'s last earnings call as CEO before [[John Ternus]] takes over on September 1, with Apple also gearing up for a redesigned [[Siri]] powered by [[Google Gemini]] and new iPhones in September.

## Core Concepts
- [[Apple]] — Q3 revenue $109.42B (+15%), net income $29.79B, EPS $2.02 beating the $1.89 estimate
- [[Tim Cook]] — outgoing CEO who led his final earnings call, citing a memory chip "hundred-year flood" and FX headwinds
- [[John Ternus]] — incoming CEO (head of hardware) who takes over September 1, will lead future earnings calls
- [[Memory Chip Crunch]] — rising costs from suppliers [[Samsung Electronics]], [[Micron Technology]], and [[SK Hynix]] already forced Mac and iPad price increases
- [[Siri]] — upcoming redesign powered by [[Google Gemini]], seen as a critical AI test for Apple
- [[Price Elasticity of Demand]] — analyst framing for whether higher iPhone prices would hurt or help Apple

## Key Takeaways
- **Q3 beat**: revenue $109.42B (+15%), EPS $2.02 vs $1.89 estimate, net income $29.79B
- **iPhone strength**: sales +22% YoY to $54.25B on iPhone 17 demand and strong upgraders
- **Weak guidance**: current-quarter revenue growth forecast at 9-11%, below the 12% consensus
- **Memory crunch cited**: Cook calls it a "hundred-year flood," already raised Mac and iPad prices
- **iPhone prices held so far**: but Cook signaled memory costs will keep rising through the year
- **Mac and iPad diverge**: Mac revenue +29% to $10.35B; iPad revenue -6% to $6.19B, missing estimates
- **Services growth**: +12% to $30.74B, with 1.5B paid subscribers across iCloud and third-party apps
- **Leadership transition**: Cook's last call as CEO; Ternus takes over Sept 1 and leads the next earnings call

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models
- **[[Price Elasticity of Demand]]**: Whether Apple can pass rising memory costs to consumers depends on whether premium buyers keep upgrading or slow their cycles instead.

## 🃏 Review Questions
**Q1**: What caused Apple's stock to slide despite beating earnings estimates?
**A**: Apple issued weak forward guidance (9-11% revenue growth vs 12% consensus), citing memory chip supply constraints and foreign exchange headwinds, which sent shares down almost 6% after hours.

**Q2**: What specific cost pressure did Tim Cook highlight, and how has Apple responded?
**A**: Cook described a "hundred-year flood" memory chip crunch that has already forced price increases on Macs and iPads; Apple has so far held iPhone prices steady but expects even higher memory costs ahead.

**Q3**: What leadership and product transitions is Apple navigating alongside this earnings report?
**A**: This was Tim Cook's last earnings call as CEO before handing over to John Ternus on September 1, while Apple prepares to launch a redesigned Siri powered by Google Gemini and new iPhones in September.
