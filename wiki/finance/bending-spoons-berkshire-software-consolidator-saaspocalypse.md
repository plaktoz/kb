## Summary

[[Bending Spoons]], the Milan-based holding company that listed on Nasdaq in July, is buying software businesses sidelined by the AI boom, and [[Berkshire Hathaway]] added a $330 million stake this week, taking its total investment above $900 million. Unlike private equity firms, it holds acquisitions for profit rather than for resale, makes deep operational and product changes, and cuts staff and raises prices after deals. The open question is whether the model can revive software while AI squeezes margins across the sector.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Bending Spoons]]**: Owns AOL, Vimeo, Eventbrite, Evernote and Brightcove; agreed to buy [[Airtable]] for about $1.3 billion (nearly 90% below its 2021 peak) and Miro for $1.36 billion. Market cap is about $30 billion after the stock jumped 41% on the Berkshire news.
- **"SaaSpocalypse"**: AI agents are displacing software developers and the products they built; Intuit, HubSpot and Adobe are down at least 30% this year and private SaaS firms struggle to raise capital.
- **Private equity comparison**: Thoma Bravo and Vista bought undervalued SaaS to cut costs and exit; Bending Spoons holds for profit, Buffett-style, but in technology and with hands-on operations.
- **[[Switching Costs]] pricing**: After buying Evernote, it lifted the personal plan from $70 to $130 a year; Mizuho's analyst argues high switching costs make such prices tolerable.
- **Deal-driven growth**: Revenue more than doubled to over $700 million in the August quarter, almost entirely from acquisitions; net income rose 171% to $177 million.
- **Cost of the model**: Layoffs at Airtable, Evernote, Vimeo and WeTransfer.

## Key Takeaways

- **Berkshire Doubling Down**: Total Berkshire investment now exceeds $900 million.
- **Buyer's Market**: Pre-ChatGPT software is treated as legacy; sellers outnumber buyers.
- **Different Playbook**: Hold-and-operate rather than cut-and-flip.
- **Pricing Power**: Evernote price rose roughly 86% after acquisition.
- **Unproven Thesis**: Revival depends on products staying desirable as AI compresses margins.
- **Acquisition-Fueled**: Growth comes almost entirely from deals, not organic revenue.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. High switching costs let an owner raise prices without losing customers** — Customers locked in by data and habit tolerate increases that are small relative to the cost of leaving. · *model:* [[Switching Costs]]

**T2. Asset prices fall until buyers see a return, creating opportunity for patient capital** — When a sector is sold off, acquirers willing to hold and operate can buy at prices that would have been irrational earlier.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Bending Spoons doing and why does it matter now?
**A**: It is acquiring software companies cheapened by the AI boom, and Berkshire's added $330 million stake gave the strategy a major vote of confidence.

**Q2**: How does its approach differ from private equity?
**A**: It holds companies to generate profits rather than to sell them, and it makes deep product, operational and staffing changes, including layoffs and price increases.

**Q3**: What risk remains for investors?
**A**: Growth has come almost entirely from acquisitions, and the products must stay desirable enough to command payment while AI squeezes software margins.
