## Summary

Investing just $100 per month in the S&P 500 over 20 years can grow to approximately $76,000, with roughly $52,000 of that coming from compounded gains rather than direct contributions. The article argues that consistency — not the size of the contribution — is the primary driver of long-term wealth building. Staying invested during market downturns is especially valuable because declining prices allow investors to buy shares at a discount.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Compound Interest]]**: The dominant engine of long-term portfolio growth; ~$52,000 of the projected $76,000 outcome comes from compounding, not the $24,000 in actual contributions.
- **[[Dollar-Cost Averaging]]**: Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals regardless of market conditions, smoothing out the impact of volatility.
- **[[S&P 500]]**: The benchmark used; has delivered roughly 10% average annual returns over the past century.
- **[[Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO)]]**: The specific vehicle cited as a low-cost way to access S&P 500 returns.
- **[[Market Downturns as Buying Opportunities]]**: Continuing contributions during declines means purchasing more shares at lower prices, improving long-term outcomes.

## Key Takeaways

- **Total outcome**: $100/month × 20 years yields ~$76,000; only $24,000 from deposits.
- **Compounding dominates**: ~$52,000 (68% of the final balance) comes from reinvested gains.
- **Consistency over amount**: Staying invested regularly matters more than the exact return rate.
- **Downturns are beneficial**: Buying during dips lowers average share cost and boosts long-term returns.
- **Accessible entry point**: Significant wealth-building does not require large starting capital.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Compounding]]**: The exponential growth of reinvested returns means time in the market matters far more than the size of any single contribution — the $52,000 gain on a $24,000 input illustrates this directly.
- **[[Loss Aversion]]**: Investors who stop contributing during downturns let this bias override rational behavior; staying the course during declines is mechanically advantageous, not just psychologically strong.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim about small monthly S&P 500 investments?
**A**: Consistent $100 monthly contributions over 20 years can produce ~$76,000, with compounded gains (~$52,000) far exceeding the $24,000 actually deposited.

**Q2**: What specific mechanism makes continuing to invest during downturns advantageous?
**A**: When markets decline, each $100 buys more shares at a lower price, lowering the average cost basis and amplifying gains when prices eventually recover.

**Q3**: How should an investor practically apply this research?
**A**: Automate a fixed monthly contribution to a low-cost S&P 500 index fund (e.g., VOO) and maintain contributions even during market pullbacks rather than pausing or reducing them.
