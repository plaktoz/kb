## Summary

[[Microsoft]]'s fiscal 2026 annual report reveals that [[OpenAI]] generated $24.1 billion in commercial revenue — roughly 7% of Microsoft's total $331.8 billion — making it the only customer named in the filing and approximately one-quarter of [[Azure]]'s record $100 billion+ revenue. While this concentration drives extraordinary backlog growth (84% YoY to $678 billion), excluding OpenAI shrinks that growth to just 25%, exposing Microsoft to significant customer-specific risk from a counterparty burning $3.7 billion per quarter.

## Core Concepts

- [[Microsoft]] — FY2026 total revenue $331.8B (+18% YoY); sole named customer relationship is OpenAI
- [[OpenAI]] — generated $24.1B in commercial arrangements with Microsoft; burned $3.7B in Q1 2026; expected cash burn of ~$280B from 2026–2030
- [[Azure]] — surpassed $100B revenue in FY2026 (+41% YoY); OpenAI arrangements represent ~25% of that figure
- [[Customer Concentration Risk]] — single-customer dependency magnifying both upside (backlog) and downside (default/exit risk)
- [[Commercial Backlog]] — $678B total (84% YoY); ex-OpenAI grows only 25% YoY per CFO [[Amy Hood]]
- [[AI Infrastructure Capex]] — Microsoft spent $115.9B on property/equipment in FY2026 (~80% YoY increase)

## Key Takeaways

- **OpenAI revenue**: $24.1B from commercial arrangements — ~7% of Microsoft's total revenue
- **Azure concentration**: OpenAI = ~25% of Azure's $100B+ annual revenue
- **Backlog illusion**: 84% backlog growth collapses to 25% when OpenAI's $250B Azure commitment is excluded
- **OpenAI cash burn**: $3.7B in Q1 2026; projected ~$280B burn 2026–2030 (per FT)
- **Revised terms (April 2026)**: Revenue-sharing payments to Microsoft now capped through 2030; OpenAI gained flexibility to use other cloud providers
- **Equity upside**: Microsoft holds ~27% of OpenAI, valued at ~$135B — both infrastructure provider and part-owner
- **Analyst stance**: "Reason for attention, not alarm" — broader business strong, but stock rated hold due to concentration risk

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When backlog growth becomes the headline metric, the market anchors on the 84% figure without scrutinizing its single-customer origin — the indicator becomes misleading precisely because it is being watched.
- **[[Counterparty Risk]]**: OpenAI's $3.7B/quarter cash burn means Microsoft's largest revenue source is also its most financially fragile customer; if OpenAI's fundraising falters, Microsoft's Azure growth narrative unravels with it.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central argument about OpenAI's role in Microsoft's business?
**A**: OpenAI is simultaneously Microsoft's biggest AI customer ($24.1B in FY2026 commercial arrangements, ~25% of Azure revenue) and its biggest risk, given OpenAI's extreme cash burn and the concentration of backlog growth in a single counterparty.

**Q2**: What specific data point reveals how dependent Microsoft's backlog growth is on OpenAI?
**A**: Microsoft's commercial backlog grew 84% YoY to $678B, but CFO Amy Hood confirmed that excluding OpenAI, backlog growth was only 25% — meaning the majority of the headline figure traces to OpenAI's single $250B Azure services commitment.

**Q3**: How did the April 2026 agreement change Microsoft's OpenAI exposure, and what does the analyst recommend?
**A**: The revised terms capped OpenAI's revenue-sharing payments to Microsoft through 2030 and gave OpenAI flexibility to use other cloud providers, weakening Microsoft's lock-in; the analyst rates MSFT a hold, citing concentration risk despite the broader business remaining strong.
