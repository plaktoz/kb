## Summary

IBM quietly dropped its publicized running total of generative AI business — a figure that had climbed from over $3 billion (October 2024) to over $7.5 billion (July 2025) — from the July 2026 Q2 earnings call, coinciding with a quarter that fell short of expectations. The company replaced that absolute metric with proportional measures, and pivoted its narrative toward [[watsonx Orchestrate]] as its core agent-building platform. Investors should watch whether IBM's Consulting segment achieves guided "low to mid-single-digit" constant-currency growth as the primary indicator of AI monetization.

## Core Concepts

- **[[IBM]] AI Bookings Metric** — cumulative generative-AI business figure ($3B → $7.5B) dropped from Q2 2026 earnings call; replaced with proportional metrics
- **[[watsonx Orchestrate]]** — IBM's new narrative centerpiece; positioned as agent-building platform replacing the bookings headline
- **[[IBM Consulting]]** — ~$21.1B annually (~30% of a $69.1B top line); signings +6%, revenue +1% constant currency in Q2 2026
- **[[Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR)]]** — IBM's recurring software base generates $24.6B ARR (up 8% YoY), representing ~80% of software revenue
- **[[Earnings Narrative Shift]]** — transition from absolute cumulative AI total to proportional metrics (genAI = ~half of Consulting signings, >30% of backlog)

## Key Takeaways

- **Metric dropped**: IBM stopped reporting its $7.5B cumulative genAI bookings figure at Q2 2026 earnings.
- **Proportional framing**: genAI now ~50% of Consulting signings and >30% of Consulting backlog.
- **Consulting scale**: $21.1B/year; signings +6%, revenue +1% constant currency.
- **ARR**: $24.6B, up 8% YoY; ~80% of software revenue is recurring.
- **Stock context**: IBM at $234.71, ~28% below 52-week high, up 24.5% over two years.
- **Key watchpoint**: Whether Consulting revenue achieves "low to mid-single-digit" constant-currency growth.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: Once a cumulative AI bookings total became IBM's headline metric, it functioned as a target — dropping it when growth slowed suggests the metric had become a proxy for momentum rather than a true measure of AI business health.
- **[[Narrative Framing]]**: Switching from an absolute cumulative figure to proportional metrics obscures the absolute growth trajectory; investors should translate proportional claims back into dollar ranges to preserve comparability across quarters.
