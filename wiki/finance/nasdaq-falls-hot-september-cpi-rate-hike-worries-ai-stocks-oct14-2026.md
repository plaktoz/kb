## Summary

Hotter-than-expected September [[Consumer Price Index|CPI]] data on October 14, 2026 sent the [[Nasdaq Composite]] down 1.85% as traders raised bets that the [[Federal Reserve]] will hike rates again, hitting AI stocks most sensitive to borrowing costs such as [[Oracle]], [[CoreWeave]] and [[Nebius]]. Rate-hike odds for this month jumped to 37% from 22% a day earlier, while the [[Philadelphia Semiconductor Index]] fell for a sixth straight session. Bank stocks also weakened as [[Bank of America]] took a $1.2 billion regulatory charge and [[Morgan Stanley]] fell despite higher profit.

## Core Concepts

- **Inflation surprise**: September consumer prices accelerated on surging gasoline costs, with price pressures broadening beyond energy, per the [[Bureau of Labor Statistics]].
- **Rate-hike repricing**: CME's [[FedWatch Tool]] showed a 37% chance of a hike this month, up from 22%; the Fed already raised rates in September.
- **AI stock sensitivity**: The year's biggest gainers, including [[Oracle]], [[CoreWeave]] and [[Nebius]], tumbled on higher-borrowing-cost fears. See [[AI Trade]].
- **AI server downgrades**: [[Barclays]] cut [[Dell Technologies]] and [[Hewlett Packard Enterprise]] to equal-weight from overweight.
- **Chip weakness**: The [[Philadelphia Semiconductor Index]] logged its longest losing streak since May 2025.
- **Narrow market**: The [[S&P 500]] is up over 12% in 2026, mostly from AI stocks, while the median component gained only 4%. See [[Market Breadth]].
- **Bank earnings**: [[Bank of America]] profit fell on a $1.2B anti-money-laundering charge; the [[S&P 500 Banks Index]] hit a four-month low.
- **Fed commentary**: Vice Chair for Supervision [[Michelle Bowman]] said before the data the Fed had time to wait and see after its September hike.
- **Berkshire endorsement**: [[Bending Spoons]] rallied after [[Berkshire Hathaway]] invested another $330 million in the Vimeo and AOL owner.
- **Quote**: [[Adam Sarhan]] of 50 Park Investments: "Inflation remains public enemy number one for the market and for the Fed."
