## Summary
Costco has beaten the S&P 500 in 16 of the past 25 years, powered by a membership model with above-90% renewal rates, growing warehouse and e-commerce expansion, and clean finances — though its premium valuation (30x+ forward earnings) leaves little room for disappointment.

## Core Concepts
- The [[Membership Model]] moat — recurring high-renewal revenue underwriting Costco's economics.
- [[Comparable Sales Growth]] and digital channel expansion as growth levers.
- Valuation premium risk — high multiple vs. execution risk (especially international, e.g. China).

## Key Takeaways
- **Long Track Record**: Beat the S&P 500 in 16 of 25 years (2000-2025), a 64% win rate.
- **Strong Renewal**: US/Canada renewal above 92%, global renewal near 90%.
- **Growth Numbers**: July 2026 net sales $23.12B (+10.7% YoY); comparable sales +8.9%; digital comparable sales +17.7%.
- **Historical Return**: ~124% over 5 years vs. ~75% for the S&P 500; over 28,000% real terms since the mid-1980s.
- **Key Risk**: Trades above 30x forward earnings — a slowdown in membership growth or international execution missteps (especially China) could hurt the stock disproportionately.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core prediction?
**A**: Costco will continue to outperform the S&P 500 over the next year, extending a pattern seen in 16 of the past 25 years.

**Q2**: What's the key mechanism behind Costco's outperformance?
**A**: A membership model with very high (90%+) renewal rates, combined with growing comparable sales, new warehouse openings, and expanding digital/delivery channels.

**Q3**: What's the main risk to this thesis?
**A**: Costco's premium valuation (30x+ forward earnings) leaves little margin for error — slower membership growth or international execution problems could trigger an outsized stock decline.
