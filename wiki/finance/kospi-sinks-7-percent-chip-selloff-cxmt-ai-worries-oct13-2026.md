## Summary

South Korea's [[Kospi]] plunged 7.4% to 5,914.35 on October 13, 2026 — its lowest since late July — as chip shares sold off amid unease over the [[AI Boom]]. [[Samsung Electronics]] and [[SK Hynix]] tumbled after reports that Chinese memory maker [[CXMT]] could increasingly challenge the world's biggest memory chipmakers, dragging chip stocks across Asia and the U.S. Oil prices eased but remained far above pre-[[Iran War]] levels amid Middle East tensions including [[Houthi]] attacks on Saudi Arabia.

## Core Concepts

- [[Kospi]] — South Korea's benchmark, which hit an all-time high above 9,000 in June on the AI boom before this sell-off.
- [[Memory Chip Competition]] — reported advances by [[CXMT]] threaten incumbents [[Samsung Electronics]], [[SK Hynix]] and [[Micron Technology]].
- [[AI Boom]] worries — investor unease about AI-driven valuations clouding semiconductor prospects.
- Regional contagion — [[Nikkei 225]], [[Tokyo Electron]], [[SoftBank Group]] (an [[OpenAI]] investor), [[Taiex]] and [[TSMC]] all fell.
- U.S. chip weakness — [[Nvidia]], [[AMD]], [[Micron Technology]] and [[Broadcom]] declined in the prior session.
- [[Oil Prices]] — [[Brent Crude]] at $105.20 vs ~$72 in late February before the Iran war; ING's [[Warren Patterson]] and [[Ewa Manthey]] flag Gulf supply risks.
- [[Bank Earnings]] — [[Citigroup]], [[JPMorgan Chase]] and [[Wells Fargo]] report this week.

## Key Takeaways

- **Kospi Plunge**: Down 7.4% to 5,914.35, lowest since late July.
- **Round Trip**: Index crossed 9,000 at a June all-time high.
- **Memory Leaders Hit**: Samsung fell 7.7%; SK Hynix plunged 8.8%.
- **CXMT Threat**: Chinese memory advances may challenge Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron.
- **Japan Slide**: Nikkei 225 −2.5%; Tokyo Electron −6.6%; SoftBank −8%.
- **Wider Asia**: Hang Seng −1%, Shanghai −1.1%, Taiex −1.9%, TSMC −1.6%.
- **U.S. Session**: S&P 500 −0.1%, Dow +0.4%, Nasdaq −0.4%.
- **U.S. Chips**: Nvidia −1.3%, AMD −2.3%, Micron −3.9%, Broadcom −1.9%.
- **Oil Eases**: Brent −1.4% to $105.20; WTI −1.5% to $92.34.
- **Pre-War Gap**: Brent still well above ~$72 late-February level.
- **FX**: Dollar slipped to 158.26 yen; euro rose to $1.1175.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What drove South Korea's Kospi down more than 7% on October 13, 2026?
**A**: A sell-off in semiconductor shares amid investor unease over the AI boom, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix falling after reports that China's CXMT could challenge the top memory chipmakers.

**Q2**: How far did the leading Korean memory stocks fall, and how did the Kospi's level compare to its peak?
**A**: Samsung fell 7.7% and SK Hynix 8.8%, while the Kospi dropped to 5,914.35 after having crossed 9,000 at its June all-time high.

**Q3**: What does the spread of losses across Asia and the U.S. imply for semiconductor investors?
**A**: Competitive threats to memory incumbents and AI-boom worries hit chip stocks globally — from Tokyo Electron and TSMC to Micron and Nvidia — so concentration in AI-linked chips carries correlated cross-market risk.
