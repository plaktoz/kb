## Summary

The September 2026 U.S. CPI report (due October 14) is expected to show headline inflation accelerating on rising gasoline prices, but its policy significance hinges on whether [[Core CPI]] also strengthens. A purely energy-driven rise would carry limited implications for the [[Federal Reserve]], while a broad-based core acceleration could force restrictive policy to stay in place longer. For markets, the report's composition relative to expectations — not the headline number alone — will drive [[Treasury Yields]], the [[US Dollar]], [[Gold]] and equities.

## Core Concepts

- [[Headline vs Core Inflation]] — headline includes volatile food and energy; core strips them out to gauge whether pressures are broad-based, though it is not a complete measure of persistence.
- [[Energy Price Shock]] — gasoline rose from $4.157/gal (Sep 7) to $4.465 (Sep 28), peaking at $4.478 (Sep 21), per [[U.S. Energy Information Administration]] data.
- [[Second-Round Effects]] — sustained energy costs can raise transport, logistics and production costs that firms pass to consumers; transmission depends on shock persistence, demand and margin absorption.
- [[Cooling Labor Market]] — September payrolls +29,000, average hourly earnings +0.1% m/m and 3.0% y/y, unemployment 4.2%.
- [[Input Cost Pressure]] — business surveys show elevated prices paid in services and manufacturing, limiting the pace of [[Disinflation]].
- [[Fed Policy Trade-off]] — keeping conditions restrictive enough to contain inflation without unnecessarily damaging employment.
- [[Yield Curve]] transmission — the 2-year yield tracks near-term policy expectations; the 10-year blends rate expectations, inflation expectations, growth and the [[Term Premium]].
- [[Real Yields]] — rising real yields raise the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding gold.

## Key Takeaways

- **Composition Over Headline**: Core vs energy split matters more than the headline print.
- **Gasoline Surge**: Gas rose ~7% in September, from $4.157 to $4.465 per gallon.
- **August Baseline**: Headline CPI +0.4% m/m; gasoline +3.9% drove over one-third; core +0.3%.
- **Pass-Through Risk**: Energy shocks broaden only if persistent and businesses pass on costs.
- **Conflicting Core Signals**: Softer wages ease services; elevated input costs slow disinflation.
- **Labor Cooling**: Payrolls +29k, wages 3.0% y/y, unemployment 4.2%.
- **Short vs Long Yields**: 2-year reacts to policy bets; 10-year to broader growth/inflation mix.
- **Scenario 1 — Broad Upside**: Yields and dollar rise; gold and growth stocks face headwinds.
- **Scenario 2 — Energy-Only Rise**: Limited yield move; dollar may weaken; gold and equities may benefit.
- **Scenario 3 — Broad Undershoot**: Lower yields, weaker dollar support gold; stocks benefit unless demand collapses.
- **Disinflation Quality**: Supply-driven disinflation differs from demand-collapse disinflation for equities.
- **Watch List**: Monthly core, shelter/services, energy contribution, post-release yields and dollar.
- **Conditional Reactions**: Market moves depend on surprise size versus what is already priced.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Prices move on surprises, not levels** — An economic release moves asset prices only to the extent it changes expectations already embedded in prices; a print that merely confirms the priced-in outcome produces little reaction. · *model:* [[Expectations Investing]]
→ explains: Conditional Reactions, Scenario 1 — Broad Upside, Watch List

**T2. Transient shocks matter for policy only if they propagate** — A price rise confined to a volatile component signals little about persistent inflation unless it spreads into broader prices through cost pass-through; policymakers therefore weight the underlying, broad-based measure over the headline.
→ explains: Composition Over Headline, Pass-Through Risk, Scenario 2 — Energy-Only Rise, Conflicting Core Signals

**T3. Higher rates lower the present value of future cash flows and raise the cost of holding yieldless assets** — When yields rise, discounting shrinks the value of distant earnings (hitting growth stocks hardest), and the forgone yield makes non-yielding assets like gold less attractive. · *model:* [[Discounted Cash Flow]]
→ explains: Scenario 1 — Broad Upside, Scenario 3 — Broad Undershoot, Short vs Long Yields

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central question the September 2026 CPI report must answer for the Fed and markets?
**A**: Whether the expected rise in inflation is concentrated in energy (a temporary shock) or accompanied by renewed strength in core, underlying prices. The composition matters more than the headline figure.

**Q2**: What data point anchored August's inflation reading, and how does September's gasoline trend compare?
**A**: August headline CPI rose 0.4% m/m with gasoline up 3.9% accounting for over one-third of the increase, while core rose 0.3%. September gasoline climbed further, from $4.157 to $4.465 per gallon.

**Q3**: How should an investor interpret a hot headline CPI with contained core?
**A**: It suggests an energy-driven, less broad-based shock, so Treasury yields may rise less and the dollar could weaken, potentially benefiting gold and equities. However, a prolonged energy shock could still weigh on spending, margins and growth.
