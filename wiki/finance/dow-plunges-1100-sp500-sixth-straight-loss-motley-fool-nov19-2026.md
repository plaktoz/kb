## Summary
On November 19, the Dow fell more than 1,100 points (2.19%) and the S&P 500 lost for a sixth straight session as selling spread across nearly every sector. The article argues the downturn has widened from a tech-valuation pullback into a broad market decline, with inflation, rates and valuations outweighing strong earnings such as Nvidia's. A recovery would need better breadth, steadier yields and the S&P 500 regaining its 200-day moving average.

## Core Concepts
- [[Dow Jones Industrial Average]] down 2.19% to 49,097, nearing correction territory; [[S&P 500]] down 2.00% to 7,177; [[Nasdaq Composite]] down 2.23% to 23,886.
- [[10-Year Treasury Yield]] eased three basis points to 5.07% but gave equities little relief.
- [[Federal Reserve]] signaling further [[Interest Rate Hikes]] may be needed, a pressure on earnings and valuations heading into 2027.
- [[Market Breadth]] and the [[200-Day Moving Average]] as recovery markers.
- Outliers: [[Insmed]] +20.58% on reported [[Sanofi]] takeover interest; [[Palo Alto Networks]] +6.42% on earnings and a $4.5B acquisition; [[Advanced Micro Devices]] -7.72% on a media report alleging ties to a blacklisted Chinese server maker.
- [[Nvidia]] earnings failed to reverse sentiment; [[Walmart]] earnings upcoming as a read on holiday consumer spending.

## Key Takeaways
- **Index Moves**: Dow -2.19%, S&P 500 -2.00% (sixth straight loss), Nasdaq -2.23%.
- **Breadth of Selling**: Industrials fell 2.61%; only energy (+0.14%) and staples (+0.04%) finished higher.
- **Safe Havens Failed**: Gold fell 2.10% to $3,663.50; 10-year yield at 5.07%.
- **Earnings Overpowered**: Strong Nvidia results could not offset inflation, rate and valuation worries.
- **Chip Regulatory Risk**: AMD fell 7.72% amid tightening U.S. export restrictions and China-link report.
- **Recovery Checklist**: Better breadth, stable yields, S&P 500 back above 200-day average.
- **Watch Next**: Treasury yields, the U.S. dollar, and Walmart earnings.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about the November 19 selloff?
**A**: The selloff has broadened from a tech-valuation pullback into a wider market downturn. Concerns about inflation, rates and valuations are outweighing strong corporate results like Nvidia's.

**Q2**: What specific data shows how widespread the selling was?
**A**: The Dow fell 2.19% and the S&P 500 had its sixth straight loss, with industrials down 2.61%. Only energy (+0.14%) and consumer staples (+0.04%) finished positive, and gold also fell 2.10%.

**Q3**: What should investors watch for before treating a rally as a real recovery?
**A**: Improving market breadth, stabilizing Treasury yields, and the S&P 500 reclaiming its 200-day moving average. Until then, isolated rallies should not be read as a broader recovery.
