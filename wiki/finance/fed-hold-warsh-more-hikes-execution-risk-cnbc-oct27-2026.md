## Summary
Markets overwhelmingly expect the Fed to hold rates on Wednesday after September's quarter-point hike, with attention on whether Chairman Kevin Warsh signals more tightening. Evercore ISI's Krishna Guha sees "material execution risk" given Warsh's reluctance to offer forward guidance amid hot inflation. Economists disagree on whether further hikes are warranted at all.

## Core Concepts
- [[Federal Open Market Committee]] decision Wednesday; benchmark rates expected unchanged following September's hike.
- [[Kevin Warsh]]: favors letting markets read the data over [[Forward Guidance]], the practice of most of the 21st century.
- [[Krishna Guha]] ([[Evercore ISI]]): warns of "material execution risk"; expects a baseline pace of about one hike per quarter.
- [[Brett Ryan]] ([[Deutsche Bank]]): expects Warsh to signal further tightening over time while skipping October.
- [[Mohamed El-Erian]] ([[Allianz]]): argues tightening is the wrong tool and favors "fiscal containment."
- [[CME FedWatch]]: about 10% odds of an October hike and better than 80% for December.
- Inflation backdrop: [[Consumer Price Index]] at 3.7% in September, core at 2.6%, mortgage rates above 7%, and a low-fire, low-hire labor market.

## Key Takeaways
- **Hold Expected**: Markets overwhelmingly expect no change after September's quarter-point increase.
- **Hot Inflation**: Headline CPI 3.7%; core 2.6%, up 0.3 points from August.
- **Futures Pricing**: About 10% odds of an October hike; over 80% for December.
- **Execution Risk**: Thin guidance on risks and pace of tightening raises chances of error.
- **Hike Cadence**: Jefferson and Williams point to hikes at every other meeting.
- **Split Views**: Citigroup sees cuts next year; El-Erian calls further hikes a mistake.
- **Uneven Impact**: Tech is insulated; housing, car loans, and consumer credit absorb the hit.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Withholding guidance shifts interpretive burden and error risk onto markets** — When a policymaker declines to signal intent, participants must infer it from data, raising the chance of misreading. Guha cites this as the source of "material execution risk."
→ explains: Execution Risk, Futures Pricing

**T2. Policy transmission is uneven across the economy** — A single blunt rate instrument lands hardest on interest-sensitive sectors while others are barely affected. El-Erian's "one minus X" problem is this principle.
→ explains: Uneven Impact, Split Views

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central expectation for this week's FOMC meeting?
**A**: The Fed is expected to hold rates steady, with focus on whether Chairman Warsh signals further hikes despite his preference to avoid forward guidance.

**Q2**: What data and market pricing frame the decision?
**A**: September CPI ran at 3.7% (core 2.6%), mortgage rates exceed 7%, and fed funds futures give roughly 10% odds of an October hike and over 80% for December.

**Q3**: Why do critics think further tightening could be a mistake?
**A**: El-Erian argues the costs would fall on housing, car loans, and consumer credit rather than tech, and that "fiscal containment, not monetary tightening" should address inflation and yields.
