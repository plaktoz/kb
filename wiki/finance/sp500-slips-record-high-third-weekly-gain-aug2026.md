## Summary
US stocks slipped on August 14, 2026, a day after the [[S&P 500]] hit a record high, as deteriorating [[Consumer Sentiment]] and a surprise drop in July retail sales weighed on markets. The S&P 500 still posted its third straight weekly gain, with attention shifting to retail earnings and Nvidia's Aug. 26 report.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Consumer Sentiment Index]]**: The University of Michigan's preliminary August reading showed Americans grew more pessimistic about the economy as inflation concerns persisted, contributing to Friday's pullback despite the prior day's record close.
- **[[Retail Sales as Leading Indicator]]**: US retail sales fell the most in over a year in July, a data point that markets weighed against strong recent earnings in assessing consumer health.
- **[[Earnings Calendar Rotation]]**: With no major reports on the day, investor focus rotated toward upcoming retailer earnings (Target, Walmart) and Nvidia's late-August report as the next catalysts.

## Key Takeaways
- **Daily move**: S&P 500 -0.17% (7,785.76), Dow -0.20%, Nasdaq -0.28%; Russell 2000 bucked the trend, +0.51%.
- **Weekly result**: Third consecutive weekly gain for the S&P 500 despite the Friday pullback.

- **Next catalysts**: Target and Walmart earnings, followed by Nvidia's report on August 26.
