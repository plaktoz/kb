## Summary

Treasury and European government bond yields fell on Monday, November 2, 2026, as Brent crude dropped 4.5% to $108.37 on renewed U.S.-Iran diplomacy. The week is dominated by the midterm elections, the Treasury's quarterly refunding announcement, and Friday's October jobs report. The UK 10-year gilt posted the biggest drop, to its lowest in nearly six weeks.

## Core Concepts

- [[Treasury Yields]]: the 10-year fell 1 basis point to 4.918%.
- [[Brent Crude]]: fell 4.5% to $108.37 on diplomatic efforts to end the U.S.-Iran conflict.
- [[Quarterly Refunding]]: Wednesday's Treasury announcement on issuance and buyback guidance.
- [[Treasury Buybacks]]: long-dated buyback volumes are a key watch item.
- [[Federal Reserve]]: markets still price a 25-basis-point hike in December.
- [[Bank of England]]: fully expected to hike on Thursday.
- [[Gilt Yields]], [[Bund Yields]], [[OAT Yields]]: European 10-year benchmarks.
- [[Fitch Ratings]]: cut France's outlook to negative while affirming A+.
- [[AI Investment Returns]]: renewed doubts boosted demand for Treasurys.

## Key Takeaways

- **Oil Drop**: Brent fell 4.5% to $108.37, easing inflation worries.
- **Treasury Move**: The 10-year yield slipped 1 basis point to 4.918%.
- **Yield Drivers**: Lower oil, weaker dollar, fewer expected Fed hikes, and AI concerns.
- **Gilts Lead**: UK 10-year hit 5.219%, lowest in nearly six weeks.
- **Other Europe**: Bund at 3.314%; French OAT fell 3.9 basis points to 4.934%.
- **France Outlook**: Fitch cut the outlook to negative on weaker growth and wider deficit.
- **Refunding Focus**: Markets watch for lower long-end issuance and more buybacks.
- **Jobs Data**: October payrolls expected at 55,000 versus 29,000 in September.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did bond yields fall on Monday, November 2, 2026?
**A**: Oil prices dropped sharply on renewed U.S.-Iran diplomacy, joined by a weaker dollar, reduced expectations of Fed hikes, and renewed doubts about AI investment returns that boosted Treasury demand.

**Q2**: What are analysts watching in the Treasury's quarterly refunding announcement?
**A**: Forward guidance on maturity composition rather than issuance sizes; the Treasury could cut long-end issuance, increase long-end buybacks, or both, and whether buybacks carry into the next quarter.

**Q3**: What events this week could move bond markets next?
**A**: Tuesday's midterm results, Wednesday's refunding announcement, the Bank of England's expected Thursday hike, and Friday's nonfarm payrolls with a forecast of 55,000 new jobs.
