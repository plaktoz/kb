## Summary

[[Microsoft]] (MSFT) generated $182.9 billion in operating cash flow in fiscal 2026 — a 34%+ increase year-over-year — supporting a $0.98/share quarterly dividend and over 20 consecutive years of annual payout raises. The central question is whether aggressive AI infrastructure capex will compress free cash flow enough to threaten this dividend streak and whether [[Azure]]'s 42% revenue growth can continue to justify heavy capital deployment.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Microsoft]] (MSFT)**: Generated $182.9B operating cash flow in FY2026 (up from $136.2B in FY2025); board declared $0.98/share quarterly dividend extending a 20+ year consecutive annual-raise streak.
- **[[Azure]]**: Reached $29.42 billion in Q4 FY2026 revenue, up 42% year-over-year, with Microsoft pivoting toward usage-based pricing across products like [[GitHub Copilot]] and cloud gaming.
- **[[AI Capital Expenditure]]**: Microsoft expects FY2027 capex to grow year-over-year; aggressive data-center and AI investment creates downside risk to free cash flow even as operating cash flow surges.
- **[[Institutional Ownership]]**: 273 hedge funds held MSFT at end of Q2 2026 (down from 282 in Q1); [[BlackRock]] holds 607.37 million shares (8.18%), [[Vanguard]] 6.53%, [[State Street]] 4.25%; short interest ~1%.
- **[[Usage-Based Pricing]]**: Microsoft is shifting products like GitHub Copilot and cloud gaming away from fixed subscription to consumption-based pricing, aiming to align revenue directly with AI workload growth.

## Key Takeaways

- **Operating cash flow surged 34%+**: $182.9B in FY2026 vs. $136.2B in FY2025 — a record for Microsoft.
- **Dividend at $0.98/share quarterly**: 20+ consecutive years of annual dividend increases, supported by recurring subscription and cloud revenue.
- **Azure at $29.42B in Q4 FY2026**: +42% YoY, outpacing overall company revenue growth.
- **FY2027 capex expected to grow**: capacity constraints and AI data-center buildout remain the primary risk to free cash flow.
- **Hedge fund conviction declining slightly**: 273 funds held MSFT in Q2 vs. 282 in Q1, but short interest stays near ~1% — no significant bearish bet.
- **BlackRock is the largest institutional holder**: 607.37M shares, 8.18% of outstanding.
- **Subscription revenues underpin dividend**: recurring software and cloud income provides durable base for payout sustainability.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Flywheel Effect]]**: Azure's usage-based pricing creates a compounding dynamic — as AI workloads scale, revenue and cash flow grow proportionally, potentially self-funding the capex cycle rather than creating a permanent drag on free cash flow.
- **[[Margin of Safety]]**: The 34%+ operating cash flow growth versus a single annual dividend raise signals a wide buffer between payout obligations and cash generation — even aggressive capex would need to absorb a large multiple of the dividend cost before raising sustainability concerns.
