## Summary

IBM suffered its worst single-day stock collapse in history on July 14, 2026, losing 25% as weak earnings exposed structural decline in its mainframe hardware business. Within weeks, IBM countered with a quantum computing acquisition, a patent sale to stablecoin issuer Circle, and reaffirmation of a dividend streak dating to 1916. The central tension is whether quantum promise can offset legacy hardware decline fast enough for the stock to recover.

## Core Concepts

- **[[IBM]] Stock Decline**: Share price fell 25% in a single session on July 14, 2026, and sits 34% below its 52-week high — described as the worst trading day in company history.
- **[[IBM Z]] Mainframe Erosion**: IBM's infrastructure division is losing customers who redirect capital toward [[AI]] chips and servers, creating structural hardware headwinds.
- **[[Red Hat]]**: IBM's software segment, anchored by Red Hat, continues growing but fails to offset mainframe decline.
- **[[Quantum Computing]]**: IBM acquired [[HRL Laboratories]] (quantum-dot pioneer, formerly owned by Boeing and GM) and, with [[Algorithmiq]], demonstrated quantum advantage simulating heterogeneous quantum materials on the [[Heron Quantum Processor]].
- **Patent Sale to [[Circle (USDC)]]**: IBM sold 680+ patent families (~1,000 granted patents) covering blockchain, banking, insurance, and cloud security; Circle plans to deploy them across Arc blockchain, AI financial tools, and the Circle Payments Network.
- **[[Dividend Longevity]]**: IBM has paid dividends every year since 1916, confirming a $1.69/share quarterly dividend (payable September 10, 2026).

## Key Takeaways

- **Historic drop**: 25% single-day collapse on July 14 — worst in IBM history.
- **Hardware in retreat**: IBM Z mainframe losing customers to AI chips and servers.
- **Software buffer**: Red Hat growth continues but cannot offset hardware decline.
- **Quantum acquisition**: HRL Laboratories deal (quantum-dot tech) expected to close Q3 2026.
- **Quantum milestone**: Heron processor simulated quantum materials beyond classical supercomputers.
- **Patent monetization**: 680+ patent families sold to Circle for undisclosed sum.
- **Revenue guidance**: Currency-adjusted growth target of 4–5% for 2026; free cash flow +~$1B.
- **Technical weakness**: RSI at 39.4, annualized 30-day volatility near 86%, fragile recovery.
- **Analyst target**: Average price target implies ~10% upside from current levels.
- **Century dividend**: Unbroken dividend payments since 1916 provide income floor.

## First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Innovator's Dilemma]]**: IBM's mainframe decline mirrors the classic pattern where an incumbent's profitable legacy product prevents full pivot to disruptive technology — quantum revenue is a decade away while hardware revenue erodes now.
- **[[Optionality]]**: The quantum bet and patent monetization preserve future value without requiring near-term profit; IBM is effectively buying time with non-core asset sales and long-horizon R&D.
