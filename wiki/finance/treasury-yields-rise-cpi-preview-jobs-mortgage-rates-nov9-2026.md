## Summary
Treasury yields rose Monday after a stronger-than-expected October jobs report, with the 10-year at 4.884%, as markets awaited Tuesday's CPI release. Consensus expects headline CPI at 3.9% and core at 2.8%, and a print at or above those levels would reinforce the [[Federal Reserve]] holding rates and keep mortgage costs elevated. Oil's drop on Iran de-escalation offered only partial relief.

## Core Concepts
- [[10-Year Treasury Yield]]: up over 2 basis points to 4.884%; the pricing benchmark for home loans.
- [[30-Year Treasury Bond]]: edged up to 5.273%.
- [[2-Year Treasury Yield]]: up over 4 basis points to 4.57% as near-term rate-cut expectations were priced out.
- [[Consumer Price Index]]: October headline forecast 3.9% (from 3.7%); core 2.8% (from 2.6%).
- [[Producer Price Index]]: due later in the week as an early read on consumer inflation.
- [[Nonfarm Payrolls]]: 165,000 jobs added in October versus 59,000 expected; unemployment 4.1%.
- [[Mortgage Spread]]: gap between the 10-year yield and 30-year mortgage rate, among the widest in recent years.
- [[West Texas Intermediate]]: December futures fell over 3% to $87 after [[Donald Trump]] called new Iran attacks "highly unlikely."

## Key Takeaways
- **Yields Climb**: 10-year rose to 4.884% after the blowout jobs report.
- **Jobs Surprise**: 165,000 jobs added versus 59,000 forecast; unemployment fell to 4.1%.
- **CPI Expectations**: Headline 3.9% and core 2.8%, both above September.
- **Fed on Hold**: Hot data reduces odds of any pivot before year-end.
- **Wide Mortgage Spread**: Spread over the 10-year compounds affordability pressure and cools origination.
- **Oil Offsets Little**: WTI fell to $87, but geopolitics rarely shifts bond direction.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did Treasury yields rise on Monday?
**A**: Markets absorbed a much stronger-than-expected October jobs report (165,000 versus 59,000) and priced out near-term rate cuts, ahead of Tuesday's CPI release.

**Q2**: What CPI figures are expected, and why do they matter for mortgages?
**A**: Consensus is 3.9% headline and 2.8% core, both up from September. A print at or above these would reinforce the Fed holding rates and add pressure to yields and home loan costs.

**Q3**: What does the article imply for mortgage brokers?
**A**: The 10-year and a wide mortgage spread keep home loan costs near multi-year highs and cool origination volumes. Falling oil gave only partial relief, and one broker warned that war is inflationary.
