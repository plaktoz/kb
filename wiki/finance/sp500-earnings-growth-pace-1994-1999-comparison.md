## Summary
The S&P 500 is projected to grow EPS by 24%-32% in 2026 despite only 12.6% price appreciation, a pace of earnings growth outside a recession recovery that has only happened twice before — 1994 and 1999. The article compares 2026's AI-driven earnings surge to both precedents and concludes the market more closely resembles 1994's mid-cycle expansion than 1999's late-cycle blow-off top.

## Core Concepts
- [[S&P 500 Earnings Growth]] outpacing price appreciation, driven by [[Magnificent Seven]] stocks now comprising about a third of the index.
- The [[1994 Market]] precedent: 39.8% EPS growth alongside a flat/falling index, driven by early internet-related chip and telecom demand plus [[Alan Greenspan]]-led Fed rate hikes.
- The [[1999 Market]] precedent: 27.7% EPS growth fueled by [[Y2K]] tech spending, led by [[Microsoft]], [[IBM]], and [[Intel]] — the "last hurrah" before the [[Dot-Com Bubble]] crash.
- [[Anthropic]] and [[SpaceX]] equity stakes inflating GAAP earnings at [[Amazon]] and [[Alphabet]].
- [[Nvidia]] and [[Micron]] as extreme EPS-growth outliers (95% and 1,000%+ respectively) tied to the current AI buildout.

## Key Takeaways
- **Rare growth pace**: 24%-32% projected 2026 EPS growth has only been matched outside a recession recovery in 1994 and 1999.
- **Company outliers**: Nvidia +95% EPS, Amazon +79% EPS (aided by an Anthropic gain), Alphabet GAAP earnings +90% (Anthropic and SpaceX gains), Micron EPS growth above 1,000%.
- **1994 case**: EPS +39.8%, but the S&P 500 fell slightly for the year due to Fed rate hikes; growth came from chip/telecom demand, cost-cutting, and 4.1% GDP growth.
- **1999 case**: EPS +27.7%, one of the best market years ever, led by established tech names ahead of the 2000 dot-com crash.
- **Mitigating factors for 2026**: Nvidia trades at reasonable valuations, there's no comparable IPO mania, and AI-boom leaders (Anthropic, SpaceX) are financially sound or near-profitable.
- **Author's conclusion**: Similarities to 1994 suggest "we're not yet at the stock market peak," despite some 1999-like traits.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Base Rate Thinking]]**: The author anchors judgment about 2026 by locating the only two historical analogs (1994, 1999) for this rate of non-recovery earnings growth, rather than treating today's AI boom as unprecedented.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim?
**A**: The S&P 500's projected 24%-32% EPS growth in 2026 is a growth rate matched outside a recession recovery only in 1994 and 1999, and history suggests the current market more closely resembles 1994's continued expansion than 1999's pre-crash peak.

**Q2**: What specific evidence differentiates 2026 from the 1999 dot-com peak?
**A**: Leading AI stocks like Nvidia trade at reasonable valuations, there is no comparable IPO mania, and key AI-boom companies (Anthropic, SpaceX) are already profitable or near-profitable, unlike the speculative dot-com-era startups.

**Q3**: What is the practical implication for investors?
**A**: Because today's fundamentals lean more toward the 1994 pattern (sustained growth, reasonable valuations) than the 1999 pattern (looming crash), the article suggests the current AI-driven rally has not yet reached its peak.
