## Summary

[[Microsoft]] (MSFT) surged over 15% on July 30 after Q4 earnings beat estimates on Azure crossing $100B in revenue for the first time, while capex of $41B came in below the anticipated $42B — reassuring investors that AI spending discipline and revenue growth can coexist. Even after the surge, MSFT remained down 5% year to date, and the reaction stood in sharp contrast to [[Alphabet]], whose stock fell 6% the prior week after guiding capex sharply higher.

## Core Concepts

- [[Microsoft]] (MSFT) — closed at $451.10 (+15.51%) on Azure's first $100B revenue quarter
- [[Azure]] — Intelligent Cloud segment revenue of $39.3B, about $1B above expectations
- [[Capex Discipline]] — $41B in quarterly capex, below the $42B anticipated, unlike Alphabet's upward revision
- [[Alphabet]] — raised 2026 capex guidance to $195–205B (from $180–190B) the prior week, sending its stock down 6%

## Key Takeaways

- **MSFT +15.51%** to $451.10, adding roughly $60.56 per share in a single session
- **Azure crossed $100B in revenue** for the first time, a key proof point for AI monetization
- **Capex came in under guidance** ($41B vs $42B expected) — the opposite signal from Alphabet's capex raise
- **Still down 5% YTD** despite the surge, reflecting how deep the prior AI-capex-driven selloff had been
- **Contrast with Alphabet**: same week, opposite investor reaction — spending discipline vs. spending acceleration

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Expectations vs Reality Gap]]**: Both Microsoft's rally and Alphabet's prior-week selloff were driven less by the absolute capex numbers than by whether they beat or missed prevailing expectations — a textbook illustration that markets price surprise relative to the consensus baseline, not the number in isolation.

**Update (additional source, 2026-08-03, Microsoft's official Q4 FY2026 press release):** Full GAAP figures for the quarter ended June 30, 2026: revenue $90.0B (+18% Y/Y), operating income $40.6B (+18%), GAAP net income $35.8B (+31%), GAAP diluted EPS $4.81 (+32%); non-GAAP (ex-OpenAI investment impact) net income $35.3B (+22%) and EPS $4.74 (+23%). Results included a $3.2B gain from Microsoft's investment in [[Anthropic]]. Segment detail: Productivity and Business Processes revenue $37.8B (+14%, including LinkedIn +12% and Dynamics 365 +13%); Intelligent Cloud revenue $39.3B (+32%, with Azure and other cloud services revenue +43%); More Personal Computing revenue $12.9B (-4%, with Windows OEM/Devices -7% and Xbox content/services -10%). Full fiscal year 2026: revenue $331.8B (+18%), GAAP net income $133.7B (+31%), GAAP diluted EPS $17.95 (+32%). Microsoft returned $10.2B to shareholders via dividends and buybacks in the quarter, and **commercial remaining performance obligation** (forward revenue backlog) rose 84% Y/Y to $678B — a leading indicator of contracted future cloud/AI revenue that goes beyond the trailing-quarter numbers covered in the original market-reaction note.
