## Summary

BlackRock's Jeffrey Rosenberg says the 80% oil-Treasury yield correlation seen in 2026 broke in October, with oil rising while yields fell, because the market now prices AI risk above geopolitical risk. Weakness in the AI trade is read as weakness in U.S. growth and as disinflationary, which makes long Treasuries the only hedge, though he warns this is not a return to bonds hedging stocks. Hyperscaler capex is also pushing a flood of AI debt into public bond markets, which adds transparency and shorting tools but not unlimited capacity.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Jeffrey Rosenberg]] / [[BlackRock]]**: Senior portfolio manager of the Systematic Alternatives ETF (IALT), speaking on CNBC "ETF Edge".
- **[[Oil Prices]] vs. [[Treasury Yields]]**: An "outsize" 80% correlation held through 2026, then stopped in October.
- **[[AI Trade]] as macro driver**: Q3 GDP was a "blowout" and "all AI-related", via equipment investment and the AI wealth effect.
- **[[Kevin Warsh]] / [[Federal Reserve]]**: Warsh argued at the FOMC press conference that the capex story is broadening, not confined to narrow pockets.
- **[[Disinflation]] and bond hedging**: In a disinflationary shock, long-end Treasuries are the only hedge.
- **[[AI Hyperscalers]] debt issuance**: Microsoft returned to the public bond market after nearly a decade; roughly $4 trillion of AI debt is expected over four years.
- **[[Credit Default Swaps]]**: Microsoft CDS near 55 (a five-year high) versus Oracle near 300; public markets allow shorting.
- **[[Alphabet]] selloff**: Google's biggest single-day decline in its history, wiping out half a trillion dollars.

## Key Takeaways

- **Correlation Break**: Oil rose in October while Treasury yields fell, ending an 80% correlation.
- **AI Over Geopolitics**: Market sees geopolitical risk as secondary to AI risk.
- **AI-Driven GDP**: Q3 business investment rose 12.7%, led by 37% equipment growth.
- **Fed Disagreement**: Warsh says capex strength is broadening beyond narrow pockets.
- **Breadth Recovery**: October breadth recovered from a 2026 low as defensive sectors rallied.
- **Long Bond Hedge**: In a disinflationary AI wobble, only the long end hedges.
- **Not Normal Yet**: Persistent inflation and oil limit bonds as a stock hedge.
- **Debt Wave**: Hyperscaler capex outruns free cash flow; about $4 trillion of AI debt expected.
- **Capacity Limit**: The bond market can absorb it, but "the market is not unlimited in capacity".
- **Credit Transparency**: Public bonds and CDS let investors price and short AI risk.
- **Ratchet Pattern**: Booms "ratchet up, reset"; the AI boom has shown only the positive part.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. A cross-asset correlation holds only while one risk driver dominates both assets** — When a different risk becomes the market's primary concern, the correlation breaks.
→ explains: Correlation Break, AI Over Geopolitics

**T2. A hedge's efficacy depends on which shock dominates** — Bonds hedge stocks when the shock is growth-negative and disinflationary, but not when inflation is the binding constraint.
→ explains: Long Bond Hedge, Not Normal Yet

**T3. Borrowing needs that outgrow internal cash flow move into public markets, where finite capacity prices in higher spreads** — Larger, repeated issuance meets limited counterparty capacity, which pushes prices down and spreads up while making risk visible and tradeable.
→ explains: Debt Wave, Capacity Limit, Credit Transparency

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did Rosenberg conclude from the October breakdown in the oil-Treasury yield correlation?
**A**: That in the market's mind geopolitical risk is secondary to AI risk. Weakness in the AI trade is being read as weakness in the U.S. economy and as disinflationary, so yields fell even as oil rose.

**Q2**: What evidence did he cite that the AI trade is driving the economy, and how did Warsh respond?
**A**: Q3 GDP was a blowout, with business investment up 12.7% (information processing equipment up 37%) and AI-fuelled consumption despite falling real incomes. Warsh said the capex story is broadening, so strength is not confined to narrow pockets.

**Q3**: How should investors think about bonds and hyperscaler debt given this?
**A**: Long Treasuries can hedge a disinflationary AI wobble, but persistent inflation and fiscal challenges mean bonds are not a return to normal stock hedges. Public hyperscaler bonds and CDS give more transparency and the ability to short, while the expected $4 trillion of AI debt will strain market capacity.
