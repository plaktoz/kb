## Summary
Microsoft shares fell about 1.2% on Friday as a sharp semiconductor sell-off weighed on big tech, even though analysts stayed bullish after the Ignite conference. The stock sits roughly 11% below its Nov. 4 record close, and the broader market worries that tech is overspending on AI infrastructure. Underlying business results remain strong.

## Core Concepts
- [[Microsoft]] and its "full-stack" AI strategy spanning cloud infrastructure, AI models, and workplace software.
- [[Microsoft Ignite]] conference (Nov. 17-20), showcasing [[Copilot]], AI agents, in-house AI models, and security features.
- [[Azure]] growth driven by new AI capacity, cited by [[UBS]] analyst Karl Keirstead.
- [[Semiconductor Sell-off]] led by [[Nvidia]] (down nearly 6%) and [[AMD]] (down more than 9%).
- [[AI Capex]] concerns: Microsoft is among the biggest spenders on data centers.
- [[Satya Nadella]] apologizing for the prior week's Outlook, Teams, and Copilot outage.

## Key Takeaways
- **Price Action**: MSFT down 1.19% to $492.39 at 11:13 a.m. ET, versus $498.30 Thursday close.
- **Relative Resilience**: Microsoft held up better than chips; Nvidia fell nearly 6%, AMD over 9%.
- **Drawdown**: Stock is about 11% below its $555.80 record close set Nov. 4.
- **Analyst Targets**: UBS $650, Mizuho $640, BNP Paribas $632, JPMorgan $625, Guggenheim $595.
- **Fundamentals**: Fiscal Q1 revenue $92.3B (+19%); diluted EPS $4.92 (+32%).
- **Outage**: Last week's global outage blamed on a software bug and traffic surge, not a cyberattack.
- **Market Worry**: Investors fear [[AI Capex]] overspending; market value is about $3.7 trillion.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about Microsoft's stock on Friday?
**A**: Microsoft shares slipped as a semiconductor rout dragged big tech lower, despite bullish analyst reactions to Ignite and a $650 UBS target. The stock held up better than chip names.

**Q2**: What evidence shows Microsoft's underlying business remains strong?
**A**: Fiscal first-quarter revenue was $92.3 billion, up 19% year over year, and diluted earnings rose 32% to $4.92 a share. Analysts cited Azure revenue growth prospects from new AI capacity.

**Q3**: What broader risk does the article say keeps the market on edge?
**A**: Concern that tech companies are spending too much on AI infrastructure, with Microsoft among the biggest spenders on data centers. This overhang can pressure the stock even when results and analyst sentiment are positive.
