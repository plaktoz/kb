## Summary

This head-to-head analysis compares [[Arm Holdings]] and [[ASML Holding]] as AI investment candidates, examining business models, financials, risks, and valuations. [[Arm Holdings]] licenses energy-efficient CPU architecture used in over 350 billion chips and reported FY2026 revenue of $4.9B (+23% YoY), while [[ASML Holding]] holds a monopoly on EUV lithography machines and generated ~€32.7B in FY2025 revenue. The valuation gap is stark — Arm trades at 125x forward P/E versus ASML's 27.9x — making ASML the more income-friendly choice and Arm the higher-risk growth play.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Arm Holdings]] Business Model**: Licenses CPU [[IP Licensing|architecture]] used in smartphones (dominant market share) and expanding into physical AI and data center markets via the [[AGI CPU]] and Neoverse chips; revenue streams include licensing fees and per-chip royalties.
- **[[ASML Holding]] Monopoly**: Sole supplier of [[EUV Lithography]] machines indispensable for advanced chip manufacturing; its monopoly position underpins pricing power and margin durability.
- **Valuation Divergence**: Arm's 125x forward P/E and 57.3x P/S reflects aggressive growth expectations; ASML's 27.9x forward P/E and 16.5x P/S reflects a mature, profitable monopoly with more modest growth priced in.
- **[[RISC-V]] Risk**: Open-source [[RISC-V]] architecture poses a long-term competitive threat to Arm's licensing model if chip designers reduce dependence on Arm's IP.
- **Geopolitical Exposure**: [[ASML Holding]] faces export restrictions on sales to China, a major semiconductor manufacturing hub, creating cyclical revenue risk.
- **Investor Profile Fit**: ASML pays a dividend (income-oriented); Arm pays none (growth-oriented).

## Key Takeaways

- **Arm FY2026**: $4.9B revenue (+23% YoY), 18.4% net margin, 0.1x D/E, 6.0x current ratio.
- **ASML FY2025**: ~€32.7B revenue (up from €28.3B), 29.4% net margin, €11.1B free cash flow, 0.1x D/E.
- **Valuation gap**: Arm at 125x forward P/E vs. ASML at 27.9x; Arm at 57.3x P/S vs. ASML at 16.5x.
- **Arm risk**: ~69% of operating cash flow consumed by stock-based compensation.
- **Arm upside**: Expanding into physical AI and data center markets — higher-growth runway.
- **ASML risk**: China export restrictions and semiconductor industry cyclicality.
- **ASML advantage**: Dividend payer with monopoly moat; lower downside risk in a valuation reset.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Picks and Shovels Investing]]**: ASML's monopoly on EUV machines makes it the indispensable toolmaker — it profits regardless of which chip designer or foundry wins the AI race, while Arm must continue to win design sockets against emerging [[RISC-V]] alternatives.
- **[[Price vs. Value]]**: Arm's business fundamentals are strong, but paying 125x forward earnings embeds years of flawless execution; ASML's 27.9x provides more margin of safety for investors who want AI exposure without extreme multiple risk.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core investment thesis distinction between Arm Holdings and ASML for AI exposure?
**A**: Arm is a higher-growth, higher-valuation play (125x forward P/E) expanding from mobile into AI data centers, while ASML is a monopoly toolmaker at a more modest 27.9x that suits income investors and those seeking lower valuation risk.

**Q2**: What is Arm's most significant financial risk highlighted in the article?
**A**: Stock-based compensation consumed roughly 69% of Arm's operating cash flow, which dilutes shareholder value even as revenue and margins look healthy on the surface.

**Q3**: How should an investor use this comparison to decide between the two stocks?
**A**: Growth investors willing to pay for expansion into physical AI and data centers might favor Arm, while income-oriented or valuation-sensitive investors are better served by ASML's dividend, monopoly moat, and 27.9x forward P/E.
