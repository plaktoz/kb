## Summary

U.S. tech stocks steadied Friday after one of their worst routs in years, as investors weighed a possible AI investment slowdown from Democratic midterm wins against the sector's long-term growth. Asian markets tied to the AI hardware supply chain suffered steep losses, while a strong October jobs report raised odds of a December Fed rate hike. Some analysts called the selloff overblown froth removal rather than the end of the AI boom.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nasdaq 100]]**: Closed up 1.1% Friday after a 6.3% plunge Thursday, its worst day since the Covid-19 pandemic.
- **[[S&P 500]]**: Rose 0.3% after a 4.4% drop that erased $2.8 trillion on paper.
- **[[2026 Midterm Elections]]**: Democratic winners pledged to rein in AI and data centers, including Texas Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa.
- **[[AI Infrastructure Cycle]]**: Drives a growing share of U.S. market gains and economic growth.
- **[[Federal Reserve]]**: A strong jobs report raised odds of a December rate hike.
- **[[Asian Semiconductor Markets]]**: Kospi, Nikkei and TAIEX fell sharply; Samsung, SK Hynix and TSMC are exposed.
- **[[Ed Yardeni]]**: Calls the declines "just speed bumps" that keep the AI bubble from overheating.

## Key Takeaways

- **US Rout**: Nasdaq 100 fell 6.3% Thursday; S&P 500 fell 4.4%.
- **Asia Losses**: Kospi fell over 8% and triggered a halt; TAIEX dropped 9%.
- **Nikkei Bear Market**: Down more than 20% from its recent high, worst week since 2020.
- **Policy Risk**: A Democratic Congress could add scrutiny, delays and costs to data centers.
- **Rate Pressure**: Strong October jobs data pushed stocks off intraday highs.
- **China Contrast**: AI spending there is strategic, so it can continue beyond profitability.
- **Still Up YoY**: Kospi up 25%, Nikkei 225 up 13%, Taiwan tech up 47%.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Exposure to one cycle sets the size of the shock** — Assets are repriced in proportion to their dependence on a single driver of demand, so a threat to that driver hits the most dependent hardest. · *model:* [[Concentration Risk]]
→ explains: Asia Losses, US Rout, China Contrast

**T2. Derived demand sustains suppliers while end demand lasts** — Even when some places to extract value are constrained, suppliers of the inputs stay needed as long as demand for the end product persists. · *model:* [[Picks and Shovels Investing]]
→ explains: Still Up YoY, Asia Losses

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did tech stocks fall so sharply, and what was the debate afterward?
**A**: Investors feared an AI investment slowdown after Democratic midterm victories by candidates pledging to rein in AI and data centers. Some analysts called the selloff overblown froth removal, such as Ed Yardeni's "speed bumps", while others expected volatility to continue.

**Q2**: Why were Asian markets hit so hard?
**A**: Taiwan, South Korea and Japan are linked to the global AI spending cycle, and much of the hardware for America's AI ambitions is manufactured there. Kospi fell over 8% and TAIEX 9%, with South Korea especially sensitive because its memory makers serve the commercial AI buildout.

**Q3**: What does the article imply for investors in AI hardware suppliers?
**A**: J.P. Morgan's Tai Hui argues that as long as global demand for computing power grows, hardware demand should stay healthy over the medium term. Until it is clear how much data center construction is threatened, volatility is likely to continue.
