## Summary

[[Microsoft]] (MSFT) trades near $493.95 — about 92% of its 52-week high — after posting record trailing-twelve-month net margins of 40.3% and operating margins of 46.8%, well above its three-year averages. The central question is whether these peak margins can persist given a $41 billion single-quarter capex run-rate, a decline in gross margin, and a stock already priced at 11.1x price-to-sales (71% up its 10-year range). [[Microsoft Cloud]] gross margin at 65% is identified as the leading indicator to watch.

## Core Concepts

- [[Microsoft]] (MSFT) — trades ~$494, ~92% of 52-week high; stock returned 20.2% over 3 months but only 0.6% over 12 months vs 19.3% for the [[S&P 500]]
- [[Azure]] — revenue grew 43% in fiscal Q4 2026; demand still exceeds capacity; gross margin dipped to 67% in Q4 due to AI infrastructure mix shift
- [[AI Capital Expenditure]] — $41 billion in a single quarter; ~two-thirds directed to short-lived assets (CPUs/GPUs); free cash flow $19.6B vs $55.4B operating cash flow
- [[Microsoft Cloud Gross Margin]] — 65% in Q4, guided roughly stable; identified as the leading indicator for margin sustainability
- [[Depreciation Policy Change]] — Microsoft extended assumed useful lives of data centers from 15 to 25 years, softening reported capex growth without changing actual build pace
- [[Price-to-Sales Ratio]] — stock trades at 11.1x P/S, approximately 71% up its 10-year range; management guides FY2027 operating margins down less than one point

## Key Takeaways

- **Record margins**: TTM net margin 40.3% (vs 36.8% three-year avg); operating margin 46.8%.
- **Azure growth intact**: +43% in Q4 2026; demand still supply-constrained, not demand-limited.
- **Capex pressure**: $41B capex in Q4; FCF $19.6B against $55.4B operating cash flow.
- **Gross margin declining**: Q4 gross margin fell to 67% YoY; management attributes to Azure AI mix.
- **Accounting change**: data center useful life extended 15 → 25 years — reduces reported depreciation, not actual spend.
- **Valuation stretched**: 11.1x P/S, near the top of its 10-year historical range.
- **Stock lags market**: 0.6% return over 12 months vs 19.3% for the S&P 500; worst drawdown was 34.5%.
- **Key indicator**: Microsoft Cloud gross margin — if it keeps declining as capex rises, peak margins are likely behind us.
- **Options market calm**: implied volatility at 44th percentile of 52-week range — no elevated downside priced in yet.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Regression to the Mean]]**: The article's core concern is that 40.3% net margins represent a peak unlikely to persist — record profitability in a capital-intensive infrastructure buildout cycle historically reverts as depreciation, maintenance, and competition normalize costs.
- **[[Leading Indicator vs. Lagging Indicator]]**: Using Microsoft Cloud gross margin as the forward signal (rather than headline earnings) is a first-principles move — gross margin reflects real-time pricing power and cost dynamics, while operating margin can be flattered by accounting choices like the depreciation life extension.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core concern the article raises about Microsoft's current profitability?
**A**: Peak margins of 40.3% net and 46.8% operating rarely persist, especially during an expensive AI infrastructure buildout — the worry is not operational failure but quiet margin erosion as capex costs compound.

**Q2**: What accounting change did Microsoft make, and why does it matter for interpreting reported capex growth?
**A**: Microsoft extended the assumed useful life of data centers from 15 to 25 years, which reduces annual depreciation charges and softens reported capex growth figures without changing the actual pace of physical infrastructure spending.

**Q3**: What metric should investors monitor to determine whether Microsoft's margin peak is behind it?
**A**: The Microsoft Cloud gross margin — currently 65% in Q4 and guided roughly stable — is the leading indicator; if it continues declining while capex rises, the margin peak is likely already in the past.
