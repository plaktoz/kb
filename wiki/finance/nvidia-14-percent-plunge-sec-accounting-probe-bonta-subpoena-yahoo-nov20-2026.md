## Summary
Nvidia fell 14% on Friday, its worst day since the DeepSeek rout of January 2025, after Reuters reported the SEC probe is examining potential accounting issues. The stock has lost about $2.7 trillion since its Oct. 6 record, and government scrutiny from Washington and California is intensifying. The selloff spread to chip stocks, which had their worst week since 2001.

## Core Concepts
- [[Nvidia]] (NVDA): fell from the world's most valuable company to fourth in the US behind [[Apple]], [[Microsoft]] and [[Alphabet]].
- [[SEC Investigation]]: reportedly probing accounting and whether disclosures misled investors.
- [[Megaspeed]]: a Singapore-based customer US officials suspect of rerouting chips to China.
- [[Circular Financing]]: deals that analysts expect to face scrutiny.
- [[Rob Bonta]]: California Attorney General who subpoenaed Nvidia.
- [[Jensen Huang]]: exited as CEO; [[Scott Bessent]] demanded answers from the board within 10 days.

## Key Takeaways
- **Record Losses**: About $490B lost Friday and nearly $900B this week, worst week since October 2008.
- **Cumulative Rout**: Roughly $2.7T lost since the Oct. 6 record.
- **Red Flags**: Delayed 10-Q, board committee investigation, and ~$104B revenue via Singapore unit.
- **Slower Payments**: Customer bills outstanding at 64 days of sales, highest since 2022.
- **Political Pressure**: Treasury demands answers; California subpoenas over China chip flows and investor disclosures.
- **Contagion**: Chip stocks had their worst week since 2001; broader market fell.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What drove Nvidia's 14% drop on Friday?
**A**: Reuters reported the SEC's probe is examining potential accounting issues, adding to an earlier WSJ report that it includes whether disclosures misled investors.

**Q2**: What specific warning signs does the article cite about Nvidia's books?
**A**: A delayed 10-Q and an independent board committee investigation, about $104B of revenue through its Singapore unit, and customers taking 64 days of sales to pay, the most since 2022.

**Q3**: What might happen next?
**A**: Pressure from Treasury and California's attorney general is building, and Baird's Ted Mortonson expects every circular-finance deal Nvidia has done to be put under the microscope.
