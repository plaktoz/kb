## Summary
Wall Street clawed back most of its steep early losses on Wednesday, with the S&P 500 ending down 0.3% after being down 3.2% earlier, as the AI-stock pullback eased. Underlying worries persist: AI valuations, Nvidia governance questions, 3.8% PCE inflation, a likely December Fed hike, and new tariffs. The S&P 500 is down 5.2% for November.

## Core Concepts
- [[Nvidia]] and [[Jensen Huang]]: Nvidia closed down 3.9% after falling over 9%; relief over Huang's CEO return is fading.
- [[Anthropic]] IPO: closed up 1.1% but about 28% below its $115 debut peak.
- [[PCE Price Index]]: 3.8% year over year in October, versus 3.6% in September.
- [[Federal Reserve]]: likely to raise rates at the December meeting.
- [[Tariffs]]: new proposals on China, the EU and others after the Supreme Court struck down broader tariffs.
- [[Oil Prices]]: Brent at $94.61, tied to the US war with Iran, feeding inflation.
- [[Market Capitalization Weighting]]: big AI stocks dominate index direction.
- [[Treasury Yields]]: 10-year yield fell to 4.87% from 4.91%.

## Key Takeaways
- **Intraday Reversal**: S&P 500 fell 3.2% at worst, closed down 0.3%.
- **Breadth**: Roughly three quarters of S&P 500 stocks rose.
- **Nvidia**: Down 3.9% at close, after a 9%+ drop intraday.
- **November Slump**: S&P 500 down 5.2% this month; Santa Claus rally in doubt.
- **Inflation Drivers**: Tariffs, Brent crude ($72-$114 range since February) and AI spending.
- **K-Shaped Spending**: Growth driven by AI build-out and upper-income households; poorer households lean on credit.
- **Politics**: Price anger helped Democrats win House and Senate majorities.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Concentrated index weights let a few stocks override the many** — When a handful of companies hold very large market values, their moves dominate index direction even when most members move the other way.
→ explains: Breadth, Intraday Reversal

**T2. Tariff costs are passed on to consumers** — Businesses tend to pass some or all of tariff costs to buyers, so higher tariffs add to inflation and household stress. · *model:* [[Tax Incidence]]
→ explains: Inflation Drivers

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did the S&P 500 stay in the red even though most stocks rose?
**A**: Large AI-focused tech companies carry outsized index weight, and Nvidia's pullback made it one of the biggest drags on the market.

**Q2**: What data points show persistent inflation pressure?
**A**: PCE inflation hit 3.8% in October (3.6% in September), with Brent crude at $94.61 and new tariffs adding costs.

**Q3**: What are the implications for rates and the economy?
**A**: The Fed is likely to hike in December to tame inflation, which could stifle growth even though Q3 GDP was revised up to 4.3%, driven largely by AI infrastructure and upper-income spending.
