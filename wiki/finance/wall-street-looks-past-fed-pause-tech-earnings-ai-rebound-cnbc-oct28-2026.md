# Wall Street Looks Past Fed Pause to Tech Earnings and AI Rebound

## Summary

Markets barely reacted to the Fed holding rates steady on Wednesday, since the decision was widely telegraphed and Chairman Kevin Warsh again avoided forward guidance. Attention shifted to third-quarter earnings, with Microsoft, Alphabet and a more muted Oracle capex outlook driving the AI trade, while higher yields, oil, the Iran war and the midterms remain risks.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Federal Reserve]]** and **[[Kevin Warsh]]**: held rates; Warsh reiterated he does not believe in [[Forward Guidance]] "absent a crisis".
- **[[Q3 Earnings Season]]**: 163 S&P 500 companies report in the busiest week, covering 36% of companies and 44% of market cap per [[Art Hogan]].
- **[[Neocloud]] and semiconductor rebound**: Roundhill Neocloud ETF up more than 16% on the week, iShares Semiconductor ETF up more than 7%, after a volatility bout from a spike in neocloud debt spreads.
- **[[Ed Yardeni]] earnings data**: Revenues beating consensus by 1.7% (vs 3.8% in Q2); earnings beating by 6.5%; forward profit margin at a record 16.8%.
- **[[Jeffrey Gundlach]]** and **[[Equal-Weight S&P 500]]**: favors a 30% equity allocation focused on equal weight; Invesco equal-weight ETF up more than 13% this year.
- **[[Macro Risks]]**: higher yields and oil, fed funds futures pricing a quarter-point hike on Dec. 9, the [[Iran War]], and the [[US Midterm Elections]].
- **[[UBS Chief Investment Office]]**: sees measured tightening as unlikely to derail the economy; suggests adding selectively to quality bonds.

## Key Takeaways

- **Muted Fed reaction**: Decision was widely telegraphed; volatility did not spike during Warsh's press conference.
- **Earnings focus**: 163 S&P 500 companies report this week, including Amazon on Thursday.
- **Slowing beats**: Revenue surprise fell to 1.7% from 3.8% in Q2.
- **Margins at record**: Forward S&P 500 margin 16.8%; S&P 493 up 2.6 points to 14.6%.
- **Rate path higher**: Futures price a quarter-point hike on Dec. 9, to 4.00%-4.25%.
- **Concentration worry**: Gundlach wants distance from the cap-weighted S&P's AI exposure.
- **Geopolitical overhang**: Renewed Iran hostilities after the midterms could send oil soaring.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Anticipated events are already priced in** — When a decision is widely telegraphed, markets have discounted it, so the announcement itself moves prices little and attention shifts to new information such as earnings. · *model:* [[Expectations Investing]]
→ explains: Muted Fed reaction, Earnings focus

**T2. Spreading holdings reduces exposure to a single theme's failure** — Investors worried about AI risk argue for equal-weight over cap-weighted exposure, which this year has paid off. · *model:* [[Diversification]]
→ explains: Concentration worry

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did markets barely react to the Fed's decision?
**A**: The hold was widely telegraphed, and Warsh stuck to his stance against forward guidance, so volatility did not spike; investors moved on to tech earnings and the AI trade.

**Q2**: What do Yardeni's numbers show about the earnings season so far?
**A**: Earnings are beating estimates by 6.5% and revenues by only 1.7% (down from 3.8% in Q2), but the S&P 500's forward profit margin is at a record 16.8%.

**Q3**: What portfolio implication does Gundlach draw from AI-related concerns?
**A**: He favors a 30% equity allocation focused on the equal-weight S&P 500 rather than the cap-weighted index, to avoid exposure to the "epicenter of the blast radius" of AI problems.
