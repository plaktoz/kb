## Summary

[[IBM]] shares fell 2.1% on July 30 to $221.77 despite positive quantum-computing news — multi-partner quantum-advantage results with [[Algorithmiq]], [[Qedma]], RIKEN, and the University of Chicago — as investors instead focused on Q2 revenue that missed consensus ($17.16B vs $17.46B expected) and new securities-law investigations tied to its earlier guidance warning. [[Circle]] separately acquired IBM's blockchain patent portfolio (~1,000 patents) for undisclosed terms.

**Update (additional source, 2026-07-31, ad-hoc-news.de):** the July 14 single-day collapse that started this stretch was a 25% rout — described as IBM's worst trading session on record — driven by weak preliminary results and mainframe (IBM Z) revenue losing ground to AI infrastructure spend. Nine days later IBM announced it would acquire [[HRL Laboratories]], a quantum-dot technology pioneer previously owned by Boeing and General Motors, expected to close by end of Q3 2026. The board also confirmed the regular $1.69/share quarterly dividend (paid September 10, record date August 10) — part of a dividend streak paid every year since **1916**, one of the longest in the US equity market. In euro terms the stock closed around €192.24, about 34% below its 52-week high; RSI sits at 39.4 (approaching but not yet oversold) with ~86% annualized 30-day volatility, and average analyst targets imply roughly 10% upside.
