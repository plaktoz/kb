## Summary
About 36% of [[S&P 500]] companies (179) report Q3 results this week, including [[Microsoft]], [[Alphabet]] and [[Amazon]], which together are worth nearly $11 trillion and may set market direction. Analysts expect 29.5% earnings growth, but with sentiment cautious and expectations high, even beats are being punished. Strategists note that excluding tech and energy, the market is merely average.

## Core Concepts
- [[Earnings Season]]: 36% of S&P 500 firms report this week; about a quarter have reported so far, 83% beating EPS estimates.
- [[FactSet]]: Source of the 29.5% expected Q3 growth and reporting-share data.
- [[Big Tech Earnings]]: Alphabet and Microsoft report Wednesday, Amazon Thursday; past one-time AI-investment gains inflated profit growth.
- [[Market Expectations]]: [[Jonathan Golub]] says narrow beats are "almost like a miss"; Ken Mahoney says a lot was already priced in.
- [[AI Trade]] sustainability, high gas prices (a war with Iran), bond-market tremors and [[CoreWeave]] debt weigh on sentiment.
- [[Median Earnings Growth]]: About 10% for the median S&P 500 company, versus 29.5% for the weighted index.

## Key Takeaways
- **Heavy Week**: 179 S&P 500 companies, 36%, report quarterly results this week.
- **Expected Growth**: Analysts forecast 29.5% Q3 S&P 500 earnings growth.
- **Beats Not Enough**: 83% beat EPS, yet narrow beats are shunned.
- **Mega-Cap Weight**: Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon, worth nearly $11 trillion, likely steer the market.
- **Index Performance**: S&P 500 up 11.4% this year, down 1.6% over the past month.
- **Distorted Average**: Median company earnings growth is about 10%, "very normal".
- **Ex-Tech-and-Energy**: Golub calls the rest of the market "pretty average".

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Price reactions depend on results relative to expectations, not on beats alone** — When expectations are high and much is already priced in, results that only narrowly beat estimates can be punished like a miss. · *model:* [[Expectations Investing]]
→ explains: Beats Not Enough, Mega-Cap Weight

**T2. A weighted aggregate can misstate the typical case** — Index-level growth skewed by a few large winners overstates how well the median company is doing. · *model:* [[Flaw of Averages]]
→ explains: Distorted Average, Ex-Tech-and-Energy

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why is this week pivotal and why is sentiment fragile?
**A**: 36% of S&P 500 companies, including Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon, report, while worries about the AI trade, gas prices and bond yields leave little room for error. Investors have left less margin even for companies that beat estimates.

**Q2**: What data show the gap between headline and typical earnings growth?
**A**: Analysts expect 29.5% S&P 500 earnings growth, but the median company is expected to grow about 10%. Golub says factoring out tech and energy, the market is "pretty average".

**Q3**: How should investors read an earnings beat in this environment?
**A**: Narrow beats may be treated as misses because expectations were "through the roof" and a lot was priced in, so the size of the beat versus elevated expectations matters.
