## Summary

Netflix repurchased a record $4.7 billion of its own shares in Q2 2026, closing the quarter with $27.1 billion remaining in buyback authorizations — enough to retire roughly 8% of the company at current prices. A collapsed [[Warner Bros]] acquisition deal provided a $2.8 billion termination fee, prompting the board to add $25 billion to the repurchase program and raising management's 2026 free cash flow forecast to ~$12.5 billion. Despite the aggressive buyback pace, the author cautions that repurchases amplify per-share metrics without changing underlying business trajectory.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Stock Buyback]] / Share Repurchase Program**: Netflix holds $27.1 billion in remaining repurchase authorization, representing ~8% of the company at ~$76/share.
- **[[Free Cash Flow]]**: Q2 2026 FCF was only $1.5 billion, while buybacks totaled $4.7 billion — repurchases ran ~3x operating cash production.
- **[[EPS Accretion]]**: Diluted share count fell ~2% YoY (4.26B vs. 4.35B), helping EPS grow 11% even as net income rose only 9%.
- **[[Warner Bros]] Termination Fee**: Netflix walked away from an acquisition attempt and received a $2.8 billion breakup fee, which management deployed toward buyback capacity.
- **[[Revenue Growth Deceleration]]**: Netflix top-line growth slowed from 16% to 13%, a signal the author weighs against buyback momentum.

## Key Takeaways

- **Record Buyback**: Netflix repurchased $4.7 billion in shares in Q2 2026 — a single-quarter record.
- **Remaining Authorization**: $27.1 billion left; ~8% of the company's market cap.
- **Unsustainable Pace**: Q2 buybacks were ~3x Q2 free cash flow of $1.5 billion.
- **Termination Windfall**: $2.8 billion from the Warner Bros. deal collapse funded the buyback surge.
- **EPS vs. Net Income**: 11% EPS growth vs. 9% net income growth — buybacks closing the gap.
- **Revenue Deceleration**: Growth slowed from 16% to 13%, limiting the bull case to buybacks alone.
- **Author's Stance**: Not buying solely on buyback momentum; underlying growth must justify valuation.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]**: When EPS becomes the headline metric, buybacks become a lever to hit that number independent of genuine earnings growth — Netflix's 11% EPS vs. 9% net income illustrates exactly this dynamic.
- **[[Margin of Safety]]**: Repurchasing shares at pace far exceeding free cash flow borrows against future capital flexibility; if business conditions tighten, the buyback program is the first lever to cut, reversing the EPS tailwind.
