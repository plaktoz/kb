## Summary

[[Microsoft]] delivered fiscal 2026 revenue of $331.8 billion (+18%) with [[Azure]] hitting $100 billion annually and posting 43% Q4 growth, yet the stock trades at a forward P/E of 26.59 — below its own five-year average of 30.52 and a premium to the sector's 23.81. The gap to its historical multiple reflects market caution about sustained heavy AI capex, not slowing growth; earnings are expected to grow ~19.77% in 2027 and the commercial backlog grew 84% to $678 billion. The discount looks real but is contingent on Azure growth holding and Copilot adoption continuing beyond 30 million paid seats.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Azure]]**: Surpassed $100 billion in annual revenue; Q4 grew 43% YoY (constant currency). Microsoft Cloud revenue reached $59.3 billion (+27%).
- **[[Multiple Compression]]**: MSFT forward P/E of 26.59 vs. its own five-year average of 30.52 — a discount to its own history despite ~20% earnings growth.
- **[[Microsoft Copilot]]**: Reached 30 million paid seats in fiscal 2026; fuelled by Azure compute and embedded in the Microsoft 365 installed base of 37.8 billion quarterly revenue.
- **[[Commercial Remaining Performance Obligation]]**: $678 billion backlog (+84% YoY), weighted average duration 2.3 years — signals durable contracted demand.
- **[[AI Capex Risk]]**: One-time items ($3.2 billion Anthropic gain, offset by severance/Xbox charges) added $0.27 EPS vs. guidance; management confirmed beat persisted after stripping them out.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Discount to Own History May Signal Mean Reversion Opportunity** — When a high-quality compounder's multiple compresses below its own historical average while fundamentals remain intact, the discount often reflects temporary sentiment rather than structural impairment. · *model:* [[Mean Reversion]]
→ explains: MSFT trading at 26.59x vs. 30.52x five-year average despite ~20% earnings growth; "priced for caution, not slowdown"

**T2. Margin of Safety Requires Distinguishing Cyclical from Structural Risk** — Heavy capex cycles create a temporary overhang on FCF and multiples; investors must judge whether the spending is eroding the business or building its moat before concluding a discount is a bargain. · *model:* [[Margin of Safety]]
→ explains: Azure backlog growth (+84%) and Copilot adoption suggest moat-building, but sustained capex without returns would destroy value

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1. What is the core valuation paradox described in this article?**
A1. Microsoft is growing revenue and earnings ~18–22% YoY with Azure at 43% growth, yet its forward P/E (26.59x) sits below its own five-year average (30.52x), suggesting the market is pricing in spending risk rather than rewarding the growth.

**Q2. What specific data points support the claim that demand is durable, not just a one-quarter beat?**
A2. The commercial remaining performance obligation hit $678 billion (+84% YoY) with a 2.3-year weighted average duration, and Microsoft 365 Copilot reached 30 million paid seats — both forward-looking indicators of locked-in revenue.

**Q3. How should a long-term investor use this valuation gap in practice?**
A3. The setup favours investors who can tolerate the heavy-spending cycle; the key triggers to monitor are Azure growth rate (watch for deceleration from 43%) and Copilot paid seat trajectory — a stall in either would justify the discounted multiple remaining or widening.
