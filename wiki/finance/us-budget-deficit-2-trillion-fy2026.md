## Summary

The U.S. recorded a $1.992 trillion budget deficit for fiscal year 2026 — up 12% from the prior year and the third-largest annual deficit in U.S. history, behind only 2020 and 2021. The 25th consecutive year of deficit spending was driven by record Social Security ($1.7T), Medicare ($1.1T), and net interest ($1T+) outlays, while corporate tax cuts from the One Big Beautiful Bill reduced receipts.

## Core Concepts

- **[[U.S. Budget Deficit]]** — $1.992T for FY2026; 12% increase over FY2025's $1.775T
- **[[National Debt]]** — Exceeded $40 trillion earlier in 2026; more than doubled over the past decade
- **[[Social Security]]** — $1.7T spending for FY2026 (largest single category); up from ~$1.6T in FY2025
- **[[Medicare]]** — $1.1T for FY2026 (second largest); up from ~$1T in FY2025
- **Net interest** — Topped $1T for FY2026; reflects high borrowing costs on $40T+ debt load
- **[[One Big Beautiful Bill]]** — Tax legislation cutting corporate income taxes; contributed to ~$70B decline in corporate tax receipts
- **Bond market reaction** — 10-year and 30-year Treasury yields hit 24-year highs the prior week
- **Tariff revenue** — Record gross customs duties in FY2026, but offset by Supreme Court-ordered refunds following ruling against Trump tariffs

## Key Takeaways

- **$2T threshold nearly breached**: Third-highest annual deficit in U.S. history.
- **25 straight deficit years**: Last surplus was 2001.
- **Interest now equals Medicare**: Net interest expense exceeded $1T — comparable to the entire Medicare budget.
- **Corporate tax cuts reduced receipts**: ~$70B corporate tax revenue decline from One Big Beautiful Bill.
- **Treasury growth strategy**: Secretary Bessent says the U.S. can "grow our way out" of the debt problem.
- **September reversal**: September posted a $27B deficit vs. a $198B surplus in the same month last year.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What made FY2026 the third-highest deficit year in U.S. history?
**A**: Record Social Security ($1.7T), Medicare ($1.1T), and net interest ($1T+) outlays combined with reduced corporate tax receipts (from the One Big Beautiful Bill) pushed the deficit to $1.992T, surpassed only by 2020 and 2021 pandemic-era spending.

**Q2**: Why is net interest now a critical fiscal concern?
**A**: Net interest topped $1T for FY2026 — roughly equivalent to the entire Medicare budget — meaning interest on accumulated debt has become a major spending category that grows automatically with the debt level and interest rates.

**Q3**: What is the Treasury's stated strategy for managing the debt, and what is its limitation?
**A**: Secretary Bessent argues the U.S. can grow its way out through global economic expansion, but this strategy requires sustained GDP growth to outpace the compounding debt-service burden — a condition not guaranteed given elevated interest rates.
