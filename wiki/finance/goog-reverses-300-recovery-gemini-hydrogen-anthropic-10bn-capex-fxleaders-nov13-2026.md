## Summary

Alphabet shares gave up their recovery above $300 and headed toward $290 despite new Gemini products and a roughly $10 billion Anthropic investment, as investors question whether surging infrastructure spending can earn adequate returns. Q3 revenue rose 25% and Google Cloud grew 103%, but EPS of $2.12 missed expectations of $3.02. Capital expenditure of $51.4 billion in the quarter is now the central shareholder concern.

## Core Concepts

- [[Alphabet]] (GOOG, GOOGL): stock falling back below the $300 level, which may now act as resistance.
- [[Gemini 4 Hydrogen]]: new everyday model, about 2.5x faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash, priced at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens.
- [[Gemini Smart Glasses]]: preorders from $395, built with Samsung, Warby Parker and Gentle Monster, shipping December 10.
- [[Anthropic]]: Google and Nvidia each agree to buy about $10 billion of shares in a planned $26 billion private placement.
- [[Google Cloud]]: $30.7 billion revenue, up 103%, with operating income of $11.1 billion.
- [[Capital Expenditure]]: $51.4 billion in Q3 versus $24 billion a year earlier.
- [[Technical Analysis]]: $300 as resistance, 50-week moving average near $320, 100 SMA as support.
- [[Data Center Opposition]]: community concerns over electricity, water and power grids.

## Key Takeaways

- **Price Action**: Alphabet fell below $300 toward $290, turning that level into possible resistance.
- **Q3 Results**: Revenue rose 25% to $127.6 billion, but EPS was $2.12 versus $3.02 expected.
- **Cloud Surge**: Google Cloud revenue rose 103% to $30.7 billion.
- **Anthropic Stake**: Google's roughly $10 billion allocation is non-voting, with ownership capped at 14.99%.
- **Capex Burden**: Q3 capex hit $51.4 billion; year-to-date spending is about $132 billion.
- **Guidance Gap**: Company guidance is $175-185 billion for 2026; external estimates near $200 billion are unconfirmed.
- **Political Risk**: Opposition to data centers could delay projects or raise infrastructure obligations.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did Alphabet's stock reverse below $300 despite product launches and strong cloud growth?
**A**: Investors question whether the company's expanding computing ambitions and rising infrastructure costs can generate sufficient returns. Q3 earnings also missed expectations, with EPS of $2.12 against $3.02 expected.

**Q2**: What data points show the scale of Alphabet's spending?
**A**: Q3 capex was about $51.4 billion versus $24 billion a year earlier, and year-to-date spending is about $132 billion. Company guidance for 2026 is $175-185 billion, while unconfirmed external estimates approach $200 billion.

**Q3**: What does the market now demand from Alphabet?
**A**: Evidence that revenue and operating cash flow can justify the infrastructure commitments. Until then, free cash flow and capital allocation remain central to the stock's valuation.
