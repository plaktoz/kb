## Summary

The [[Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis]] tracks quarterly credit card delinquency data, revealing a divergence between large and small US banks in Q2 2026. Overall delinquency rates are improving but remain historically elevated, while community banks are bucking the trend with rising delinquencies driven by their higher-risk customer base. Despite the stress signals, bank stock indices are up roughly 14–16% year-to-date.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Credit Card Delinquency Rate]]**: The share of credit card balances 30+ days past due, tracked quarterly by the [[Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis]].
- **[[Large Banks vs Small Banks]]**: Top-100 banks benefit from diversified income (investment banking, institutional trading) allowing tighter lending standards; community banks lack this buffer and absorb more credit risk.
- **[[Community Banks]]**: Smaller regional and local banks that serve customers with lower credit scores, making them more vulnerable to consumer financial stress.
- **[[KBW Nasdaq Bank Index]]**: A benchmark tracking US bank stocks, up ~14% YTD despite rising delinquency concerns.
- **[[Loan Charge-Offs]]**: When delinquent loans are written off as losses, directly reducing bank earnings.
- **[[Provision for Credit Losses]]**: Reserves banks set aside to cover expected loan defaults; rising provisions reduce reported earnings.

## Key Takeaways

- **Overall rate**: Q2 2026 delinquency rate at 2.85%, down from 2.91% in Q1 2026 and 3.04% in Q2 2025.
- **Historical context**: Current 2.85% remains above the sub-2.85% range seen across 2012–2023.
- **Large banks improving**: Top-100 banks fell to 2.58%, down from a 3.10% peak in Q3 2024.
- **Small banks worsening**: Community banks rose to 6.49% in Q2, up from 6.44% in Q1.
- **Earnings impact**: Higher delinquencies cut interest income, trigger charge-offs, and force larger loss reserves.
- **Stock resilience**: [[KBW Nasdaq Bank Index]] up ~14% YTD; Regional Banking Index up ~16% YTD.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Risk Stratification]]**: The large/small bank delinquency gap illustrates how portfolio composition determines risk exposure — community banks' lower-credit-score customer base means consumer stress translates directly into bank balance sheet stress, while large banks' diversification insulates them.
- **[[Divergence as Signal]]**: When aggregate metrics improve while a sub-segment deteriorates, the aggregate can mask stress concentrated in a vulnerable cohort — the overall 2.85% rate understates the 6.49% reality faced by community banks and their borrowers.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the overall US credit card delinquency rate in Q2 2026, and is the trend improving or worsening?
**A**: The overall rate is 2.85% in Q2 2026, down from 2.91% in Q1 and 3.04% in Q2 2025 — improving, but still elevated relative to the 2012–2023 historical range.

**Q2**: Why do small community banks have a much higher delinquency rate (6.49%) than large banks (2.58%)?
**A**: Community banks lack diversified revenue streams and serve customers with lower credit scores, forcing them to take on higher credit risk; large banks can tighten lending standards because investment banking and trading revenues reduce their dependence on consumer credit.

**Q3**: How do rising delinquencies hurt bank earnings, and what does the stock market's reaction suggest?
**A**: Higher delinquencies reduce interest income, force loan charge-offs, and require larger provisions for credit losses — all of which compress earnings. However, the KBW Bank Index is up ~14% YTD, suggesting investors are currently discounting these risks or expect further improvement.
