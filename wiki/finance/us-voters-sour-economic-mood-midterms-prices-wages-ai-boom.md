## Summary

On the eve of the midterms, voters are sour on the economy, blaming tariffs and the Iran war for inflation, even though unemployment is low, growth is solid, and AI-driven stock gains have padded portfolios. Consumer prices and hourly earnings have both risen about 5.5% since January 2025, leaving workers no better off. Economists warn that a sharp reset in AI expectations could trigger a stagflationary environment.

## Core Concepts

- [[Consumer Sentiment]]: the University of Michigan index hit a near-record low and is "more personal."
- [[Consumer Confidence]]: the Conference Board index perked up in October and tracks the macroeconomy.
- [[Inflation]]: elevated, driven by the Iran war, tariffs, and AI demand.
- [[Real Wages]]: wages and prices both up ~5.5% since January 2025.
- [[AI Investment Boom]]: business investment rose 12.7% annualized; sustainability worries are growing.
- [[Stagflation]]: Torsten Slok's risk scenario of slowing growth with persistent price pressure.
- [[Federal Reserve]] and [[Kevin Warsh]]: rate hike in September, another expected in December.

## Key Takeaways

- **Voter Mood**: Prices and tariffs/Iran-war blame drive a sour economic outlook.
- **Labor Strength**: Unemployment 4.2%; claims near a 57-year low.
- **Growth**: Economy grew 3.4% annualized last quarter, the strongest this year.
- **Sentiment vs Confidence**: Sentiment drives elections; confidence drives markets.
- **Stalled Purchasing Power**: Prices and earnings both rose ~5.5% since January 2025.
- **Housing Squeeze**: 30-year mortgage rates above 7% and tight supply block buyers.
- **AI Fragility**: Weak earnings from Amazon and Intel raised doubts about AI spending.
- **Stagflation Risk**: An AI reset plus oil, tariffs, and immigration limits could hurt.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Real purchasing power depends on wages relative to prices** — Nominal gains in pay mean nothing when prices rise equally; living standards change only when wage growth outpaces inflation.
→ explains: Stalled Purchasing Power, Voter Mood

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why are voters sour on the economy despite solid headline numbers?
**A**: Persistent inflation, which many blame on tariffs and the Iran war, hits households through gas, utilities, food, and housing, even as unemployment is low and growth is strong.

**Q2**: What data show workers are no better off than when Trump took office?
**A**: BLS data show consumer prices and average hourly earnings have both risen about 5.5% since January 2025, so purchasing power is unchanged.

**Q3**: What is the main risk to the economy highlighted by Apollo's Torsten Slok?
**A**: A sharp reset in AI expectations could cut corporate capex and weigh on GDP just as oil, tariffs, and immigration limits push inflation up, risking stagflation.
