## Summary

Tickeron reports that [[Nvidia]] shares fell about 26% in the 30 days to mid-November, from near $221 to $162.85, leaving the stock roughly 30% below its October 6 record and at a 52-week low. The article attributes the slide to CEO [[Jensen Huang]]'s resignation, renewed doubts about AI spending, and hotter-than-expected inflation. It frames the quarter's 28% decline as a broader cooling of the AI trade, with Nvidia's November 17 earnings the next catalyst.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Nvidia]]**: GPU and accelerated-computing maker; data center is about 92% of revenue.
- **[[Jensen Huang]]**: Co-founder and CEO who resigned November 12 after a board review; CFO [[Colette Kress]] is interim CEO.
- **[[AI Capital Expenditure]]**: Investors question whether AI spending will turn into sustained profitability.
- **[[Analyst Downgrades]]**: Bernstein cut to market perform, Baird to neutral, Morgan Stanley dropped it from top picks.
- **[[Federal Reserve Rate Hike]]**: Hot October CPI raised the odds of a December hike, pressuring growth stocks.
- **[[Valuation Multiple Compression]]**: Multiples compressed sharply despite strong fundamentals.
- **[[CUDA]] ecosystem**: The software moat that makes Nvidia a bellwether for the AI trade.

## Key Takeaways

- **30-Day Drop**: Roughly 26%, from about $221 to $162.85 by November 13.
- **Record High Gap**: Stock sits about 30% below the $243.37 October 6 high.
- **Leadership Shock**: Huang resigned November 12; shares fell 4.3% after a 14.3% intraday drop.
- **Political Trigger**: November 5 fell about 9% after Democrats won both chambers of Congress.
- **Analyst Reaction**: Bernstein and Baird downgraded; Morgan Stanley removed it from top picks.
- **Strong Fundamentals**: Revenue grew more than 100% year over year last quarter.
- **Next Catalyst**: Earnings November 17; watch data center growth, margins, guidance, CEO search.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core account of Nvidia's 26% drop?
**A**: Several overlapping catalysts hit at once: CEO Huang's resignation, doubts about AI spending, hotter inflation and analyst downgrades. The quarterly decline reflects a broader cooling of the AI trade rather than a single event.

**Q2**: What specific events marked the sharpest moves in November?
**A**: Shares fell more than 9% on November 5 after the Democratic congressional wins and weak Qualcomm and Arm outlooks. They fell 4.3% on November 12, after dropping as much as 14.3% intraday, when Huang resigned.

**Q3**: What should investors watch after the drop?
**A**: The November 17 earnings: data center revenue growth, gross margins, guidance and new-leadership commentary. Also inflation and Fed signals, hyperscaler capex, export controls, the permanent CEO search and competition from AMD and Broadcom.
