## Summary

Gabriel Perez, a former White House teleprompter operator, settled CFTC charges for $172,000 after using advance knowledge of presidential speech content to trade event contracts on prediction platforms. Between December 2025 and February 2026, he earned over $107,500 by betting on "presidential mention market" contracts that paid out based on specific words Trump used. The case is one of several recent enforcement actions signaling growing regulatory scrutiny of insider trading on prediction markets.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Prediction Markets]]** — platforms where users trade event contracts whose value depends on real-world outcomes; examples include [[Kalshi]] and [[Polymarket]]
- **[[Insider Trading]]** on event contracts — using material nonpublic information (e.g., advance speech content) to gain an edge on prediction platforms
- **[[CFTC]] Enforcement** — the Commodity Futures Trading Commission asserted jurisdiction over prediction market contracts under the [[Commodity Exchange Act]]
- **[[Kalshi]]** — regulated U.S. prediction market exchange that assisted the CFTC investigation and has since implemented new safeguards
- **[[Gabriel Perez]]** — former White House teleprompter operator who had access to presidential speech text before delivery

## Key Takeaways

- **Settlement terms**: $107,500 disgorgement + $65,000 penalty + 3-year trading ban.
- **Reduced penalty**: CFTC credited Perez's cooperation and Kalshi's help under a new cooperation policy.
- **Market type**: "Presidential mention markets" — contracts paying based on specific words in speeches.
- **Pattern emerging**: Similar probes include a U.S. soldier linked to Polymarket and a MrBeast editor tied to Kalshi.
- **Platform response**: Kalshi introduced new safeguards and cleared a backlog of suspicious-activity reviews.
- **Regulatory signal**: CFTC is actively extending insider-trading doctrine to event contract markets.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Information Asymmetry]]**: Prediction markets assume participants have roughly equal information access; Perez exploited structural access to nonpublic data, showing that asymmetry invalidates market fairness and invites regulatory intervention.
- **[[Incentive Alignment]]**: The higher the payout for correctly predicting outcomes, the stronger the incentive to seek an insider edge — prediction markets inadvertently reward information arbitrage until enforcement catches up.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What was the core violation Gabriel Perez committed?
**A**: He traded "presidential mention market" event contracts on Kalshi using nonpublic advance knowledge of speech content while working as a White House teleprompter operator.

**Q2**: What were the specific settlement terms imposed by the CFTC?
**A**: Perez was required to disgorge $107,500 in profits, pay a $65,000 civil penalty, and accept a three-year ban from trading; the penalty was reduced due to his cooperation and Kalshi's assistance.

**Q3**: What does this case imply for prediction market platforms?
**A**: It signals that prediction markets face the same insider-trading scrutiny as traditional financial markets, pushing platforms like Kalshi to implement safeguards and cooperate with regulators proactively.
