# Billionaire Bill Ackman Just Sold Amazon and Bought Microsoft Stock. Which Cloud Computing Behemoth Is the Better Buy Today?

## Summary

[[Bill Ackman]]'s [[Pershing Square]] cut its [[Amazon]] stake by over 25% in Q2 while boosting [[Microsoft]] holdings by nearly 10%, though the stale 13F filing may no longer reflect current positioning since Microsoft has since risen far more than Amazon. Comparing Q2 results, Amazon's [[AWS]] posted stronger growth than Microsoft overall, and Amazon accelerated data center capital spending while Microsoft held its guidance flat. The author concludes Amazon is the better buy today despite its higher trailing P/E, citing superior growth and heavier infrastructure investment.

## Core Concepts

- [[Bill Ackman]] and [[Pershing Square]] adjusted their portfolio mix between two [[Cloud Computing]] giants.
- [[13F Filings]] disclose institutional holdings but lag actual trades by 45 days.
- [[Amazon]] and [[AWS]] compared against [[Microsoft]] on revenue growth, operating income, and valuation.
- [[Capital Expenditure]] guidance as a signal of confidence in future cloud demand.

## Key Takeaways

- **Portfolio Shift**: Pershing Square cut Amazon over 25%, raised Microsoft nearly 10% in Q2.
- **Stale Data**: 13F filings lag 45 days; positioning may have already changed.
- **Price Divergence**: Since Q2 end, Microsoft rose ~30% vs. Amazon's ~10%.
- **Amazon Q2**: Revenue up 20%, operating income up 43%, AWS income up 64% YoY.
- **Microsoft Q2**: Revenue and earnings each grew 18%.
- **AWS Weight**: AWS drives roughly 60% of Amazon's total operating income.
- **Capex Signal**: Amazon raised 2026 data center capex guidance to $220 billion; Microsoft held flat.
- **Author's Call**: Amazon favored as the stronger buy on growth and infrastructure investment.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Map Is Not the Territory]]**: A 13F filing is a 45-day-old snapshot of a portfolio, not its current state — by the time it's public, Microsoft had already rallied ~30% and Amazon only ~10%, so trading on the "map" alone would mislead an investor about today's "territory."

**A**: Despite Ackman's 13F showing a shift from Amazon toward Microsoft, the author argues Amazon is actually the better buy today because of AWS's stronger growth and heavier capex investment.

**A**: Amazon's revenue grew 20% and operating income 43% (with AWS operating income up 64% YoY), versus Microsoft's 18% growth in both revenue and earnings.
