## Summary

The [[S&P 500 Momentum Index]] slipped into correction territory in July 2026, down 10.6% from its June peak after a record 44% gain in Q2, driven by a selloff in AI-infrastructure and semiconductor stocks. Historical data from Dow Jones shows that following prior SPMO corrections, average 3-month returns were 12.4% and 1-year returns exceeded 24% with ~90% win rates. Analysts note that momentum drawdowns tend to be short-lived, as the composition of momentum indexes shifts toward new winners.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Momentum Factor]]**: Strategy of buying recent outperformers; exposed to sharp, fast reversals during rotation events
- **[[S&P 500 Momentum Index]]** / [[SPMO ETF]]: Invesco's momentum ETF that rebalances semi-annually; fell 10.6%+ from June 2026 high
- **[[MTUM ETF]]**: iShares MSCI USA Momentum Factor ETF, rebalances quarterly — faster index turnover than SPMO
- **[[Momentum Crash]]**: Sudden reversal when crowded momentum longs unwind; Goldman Sachs high-beta momentum basket fell 30%+ from peak
- **[[Factor Rotation]]**: Energy, retail, and bank stocks rising as AI-related momentum names fall; classic mean-reversion dynamic
- **[[Correction Territory]]**: Defined as 10%+ drawdown from recent high; average max drawdown across 10 prior SPMO corrections was ~15%

## Key Takeaways

- SPMO fell 10.6% from June 22 record close as of July 21, 2026.
- Q2 2026 was SPMO's strongest quarterly performance on record: +44%.
- After past corrections, SPMO averaged +12.4% over 3 months, +24% over 1 year.
- Win rate across prior corrections: ~90% over a 1-year horizon.
- Average max drawdown in those 10 events: ~15% — selloff may deepen before recovering.
- Goldman's high-beta momentum basket down 30%+ from peak.
- Russell 1000 stocks up 100%+ in H1 2026 have since fallen avg 21.6% in July.
- Stocks down 50%+ in H1 bounced 10.6% — textbook rotation trade.
- Index composition rebalances could shift momentum exposure as early as August.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Mean Reversion]]**: Momentum crashes are self-correcting — the very act of selling last cycle's winners redirects capital into tomorrow's momentum names, shortening the drawdown window.
- **[[Base Rate Neglect]]**: Investors fleeing momentum after a 10% drawdown ignore the 90% historical win rate on 1-year forward returns — recency bias amplifies perceived risk beyond what data supports.
