## Summary

U.S. stock futures were roughly flat on the night of August 11, 2026 ahead of the July [[CPI]] report, with SoFi strategist Liz Thomas noting September rate-hike odds were sitting "50-50" and would likely be resolved by the inflation print.

## Core Concepts

- **[[CPI Report]]**: Expected to show 0.1% headline / 0.2% core monthly inflation, with 12-month rates of 3.4%/2.5% — still above the Fed's 2% target.
- **[[Producer Price Index]] (PPI)**: The following day's release (Thursday) was flagged as the next hurdle after CPI, needing to confirm the prior month's softer wholesale-inflation reading.
- **Earnings bellwethers**: [[Super Micro Computer]] shares rallied 6%+ on rosy Q1 guidance; [[CoreWeave]] surged 14% on a 5% adjusted operating margin beating expectations.

## Key Takeaways

- Dow futures +0.03%, S&P 500 futures +0.07%, Nasdaq 100 futures +0.1% overnight.
- Three Fed officials dissented at the last meeting in favor of raising rates, reflecting internal disagreement about the inflation trajectory.
- 10-year Treasury yield near 4.7%, 2-year near 4.2%, both already pricing some probability of a more restrictive rate path.
- Oil prices pushing above $83/barrel added to inflation anxiety as Strait of Hormuz reopening hopes dwindled.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this article?
**A**: Stock futures were little changed as markets waited on the July CPI report, which was seen as the deciding factor for September Fed rate-hike odds currently split 50-50.

**Q2**: What specific data point illustrates the market's rate uncertainty?
**A**: SoFi's Liz Thomas noted the odds of a September hike were exactly 50-50 heading into the report — meaning the CPI print itself, not prior guidance, would determine market positioning.

**Q3**: Why does the article flag Thursday's PPI report as still relevant even after CPI?
**A**: Because a single soft CPI print doesn't confirm an inflation trend — PPI (wholesale/producer prices) often leads consumer prices, so confirming the prior month's softer PPI reading would strengthen the case that disinflation is durable rather than a one-off.
