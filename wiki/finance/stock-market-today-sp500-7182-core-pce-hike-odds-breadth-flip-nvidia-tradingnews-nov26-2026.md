## Summary
On Wednesday, Nov. 25, the S&P 500 fell as much as 3.2% before closing down just 0.25% at 7,182.78, as a hot core PCE print lifted December Fed hike odds to 97.8%. Losses were concentrated in AI names (Nvidia, Oracle, memory chips) while 370 S&P 500 stocks rose, midcaps and equal-weight outperformed, and Treasury yields fell. The author reads the session as selling exhaustion with a bullish tilt and watches 7,200, 7,340 and 7,420 for Friday's half-session.

## Core Concepts
- [[Core PCE]]: 3.3% year over year against a 3.2% forecast; Q3 GDP revised up to 4.3%.
- [[Federal Reserve]]: December 25 basis point hike priced at 97.8%; debate shifts to 2027 guidance and the dot plot.
- [[Treasury Yields]]: 10-year fell 6 basis points to 4.85%, an 11-week low.
- [[Section 301 Tariffs]]: proposed on 14 partners, milder than feared because they do not stack on July tariffs.
- [[Nvidia]] and [[Jensen Huang]]: stock at $149.30, S&P Global CreditWatch Negative on governance, down 27% in November.
- [[Anthropic]] IPO: closed at $82.79, just under its $83 offer price.
- [[Market Breadth]]: 370 advancers to 128 decliners; equal-weight RSP up 0.83% versus the cap-weighted index down 0.25%.
- [[Circular Financing]] in AI: developers, chip vendors and cloud providers mark down together when end-demand wobbles.
- [[VIX]]: closed at 28.29, signaling hedging into the holiday and the December hike.
- Cross-asset moves: Brent at $94.61, gold near a 15-month low, Bitcoin at $60,119, Kospi down 6.16%.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Known information is already priced** — When an outcome is almost fully expected, the outcome itself moves prices little; the risk shifts to what is not yet priced, such as guidance. · *model:* [[Expectations Investing]]
→ explains: Hike Odds, Yields Fell

**T2. Concentrated weights let a few stocks override the many** — A cap-weighted index can fall while most members rise when the largest names fall hard. · *model:* [[Market Capitalization Weighting]]
→ explains: Breadth Flip, Intraday Reversal

**T3. Circular financing couples valuations together** — When vendors invest in customers who spend the money back with them, revenue depends on continued spending, and a wobble in end-demand marks the whole loop down together. · *model:* [[Vendor Financing]]
→ explains: AI Split, Nvidia Governance

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did the S&P 500 close nearly flat after falling as much as 3.2%?
**A**: Losses were confined to the AI complex while 370 of 500 stocks rose, helped by falling yields and milder-than-feared tariffs. Sellers ran out of ammunition by lunch.

**Q2**: Why did the market largely shrug off hot core PCE and 97.8% hike odds?
**A**: The hike was already priced, leaving little room for a surprise on the decision itself. The risk moves to forward guidance and the dot plot in mid-December.

**Q3**: What signals would confirm or invalidate the reversal on Friday's half-session?
**A**: A cash open above 7,200 that holds through the first hour confirms it, with 7,340 and 7,420 as upside targets. A close below 7,000 would invalidate it and reopen a retest of the 6,970 low.
