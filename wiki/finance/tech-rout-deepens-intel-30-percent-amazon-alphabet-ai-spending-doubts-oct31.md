## Summary

US tech giants were pummeled on Friday after a week of earnings in which [[Microsoft]], [[Alphabet]] and [[Amazon]] each signaled continued heavy data center and chip spending while investors grew impatient for returns. [[Intel]] fell as much as 30% on a weak Q4 forecast, and analysts argued the stocks had been priced for perfection. [[Dan Ives]] called it "a tale of two cities" and still expects 2027 to be when AI revenue begins to justify the spending.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Capital Expenditure]]**: With [[Meta]], the four companies are on track to spend over $700 billion this year, mostly on AI infrastructure.
- **[[Intel]]**: Shares sank as much as 30% on a weak Q4 revenue outlook tied to PC-market weakness and manufacturing bottlenecks.
- **AI build-out component squeeze**: The race to build AI infrastructure crowds out supply of key components, raising memory prices and laptop, console and smartphone prices.
- **[[Amazon]]**: Missed Q3 expectations; strong cloud demand nearly doubled AI spending, implying more debt, while retail revenue was weak.
- **[[Alphabet]]**: Shares fell 12% on Thursday, its worst day since the 2004 IPO, on weaker [[Google Search]] advertising and "not very good" AI returns.
- **[[Priced for Perfection]]**: [[Steve Sosnick]] says anything less than perfect bites richly valued shares.
- **[[Dan Ives]]**: Sees strong cloud results and in-line spending, but uncertainty over which companies AI will disrupt.
- **[[Christopher Low]]**: Says company-specific stories are being read as signs of sector-wide slowdown.

## Key Takeaways

- **Capex scale**: Four tech giants on track for over $700 billion in capital spending.
- **Intel plunge**: Shares fell up to 30% on weak revenue forecast.
- **Self-inflicted squeeze**: AI build-out raises component prices, hurting consumer-device demand and Intel's PC business.
- **Debt financing**: Amazon's near-doubled AI spending implies more debt; investors disliked it.
- **Search threat**: Alphabet's search ads weakened; AI search may avoid ads.
- **Record drop**: Alphabet's 12% fall was its worst day since 2004.
- **Analyst split**: Cloud strong, but AI-to-revenue conversion slower; Ives expects 2027 payoff.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Expectations set the bar for reactions** — When a stock price already embeds flawless outcomes, any shortfall relative to expectations triggers a sharp repricing, regardless of absolute results. · *model:* [[Expectations Investing]]
→ explains: Record drop, Analyst split, Intel plunge

**T2. Cannibalization of the core** — A new product that serves the same need in a way that avoids the incumbent's monetization threatens the incumbent's profit pool. · *model:* [[Disruptive Innovation]]
→ explains: Search threat

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did US tech stocks sell off sharply after the week's earnings?
**A**: Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon signaled continued heavy AI spending while investors grew impatient for returns, and shares priced for perfection were punished by anything less. Intel's weak forecast added to the pressure.

**Q2**: How does the AI build-out itself hurt Intel?
**A**: AI infrastructure demand crowds out the supply of key components, pushing up memory prices and device prices. This hurts consumer demand for PCs and weakens Intel's PC business.

**Q3**: What is Dan Ives' stance on whether investors should flee tech?
**A**: He says it is not the time to panic and sell the tech winners, and expects 2027 to be the year AI revenue begins to justify the heavy spending.
