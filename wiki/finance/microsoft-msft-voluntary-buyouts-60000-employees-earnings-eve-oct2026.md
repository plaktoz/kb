## Summary

[[Microsoft]] offered voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees, roughly 27% of its global workforce, to contain costs amid heavy [[AI Capex]] investment. Barchart says lower recurring payroll could protect profitability, but eligibility is not departures and severance adds upfront cost, so investors need participation figures and savings estimates. The news came a day before Q1 earnings, with the stock up about 25% over six months and analysts at "Strong Buy."

## Core Concepts

- **[[Voluntary Buyouts]]**: Time-limited program for staff at internal level 70 and below in the U.S., Canada, India, and the U.K.
- **[[Cost Discipline]]**: Payroll restraint to offset rising AI infrastructure spending.
- **[[Severance Costs]]**: Upfront expense that arrives before any savings.
- **[[Earnings Preview]]**: Q1 report due Oct. 28; consensus EPS of $4.71, about 14% year-over-year growth.
- **[[Technical Analysis]]**: Stock above major [[Moving Averages]], with [[RSI]] in the late 50s.
- **[[Analyst Price Targets]]**: Mean target of about $595, nearly 12% upside.

## Key Takeaways

- **Scope**: About 60,000 eligible employees, roughly 27% of the global workforce.
- **Affected divisions**: Copilot, Xbox, Windows, finance, and HR reportedly included, per Reuters source.
- **Excluded teams**: Marketing, commercial business, and security are excluded.
- **Eligible is not departing**: Actual exits depend on participation.
- **Cost trade-off**: Severance raises near-term costs before savings appear.
- **Earnings setup**: Consensus Q1 EPS of $4.71, up about 14% year over year.
- **Stock context**: Up about 25% over six months; consensus "Strong Buy."
- **Valuation**: Mean price target near $595 implies nearly 12% upside.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What did Microsoft announce, and why does it matter for the stock?
**A**: It offered voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees (about 27% of its workforce) to contain costs amid heavy AI investment. A lower recurring payroll bill could help protect profitability as infrastructure spending expands.

**Q2**: Why does the 60,000 figure not equal expected savings?
**A**: It counts eligible employees, not confirmed departures, which depend on participation. Severance would also increase upfront costs before savings emerge.

**Q3**: What do investors need before judging the buyouts' effect on earnings?
**A**: Participation figures and management's savings estimates. Until then, greater spending discipline may support shares, but the effect on future earnings is unquantified.
