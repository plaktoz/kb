## Summary
September inflation accelerated, with CPI at 3.7% and core CPI at 2.6%, making further [[Federal Reserve]] rate hikes more likely after the September quarter-point increase. The author notes that after the first hike of past cycles, the [[S&P 500]] and [[Nasdaq Composite]] have dropped an average of 10% and 12% within three months. He still advises focusing on high-quality stocks with reasonable valuations, since the S&P 500 has recovered from every past drawdown.

## Core Concepts
- [[Consumer Price Index]]: September CPI 3.7%, above the 3.6% consensus; core CPI 2.6%.
- [[Energy Price Shock]]: Military action against Iran keeps oil prices elevated, spilling into manufacturing and transportation costs.
- [[Kevin Warsh]]: Fed Chairman vowing to return inflation to the 2% target.
- [[Federal Funds Rate]]: CME [[FedWatch]] expects hikes in December and March, reaching 4.25% to 4.5%.
- [[Rate Hike Cycle]]: Three cycles in 25 years; stocks typically performed poorly afterward.
- [[Warren Buffett]]: Compared interest rates to gravity on stock valuations.
- [[Lisa Shalett]]: Morgan Stanley CIO sees the bull case intact but flags yields, oil, policy uncertainty, and consumer strain.

## Key Takeaways
- **Inflation**: CPI 3.7% was the highest since May; core 2.6% highest since June.
- **Rate Path**: Second hike likely in December, third in March.
- **Reversal**: January pricing implied two cuts in 2026.
- **Two Channels**: Higher rates favor bonds over stocks and raise corporate borrowing costs.
- **History**: S&P 500 and Nasdaq fell 10% and 12% on average after first hikes.
- **Year to Date**: Both indexes up 12%, on pace for a fourth straight double-digit year.
- **Posture**: Stay cautiously optimistic; favor quality and reasonable valuations.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central warning about the stock market?
**A**: Accelerating inflation makes more Fed rate hikes likely, and new hike cycles have frequently correlated with market corrections.

**Q2**: What historical evidence supports the correction risk?
**A**: After the first hike in each of the last three Fed hike cycles, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite dropped by an average of 10% and 12% at some point over the next three months.

**Q3**: How should investors respond, according to the author?
**A**: Stay cautiously optimistic, favor high-quality stocks with strong fundamentals and reasonable valuations, and be mentally prepared for a decline, since the S&P 500 has recovered from every past drawdown.
