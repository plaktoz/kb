## Summary

October 2026 reversed the AI trade as worries about free cash flow and borrowing costs hit tech, with the S&P 500 tech sector down 6.8% and momentum ETFs down about 10%. Investors moved into quality stocks and defensive sectors, with consumer staples posting their best month ever (+10.9%) and the quality ETF QUAL ending flat. Strategists warn staples now look overvalued, while an equal-weight S&P 500 gained almost 5% as the cap-weighted index fell 0.9%.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Momentum Factor]]**: SPMO lost 10.1% and MTUM 10.5% in October because both are heavily weighted to tech.
- **[[Quality Factor]]**: Low debt, consistent earnings, and embedded growth; QUAL gained 0.1%.
- **[[Defensive Sectors]]**: [[Consumer Staples]], [[Utilities]], and [[Health Care]] rallied; XLP had its largest monthly gain since 1998.
- **[[AI Trade]]**: Chip index fell over 15%, Intel dropped 28.6% in a day, and the Magnificent Seven ETF fell 4.5%.
- **[[Equal-Weight Indexing]]**: RSP rose almost 5% for the month versus the S&P 500's 0.9% loss.
- **Strategists**: [[Jeff Buchbinder]] (LPL Financial) and [[Marta Norton]] (Empower) favor quality; [[Russell Price]] (Ameriprise) flags discretionary spending risk.

## Key Takeaways

- **Tech Rout**: S&P 500 tech fell 6.8%, its worst month since March 2025.
- **Momentum Losses**: SPMO and MTUM fell about 10% as tech-heavy holdings cracked.
- **Quality Held Up**: QUAL rose 0.1% while the Nasdaq fell 5.3%.
- **Staples Record**: XLP gained 10.9%, its best month since 1998 inception.
- **Valuation Caution**: Empower calls staples "overvalued" after the surge.
- **Equal-Weight Win**: RSP gained almost 5% while the S&P 500 lost 0.9%.
- **Unclear Winners**: Norton says AI winners and losers remain unknown, so the market is judging early.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Uncertainty pushes capital toward low-risk assets** — When confidence in a leading theme breaks, investors shift into assets with stable earnings and low debt, which cushions drawdowns. · *model:* [[Risk-On / Risk-Off]]
→ explains: Quality Held Up, Staples Record, Valuation Caution

**T2. Concentrated indexes inherit the risk of their largest holdings** — Cap-weighted and momentum-weighted portfolios lean on a few leaders, so when those fall the index falls more than the average stock. · *model:* [[Concentration Risk]]
→ explains: Momentum Losses, Equal-Weight Win

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What happened to the stock market's leaders in October 2026, and why?
**A**: Tech and momentum stocks fell sharply, with S&P 500 tech down 6.8% and momentum ETFs down about 10%. Worries about dwindling free cash flow and rising borrowing costs hit the AI trade.

**Q2**: What data shows where investors sought shelter?
**A**: Consumer staples ETF XLP gained 10.9%, its biggest month since 1998, and QUAL rose 0.1% while the Nasdaq fell 5.3%. Equal-weight RSP gained almost 5% against a 0.9% loss for the S&P 500.

**Q3**: How should investors treat the defensive rotation going forward?
**A**: Strategists favor quality for its value tilt and lower AI exposure, but Empower warns staples look overvalued after the surge. Winners and losers in AI are not yet known, so the market may be judging too early.
