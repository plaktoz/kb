## Summary

Amazon's two worst stock years over the past 15 (2014: -22%; 2022: -50%) both coincided with net losses during heavy capital investment cycles, and both were followed by massive rebounds (2015: +118%; 2023: +81%). The distinguishing factor was not capex size alone, but the collapse of operating profit alongside spending. In 2026, Amazon is in another record capex cycle (~$220B projected), but operating income is still growing strongly (+43% YoY), making the current period structurally different from 2014 and 2022.

## Key Takeaways

- **2014 worst year**: Capex $4.9B (+42% YoY); net loss $241M; stock fell 22%.
- **2015 rebound**: Operating income surged tenfold; stock rose 118%.
- **2022 worst year**: Capex $58.3B; net loss $2.7B (Rivian write-down); stock lost ~50%.
- **2023 rebound**: Net income hit $30.4B; stock rose 81%.
- **Spending alone isn't the trigger**: 2012 net loss year saw stock gain 45%.
- **Key distinguisher**: Operating profit collapsed alongside capex in 2014 and 2022.
- **2026 picture**: Capex ~22% of revenue, but operating income up 43% YoY to $27.5B.
- **AWS Q2 2026**: Revenue grew 37% YoY — fastest pace since 2021.
- **Watch metric**: Whether operating income continues growing through the build-out.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Base Rate Neglect]]**: Investors fixating on capex size risk ignoring the historical base rate — four other heavy-spend years (2021, 2024, 2025, 2012) produced positive stock returns, showing spending alone is not the causal variable.
- **[[Leading vs. Lagging Indicators]]**: Operating income is a leading signal of whether an investment cycle will pay off; net loss and stock price are lagging outcomes. Tracking the leading indicator (operating income trajectory) avoids reacting to noise.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What was the common factor that distinguished Amazon's two worst stock years (2014 and 2022) from other heavy-investment years?
**A**: In both 2014 and 2022, operating profit collapsed *alongside* the spending surge, resulting in net losses — unlike other high-capex years where operating income remained positive.

**Q2**: What specific data points describe the 2022–2023 cycle?
**A**: In 2022, capex reached $58.3B and Amazon posted a $2.7B net loss (partly from a Rivian write-down), causing the stock to fall ~50%; in 2023, net income rebounded to $30.4B and the stock rose 81%.

**Q3**: Given the 2026 capex cycle, what metric should investors monitor to assess whether history will repeat the post-loss rebound pattern?
**A**: The key metric is whether operating income continues growing through the build-out; as of Q2 2026, operating income rose 43% YoY to $27.5B, suggesting the current cycle differs structurally from 2014 and 2022.
