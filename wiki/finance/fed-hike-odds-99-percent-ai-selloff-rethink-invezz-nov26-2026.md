## Summary
CME FedWatch puts the odds of a December Fed rate hike at 99.4% after hot PCE inflation and strong Q3 growth. The AI-led selloff, which briefly pushed the Nasdaq 100 into a bear market, raises the question of whether tighter financial conditions could change the Fed's mind. Fed officials and most economists doubt it, while Bill Ackman and Torsten Slok argue for caution.

## Core Concepts
- [[Federal Reserve]] under Chair [[Kevin Warsh]] raised rates in September and is expected to hike again at the December 8-9 meeting.
- [[CME FedWatch]] implied probability: 99.4%, up from about 97% a day earlier.
- [[PCE Inflation]] at 3.8% year over year and core PCE at 3.3% keep the [[Inflation Target]] of 2% out of reach.
- [[Financial Conditions]] tighten when equity declines raise capital costs and cut household wealth.
- [[Nasdaq 100]] fell 20% below its October peak intraday, meeting the [[Bear Market]] definition before recovering.
- [[John Williams]], [[Torsten Slok]], [[Claudia Sahm]] and [[Bill Ackman]] represent the range of views on whether the selloff should change policy.
- [[Nvidia]] and [[Jensen Huang]] controversy adds to worries about the AI trade.

## Key Takeaways
- **Hike Odds**: CME FedWatch shows 99.4% probability of a December hike to 4%-4.25%.
- **Inflation Data**: October PCE rose 3.8% year over year; core PCE hit 3.3%, 0.1 point above forecasts.
- **Growth**: Q3 GDP was revised up to a 4.3% annualised rate.
- **Fed Resolve**: Williams said getting inflation back to 2% is "100% essential".
- **AI Selloff**: Nasdaq 100 briefly fell 20% below its October peak on November 25.
- **Dovish Dissent**: Slok says inflation argues for hikes while the AI outlook argues for cuts.
- **Skeptic View**: Sahm says stock declines would need to be considerably larger to sway the Fed.
- **Ackman's Case**: He argues conditions have already tightened and a 3% target would be more realistic.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Asset-price declines act as tightening** — Falling equity prices raise the cost of capital and reduce household wealth, so they tighten financial conditions without any policy move. The weight a central bank gives this depends on how large the decline is.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the central question the article poses about the Fed?
**A**: It asks whether the AI-driven stock selloff could force the Fed to drop its planned December rate hike. Markets, the data, and most Fed officials say no for now, since inflation is persistent and growth is resilient.

**Q2**: What data supports the near-certain December hike?
**A**: October PCE inflation was 3.8% year over year, core PCE was 3.3%, October CPI rose 0.4% on the month, and Q3 GDP was revised up to a 4.3% annualised rate. CME FedWatch put the hike probability at 99.4%.

**Q3**: How do Slok, Sahm and Ackman differ on what the Fed should do?
**A**: Slok sees the AI outlook as a strong argument against aggressive hikes. Sahm thinks the Fed will stay focused on inflation unless stock declines grow much larger. Ackman warns that further hikes risk a recession and wants a 3% inflation target.
