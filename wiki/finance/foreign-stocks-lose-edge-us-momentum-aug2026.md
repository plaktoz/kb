## Summary

International equities (VXUS) are still ahead of US stocks (SPY) for 2026 — up 15.7% vs. 14.0% — but the VXUS:SPY ratio's 50-day average has slipped below its 200-day average for the first time since the Iran war began, signaling foreign relative strength may be peaking as US AI-driven momentum reasserts itself.

## Core Concepts

- [[Relative Strength Ratio]] — comparing VXUS:SPY over time to identify which region is leading; a falling ratio (50-day below 200-day) signals the leadership shift is turning toward the US.
- [[Regional Divergence]] — within the ex-US basket, Asia ex-Japan (AAXJ, +21.5% YTD) has led but stumbled recently, while Japan (EWJ) is near record highs and offsetting some of that weakness.
- [[AI-Powered Momentum]] — the article frames the reversal as driven by continued enthusiasm for US AI/tech stocks pulling capital back toward American equities.

## Key Takeaways

- **YTD scoreboard**: VXUS +15.7% vs. SPY +14.0% — international still ahead, but the gap is narrowing.
- **Turning signal**: VXUS:SPY 50-day average fell below its 200-day average for the first time since the Iran war began.
- **Regional detail**: Asia ex-Japan leads YTD but has stumbled; Japan is near a record high; emerging markets (VWO) show a similar soft patch.
- **Framing**: If investors keep favoring US tech/AI, international outperformance "may prove to be just another brief detour in a long U.S. bull narrative."
- **Relevance**: Directly informative for a global-equity holding (VWRA) that blends US and ex-US exposure.
