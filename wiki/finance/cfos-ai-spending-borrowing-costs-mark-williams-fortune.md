## Summary

Finance expert [[Mark Williams]] told CFOs at a Fortune Modern CFO dinner that rising financing costs and economic uncertainty will likely reduce [[AI Spending]], but that pulling back too hard risks losing competitive edge. He advised CFOs to assume the [[Cost of Capital]] is rising, set explicit success measures, and tie AI project spending to financial results. Pressure will fall unevenly, hitting lower-rated AI borrowers harder than strong balance sheets.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Mark Williams]]**: Boston University Questrom lecturer and former Federal Reserve Bank examiner.
- **[[Cost of Capital]]**: Persistent inflation could keep borrowing costs elevated and raise the hurdle for new investments.
- **[[Net Present Value]]**: Regulatory uncertainty lowers growth assumptions and project NPV, making fewer investments attractive.
- **[[Credit Quality Divide]]**: Highly rated tech firms versus lower-rated, leveraged AI borrowers.
- **[[Corporate Finance]] discipline**: Explicit success metrics, spending tied to financial results, shorter-term financing.
- **[[Risk Management]]**: Chief risk officers, accounts receivable analysis and consumer spending outlook.
- **[[Stanford AI100 Report]]**: Cited in the newsletter's "Big Deal" section on AI governance and bubble concerns.

## Key Takeaways

- **Spending Outlook**: Williams expects AI spending to be reduced amid tougher financing.
- **Inaction Risk**: "If we do nothing, we're going to lose our competitive edge."
- **Discipline**: Define success or failure measures and link spending to financial results.
- **Financing**: Consider shorter-term financing instead of automatically borrowing long term.
- **Uneven Pressure**: Weaker credits face greater financing pressure than strong balance sheets.
- **Planning Assumption**: Assume the cost of capital is going up.
- **Beyond Budgets**: Review receivables and customers' ability to pay if conditions weaken.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Higher discount rates shrink the set of viable investments** — When capital costs and uncertainty rise, projected returns must clear a higher hurdle, so fewer projects have positive value. · *model:* [[Discounted Cash Flow]]
→ explains: Spending Outlook, Planning Assumption

**T2. Leverage makes firms fragile to financing shocks** — Companies with more debt are forced to rethink plans first when borrowing gets costlier. · *model:* [[Leverage]]
→ explains: Uneven Pressure

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Williams's core message to CFOs about AI spending?
**A**: Rising financing costs will likely reduce AI spending, but retreating too aggressively risks a competitive disadvantage. CFOs should be more discerning about where they place bets.

**Q2**: How does uncertainty make fewer AI investments attractive?
**A**: Greater regulatory and economic uncertainty lowers growth assumptions and reduces a project's net present value. Persistent inflation also keeps borrowing costs elevated, raising the hurdle for new investments.

**Q3**: What should CFOs do before year-end?
**A**: Assume the cost of capital is rising, set explicit success metrics, tie spending to financial results, consider shorter-term financing, and review accounts receivable. He also suggested keeping chief risk officers close.
