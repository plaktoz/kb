## Summary

The article compares [[IBM]] (NYSE: IBM) and [[D-Wave]] (NASDAQ: QBTS) as quantum computing investment vehicles, arguing that financial strength and enterprise staying power may matter more than being first to achieve quantum breakthroughs. The core thesis is that endurance — not speed — is the decisive factor in picking the long-term quantum computing winner.

## Core Concepts

- **[[IBM]]** — established enterprise tech giant with strong financials and broad market reach
- **[[D-Wave]]** — pure-play quantum computing company trading under ticker QBTS
- **[[Quantum Computing]]** — emerging computing paradigm both companies are pursuing
- **[[Endurance Investing]]** — thesis that capital durability and staying power outweigh first-mover advantage in nascent tech
- **[[Enterprise Reach]]** — IBM's existing customer relationships as a durable competitive moat in quantum adoption

## Key Takeaways

- **Endurance over speed**: The quantum winner may be the company that stays in the race longest.
- **IBM's moat**: Financial strength and enterprise relationships give IBM staying power.
- **D-Wave's risk**: Pure-play quantum exposure means higher upside but also higher failure risk.
- **Price snapshot (Aug 13, 2026)**: IBM at $235.59 (-1.34%); D-Wave at $16.99 (-5.08%).

- **[[Innovator's Dilemma]]**: In nascent technology races, incumbents with diversified revenue (IBM) often outlast pure-play disruptors (D-Wave) simply because they can absorb long development timelines — the article's endurance thesis is precisely this dynamic.
