## Summary

Despite the 10-year Treasury yield reaching 4.93% — its highest since 2023 — the S&P 500 remains roughly 2% below its all-time closing high, puzzling many investors. ClearBridge Investments analyst Jeff Schulze argues the market's resilience stems from exceptional earnings strength (S&P 500 profits up 52% YoY in Q2) and the fact that rising yields reflect stronger growth and AI infrastructure investment rather than inflation fears. Historical data reinforces the case: when the S&P 500 gains more than 10% through August, it has advanced September–December in 89% of historical instances.

## Core Concepts

- **[[S&P 500]] earnings resilience**: Q2 profits surged 52% year-over-year, providing a strong fundamental floor beneath equities.
- **[[Bond Yields]] vs. equities**: The 10-year Treasury at 4.93% and 30-year near 5.33% would normally pressure stocks, but the driver matters.
- **[[Real Rates]] vs. inflation expectations**: The yield rise was driven primarily by higher real rates (+50 bps since late February), not inflation expectations (+15 bps) or term premium (+17 bps).
- **[[AI Infrastructure]] investment**: Schulze identifies AI capital spending as a key contributor to the growth-driven yield increase — a bullish signal, not a threat.
- **[[Seasonality]] and historical base rates**: An 89% win rate (25 of 28 instances) for Q4 gains when S&P is up 10%+ through August.
- **[[Federal Reserve]] rate hike probability**: Markets priced ~90% odds of a 25-bps hike following above-expected core inflation data.
- **[[Jeff Schulze]]** — ClearBridge Investments analyst quoted as the primary source.

## Key Takeaways

- S&P 500 is ~2% below all-time high despite 10-year Treasury yield at 4.93%.
- Q2 S&P 500 earnings grew 52% YoY — the primary support for stock prices.
- Real rates drove most of the yield rise (+50 bps); inflation expectations rose only +15 bps.
- Growth- and AI-driven yield increases are bullish; inflation-driven increases would be bearish.
- 10%+ S&P gains through August historically predict Q4 advance 89% of the time.
- Fed rate hike (~25 bps) pricing at ~90% probability following hot CPI print.
- "If yields signaled a material threat, stocks would likely be much lower" — Schulze.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Base Rate Neglect]]**: Investors anchoring on the surface signal (higher yields = bad for stocks) ignore the 89% historical base rate for Q4 gains after a strong August — and miss that *why* yields rise matters as much as the level.
- **[[Signal vs. Noise]]**: Real-rate increases driven by growth and AI capex are fundamentally different from inflation-driven increases; treating them identically misreads the underlying economic message.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why haven't higher bond yields caused a significant stock market decline in this environment?
**A**: Schulze argues markets are following the strong earnings environment (52% YoY Q2 profit growth), and the yield rise reflects stronger growth and AI infrastructure investment rather than inflation fears — making it a fundamentally different and less threatening catalyst.

**Q2**: What drove the recent increase in the 10-year Treasury yield to 4.93%?
**A**: The move was primarily driven by higher real rates (up 50 basis points since late February), with inflation expectations rising only 15 basis points and term premium up 17 basis points — indicating growth expectations, not inflation fears.

**Q3**: How should investors use the historical seasonality data about S&P 500 gains?
**A**: When the S&P 500 gains more than 10% through August, it has advanced from September through December in 25 of 28 historical instances (89%), providing a probabilistic tailwind for continued gains into year-end.
