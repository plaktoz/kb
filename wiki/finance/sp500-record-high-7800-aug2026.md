## Summary
The [[S&P 500]] hit a fresh intraday record of 7,814.88 and closed above 7,800 for the first time in mid-August 2026, driven by cooler-than-expected July CPI (+0.1% M/M, 3.4% annual) and PPI data, a steady [[Federal Reserve]] holding rates at 3.50-3.75%, and broadening market participation beyond mega-cap tech.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Disinflation Rally]]**: Both CPI and PPI came in below forecasts, removing the worst-case rate-hike scenario investors had been pricing in and triggering a relief rally led by rate-sensitive technology stocks.
- **[[Market Breadth]]**: Unlike earlier narrow rallies concentrated in mega-cap tech, mid-caps, small-caps (Russell 2000), and international markets participated in the August surge — a historically bullish signal for rally durability.
- **[[Mixed Labor Signals]]**: July payrolls unexpectedly fell 23,000 (vs. +85,000 expected) — the first monthly decline in two years — even as unemployment ticked down to 4.1%, illustrating how equity markets can weigh good inflation news over bad employment news.

## Key Takeaways
- **Record level**: S&P 500 intraday high of 7,814.88; first close above 7,800.
- **Fed stance**: Rates held at 3.50-3.75% for a fifth consecutive meeting; three FOMC members (Logan, Kashkari, Hammack) dissented toward a more hawkish stance.
- **Jobs surprise**: July nonfarm payrolls fell 23,000, the first monthly decline in over two years, but didn't derail the rally.
- **Breadth improvement**: Russell 2000 and international markets joined the advance alongside AI-driven tech gains.
