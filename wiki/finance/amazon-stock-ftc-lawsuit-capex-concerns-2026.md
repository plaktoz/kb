## Summary

[[Amazon]] (AMZN) stock has dropped roughly 11% from its August all-time high, falling below the 100-day moving average and underperforming the [[S&P 500]] in 2026. Two converging headwinds explain the decline: a major [[FTC]] lawsuit alleging $20 billion in advertiser overcharges, and analyst projections of deeply negative free cash flow through 2028 driven by an escalating AI capex cycle.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Amazon]] stock technical breakdown** — fell below 100-day moving average; down ~11% from August highs; +10% YTD vs. S&P 500's +12%
- **[[FTC]] Lawsuit** — FTC and 22 states allege Amazon's ad platform used undisclosed reserve-pricing to overcharge ~1.2 million advertisers by $20 billion (2019–present)
- **[[AI Capex]] escalation** — 2026 capex already expanded to ~$220 billion; Evercore ISI projects $320B in 2027 and $370B in 2028
- **[[Free Cash Flow]]** — Evercore ISI warns of roughly -$50 billion FCF in both 2027 and 2028 under current capex trajectory
- **[[AWS]]** — cited as the key long-term AI demand driver that underpins the bull case despite near-term headwinds

## Key Takeaways

- **Stock pullback**: AMZN off ~11% from August all-time high, underperforming S&P 500 YTD.
- **FTC claim**: ~1.2 million advertisers allegedly overcharged $20B via hidden reserve-pricing (2019–present).
- **Amazon's response**: Company disputes the FTC allegations.
- **Bull case**: Citi's Ronald Josey calls any selloff a buying opportunity, citing AWS AI demand and retail tailwinds.
- **Bear case**: Evercore's Mark Mahaney projects $320B capex in 2027 and $370B in 2028, implying -$50B FCF in each year.
- **Capex trajectory**: Each year's projected spend is larger than the prior — a compounding concern for FCF.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Opportunity Cost]]**: Amazon's record capex commitment locks capital into long-duration AI infrastructure; the -$50B FCF projection makes the cost of being wrong on AI ROI extremely high, raising the opportunity cost of the bull thesis.
- **[[Mr. Market]]**: The 11% drawdown illustrates how the market reprices risk when two independent negative catalysts (legal and financial) arrive simultaneously — even when the underlying business fundamentals remain intact.
