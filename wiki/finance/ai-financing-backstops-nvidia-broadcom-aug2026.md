## Summary
[[Nvidia]] and [[Broadcom]] have launched large third-party financing backstops ($500B and $35B respectively) with major asset managers, letting AI compute buyers fund infrastructure outside hyperscaler balance sheets. Barclays estimates this "backstopped" capex could reach 20%+ of industry capex in 2027 and half by 2028, as hyperscalers approach natural debt and power-agreement limits.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Special-Purpose Vehicle (SPV) Financing]]**: A structure that separates long-lived datacenter assets (25-30 year life) from faster-depreciating compute assets (GPUs/accelerators), each financed differently to match their risk and obsolescence profiles.
- **[[Hyperscaler Capex Limits]]**: The article frames $1.5 trillion in AI capex since 2023 as approaching structural limits around debt capacity and power-purchase agreements, forcing new off-balance-sheet financing models.
- **[[AI Buildout Risk]]**: By letting non-hyperscaler compute buyers build infrastructure "in small chunks" backstopped by chipmakers, the financing shifts and potentially concentrates credit risk with Nvidia and Broadcom rather than diversified hyperscalers.

## Key Takeaways
- **Nvidia backstop**: $500 billion in third-party capital, with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR as partners.
- **Broadcom backstop**: A $35 billion compute financing platform established in June 2026 with Apollo and Blackstone.
- **Projected scale**: Barclays estimates backstopped capex reaches 20%+ of industry capex in 2027, up to 50% by 2028.
- **Structural rationale**: Separating datacenter financing from compute financing addresses the faster depreciation and obsolescence risk of GPUs versus buildings.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Risk Transfer]]**: By backstopping financing themselves, Nvidia and Broadcom effectively underwrite demand for their own chips — a vertically-integrated risk transfer that boosts near-term sales but concentrates exposure if AI demand growth slows.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of the article?
**A**: New financing structures backstopped by Nvidia and Broadcom are letting AI compute buyers build infrastructure independent of hyperscalers, expanding the scale and risk footprint of the AI buildout.

**Q2**: What mechanism underlies these new financing structures?
**A**: They use special-purpose vehicles that separate long-lived datacenter assets from shorter-lived, faster-depreciating compute assets, financing each with structures suited to its risk profile.

**Q3**: What is the practical implication for investors tracking AI infrastructure spending?
**A**: A rising share of AI capex (20%+ by 2027, up to 50% by 2028 per Barclays) will move off hyperscaler balance sheets into chipmaker-backstopped vehicles, meaning traditional hyperscaler capex figures will increasingly understate total AI infrastructure investment.
