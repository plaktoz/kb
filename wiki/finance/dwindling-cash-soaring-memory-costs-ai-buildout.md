## Summary

Amazon, Alphabet, and Tesla all reported negative free cash flow in Q2 2026, while Meta's cash generation fell 91%, as AI infrastructure spending distorts the balance sheets of the world's largest tech companies. A worsening memory chip shortage — driven by insatiable demand for AI processors — is pushing costs higher than anticipated, with [[Goldman Sachs]] projecting megacap AI spend to reach $765 billion in 2026 and nearly $1.2 trillion by 2027. Investor reactions diverged sharply: [[Microsoft]] surged on strong cloud results while [[Meta]] and [[Alphabet]] sold off on weak outlooks and negative cash flow.

## Core Concepts

- **[[AI Capex Cycle]]**: The sustained, accelerating investment in data centers, chips, and AI systems by hyperscalers — now distorting balance sheets even at companies historically known for strong cash generation.
- **[[Memory Crisis]]**: A supply crunch in high-bandwidth memory chips, controlled by a small number of vendors, driving up costs for both AI infrastructure builders and consumer device makers.
- **[[Hyperscalers]]**: [[Amazon]] (AWS), [[Alphabet]] (Google Cloud), [[Microsoft]] (Azure), and [[Meta]] — the four dominant cloud and AI infrastructure platforms bearing the brunt of AI capex.
- **[[Free Cash Flow]]**: Key metric under pressure; [[Alphabet]] turned negative for the first time ever; Amazon reported -$7.6B trailing 12-month FCF.
- **[[Micron]]**: A primary memory supplier benefiting from the AI-driven memory crunch; mentioned favorably by [[Elon Musk]] (Tesla) for favorable allocation terms.
- **[[OpenAI]]** and **[[Anthropic]]**: Both valued near $1 trillion on private markets; their competitive position faces pressure from cheaper Chinese open-weight models.

## Key Takeaways

- **Capex surge**: Amazon raised 2026 capex forecast to $220B, highest among hyperscalers.
- **Cash flow collapse**: Alphabet went FCF-negative for the first time; Meta FCF fell 91% YoY.
- **Memory shock**: [[Elon Musk]] called memory pricing "insane"; [[Andy Jassy]] cited it as driver of Amazon's raised capex.
- **Apple impact**: Memory shortage forced Mac/iPad price hikes; weaker Q4 revenue guide issued by Tim Cook.
- **Market divergence**: Microsoft rallied (best day since 2008); Meta, Alphabet, and Apple fell post-earnings.
- **China risk**: Chinese AI labs releasing competitive models at lower cost, pressuring [[OpenAI]]/[[Anthropic]] dominance.
- **Investor scrutiny**: JPMorgan analyst notes market is becoming "more discriminating" on AI ROI across hyperscalers.
- **Goldman projection**: Megacap AI spend rising from $765B (2026) to ~$1.2T (2027).

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Prisoner's Dilemma]]**: Each hyperscaler must keep spending on AI capex or risk ceding market position to rivals — even if collective over-investment destroys industry-wide returns, no single player can unilaterally stop.
- **[[Jevons Paradox]]**: As AI infrastructure becomes more efficient and cheaper to deploy, total demand for compute and memory expands faster than efficiency gains, driving aggregate costs higher rather than lower.
