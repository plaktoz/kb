## Summary

Reuters' Morning Bid column for October 8, 2026 says stocks stalled as the bond crunch continued: Fed minutes and Governor Christopher Waller both pointed to more rate hikes, and the 10-year term premium climbed to a 12-year high. The column also flags rising debt-financed "circular" deals between AI chipmakers and their customers, which raise the stakes for the coming earnings season. Strong results from TSMC and Samsung were not enough to calm worries that chip growth and prices may cool.

## Core Concepts

- **[[FOMC Minutes]]**: Most policymakers in September expected more rate rises; markets price three more hikes over the next year, starting in December.
- **[[Christopher Waller]]**: Said more hikes will likely be needed to bring inflation to 2%, with "flexibility" on pace.
- **[[Treasury Yields]]**: 10-year yields hit another 24-year high before a solid 10-year auction brought some relief; a 30-year auction followed.
- **[[Term Premium]]**: Extra compensation investors demand to hold the 10-year to maturity; highest since 2014 on the [[New York Fed]] model.
- **[[Scott Bessent]]**: The Treasury Secretary called the bond moves global, not US-specific.
- **[[Circular Financing]] / [[Vendor Financing]]**: Debt deals between [[Broadcom]] and [[OpenAI]]; [[SpaceX]] and [[Nvidia]] arranging up to $40B in chip finance; Broadcom and [[Anthropic]] in talks on a similar scale.
- **Chip earnings**: [[TSMC]] quarterly revenue beat forecasts again; [[Samsung]] operating profit rose 700%+, yet its shares fell 1%+.

## Key Takeaways

- **Rate path**: Markets still price three more Fed hikes, starting in December.
- **Term premium**: 10-year term premium at its highest since 2014.
- **Yield drivers**: Rising yields mostly reflect the Fed rethink; long-run inflation expectations are stable.
- **Premium drivers**: Debt levels, political risk, and uncertainty about long-term government financing.
- **AI debt**: Debt-financed chip deals look to some like vendor financing, which raises earnings-season stakes.
- **Samsung paradox**: 700%+ profit growth, yet shares fell on signs of cooling.
- **Watch list**: Jobless claims, the 30-year auction, and speeches by Fed's Kashkari and Musalem.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Reflexivity]]**: When chipmakers finance their own customers' purchases, the demand that props up their revenue depends partly on their own credit, so the boom can feed itself until financing tightens.
- **[[Priced In]]**: Samsung's record profit growth still pushed its shares down, because the market had already priced in the results and reacted to signs of slower growth ahead.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did stock markets stall on October 7–8, 2026, according to the Morning Bid?
**A**: The bond crunch continued: Fed minutes and Governor Waller signaled more rate hikes, and 10-year yields hit another 24-year high. Attention also turned to earnings season and to circular financing in AI.

**Q2**: What does the rising term premium signal, and how is it different from the overall rise in yields?
**A**: The overall rise in yields mostly reflects a rethink of the Fed's rate path, while long-run inflation expectations have stayed stable. The term premium, at its highest since 2014, reflects concern about debt levels, political risk, and long-term government financing.

**Q3**: Why does debt-financed AI chip buying matter for investors heading into earnings?
**A**: Deals like Broadcom–OpenAI and SpaceX–Nvidia (up to $40B) look like vendor financing. That makes earnings updates on how durable AI demand really is more important.
