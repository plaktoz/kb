## Summary
[[IBM]] stock steadied at $236.71 in mid-August 2026, partially recovering from a 25% single-day crash on July 14 — its worst since 1968 — triggered by a Q2 revenue miss and warnings that customers were shifting budget from IBM software to AI hardware. Growth in Data (+19%) and Hybrid Cloud (+11%) is offsetting decline in legacy Transaction Processing (mainframe) revenue, while an active securities fraud investigation adds legal overhang.

## Core Concepts
- **[[Software Mix Shift]]**: IBM's software growth slowed to 5% (from a 14% Q4 2025 peak) as legacy Transaction Processing/mainframe revenue declined even as Data (+19%) and Hybrid Cloud/Red Hat (+11%) grew — illustrating a company mid-transition between legacy and growth product lines.
- **[[Recurring Revenue Cushion]]**: Roughly 80% of IBM's software business is recurring, providing revenue visibility that partially offsets volatility from one-time license sales in the declining segments.
- **[[Securities Fraud Overhang]]**: The July 14 crash triggered a fraud investigation tied to how IBM communicated its Q2 results, adding a legal risk layer distinct from the underlying business fundamentals.

## Key Takeaways
- **July 14 crash**: -25% in one day, IBM's worst since 1968, after Q2 revenue of $17.16B missed the $17.46B consensus.
- **Q2 EPS**: $2.93, matching estimates, up from $2.80 a year earlier; net margin 15.52%, ROE 35.65%.
- **Segment divergence**: Data revenue +19%, Hybrid Cloud (incl. Red Hat) +11%, vs. declining Transaction Processing (mainframe).
- **Guidance**: Reiterated 4-5% full-year growth; consensus full-year 2026 EPS of $12.33.
- **Dividend**: $1.69/share quarterly, 2.86% yield, well covered by expected earnings.
- **Relative performance**: IBM -1.89% on Aug 14 even as the S&P 500 hit a record 7,799 — lagging the broader market.
- **Next catalyst**: Q3 earnings expected October 21, 2026.
- **Update (2026-08-18, [source](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/international-business-machines-nyseibm-shares-up-17-should-you-buy-2026-08-18)):** Shares rose another 1.7% to $232.74 intraday; analyst sentiment stayed split — Citigroup cut its price target to $245 (from $255) on July 24 while keeping a Buy rating, and KeyCorp downgraded IBM to "sector weight" on June 23. Aggregate coverage: 15 Buy, 11 Hold, 1 Sell.
