## Summary
NPR reports that AI-linked stocks drove a sharp sell-off in the week of Nov. 16-20, 2026, with the Nasdaq down 10% and the S&P 500 and Dow in correction. The article argues that investors now fear an AI bubble could burst, aggravated by Fed rate hikes, a shift toward tougher AI regulation, and turmoil at [[Nvidia]]. Its central concern is that the tightly interlinked financing of AI companies could spread losses well beyond tech.

## Core Concepts
- [[Nasdaq]] fell 10% for the week, its worst performance since the COVID-19 lockdowns in March 2020.
- [[Anthropic]] gained 17% on its IPO debut, then slumped more than 8% on Friday to close near its IPO price.
- [[Federal Reserve]] raised interest rates in September for the first time in over three years, making debt-funded AI buildouts costlier.
- [[Nvidia]] CEO [[Jensen Huang]] resigned this month after being suspected of an affair with a woman believed to have spied for China.
- [[Vendor Financing]]: Nvidia owns shares in and lends money to AI companies that in turn buy its chips.
- [[CoreWeave]] borrows heavily to build data centers, and Nvidia is a major investor in it.
- [[AI Bubble]] fears are compared with the [[Dot-Com Bubble]] of 2000.
- [[Concentration Risk]]: AI stocks make up a substantial share of the [[S&P 500]] and sit in index funds, pensions and 401(k)s.

## Key Takeaways
- **Worst Week**: Nasdaq fell 10%, its worst week since the March 2020 lockdowns.
- **Correction**: S&P 500 and Dow entered correction, down 10% from recent highs.
- **Anthropic Slump**: IPO gained 17% on debut, then fell over 8% by Friday.
- **Rate Pressure**: September Fed hike makes AI companies' borrowing for data centers more expensive.
- **Nvidia Turmoil**: CEO resignation this month, though Nvidia still posted strong results.
- **Circular Financing**: Nvidia invests in and lends to customers that buy its chips.
- **Bubble Debate**: Critics compare the AI trade with the 2000 dot-com bust.
- **Broad Exposure**: AI stocks are held through index funds, pensions and 401(k)s.
- **Economic Stake**: AI investments helped drive 3.4% annual growth last quarter.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Supplier financing transmits customer losses back to the supplier** — When a supplier lends to its own customers, one customer's failure returns to the supplier as a loss. · *model:* [[Vendor Financing]]
→ explains: Circular Financing, Bubble Debate

**T2. Concentrated holdings turn one sector's fall into a market-wide loss** — When one sector dominates an index and the funds that hold it, its decline spreads to everyone who owns the index. · *model:* [[Concentration Risk]]
→ explains: Broad Exposure, Correction

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's central claim about the recent AI-stock sell-off?
**A**: The sell-off raises fears that the AI boom is a bubble that could burst, with consequences beyond the high-tech sector because AI is central to the broader economy.

**Q2**: What mechanism does the article identify as a contagion risk?
**A**: Nvidia owns stakes in and lends to AI companies that buy its chips, and CoreWeave borrows heavily, so trouble at one or two firms could start a chain reaction.

**Q3**: What is the implication for ordinary investors?
**A**: Many people hold AI stocks through index funds, pensions and 401(k)s, so an unwinding of the AI trade could ripple far beyond the high-tech world.
