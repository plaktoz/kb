## Summary

[[IBM]] trades near $234, about 29% below its June 2 record of $329.23, ahead of its Oct. 21 third-quarter report. The author argues the stock hinges on whether [[IBM Software Segment|software growth]] recovers from a Q2 slowdown to 5%, all of it acquisition-driven. At about 18 times 2027 earnings and a roughly 2.9% yield, he would consider buying only if software growth climbs toward the high single digits.

## Core Concepts

- **[[IBM]]**: Down about 21% in 2026 after a 25% one-day drop on July 14 when Q2 revenue missed its own expectations.
- **[[IBM Software Segment]]**: Largest segment by revenue and profit ($7.8B in Q2); growth slid from 14% to 11% to 5%, with [[Confluent]] supplying all of the latest 5%.
- **[[Recurring Revenue]]**: About 80% of software sales; grew 7% in Q1 and 8% in Q2, with the high-end case reaching about 10% in H2.
- **[[Red Hat]]**: Hybrid cloud business that grew 11%; data software grew 19% while transaction processing fell 8%.
- **[[Dividend Yield]]**: $6.76 a year, a 31st straight annual raise (by one cent), covered by roughly $14.7B of 2025 free cash flow.
- **[[Price-to-Earnings Ratio]]**: About 18x expected 2027 earnings versus about 25x at the June high.
- **[[Arvind Krishna]]** and **[[James Kavanaugh]]**: CEO and CFO who blamed timing: budgets shifted to hardware and large deals slipped.

## Key Takeaways

- **Stock drawdown**: Down 29% from the $329.23 high; down about 21% year to date.
- **Software slowdown**: Growth fell 14% to 11% to 5%; flat without acquisitions.
- **Management explanation**: Customers moved budgets to hardware; tens of large deals failed to close.
- **Guidance**: Software growth 6%-8% for 2026; Q3 total revenue growth 4%-5% versus 1% in Q2.
- **Deal recovery**: About a third of delayed deals closed in the first three weeks of Q3.
- **Valuation**: About 18x 2027 earnings versus 25x in June; yield near 2.9%.
- **Decision rule**: Software growth toward high single digits and recurring revenue near 10% would make IBM worth considering.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the author's central view on buying IBM before its Q3 report?
**A**: The decision depends on software growth: if it climbs toward the high single digits with recurring revenue near 10%, IBM is worth considering at about $234; if it resembles Q2, keep waiting.

**Q2**: Why is Q2 software growth of 5% a concern?
**A**: Without acquisitions such as Confluent it was flat, and growth had slowed from 14% in Q4 2025 and 11% in Q1 2026, while transaction processing fell 8%.

**Q3**: How does valuation and the dividend factor into the case?
**A**: The stock already prices in disappointment at about 18x 2027 earnings (versus 25x in June), and the roughly 2.9% yield is well covered, but it is modest pay for waiting on an unproven software recovery.
