## Summary

Wall Street rallied on August 4, 2026, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average closing at a record high, powered by optimism surrounding progress in Iran talks. Tech megacaps and high-growth names led the advance, while the VIX fell and gold continued to climb.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Dow Jones Industrial Average]]**: Closed at a record high, reflecting broad market confidence following geopolitical de-escalation signals from Iran negotiations.
- **[[US-Iran Conflict 2026]]**: Ongoing diplomatic talks between the US and Iran drove a positive risk-sentiment shift, reducing the geopolitical risk premium embedded in equities.
- **[[VIX]]**: Fell to 15.86 (-0.81%), signaling diminished near-term fear and elevated investor risk appetite.
- **[[Geopolitical Risk Premium]]**: Markets repriced lower as Iran talks progress reduced the probability of an escalation scenario, releasing pent-up buying pressure.

## Key Takeaways

- **Dow Jones closed at a record high**, with Dow Futures at 53,459 (+0.23%).
- **S&P Futures at 7,634.50** (+0.08%); Nasdaq Futures slightly negative at 28,870.75 (-0.07%).
- **CRWV surged +19.49%** and **SOFI +10.55%** — among the session's biggest movers.
- **NVDA +2.93%, AMZN +4.58%** — large-cap tech participated in the rally.
- **Gold at $4,112.20** (+0.53%), maintaining its safe-haven bid despite risk-on equity action.
- **VIX at 15.86**, below the 16 threshold often associated with elevated anxiety.

## Update (2026-08-05, additional sources: Investopedia, Yahoo Finance)

Earnings, not just Iran diplomacy, did much of the work on August 4: the S&P 500 and Dow both closed at fresh records (Nasdaq +2.6%, S&P 500 +1.8%, Dow +1.7%/~900 points) after [[Palantir]] soared nearly 30% — its best day in over a year — on what CEO Alex Karp called an "otherworldly" quarter, with US government commercial revenue up 90% year over year. [[Caterpillar]] surged about 10% after revenue topped $20 billion for the first time, with CEO Joe Creed citing "broadening momentum" from customers building AI data centers. The rally also lifted chip stocks broadly (PHLX Semiconductor Index +6%).

[[Amazon]] was a rare decliner (-2.5%) after a regulatory filing showed founder [[Jeff Bezos]] intended to sell 15 million shares worth over $4 billion — a pullback that came just a day after Amazon joined the $3 trillion market-cap club. Oil extended its slide after Treasury Secretary [[Scott Bessent]] said a deal to reopen the [[Strait of Hormuz]] could come "today or tomorrow"; WTI crude fell 5.8% to $75.70/barrel, and energy stocks (NRG Energy, Diamondback Energy) were among the day's worst performers even as the broader market rallied.

Separately, Philadelphia Fed president [[Anna Paulson]] said the same day that she supported the prior week's rate hold and is "keeping an open mind" on next steps, estimating underlying inflation (stripping out tariffs/energy) at 2.4%–2.8% — a reminder that the rally's macro backdrop (a Fed still undecided on its next move) hadn't fully resolved even as equities hit records.


## Update (2026-08-06, additional sources: Investor's Business Daily, Investopedia — August 5 session)

The rally continued into Wednesday, August 5, with the Dow extending its winning streak to five sessions and posting a third straight record close (+0.5%), even as the S&P 500 (-0.2%) and Nasdaq (-0.8%) ended lower, dragged by post-earnings drops in [[SpaceX]] (-12% to -14%) and [[AMD]] (-6.5% to -8%) despite both topping Wall Street estimates on revenue and EPS — AMD's own third-quarter guidance ($13B) still undershot the market's appetite, and SpaceX's high AI/space capex weighed on sentiment. [[Arista Networks]] surged 11%+ on an earnings beat and raised 2026 revenue growth guidance to 40% (from 28%), riding strong AI cloud-networking demand.

**Other August 5 earnings movers**: [[Shopify]] soared 17-28% on a "monster quarter" (34% revenue growth, GMV up 32% to $115.57B); [[Booking Holdings]] rose 6.5% despite Middle East-linked travel-demand volatility; [[Eli Lilly]] gained ~5%; [[Walt Disney]] gained ~3-4% on a profit beat and a new TikTok content partnership; [[Insulet]] plunged 20% on cut Omnipod revenue guidance; Pinterest, Uber, and CVS Health each declined.

**Alphabet's AI leadership reshuffle roiled Dow trading**: shares fell 3.5% to lead Dow decliners after Sundar Pichai announced Jeff Dean's departure (to found [[Discovery Loop]]) and Demis Hassabis's move to DeepMind Chair/Alphabet Chief Scientist — see [[Google DeepMind Leadership Shakeup]] for full detail. Despite the pullback, Alphabet remains up ~16% year-to-date.

**Nvidia rose ~3.5%** after Elon Musk said on SpaceX's first public-company earnings call that SpaceX will buy Nvidia chips "exclusively" going forward, citing the Vera Rubin architecture; Nvidia is up 17% YTD but still ~7% off its May highs.

Weaker-than-expected ADP private payrolls (+44,000 in July vs. +75,000 expected, down from +98,000 in June) were a soft spot in an otherwise risk-on session. Oil stayed roughly flat (WTI ~$75.15-75.60/barrel) as investors awaited a possible US-Iran deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. *(An additional CNBC recap of the prior day's August 4 session — Dow +907pts, Palantir +29%, Caterpillar earnings beat, Bezos's $4.1B Amazon share sale, Fed's Anna Paulson comments — largely duplicated ground already captured in the update above; no new facts added from that source.)*
