## Summary

[[Simply Wall St]] argues that [[IBM]] shares, at US$225.62 after a retreat driven by legal scrutiny and a 25% single-day drop on weak [[IBM Z]] performance, could be about 18% undervalued on a [[Discounted Cash Flow]] basis. Its model treats IBM as a mature cash generator, with free cash flow rising from about $13.1B (last twelve months) to roughly $19.1B by 2030, then growing at low single digits. Community narratives are split: the bull case says the stock is 6% undervalued because of recurring software and AI revenue, while the bear case says it is 35% overvalued because of execution risk.

## Core Concepts

- **[[IBM]]**: Enterprise technology company whose share price fell after an IBM Z shortfall and a related investigation, even though its 5-year total return is 101.7%.
- **[[Discounted Cash Flow]] (DCF)**: Projects the cash IBM could return to shareholders and discounts it back to today. The resulting [[Intrinsic Value]] is meaningfully above the current price.
- **[[Free Cash Flow]]**: The model's core input. Analyst forecasts lift annual FCF from ~$13.1B (LTM) toward ~$19.1B by 2030.
- **[[IBM Z]] shortfall**: Disappointing mainframe activity and the related investigation raise questions about future earnings quality, cash conversion, and when large hardware-driven cash inflows arrive.
- **[[Fair Value Narrative]]**: Simply Wall St's Community Narratives spell out the growth, margin, and earnings assumptions that would make the shares worth more or less than today's price. Each narrative is a thesis that can be revisited over time.
- **[[Hybrid Cloud]] and AI software**: The bull thesis. Software is nearly 45% of total revenue, and about 80% of that software revenue is recurring.
- **[[Insider Selling]]**: Simply Wall St's checks flagged recent insider trades, with specific sellers and transaction sizes left for the reader to review.

## Key Takeaways

- **Price**: US$225.62 after legal scrutiny and weak IBM Z results.
- **Headline valuation**: The DCF suggests IBM is about 18% undervalued.
- **Cash flow path**: ~$13.1B LTM FCF, rising to ~$19.1B by 2030.
- **Terminal assumption**: Low-single-digit growth after 2030, consistent with a mature cash generator.
- **Discount driver**: A 25% single-day drop tied to the IBM Z shortfall and investigation.
- **Long-run return**: The 5-year total shareholder return is 101.7%.
- **Bull narrative**: 6% undervalued; software is ~45% of revenue and ~80% recurring.
- **Bear narrative**: 35% overvalued; the risk is converting AI partnerships into sustained growth.
- **Open question**: Did the market overweight a single hardware cycle?

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Mr. Market]]**: The article asks whether the 25% one-day reaction to one weak IBM Z cycle reflects the long-term cash profile or just the market's mood. A large gap between price and cash-flow-implied value is exactly the kind of mispricing Mr. Market can offer.
- **[[Margin of Safety]]**: The fair-value estimates range from 18% undervalued (DCF) to 6% undervalued (bull) to 35% overvalued (bear). That range shows how much the conclusion depends on assumptions, so the size of any discount matters more than the point estimate.
