## Summary

Three AI-infrastructure stocks — [[Sandisk]] (SNDK), [[Micron Technology]] (MU), and [[Lumentum]] (LITE) — have surged 500%+ over the past year and may still have room to run. The gains are driven by structural supply constraints and surging AI demand across NAND flash memory, [[HBM (High Bandwidth Memory)]], and optical data-transmission components. Despite massive price appreciation, all three trade at historically low forward P/E valuations, suggesting the market may not yet be pricing in durable earnings growth.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Sandisk]]**: Pure-play NAND flash memory maker; benefited from a post-pandemic supply-demand imbalance as AI storage demand surged while capacity was reduced. Signed long-term supply deals worth a minimum of $42 billion and projects "mid-to-high teens" revenue growth through 2030.
- **[[Micron Technology]]**: Major US memory maker; 76% of revenue from [[DRAM]], with high-bandwidth memory ([[HBM (High Bandwidth Memory)|HBM]]) as the primary AI-driven growth engine. Structural supply constraint: HBM requires up to 3x the wafer capacity of regular DRAM, sustaining elevated prices.
- **[[Lumentum]]**: Maker of indium phosphide lasers for optical data transmission; holds up to 60% market share with 1,000+ patents. Poised to benefit as [[AI Data Centers]] shift away from copper wiring to optical interconnects.
- **[[NAND Flash Memory]]**: Non-volatile storage technology critical for AI data centers; Sandisk is a pure-play producer.
- **[[Forward P/E Ratio]]**: A valuation metric using projected earnings; all three stocks trade at unusually low multiples (Sandisk ~7x, Micron ~6x) relative to their growth rates.
- **[[AI Data Center Infrastructure]]**: The physical layer enabling AI compute — storage, memory, and networking — driving demand across all three companies.

## Key Takeaways

- **Sandisk up ~2,900%**: Benefited from NAND supply cuts meeting surging AI storage demand.
- **$42B in long-term deals**: Sandisk secured committed revenue; projects mid-to-high-teens growth through 2030.
- **Sandisk forward P/E ~7**: Extremely low for a company with locked-in multi-year growth visibility.
- **Micron up ~700%**: HBM demand the primary driver; 76% of revenue from DRAM.
- **HBM supply constraint**: Requires 3x wafer capacity vs. standard DRAM — limits supply, supports pricing.
- **Micron forward P/E ~6**: Arguably undervalued given structural HBM scarcity.
- **Lumentum up ~550%**: 60% market share and 1,000+ patents in indium phosphide lasers.
- **Optical shift underway**: AI data centers moving from copper to optical — durable tailwind for Lumentum.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Supply Constraint Moat]]**: When supply is structurally limited — HBM needing 3x the wafer area, or optical laser manufacturing requiring rare expertise — pricing power accrues to producers independent of demand cycles, making valuation multiples misleading if based on cyclical earnings.
- **[[Picks and Shovels Investing]]**: Rather than betting on which AI model wins, owning the infrastructure layer (memory, optical interconnects) captures value regardless of which AI application succeeds — these companies sell the foundational hardware all AI workloads depend on.
