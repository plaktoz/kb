## Summary

August CPI data released September 11, 2026 showed headline inflation at 3.4% year-over-year (in line with estimates) and core CPI beating forecasts at 0.3% month-over-month, pushing [[CME FedWatch]] September rate-hike probability from ~72% to ~87%. Despite rate hikes being broadly negative for equities, the Dow surged over 500 points on the day. The paradox may be explained by bond market dynamics — investors interpreted a likely hike as the Fed finally committing to its job, which could cap long-term yields and support equity valuations.

## Core Concepts

- [[Consumer Price Index]] (CPI) — the primary government measure of consumer-level inflation; headline and core variants tracked monthly.
- [[Core CPI]] — CPI excluding food and energy; August print of 0.3% MoM came in 0.1 above consensus.
- [[CME FedWatch Tool]] — market-implied probability gauge for [[Federal Reserve]] rate decisions derived from fed funds futures.
- [[FOMC]] — the Fed's policy-setting committee; three voting members had already dissented in favor of a hike at the prior meeting.
- [[10-Year Treasury Yield]] — sitting near 4.95% at the time; serves as a benchmark for long-term borrowing costs and equity discount rates.
- [[30-Year Treasury Yield]] — approximately 5.34%, reflecting long-end pressure from persistent inflation expectations.
- [[Kevin Warsh]] — incoming Fed Chair navigating a hawkish FOMC with growing dissent.

## Key Takeaways

- **Headline CPI**: +0.4% MoM, +3.4% YoY — matched expectations.
- **Core CPI**: +0.3% MoM — beat forecasts by 0.1 percentage point.
- **Rate hike odds**: jumped from ~72% to ~87% following the print.
- **Dow reaction**: surged 500+ points despite hawkish data.
- **Bond market silver lining**: a hike may signal Fed credibility, easing long-term yield pressure.
- **FOMC dissent**: three members already voted for an immediate hike at the prior meeting.
- **10-year yield**: ~4.95%; 30-year yield: ~5.34%.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Reflexivity]]**: The stock market rally on hot inflation data inverts the intuitive rate-hike negative — bond investors price in reduced long-term yield risk if the Fed acts decisively, making equities relatively more attractive even as short-term rates rise.
