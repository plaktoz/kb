## Summary

Wall Street earned $45.9 billion in first-half 2026 profits, up 51% year over year, per the New York State Comptroller's securities industry report, putting the industry on pace for a record $90 billion-plus year. The boom is driven by AI investment, volatile-market trading, and an IPO and M&A pickup aided by a deregulatory Trump administration. Comptroller Thomas DiNapoli warns that the 10-year Treasury yield hitting 5.35% — the highest since 2002 — could dampen dealmaking, trading returns, and the consumer, exposing the industry and New York's public finances to a downturn.

## Core Concepts

- **[[New York State Comptroller]] securities industry report**: Authored by [[Thomas P. DiNapoli]], tracks profits, pay, and tax contributions of the NYC securities industry.
- **[[AI Boom]] as profit driver**: AI venture spending and AI-linked IPOs fuel trading, underwriting, and advisory fees.
- **[[IPO Market]] and [[Mergers and Acquisitions]]**: Underwriting revenue rose sharply; the expected [[Anthropic IPO]] is cited as another fee opportunity.
- **[[10-Year Treasury Yield]]**: Benchmark that sets economy-wide rates; its surge threatens borrowing costs, stocks, and deal flow.
- **[[Deregulation]] under the [[Trump Administration]]**: Business-friendly policy credited with aiding Wall Street's fortunes, but also flagged as a risk.
- **Regional fiscal exposure**: Securities industry tax collections underpin [[New York City]] and State budgets.

## Key Takeaways

- **H1 profits**: $45.9B, up 51% year over year.
- **Record pace**: Full-year 2026 could exceed $90B, beating the inflation-adjusted 2009 record.
- **Forecast blown**: May forecast of $45.3B for the full year already surpassed.
- **AI venture spending**: $407B in H1 2026 vs. $264B for all of 2025.
- **Underwriting revenue**: Up 68% year over year in H1 2026.
- **Anthropic IPO**: Potential ~$2T valuation offers banks major underwriting and trading fees.
- **Pay**: 2025 average securities salary rose 11.1% to $561,770.
- **Bonuses**: 2025 bonus pool $49.2B; average bonus $246,900, up 6%.
- **Tax contribution**: Industry's share of NYC tax collections rose 15.8% in FY2026.
- **Yield risk**: 10-year yield hit 5.35% Monday, highest since 2002.
- **Named risks**: Geopolitics, inflation, rates, AI concentration, and deregulation.
- **Macro contrast**: Wall Street booms while consumer sentiment sits near historic lows.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Concentration Risk]]**: DiNapoli flags the "outsized contributions" of AI — profits, and the tax revenue built on them, lean on one theme, so fading AI enthusiasm would hit the industry and public finances together.
- **[[Procyclicality]]**: Wall Street fees depend on deal volume and market activity, which rising rates suppress, so the same cycle that drove record profits can reverse them.
