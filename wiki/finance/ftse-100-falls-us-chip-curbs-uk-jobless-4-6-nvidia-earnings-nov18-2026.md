## Summary
British stocks extended their fall on Tuesday as new U.S. curbs on AI chip shipments and caution ahead of Nvidia's earnings outweighed an unexpected drop in UK unemployment to 4.6%. The FTSE 100 fell 1.8%, the DAX 2% and the CAC 40 1.6%. Jefferies called the unwind of AI-related trades the key market driver.

## Core Concepts
- **[[FTSE 100]]**: Fell 1.8%, alongside the [[DAX]] (-2%) and [[CAC 40]] (-1.6%).
- **[[US AI Chip Export Controls]]**: A rule requiring licences for advanced chip shipments to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil, exempting U.S.-run data centres.
- **[[Nvidia]]**: Reports Q3 results after the close, its first since [[Jensen Huang]]'s departure, with interim CEO [[Colette Kress]] under scrutiny.
- **[[Circular Financing]]**: Investors want to know whether Nvidia will continue financing arrangements with AI customers.
- **[[UK Unemployment]]** and the **[[Bank of England]]**: Jobless rate fell to 4.6%; an 80% chance of a December hike is priced in.
- **[[US-China Trade Tensions]]**: Beijing vowed "necessary measures"; Trump is due to meet [[Xi Jinping]] on Thursday.
- **[[Commodities]]**: Brent $93.18, WTI $84.96, gold futures $3,872.86.

## Key Takeaways
- **Market Moves**: FTSE 100 down 1.8%, DAX down 2%, CAC 40 down 1.6%, sterling $1.3225.
- **Chip Rule**: Licences now required for advanced chips to five countries; Beijing calls it economic coercion.
- **AI Trade Unwind**: Jefferies sees the AI trade unwind as the key driver, and a buying opportunity.
- **UK Jobs**: Unemployment fell to 4.6% against 4.7% expected, lowest since April 2025.
- **Labour Weakness**: Payrolls fell 34,000; vacancies hit 690,000, lowest since 2014 outside the pandemic.
- **BoE Outlook**: Data unlikely to shift tightening plans; UK inflation data due Wednesday.
- **Nvidia Test**: First earnings since Huang's exit; analysts flag U.S.-China and chip-access risks.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What drove the FTSE 100's decline on Tuesday?
**A**: New U.S. curbs on China's access to AI chips and caution ahead of Nvidia's earnings weighed on British stocks, despite a surprise drop in UK unemployment to 4.6%.

**Q2**: What did the UK labour data show beneath the headline jobless rate?
**A**: Payrolls fell by 34,000 and vacancies slid to 690,000, the lowest since 2014 outside the pandemic, suggesting firms would cut hiring. An 80% chance of a December BoE hike is still priced in.

**Q3**: What should investors watch from Nvidia's earnings?
**A**: Clarity from interim CEO Colette Kress on strategy and whether Nvidia will continue circular financing with AI customers. Analysts flag U.S.-China tensions, national security probes and chip-access limits as key risks.
