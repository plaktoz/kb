## Summary

The [[Dow Jones Industrial Average]] fell 464 points (0.85%) on August 6, 2026, ending a six-day winning streak as [[Salesforce]], [[Boeing]], and [[UnitedHealth]] dragged the index lower, while the Nasdaq finished roughly flat. Oil rose on optimism over a draft [[Strait of Hormuz]] reopening deal between Iran and Oman, and [[SpaceX]] shares rose 6–3% even as a 911-million-share post-IPO [[lockup expiration]] added new selling pressure.

## Core Concepts

- [[Lockup Expiration]] — the post-IPO date when restricted shares (here, ~911 million SpaceX shares, ~$98B at recent prices) become eligible to trade, typically pressuring the stock as early investors can finally sell
- [[Strait of Hormuz]] — the shipping chokepoint at the center of Iran-Oman negotiations that moved oil prices during the session
- [[Winning Streak Reversal]] — the Dow's six-session, three-record-close run ended by earnings-driven declines in individual components rather than a broad macro shock
- [[Memory Chip Earnings Miss]] — Sandisk and Western Digital both reported strong growth but missed elevated investor expectations, dragging the Nasdaq

## Key Takeaways

- **Dow**: fell 464 points (0.85%), breaking a six-session winning streak and three straight record closes.
- **SpaceX**: rose 3–6% on lockup day despite ~$98B of newly tradable stock, after plunging nearly 14% the prior day on its first public earnings report; shares remained ~20% below IPO price and 52% below their post-IPO peak.
- **Oil**: Brent crude rose 3.8% to $82.49/barrel on hopes for a Strait of Hormuz reopening agreement between Iran and Oman.
- **Nasdaq**: finished about flat, weighed down by Sandisk (-15%) and Western Digital (-11%) despite both posting strong underlying growth.
- **Magnificent Seven**: mixed — all pointed higher pre-market the next day except Microsoft, after Nvidia led gains (+3.5%) and Alphabet led declines (-4%) the prior session.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did the Dow break its winning streak on August 6, 2026, despite a broadly positive open?
**A**: Declines in individual components — Salesforce, Boeing, and UnitedHealth — dragged the index down 464 points even though it had opened higher on track for a longer streak.

**Q2**: Why did SpaceX stock rise even as hundreds of millions of shares became newly tradable?
**A**: The lockup expiration added supply, but the stock showed resilience and rose 3–6% anyway, suggesting demand absorbed the new float better than feared — even though shares remained well below their IPO and post-IPO peak levels.

**Q3**: What was driving oil prices higher that day?
**A**: Investor optimism that Iran and Oman were finalizing a draft agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a key global shipping chokepoint.
