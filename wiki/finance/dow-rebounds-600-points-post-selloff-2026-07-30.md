## Summary

The [[Dow Jones Industrial Average]] climbed 614 points (1.2%) on July 30, 2026 in a "snap back" day following the sharp Fed-day selloff, with the [[Nasdaq Composite]] leading at +2.8% and the [[S&P 500]] up 1.7%. However, the rally was heavily concentrated in tech: the S&P 500 ex-Information Technology was negative, and nearly two-thirds of S&P 500 components declined on the day. [[Microsoft]] had its best single-day performance since 2008, masking broad weakness across the Dow and broader market.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Market Rebound]] mechanics**: Index-level gains driven almost entirely by [[Technology Sector]] weightings, masking deterioration in breadth
- **[[Equal-Weight S&P 500]]**: A breadth indicator; its decline while cap-weighted S&P 500 rose signals narrow, concentrated buying
- **[[Fed Day Volatility]]**: Pattern of exaggerated moves on FOMC days followed by snapback the next session
- **[[Microsoft]] (MSFT)**: Best single-day gain since 2008, post-earnings rally offsetting Dow laggards
- **[[Memory Stocks]]**: Surged; [[Sandisk]] led S&P 500 gainers on the session
- **[[Japanese Yen]]**: Sharp spike raised currency-intervention concerns, a cross-market risk signal
- **[[Amazon]] (AMZN)**: Shares rose ahead of earnings; market watching whether it would follow [[Meta]] or Microsoft's earnings trajectory

## Key Takeaways

- **Narrow Rally**: S&P 500 ex-Tech was down; ~2/3 of S&P 500 components declined.
- **Snapback Pattern**: Carson Group's Ryan Detrick called it a "typical snap back day after an exaggerated Fed Day move."
- **Microsoft Surge**: MSFT posted best day since 2008, providing outsized Dow support.
- **Memory Stocks Outperform**: Sandisk topped S&P 500 gainers; memory sector surged.
- **Yen Risk**: Spike in Japanese yen raised currency-intervention worries.
- **Amazon Watch**: Pre-earnings rally; investors comparing to MSFT/Meta outcomes.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Index Illusion]]**: Cap-weighted index gains can mask broad market weakness — the headline number hides that most stocks fell, a recurring pattern when mega-cap tech diverges from the rest of the market.
