## Summary
[[Alphabet]]'s $514 billion [[Cloud Backlog]] is a promise of future revenue, not revenue itself. Ahead of Q3 earnings on October 28, the author argues cloud margins, capex and free cash flow, and Search growth matter more than the backlog number. The long-term case rests on profitability, diversification, and heavy investment in AI demand.

## Core Concepts
- [[Alphabet]]: Reports Q3 earnings after the close on October 28.
- [[Cloud Backlog]]: $514B of signed contracts not yet recognized; a little over half expected within 24 months.
- [[Google Cloud]]: Q2 revenue up 82% to $24B; operating income above $8.8B; margin above 35% versus about 20%.
- [[Tensor Processing Unit]]: Custom AI chip sales are included in the backlog, with most revenue expected in 2027.
- [[AI Infrastructure Capex]]: Nearly $45B in the latest quarter; 2026 forecast raised to $195B-$205B.
- [[Free Cash Flow]]: Negative $5.9B in Q2, against trailing 12-month operating cash flow of $185.7B.
- [[Google Search]]: AI features are driving usage; Search and other ads brought in $63B in Q2, up 17%.
- [[Sundar Pichai]]: Said Alphabet has "gotten more bullish on the opportunities ahead."

## Key Takeaways
- **Backlog Caveat**: A backlog is a promise, not recognized revenue.
- **Cloud Margins**: Third-party capacity may cause slight margin pressure in Q3.
- **Capex Load**: 2026 capex guidance of $195B-$205B, with higher spending next year.
- **Cash Flow**: Free cash flow was negative $5.9B; investors want capex-to-revenue proof.
- **Search Watch**: Continued AI-driven Search usage growth is a key figure.
- **Ad Engine**: Advertising cash flow funds the AI build-out and diversifies risk.
- **Downside Case**: Margin slips or revenue misses could outweigh a large backlog.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Contracted demand is not realized revenue** — A backlog only becomes earnings when it is delivered and recognized, so the conversion rate and margin matter more than the headline size.
→ explains: Backlog Caveat, Cloud Margins, Downside Case

**T2. Heavy investment must show a path to returns** — When spending outruns internal cash generation, investors judge the build-out by whether capex turns into revenue.
→ explains: Capex Load, Cash Flow, Search Watch
