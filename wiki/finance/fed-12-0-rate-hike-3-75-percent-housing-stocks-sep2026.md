## Summary

The [[FOMC]] unanimously voted 12-0 to raise the [[Federal Funds Rate]] by a quarter point to 3.75%–4%, the first rate hike in three years. [[Fed Chair Kevin Warsh]] struck a hawkish tone, citing persistently high inflation, with most members projecting at least one more hike in 2026 and no rate cuts until 2028 or later. The housing sector faces mounting pressure, with mortgage applications down 19% year-over-year and rates approaching 7%.

## Core Concepts

- [[FOMC]] — Federal Open Market Committee; voted unanimously 12-0 to raise rates, signaling broad consensus on the need for further tightening.
- [[Federal Funds Rate]] — now at 3.75%–4%, the first increase in three years; serves as the benchmark for borrowing costs across the economy.
- [[Kevin Warsh]] — [[Fed Chair]] who stated "inflation is too high, and has been for too long," noting that summer inflation data showed no meaningful improvement.
- [[Mortgage Rate]] — approaching 7%, suppressing home purchase and refinancing activity.
- [[Mortgage Applications]] — down 19% year-over-year, a direct indicator of housing market demand destruction.
- [[Rocket Companies]] — mortgage originator cited as a higher-quality pick for investors seeking limited housing exposure ahead of a potential rate reversal.
- [[Home Depot]] (HD) — home improvement retailer also recommended as a higher-quality name given its resilience relative to more rate-sensitive peers.
- [[Rate Cycle Reversal]] — easing geopolitical tensions or economic slowdown flagged as conditions that could eventually prompt rate reductions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Unanimous hike**: FOMC voted 12-0 to raise rates to 3.75%–4%, first hike in three years.
- **More hikes ahead**: Most members expect at least one additional hike in 2026.
- **Rate cuts delayed**: Majority of FOMC members don't foresee cuts until 2028 or later.
- **Hawkish chair**: Warsh cited persistently high inflation with no summer improvement.
- **Housing pain**: Mortgage applications fell 19% YoY; rates approaching 7%.
- **Dual squeeze**: Higher rates hurt both home purchases and refinancing simultaneously.
- **Stock positioning**: Higher-quality names (Rocket Companies, Home Depot) preferred over more rate-sensitive peers.
- **Reversal conditions**: Geopolitical easing or economic slowdown could be the catalyst for cuts.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Long and Variable Lags]]**: The 19% drop in mortgage applications illustrates Friedman's principle — monetary tightening from prior hikes is still working through the housing market, and this latest hike adds further delayed pressure on top of that.
- **[[Second-Order Thinking]]**: Investors focused only on mortgage originators miss the second-order hit to home improvement retailers and consumer spending that follows a sustained housing slowdown — a reason the analysis extends to [[Home Depot]] alongside [[Rocket Companies]].
