## Summary

Crude oil hit a six-month high, with December WTI up 4.46% to $103.44 and Brent up 6.24% on the week to about $116.93, as Houthi attacks and Saudi-Houthi fighting intensified. [[Goldman Sachs]]' Daan Struyven said refiners see a "high probability of a sharp drop in supply" and are buying precautionarily with inventories low, while investors buy oil as a hedge. Goldman's base case is still Brent at $90 by year-end and $80 average in 2027, with upside risks to forecasts.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Crude Oil]]**: WTI and Brent futures at six-month highs; USO and UCO ETFs up about 4%.
- **[[Houthi Attacks]]**: Iran-aligned Houthis escalating shipping attacks and infrastructure strikes; Saudi Aramco evacuated its Dhahran headquarters.
- **[[Daan Struyven]]**: Goldman Sachs head of Oil Research who explains physical and financial buying.
- **[[Precautionary Buying]]**: Refiners securing barrels while inventories are low.
- **[[Risk Premium]]**: A more persistent premium is a key upside risk to Goldman's forecasts.
- **[[Refined Product Exports]]**: Gulf diesel exports at about 40% of 2025 average versus crude at about 110%.
- **[[Hedging]]**: Investors buy oil because bonds and equities have become unusually sensitive to crude.

## Key Takeaways

- **Price Surge**: WTI rose 4.46% to $103.44 after a 7.81% gain the prior week.
- **Brent Move**: Brent up 6.24% this week to about $116.93.
- **Refiner Behavior**: Refiners buy more crude, expecting a "high probability of a sharp drop in supply."
- **Financial Hedging**: Investors hold oil as a hedge against higher energy prices.
- **Goldman Forecast**: Base case Brent $90 end-2026, $80 average 2027; risks skewed upward.
- **Product Disparity**: Persian Gulf diesel exports at 40% of 2025 average; crude at 110%.
- **Beyond Middle East**: Underinvestment and Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian refineries tighten diesel supply.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Expected supply shocks pull demand forward when buffers are thin** — When inventories are low and a disruption looks likely, buyers secure supply early, which bids prices up before any physical shortfall occurs. · *model:* [[Supply and Demand]]
→ explains: Refiner Behavior, Price Surge, Brent Move

**T2. Assets sensitive to a risk factor become hedges against it** — When bonds and equities react strongly to oil, investors buy oil itself to offset exposure, adding financial demand on top of physical demand.
→ explains: Financial Hedging

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: According to Goldman's Struyven, why have crude prices risen to a six-month high?
**A**: Growing Middle East tensions and downside risks to supply led physical buyers and investors to bid up barrels. Refiners see a "high probability of a sharp drop in supply" amid Houthi attacks.

**Q2**: What data point shows refined products are tighter than crude?
**A**: Persian Gulf crude exports ran at about 110% of 2025 average levels, but refined-product exports like diesel were only about 40%, partly due to refinery damage and harder ship-to-ship transfers.

**Q3**: What are Goldman's forecasts, and what could push prices above them?
**A**: The base case is Brent at $90 at end-2026 and $80 average in 2027. Upside risks include intensified shipping attacks, damaged oil infrastructure, and a more persistent risk premium.
