## Summary
AI stocks rebounded Friday after a two-day worldwide sell-off tied to Democratic midterm wins and fears of slower data center construction, leaving the [[S&P 500]] down 2.7% for the week, its worst since April 2025. Strong jobs data supported Treasury yields and raised the odds of a December [[Federal Reserve]] rate hike. Split control of Washington may limit federal efforts to rein in AI.

## Core Concepts
- [[Nvidia]]: the face of the AI boom and possible bust, up 2.8% after drops of 9.3% and 3.2%.
- [[AI Data Center Build-Out]]: Democratic statehouse gains and voter dislike of power-bill increases threaten construction, particularly in Texas.
- [[Federal Reserve]] rate-hike odds: strong hiring raised December hike probability per CME Group data.
- [[10-Year Treasury Yield]]: rose to 4.86% from 4.84%, after topping 5.30% last month.
- [[AI Bubble]]: critics had said AI stock prices had shot too high before the sell-off.
- [[Inflation Expectations]]: University of Michigan survey showed expectations for the coming year easing.

## Key Takeaways
- **Weekly Loss**: S&P 500 fell 2.7% for the week, its worst since April 2025.
- **Global Contagion**: Tokyo and Seoul indexes dropped 6%; Germany's DAX lost 1.9%.
- **Rebound Leaders**: IREN jumped 20%, Lam Research 8.2% on a $25 billion buyback, CoreWeave 6.3%.
- **Policy Risk**: Split Congress and Trump veto power could limit federal AI curbs.
- **Index Levels**: S&P 500 closed 7,376.86, only 5.7% below its all-time high.
- **Jobs Dilemma**: Strong hiring may cushion an AI slowdown but could push inflation higher.
- **Non-AI Losers**: Axon fell 18.2% and Charter Communications sank 6.2% on weak quarterly results.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What drove the worldwide AI stock sell-off this week?
**A**: Democratic midterm victories raised fears that data center construction would slow, on top of expensive borrowing costs and earlier bubble worries. Voters across parties say they dislike power-bill increases from data centers.

**Q2**: What mixed signals did the Friday jobs report send?
**A**: Employers hired many more workers than expected, suggesting the economy could absorb slower data center construction. But it also raised the odds of a December Fed rate hike by pushing upward on inflation.

**Q3**: Why might the sell-off not translate into strict federal AI regulation?
**A**: Political power remains split between a Democratic Congress and a Republican White House, and Trump retains veto power. That could limit federal efforts to rein in the AI industry.
