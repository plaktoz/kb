## Summary

[[IBM]] stock fell about 22% in Q3 2026, its worst quarter since late 2018, and in 11 of 13 prior quarterly drops of 18% or more since 1962 the stock was higher a year later (median gain ~18%). However, those rebounds almost always tracked a rising [[S&P 500]], which makes the historical record a weak guide to IBM's own business. Because the Q1 2026 drop has failed to follow the pattern despite a strong market, the author argues a rebound now depends on IBM's [[IBM Software Segment|software growth]] turning back up.

## Core Concepts

- **[[IBM]] Q3 2026 decline** — shares finished Q3 around $220, down ~22%; most of the loss came on July 14, when the stock sank 25% (its worst day on record) after preliminary Q2 results that CEO [[Arvind Krishna]] called "disappointing."
- **Historical drawdown study** — using daily closes back to 1962, the author finds 13 prior quarters with 18%+ declines; shares were higher a year later in 11, with a median one-year gain of ~18%.
- **Higher vs. recovered** — in only 3 of the 13 cases (late 2000, Q3 2002, late 2008) was the stock above the rough quarter's starting level a year later.
- **Market dependence** — the two exceptions (Q3 1973, Q3 2001) occurred in [[Bear Market|bear markets]]; in all nine cases where the [[S&P 500]] rose the following year, IBM also rose, including after IBM's record $5.5B loss in Q4 1992.
- **Q1 2026 anomaly** — an 18% drop that included a 13% fall on Feb. 23 after [[Anthropic]] said [[Claude Code]] could speed up [[COBOL]] modernization; the stock hit a record $329.23 on June 2, then fell back to ~7% below the Q1 close while the S&P 500 rose ~17%.
- **[[IBM Software Segment]] slowdown** — software revenue growth decelerated from 14% (Q4 2025) to 11% (Q1 2026) to 5% (Q2 2026); total Q2 revenue rose just 1%.
- **Guidance cut** — full-year constant-currency revenue growth forecast lowered to 4%–5%, from more than 5% in April.
- **Valuation** — about 17x 2027 expected earnings, roughly 30% below the [[Price-to-Earnings Ratio|P/E]] at June's record high on the same estimates.
- **Catalyst** — Q3 report scheduled for Oct. 21, per IBM's investor relations site.

## Key Takeaways

- **Worst quarter since 2018**: IBM lost ~22% in Q3 2026; late 2018 saw 25%.
- **Record single-day drop**: July 14 fall of 25% exceeded the ~24% Oct. 19, 1987 plunge.
- **Base rate**: 11 of 13 prior 18%+ quarterly drops were higher a year later.
- **Median rebound**: ~18% one-year gain across all 13 cases.
- **Big rebounds**: +55% after Q4 2008 drop; +18% in 2019 after late-2018 drop.
- **Rarely fully recovered**: Only 3 of 13 cases exceeded the pre-drop level within a year.
- **Market-linked**: IBM rose in all 9 cases where the S&P 500 rose.
- **Down markets split**: IBM gained in 2 of 4 falling-market cases, fell in 2.
- **Pattern broken in 2026**: Q1 drop still below its close despite S&P 500 up ~17%.
- **Software deceleration**: 14% → 11% → 5% growth over three quarters.
- **Author's condition**: Wants software growth to rebound before buying shares.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Reference Class Forecasting]]**: The 13 prior drawdowns form a reference class with an attractive base rate, but the author questions whether 2026 belongs in it, since the trouble is company-specific rather than market-driven.
- **[[Confounding Variable]]**: IBM's apparent post-drop "rebound tendency" is largely explained by the market rising afterward, so the pattern reflects market beta more than anything about IBM's business resilience.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What does history say about IBM stock after quarterly drops of 18% or more, and why is that record of limited use?
**A**: In 11 of 13 such cases since 1962 the stock was higher a year later, with a median gain of ~18%, but the rebounds mostly coincided with a rising S&P 500, so they say little about IBM's own business.

**Q2**: What evidence suggests the 2026 situation may not fit the historical pattern?
**A**: After the 18% Q1 2026 drop, IBM trades ~7% below the Q1 close while the S&P 500 is up ~17%, and software growth has slowed from 14% to 11% to 5% over three quarters.

**Q3**: What would the author need to see before buying IBM shares?
**A**: Software growth turning back up, ideally shown in the Oct. 21 Q3 report, since a rising market alone has proven insufficient when the problem is IBM's own.
