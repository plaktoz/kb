## Summary

On August 25, 2026, markets opened higher as declining oil prices and easing Treasury yields lifted sentiment despite ongoing US-Iran and US-Canada geopolitical tensions. [[XLK]] tech stocks rebounded from Monday losses, with broad gains across semiconductors, software, and hardware. Softening inflation fears drove a sector rotation out of [[Consumer Staples]] (XLP) and into growth-oriented technology names.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Sector Rotation]]**: Capital moved from defensive [[Consumer Staples]] (XLP) into offensive [[Technology]] (XLK), a classic risk-on shift.
- **[[Inflation Expectations]]**: Easing inflation jitters reduced the appeal of inflation-resistant staples and made higher-multiple growth stocks more attractive.
- **[[Treasury Yields]]**: Declining yields lower the discount rate applied to future earnings, directly boosting valuations for growth/tech companies.
- **[[Nvidia]] (NVDA)**: Rose ~2%, potentially ending a seven-day losing streak ahead of its upcoming earnings report — a closely watched sentiment barometer for the [[AI Trade]].
- Notable stocks in focus: [[Sandisk]] (SNDK), [[Marvell]] (MRVL), [[Intel]] (INTC), [[SK Hynix]] (000660.KS), [[AMD]], [[Bloom Energy]] (BE), [[Lam Research]] (LRCX).

## Key Takeaways

- **Tech Rebound**: XLK gained broadly after Monday's losses across semis, software, hardware.
- **Staples Sold**: XLP faced selling pressure — reversing the prior day's defensive bid.
- **Inflation Driver**: Easing inflation concerns directly catalyzed the rotation into growth.
- **Nvidia Momentum**: NVDA up ~2%, potentially snapping a 7-day losing streak pre-earnings.
- **Geopolitical Backdrop**: US-Iran and US-Canada tensions persisted but did not derail risk appetite.
- **Oil & Yields**: Both declined, providing a macro tailwind for equities.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Risk-On / Risk-Off]]**: Lower inflation expectations reduce the probability of further rate hikes, shifting the risk calculus toward growth assets — a textbook risk-on rotation dynamic.
- **[[Discount Rate Effect]]**: Falling Treasury yields mechanically increase the present value of distant tech earnings, explaining why growth outperforms defensives when yields drop.
