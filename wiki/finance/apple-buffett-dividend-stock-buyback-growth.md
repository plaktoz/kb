## Summary

[[Apple]] (AAPL) is worth considering as a dividend stock despite its low 0.33% yield, because the payout consumes only 12% of 2026 earnings estimates and analysts project ~13% average annual earnings growth over the next three to five years. [[Warren Buffett]]'s [[Berkshire Hathaway]] holds Apple as its largest position, anchored in the company's brand loyalty and sticky hardware-plus-services ecosystem. An aggressive buyback program — over $82 billion in the past four quarters — compounds per-share dividend growth by steadily reducing the share count.

## Core Concepts

- [[Apple]] — mega-cap consumer tech company; Berkshire Hathaway's largest equity holding
- [[Warren Buffett]] / [[Berkshire Hathaway]] — value investor holding Apple as top position due to ecosystem moat
- [[Dividend Growth Investing]] — strategy focused on low-yield, high-growth payers rather than high current yield
- [[Share Buybacks]] — Apple's $82B+ annual repurchase program reduces share count, amplifying per-share earnings and future dividend capacity
- [[Payout Ratio]] — Apple's dividend is only 12% of 2026 earnings estimates, leaving substantial headroom
- [[Ecosystem Lock-in]] — hardware-plus-services flywheel that sustains pricing power and recurring revenue

## Key Takeaways

- **Low but growing yield**: AAPL yields ~0.33%; payout ratio is just 12% of 2026 EPS estimates
- **EPS growth runway**: Analysts project ~13% average annual earnings growth over 3–5 years
- **Buybacks as dividend multiplier**: $82B+ repurchased in past four quarters; share count down 31.5% over a decade
- **Indirect dividend benefit**: Fewer shares outstanding → higher per-share earnings → faster dividend growth
- **Consumer base expansion**: Entry-level laptop and new leasing programs extend ecosystem reach
- **Buffett conviction**: Berkshire holds Apple as its largest position, anchored in brand loyalty and sticky ecosystem

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Compounding]]**: Apple's buybacks reduce the denominator (shares outstanding) over time, so even a flat dividend dollar amount translates into rising per-share purchasing power — a form of compounding that is invisible in the headline yield figure.
- **[[Second-Order Thinking]]**: Dismissing Apple as a dividend stock based solely on its 0.33% yield ignores the first- and second-order effects of buybacks on future dividend-per-share growth — the surface metric obscures the deeper dynamic.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does the article argue Apple qualifies as a dividend stock despite its 0.33% yield?
**A**: The dividend consumes only 12% of 2026 earnings estimates and the company is projected to grow EPS ~13% annually, creating substantial room for dividend growth.

**Q2**: How do Apple's share buybacks indirectly boost the dividend's value to investors?
**A**: By spending $82B+ per year on repurchases and cutting the share count by 31.5% over a decade, Apple raises per-share earnings, which lets the company grow its dividend per share faster than the raw payout ratio implies.

**Q3**: What strategic moves is Apple making to sustain its ecosystem expansion?
**A**: Apple is launching an entry-level laptop and new leasing programs to broaden its consumer base and extend ecosystem lock-in.
