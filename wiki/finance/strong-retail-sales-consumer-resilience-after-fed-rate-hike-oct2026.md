## Summary
September retail sales beat expectations at +0.8%, but gasoline price increases drove much of the gain while underlying spending, including the control group, slowed sharply. Hot September CPI and PPI strengthen the case for another [[Federal Reserve]] rate hike, even as jobless claims stay near 199,000. A tech-led equity sell-off and a flight to Treasuries flattened the yield curve, while markets still price three more 25-basis-point hikes on a slower path.

## Core Concepts
- [[Retail Sales]]: +0.8% MoM (consensus +0.3%) and +6.3% YoY; gasoline stations +6.4% MoM contributed 0.55 percentage points.
- [[Control Group Retail Sales]]: +0.1% in September versus +1.2% in August (consensus +0.3%); feeds into GDP calculations.
- [[Consumer Price Index]] (CPI): +0.6% MoM, 3.7% YoY; energy +5.2%, gasoline +9.1%; core CPI +0.3% MoM, 2.6% YoY.
- [[Producer Price Index]] (PPI): +1.3% MoM (largest since March 2022), 6.3% YoY; core PPI +0.7% MoM.
- [[Jobless Claims]]: initial claims 199,000, below year-ago levels for the 35th consecutive week; continuing claims 1.710 million.
- [[Federal Reserve Rate Hikes]]: markets expect three more 25 bp hikes (December 2026, March 2027, and the third moved from June to September 2027).
- [[Treasury Yield Curve]]: 2-year 4.74%, 5-year 4.89%, 10-year 5.09% for the week ending Oct. 16, flattening on a flight to safety.
- [[Oil Prices]]: Brent $109.86 (highest close since May 19); WTI $101.41 after rising 10.4%; drivers include Strait of Hormuz disruption and Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries.

## Key Takeaways
- **Headline vs Underlying**: Retail sales ex-gasoline rose only 0.2%; control group just 0.1%.
- **Consumer Momentum**: Spending is normalizing after August's 1.3% jump, not collapsing.
- **Hot CPI**: Headline 3.7% YoY, up from 3.4%; core goods biggest gain since January 2025.
- **Wholesale Shock**: PPI up 1.3% MoM versus 0.6% expected; core PPI 5.0% YoY.
- **Labor Resilience**: Claims at 199,000 signal employers are holding workers.
- **Equity Sell-off**: Nasdaq fell 4.5% and S&P 500 1.9%, weakest weeks since June.
- **AI Concerns**: Borrowing costs and data center returns pressured tech and AI stocks.
- **Slower Hike Path**: Third expected hike pushed from June to September 2027.
- **Week Ahead**: Housing starts, jobless claims, and preliminary S&P Global PMI readings.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does the strong September retail sales headline overstate consumer strength?
**A**: Much of the 0.8% gain came from higher gasoline prices, with gas stations adding 0.55 percentage points. Sales ex-gasoline rose only 0.2% and the control group just 0.1%, down from 1.2% in August.

**Q2**: What do the September inflation reports show about price pressures?
**A**: CPI rose 0.6% MoM (3.7% YoY) with energy up 5.2%, and PPI rose 1.3% MoM, the largest since March 2022. Core measures also exceeded expectations, indicating pressure broader than energy.

**Q3**: What do markets expect for Fed policy and how did Treasuries respond?
**A**: Markets still price three additional 25 bp hikes, with the first two in December 2026 and March 2027 and the third delayed to September 2027. Yields fell for the week on a flight to safety, with the 10-year down 15 bp to 5.09%.
