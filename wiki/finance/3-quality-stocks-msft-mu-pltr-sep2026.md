## Summary

The article identifies three quality stocks — Microsoft, Micron Technology, and Palantir — as well-suited for a high-borrowing-cost environment, filtering on strong balance sheets, high returns on equity, and resilient business performance. Each company demonstrates durable revenue streams tied to AI infrastructure demand, with management of capex and debt that sets them apart from weaker peers. Investors are cautioned about specific risks including regulatory exposure (MSFT), cyclical memory pricing (MU), and premium valuation (PLTR).

## Core Concepts

- **[[Quality Investing]]** — selecting stocks with strong balance sheets, high [[Return on Equity (ROE)]], and durable margins regardless of macro conditions
- **[[Microsoft (MSFT)]]** — ~$3.8T market cap; revenue driven by [[Azure]], [[Microsoft 365]], and [[Dynamics 365]]; three segments: Intelligent Cloud (~$138B), Productivity/Business Processes (~$140B), Personal Computing (~$54B)
- **[[Micron Technology (MU)]]** — ~$1.1T market cap; 50.1% ROE, 55.9% net margin; products include [[HBM]], [[DRAM]], and SSD; revenue leaders Cloud Memory (~$31.3B) and Mobile/Client (~$27.2B); long-term customer agreements reduce cyclical risk
- **[[Palantir Technologies (PLTR)]]** — ~$438.6B market cap; ~31% ROE, ~49% net margins; government revenue ~$3.2B, commercial ~$2.9B; zero debt on balance sheet
- **[[AI Infrastructure Demand]]** — common tailwind driving all three companies' growth and justifying elevated capital expenditure
- **[[Cyclicality Risk]]** — flagged for Micron given memory sector's historical boom-bust patterns and Chinese competition
- **[[Valuation Risk]]** — Palantir's premium multiple leaves limited room for growth disappointments

## Key Takeaways

- **Microsoft**: Three high-margin recurring revenue segments; AI/data center capex manageable given strong FCF.
- **Micron ROE**: 50.1% ROE and 55.9% net margin make it a standout in the memory sector.
- **Micron risk**: Chinese competition and peak-cycle risk are the main concerns flagged.
- **Palantir margins**: ~49% net margins; zero debt — balance sheet is a key quality marker.
- **Palantir caution**: Premium valuation leaves little margin of error if growth disappoints.
- **Shared theme**: All three benefit from AI workload demand — cloud, memory, and data analytics.
- **Screening criteria**: Strong balance sheets + high ROE + resilient performance in elevated-rate environment.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Margin of Safety]]**: Palantir's premium valuation narrows the margin of safety — the article explicitly warns that any growth shortfall is punished severely at stretched multiples.
- **[[Cyclicality vs. Durability]]**: Long-term customer agreements at Micron directly address the semiconductor sector's notorious boom-bust cycle, trading upside optionality for downside protection — a classic quality-vs-growth tradeoff.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What shared characteristic makes MSFT, MU, and PLTR "quality" picks in a high-rate environment?
**A**: All three feature strong balance sheets, high returns on equity, and resilient margins — characteristics that reduce vulnerability when borrowing costs remain elevated.

**Q2**: What specific metrics highlight Micron's quality, and what is its primary risk?
**A**: Micron's 50.1% ROE and 55.9% net margin are standout figures; the primary risks are Chinese competition and the semiconductor memory sector's cyclical pricing volatility.

**Q3**: Why does the article caution investors on Palantir despite strong financials?
**A**: Palantir's premium valuation leaves little room for growth disappointments — even solid performance may not justify the multiple if it falls short of elevated expectations.
