## Summary

On October 15, 2026 the [[US Dollar]] retreated from an 18-month high as investors looked past hot September [[Producer Price Index|PPI]], strong retail sales and stable jobless claims to focus on a selloff in AI data center and chip stocks. Treasuries rallied on safe-haven flows and cautious remarks from [[Federal Reserve]] officials, cooling bets on an October rate hike. Strategists argued much of the Fed hawkishness was already priced in, leaving limited upside for yields and the dollar.

## Core Concepts

- **Macro data**: US producer prices posted the biggest rise in 4-1/2 years in September, signaling inflation spreading beyond energy; [[Retail Sales]] beat expectations on gasoline prices; weekly jobless claims rose marginally.
- **FX moves**: The [[Dollar Index]] fell 0.44% to 102.00; the [[Euro]] rose 0.48% to $1.1210 from an 18-month trough; [[Sterling]] gained 0.45% to $1.3251 after UK GDP unexpectedly grew 0.1% in August; the [[Australian Dollar]] fell 0.88% to $0.6911 on weak jobs data; the [[Japanese Yen]] firmed 0.32% to 157.82.
- **Rate expectations**: [[CME FedWatch]] showed a 72% chance of an unchanged rate at the October 27-28 [[FOMC]] meeting vs. 53% a month earlier; hike odds spiked post-CPI then eased after [[Michelle Bowman]] and [[Mary Daly]] said the Fed could wait for clarity.
- **AI debt concerns**: Renewed worries over AI companies' heavy debt burdens drove [[Safe Haven Assets|safe-haven]] flows into [[Treasury Yields|Treasuries]].
- **Voices**: [[Joel Kruger]] (LMAX Group), [[Uto Shinohara]] (Mesirow Currency Management), and [[Juan Perez]] (Monex USA), who flagged [[Stagflation]] risk if Fed tools don't work.
- **Ahead**: Remarks from Fed Chair [[Kevin Warsh]] at IMF-World Bank meetings in Bangkok.

## Key Takeaways

- **Dollar Pullback**: Dollar index down 0.44% to 102.00 after 18-month high.
- **Hot PPI**: Producer prices rose most in 4-1/2 years, spreading beyond energy.
- **Resilient Consumer**: Retail sales beat on gasoline; jobless claims only marginally higher.
- **Priced-In View**: Fed hawkishness largely priced in, limiting yield and dollar upside.
- **Safe-Haven Bonds**: Chip-stock selloff supported bonds, capping yields despite inflation signals.
- **Hold Odds Rise**: 72% chance of October hold, up from 53% a month ago.
- **Fed Caution**: Bowman and Daly said Fed could wait for more clarity.
- **Stagflation Worry**: Ineffective Fed tools would raise stubborn stagflation fears.
- **Euro Bounce**: Euro on track for biggest daily gain in nearly two months.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Prices move on surprises relative to expectations, not on news itself** — Once a hawkish outcome is already reflected in market prices, further confirming data has limited capacity to push yields or currencies higher. · *model:* [[Expectations Investing]]
→ explains: Priced-In View, Dollar Pullback, Hot PPI

**T2. Risk-asset stress pulls capital into safe havens** — A selloff in risky equities drives flows into government bonds, which can hold yields down even against inflationary data. · *model:* [[Safe Haven Assets]]
→ explains: Safe-Haven Bonds, Dollar Pullback

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did the dollar fall on October 15, 2026 despite strong US inflation data?
**A**: Investors focused on a shakeout in AI data center and chip stocks while cautious Fed remarks cooled October hike bets, and strategists viewed Fed hawkishness as already priced in.

**Q2**: How did rate-hike expectations shift around the CPI and PPI releases?
**A**: Hike odds rose sharply after the hot CPI, but eased after Bowman and Daly said the Fed could wait; FedWatch showed a 72% chance of a hold versus 53% a month earlier.

**Q3**: What is the implication if inflation keeps running hot despite a hawkish Fed?
**A**: According to Monex USA's Juan Perez, if the Fed's tools aren't working, the worry shifts to stubborn stagflation.
