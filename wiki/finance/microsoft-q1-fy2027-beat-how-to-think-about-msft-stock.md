## Summary

[[Microsoft]] beat expectations on both revenue and adjusted earnings in its fiscal Q1 2027 report, with revenue up 18.8% to $92.28 billion. Barchart argues a good quarter does not make the stock a buy by itself, because the price already reflects expected growth. The article leans toward gradual buying rather than chasing the beat.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Microsoft]] (MSFT)**: Software, cloud, and devices company; market value of about $3.9 trillion.
- **[[Azure]]**: Revenue grew 48% in the quarter, the main driver of the beat.
- **Commercial remaining performance obligations**: Contracted future revenue up 73% to $677 billion.
- **[[Microsoft 365]] commercial cloud**: Up 17% as reported and 18% after normalizing a prior revenue recognition benefit.
- **Devices and Consumer**: Revenue of $15.5 billion, down 3%; search and advertising ex-traffic acquisition costs up 12%.
- **[[Expectations Investing]]**: Whether a beat rewards buyers depends on how much growth the price already assumes.
- **[[Price-to-Earnings Ratio]] vs. [[PEG Ratio]]**: Forward P/E looks cheap against the sector, but the PEG ratio shows the discount narrows once growth is counted.
- **[[Maia 300]]**: Microsoft's custom inference chip, with multi-gigawatt deployment planned from 2027.
- **[[Microsoft-Decision-1]]**: Lower-cost model for routing, classification, and output checks.
- **[[Michael Burry]]**: Critic of AI data center spending and off-balance-sheet obligations.

## Key Takeaways

- **Beat Quality**: Revenue of $92.28B beat the $90.64B consensus by 1.8%.
- **Earnings Beat**: Adjusted EPS of $4.89 came in ahead of estimates; GAAP EPS was $4.92.
- **Azure Growth**: Azure grew 48%, and Microsoft Cloud revenue rose 30% to $63.9B.
- **Backlog Expansion**: Commercial performance obligations rose 73% to $677 billion.
- **Valuation Discount**: Forward P/E of 27.03x trails the sector median of 29.85x.
- **PEG Caveat**: Higher 1.67x PEG suggests the discount disappears after growth.
- **Expectations Test**: A good quarter does not automatically justify buying at today's price.
- **Cost Cut Report**: Reported 20% Azure cuts are disputed; savings are not yet confirmed.
- **Cheaper Inference**: Microsoft-Decision-1 costs $0.042 per million input tokens.
- **Maia 300 Timing**: Deployment starts in 2027; expected 40% more tokens per megawatt.
- **Analyst Upgrade**: Stifel upgraded MSFT to Buy and raised its target to $575.
- **Spending Warning**: Burry questions AI spending and roughly $3 trillion in off-balance-sheet obligations.
- **Consensus Upside**: Average target of $594.15 implies about 12% upside from $530.14.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does Barchart say a strong quarter is not enough reason to buy Microsoft stock?
**A**: The price already reflects expected growth, so buyers need results that go beyond those expectations. The article says the real question is whether growth can justify the price paid today.

**Q2**: How does the valuation picture change when growth is factored in?
**A**: Microsoft's forward P/E of 27.03x trails the sector median of 29.85x, but its PEG of 1.67x versus 1.19x shows the discount disappears once expected growth is considered.

**Q3**: What should an investor do with this information, according to the article?
**A**: The article concludes that Microsoft looks worth buying gradually rather than chasing the earnings beat. It recommends measured expectations given the spending risk and roughly 12% upside to analyst targets.
