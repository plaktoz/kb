## Summary

Microsoft rolled out a redesigned [[Microsoft Copilot]] app merging office-productivity and coding tools into three components — Code, Cowork, and [[Autopilot]] — with the new tools billed by usage rather than flat seat pricing. Despite strong fundamentals (18% revenue growth to $90 billion, 43% [[Azure]] growth) and rising Copilot seat counts, MSFT stock is up only 9% year-to-date, trailing the Nasdaq and other megacap peers as investors weigh heavy capital spending against unproven AI returns. The article frames successful adoption of the unified app as the key lever for closing that performance gap.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Microsoft Copilot]]** — redesigned into one app merging office-productivity and coding tools; an idea [[Satya Nadella]] had floated as a Copilot "super app"
- **[[Cowork]]** — new Copilot component for handling complex, multistep tasks; billed by usage
- **[[Autopilot]]** — Copilot component for building custom agents usable in Teams and Outlook; billed by usage
- **[[Microsoft 365 Copilot]]** — remains priced at $30 per user per month while the new Cowork, Code, and Autopilot tools shift to usage-based billing
- **[[GitHub Copilot]]** — Microsoft's coding assistant, now reaching 50 million users, cited as context for folding coding into the unified app
- **[[Satya Nadella]]** — Microsoft CEO who describes the combined seat-plus-usage pricing approach as making the market opportunity "much more expansive"
- **[[Azure]]** — grew 43% in the June quarter, with about 45% growth projected next quarter
- **[[Capital Expenditures]]** — $41 billion spent last quarter; roughly $175 billion projected for calendar 2026, a central source of investor caution
- **[[MSFT Stock Performance]]** — up only 9% year-to-date as of Sept. 25, trailing the Nasdaq and other megacap tech peers despite strong financials

## Key Takeaways

- **Pricing shift**: Cowork, Code, and Autopilot billed by usage; M365 Copilot stays $30/user/month.
- **Low adoption**: Fewer than 7% of 450M+ commercial Office 365 seats pay for Copilot.
- **Seat growth**: Copilot paid seats topped 30 million; additions more than doubled quarter-over-quarter.
- **GitHub Copilot scale**: Coding assistant has reached 50 million users.
- **Financial strength**: Revenue grew 18% to $90 billion; Azure grew 43% (45% projected next quarter).
- **Capex concern**: $41B spent last quarter; ~$175B projected for 2026 fuels investor caution.
- **Stock underperformance**: MSFT up only 9% YTD, trailing Nasdaq and megacap peers.
- **Catch-up admission**: An unnamed Microsoft executive said folding coding in means playing catch-up.
- **Analyst view**: Price target of $577 versus a $516 share price (Sept. 25); 52-week high of $554.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What change did Microsoft make to Copilot, and how does the article frame it relative to the stock?
**A**: Microsoft unified its Copilot products into one app with Code, Cowork, and Autopilot components, and the article frames this launch against MSFT stock rising only 9% YTD and lagging megacap peers, suggesting adoption success could help close that gap.

**Q2**: How is Microsoft changing how it charges for the new Copilot components, and what do current adoption numbers show?
**A**: Cowork, Code, and Autopilot will be billed by actual usage instead of flat per-seat pricing (M365 Copilot stays $30/user/month), while fewer than 7% of Microsoft's 450 million-plus commercial Office 365 seats currently pay for Copilot, even though paid seats topped 30 million with additions doubling quarter-over-quarter.

**Q3**: What tension does the article highlight between Microsoft's fundamentals and its stock price?
**A**: Despite 18% revenue growth, 43% Azure growth, and rising Copilot/GitHub Copilot usage, investors remain cautious about heavy capital spending ($41B last quarter, ~$175B projected for 2026), holding MSFT to just 9% YTD gains versus faster-moving megacap peers.
