## Summary

U.S. stocks sold off sharply on Aug. 20, 2026 after long-dated Treasury yields resumed climbing, undoing relief from a Treasury buyback announcement the day before. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq fell roughly 1%, putting major indexes on pace for weekly losses, while Asian markets tracked the pullback lower on Friday.

## Core Concepts

The episode illustrates the limits of [[Treasury Buybacks]] as a tool to manage [[Bond Market]] stress: JPMorgan's James Sullivan compared the Treasury's move to "paying your mortgage with your credit card," since buying back long bonds while issuing more short bills doesn't reduce the underlying [[National Debt]] burden, it just reshapes its maturity profile. Simmering [[US-Iran Tensions]] added a geopolitical risk premium alongside the bond-driven volatility.

## Key Takeaways

- **Weekly losses**: S&P 500 down 1.9% and Nasdaq off 2.5% for the week, snapping a three-week winning streak.
- **Treasury relief was temporary**: Doubled buybacks briefly lowered yields Wednesday, but yields rebounded Thursday.
- **Structural critique**: JPMorgan calls the buyback approach a maturity reshuffle, not a debt-burden fix.
- **Geopolitical overlay**: Bessent vowed "the greatest coordinated economic isolation" against Iran, adding uncertainty.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of this update?
**A**: A renewed rise in Treasury yields drove a broad U.S. stock selloff on Aug. 20, 2026, despite a Treasury intervention meant to calm bond markets.

**Q2**: Why did the Treasury's buyback plan fail to durably calm yields?
**A**: Because buying back long-term debt while issuing more short-term bills only reshapes the maturity profile of outstanding debt — it doesn't reduce the total financing need, so it provided only temporary relief.

**Q3**: What's the practical implication for investors watching this period?
**A**: Equity markets remain highly sensitive to bond-market signals, and government interventions in debt markets may buy only short-term relief rather than resolving the underlying fiscal pressure.
