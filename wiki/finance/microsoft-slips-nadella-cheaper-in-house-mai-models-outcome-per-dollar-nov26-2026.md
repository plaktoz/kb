## Summary
Microsoft shares slipped 1.25% to $489.93 on Nov. 25 after [[Satya Nadella]] said its in-house MAI models, trailing frontier labs by three to six months while using 5% to 20% of the compute, are a great position. Copilot already routes among OpenAI, Anthropic and MAI models per task. The cost-first strategy answers fears of runaway AI capex, with fiscal Q2 results in January as the test.

## Core Concepts
- [[Microsoft]] (MSFT) — opened down 3.53% before recovering most of the loss
- [[Satya Nadella]] — spoke at the New York Times [[DealBook Summit]]; wrote "Outcome per dollar is the frontier"
- [[MAI]] models — seven announced at Ignite on Nov. 17; trail [[OpenAI]] and [[Anthropic]] by three to six months
- [[Copilot]] — chooses among OpenAI, Anthropic and MAI models per task; most users pick automatic
- [[Commoditization]] — Nadella argues AI should become a commodity like cloud computing
- [[GF Value]] — $606.01, implying a 19.2% discount

## Key Takeaways
- **Lag is acceptable**: Trailing frontier models by 3-6 months is fine at 5-20% of compute.
- **Model routing**: Copilot picks the model per task; users mostly accept automatic.
- **Outcome per dollar**: Nadella frames cost efficiency, not raw capability, as the frontier.
- **Margin defense**: Cheaper in-house models could hold AI gross margins as token prices fall.
- **OpenAI access**: Royalty-free access to a frontier model until 2032.
- **Next test**: Fiscal Q2 results in January show Copilot margins and Azure growth.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Nadella's argument about Microsoft's in-house AI models?
**A**: MAI models trailing OpenAI and Anthropic by three to six months is a great position if they use 5% to 20% of the compute. He says outcome per dollar is the frontier.

**Q2**: How does Copilot use multiple models?
**A**: Copilot chooses among OpenAI, Anthropic and MAI models for each task, and most users simply select the automatic option.

**Q3**: Why does this matter for investors?
**A**: It speaks to fears of runaway AI capex: if Copilot runs largely on cheaper in-house models, AI gross margins could hold even as token prices fall. Fiscal Q2 results in January will test it.
