## Summary

Meta Platforms has never split its stock since its 2012 IPO, and shares now trade around $721 — the highest of the Magnificent Seven — prompting speculation about whether a split could pave the way for Dow inclusion. The author argues a split is purely cosmetic and that price alone isn't the real obstacle to joining the Dow, since [[Alphabet]] already filled the communication-services slot after replacing Verizon in June. The piece concludes a near-term split is unlikely and wouldn't meaningfully change Meta's Dow prospects either way.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Meta Platforms]]**: Has never split its stock since its 2012 IPO; shares trade around $721, the highest among the [[Magnificent Seven]].
- **[[Stock Split]]**: A cosmetic corporate action that divides existing shares into more units without changing total company value or valuation multiples (e.g., P/E ratio stays constant).
- **[[Dow Jones Industrial Average]]**: A price-weighted index (unlike market-cap-weighted indices), meaning high nominal share prices carry outsized influence on the index.
- **[[Alphabet]]**: Replaced Verizon in the Dow in June, filling the communication-services slot that Meta might otherwise have occupied.
- **[[Amazon]]** and **[[Nvidia]]**: Both split their shares before joining the Dow, motivating the comparison to Meta.

## Key Takeaways

- Meta shares hit a record close near $790 in August 2025, still unsplit.
- A hypothetical 10-for-1 split would shrink EPS proportionally but leave P/E unchanged.
- Meta's closest prior split-like move was a 2016 nonvoting-share plan, abandoned in 2017.
- High-priced stocks like Goldman Sachs and Caterpillar already sit in the Dow, undercutting the "price is the obstacle" theory.
- At its current price, Meta could carry outsized weight in the Dow; a split would shrink that weighting to under 1%.
- Meta and Tesla remain the only Magnificent Seven members not yet in the Dow.
- Author's view: a near-term split is unlikely, and the Dow isn't a strong reason to pursue one.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about why Meta hasn't been added to the Dow?
**A**: The main obstacle isn't Meta's high share price but that Alphabet already filled the Dow's communication-services slot after replacing Verizon in June, leaving Meta and Tesla as the only Magnificent Seven stocks not yet included.

**Q2**: How does a stock split mechanically affect a company's valuation, according to the piece?
**A**: A split only divides existing shares into more units — it doesn't change total company value; a hypothetical 10-for-1 split would shrink EPS proportionally while the P/E ratio stays the same.

**Q3**: What would happen to Meta's influence on the Dow if it split its stock before joining?
**A**: Because the Dow is price-weighted, splitting would shrink Meta's index weighting to under 1%, whereas joining at its current unsplit price could give it outsized influence.
