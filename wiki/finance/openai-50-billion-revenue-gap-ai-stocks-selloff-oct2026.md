## Summary

[[Nvidia]], [[Broadcom]], [[Oracle]], and other AI-linked stocks fell sharply on October 8, 2026 after a [[Financial Times]] report that [[OpenAI]]'s annualized revenue was approaching US$50 billion at end of September, about US$20 billion below earlier media estimates of US$70 billion. The gap largely reflects [[Revenue Recognition]] differences — OpenAI reports cloud-partner revenue on a net basis while [[Anthropic]] books certain marketplace sales gross — not a collapse in business. The selloff shows how sensitive richly valued AI infrastructure stocks have become to any hint that AI revenue may not keep pace with chip and data-center spending.

## Core Concepts

- **[[OpenAI]]**: Told investors annualized revenue neared US$50 billion, with growth of more than 70% over the period covered.
- **[[Revenue Recognition]] (Gross vs. Net)**: Anthropic recognizes gross value of some cloud marketplace sales; OpenAI reports comparable partner revenue net, shrinking headline figures.
- **[[Anthropic]]**: Used as the comparison point for how partner revenue is reported.
- **[[Nvidia]]**: Dominant AI accelerator supplier; fell more than 2%.
- **[[Broadcom]]**: Exposed to custom AI chips and networking; fell nearly 4%.
- **[[Oracle]]**: Heavily committed to cloud and data-center expansion; lost more than 4%.
- **[[Microsoft]]**: Also moved lower; the [[Nasdaq Composite]] fell about 1.4%.
- **[[AI Infrastructure Spending]]**: Hundreds of billions invested in chips, networking, cloud capacity, and data centers that must eventually earn returns.
- **Expectations Reset**: When valuations price in huge growth, even a reporting difference can trigger sharp selling.

## Key Takeaways

- **Headline gap**: OpenAI run rate ~US$50B vs. ~US$70B in earlier media estimates.
- **Not lost revenue**: The US$20B gap is mostly net-versus-gross accounting.
- **Still growing fast**: OpenAI revenue grew more than 70% over the reported period.
- **Stock moves Oct 8**: NVDA -2%+, AVGO ~-4%, ORCL -4%+, Nasdaq ~-1.4%.
- **Core worry**: Can AI revenue justify massive chip and data-center capex?
- **Watch list**: OpenAI revenue updates, cloud demand, AI infrastructure orders, capex plans.
- **Verdict**: Not the end of the AI boom, but Wall Street demanding returns.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Expectations Investing]]**: Stock prices embed assumed future growth, so the market reacted not to OpenAI shrinking but to a gap between reported revenue and what prices already assumed.
- **[[Anchoring Bias]]**: Investors anchored on the earlier US$70 billion media figure, so a differently measured US$50 billion read as a shortfall even though the underlying business was growing over 70%.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why did Nvidia, Broadcom, and Oracle fall on October 8, 2026?
**A**: A Financial Times report said OpenAI's annualized revenue was approaching US$50 billion, about US$20 billion below earlier estimates, raising doubts about how quickly AI infrastructure spending will generate returns.

**Q2**: What explains most of OpenAI's US$20 billion revenue gap?
**A**: Reporting methodology: Anthropic recognizes the gross value of certain cloud marketplace sales, while OpenAI reports comparable partner revenue on a net basis, so the US$50 billion is a clearer measure rather than lost revenue.

**Q3**: What does this selloff imply for AI stock investors?
**A**: Highly valued AI stocks are now very sensitive to any sign revenue lags spending, so investors should track OpenAI revenue updates, cloud demand, infrastructure orders, and capex plans from Nvidia, Broadcom, Oracle, and Microsoft.
