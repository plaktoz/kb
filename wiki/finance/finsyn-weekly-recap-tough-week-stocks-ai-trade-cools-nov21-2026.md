## Summary
In the week ending Nov. 21, 2026, the [[S&P 500]] fell 8.6%, its worst week since April 2025, and closed more than 10% below its October record as AI and technology shares led a broad decline. High-quality bonds and defensive stock sectors cushioned the damage, and the 10-year yield fell to 4.92% while the [[Federal Reserve]] signaled a possible December rate hike. The advisor's perspective is that 10% pullbacks are normal and that the week showed the value of diversification.

## Core Concepts
- [[S&P 500]] correction: down 8.6% for the week, closing Friday at 6,910, up 0.9% for the year.
- [[Nasdaq Composite]], [[Dow Jones Industrial Average]], and [[Russell 2000]]: fell 10.0%, 8.0%, and 7.5%.
- [[Nvidia]]: revenue of $115.8 billion and an above-estimate outlook, yet shares fell about 23% on the week and 14% Friday on reports of a regulatory probe.
- [[Anthropic]] IPO: shares opened well above the offer price but gave back part of the gains by Friday, read as profit-taking in AI stocks.
- [[Treasury Yields]]: 10-year fell to 4.92% from 5.15%; 2-year fell to 4.63% from 4.88%.
- [[Defensive Sectors]]: consumer staples and utilities held up; Walmart, Target, and TJX reported solid results.
- [[Federal Reserve]] outlook: October minutes show almost all officials see more hikes as likely appropriate; the December 8-9 meeting is in focus.
- [[Diversification]] and [[Rebalancing]]: the advisor's framing for volatility.

## Key Takeaways
- **Worst Week**: S&P 500 fell 8.6%, declining all five days, the worst since April 2025.
- **AI Selling**: Technology sector fell about 12.5% despite Nvidia beating expectations.
- **Bond Cushion**: Treasuries rallied as investors sought safety; high-yield bonds dipped modestly.
- **Broad Declines**: All 11 sectors fell, but staples and utilities fell far less.
- **Economy Steady**: Retail sales rose 0.2%; jobless claims stayed low at 203,000.
- **Housing Softness**: Permits for new homes fell 7.4% amid higher mortgage rates.
- **Inflation Expectations**: Consumer one-year expectations fell to 3.7% from 4.7%.
- **Week Ahead**: October PCE, Q3 GDP update, and Beige Book on Nov. 25; markets close early after Thanksgiving.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Uncorrelated assets cushion portfolio losses** — When one asset class falls sharply, holdings that respond differently (safe-haven bonds, defensive sectors) offset part of the loss. · *model:* [[Diversification]]
→ explains: Bond Cushion, Broad Declines
