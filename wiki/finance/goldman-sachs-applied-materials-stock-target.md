## Summary

Goldman Sachs raised its 12-month price target on [[Applied Materials]] (AMAT) from $520 to $645, maintaining a buy rating at roughly 32x normalized EPS of ~$20/share. The upgrade reflects confidence in durable long-term demand driven by DRAM and HBM buildout, with AMAT posting record Q2 revenue of $7.91B (+20% YoY). Goldman's non-GAAP EPS estimate of $14.15 for 2026 sits ~6% above consensus, while AMAT's 2026 YTD gain of ~127% has already surpassed the average analyst target of ~$617.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Applied Materials]] (AMAT)**: Manufacturer of deposition, etch, and packaging equipment used across semiconductor fabrication — a direct bet on industry-wide [[capital expenditure]] cycles.
- **[[DRAM]] and [[HBM]] demand**: Primary demand driver cited by Goldman; memory chip investment is accelerating as AI workloads require high-bandwidth memory.
- **[[Goldman Sachs]] buy rating**: Target raised to $645 at 32x normalized EPS of ~$20/share, reflecting a valuation anchored to earnings durability rather than a single quarter.
- **[[Semiconductor Equipment]] cycle**: CEO Gary Dickerson noted customers are forecasting equipment demand two or more years out, with some plans extending to 2030 — unusually long visibility.
- **Export restrictions risk**: Advanced tool export controls and growing [[Chinese domestic semiconductor equipment]] competition cited as key downside risks.

## Key Takeaways

- Goldman raised AMAT price target to $645 from $520, buy rating maintained.
- AMAT Q2 revenue: record $7.91B, +~20% YoY; EPS $2.86 beat $2.68 estimate.
- Goldman 2026 EPS estimate: $14.15 (~6% above consensus).
- AMAT YTD 2026: ~+127% vs. S&P 500 ~+10%.
- Average analyst target: ~$617 across 29 analysts — stock already past consensus.
- Customer demand forecasts extend 2+ years, some to 2030.
- Key risks: export restrictions, Chinese domestic equipment competition.
- Next catalyst: August 13 earnings (consensus $3.39/share, $8.94B revenue).
