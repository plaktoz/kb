## Summary
Asian shares mostly gained on Thursday after a U.S. rally following the midterms, but AI-linked chip stocks kept falling, with the Kospi briefly down over 4%. The article argues the election matters little for the broad market long term, while flagging risks for Trump Media, the AI industry, defense, oil, and Elon Musk's businesses.

## Core Concepts
- [[Kospi]]: Briefly sank over 4%, trading 3.5% lower at 5,484.38 on chipmaker selling; [[Samsung Electronics]] fell 5.7% and [[SK Hynix]] 7.4%.
- [[Nikkei 225]]: Fell 0.5% to 61,914.53; [[SoftBank Group]] -5.3%, [[Tokyo Electron]] -7.7%, [[Advantest]] -9.4%.
- [[2026 US Midterm Elections]]: Republicans lost sole control; the S&P 500 rose 1.6% and the Dow surged over 1,500 points on Wednesday.
- [[Split Government and Markets]]: Investors generally prefer divided control because it lessens the risk of transformative economic change.
- [[AI Stock Valuation Risk]]: The Republican setback adds to worries that AI-boosted share prices have flown too high.
- [[Brent Crude]]: Fell 8.7% to $94.62 on hopes of a pullback from the [[Iran War]], hurting [[ExxonMobil]] and [[ConocoPhillips]].
- [[Defense Stocks]]: [[Lockheed Martin]] -6.9%, [[Northrop Grumman]] -4.5%, [[General Dynamics]] -2.6%.
- [[Elon Musk]] and [[Trump Media & Technology Group]]: Trump Media -24.5%, Tesla -3.3%, SpaceX -3.8%.

## Key Takeaways
- **Regional Split**: AI-exposed Asian markets fell; less exposed Hang Seng (+2.8%) and ASX 200 (+1.9%) rose.
- **Chip Selloff**: Kospi, Taiex (-1.6%), and Tokyo chip names extended AI-related losses.
- **Post-Midterm Pattern**: U.S. stocks usually gain after midterms regardless of the winner.
- **Fundamentals First**: Stock prices depend more on profits and interest rates than on Washington control.
- **Oil Drop**: Brent near $94, around its lowest in two months.
- **Clean Energy Lift**: First Solar +4.6% and Enphase +3.7% on Democratic Congress hopes.
- **Currency**: Dollar slipped to 157.96 yen; euro rose to $1.1256.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Fundamentals outweigh politics** — Over the long run, prices follow expected corporate profits and interest rates rather than who holds political power; politics matters mainly through the risk of economic change.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the article's core claim about the midterms and markets?
**A**: The election may not matter much for the broad market in the long term, since prices depend more on company profits and interest rates. Investors do prefer split control because it lessens the risk of transformative economic change.

**Q2**: What evidence shows the AI selloff diverged from the broader Asian rally?
**A**: The Kospi briefly fell over 4% as Samsung dropped 5.7% and SK Hynix 7.4%, while less AI-exposed markets climbed, with the Hang Seng up 2.8% and the ASX 200 up 1.9%.

**Q3**: What sectors face risk or opportunity after the Republican setback?
**A**: Risks include Trump Media, AI, defense, oil, and Musk-owned businesses, given possible Iran war pullback and investigations. Solar and wind companies such as First Solar and Enphase may benefit from Democratic control of Congress.
