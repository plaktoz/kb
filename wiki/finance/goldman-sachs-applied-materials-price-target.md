## Summary

Goldman Sachs raised its 12-month price target on [[Applied Materials]] (AMAT) to $645 from $520, maintaining a buy rating at ~32x normalized EPS of ~$20/share. The upgrade reflects confidence in long-term durability driven by DRAM and HBM demand, after record Q2 revenue of $7.91B (+20% YoY). AMAT has already surpassed the analyst consensus fair value, trading +127% YTD versus the S&P 500's +10%.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Applied Materials]] (AMAT)** — manufacturer of deposition, etch, and packaging equipment for chipmaking; a proxy bet on semiconductor industry capex
- **[[DRAM]] and [[HBM]] demand** — key demand drivers cited by Goldman; memory buildout fueling equipment orders
- **[[Goldman Sachs]] analyst upgrade** — price target raised to $645; non-GAAP EPS projection of $14.15 for 2026, ~6% above consensus
- **[[Semiconductor Capital Expenditure]]** — CEO Gary Dickerson noted customers providing demand forecasts 2+ years out, some extending to 2030
- **Export controls risk** — advanced tool restrictions and competition from domestic [[Chinese Semiconductor Equipment]] suppliers cited as key risks

## Key Takeaways

- Goldman raised AMAT target to $645 (~32x normalized EPS of ~$20).
- Q2 revenue: $7.91B (+20% YoY); EPS $2.86 vs. $2.68 expected.
- 2026 non-GAAP EPS forecast: $14.15 (~6% above consensus).
- AMAT +127% YTD; S&P 500 +10%.
- Customer demand visibility extends 2+ years, some to 2030.
- AMAT already exceeds 29-analyst average target of ~$617.
- Next catalyst: August 13 earnings (consensus $3.39 EPS, $8.94B revenue).
- Risks: export restrictions on advanced tools; Chinese domestic competition.
