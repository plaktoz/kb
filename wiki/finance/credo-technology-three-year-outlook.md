## Summary
Credo Technology sells the connectivity layer (active electrical cables, optics, and link-monitoring software) that wires AI GPU clusters together, and it more than tripled revenue in fiscal 2026. Its three-year outlook depends on continued hyperscaler capex and a crowded new-product pipeline, with analysts projecting revenue to rise from ~$1.3B to ~$3.9B by fiscal 2028. The business will likely be much bigger, but the stock's premium valuation means investors are betting the AI data center boom keeps going.

## Core Concepts
- [[Picks and Shovels Investing]]: Credo supplies the "extension cords" to the AI gold rush
- [[Active Electrical Cables]]: copper AECs for short in-rack hops, plus a growing optical lineup
- [[Link Flaps]]: intermittent connection failures that force cluster resets and cut GPU utilization
- [[Hyperscaler Capex]]: big-four spending is expected to rise from $413B to ~$745B in 2026
- [[Concentration Risk]]: the top two customers made up 61% of Q1 revenue
- [[Co-Packaged Optics]]: a technology shift that could shrink Credo's pluggable-component market
- [[PEG Ratio]]: a growth-adjusted valuation that looks cheaper than the trailing multiples

## Key Takeaways
- **Hypergrowth:** Revenue more than tripled in FY2026; management guides >85% growth this year.
- **Reliability moat:** PILOT software monitors every link in real time, like a check-engine light.
- **GPU waste:** Some customers lost 10–20% of GPU utilization to link flaps.
- **Faster bring-up:** One customer cut cluster bring-up from seven weeks to five days.
- **Medium-agnostic roadmap:** MicroLED 30m cables, gearbox chips, and dense in-rack optics by FY2028.
- **Revenue forecast:** Analysts expect ~$1.3B (FY2026) to grow to ~$3.9B (FY2028).
- **Competition:** Broadcom and Marvell compete for the same hyperscaler contracts.
- **Rich valuation:** 77x trailing earnings and 25x sales; PEG ~0.4; ~22x FY2028 EPS.

## 🃏 Review Questions
**Q: Why does the article expect Credo to be a much bigger company in three years?**
A: It sells essential AI-cluster interconnects while hyperscaler capex surges, and analysts project revenue rising from ~$1.3B to ~$3.9B by FY2028.

**Q: What problem does Credo's PILOT platform solve, and how much does it matter?**
A: It detects "link flaps" (intermittent connection failures) in real time. These can cost customers 10–20% of GPU utilization, and PILOT cut one customer's cluster bring-up from seven weeks to five days.

**Q: How should an investor weigh Credo's valuation against its risks?**
A: At 77x trailing earnings, Credo looks expensive. Its PEG of ~0.4 and ~22x forward earnings look cheaper only if AI capex continues and customer concentration, Broadcom/Marvell competition, and co-packaged optics don't hurt growth.
