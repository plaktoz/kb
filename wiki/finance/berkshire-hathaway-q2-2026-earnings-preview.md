## Summary

[[Berkshire Hathaway]] reports Q2 2026 earnings Saturday, its first full look at new CEO [[Greg Abel]]'s second quarter running the company, headlined by a $6.8 billion acquisition of homebuilder [[Taylor Morrison]] and continued share buybacks.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Greg Abel]]**: Succeeded Warren Buffett as Berkshire CEO; the Taylor Morrison deal and continued buybacks are read as early signals of his capital-allocation style.
- **[[Taylor Morrison]] acquisition**: A $6.8 billion bet on the US housing market, publicly praised by Buffett for being executed "faster" and "smoother" than he could have managed.
- **Share buybacks**: UBS estimates Berkshire repurchased $8.5 billion of shares between mid-April and mid-July; continued buybacks are viewed as a bullish signal since Abel has said he'll keep buying "as long as our intrinsic value exceeds the market value."
- **[[Alphabet]] stake**: Berkshire significantly expanded its Alphabet investment in June, a move Buffett told CNBC he personally initiated.

## Key Takeaways

- UBS raised its Berkshire Class B price target to $585 from $570, expecting a full recovery to new highs within 12 months.
- Berkshire shares have gained more than 10% since the start of June but remain below the ~$540 high set in May 2025, just before Buffett's planned step-down was announced.
- UBS expects "solid results," with higher earnings from the [[BNSF]] railroad unit and fewer insurance-arm catastrophe losses.
- Berkshire's cash pile hit a record $374 billion in Q1 and has likely declined following the Taylor Morrison and Alphabet purchases.
- Investors are watching this report closely for early evidence of how Abel's capital-allocation philosophy differs from Buffett's.
