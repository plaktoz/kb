# GE Aerospace's LEAP Engine Deliveries Jumped 41% This Year. Here's Why Boeing and Airbus Both Need That Number to Keep Climbing.

## Summary

[[CFM International]], the [[GE Aerospace]]/[[Safran]] joint venture, increased [[LEAP Engine]] deliveries 41% in H1 2026 versus H1 2025. The LEAP is the sole engine option for [[Boeing]]'s 737 MAX and one of two options for [[Airbus]]'s A320neo, making engine supply the binding constraint on both manufacturers' ability to clear enormous order backlogs. The production ramp pressures GE Aerospace's near-term margins even as it builds long-term aftermarket earnings potential.

## Core Concepts

- [[CFM International]]: joint venture between [[GE Aerospace]] and [[Safran]] that manufactures the [[LEAP Engine]].
- [[LEAP Engine]]: sole powerplant for the [[Boeing]] 737 MAX and one of two engine options for the [[Airbus]] A320neo family.
- Aerospace [[Supply Chain Bottleneck]]: engine deliveries, not airframe production capacity, currently cap how fast Boeing and Airbus can work through backlogs.
- [[Aftermarket Services Model]]: engine makers sell new units near or below cost and recoup profit later through long-term service agreements and spare-parts sales.

## Key Takeaways

- LEAP engine deliveries rose 41% in H1 2026 vs. H1 2025.
- Boeing holds ~4,381 737 MAX orders, about 7.8 years of backlog.
- Airbus holds ~7,500 A320neo orders, about 9 years of backlog.
- Engine supply, not airframe capacity, is the binding production constraint.
- Unfinished aircraft awaiting engines tie up manufacturer cash flow.
- New engines are sold near a loss; profit comes from aftermarket service revenue.
- Faster deliveries pressure GE Aerospace's near-term margins but grow its installed base.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core claim of the article?
**A**: LEAP engine delivery growth is the critical bottleneck determining how quickly Boeing and Airbus can convert their massive order backlogs into revenue.

**Q2**: What data point illustrates the scale of the backlog problem?
**A**: Boeing has roughly 4,381 737 MAX orders (about 7.8 years of production) and Airbus has roughly 7,500 A320neo orders (about 9 years of production), both effectively capped by LEAP engine supply.

**Q3**: What is the business implication for GE Aerospace itself?
**A**: Because new engines are sold near cost, ramping deliveries creates a short-term margin headwind for GE Aerospace, but it expands the installed base that drives higher-margin long-term service and spare-parts revenue.
