## Summary
The S&P 500 and Nasdaq are in correction territory (down about 10% and 17% from October highs) on AI-spending worries, persistent inflation and rate-hike expectations. New tariff proposals on 14 trading partners on Nov. 25 could keep inflation elevated and push the Federal Reserve toward a more hawkish stance. The author still points to strategist targets of 8,700 to 8,900 and advises treating dips as buying opportunities.

## Core Concepts
- [[Donald Trump]] tariff proposals: new import taxes on 14 major partners, including China and the European Union.
- [[Tariffs]]: studies attribute 0.5 points of headline and 0.2 to 0.4 points of core inflation to them.
- [[Federal Reserve]]: raised rates a quarter point in September to 3.75%-4%; another hike is widely expected in December.
- [[Inflation]]: persistent, with tariff effects feeding through supply chains slowly.
- [[Interest Rates]] and equity valuations: higher rates weigh on spending, earnings and stock valuations.
- [[AI Infrastructure Spending]]: investor worry about tech overspending is a driver of the sell-off.
- [[S&P 500]] and [[Nasdaq Composite]]: in correction, with Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and UBS targets well above current levels.

## Key Takeaways
- **Correction**: S&P 500 down roughly 10%, Nasdaq roughly 17%, from October highs.
- **New Tariffs**: Proposed Nov. 25 on 14 partners, including China and the EU.
- **Inflation Impact**: St. Louis Fed: tariffs added 0.5 points to headline inflation by June.
- **Who Pays**: New York Fed: nearly 90% of tariff burden falls on US firms and consumers.
- **Domestic Prices**: Tariffs also lifted domestic goods prices via imported inputs and less competition.
- **Lagged Effects**: Indirect price increases took up to 12 months to move through supply chains.
- **Rate Path**: Fed hiked in September; December hike widely anticipated per CME FedWatch.
- **Rate Impact**: Higher rates curb spending and make bonds more attractive than stocks.
- **Upside Targets**: Goldman and Morgan Stanley see 8,700 (21% upside); UBS sees 8,900.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Tariff costs land on domestic buyers** — Import taxes are paid mostly by domestic firms and consumers, and they also let domestic producers raise prices, so tariffs add to inflation. · *model:* [[Tax Incidence]]

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does the author say the new tariff proposals are bad news at a bad time?
**A**: Stocks are already in correction on AI-spending and inflation worries, and new tariffs could keep inflation high. That could force the Fed into a more hawkish response.

**Q2**: What evidence shows tariffs have fed inflation so far?
**A**: The St. Louis Fed estimates 0.5 points of headline inflation by June, and the Minneapolis Fed estimates 0.2 to 0.4 points of core inflation by July. The New York Fed says nearly 90% of the burden falls on US firms and consumers.

**Q3**: How does the author suggest investors respond?
**A**: Stay calm and treat significant dips as buying opportunities. Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and UBS targets of 8,700 to 8,900 imply 21% to 24% upside from 7,182.
