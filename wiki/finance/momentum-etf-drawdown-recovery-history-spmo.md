## Summary

The [[S&P 500 Momentum Index]] fell 10.6% from its June 22 record close into correction territory after a record-setting 44% second-quarter rally, driven by a rotation out of semiconductor and AI-infrastructure names. Historical data on the [[Invesco S&P 500 Momentum ETF]] ([[SPMO]]) shows that after each of the last 10 corrections, the fund rose 12.4% on average over the following three months and more than 24% a year later, with roughly 90% win rates — though average drawdowns of ~15% suggest more short-term pain is possible.

## Core Concepts

- **[[SPMO]]** — Invesco S&P 500 Momentum ETF; rebalances semi-annually and is one of the holdings in this portfolio
- **[[MTUM]]** — iShares Edge MSCI USA Momentum Factor ETF; rebalances quarterly, referenced as a comparison momentum vehicle
- **[[Momentum Drawdown Pattern]]** — historically large but short-lived, since underlying momentum constituents change relatively quickly
- **[[Sector Rotation 2026]]** — energy, retail, and bank stocks trending higher as AI-related momentum names struggle

## Key Takeaways

- **S&P 500 Momentum Index fell 10.6%** from its June 22, 2026 record close into correction territory.
- **Historical SPMO rebound**: average +12.4% over 3 months and +24% over 1 year after past corrections, ~90% win rate.
- **Average max drawdown in past corrections was ~15%**, suggesting the current pullback may not be finished.
- **Goldman's high-beta momentum basket fell over 30%** from its peak, more severe than SPMO itself.
- **Rebalancing could reshape momentum indexes** as soon as next month, changing which stocks qualify as "momentum."
