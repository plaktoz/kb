## Summary
CNBC frames [[Anthropic]]'s Nasdaq debut as the most important tech IPO in years, landing as rising rates, an AI backlash, Democratic midterm wins and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's ouster strain the industry. Anthropic carries over $900 billion in spending commitments and a $104 billion nine-month net loss, so the IPO could add over $90 billion to $132 billion of existing cash. Partners such as Google, Amazon and Broadcom are tied to the outcome through equity stakes, backstops and chip deals.

## Core Concepts
- [[Anthropic]]: five-year-old Claude maker, expected on the Nasdaq Thursday at $70-$78 a share.
- [[Initial Public Offering]]: could yield over $90 billion on top of $132 billion in existing cash.
- [[Compute Commitments]]: over $900 billion outstanding, including at least $110 billion with Amazon over a decade and $125-$150 billion of Google chips from 2028.
- [[Jensen Huang]] and [[Nvidia]]: Huang's ouster after reported affairs sent shares down 13%; interim CEO Colette Kress reports earnings Tuesday.
- [[Google]]: expected 13% owner and backstopper of $19 billion of [[TeraWulf]] lease commitments.
- [[OpenAI]]: the rival whose equally aggressive buying drives the rush for scarce GPU capacity.
- [[Broadcom]] chip financing: Bank of America, Blackstone, Citigroup and Morgan Stanley offered $63 billion for rented racks, with a Broadcom backstop of up to $43 billion.

## Key Takeaways
- **Scale of Obligations**: Over $900 billion committed; cloud partners are 31% of contractual obligations.
- **Funding Gap**: $104 billion net loss in nine months; IPO could raise over $90 billion.
- **Contract Length**: Amazon deal runs about ten years, twice the typical five-year contract.
- **Capacity Fear**: Anthropic says it previously underestimated compute needs and was capacity constrained.
- **Partner Stakes**: Seven suppliers invest $26 billion at the IPO price; Amazon's stake could be hundreds of billions.
- **Weak Backdrop**: Nasdaq fell 5.3% in October; Nvidia dropped 13% to its lowest since July 2025.
- **Google Backstop**: Google pays rent or termination fees if Anthropic becomes insolvent.
- **Skeptic View**: Beyond three to four years, contracts are "darts in the dark" and likely renegotiated.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Scarce capacity pushes buyers into long, early commitments** — When supply is limited and rivals are buying aggressively, locking in capacity early is rational even if long-dated terms will probably be renegotiated. 
→ explains: Capacity Fear, Contract Length, Skeptic View

**T2. A guarantee moves a counterparty's default risk onto the guarantor** — Backstops let financing happen, but the guarantor now carries the borrower's failure risk. · *model:* [[Counterparty Risk]]
→ explains: Google Backstop, Partner Stakes

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: Why does CNBC call Anthropic's IPO make-or-break for tech?
**A**: Almost every major tech company is tied to Anthropic as an investor, supplier or backstopper, and the IPO arrives during rate pressure, an AI backlash and Nvidia's CEO crisis. Anthropic also needs the proceeds to fund over $900 billion in commitments.

**Q2**: What numbers show how large Anthropic's funding need is?
**A**: It lost $104 billion in the first nine months of 2026 and holds $132 billion in cash. The IPO could add over $90 billion, against commitments including at least $110 billion with Amazon and $125-$150 billion for Google chips.

**Q3**: What risk does Google's lease backstop illustrate?
**A**: If Anthropic goes insolvent, Google must pay the remaining rent or a termination fee on its $19 billion of TeraWulf lease commitments. Partners' exposure to Anthropic therefore goes beyond their equity stakes.
