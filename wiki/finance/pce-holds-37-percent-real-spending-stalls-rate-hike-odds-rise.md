# PCE Holds at 3.7%, Real Spending Stalls: September Rate Hike Odds Rise to Four-in-Ten

## Summary

July's [[Personal Consumption Expenditures]] price index held at 3.7% year-over-year and [[Core PCE]] stayed at 3.3% for a fourth straight month, while real consumer spending grew less than 0.1% — a combination that pushed CME FedWatch September rate-hike odds from 33% to roughly 40% just before incoming Fed Chair [[Kevin Warsh]]'s first [[Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium]] keynote. Sticky [[Services Inflation]] (2.5% YoY, nearly double goods inflation) driven by wages and shelter, plus durable energy cost pressure from the [[Strait of Hormuz]] disruptions, is undercutting Warsh's thesis that AI-driven productivity gains will do the Fed's disinflation work for it. Three [[FOMC]] members already dissented in favor of an immediate July hike, and the August jobs report and CPI — due before the September 15-16 meeting — will likely decide whether four more members join them.

## Core Concepts

- [[Personal Consumption Expenditures]] price index — the Fed's formally targeted inflation gauge, released monthly by the Bureau of Economic Analysis.
- [[Core PCE]] — PCE excluding food and energy, the figure the [[Federal Reserve]] weights most heavily.
- [[Kevin Warsh]] — incoming Fed Chair whose disinflation thesis rests on AI-driven productivity gains.
- [[Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium]] — annual Fed policy conference where Warsh delivered his first keynote as Chair.
- [[Services Inflation]] vs. goods inflation — services (wages, shelter) respond to tightening on a 12-18 month lag, far slower than goods.
- [[FOMC]] dissent — Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari, and Dallas's Lorie Logan voted for an immediate July hike.
- [[Stagflation]] — simultaneous inflation persistence and demand weakness, the signal this data handed Warsh.
- [[Strait of Hormuz]] disruptions and the diesel crack spread — durable energy-cost pressure feeding into consumer prices.
- [[Walmart]] — its slowest US same-store sales growth in six years signals consumer pullback at the ground level.
- CME [[FedWatch]] tool — market-implied probability tracker that moved September hike odds from 33% to 40%.

## Key Takeaways

- **No progress**: Core PCE stuck at 3.3% YoY for four straight months (April–July).
- **Spending stall**: Real consumer spending rose just $1.3 billion, under 0.1%, in July.
- **Rate odds jump**: September hike probability rose from ~33% to ~40% post-release.
- **Hawkish dissent**: Three FOMC regional presidents already voted for a July hike.
- **Services lag**: Services inflation (2.5%) runs nearly double goods inflation (1.3%).
- **Consumer strain**: Walmart posted its weakest US same-store growth in six years.
- **Energy pressure**: Diesel crack spread hit a record near $102/barrel in August.
- **Saving up**: Personal saving rate ticked up to 3.0% as households pull back.
- **Warsh's test**: His AI-disinflation thesis is unproven within the current policy horizon.
- **Fed skepticism**: ~60% of economists surveyed see near-zero AI inflation impact over two years.
- **Decisive data ahead**: August jobs report and CPI, due before the Sept 15-16 FOMC meeting, will likely decide the vote.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Long and Variable Lags]]**: Milton Friedman's observation that monetary policy affects the economy with unpredictable delay explains why services inflation — tied to wages and shelter — is still elevated years into Fed tightening, even as goods prices have cooled within quarters.
