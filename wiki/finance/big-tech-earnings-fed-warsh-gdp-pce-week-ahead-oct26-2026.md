## Summary
The week of October 26, 2026 centers on Wednesday's [[Federal Reserve]] decision and a press conference by Chair [[Kevin Warsh]], alongside earnings from [[Alphabet]], [[Microsoft]] and [[Amazon]] that test whether AI spending is paying off. Thursday's Q3 GDP and Core PCE data arrive the same morning. Crude oil and rising bond yields remain the key risks.

## Core Concepts
- [[Federal Reserve]]: rate decision Wednesday 2:00pm; [[Kevin Warsh]] press conference 2:30pm.
- [[Core PCE]]: the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, released with preliminary Q3 [[GDP]] Thursday at 8:30am.
- [[Big Tech Earnings]]: Alphabet and Microsoft report Wednesday; Amazon and Intel Thursday.
- [[AI Capex Returns]]: cloud growth and capex guidance matter most.
- [[Crude Oil]]: Wednesday inventories at 10:30am; Exxon Mobil and Chevron report Friday.
- [[Treasury Yields]]: rising yields have raised expectations of a firmer Fed stance.
- [[Stagflation]]: weak growth with firm prices would revive these concerns.

## Key Takeaways
- **Fed Day**: Decision Wednesday 2:00pm, Warsh press conference 2:30pm.
- **Hawkish Risk**: A hawkish tone could pressure growth stocks reliant on lower borrowing costs.
- **Data Collision**: Q3 GDP and Core PCE land hours after the rate decision.
- **AI Test**: Strong guidance could steady chips and software; weak guidance deepens selloff.
- **Energy Link**: Higher oil adds inflation pressure and keeps yields elevated.
- **Consumer Read**: Coca-Cola reports Tuesday on whether consumers accept higher prices.
- **Industrial Read**: Boeing Tuesday, Caterpillar Thursday, Chicago PMI Friday.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What are the main events for markets this week?
**A**: The Fed decision and Warsh press conference Wednesday, Big Tech earnings from Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon, and Thursday's Q3 GDP and Core PCE data.

**Q2**: How could the GDP and Core PCE data shape the Fed outlook?
**A**: Strong growth with sticky inflation would strengthen the hawkish case, while weak growth would revive stagflation concerns and raise questions about the Fed's next move.

**Q3**: Why does oil matter for the Fed this week?
**A**: Higher energy prices add inflation pressure, which would complicate the Fed's path and keep bond yields elevated.
