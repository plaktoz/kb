## Summary

Adam Levy predicts [[Alphabet]]'s Google Cloud revenue will more than double year over year in Q3 (reported Oct. 28), after accelerating to 82% growth in Q2. He points to accelerating capex, third-party neocloud capacity, and direct [[TPU]] sales as drivers. He argues the stock, at 26 times forward earnings, is the cheapest of the big three cloud providers despite negative free cash flow.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Google Cloud]]** — On track to be Alphabet's largest driver of operating income growth in coming quarters.
- **[[Capital Expenditure Lag]]** — Data centers take about two years to build; servers take about three months to come online after purchase.
- **[[Revenue Backlog]]** — $514 billion; half expected to be recognized over 24 months, about $32 billion per quarter, nearer $50 billion if customers exceed commitments by 50%.
- **[[Neocloud]] bridging capacity** — CFO Anat Ashkenazi plans more third-party capacity in Q3, pressuring cloud margin.
- **[[TPU]] direct sales** — Inventory rose to $10 billion from $2.4 billion at the start of the year.
- **[[Free Cash Flow]] pressure** — Alphabet posted its first negative free cash flow as a public company last quarter.
- **Valuation** — 26 times forward earnings, lowest of the big three cloud platforms.

## Key Takeaways

- **Target**: 100% growth requires Cloud revenue above $30.3 billion, about $5.5 billion over Q2.
- **Capex signal**: Capex has accelerated every quarter since early 2025, implying accelerating revenue.
- **Capacity-constrained**: Servers are highly utilized immediately, per management.
- **Backlog**: $514 billion backlog; customers exceeding commitments by over 50%.
- **Margin tradeoff**: Third-party capacity pressures Cloud margin but captures more overall value.
- **TPU inventory**: Balance-sheet inventory of $10 billion implies contracted TPU sales ahead.
- **Valuation gap**: 26 times forward earnings despite faster cloud growth than rivals.
- **Cash flow risk**: Free cash flow expected to fall deeper into the red.

## 🧠 Core Principles

**T1. Capital spending leads revenue by a predictable build-and-deploy delay.** Because assets take time to become revenue-producing, sustained acceleration in spending signals later acceleration in revenue. · *model:* [[Leading vs. Lagging Indicators]]
