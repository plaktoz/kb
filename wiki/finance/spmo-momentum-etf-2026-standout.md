## Summary
The Invesco S&P 500 Momentum ETF ([[SPMO]]) outperformed both the broader S&P 500 and its closest peer MTUM over the trailing 12 months (26.89% vs. 17.43% and 26.18% respectively) as momentum emerged as one of 2026's strongest equity factors, drawing $3.59 billion in three-month net inflows.

## Core Concepts
- **[[SPMO]]**: Invesco's S&P 500 Momentum ETF, which selects and weights holdings by risk-adjusted price momentum rather than market cap, drawing exclusively from S&P 500 constituents.
- **[[Factor investing]]**: An investing approach targeting specific return drivers (momentum, value, quality) rather than broad market-cap-weighted exposure; momentum was the standout factor in 2026 amid macro uncertainty.
- **Semiannual rebalancing**: SPMO's index refreshes twice a year, keeping the portfolio aligned with evolving market leadership as momentum characteristics shift.

## Key Takeaways
- **SPMO returned 26.89%** over the trailing 12 months versus 26.18% for MTUM and 17.43% for the S&P 500 Total Return Index.
- **$3.59B in three-month net inflows** reflect investor rotation into momentum as leadership broadened beyond mega-cap tech into industrials, financials, and cyclicals.
- **Construction difference matters**: SPMO's S&P-500-only, momentum-weighted design captures more concentrated exposure to leading stocks than MTUM's broader large-and-midcap universe.
- **Low cost**: SPMO carries a 0.13% expense ratio, competitive for a factor-based strategy.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is the core reason momentum has outperformed in 2026?
**A**: Persistent macro uncertainty around inflation, rates, valuations, and earnings has pushed investors toward companies already showing sustained relative strength, and market leadership has broadened beyond mega-cap tech into industrials, financials, and cyclicals.

**Q2**: How does SPMO's methodology differ from MTUM's?
**A**: SPMO selects exclusively from S&P 500 companies and weights by momentum characteristics, while MTUM draws from a broader large- and mid-cap universe using a risk-adjusted momentum approach.

**Q3**: As an SPMO holder, what should this data suggest about position monitoring?
**A**: SPMO's edge has come from higher exposure to the market's strongest performers via momentum weighting and semiannual rebalancing — a strategy that outperforms in trending markets but could underperform sharply if leadership rotates suddenly between rebalances.


## Update (2026-08-08, additional source: Seoul Economic Daily — Aug 7)

A Korean feeder product tracking the same underlying index — the "KIWOOM US [[S&P 500]] Momentum" ETF, nicknamed "Korea's [[SPMO]]" — recorded a 22.95% year-to-date return as of August 6, more than 10 percentage points ahead of the S&P 500 (12.42%) and 6.42 points ahead of the Nasdaq 100 (16.53%) over the same period. The fund compresses the roughly 100 top S&P 500 stocks by risk-adjusted momentum and rebalances twice yearly (March, September); its March rebalance saw 54.5% stock-level turnover, raising IT-sector weight from 33.2% to 44.4% while cutting financials from 19.5% to 6.3% — adding [[Micron]] as top holding while trimming [[Meta]], [[JPMorgan]], and [[Visa]]. Kiwoom Asset Management's Lee Kyung-jun called momentum "an index investment method optimized for the AI era," where winners keep changing and systematic rebalancing captures new leaders.
