## Summary

At the annual Hot Chips conference, [[IBM]] announced the first dual-architecture mainframe processor, designed to let enterprises run operating systems and applications on both IBM and [[Arm]] compute platforms in future [[IBM Z]] and [[LinuxONE]] systems — the first processor milestone from the IBM-Arm collaboration established in April 2026.

## Core Concepts

- **Dual-architecture processor**: A single mainframe processor supporting both IBM's native architecture and Arm-based compute, rather than requiring separate hardware for each.
- **[[IBM Z]] and [[LinuxONE]]**: IBM's mainframe and Linux-native enterprise system lines, positioned as the target platforms for this new processor.
- **IBM-Arm collaboration**: Established April 2026, aimed at enabling enterprises to run workloads across both compute platforms within IBM's enterprise systems.

## Key Takeaways

- **First milestone**: This processor is the first concrete product outcome of the April 2026 IBM-Arm partnership.
- **Enterprise motivation**: Positioned as extending the systems that have "made IBM systems the foundation for how businesses run today" to interoperate with Arm-based compute.
- **Venue**: Announced at Hot Chips, a conference focused on high-performance and specialized processor architecture.
