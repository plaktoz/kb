## Summary

A federal judge rejected the [[Department of Justice]]'s attempt to force [[Alphabet]] to divest its ad exchange and related tools, removing a major structural risk for shareholders. Despite the ruling generating little market enthusiasm amid a broader downtrend, advertising still accounts for 68% of Alphabet's Q2 2026 revenue, making its preservation critical to funding the company's massive AI and cloud investments. The gradual, organic decline of Google Advertising's revenue share — rather than a forced divestiture — gives Alphabet a smoother transition toward cloud and autonomous vehicle revenues.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Alphabet]] (GOOGL)** — parent company of [[Google]], facing antitrust scrutiny over its digital advertising business
- **[[Google Advertising]]** — Alphabet's dominant revenue segment (68% of Q2 2026 revenue), including its ad exchange and ad tools
- **[[Google Cloud]]** — fast-growing segment at 21% of Q2 2026 revenue, increasingly important to Alphabet's future
- **[[Waymo]]** — Alphabet's autonomous vehicle unit; CEO [[Sundar Pichai]] expects it to contribute meaningfully to revenue as soon as next year
- **[[Ad Exchange Divestiture]]** — the DOJ-rejected proposal that would have forced Alphabet to sell its ad exchange and related infrastructure
- **[[Free Cash Flow]]** — Alphabet's trailing 12-month FCF reached $53 billion as of Q2 2026, underpinned by advertising revenue
- **[[Capital Expenditures]]** — $132 billion in capex over the trailing 12 months, reflecting Alphabet's aggressive AI infrastructure spending

## Key Takeaways

- **Antitrust Win**: Federal judge rejected DOJ's bid to force Alphabet to divest its ad exchange.
- **Advertising Dominance**: Google Advertising = 68% of Q2 2026 revenue, down from 74% a year prior.
- **Long-term Decline**: Ad share has fallen from 89% a decade ago — gradual, not abrupt.
- **FCF vs. Capex**: $53B trailing FCF vs. $132B capex — advertising keeps the books positive.
- **Waymo Catalyst**: Sundar Pichai signals Waymo revenue contribution possible next year.
- **Smooth Transition**: Organic ad decline lets Alphabet pivot to cloud/AV without a disruptive forced sale.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Margin of Safety]]**: The antitrust ruling eliminates the worst-case scenario for Alphabet's moat — preserving optionality for a multi-year transition, which is the core of value investing in incumbent tech.
- **[[Competitive Moat]]**: Google Advertising's sheer scale ($53B FCF) funds Alphabet's AI and cloud bets — the ad engine is the moat that pays for the next moat being built.
