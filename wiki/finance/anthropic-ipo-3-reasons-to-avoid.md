## Summary

Motley Fool contributor Sean Williams argues investors should avoid [[Anthropic]]'s upcoming IPO, which targets a ~$2 trillion valuation potentially surpassing [[SpaceX]]'s record $1.77 trillion listing. Three historical patterns form the core bear case: tech IPOs routinely suffer steep first-year drawdowns, price-to-sales ratios above 30 are historically unsustainable, and every major technology since the 1990s internet boom has triggered an early-innings bubble-bursting event.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Anthropic]] IPO**: Confidentially filed to go public; targeting a ~$2 trillion valuation and potentially the largest IPO in history, surpassing SpaceX's record.
- **Tech IPO Drawdowns**: Truist Financial studied the last 30 major tech IPOs since mid-2012 and found an average year-one maximum drawdown of 55%.
- **[[Price-to-Sales Ratio]]**: Three decades of market data show companies cannot sustain P/S ratios above 30 for long; Anthropic at $2T would debut well above that threshold relative to its trailing revenue.
- **[[AI Bubble]]**: Every transformative technology since the 1990s internet boom — including dot-com, mobile, genomics, and crypto — has undergone an early-innings bubble-bursting correction driven by investors overestimating adoption speed.
- **Near-Term Profitability Pressure**: Despite reporting a small Q2 adjusted profit on over $11.5 billion in sales, Anthropic's infrastructure costs will weigh on sustained near-term profitability.

## Key Takeaways

- Truist Financial: average year-one maximum drawdown for major tech IPOs is 55%.
- Anthropic's $2T target valuation exceeds a 30x price-to-sales ratio historically shown to be unsustainable.
- AI adoption speed is likely being overestimated — a pattern repeated with every prior transformative technology.
- Anthropic reported a small Q2 adjusted profit on $11.5B+ in sales, but infrastructure costs remain a headwind.
- Author concludes Anthropic may be a great company but a poor near-term IPO investment based on historical base rates.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Base Rate Neglect]]**: Investors focusing on Anthropic's growth narrative ignore the 55% average first-year drawdown base rate for major tech IPOs — the inside view (company-specific optimism) crowds out the outside view (historical distribution).
- **[[Mean Reversion]]**: Decades of data show P/S ratios above 30 revert downward; Anthropic's debut valuation would be structurally exposed to this force regardless of business quality.
