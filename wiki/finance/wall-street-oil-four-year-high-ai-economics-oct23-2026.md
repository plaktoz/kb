## Summary
Wall Street closed lower Friday as Brent crude hit $116.72, its highest since June 2022, with nearly three in four S&P 500 stocks falling and the index posting a second straight weekly loss. Investors are increasingly questioning AI investment economics while high rates and stubborn inflation keep Fed hike expectations alive. A war with Iran has severely cut global oil supply, driving up costs across the economy.

## Core Concepts
- [[S&P 500]]: fell 0.5% (39.75 points) to 7,623.68; the Dow fell 1.2% to 52,215.16; the Nasdaq slipped 0.2% to 25,955.34.
- [[Brent Crude]]: up 3.1% to $116.72, from under $97 at the start of October.
- [[AI Investment Economics]]: worries over upfront infrastructure costs, debt-funded spending, and uncertain return timing, per Edward Jones strategist Angelo Kourkafas.
- [[Federal Reserve]]: raised rates at its last meeting; expected to hold next week with a December hike heavily favored.
- [[Treasury Yields]]: the 10-year edged up to 4.99% from 4.96%.
- [[Inflation]]: stubbornly above 3%; PCE expected at 3.7% for September versus the 2% target.
- [[Iran War]] oil supply shock: cut world oil supply and lifted gasoline, food, and clothing costs.

## Key Takeaways
- **Broad Decline**: Nearly 75% of S&P 500 stocks fell as oil climbed.
- **Oil Surge**: Brent up from under $97 to $116.72 in October.
- **AI Divergence**: Micron +2.5%, Intel +1.7%, Microsoft +0.7%, AMD +1.7% cushioned the market.
- **Financial Weakness**: American Express fell 3.7% on first major job cuts in about a decade.
- **Energy Winners**: Chevron rose 2.9%; ConocoPhillips gained 3.6%.
- **Rate Outlook**: Fed expected to hold next week, hike likely in December.
- **Earnings Test**: Alphabet, Microsoft, and Amazon report next week on AI payoffs.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What drove Wall Street lower on Friday?
**A**: Oil prices climbed to their highest level in over four years, with Brent at $116.72, pressuring the broader market. Nearly three of every four S&P 500 stocks fell.

**Q2**: Why are investors worried about AI-related stocks?
**A**: They question whether high prices are justified, since many companies borrow to fund AI investment and high interest rates make those loans costlier. Returns on the large upfront infrastructure costs are uncertain in timing.

**Q3**: What events next week could shift the market outlook?
**A**: Earnings from Alphabet, Microsoft, and Amazon, the Fed meeting, and the PCE inflation reading (expected 3.7% for September) are all due.
