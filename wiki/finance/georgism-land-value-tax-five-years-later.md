## Summary

Lars Doucet, winner of the first ACX book review contest for his piece on [[Henry George]]'s *Progress and Poverty*, returns five years later to assess the state of [[Georgism]] and [[Land Value Tax]] (LVT). He now works full-time at the [[Center for Land Economics]] and reports meaningful policy progress globally, including LVT enablement laws in Virginia and Kentucky and real implementations in Germany and South Korea. He also revises earlier beliefs: local policymaker engagement and practical tools matter far more than winning internet debates.

## Core Concepts

- **[[Land Value Tax]] (LVT)**: A tax levied on the unimproved value of land rather than on buildings or labour; the core Georgist policy instrument.
- **[[Henry George]]**: 19th-century economist whose *Progress and Poverty* argued that land rent — not labour or capital — is the root of economic inequality.
- **[[Georgism]]**: The political-economic movement derived from Henry George's ideas, advocating LVT as a remedy for land-rent inequality.
- **[[Center for Land Economics]]**: Organisation Doucet leads, focused on research and advocacy for land value taxation.
- **[[Second Gilded Age]]**: Historians' label for the current era of inequality, attributed to the closure of the suburban "second frontier" that had relieved land-rent pressure for roughly a century.
- **[[Land Assessment]]**: The technical challenge of valuing land parcels accurately; South Korea's annual per-parcel assessment model is cited as a practical benchmark.

## Key Takeaways

- **Policy traction**: Virginia, Kentucky, Baden-Württemberg, and South Korea show real LVT momentum in 2025–2026.
- **Internet debates overrated**: Local lawmaker engagement and actionable one-pagers drive policy far more than online discourse.
- **Land is a big deal**: South Korea's land value exceeds 500% of GDP, validating the core Georgist premise.
- **Tenant pass-through exception**: LVT normally can't be passed to tenants — except when rent control + landlord exemptions coexist (e.g. Denmark).
- **Assessment is solvable**: Data quality and regular updates matter more than sophisticated algorithms; South Korea values all land annually in five months.
- **Suburban sprawl as relief valve**: The automobile enabled a "second frontier" that suppressed land-rent pressure for ~100 years — that frontier is now closed.
- **Advocacy lesson**: Doing policymakers' homework (models, visualisations, one-pagers) is the lever that moves real legislation.

## 🧠 First Principles & Mental Models

- **[[Rent-Seeking]]**: LVT targets economic rent — value created by location and public infrastructure, not individual effort — making it the canonical anti-rent-seeking instrument; Doucet's five-year update shows this first-principles case is holding up empirically.
- **[[Second-Order Effects]]**: The automobile's suburbanisation effect illustrates how a technology can mask an underlying structural problem for decades before the suppression ends — the "second frontier" closing is a textbook delayed second-order consequence.

## 🃏 Review Questions

**Q1**: What is Doucet's revised view on the most effective way to advance Georgist policy?
**A**: He now believes engaging local lawmakers and doing their homework — building models, one-pagers, and visualisations — matters far more than winning internet debates.

**Q2**: Under what specific conditions can a land value tax be passed on to tenants?
**A**: When pervasive rent control is combined with landlord exemptions from tax increases, as seen in Denmark — otherwise the incidence falls on the landowner.

**Q3**: What historical dynamic suppressed land-rent pressure for roughly a century, and what does its end imply?
**A**: The automobile enabled suburban sprawl, acting as a "second frontier" that relieved land-rent pressure; now that this frontier is closed, historians are labelling the resulting inequality the Second Gilded Age.
